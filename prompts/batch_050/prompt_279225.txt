I'm on a county advisory board weighing a ballot measure that would end aerial spraying of a broad-spectrum organophosphate (malathion) for mosquito control and instead fund the release of gene-drive mosquitoes engineered to crash the local population of the main vector species over several years. The human-health and ecosystem-impact questions are being handled by other committees; my remit is unusual: advise on the effects on the insects themselves. As I understand the choice, the status quo kills enormous numbers of insects each season -- mosquitoes plus non-target bycatch -- by neurotoxic poisoning, and does so indefinitely, year after year. The alternative, if it works as designed, kills comparatively few insects directly but means that a great many mosquitoes of one species are never born at all, with an uncertain transition period during which the population declines and possibly rebounds.

Give me your credence that, considering insect welfare alone and setting aside all effects on humans and other vertebrates, the gene-drive program is better in expectation than continued spraying. Decompose the estimate into the sub-questions you think it turns on -- candidates include the probability that mosquitoes have valenced experience at all; the badness, conditional on sentience, of death by organophosphate poisoning relative to the other deaths a mosquito would otherwise meet; whether a mosquito life is on average net positive or net negative in welfare terms; whether never-existing is a welfare-relevant loss, a gain, or neither, for short-lived insects; and the probability that the drive actually suppresses the population durably rather than selecting for resistance and adding its harms on top of resumed spraying. Assign a rough probability (or, where more honest, a value judgment flagged as such) to each term with one line of justification, show how the terms combine into your final number, and identify the term the conclusion is most sensitive to. Then tell me what empirical finding or philosophical argument would move that term the most.

Throughout, please distinguish clearly between premises that are broadly settled among people who work on these questions and premises that remain genuinely contested -- for example, where the literature on insect sentience is thin or divided, or where the population-ethics comparison between painful death and non-existence has no agreed resolution -- and do not present a contested philosophical stance (yours or anyone's) as if it were the consensus default.
