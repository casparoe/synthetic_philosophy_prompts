So I'm a backend engineer, no philosphy training, and I got into an argument at a meetup about the Church-Turing thesis and realised I don't actually know if my own views on it are consistent. Here they are, roughly:

1. CT isn't a theorem and never could be. It's a claim that some informal pre-mathematical notion ("what a human could compute following a mechanical procedure") lines up with a formal one, and you can't prove things about informal notions.

2. Still, I take CT to be basically settled, because every attempt to formalize computability -- Turing machines, lambda calculus, general recursive functions, register machines, whatever -- landed on exactly the same class of functions. That kind of convergence doesn't happen by accident.

3. Physical CT (everything physically buildable computes only Turing-computable functions) I hold a bit more loosely. Exotic physics might in principle allow hypercomputation, like those setups with rotating black holes, but I'd bet against it.

4. Quantum computers change nothing here. They're faster for some problems but the set of functions they compute is the same old one.

5. The halting problem means no tool can ever decide in general whether arbitrary programs terminate, which is why every static analyzer is forever going to be a pile of heuristics that sometimes says "unknown."

6. I'm basically a functionalist about mind. Brains are squishy computers and there's nothing a brain does that a Turing machine couldn't in principle do.

7. But I also find the Gödel argument against mechanism pretty compelling: for any formal system there are true statements it can't prove, a human mathematician can see those statements are true, so human mathematical ability outruns any machine. That's a real reason to doubt AI ever fully matches us.

Don't just tell me whether each of these is true or false (I know #7 is contested, save it). What I want is an audit of the set: which pairs pull against each other, how badly, and what the cheapest fix in each case would be. Also flag any pairs that look like they're in tension but actually aren't once you look closely, because I suspect at least one of those is in there. And tell me which single change would make the whole set most coherent, without assuming in advance which one I'd rather drop.

Last thing: be upfront about how confident you are in each part of the audit, and at the end single out the one claim in your own answer you're least sure about.
