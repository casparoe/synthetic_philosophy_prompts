Backend dev here, no philosophy background, so forgive me if this is a dumb question. Got into an argument with a coworker about the nature of time at like 1am during an oncall shift, and I realized I hold a view I can't find a name for anywhere.

Here's the picture. I think the past is real. The deploy that broke prod last Tuesday isn't just a story we tell each other, it actually happened and it's still out there in some sense, like an old commit in the repo. The future is NOT real yet, there's no commit for it, not even a draft. So far I guess this is some middle position between "only right now exists" and "everything past and future already exists in one giant block" (which I gather from youtube people call the block universe?).

But here's the part I can't find anyone else saying. I don't think the past stays fully real forever. I think it degrades. Like how we compact old logs: you keep every request for a week, then hourly aggregates for a month, then eventually just one number per day. The fine-grained texture of last Tuesday is already gone, and in a million years the difference between the two lunches I almost had today won't be part of reality at all, it just gets smeared into coarse averages. The past is real but lossy, and it gets lossier the further back you go. The present is where full detail gets written, and from there it decays.

So: does this have a name? Everything I've come across is either "past and future are both fully real forever," or "only the present exists," or "the past exists and keeps growing but stays exactly as it was." Nobody seems to hold my lossy-compression version. If it's a known position, does anyone actually defend it? And if nobody does, what's wrong with it, because it seems obviously right to me. It would even explain why memory gets fuzzier the further back you go (kidding. mostly).

Also one thing I keep getting stuck on: on my view, are statements about deep-past events still true or false, just super underdetermined in their details? Or does the smearing mean they're somehow not quite either? I'd definately rather get the actual carving than just a label -- what exactly is the thing I'm describing, what are the closest established positions and how does mine differ from each one, and where does my version break?
