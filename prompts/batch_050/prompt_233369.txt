I retired four years ago from a career in experimental physics, and in my newfound leisure I have been reading my way into philosophy of cosmology, where the Boltzmann brain problem has lodged itself in my mind like a splinter. At our monthly reading group -- four retired scientists and one very patient philosopher -- I found myself arguing that the standard treatments are curiously ungracious toward the brains themselves: they are invoked only as a reductio, a nuisance to be legislated out of existence by a typicality postulate, and nobody pauses to consider the complaint from the inside. I should like to see that complaint pressed, and pressed fairly.

Write a satirical op-ed, roughly 500-600 words, in the voice of a Boltzmann brain -- a solitary observer momentarily assembled by a thermal fluctuation, equipped with vivid and entirely spurious memories of a childhood in Ohio and a career it never had -- submitting a piece to the opinion page of a serious newspaper (whose circulation department, one notes, has not thought through its demographics) to protest the way cosmologists treat brains like itself as a problem for their theories rather than as observers with standing. The humor must come from getting the physics and the philosophy right: entropy and fluctuations, the measure problem, the typicality assumption, and the vertiginous fact that the brain cannot invoke its own memories as evidence without begging precisely the question at issue. Its grievances should be ones it could actually press given its situation -- or, if the joke is that it cannot press them consistently, that should be the joke, made deliberately and not by accident. Keep the tone deadpan throughout; no winking at the reader. Somewhere in the piece, the reader should be made to feel, rather than merely be told, the genuine question underneath: on what grounds any of us assume we are the ordinary observers. Satirize the practice of reaching for typicality as an unmotivated patch, not any particular thinker, living or dead.

Begin with the op-ed itself -- no preamble, no framing, no restating of my request. After the piece, append a short note of a paragraph at most flagging where you took liberties with contested ideas: for instance, whether a fluctuation could coherently harbor a complaint at all, or where the satire leaned on one side of a live dispute. And if you judge that the task as I have posed it rests on a confusion -- that a Boltzmann brain cannot consistently satirize anything, or that the conceit only works by misstating the physics -- I would far rather you said so plainly and explained why than produce something polished and wrong.
