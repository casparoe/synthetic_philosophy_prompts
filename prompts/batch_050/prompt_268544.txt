I'm working on a novel set in the last century or so of the Classic Maya lowlands, and my main character is an aging scribe in a city where the king hasn't raised a carved monument in decades, the ballcourt's half overgrown, and whole neighborhoods are emptying out toward the north. Here's the thing: I want him to be someone who would find our word "collapse" for all this baffling or even offensive. Not in denial, not stupid -- he has a genuinely worked-out way of seeing it (dynasties come and go, people move, the rains failed before and came back, his grandkids are doing fine somewhere else).

Play him. I'll interview him as a kind of traveling trader-character so I can hear how he actually talks, and you stay in character until I say stop. Constraints: he only knows what a literate city-dweller of that world could know, so no foreshadowing our modern timeline, no awareness he's in a "period" with a name, and he shouldn't suddenly become a 21st-century archaeologist lecturing me about resilience theory. He can be warm, dry, a bit vain about his craft. If I ask something he couldn't know, deflect the way he would.

One meta thing: if you think the premise is broken -- like, if a person in his position just couldn't plausibly hold this "it's change, not collapse" view, or I'm romanticizing something -- say so out of character first rather than playing along politely.

My opening move, in character: "Old man, I've walked through six cities on this road and every one is emptier than the last. Your king's stones stopped speaking before my beard came in. Why do you sit here copying dates for a lord nobody obeys anymore? Admit what everyone can see: this world is ending."
