My classmates have all done intro epistemology, so they know Gettier cases and reliabilism and the basic skepticism arguments, but zero Indian philosophy. Their default assumption will definately be that "intrinsically valid" means "can't be wrong," which is exactly backwards, since as I understand it the Mīmāṃsā line on error is that a false cognition is incomplete rather than positively wrong — you saw a piece of the truth and missed the rest.

If validity is intrinsic for all cognitions including testimony, then testimony with a human author inherits that author's fallibility — so scripture that's never wrong pretty much has to be authorless.
