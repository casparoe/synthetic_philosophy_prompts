So I help run a monthly "big ideas" night at our local library -- totally informal, mostly adults, no degrees required, usually somewhere between 12 and 16 people, and we get the room for about 75 minutes. Next month is my turn and I want to do something on how minds evolved. I got hooked on this idea I found in a pop-science book (I think the ladder originally comes from a philosopher named Dennett but don't quote me on that): creatures come in roughly four kinds. First there are things that just get shaped by selection over generations, no learning at all -- hardwired, take it or leave it. Then creatures that can learn from trial and error within their own lifetime, like a rat in a maze. Then -- and this is the jump that really grabbed me -- creatures that can test out actions *in their heads* before risking them in the world, so their bad ideas die instead of them. And finally creatures like us, who offload thinking into language, writing, tools, and other people's brains, so we don't even have to simulate everything ourselves.

Here's the thing: I don't want to just lecture this, because the whole point is that each level *feels* different from the inside, and I want people to feel the difference between "my body gets punished" and "my plan gets rejected" and "someone else's written-down idea saves me the trouble." So, act as someone who both knows the philosophy of mind stuff and has experience designing party games / workshop activities: design me a game. My rough thought is everyone solves the same task (some puzzle or challenge with tokens as points, no real money) across several rounds, and each round's rules force them to behave like one of the creature types -- round one you literally cannot learn, round two you can only learn by trying and failing, round three you get to plan on paper before committing, round four you can consult hints or each other. But I don't know how to make the restrictions enforceable rather than just "please pretend," and that's where I need your brain.

Please think this through carefully and brainstorm a bit before settling. I need: concrete rules and materials for each round (stuff I can get at a dollar store), how the scoring works so the advantages of each level actually show up in the points, a timeline that fits 75 minutes, and a debrief plan that maps each round onto the creature it was simulating -- ideally so people name what was different themselves before I give them the labels. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (someone will definitely cheat at the "no learning" round, right?) and how to handle it. And be honest with me about which parts of the idea the game *can't* capture -- like, one person playing four rounds isn't actually the same as evolution across generations, so tell me where I'll have to just say that in words instead of showing it.
