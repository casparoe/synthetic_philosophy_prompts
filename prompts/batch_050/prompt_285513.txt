So I'm a novelist, working on a book set in the 1640s, and I've been picking through this craft book on historical fiction that my agent swears by. It has a chapter on something called Whig history, and the chapter is making me paranoid, but I also suspect it's a little glib. Here's how it summarizes the idea, roughly:

"The historian Butterfield, in an old book called The Whig Interpretation of History, named a habit that historians fall into: writing the past as a steady march toward the present, where the people who turned out to be on the winning side become the story's heroes and everyone else is an obstacle. This is bad history, because the people of the past didn't know they were heading toward us, and judging them by what they contributed to now is unfair to them. Since moral judgment is the engine of this mistake, Butterfield held that historians should never pass moral judgment on the past at all, and that honest history has to be written from no point of view. A novel always has a point of view and always wants you to root for someone, so historical fiction is basically Whig history by definition -- the best a novelist can do is wink at the reader now and then to show you know you're doing it."

The reason this stings is that in my draft, the character you're meant to love is definately the one whose instincts most resemble a modern reader's -- religious tolerance, queasy about witch trials, the works. Meanwhile the characters who'd feel authentic to the period read as cold. So if the craft book is right, I'm commiting the sin structurally, not just by accident.

But the "never pass moral judgment at all" and "no point of view" bits smell off to me -- like the kind of thing a craft book says because it sounds clean, not because anyone actually argued it. I don't know the source well enough to push back, though.

Can you audit this summary for me? Then the part I really care about: does the craft book's conclusion -- fiction is doomed to this, just wink at the reader -- actually follow from the corrected version of the idea, or is it smuggling in the errors?
