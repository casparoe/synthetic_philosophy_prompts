I'm writing a long-form piece on the ethics of immigration control, and two philosophers have agreed to let me publish the outcome of a year-long adversarial collaboration. One defends the state's right to exclude would-be immigrants, grounded in citizens' collective self-determination and freedom of association; the other argues that border control as practised is arbitrary discrimination, morally akin to allocating life prospects by feudal birthright. Draft the joint statement they would sign. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept, including concessions that cost each side something — for instance, the exclusionist should grant that place of birth is morally arbitrary, the open-borders advocate that some forms of exclusion (who one lives with, whom a small association admits) are plainly legitimate; (2) a table of the residual disagreements, each phrased so that both parties accept the wording of their own position; (3) the cruxes — I suspect these include whether a state is relevantly like a private club and whether the analogy survives scaling from a dozen members to fifty million, and whether coercion at the border demands justification to the excluded in a way that internal law does not — and for each crux, one concrete case or thought experiment both agree bears on it, with each side's verdict recorded; (4) a short section from each author stating what evidence or argument would most weaken their view, plus one counterintuitive implication of their own position they are prepared to defend rather than disown. If any part of their disagreement turns out to be verbal, mark it as such. Write in their voice — first-person plural where they agree, measured and quotable, with no technical term left unexplained — since the piece will reproduce the statement directly.
