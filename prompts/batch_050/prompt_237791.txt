I run a small philosophy of physics reading group, and last week we got into a fight I want to relitigate properly. The question was whether erasing information could have any thermodynamic cost. One member said obviously not: information is abstract, it's about what's represented, and abstract things don't dissipate heat. Another member just said "information is physical," like that ended it, and then deployed the same sentence again an hour later during Maxwell's demon -- something about the demon needing a memory and the entropy showing up when the memory gets whiped.

Since then I've noticed people use this slogan for basically everything. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims it might be making, and give a verdict on each -- platitude, substantive-and-defensible, or actually false? Roughly the readings I can half-glimpse:

- a minimal one: you can't store or transmit information without some physical bearer
- an engineering-ish one: erasing a bit costs some minimum amount of energy (kT ln 2, I think? at what temperature though, and is that per bit erased or per operation or what)
- an identification: Shannon entropy and thermodynamic entropy are literally the same quantity, not just analogous
- a maximalist one: the universe at bottom just is information, and matter is the derivative thing

What I actually want answered:

1. Which of these does my second member need for the rebuttal of "abstract things don't dissipate heat"? I suspect the minimal reading doesn't get him there but I can't quite say why not.
2. Which reading does the standard resolution of Maxwell's demon rely on? Does saving the second law via the demon's memory require the full identification of the two entropies, or only the erasure-cost claim? Because those feel very different in how committal they are.
3. Is there a reading on which the slogan is true but boring in a way my first member could happily accept? I have a feeling the real disagreement is about something else entirely and the slogan is letting both of them avoid saying what.

Keep the readings as a menu rather than picking your favorite -- I want to bring it back to the group and watch people commit to things.
