I've been collecting objections to Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility (the argument that, since you act as you do because of the way you are, true responsibility for your actions would require responsibility for the way you are, which would require an impossible regress of self-creation). Here are the ones I already have: (1) the compatibilist denial of the second premise — responsibility for an action requires only that it issue from one's own evaluational structure or be reasons-responsive, not that one be responsible for how that structure arose; (2) the attributability/accountability split — the argument at most impugns deep attributability, while accountability as answerability within our moral practices never demanded self-origination; (3) Kane-style libertarianism — character is progressively formed through undetermined 'self-forming actions' grounded in competing efforts of will, so no intentional self-creation is needed; (4) the overreach objection — if the argument succeeds, it also undermines responsibility for one's own reasoning, and so for one's confidence in the Basic Argument itself; and (5) the charge that the argument equivocates between being responsible for one's nature and being responsible for what flows from one's nature. Is there an objection I'm missing? It should be genuinely distinct from these five — not a rewording, special case, or combination of them. State it precisely, and then give me your honest assessment of whether it succeeds against Strawson's likely replies.
