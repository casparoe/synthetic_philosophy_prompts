I work as a researcher at a think-tank, and I'm drafting a briefing on the governance of frontier AI systems. In our internal discussions, one comparison keeps doing an enormous amount of argumentative work: that transformative AI is to this century what nuclear weapons were to the last. My colleagues deploy it to justify a fairly specific package of proposals — an international inspectorate modelled on the IAEA, verification regimes for large training runs analogous to safeguards on fissile material, non-proliferation norms aimed at a small club of capable actors, and some form of deterrence-by-mutual-restraint between the leading laboratories and states. I'm broadly sympathetic to the destination but increasingly uneasy about the route, and I want the briefing to say explicitly what the analogy buys us and what it smuggles in.

Could you give me a proper audit of it? I'd like, first, the correspondence table: what plays the role of fissile material, of enrichment cascades, of test explosions, of the weapons themselves, of the state monopoly on proliferation decisions, of mutually assured destruction. Second, the places where the mapping breaks — I'm already thinking of the dual-use character of AI (civilian and military applications being the same artefact, not separable production lines), the fact that the relevant actors are private firms rather than states, the intangible and copyable nature of model weights compared to physical stockpiles, the absence of anything like a detectable test detonation, and the sheer pace of capability change relative to the decade-scale diplomacy the nuclear regime assumed. Third, and most important for my purposes: which of these disanalogies actually matter for the governance conclusions my colleagues draw, and which are superficial? Does the case for verification, inspectorates, and non-proliferation survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the nuclear comparison carrying more weight than it can bear — and if the latter, do the printing press or the Industrial Revolution do better for some of these claims, even if worse for others?
