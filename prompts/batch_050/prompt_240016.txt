I am trying to get clear on Moore's paradox before committing to any published account of it, and I would rather build the standard of adequacy first and measure accounts against it than inherit someone's framing. So: write the desiderata for a theory of Moorean absurdity -- what any adequate explanation of what goes wrong in utterances or thoughts of the form "it's raining, but I don't believe it" has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

To fix the scope, your list should at minimum address, and go beyond, the following pressure points. An account must say what kind of defect Moorean utterances have, given that "p, but I don't believe that p" is not a contradiction and can in fact be true; whatever defect it names must be one the sentence itself plausibly carries, not merely one that attaches to a speaker who happens to be insincere. It must handle the contrast between the omissive form ("p, but I don't believe it") and the commissive form ("p, and I believe that not-p"), since both are absurd but arguably not absurd in exactly the same way, and it must not predict absurdity where there is none: the same sentences are perfectly fine in the past tense, in the third person, in conditional antecedents, and when conjoined with a supposition. It must explain the relationship between the assertoric case (saying the sentence aloud) and the doxastic case (merely believing the conjunction, or judging it true), and whether these are one phenomenon or two. And it must say something principled about first-person authority more generally: why "it is raining and I believe it is raining" is an odd thing to assert even though nothing seems wrong with the belief it reports.

For each desideratum, give the motivating case and indicate whether you take it to be a genuine constraint any theory must meet or a prejudice of one camp -- for instance, whether demanding a single unified explanation of both the assertion and belief cases is a fair requirement or an aesthetic preference. If any pair of desiderata on your list cannot be jointly satisfied, say so and argue for which one should be dropped, since an inconsistent specification is itself a substantive result. Flag also which single requirement, in your judgment, excludes the most candidate theories.

Then, briefly -- this part should be shorter than the list -- score the main families of account against it: Moore's own line that the absurdity arises because assertion normally implies belief; constitutivist accounts on which belief aims at truth or is governed by a norm the Moorean believer necessarily violates; and accounts that ground the absurdity in the structure of first-person belief ascription or self-knowledge. Note where each fails a desideratum you marked non-negotiable.
