So last week I got into a surprisingly heated argument with a coworker about those algorithmic risk scores that a lot of US courts now use at bail and sentencing hearings -- the ones where the judge gets a number, like 1 to 10, estimating how likely this defendant is to reoffend or skip court, and the number is computed entirely from things like age at first arrest, prior convictions, employment status, and so on. Nobody interviews the defendant, nobody reads their letter of remorse, the tool just looks at how people *with that profile* have behaved historically. My coworker said this is obviously fairer than a judge going on gut feeling, and I said there's something creepy about judging a person by what "people like them" tend to do, but I couldn't articulate what exactly the creepiness consists in, philosophically.

Here's what I want to understand: what does a practice like this *presuppose* about prediction and about persons? Like, does using it commit you to the view that the right way to predict what an individual will do is to find the reference class they fall into and check the base rate -- and if so, why is *that* the right reference class (age + priors + employment) rather than some finer or coarser one, since presumably the base rate changes depending on which class you pick? Does the practice only make sense if you think individuals don't really have some irreducible particularity that a judge could in principle detect, or is it compatible with thinking they do but we can never reliably access it? And here's the part that really bugs me: the same legal system that uses these scores also insists elsewhere that each defendant be judged as an individual on their own merits, and insurance companies do the reference-class thing to me every day (I pay more for car insurance because of my age bracket, even though I've never had an accident) and nobody calls *that* unjust -- so are these practices presupposing the same thing, or is something different going on in the courtroom case that makes the statistics feel objectionable there but not at Geico?
