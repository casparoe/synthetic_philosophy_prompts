I'm writing the lit review section of a paper on the knowability paradox (Fitch's result: if every truth is knowable, then every truth is known) and I want to make sure I'm not missing a whole family of responses. The responses I already have covered:

1. Restrict the principle. Only some truths need to be knowable — e.g. Tennant's move of limiting knowability to 'Cartesian' propositions, ones whose being known never leads to absurdity, so Moorean truths like 'p and nobody knows p' get excluded by design.
2. Bite the bullet / treat it as a refutation: the derivation is sound, strong verificationism is false, and the real lesson is that anti-realists overstated their case (roughly the reaction of realists who were happy with the result anyway).
3. Dialetheism: accept that Moorean propositions generate contradictions when you suppose them known, and treat those contradictions as true instead of revising anything (Beall).
4. The dynamic/diachronic response: knowledge can destroy the truth known — coming to know a Moorean sentence makes it false, because now someone knows p — so the derivation slides between what's true at different points of the story (van Benthem).
5. Actualist restriction: replace 'possibly known' with something like 'actually known in some counterfactual circumstance' — the quantification over possible knowers has to stay anchored to the actual world (Edgington).
6. Attack the epistemic logic: deny the distribution step, i.e. reject that knowing a conjunction lets you infer knowing each conjunct in the way the proof needs.

What I want is a seventh response that is NOT a variant of any of these. So in particular: not another restriction principle with a different cutoff (that's type 1 no matter where you draw the line), not a tweak to some other step of the derivation (that's 6 in disguise), and not 'the paradox actually vindicates verificationism because...' bullet-biting in reverse. Give me a genuinely different angle — maybe questioning how 'knowable' gets formalized, the epistemic operator itself, the notion of truth in play, or something else I haven't thought of.

Then, the important part: tell me honestly whether your proposal actually dodges the derivation, or whether it collapses into one of the six after a bit of prodding. If the most original thing you can come up with turns out to be type 1 with extra steps, say so rather than dressing it up.
