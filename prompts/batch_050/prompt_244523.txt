So I fell down a rabbit hole about the Gettier problem again last night (blame an old SSC comment thread). Its been sixty-odd years since those three pages came out, and as far as I can tell the "patch justified true belief" industry just kept going: no-false-lemmas, defeasibility conditions, reliabilism, safety, sensitivity, each patched analysis getting counterexampled in turn, until a chunk of the field gave up entirely and went knowledge-first or virtue epistemology or whatever. What I want is a diagnosis, not another survey. My pet theory is that knowledge is a cluster concept that simply doesn't have necessary and sufficient conditions, so the whole post-Gettier project was doomed and both sides were really just arguing about which intuitions to privilege — but I could also believe it's a genuine substantive disagreement where one camp is simply wrong, or that the experimental philosophy stuff about intuitions varying across cultures changes the picture. Tell me what the impasse actually shows, engaging with what the main camps actually claim rather than caricatures, and what if anything would count as settling it — a formal result, better empirical data, a change of question — or make the case that nothing could. Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction and no conclusion, and in British English please.
