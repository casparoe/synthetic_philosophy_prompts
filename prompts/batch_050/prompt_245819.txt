I am designing the specification for a hypothetical advisory system meant to act not on a user's stated preferences but on what the user would want under some idealization — better information, fuller reflection, fewer cognitive distortions. Before I evaluate any particular proposal (ideal-advisor theories, full-information accounts of a person's good, coherent extrapolated volition, plain informed-desire views), I want the requirements list first. Write the desiderata any adequate theory of idealized preference must meet, with the case or consideration behind each. At minimum, address three categories: (i) the cases it must get right — adaptive preferences (the contented slave who has learned not to want freedom), the addict whose considered judgment endorses the addiction, the person who would abandon a central value if fully informed but does not want the information, and extrapolations that converge on something monstrous (the bigot who, fully informed and fully reflective, remains a bigot); (ii) the distinctions it must respect — informing an agent versus replacing them, the order-dependence of what an agent learns first, what my idealized self would choose versus what I now endorse about that counterfactual, and extrapolating one person versus extrapolating a population; (iii) the results it must stay consistent with — that changing one's mind is sometimes rational and stated preferences sometimes simply mistaken, but also that an idealization powerful enough to correct every error licenses overriding nearly everyone about nearly everything. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are commitments of particular camps, identify any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — I have in mind a possible tension between requiring that the extrapolated self remain recognizably me and requiring that the extrapolation be free of every distorting influence — and name the single requirement that excludes the most candidates. Score the main contenders against the list only briefly; the criteria are the deliverable.
