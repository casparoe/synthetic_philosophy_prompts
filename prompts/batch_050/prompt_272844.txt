A lecturer on a podcast I half-listened to summarised Gongsun Long’s white horse dialogue like this:

“The School of Names was mainly concerned with the political project of rectifying names: social order depends on getting words to correspond neatly to realities. Gongsun Long’s claim that a white horse is not a horse sounds absurd only because English makes us confuse identity with membership. He is not denying that white horses belong among horses; he is saying that the expressions ‘white horse’ and ‘horse’ do not mean the same thing. ‘White’ picks out a colour and ‘horse’ a shape, so the compound expression has different conditions of application from either part on its own. If someone asks for a horse, a black horse will do; if they ask for a white horse, it won’t. The dialogue therefore anticipates the modern distinction between a set and one of its subsets, and its deeper moral is that classifications are artefacts of naming rather than natural divisions in the world.”

Some of this sounds sensible, especially the bit about a request for a horse being satisfiable by horses of other colours. But the jump from “the phrases mean different things” to “a white horse is not a horse” still feels slippery, and I’m suspicious of the stuff about set theory and natural kinds. Could you go through the summary and seperate what is textually grounded, what is a plausible modern reconstruction, and what is just the lecturer reading fashionable ideas into it? In particular, would a charitable defender of Gongsun Long accept the identity-versus-membership diagnosis, or does that tidy up the argument too much? And does anything in the dialogue really support the conclusion that all classifications are merely artefacts of naming?
