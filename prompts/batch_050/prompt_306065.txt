Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a spacetime substantivalist and a relationist on the significance of Newton's rotating-bucket argument for the metaphysics of space and time. Organize it in three parts. First, the agreed claims: what both sides can sign concerning the phenomenology of the bucket (the concave surface, its insensitivity to the water's rotation relative to the bucket walls), concerning what any adequate physics must explain about inertial effects, and concerning what general relativity does and does not settle about the existence of spacetime. Second, the residual disagreements, each phrased so that both parties would accept the wording of their own position: in particular, whether absolute acceleration demands spacetime as a genuine substance with monadic structure, or whether relationism can recover inertial phenomena from material relations alone, and what bearing the hole argument has on this question. Third, the cruxes: identify the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side, state what each party would count as an argument against its own view, and mark any part of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, with reasons.
