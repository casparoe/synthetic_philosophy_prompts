I'm giving a talk at an interdisciplinary workshop next month -- the room will be roughly half statistical learning theorists and half philosophers of science, plus a couple of ML practioners who do AutoML stuff for a living. My thesis: the no-free-lunch theorems don't just formalize Hume's problem of induction, they make it strictly worse, because they show that even the meta-level question "which inductive bias should I use for this domain?" is itself an inductive problem with no principled solution. My argument, briefly: (1) NFL says averaged over all possible target functions every algorithm performs identically, so good performance requires a prior match between algorithm and world; (2) we can only justify that match by appeal to past performance of similar algorithms in similar domains; (3) but "similar domains" is doing all the work -- similarity itself has to be induced; (4) so we get a regress of meta-inductions, and NFL-type results apply at each level. I call this the meta-NFL regress and I claim it's Hume's problem with a proof attached.

Prep me for the Q&A. I want the objections I'll actually get from this specific crowd -- which means the learning theorists will come at me very differently than the philsci people -- phrased the way they'd actually phrase them (the theorists will say things like "that's just a statement about uniform priors over targets, nobody believed that anyway"). Order them by how much damage they'd do to my position, and for each one give me a two-minute reply plus an honest note about where the reply is soft. Also tell me:
