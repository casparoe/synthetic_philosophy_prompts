I moderate a mystery book club and last week we got into an argument I couldn't referee, and I want to know if the thing we stumbled into has a name. Conan Doyle never writes down how many toes Sherlock Holmes has, but everyone at the table agreed he has ten — that's just part of the story even though it's nowhere on the page. But then someone asked what his blood type is, and we all agreed there's no answer at all, not even a secret one the author knew but didn't share. And then someone said Sherlock Holmes is the most famous detective who ever lived, and half the table said that's obviously true and half said it can't be true because there's no such guy to be famous. So the same made-up person seems to generate three different situations: facts nobody wrote down that still count, questions with no answer whatsoever, and statements about the real world that mention someone who never existed but still seem true or false. I've heard people throw around words like make-believe and fictional truth, but is there an established name for this three-way split, or especially for the middle case — the question that has no answer rather than a hidden one? How does that differ from a story merely leaving something unstated but settled, and from a story being genuinely ambiguous between two readings? If there's no term that fits, coin one, give a definition, and walk through a few boundary cases so I can tell what's in and what's out. A bare label won't satisfy me; I want the carving. Please answer in American English, and end with a final line of the form \answer{the best-fitting existing term, or your coinage}.
