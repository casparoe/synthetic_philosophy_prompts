If DNA is a code, the information in a sequence should be fixed by the sequence and the coding rule, much as a message is fixed once the cipher and key are known. Yet the same sequence can support different products and effects through alternative splicing, RNA editing, and cell-type-specific regulation. Does this mean the real information lies in the cell’s interpretive machinery rather than in DNA, so that an enhancer mutation associated with disease should be called a changed input to that machinery rather than genetic information about disease risk?
