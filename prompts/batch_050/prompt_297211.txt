I'm an ICU doctor who picked up causal inference a few years ago and now it's the main way I think -- DAGs, the do-operator, counterfactuals, potential outcomes, all of that. I use it for actual clinical questions (what happens to mortality if we extubate this patient, what happens if we don't start pressors) but I've never been able to make it do the moral work everyone around me seems to think is obvious.

Here's what I want. Take the doing vs. allowing distinction -- killing vs. letting die, withdrawing vs. withholding treatment, that whole cluster -- and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary of causal inference. In my world an intervention is an intervention: do(x) cuts the incoming edges, and the graph doesn't care whether x is "turn off the ventilator" or "refrain from starting the ventilator." The counterfactual mortality is what it is. So I genuinely don't see where the moral asymmetry could live in this vocabulary, and I want you to either show me where it lives or tell me it doesn't.

Concretely:

- Work through a few standard cases from the ethics literature and translate each one: withdrawing vs. withholding, the trolley problem, maybe the loop variant if you think it stresses the translation, and one of the organ-harvest style cases. For each, say what the causal model actually is and whether the moral verdict people usually have has any counterpart in the model's structure.
- Then the residue report. Which parts of the distinction translate cleanly (I suspect the redirecting-a-threat cases do?), which parts only translate with distortion, and which have no expression at all. Candidates I'd guess: the role of intention, the idea that some causal paths go *through* another person in a way that's different from paths that don't, the difference between a threat you created and one you merely failed to stop.
- And then the verdict: is the residue a sign that my vocabulary is missing something a doctor actually needs, or is it a sign that the doing/allowing distinction is a moral illusion that evaporates once you write down the graph? I have colleagues who think withdrawing and withholding are morally identical and I can never tell if they've seen through something or just gotten drunk on the formalism.

Don't smuggle morality back in by hand -- if you need a node for "what the agent intended" or "who had a duty," say so explicitly and tell me whether that's still causal vocabulary or something bolted on.
