I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about when one thing is better than another, so here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and I want to know what principle (if any) is hiding underneath them. If no single principle gets them all, tell me which verdict is cheapest to give up.

First, the long-life ladder. Life 1: eighty wonderful years. Life 2: every day a tiny bit less wonderful than Life 1, but it lasts twice as long. Life 3: same again off Life 2, and so on down to Life 1000: billions upon billions of years of the drabbest existence that's still just barely worth living -- droning background music, boiled potatoes, mild boredom forever. On each adjacent pair my verdict is that the longer life is better; the dip in quality at each step is so small that doubling the time obviously wins. But comparing Life 1 to Life 1000 directly, Life 1 is clearly better and it isn't close.

Second, the coffee row. Two hundred cups in a line, each with one more grain of sugar than the last, from black to syrup. Any two neighbours: I'd say neither is better, I can't taste the difference, and even with both in front of me I'd call them equally good. But cup 200 is definitely better than cup 1, because I like my coffee sweet. I have a feeling this case should bother me in the same way the ladder does but I can't articulate why, and if your principle treats them differently I want to hear the justification.

Third, the jobs. Job X versus Job Y: X pays slightly more, Y has slightly nicer colleagues. Verdict: neither is better, and I'd resist even calling them exactly equal -- they're just on a par. Same for Y versus the next job Z. But X versus Z: X is clearly better. So here even the on-a-par relation seems to chain into a strict betterness, which bothers me more than the sugar one did for some reason.

Fourth, the holiday cycle. Package A (beach), B (mountains), C (city). I compare them on price, food, and activities, and my gut goes with whichever wins two out of three: A beats B, B beats C, and C beats A. Yes, I realise how that looks. No, stating the three verdicts out loud doesn't make any of them feel wrong to me.

Fifth, one I won't commit on: one hour of headache at intensity 5 versus two hours at intensity 4. I genuinely can't say whether one is worse, and it doesn't feel like ignorance on my part -- it feels like there's just no fact of the usual kind there. Not sure if that's a verdict or the absence of one.

So: is there a coherent account of 'better than' that captures as much of this as possible, and what does it make of each case? If something has to go, rank my verdicts by how expensive it'd be to drop them. And tell me what your account predicts for the case I keep flip-flopping on: trading one year of my actual life for ten extra years tacked on at ninety percent of my current quality -- some days I'd take it, some days it seems like a terrible deal, and I'd like to know whether that wobble is allowed or is itself the symptom of my mistake.
