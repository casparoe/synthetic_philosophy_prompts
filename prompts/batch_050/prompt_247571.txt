I'm a PhD student in philosophy and my advisor has a set of pretty settled intuitions about rationality cases that I can't pin down to one principle. Her verdicts, as I understand them:

- Parfit's hitchhiker (you're stranded in the desert, a driver will give you a ride to town only if he can tell you're the kind of person who'd pay him $100 on arrival, and he's a perfect judge of character): she says that once you arrive it's rational to actually pay, not to just walk off.
- Kavka's toxin puzzle (a billionaire pays you a fortune if at midnight you genuinely intend to drink an awful-but-not-dangerous toxin the next day; he only ever checks the intention): she says it's rational to form the intention, but when the next day comes it's irrational to actually drink the stuff.
- Newcomb's problem: one-box, obviously.
- A deterrence case (a shopkeeper publicly commits to suing any vandal at great cost to himself, which is the only reason the threat deters; one vandal calls his bluff): she says making the threat was rational and actually carrying it out is rational too.

Now the new case I want to ask her about: the Newcomb predictor announces she's retiring. This is her last prediction ever, the boxes are already filled, she's deleting all records and will never publicize or even learn your choice, and no one will ever run such an experiment on you or anyone you know again. Does my advisor one-box or two-box?

What I'm after: what's your best guess for her verdict, and which earlier verdict is the real closest analog -- ordinary Newcomb, or the toxin-drinking (where the benefit is already locked in and the act itself gains nothing)? It seems like at least two rules fit her whole track record, roughly one where following through is rational because your having the disposition is what got you the benefit in the first place, and one where following through is rational because dumping dispositions the moment they stop paying makes you worse at keeping the ones that still pay. Do both genuinely survive all four verdicts, or does one crack somewhere? And if her record really underdetermines the retirement case, what further case would force her to show her hand?
