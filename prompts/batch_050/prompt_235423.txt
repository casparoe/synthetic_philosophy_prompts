I am a high school student on my school's student council, and we have spent three meetings deadlocked over whether our school's competitive video-gaming team "really is" a sport and so qualifies for money from the athletics budget. While looking for help, I found the philosopher Rudolf Carnap's essay "Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology" (1950), where he writes:

"If someone wishes to speak in his language about a new kind of entities, he has to introduce a system of new ways of speaking, subject to new rules; we shall call this procedure the construction of a linguistic framework for the new entities in question. And now we must distinguish two kinds of questions of existence: first, questions of the existence of certain entities of the new kind within the framework; we call them internal questions; and second, questions concerning the existence or reality of the system of entities as a whole, called external questions."

I take Carnap to be suggesting that some "does X really count as Y?" questions can be settled by checking facts against rules already in place, while others are really practical decisions about which rules to adopt. Please locate our esports dispute on this distinction. Give me a single number from 0 to 1, where 0 means the question is purely internal (settle it by checking the facts against the rules the school already has) and 1 means it is purely external (settle it by deciding, on practical grounds, which rules we ought to have). Do not default to 0.5 unless the dispute genuinely is evenly mixed. Explain your placement in two or three paragraphs: say which considerations in our debate (physical exertion, injury rates, what other schools fund, encouraging student activity, rewarding teamwork, distributing limited money fairly) fall on each side of Carnap's line, and flag anything about the esports case that resists being sorted by his distinction at all, given that he drew it for existence questions rather than funding eligibility. State your final number as the last line in the form \answer{...}.
