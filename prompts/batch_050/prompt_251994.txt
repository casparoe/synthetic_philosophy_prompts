ok so I'm in this applied ethics seminar and we just spent two weeks on the ticking time bomb argument for torture and I kind of cant stop thinking about how the argument only works because the scenario hands you this magical certainty -- you KNOW there's a bomb, you KNOW this guy planted it, you KNOW he'll talk under torture and tell the truth. Meanwhile the people who love the thought experiment also want actual policies, like proposals for "torture warrants" and stuff, which is where the fairy-tale certainty meets real institutions.

So for a creative assignment I want to write a satire. Write it for me as a model I can work from:

Write a deadpan internal FAQ / guidance document (about 600-750 words) from a fictional government body -- call it something like the Office of Exceptional Circumstances -- that has fully institutionalized the ticking time bomb argument. It's written by calm, reasonable bureaucrats who genuinely beleive they're the moderates in the room: they only authorize torture in the rarest of cases, and they've built a whole administrative apparatus to prove it.

The humor has to come from the real weak points of the argument, exaggerated through procedure, not from making the officials cartoonishly evil. Things like:

- the certification process for "sufficient certainty" that a suspect knows something (there should be a form, and a threshold, and nobody should be able to say how anyone ever meets it)
- the timeline problem: the paperwork takes longer than the bomb's timer, so there's an expedited track, then a pre-authorized track, and eventually warrants issued in advance for bombs that might exist
- the category creep from "the one terrorist who definitely knows" to his family, to people who might know someone who knows, each handled as routine casework
- the office's pride in its restraint (statistics about how few applications are approved, celebrated while the raw numbers climb)
- at least one clause where the straight-faced logic lands somewhere genuinely chilling, like a standard procedure for cases involving the suspect's child, written in the same neutral tone as everything else
- maybe a closing line about how the office exists to ensure torture remains exceptional

Keep the voice totally flat and bureaucratic the whole way through -- no winking, no narrator telling me this is bad. A reader should be able to tell exactly what's being satirized purely from the content, and someone who defends the thought experiment should feel the satire is aimed at the actual structure of their argument (the stipulated certainty, the slide from one case to a standing policy) rather than a strawman. Format it like a real document with section headers and numbered questions.
