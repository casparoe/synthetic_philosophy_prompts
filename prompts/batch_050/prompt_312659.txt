I write a long-form magazine column on science and ideas, and I'm drafting a feature about non-human causes in history -- pathogens, climate swings, volcanic eruptions. My editor keeps flagging some of my causal claims as environmental determinism while waving others through, and I can't articulate the line she's drawing, or whether I even agree with it. Here are my instincts on the claims, my own and contributors', that have crossed my desk. The demographic collapse of the Americas from introduced disease after 1492 as a necessary condition of the speed of European conquest: run it, central causal claim, no determinism problem. The 1815 Tambora eruption producing the failed harvests and unrest of 1816: run it. A pitch arguing the Little Ice Age caused the French Revolution: killed it -- decades of background chill can't explain why 1789. The old hypothesis that lead poisoning materially contributed to Rome's fall: rejected as crankish. The tree-ring case that unusually wet decades enriched the steppe and thereby enabled the Mongol conquests: publishable as a contributing cause, though I'd refuse the headline that climate made Genghis Khan. The argument, from respectable economists, that disease ecology explains long-run African underdevelopment: I hesitate, and I can't say why it troubles me when the Americas claim doesn't. And a draft tracing modern capitalism back through labor scarcity to the Black Death: each link seems defensible, yet the whole feels like overreach. So: is there a principle that captures these verdicts -- something about events versus standing conditions, or chain length, or whether the mechanism routes through human response rather than around it? State the best candidate precisely, show which of my verdicts it saves and which it condemns, and tell me which verdict I'd have to surrender. Then apply it to a case I haven't settled: a contributor wants to argue that the volcanic winters of the 530s and 540s so weakened Byzantium and Persia that they cleared the path for the Arab conquests a century later. Should the piece run it, and if my principle says no, is that the principle's problem or the claim's?
