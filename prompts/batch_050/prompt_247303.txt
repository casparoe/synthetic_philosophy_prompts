I host a small philosophy podcast and my next guest is a philosopher of cosmology with a somewhat unfashionable take on Boltzmann brains. Her view is that the real problem is not simply that a cosmological model contains huge numbers of isolated brains with false memories. On her diagnosis, a theory gets into trouble only when its observer-selection rule makes people with my entire apparent history less typical than short-lived duplicates who merely have my present experiences. She calls this a history-first criterion, and she thinks it avoids both panicking about every possible duplicate and assuming without argument that we must be ordinary observers.

Draft 8-10 interview questions that really probe that position rather than asking for an intro to Boltzmann brains. I want the sequence to cover at least:

- why sharing all of my current evidence is supposedly not enough to make a fluctuating brain epistemically relevant;
- how she individuates an apparent history without just defining Boltzmann brains as not having genuine memories;
- whether the criterion depends on a contested measure over cosmological observers, and what changes if ordinary observers are finite while fluctuating observers keep occuring forever;
- how her view differs from saying that Boltzmann-brain-dominated theories are self-undermining because they make reasoning unreliable;
- whether the criterion is empirical, anthropic, or just a way of encoding the prior conviction that we are normal observers;
- what it says about ordinary observers who have highly unlikely lives, since plenty of real people are atypical in some respects;
- whether the same argument would protect us from worries about simulations or duplicated uploads, and if not, where the disanalogy is;
- what concrete cosmological prediction would count against her criterion.

For every question, add a follow-up based on the answer she is most likely to give, especially the likely reply that a theory should be evaluated by how well it predicts the evidence we actually have. I'd like the questions to force her to say exactly which feature of that evidence is doing the work: its coherence over time, its causal origin, its reliability, or something else. One typo in my notes says she thinks memories need the right kind of causal ancestory, so you can use that formulation if it helps.
