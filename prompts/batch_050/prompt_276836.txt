So I'm in this philosophy of mind seminar and last week we did the dream argument from Descartes' first meditation, and the discussion completely derailed (in a good way). One person in the class brought up this idea from Dennett that dreams might not be experiences at all in the way we assume -- his rough picture was that what we call a dream isn't a little movie that plays while you sleep, but more like a prerecorded cassette that gets inserted into memory at the moment you wake up, so you wake with the full false impression of having just lived through something that never actually unfolded in your head at all. And someone else mentioned Malcolm's related point that there might be no fact of the matter about what "happened" during the dream beyond whatever you report on waking, since there's no way to check. This definately broke something in my brain because the whole Descartes setup seemed to rest on dreams being real experiences that just mimic waking ones.

So here's what I actually want: suppose we could sit Descartes down and brief him on the cassette theory and Malcolm's worry. How would he react? Concretely: (1) Does his dream doubt depend on dreams being genuine experiences, and if so does Dennett's picture weaken the doubt (you can't be deceived by a dream if there was never any dream-experience doing the deceiving) or actually make his skeptical situation worse (because now it's not just tonight's dream that's suspect -- the cassette mechanism would seem to threaten ALL my memories, including the memory of what I did five minutes ago)? (2) Would he just say the cassette theory is the evil demon hypothesis wearing a different hat, i.e. a specific story about how the deception gets implemented, which his argument was always meant to be neutral about? (3) The cogito is supposed to be the move that survives all this because doubting is a present-tense occurent thought that can't be faked -- does that move still work if it turns out that even apparent present-tense experiences during sleep aren't really experiences? Basically I want to know whether Descartes would have to rewrite the first meditation or whether he'd shrug and say nothing essential changed, and I'd like the answer to actually take a side rather than just laying out options.
