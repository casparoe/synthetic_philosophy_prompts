I am preparing a history of a company that, during the 1940s, excluded women from management, relied at times on prison labor, and fired employees whose political views troubled local officials. Some of my evidence shows that employees objected to these practices at the time, while other evidence suggests that many managers regarded them as ordinary business necessities. My editor wants me to evaluate the company plainly by today’s standards; I worry that doing so will obscure how the people involved understood their choices, but refusing to evaluate them can make the history sound like an excuse. How should I decide which standards to use when describing, explaining, and morally assessing these practices? Please give your response as exactly five numbered items of one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion. Where relevant, distinguish what is broadly accepted historical practice from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a contested methodological position as the consensus.
