I am preparing an application for a one-year research fellowship in philosophy of mind and need a two-page research proposal. The subject is the familiar taxonomy that sorts minds into four cumulative kinds — creatures that adapt only through natural selection (Darwinian), creatures that can also learn through individual trial and error (Skinnerian), creatures that can pre-test hypotheses in an inner model before acting (Popperian), and creatures whose thinking is scaffolded by culturally inherited tools, especially language (Gregorian).

My half-formed idea is this. The taxonomy is almost always presented as a nested hierarchy, with each kind containing the capacities of the previous one as a proper subset. I suspect this nesting is doing unexamined work. In particular, I want to investigate whether the four kinds are conceptually independent dimensions that happen to be correlated in terrestrial evolution, or whether the dependence relations between them are genuine necessities — for instance, whether a creature could possess an inner environment for testing hypotheses without ever having been shaped by individual reinforcement learning, or whether culturally transmitted tools could be acquired and used by a mind that lacked the Popperian capacity entirely. I also want to ask what follows for classifying artificial systems, which were produced by neither biological selection nor anything much like individual conditioning, and which seem to arrive at Gregorian-like behaviour by a route the taxonomy does not describe.

Please turn this into a proper proposal document. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will supply those myself, so refer to positions and arguments by description rather than by author and year.

Structure the proposal under the following headings, in this order:

1. The Question — a precise statement of the question, formulated so that it has determinate possible answers rather than being a general theme (roughly 150 words).
2. Why the Standard Picture Leaves It Open — an account of why the usual nested presentation of the taxonomy neither settles the dependence question nor was designed to, and why merely reclassifying known animal cases will not resolve it (roughly 300 words).
3. Approach — the method I would actually use: conceptual analysis of what each capacity minimally requires, combined with the construction of limiting cases (real or hypothetical) that would discriminate between the necessity and independence readings; explain why this approach rather than, say, a survey of comparative cognition or a purely formal treatment (roughly 300 words).
4. Plan of Work — a month-by-month breakdown across the twelve months, with each month tied to a concrete task and intermediate output (roughly 200 words).
5. What a Result Would Look Like — the form a positive answer would take in each direction, and how the classification of artificial systems would serve as a test case rather than a distraction (roughly 250 words).
6. Failure Conditions — what would count as the project failing, including the possibility that the taxonomy turns out to be too vague to support the question, and what I would do in that event (roughly 150 words).

Keep the total to roughly two pages, and write in the register of a serious fellowship application: precise, non-polemical, and candid about the risks.
