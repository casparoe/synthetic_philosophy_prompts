I sit on the executive board of my country's national architects' association. A historian's report last year confirmed that under the old dictatorship our predecessor body (same profession, same building, same archives) struck all its Jewish members off the register in 1952 and handed their files to the police; several were later deported. That body was formally dissolved in 1961 and our association was only founded in 1976, but it took over the offices and membership records, and our website still proudly celebrates the original 1919 founding date. A group of descendants is now asking for a public apology plus a funded memorial fellowship. Half the board says guilt can't attach to people who weren't even born yet, and we're a seperate legal entity anyway, so at most we issue some vague statement of regret. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote: recommend a concrete course of action with a rough timeline, and engage the strongest version of the opposing view rather than a strawman. I'm especially stuck on whether an institution can be guilty in a way its current members personally aren't, and whether celebrating the continuity (founding date, archives, "our proud history") creates obligations that legal discontinuity doesn't erase.
