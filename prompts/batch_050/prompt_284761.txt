I've been reading about hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy, and they seem to me to show that historical explanation is impossible in principle: since we only ever learn how events turned out and then stitch causes onto the outcome after the fact, every causal account is a retrospective confabulation dressed up as analysis. If that's right, why do historians still bother debating, say, the causes of the First World War, instead of simply compiling neutral chronologies and conceding that anything beyond "what happened, in what order" is storytelling? Wouldn't a discipline that genuinely took these findings seriously just stop explaining altogether?
