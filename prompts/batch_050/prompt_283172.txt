I teach an introductory psychology module, and a classroom demonstration has left me with a question I cannot locate in any of my textbooks, so I am hoping there is an established name for what I keep observing.

The demonstration uses two small lamps fixed to a board, roughly a hand's width apart, wired so that they light in strict alternation: left on, left off, right on, right off, with a carefully chosen gap in between. When the gap is long, students report exactly what is happening -- one lamp, then the other, two separate events. When the gap is very short, they report the two lamps flashing simultaneously. But in a narrow band in between, nearly everyone reports something neither condition licenses: a single lamp traveling from one side of the board to the other. What unsettles me is not the illusion of motion itself but its apparent timing. To see a lamp in transit, arriving at the right-hand position, one must somehow already register the right-hand lamp as lit -- yet the experience of movement seems to begin before that second lamp could have been processed, as though the visual system showed me the middle of the journey before it could have known the destination. I have noticed a structurally similar wrinkle when I glance at a wall clock: the first second after my eyes land on it seems to stretch, as though the clock paused for me, which again feels like my experience containing information my brain could only have acquired a moment later.

My questions are these. First, does the traveling-lamp illusion have an established name, and is the timing puzzle I have described the standard reason it is discussed, or have I latched onto something incidental? Second, does the clock case belong to the same family or is it a separate phenomenon with a name of its own? Third, and most important to me: I can think of exactly two ways to resolve the timing puzzle -- either the brain delays the experience until it has all the information and then presents the sequence as having occurred earlier than it did, or the experience occurred on time but in a cruder form and my memory of it was subsequently rewritten to include the destination. Do these two positions have established names in the literature, is there any observation or experiment that could in principle decide between them, or is the distinction between them somehow not a real one?

Please do not simply hand me labels. I want to know precisely what each named phenomenon is, where its boundaries lie, how the lamp case and the clock case relate, and, if the two resolutions I sketched correspond to known positions, which considerations have been taken to favor one over the other -- or, if the consensus is that the question is ill-posed, why.
