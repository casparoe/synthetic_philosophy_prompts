A friend of mine who works in social policy has strong, consistent-seeming instincts about cash transfer programs, and I want to figure out what rule she is actually running. Her verdicts so far:

1. A flat monthly child benefit paid to all families, phased out above a high household-income threshold: approve.
2. An earned income tax credit that rises with earnings, then tapers: reject.
3. A negative income tax guaranteeing an income floor, withdrawn at 50% of each dollar earned: approve.
4. A one-off stimulus payment to every adult below an income cap during a recession: approve.
5. Unemployment insurance conditional on documented job search: reject.
6. A government job guarantee at a living wage for anyone who wants work: reject.

Now two new proposals are on the table, and I want to predict her verdicts. Proposal A: a universal basic income of $1,000 per month paid to every adult, no conditions, no phase-out -- billionaires get it too. Proposal B: the same $1,000 per month paid to every adult, but fully clawed back through the income tax system as earnings rise, so that its net effect is identical to the negative income tax in (3).

Your task: identify at least two distinct rules that are each consistent with all six verdicts in (1)-(6), state what each rule predicts for Proposals A and B, give your best prediction of her actual verdicts with reasons, and specify one further hypothetical program whose verdict would best discriminate between your candidate rules. Note that at least one of your rules should treat Proposals A and B differently despite their economic equivalence, and you should say whether treating them differently is defensible.

Please return your answer as a JSON object with the following fields: "candidate_rules", an array of objects each with fields "rule" (string), "verdict_proposal_a" ("approve" or "reject"), and "verdict_proposal_b" ("approve" or "reject"); "predicted_verdicts", an object with fields "proposal_a" and "proposal_b"; "most_plausible_rule" (string, with justification); "tiebreaker_program" (string describing the discriminating hypothetical and what each rule would say about it); and "is_a_b_asymmetry_defensible" (boolean, plus a one-sentence rationale in a field "asymmetry_rationale").
