I’m a junior on student council, and somehow I’ve ended up on the committee that reviews proposals to change old school rules and traditions. We get a few every month, and people constantly use Chesterton’s fence in both directions: either we can’t remove anything until we know exactly why it exists, or the rule is obviously stupid so we should just tear it down and see what happens. Neither version seems right. I get the basic Burkean point that institutions can contain practical knowledge that current people don’t consciously understand, but I also don’t want a procedure that makes “it’s old” into an automatic reason to keep something unfair.

Can you turn the defensible part of Chesterton’s fence and Burkean conservatism into an actual procedure our committee could follow? It needs to take under thirty minutes per proposal, since we meet after school and usually have several items. Assume we can ask one teacher, one administrator, and maybe a custodian or coach, but we can’t do archival research. Please give the questions in order, say what would count as a good enough answer to each one, and make clear where the procedure should stop with no change, where it should recommend a reversible pilot or partial replacement, and where it has to hand things over to a judgment about values. I especially want help distinguishing a genuine hidden function from a rationalization someone invents after the fact. The procedure should also deal with status quo bias, path dependence, who benefits from the tradition, who pays its costs, whether the people affected ever consented, and whether the hidden function could be preserved in a less exclusionary way. Don’t simplify those distinctions away unless they genuinely don’t matter for school decisions.

Then run the procedure on these cases. First, students want to abolish the hated rule against food and drinks in the courtyard; nobody knows why it started, but the custodian says it used to prevent ants and slippery spills, while lunch is now shorter than when the rule was created. Second, some students want to get rid of the seniors-only bench because it seems unfair; alumni say it gives students something to look forward to, while younger students say it just makes hierarchy official. Third, students want to replace the annual teachers-versus-students basketball game with an open talent show because lots of people can’t or don’t want to play basketball; the game is supposedly the one event where some normally unnoticed students get recognition, but in practice mostly athletes participate. Include at least one outcome I probably won’t like, such as keeping or only cautiously changing something I personally want scrapped, and explain why the procedure forced that result. Also say what kinds of proposals the procedure cannot settle and where it might misfire even if everyone follows it honestly.

Please answer in continuous prose only, like an email: no headings, bullet points, bold text, numbered lists, or tables.
