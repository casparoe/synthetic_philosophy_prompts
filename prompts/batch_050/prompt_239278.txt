Got into it at a lab meeting last week (I'm on the neuro side, he's ML) about whether mind uploading is even coherent, and he shut the whole thing down with 'the brain is a computer, so of course you could run it on different hardware.' I've heard this slogan a thousand times and I genuinely still can't tell what it commits anyone to. Can you unpack the different precise claims it could be making? Some candidate readings to get you started -- that the brain can in principle be simulated by a computer, that thinking literally is computation over representations, that the brain is digital the way my laptop is digital, that what matters about the brain is its abstract program rather than its wetware -- but I suspect there are more, and honestly I'm not even sure the ones I listed are all distinct from each other. For each reading: is it a platitude, a live empirical or philosophical thesis, or just false? And the part I actually care about: which readings would support his move about uploading, and which wouldn't? I have a nagging feeling the version that's obviously true (something something Church-Turing) is way too weak for that, while the version strong enough for that is not obviously true, and that people slide between the two mid-sentence. One more thing: if 'computation' gets defined loosely enough that a rock or a wall is computing something too, does the slogan go trivially true and therefore useless -- and is there a way to state it so it avoids that without already assuming the uploading conclusion? Lay the readings out as a menu, not one long essay on your favorite.
