I am a novelist at work on a book about two epistemologists — former collaborators, now estranged — who co-authored a paper years ago and have since come to disagree bitterly about whether its central argument was sound. One, on encountering the other's defection, treated it as decisive evidence that she should lower her confidence; the other held his ground, insisting that conciliating in the face of every disagreement is a recipe for intellectual spinelessness. For a chapter set at a conference where the two are forced onto the same panel, I need the document they might have produced had they attempted an adversarial collaboration before their falling-out: a joint statement between a committed conciliationist and a committed steadfast theorist about peer disagreement.

Please draft that document. I want (1) a numbered section of claims both parties can sign — including points about what higher-order evidence is, what makes someone an epistemic peer, and which cases (e.g., the restaurant-bill calculation) both accept as legitimately moving; (2) a disagreements table in which each residual dispute is stated in wording its own side would endorse, neither vacuous nor slanted toward conciliationism or steadfastness; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party; and (4) for each crux, a case, argument, or consideration both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction or verdict recorded in advance. I would like you to give real attention to one wrinkle my novel leans on: disagreements about peerhood itself, where each party doubts whether the other still counts as a peer — does the conciliationist's view apply there, or does it quietly presuppose a prior judgment of peerhood that cannot itself be conciliated away? If you conclude that some part of the standard debate is merely verbal, mark which part and explain why.

The chapter ends with the panel moderator asking which single question, if answered, would do the most to resolve the dispute, so please conclude with that judgment stated in fixed form on the last line: \answer{the name or short description of the single most load-bearing crux}.
