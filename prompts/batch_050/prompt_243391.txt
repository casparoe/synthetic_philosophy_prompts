For a graduate seminar on comparative moral philosophy, I am putting together the specification against which students will assess rival reconstructions of karma, and I want the specification itself to do the philosophical work. Write the list of desiderata that a doctrine of karma must meet to count as a theory of moral desert — as opposed to a doctrine of cosmic luck, a motivational psychology, or a theodicy — giving the consideration or hard case that motivates each requirement, and marking which requirements are genuine adequacy conditions and which are parochial demands of one interpretive camp.

The list must engage at least the following pressure points: that desert must attach to an agent's own deeds, so that any version permitting the transfer or inheritance of karmic merit between persons needs a defense rather than an assumption; the cross-lifetime identity problem, where the theory must state what makes a reborn subject the proper bearer of desert generated by a predecessor who shares neither memory nor body with them; proportionality between deed and outcome, including whether consequences outside the agent's intention enter the ledger, and what that implies about moral luck; the epistemic constraint that the theory must not license inferring desert from observed suffering — that is, it must block the just-world inference from this person suffers to this person deserves to suffer; whether karmic consequences run through ordinary psychological and social mechanisms or require a non-natural causal channel, and what each option costs in explanatory reach; whether karma is retributive or educative, i.e., whether the theory must explain how karmic consequence can improve the agent rather than merely balance a ledger; and whether non-moral means such as ritual or austerity can discharge karmic debt, since if they can, the theory's claim to be about desert is in question.

Then say whether some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — candidate tensions include strict proportionality combined with the denial of moral luck, full retributive stringency combined with the epistemic constraint, and detectability of karmic justice within a single lifetime combined with any of the above — and identify which single requirement excludes the most candidate reconstructions. Open your response with one sentence stating that most-excluding requirement, or that the full list is jointly unsatisfiable if that is your finding, then proceed directly to the list: no preamble, no restating of this brief, and no scoring of the classical schools, which the seminar will do itself.
