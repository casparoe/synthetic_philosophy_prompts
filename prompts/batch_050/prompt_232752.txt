So my dad's memory has been getting worse and the whole family just went through the advance directive wringer, because back in his 50s he wrote this very stern document about what should happen if he ever got dementia -- no TV he considered trash, keep him on his routine, absolutely no moving him somewhere with "institutional food." Except now he's genuinely delighted by exactly the stuff he banned. He watches cooking competition shows for hours and laughs, he loves the cafeteria-style lunch at his day program, and honestly he seems happier than he did when he was "himself." I've been reading a bit about this -- apparently philosophers fight about whether the earlier self gets to overrule the current self, precedent autonomy and all that, and there's this famous case about a woman with Alzheimer's who seems content but whose past self would be horrified. Anyway it got me thinking about what a care home would look like if it took the "your past self is the real boss" view completely seriously, like as its whole business model.

Write me the "Welcome to Your New Home" orientation letter that such a facility sends to incoming residents -- deadpan, corporate-warm, the tone of a hotel welcome packet crossed with a law firm. Around 600-700 words, broken into a few short sections or FAQ entries. The comedy should come from the philosophy being taken literally: the facility's duty runs to the signer, not the signee; the chef is contractually obligated to ignore what you now enjoy eating because 1997-you was a vegetarian; the common room only plays the music on file in your directive; there's an appeals process but your current testimony is inadmissible on grounds of incompetence, though you may submit written objections *in advance*, which you cannot do, because that would require foresight. Include at least one nod to the Ulysses-and-the-sirens logic (a "no untying" clause) and one genuinely uncomfortable beat where the letter congratulates a contented resident on wishes she no longer remembers making. The satire should land the real pressure points of the debate -- whose interests count when the person persists but the values don't, whether a happy demented self is being wronged or liberated -- without ever stepping out of character to explain them. Don't wink at the reader; let the brochure stay fully sincere the whole way through, and end on the signature line of the facility's director, who has helpfully included his own advance directive should you wish to review his credentials.
