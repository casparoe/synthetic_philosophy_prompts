So I'm on a panel next month about enhancement policy and I wrote up my "position" for the others to read beforehand, and now I'm worried it doesn't actually hang together. Here's what I hold, roughly:

(1) The therapy/enhancement line has no moral weight in itself. Whether an intervention restores species-typical functioning or goes beyond it doesn't matter; what matters is how much good it does, for whom, and whether anyone's made worse off.

(2) Public healthcare is for treating disease. Nobody has a claim on the state for enhancements — if you want a memory boost or an extra inch of height, that's between you and the market, like cosmetic surgery.

(3) I want "disease" understood naturalistically, something like departure from species-typical functioning — not just whatever a given society happens to find undesirable. I don't want the concept gerrymandered to track what we disapprove of.

(4) Aging is not a disease. It's what human bodies normally do; nothing has malfunctioned when an 85-year-old has brittle bones and a bad memory.

(5) If a genuine anti-aging intervention existed (suppose something that reliably slowed or reversed the whole cluster of age-related decline), it should be publicly funded the way other medicine is. Withholding it on the grounds that "aging isn't a disease" seems monstrous to me.

(6) I'm fine with a doctor prescribing stimulants to a healthy grad student who wants to get through their qualifying exams, same way I'm fine with coffee.

So: does this package survive contact with itself? Tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do. I'm especially unsure whether (1) is doing the work I think it's doing -- I had it in my head that (1) is what lets me hold both (2) and (5), but now I'm not sure it helps either of them. Rank any real tensions by how bad they are, and for the worst one tell me which single commitment is cheapest to give up and what it would cost me. Also: is (6) actually consistent with whatever principle ends up rescuing (2), or am I only okay with it because I don't take grad school seriously the way I take medicine?
