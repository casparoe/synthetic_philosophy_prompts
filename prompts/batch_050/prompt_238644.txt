I'm doing my MBA and for our capstone my team is pitching an insect protein startup — cricket flour bars, basically. One of my teammates, Nina, did a philosophy minor and is insisting that our pitch deck include a whole section on "cricket welfare": stuff like chilling them before slaughter so they supposedly go numb first, lower stocking densities in the bins, not letting them get dehydrated. This adds real cost — our margins are thin and the investor judges are 100% going to ask why we're burning money on bugs.

Her argument, as best I can reconstruct it, isn't that crickets definitely suffer. She freely admits maybe there's only a 1% chance, or even 0.1%, that they experience anything at all. But our facility would process something like two billion crickets a year, and she says once you multiply even a tiny chance of suffering by numbers that big, the "expected suffering" dwarfs whatever we'd spend, so the welfare measures are obviously worth it. I pushed back: by that logic shouldn't she also donate her salary to some astronomer who claims there's a 0.001% chance that dust clouds or dark matter are conscious, or fund research into whether plants scream? She said that's different but couldn't quite articulate why, which is what got me suspicious of the whole line of reasoning.

I have to decide whether to back her in front of the judges or side with our finance guy who wants the section cut. A few things I'm stuck on: is there any principled way to set a floor — some probability small enough that you just ignore it no matter how large the stakes get — or is that an evasion? If I grant her framework, does it actually support the specific things she wants (chilling, density), or does it really just imply we shouldn't farm insects at all — and is she being inconsistent given that she happily eats the bars herself? And practically: is there a defensible middle position, like only adopting the welfare measures that cost almost nothing, or is that just incoherent fence-sitting under her own logic? Don't tell me whether crickets are conscious — nobody knows — tell me whether her way of reasoning holds up and exactly where it breaks.
