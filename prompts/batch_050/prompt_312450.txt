Next month I'm presenting at a phil-of-biology colloquium, defending the thesis that Dennett's own favorite crane for culture -- memes, ideas as replicators that literally copy themselves -- is itself a case of the greedy reductionism he warns about, and that the cultural attraction crowd (Sperber, Claidière and co.) have it right: culture is mostly ordinary minds reconstructing ideas from scratch each generation, with copying doing much less work than the meme picture needs. The audience is tricky: one devoted Dennett student who thinks natural selection is substrate-neutral and so obviously applies to culture, two cultural evolution researchers in the Boyd/Richerson modeling tradition, and a couple of anthropologists already on my side. Prep me for the Q&A as a numbered list of exactly five objections, each phrased the way that specific audience member would actually put it (their vocabulary, their priors), with a sentence on what I should concede versus hold, and no intro or conclusion. One of the five has to be the objection I'm dreading: that I've just rebranded memes as "attraction" and the whole disagreement is verbal.
