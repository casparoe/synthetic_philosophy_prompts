I'm the compliance officer for a mid-sized development NGO running health and agriculture programs in about a dozen countries, mostly places where gift-giving to officials, hiring the village chief's nephew, and long lunches with ministry staff are just how things get done. I review maybe 30 flagged expenses and procurement decisions a month, and my calls can get staff fired or get us kicked out of a country, so I can't just wing it.

Take the philosophical debate about whether corruption is culturally relative — gift economies, clientelism, patrimonial obligations, the claim that Western anti-bribery norms are just one culture's rulebook — and turn it into a decision procedure I can actually run on each case. I want tests applied in order, not a vibes-based "does this feel corrupt?" The procedure has to at least distinguish: gifts that maintain relationships vs payments that buy a specific decision, diffuse clientelistic loyalty vs explicit quid pro quo, and practices that are locally normal but still quietly screw over third parties (e.g. rivals who can't pay) vs locally normal and genuinely harmless. Tell me which distinctions from the theory survive being turned into procedure and which ones I just have to drop, and mark the steps where judgment can't be eliminated rather than pretending it's all mechanical. Then walk through two or three worked examples — including one where the procedure condemns something I'd honestly rather approve (a small customary gift to the clerk who processed our permits on time) and one where it clears something my London HQ would find shocking. Also say what the procedure flat-out can't settle.
