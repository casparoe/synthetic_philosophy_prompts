In my moral psychology seminar, the situationist critique of virtue ethics has ended not in resolution but in a strange armistice, and I would like a diagnosis. The state of play, as I understand it: critics, following Gilbert Harman and John Doris, argued that familiar findings — seminarians' willingness to help a slumped stranger varying with how much of a hurry they are in, a dime planted in a phone-booth return slot predicting helping, the low consistency of children's honest behavior across different kinds of test in the 1920s studies — show that behavior tracks trivial situational variables rather than robust, cross-situationally consistent character traits; and since virtue ethics rests its normative and educational program on such traits, its psychology is empirically inadequate. Defenders replied that virtue was always supposed to be rare, so population-level experiments cannot touch it; that the experiments measure behavior but not the reasons for which agents act, which is where virtue lives; that nothing in the view requires global traits rather than situation-specific ones; and, later, that the cognitive-affective personality system — stable if–then behavioral signatures — rehabilitates character in a form the data actually support. Both sides now largely talk past these replies; the critique is still cited as devastating and still dismissed as a misunderstanding.

My hypothesis is that the stall conceals a disagreement about what establishes a psychological posit as real: the critics work with a psychometric criterion — a trait exists only if it yields predictive reliability across situations — while the defenders work with an agential criterion — a trait is real if it figures in the structure of practical reasoning, however situationally gated its manifestations. If so, the empirical literature was never going to settle anything. Test or refute this hypothesis. Identify what kind of disagreement this actually is: a factual one about effect sizes, a conceptual one about what a disposition is, a normative one about what virtue ethics is committed to, or two research programs answering different questions under the shared label character. Then say, given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — an agreed operationalization, a decisive study design, a distinction nobody has yet drawn — or argue that none should be expected, and why.
