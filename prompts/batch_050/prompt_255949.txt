I've been a longtime reader of the rationalist blogs and forums, so I'm comfortable with disambiguation-as-philosophy, but I got stuck in an argument with friends last weekend that I couldn't dissect on the spot. The setup: one friend had promised, months ago and rather casually, to help another friend move apartments. When the day came, he'd been offered a shift at work he badly wanted, and he asked whether he was really bound, given that the promise was made offhand, that she could hire movers for not much money, and that she'd probably have forgiven him. A third friend shut the whole discussion down with "a promise is a promise," as though that settled it. Everyone nodded and moved on, and I've been irritated ever since, because the sentence is either a tautology or doing an enormous amount of unstated philosophical work, and I genuinely don't know which.

Here is what I'd like from you. Take "a promise is a promise" as a piece of language and lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise theses it could be expressing. I can gesture at the shape of the space: it might be a bare definitional claim (a promise just is a binding commitment, so the sentence is analytic); it might be a claim about a natural or pre-conventional moral fact that promising triggers; it might be a practice-relative claim (within the institution of promising, the rules admit no exceptions of this kind); it might be a reliance-based claim (she organized her plans around his word, and that's what does the binding); it might be a claim about the expressive or communicative act itself; it might even be a covert normative claim about what kind of person one should be, dressed up as a description. For each reading you identify: say whether it's a platitude, a substantive but defensible position, or simply false; note which standard puzzle cases (promises extracted under duress, promises to the dead, promises about trivial matters, promises made under a misapprehension) it handles well or badly; and, most importantly, state whether it would actually license my third friend's conversational move of ending the debate about the apartment, since the precisification that matters is the one his intervention needed to be true.

You needn't resolve the original question of whether he should have skipped the shift; what I want is the disambiguation with verdicts, so I can bring it back to the group. Please give the readings as a numbered list rather than picking one and defending it. End with a single final line beginning "Verdict:" that names, in a short phrase, the one reading under which "a promise is a promise" would actually settle whether my friend had to help with the move.
