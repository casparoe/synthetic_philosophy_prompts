I am writing up my position on Maxwell's demon and the thermodynamics of information for a philosophy-of-physics working group, and before I circulate it I would like an audit of whether my commitments are jointly tenable. Here is the set as it currently stands.

First, I accept the standard resolution associated with Bennett: the demon fails to violate the second law because resetting its memory to a blank state carries a cost of roughly kT ln 2 per bit, in line with Landauer's principle, and this cost exactly offsets the entropy the demon squeezes out of the gas; the measurement itself, by contrast, can in principle be carried out with arbitrarily little dissipation.

Second, I take Landauer's principle to be a theorem rather than an empirical conjecture: given the second law and the logic of irreversible computation, no physically realizable device, present or future, can erase information at lower cost.

Third, I hold that the second law is fundamentally statistical. Fluctuation theorems and simple probabilistic reasoning show it can be violated with nonzero probability, especially in small systems, so it has no exceptionless content.

Fourth, I am sympathetic to the view that entropy is partly observer-relative: what counts as a macrostate, and hence what entropy a system has, depends on the coarse-graining an agent adopts, so two observers with different measurement and control capabilities can correctly assign different entropies to the same gas.

Fifth, I insist that the heat dissipated when the demon erases its memory is an objective physical quantity: it warms a real thermal bath regardless of what anyone knows or believes, and no change in an observer's state of information can make it go away.

Please examine this set and tell me where it is in trouble. For each tension you find, say how severe it is, what exactly pulls against what, and what the cheapest fix would be and what that fix costs. Please distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones, since I suspect at least one apparent conflict dissolves once the relevant claims are stated at the right level of precision, and I would rather not be told that everything is equally problematic. Rank any genuine tensions by severity, and conclude by telling me which single revision would clean up the most of the set while preserving as much of my original position as possible. Write your answer as continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
