I serve on the examination committee for an upper-level undergraduate course in the philosophy of social science, and this year's paper needs a question on Karl Popper's The Poverty of Historicism. Our students have read Popper alongside some critics, and in past years they have done depressingly well by simply reciting his conclusion that there can be no laws of historical development and no scientific prediction of the course of history. I want a question that forces them to actually deploy his arguments rather than summarize them, so please draft a single essay question, answerable in 75 minutes without access to outside sources, built around the following test case you should weave into the question itself: a historical demographer claims to have discovered a law stating that every agrarian society in which adult literacy exceeds 60 percent industrializes within two generations, and she presents it as a refutation of Popper. Candidates should be asked whether Popper's arguments rule out her alleged law, which requires them at minimum to distinguish unconditional laws from trends that hold only conditionally on initial conditions, to assess whether her claim is a law in Popper's sense or merely a conditional generalization he never denied, and to grapple with his argument that the future growth of human knowledge is in principle unpredictable and that history depends on that growth. The question should also push on whether that knowledge argument proves too much: if it works, does it also rule out laws in evolutionary biology or macroeconomics, and if not, what exactly is distinctive about history?

Along with the question, provide a model answer of roughly the length a strong candidate could produce in the allotted time, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points with marks assigned to each (totaling 20). For each point, add a one-line note describing how candidates typically get it wrong — for instance, treating Popper as denying that any useful generalizations about history exist, or conflating his attack on historicist prophecy with an attack on piecemeal social engineering, which he in fact endorsed. The difficulty of the question should come from making recitation insufficient: a candidate who has memorized Popper's chapter structure but cannot say what would have to be true for the demographer's claim to count as a law of history should not be able to pass. Please write the question, model answer, and marking scheme in American English.
