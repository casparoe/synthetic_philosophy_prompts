I hold the position that the apparently life-permitting values of various physical constants are better explained by a large ensemble of universes with varying parameters plus an observer-selection effect — we could only ever find ourselves in a universe that permits observers — than by any designer hypothesis. What would count against this? Please sort the candidate defeaters by how decisive they would be: empirical developments in cosmology or particle physics that would undermine the ensemble's motivation; formal problems, such as the difficulty of defining a probability measure over an infinite collection of universes; the worry that a typical-observer reasoning that vindicates the ensemble also generates unwelcome predictions about what kind of observers we should expect to be; and philosophical arguments that a selection effect explains nothing beyond what we already knew we would observe. Distinguish objections that serious proponents of the ensemble view would themselves concede as genuine costs from objections pressed only by critics, note which defeaters have arguably already materialized, and close with an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified into something that risks no possible observation — and, if so, whether that is fatal for a hypothesis of this particular kind.
