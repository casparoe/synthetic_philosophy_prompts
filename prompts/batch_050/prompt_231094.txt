I've spent enough years reading rationalist forums to notice a pattern: whenever the ontology of mathematics comes up, the discussion stalls because everyone is arguing from labels rather than commitments. Someone calls themselves a "structuralist" and it turns out they mean that mathematical objects don't exist at all; someone else uses the same word to mean that numbers exist but have no properties beyond their structural relations; a third person thinks the whole question dissolves once you notice that "2 ∈ 4" is true under the von Neumann reduction of the naturals but false under Zermelo's. I'd like to build a decision-tree handout — call it "which philosophy of mathematics do you actually hold, as applied to the identity of numbers" — that sorts people by their verdicts on concrete cases rather than by which -ism they find aesthetically appealing. The audience is smart laypeople who know some set theory but have never read Benacerraf, so every diagnostic question must be answerable from the case itself, without philosophical vocabulary.

Please construct the tree from the logical space first and locate actual positions in it second. The diagnostic nodes should turn on cases where the views genuinely come apart, and I'd like you to include at least these: whether "is 2 an element of 4?" is a genuine question with an answer, a genuine question with no fact-of-the-matter answer, or a malformed question that only looks grammatical; whether the natural number 2 and the real number 2 and the complex number 2 are one object, several objects, or a pseudo-question of the same kind; whether the fact that set theory offers many equally good reductions of the naturals (von Neumann, Zermelo, others) shows that numbers aren't sets, that numbers are one particular reduction we just haven't identified, or that arithmetic is only about what all reductions share; and whether "two" could in principle turn out to be Julius Caesar — i.e., whether cross-categorical identifications have truth values at all. Each leaf should name the position located there (I'd expect, among others: set-theoretic reductionism of a committed single-reduction kind, eliminative structuralism, non-eliminative or ante rem structuralism, abstractionist views in the neo-Fregean neighborhood, and fictionalism) and give one sentence on its most awkward consequence — the thing its defender has to swallow.

Two further requests. First, flag any combinations of answers that are logically available but unoccupied by any position you know of, and briefly diagnose why nobody sits there: incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Second, be honest about the tree's resolution limits: if two positions (say, certain flavors of eliminativism and certain flavors of nominalism) give identical verdicts on every diagnostic question, say so explicitly and note what further case or commitment would be needed to separate them, rather than papering over the gap with a definitional sleight of hand. Aim for six to nine diagnostic nodes, and end with a one-paragraph note on which leaf, if any, you think a typical rationalist-forum reader would be surprised to find themselves at.
