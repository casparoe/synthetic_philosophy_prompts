I teach a graduate seminar in aesthetics, and our final meeting concerns the ontology of musical works: whether a symphony is an abstract type instanced by its performances, a concrete historical individual, or something else again. I would like to close the session by reading aloud a villanelle that enacts the type-token structure rather than merely describing it. The two refrains should function as the work itself, returning unaltered across the poem the way the work is said to persist through its performances, while the tercets between them should play out three distinct performances of the same piece: one scrupulously faithful and somehow bloodless, one willfully ornamented by a performer who insists the work licenses it, and one derailed by a wrong note that the audience never notices. The closing quatrain should introduce a recording and let it put pressure on the whole scheme, since a recording fixes a single token into the very thing we return to as though it were the type. The governing conceit should be that the poem is itself a work of the contested kind, with each recitation a performance of it, so that the reader feels the difference between missing a line and missing the poem. Keep the metaphysics accurate enough that a philosopher of art could identify the commitments each stanza flirts with, including the nominalist's unease in the ornamented performance and the Platonist's reliance on the refrains, without the poem ever lapsing into lecture. Nineteen lines, in strict villanelle form, with the refrains doing the argumentative work of showing what survives variation and what, in the recording's case, perhaps does not.
