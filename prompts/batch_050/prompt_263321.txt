I help run a math circle for strong high-school students, and I've promised one two-hour session titled "Is infinity real?" My honest goal is not to convince them of anything but to get them to see that Cantor's diagonal argument and the finitist's refusal to accept its conclusion are not a case of proof versus stubbornness -- that the disagreement is about what one is permitted to posit, not about whether any step of reasoning is valid. Plan the session for me. Do not deliver the session; tell me how to run it.

Specifically: (1) The order of ideas. Something has to come before both Hilbert's hotel and diagonalization -- my instinct is the potential/actual distinction and the idea that "larger than any finite set" and "a completed infinite totality" are different claims that finite experience underdetermines -- but tell me whether that is right or whether the bijection criterion for same-size must come first, and justify the sequence by saying what each stage depends on. (2) The carrying example. Hilbert's hotel is tempting for the actual-infinity half, but I worry it encourages the students to think of infinity as a weird number with funny arithmetic. Say whether to use it anyway and what I must strip from the standard telling, and say what example should carry the diagonal half -- arbitrary decimal expansions, characteristic functions of sets of naturals, or something else -- given that the students have no set theory. (3) The misconception map. I expect at least: that infinity-plus-one arithmetic is what the debate is about; that 0.999... is infinitesimally short of 1; that the diagonal construction "shows there are uncountably many digits"; and that the finitist denies that very large finite numbers exist or that any process can go on without bound. For each, tell me at which point in the sequence to head it off and how, including the moment where I must make clear that the finitist reads the diagonal construction as a rule for refuting any proposed enumeration rather than as a survey of a finished list -- and what the finitist is actually rejecting (a premise about totalities, presumably, or power set), not an inference. (4) A closing check that distinguishes "got it" from "nodded along": I want one question whose answer reveals whether a student can state the finitist position in a form the finitist would accept, and one whose answer reveals whether they can locate the exact point in the proof where the commitment to completed infinity enters. (5) What to leave out and say so: I assume ordinals, the continuum hypothesis, and the distinction between finitism and ultrafinitism should all be excluded, but tell me if any of them is needed to prevent a predictable confusion.
