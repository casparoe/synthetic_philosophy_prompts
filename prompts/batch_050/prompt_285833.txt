Write me a short story (somewhere in the 800–1200 word range) about two characters stuck playing something like the centipede game with each other. Say, two people on a long road trip deciding at every gas stop whether to keep splitting the pot they pooled for the trip or grab it and ditch the other, or come up with your own setup -- the key is a repeated situation where either can end it now for a bit more than they'd get if the other ends it next, but both do way better if it keeps going.

Here's the twist I want: one character has actually read the game theory. They're the type who reasons it through -- if we're both rational, the other person will take it at the last step, so I should take it one step earlier, so they should take it two steps earlier, all the way down -- and this reasoning basically poisons everything. The other character has no idea about any of that and just... trusts, or is stubborn, or too lazy to do the math. Don't tell us in so many words which approach ends up "right"; let the story earn whatever ending you give it.

Try to make the game-theory person's chain of reasoning feel real and lived-in (the moment they can't stop running it, even when they want to) rather than like a textbook explantion. No character should ever say the words "backward induction" or "centipede game."
