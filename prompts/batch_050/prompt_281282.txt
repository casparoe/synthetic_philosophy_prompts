So a friend and I (both longtime LessWrong/ACX readers, the kind who unironically say "Goodhart" at parties) got into an argument about whether alignment discourse has secretly reinvented theology, and I want to settle this properly. Some background: I know the Euthyphro dilemma decently from a phil elective I took ages ago. Is the pious loved by the gods because it's pious, or pious because the gods love it? And I roughly know the standard moves: horn one gives you divine command theory where morality is arbitrary because whatever God happens to command is good by definition, horn two makes goodness independent of God so that God is just an infallible reporter of a standard he doesn't control, and then there's the apologist's third option where goodness is grounded in God's nature, so it's neither arbitrary nor external.

Here's the ask. Restate the dilemma and all three of those responses in the vocabulary I actually think in — reward models, RLHF, human feedback, reward hacking, idealized judgment, distribution shift, all that stuff. Roughly I expect horn one to come out as "good is just whatever the reward model approves of, full stop," horn two as "the reward model approves of things because they're good, it's a measurement device for a standard that exists independently of it," and the third option as something like "good is what my idealized, fully-reflected self would endorse." Do the translation as precisely as you can, being honest about where you have to stretch. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the dilemma translate cleanly, which parts you had to fudge, and which parts of each side have no expression at all on the other side. Two things I already suspect, tell me if I'm wrong: (1) the metaphysical necessity of God's nature doesn't seem to have a counterpart — my idealized self could have been otherwise, God's nature (on the classical view) couldn't, so does the third option survive translation or does it become a much weaker move? (2) the gods can't be Goodharted — nobody can wirehead Zeus — so is the whole control problem just residue that the Euthyphro framework literally cannot express?

One more thing that bugs me. If horn one is right and reward model approval just IS goodness, then reward hacking becomes definitionally impossible, which is obviously false — we observe it alot. Does that mean horn one was always incoherent, or does it show that the translation is broken because "good" in alignment-land is doing double duty as both the target and the metric, whereas in the theological debate those roles are cleanly separated? Basically I want to know whether my intuition that alignment theory is applied metaethics with worse vocabulary is right, or whether there's something in the Euthyphro debate that my RLHF brain can't even say.
