Just got home from a three hour argument with a friend who's deep into Kuhn and thinks basicly every change in science is a paradigm shift. Except, weirdly, not everything. Her actual verdicts, as best I could pin her down:

- Ptolemy to Copernicus: paradigm shift, and genuinely incommensurable.
- Phlogiston to oxygen chemistry: shift, incommensurable.
- Newtonian to relativistic mechanics: shift, but only partly incommensurable (she grants Newton's equations survive as a limiting case, so translation isn't hopeless).
- Continental drift to plate tectonics: NOT a shift. Her line was that everyone agreed on the data and on what would settle the dispute (find a mechanism), and most geologists converted within a decade or so once seafloor spreading showed up, so it was just normal science doing its job.
- Discovery of the neutron: not a shift, just an addition.

The fight was about a new case: the ulcer revolution. Until the early 80s ulcers were a stress and acid disease, treated with antacids, bland diets, and surgery; then Warren and Marshall argued most were caused by H. pylori and curable with antibiotics, the gastroenterology establishment fought them hard for years, and then the field flipped completely. Shift or not? And if it's a shift, incommensurable or not?

What I want from you: predict her verdict, and tell me what rule she seems to be running. I can see at least three candidates -- meaning change in the key terms ("planet" and "mass" changed, "continent" and "neutron" didn't), change in what counts as a legitimate explanation or solution to the field's problems, or the sociological thing about whether the old guard converts or has to die off -- and as far as I can tell all three fit her five verdicts but disagree about ulcers. If I'm wrong that they all fit, show me where. And tell me which further case, real or invented, would most cleanly seperate her actual rule from the pretenders.
