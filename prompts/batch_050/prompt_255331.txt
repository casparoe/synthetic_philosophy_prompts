About a year ago I basically converted to hard epistemic modesty. I'd been reading a bunch of the judgment-and-forecasting stuff (Tetlock, the planning fallacy literature, Kahneman's inside view vs outside view distinction, some Hanson) and got convinced enough to adopt it as an actual personal policy: whenever my first-person take on how something will go for ME conflicts with the relevant base rate or with expert consensus, the outside view wins by default, and the burden is on me to show why I'm a legitimate exception rather than just another person who thinks they're special. It felt like finally growing up epistemically. My friends think I've become insufferable, but honestly the policy seemed to be working.

Then it got personal and now I'm less sure. Two things happened. First, I used the policy to talk myself out of quitting my job to start a company with two friends — base rates for startups are brutal — and now I'm watching them do well without me and wondering what exactly I deferred to, since the base rate I used includes a lot of people nothing like us. Second, and this is the part that actually bugs me: I noticed that applying the policy constantly requires me to make inside-view judgments. Which reference class is the right one? Is my situation exceptional in the relevant respect? Is this consensus independent or just people copying each other? Answering any of those seems to require exactly the kind of first-person reasoning the policy tells me to distrust. So the modesty policy seems to secretly run on the fuel it forbids, and I can't tell if that's a real problem or just me being clever.

So here's my ask: I've stated the strong modesty position as I actually hold it. What would count against it? I don't want the lazy answer ("sometimes the consensus is wrong, e.g. [famous example]" — those are survivorship-biased and I know it). I want the observations, cases, or arguments that should genuinely move someone in my position: things like, would it matter if it turned out that people who follow outside-view policies don't actually have better outcomes than confident inside-view reasoners? Would it matter if the self-reference problem above can't be cleanly solved? What about selection effects in the base rates themselves — is that a defeater or just an adjustment the view absorbs? Please sort the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones committed modesty defenders would themselves concede count against the view (as opposed to what critics say), and give me an honest verdict on whether the position has quietly become unfalsifiable — where every apparent failure gets re-described as "you picked the wrong reference class" — and if so, whether that's actually a problem for what is essentially a policy rather than a theory.
