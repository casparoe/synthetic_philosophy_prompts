So I write literary fiction, and I'm stuck on something that's gone from craft worry to full-on philosophical itch. My new novel has a recurring metaphor -- the narrator describes her grief as a tenant who stopped paying rent years ago but can't be evicted. My beta reader wrote in the margin that the line "means" the narrator feels exploited by her own mourning, like grief is extracting something from her. Honestly that's not what I meant, I was going for helplessness, the landlord's powerlessness not the cost. But her reading is... good? Better than mine, maybe? And this got me spiralling: if the sentence means something I didn't intend and didn't even notice, then metaphorical meaning can't just be whatever the literal words say plus whatever I hoped readers would feel.

Which dragged me back to a debate I half-remember from a philosophy of language course I sat in on years ago: Donald Davidson arguing that metaphors have no meaning beyond their literal meaning -- they just make us notice things, like a nudge or a photograph -- versus the Lakoff-style conceptual metaphor people who think metaphor is deeply systematic, that we think argument IS war and time IS money, so metaphorical content is real and structured, not just vibes. My whole craft kind of presumes the Lakoff side is right, but Davidson's view would elegantly explain my beta reader situation (the sentence didn't mean her reading, it just caused her to notice it).

So: give me your actual credence that Davidson is basically right that metaphor belongs to use, not meaning -- that there is no distinct metaphorical meaning a sentence has. Don't just shrug and say both sides have points. I want a number, and I want the decomposition: how much rides on whether the supposed content of a metaphor is ever fully paraphrasable (and whether failure to paraphrase shows non-propositional meaning or just lazy readers), how much on whether conceptual metaphor theory's evidence about systematic mappings actually contradicts Davidson or is compatible with him, how much on whether Davidson can explain why some metaphors are just wrong or forced if they're not asserting anything, and whatever other sub-questions you think the credence really depends on. Then tell me which term in your decomposition you're least sure about, and what would move it. Bonus: whatever your number is, tell me what it implies for whose reading of my tenant line is correct -- mine, hers, or neither.
