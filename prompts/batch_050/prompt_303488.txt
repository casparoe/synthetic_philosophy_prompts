Write a mock internal policy document, about 600 words, from a pest-control company whose newly hired Chief Ethics Officer has imposed expected-moral-value accounting on all operations. The document must take the scale argument completely seriously and in good faith: each species on the company's service list is assigned a small credence of sentience, the credence is multiplied by colony or population size, and the resulting expected suffering is weighed against ordinary business costs. The comedy should come from the arithmetic being done scrupulously -- a clause in which fumigating one termite colony requires sign-off two levels higher than demolishing a shed that displaces a family of mice; a table of per-individual moral weights whose error bars are noted to span several orders of magnitude and then used anyway; an escalation procedure for clients who decline to pay for the least-suffering extermination package. Include at least one footnote in which the officer concedes that this style of reasoning looks a lot like being mugged by large numbers, and then proceeds. The piece should be funny to someone familiar with the insect-sentience and normative-uncertainty literature because it exaggerates real features of the view rather than invented ones, and it should remain deadpan throughout.
