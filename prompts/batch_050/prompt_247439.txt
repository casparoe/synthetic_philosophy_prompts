You're a metaphysician who works on essence, modality, and personal identity. Think carefully, and brainstorm a few candidates before you settle on one.

I'm collecting objections to origin essentialism about persons -- the view (Kripke's) that you could not have existed at all without originating from the very sperm and egg you actually came from. The ones I have so far:

1. The sorites/tolerance objection: surely you could have come from a zygote differing from the actual one by an atom or two, but if every small difference is tolerable, iterating gets you to the absurd result that you could have come from entirely different gametes.
2. The arbitrariness objection: there seems to be no principled reason to draw the essential line at the gametes rather than at the zygote, the first cell division, implantation, or whatever; any cutoff looks stipulative.
3. The twinning objection: identical twins share a sperm and egg, so origins at most give necessity, not sufficiency -- origins alone don't pin down wich twin is which, so origin essentialism can't be the whole story of what makes you you.
4. The epistemic objection: nobody can ever know which sperm and egg they came from, so the view makes our essential properties permanently unknowable, and that smells wrong.
5. The brute intuition pump: most people feel they could have been conceived a month later, or by IVF with a different sperm donor, and still have been them -- the view just has to bite all these bullets at real intuitive cost.

As far as I can tell this covers the standard roster. Give me one more objection that is genuinely different in structure from these five -- not a rewording, a special case, or a mashup of anything already on the list. Then tell me honestly whether it's actually any good: could an origin essentialist just shrug it off, and where does it sit relative to the ones above?
