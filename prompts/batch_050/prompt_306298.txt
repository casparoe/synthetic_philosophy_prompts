Question: Explain the Rietdijk--Putnam argument that special relativity supports a block-universe (eternalist) picture of time. Use the Andromeda scenario to illustrate. Then state one serious objection to the argument and assess whether it succeeds.

Student's answer:

Special relativity says there's no absolute simultaneity: whether two spacelike-separated events happen "at the same time" depends on your frame of reference. The Andromeda scenario makes this vivid. Two people standing on Earth, one walking toward the Andromeda galaxy and one walking away from it, will assign different times to distant events. Events that are simultaneous-with-now for the walker are days in the past (or future) of the still-stander's "now" slice, because even slow walking tilts your simultaneity plane enough that at Andromeda's distance the planes differ by days.

Rietdijk and Putnam argue: if what's real is observer-dependent in this way -- if whether the Andromedan fleet has launched yet is real for one observer and not-yet-real for another, standing right next to each other -- then we can't hold that only the present exists. The presentist's "now" would have to be relativized to each observer, which makes existence itself observer-relative, and that's absurd. So all events, past present and future, must be equally real: the block universe.

This also shows determinism is true. If events in my future are already real for some possible observer, then they're already fixed, so the future couldn't have been otherwise. Free will is an illusion generated by our position inside the block.

One objection: the argument conflates frames with observers. Relativity of simultaneity is a fact about coordinate systems, not about what any particular person can know or perceive. The walkers disagree about simultaneity, but nothing in special relativity forces us to say a simultaneity plane marks out what's real -- the "now" slice is a bookkeeping device, and both walkers agree about every event once it's in their past light cone. This objection fails, though, because it just reasserts presentism without answering the argument: if there's a fact about what's real and it isn't frame-dependent, the objector owes us an account of which frame's simultaneity plane is the real one, and special relativity forbids a privileged frame.

My assessment: the argument succeeds against presentism but not against the growing-block view, since the growing block doesn't require a privileged frame either -- the edge of becoming could be defined relative to each event's light-cone structure.
