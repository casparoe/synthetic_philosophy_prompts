Write the employee handbook — roughly 600 words, formatted with section headings — for the Scandinavian branch of an international firm whose corporate culture has been built, with total sincerity, on the Law of Jante. The handbook should be deadpan HR prose throughout: an onboarding welcome that gently warns new hires against being impressive, a performance-review policy explaining why the highest scorer is scheduled for a conversation about visibility, the discontinued Employee of the Month program, guidelines for deflecting compliments in a way that does not itself draw attention, rules for email signatures, and a section on the one permitted form of pride (collective pride in the company's lack of pride). Include a short appendix on handling transfers from the American office, whose habit of listing their accomplishments must be corrected without anyone ever saying that this is what is happening. The humor has to come from exaggerating features the tall poppy ethos actually has — modesty enforced as conformity, equality maintained by leveling down, the suspicion that standing out is a form of aggression — so that a reader sympathetic to egalitarian workplace norms recognizes a fair critique of their own instincts rather than a straw man, and so that the genuine goods being mocked (freedom from self-promotion, solidarity, the relief of not having to brand yourself) are visible underneath the absurdity. Never state the critique outright, and never let the handbook notice that it is satire.
