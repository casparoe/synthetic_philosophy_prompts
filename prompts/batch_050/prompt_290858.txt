So the Browning vs. Goldhagen fight over Police Battalion 101 is one of those debates I've watched people rehash on the internet for years, and it always feels like they talk past each other -- Browning with his ordinary men, conformity and situational pressure, Goldhagen with his willing executioners driven by eliminationist antisemitism. Somebody should just lock them in a room adversarial-collaboration style. Since that's not happening, draft the joint statement they'd produce if they did.

Concrete things I want in it:

1. A section of claims both could actually sign. I suspect this includes stuff like: the battalion's men were mostly middle-aged reservists, not specially selected fanatics; Major Trapp offered the older men a way out of the first massacre and most didn't take it; both sides rely heavily on the same postwar judicial interrogations as their main source base. But check whether these really are agreed, and flag any that only look agreed.

2. A disagreements section where each position is stated in wording that side would actually accept for itself -- no strawmen, no "Goldhagen thinks every German was a bloodthirsty monster" nonsense. I want the disagreement made as precise as possible: is it about the actual distribution of motives in the battalion, or about what counts as an *explanation* of their behavior at all? A thing I've long suspected is that part of the fight is really about whether "they could have refused and few did" supports Goldhagen or Browning -- same fact, opposite readings -- so say if you think some of the dispute is like that, i.e. about interpretation rather than facts.

3. A cruxes section: the sub-questions where answering one way would actually move one side. For each crux, give some evidence or case both would agree bears on it (e.g. participation rates in the optional killings vs. the non-optional guard duty, how the men behaved when transfer out was possible, comparisons with units that behaved differently), and write down what each camp would predict it shows.

Also: state how confident you are in the attributions -- Browning and Goldhagen wrote a lot after their famous books and shifted around some, so if you're unsure whether a claim belongs to someone's 1990s position vs. their later work, say so -- and name the single point in the document you're least sure of. Hard cap of 800 words for the whole thing.
