I recently spent some time with the MU-puzzle from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach: you are given a starting string and a small set of rules for rewriting it, and the challenge is to reach a certain target string. What struck me is that the resolution does not come from applying the rules more cleverly or searching longer. It comes from stepping back and noticing a feature of the strings that none of the permitted rules can alter, after which the target is obviously unattainable and no amount of further rule-pushing could change that.

Since then I keep noticing the same shape of solution in other places. A checkerboard with two diagonally opposite corners removed cannot be tiled by dominoes, and the way you show this is not by attempting tilings but by pointing to something every legal domino placement preserves, which the mutilated board lacks. In certain disagreements, too, you can settle the matter not by finding a better move inside the other party's framework but by showing that every move their framework permits leaves some feature of the situation fixed, and that what they want is on the wrong side of that fixed feature.

My question is whether this pattern — solving a question about what a system can reach by identifying what the system's permitted moves cannot change — has an established name. I know Hofstadter discusses jumping out of the system as a general move, and I know mathematicians have a term for properties that an operation preserves, but I am not sure either label is quite right. The first suggests abandoning the rules rather than reasoning about them from outside, which is not what happens in either puzzle; the second names the preserved feature rather than the strategy of solving-by-preserved-feature. If an established name exists, please explain how the thing I am describing differs from its nearest neighbors: from ordinary trial-and-error search, from proof by contradiction, from genuinely breaking or rewriting the rules of the system, and from merely shifting to a different problem. I would also like the edges drawn: does it still count when the preserved feature is stumbled upon rather than derived, when it is only approximately preserved, or when the argument shows reachability rather than unreachability? If nothing established fits, I would rather you say so plainly and propose a term with a definition and two or three boundary cases than force my pattern under the closest existing label. Whatever you conclude, state how confident you are in each part of your answer and single out the claim you are least sure of.
