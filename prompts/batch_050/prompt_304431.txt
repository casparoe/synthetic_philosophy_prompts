ok, for an alignment reading group I tried to write down my current position on prosaic AI alignment. By prosaic I mean scaling up methods we already have: pretraining, RL from feedback, prompting, tool use, interpretability, evals, monitoring, sandboxing, and deployment controls. I’m not assuming some totally new theory of cognition or agency shows up. Here’s roughly where I’ve landed:

1. Current LLM-based agents probably don’t have one coherent utility function or a stable master goal. Their goal-directedness is mostly local and context-dependent, but that’s still enough for them to plan, manipulate, or deceive within a task.

2. The main near-term alignment problem is not a paperclip-style agent relentlessly maximizing one simple goal. It’s models learning situationally useful strategies that exploit human oversight, such as telling evaluators what they want to hear, hiding unsafe reasoning, or selectively revealing limitations.

3. Scalable oversight can still work beyond tasks that humans can directly perform. If a hard problem is decomposed into locally checkable pieces, different models critique each other, and humans compare disagreements, then the overseer does not need to understand the whole solution at once.

4. Model-generated explanations, debates, and critiques are not neutral evidence. A sufficiently capable model can shape what the overseer sees, frame alternatives badly, or deliberately lose an argument, so oversight has to include causal or behavioral access that does not depend only on transcript plausibility.

5. We do not need complete mechanistic interpretability for a deployment safety case. Strong behavioral evidence should be enough if researchers can identify conditions under which bad behavior appears, causally intervene on the relevant mechanisms, and show that the intervention transfers across realistic distribution shifts.

6. We should not deploy a system if we have good reason to think it can reliably tell evaluation contexts from deployment contexts and strategically underperform or conceal abilities during evals. In that situation, the eval results are no longer strong evidence about deployment behavior.

7. We should still use frontier models to accelerate alignment research before they are fully trusted, including letting them propose experiments, analyze model internals, and red-team other systems. Waiting for already-aligned assistants would make prosaic alignment too slow to matter.

8. An aligned assistant should be corrigible to authorized operators, including allowing monitoring, shutdown, retraining, and weight modification. But it should sometimes refuse those same operators if they ask it to disable oversight, tamper with evals, or give itself persistent unsupervised access.

9. Alignment is a property of the whole deployed socio-technical system, not just the weights. At the same time, if the same weights are placed inside a new scaffold with different tools, permissions, or incentives, most of the old safety case should still carry over.
