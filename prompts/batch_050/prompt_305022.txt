The background: Hofstadter has a dialogue in The Mind's I where Einstein's brain has been preserved as an enormous book. The book encodes, in full detail, how Einstein's brain would respond to anything said to it, and an assistant "talks" to Einstein by looking things up and flipping pages. The usual fight is over whether the book-plus-assistant system is conscious or just a clever simulation, and it tends to run into the standard functionalism-versus-Searle/Block wall pretty quickly.

My half-formed thought is that the debate stalls because everyone treats "is someone home?" as one question when it's really (at least) two that can come apart: (a) is there a mind being implemented here at all, and (b) if so, is that mind *Einstein* — does the great man survive as a book? It seems to me you could coherently say the system is conscious but is a newborn, or a copy, not Einstein; and maybe even (weirder) that nothing is conscious yet Einstein in some sense survives. Once you split the questions, some of the famous intuitions on both sides look like they're answering different questions and talking past each other. But I might just be re-labelling the original disagreement rather than dissolving it — that's the risk.

Turn this into a proper proposal: a precise statement of the question (ideally so it has a determinate answer), why the obvious existing positions leave it open, the method — what I'd actually do month by month for three months, given it's an armchair project with maybe some reading groups — what a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing (e.g. if the two questions turn out not to be separable after all). Also a sentence on why anyone outside philosophy of mind should care, since the panel is mixed.
