Whenever I book a flight these days there's a little checkbox at checkout: offset your emissions for like $11.40, plant some trees, fund a cookstove project somewhere. Same with "carbon neutral" shipping on parcels, net-zero beef, all that stuff. I've started wondering what picture of individual climate responsibility is baked into the whole setup.

Like, what does the practice presuppose the moral problem even is? It seems to treat my emissions as a kind of debt denominated in tonnes, which I can settle by paying someone else to not-emit in a totally different place, as if harm were fully fungible across people, projects, and decades. On that picture the duty isn't "don't emit," it's "make sure the ledger nets out" -- so someone who flies every month but offsets diligently is doing fine, morally, while someone who flies once and doesn't offset is doing worse.

But there's a rival picture where that's backwards: on a fair-share or do-no-harm view, I have a duty not to blow past my slice of a finite carbon budget, and a stranger in Kenya planting trees doesn't discharge my duty any more than a stranger donating blood discharges mine. On that view offsets are at best a nice donation and at worst a licence to keep doing the harm.

So here's what I want: reconstruct the implicit theory of the offset practice. Which theories of responsibility does it only make sense under, and which does it quietly rule out? Is it really committed to full fungibility of harm, or could you defend offsets on a don't-emit view too (the atmosphere doesn't care where reductions come from, so why should we)? Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined -- could a consequentialist and a deontologist both sign up for it but then disagree about something concrete, like whether offsets need to be permanent, or "additional," or whether avoiding emissions elsewhere counts the same as removing carbon? And what would the practice actually have to look like if it were rebuilt on the rival view -- personal carbon budgets, rationing, just fewer flights? I genuninely can't tell whether the offset checkbox is a coherent institution with a defensible theory behind it or a workaround that only feels fine because nobody's stated the theory out loud.
