I've been chewing on Hofstadter's careenium, and I want a thought experiment built to order. For anyone rusty on the image: the careenium is a two-level device. At the lower level there are simmballs, small mechanical components whose motion is entirely fixed by local substrate physics -- collisions, forces, nothing else. At the upper level there are simms, symbols that interact according to symbol-level rules. The device is rigged so that when the simms settle into certain configurations, simmballs get pushed around, and the natural thing to say is that the symbols did the pushing. The puzzle is whether that saying is honest downward causation or merely a higher-level gloss on what the substrate was going to do all along. This matters to me for a distinctly LessWrong-ish reason: people constantly say things like the optimizer caused this weight update or the agent's belief caused its output, and I want to know whether such talk is causally literal or just convenient narration over weight-space dynamics.

Construct a thought experiment to these specifications:

1. It features two systems, A and B, which produce identical observable behavior across every actual circumstance but differ in whether the symbol-level description has genuine causal traction. You may choose the lever (an algorithm-implementing system versus a giant lookup table is the obvious candidate, but you may use another if it isolates the variable more cleanly).
2. The difference between A and B must be revealed by interventions and counterfactuals -- what breaks, adapts, or persists when you poke each system -- not stipulated by the narrator. If I can reread the story with the verdicts swapped and nothing else changes, the case fails.
3. Physical closure holds throughout: the substrate laws are complete and local, and nothing in either system violates or supplements them. Whatever causal efficacy the symbol level has must survive this constraint rather than bypass it.
4. No appeal to consciousness, qualia, or what it's like to be the system. The verdict should turn on causal structure alone, so the case stays usable for people who think minds are beside the point here.

After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each specification. In particular: (a) walk through what an interventionist account of causation says about A and B, and why; (b) walk through what the causal exclusion argument says -- if the substrate fully determines every outcome, either the symbol level is identical to some substrate-level cause or it is causally idle -- and which horn your case impales; (c) identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and describe what happens to the verdicts if that feature is removed; (d) state, in the strongest form you can, how a defender of the side that comes out badly would attack the case, and whether the attack succeeds.

Keep the scenario itself under two paragraphs and free of science-fiction machinery beyond the careenium setup itself. I want to be able to deploy this the next time someone on the forum claims that a high-level description of a neural net is just a story we tell about the weights.
