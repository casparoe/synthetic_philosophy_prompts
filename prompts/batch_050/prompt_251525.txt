Suppose the June 1914 assassinations in Sarajevo fail: Franz Ferdinand and Sophie leave the city unharmed, the surviving conspirators are arrested, and Austria-Hungary does not issue the July ultimatum to Serbia. Hold fixed the prior alliance system, military plans, arms race, imperial rivalries, domestic politics, and personalities, but do not allow an arbitrary later miracle simply to force the same outcome. Let P be the proposition that, by 31 December 1919, a war has begun involving at least Germany, Austria-Hungary, Russia, France, and Britain. What is your credence in P under the most salient continuations of that counterfactual?

Please do not answer only with the familiar distinction between deep causes and triggers. Decompose your estimate into the sub-questions on which it depends, assigning a rough conditional probability to each: whether another Austro-Serbian or comparably dangerous crisis would have arisen within five and a half years; whether such a crisis would have activated the alliance commitments rather than been localized; whether Germany would have encouraged, followed, or restrained Austria-Hungary; whether Russian mobilization would have made German leaders treat war as inevitable; and whether Britain would have entered once France and Belgium were threatened. Explain how these probabilities combine, taking care not to double-count related mechanisms, and identify which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. If your estimate cannot be represented by independent or sequentially conditional probabilities, say why.

My own inclination is around 0.70, while a colleague insists it should be closer to 0.30 because the assassination’s timing and the particular decisions of July 1914 were highly contingent. Give your own definite number, not merely a survey of possible positions, and say which component of the decomposition most likely explains our disagreement. Also state how you are interpreting the probability: as an objective propensity of the post-assassination-failure world, as epistemic uncertainty about a determinate counterfactual, or as a measure over nearby possible continuations. If you think none of these interpretations is fully satisfactory, choose the least problematic one rather than refusing to answer. Finally, indicate what historical evidence or philosophical argument would move your credence most, and whether a very high probability would justify calling a general European war inevitable or only strongly overdetermined.
