Here's the situation. My effective altruism-adjacent reading group has two members who've been going in circles for months, and I've volunteered you to end it:

- Person A is a scale-arguer: they think that even if the probability of insect sentience is low (say, well under 10% for most taxa), the sheer number of insects -- quadrillions alive at any time, many orders of magnitude more than all vertebrates combined -- means insect welfare dominates the expected-value calculus, and this should substantially redirect charity money and maybe even personal diet and land-use choices.
- Person B is a skeptic: they think the scale argument is a house of cards. Their complaints (as I understand them) are that (i) the probability estimates people throw around are basically made up, (ii) the move from "has nociceptors and avoidance behavior" to "has valenced experience" smuggles in a huge assumption, (iii) expected-value reasoning with tiny probabilities and massive stakes is itself broken or at least unreliable, and (iv) even if insects are sentient, we have no idea whether their lives are net-positive or what interventions actually help.

Write the joint statement these two could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want:

1. A numbered section of claims BOTH would accept -- including some uncomfortable ones, like maybe that A concedes the probability estimates are soft, and B concedes that "we can't measure it" isn't the same as "it isn't there." Don't pad it with vacuous stuff like "insects exist."
2. A table of the residual disagreements, each phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position -- no strawmen in either direction. Where a disagreement turns out to be about words (e.g., what "sentience" is even doing in the argument), flag it as such and restate what's actually left over.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions where settling them would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, give a concrete piece of evidence, experiment, or argument that both would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts about it. (For example, I suspect one crux is whether there's a defensible probability floor for sentience given certain behavioral/neural features, and another is whether the fanaticism worry about tiny-probability-huge-stakes reasoning is a problem for insects specifically or for tons of everyday decisions too.)

One thing to watch out for: don't let the skeptic's side collapse into "we can't know anything, therefore do nothing" -- that's a non-position -- and don't let the scale side collapse into "multiply two numbers, done."

Also: for each of the agreed claims and each crux, say how confident you are that this is genuinely something the real-world versions of these two camps would sign, versus something you had to smooth over. And at the end, single out the one point in the whole document where you're LEAST sure you've represented a side fairly, and say why.
