I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for well over a decade, and over the years I have absorbed Dennett's vocabulary of cranes and skyhooks almost natively: cranes being the mindless, local, incremental processes (natural selection paradigmatically, but also learning, trial and error, cultural transmission) that build up complex design without anything reaching in from outside, and skyhooks being the posited exceptions -- minds, creators, inexplicable leaps -- that allegedly do the work all at once without having been built themselves. I also take seriously his companion notion of greedy reductionism: the error of trying to skip the intermediate layers of cranes and explain some high-level phenomenon directly in terms of the lowest level available. Here is my situation, though: by profession I am a machine learning researcher, and the vocabulary in which I actually think is optimization. When I try to cash out Dennett's framework in my own terms, I get a rough mapping -- cranes look like local search and gradient descent, skyhooks look like a designer hard-coding the solution or a planner with a perfect model acting from outside the search -- but I can feel the seams, and I want the translation done carefully rather than vibes-based.

So here is the request. Restate the cranes-versus-skyhooks framework, and the associated critique of greedy reductionism, in the vocabulary of optimization and learning: hill-climbing and local update rules versus global search and planning, model-free versus model-based methods, reward shaping, loss landscapes and local optima, myopia, amortization, and the distinction between a learning process and the artifact it produces. Take Dennett's core commitments one at a time -- that complex design arises from mindless local processes rather than miracles, that positing an unbuilt builder is question-begging, that skipping explanatory levels is a characteristic intellectual sin, and that culture and ideas are themselves crane-built rather than delivered from on high -- and for each, say what translates cleanly into optimization terms, what only translates with distortion (name the distortion), and what has no expression in that vocabulary at all. Be honest about the direction of fit: do not quietly import Dennett's assumptions into the translation and then announce that everything mapped.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about. If something in Dennett's framework resists expression in optimization vocabulary, is that because my vocabulary is missing a concept it should have, or because that part of the framework was doing less work than it appeared to be? And take a position on the hard case that motivates all this for me: a trained system, like a large model or a model-based RL agent, is produced by a paradigmatically crane-like process, but its internal computations can look skyhook-like -- it plans, it reasons from a model, it appears to reach answers without locally searching for them. Call this an amortized skyhook. Does the crane/skyhook distinction survive the existence of such systems, does it need revision, or does it turn out to have been a distinction about provenance rather than about mechanism all along -- and if the last, is that a vindication or a deflation of Dennett's original point?
