The background: marxists apparently say the economic "base" (how stuff gets produced, who owns what) explains the "superstructure" (law, religion, politics, culture). And everyone always objects: but look, two societies can have the same economy and totally different religions, so the base can't explain everything. I think I found a way the marxist can answer this, but I also suspect my answer makes the whole theory empty. Here's the essay:

---

Does the economy explain everything else?

The usual objection to historical materialism is that economies and cultures don't match up one-to-one. Same plows, same markets, different gods. So the base must not determine the superstructure.

I think this objection assumes the wrong picture of explanation. Think of a thermostat. The thermostat does not push the temperature up every second. Most of the time the temperature drifts around on its own for all sorts of local reasons (someone opened a window, the oven is on). The thermostat only acts when the drift gets too far. It sets the band, not the value.

Maybe the economic base works like that. Religion and law drift around for their own reasons, which is why similar economies have different cultures. But the base sets a band of tolerance. If a law or a religion drifts too far out of line with how production actually works, it gets reformed, ignored, or the society falls apart and gets replaced. Feudal law couldn't survive capitalism not because capitalism wrote new laws directly but because feudal law kept losing every fight it picked.

Second idea: lag. Even when the superstructure does track the base, it tracks it slowly, like starlight. The light you see shows the star as it was years ago. So at any moment, a society's laws and morals might reflect the economy of fifty years ago, not today's. Which means the mismatch people point to might just be the lag.

So: the base doesn't dictate the superstructure, it selects it, and with a delay. The objection about mismatched cultures doesn't touch this version.

But here's what worries me. With a wide enough band and a long enough lag, doesn't the theory become impossible to prove wrong? Any match counts as evidence for it, and any mismatch gets explained away as drift or lag. A thermostat you can never check against the actual temperature isn't controlling anything, you just believe it is. Have I "saved" historical materialism by emptying it out?
