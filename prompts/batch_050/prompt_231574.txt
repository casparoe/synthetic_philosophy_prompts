So I keep seeing people on the blogs (and in old LW comment threads) say that moral statements are just cheering and booing, applause lights, expressions of approval rather than claims about anything. Fine. But I finally sat down with the Frege-Geach problem and it seems like a genuine wrecking ball for that view, and I want the spec nailed down before I read anyone's proposed fix, because every expressivist semantics I've skimmed seems to be answering a slightly different question. The setup as I understand it: take "if stealing is wrong, then getting your little brother to steal is wrong too." Inside the conditional nobody cheers or boos anything, the clause isn't even asserted, yet the inference to "getting your little brother to steal is wrong" once you add "stealing is wrong" looks valid in the boring old formal sense, and the words have to mean the same thing in both spots on pain of equivocation. So write me the desiderata: what does any account of moral language have to deliver to count as actually solving Frege-Geach rather than renaming it? Which embeddings must it handle and why those in particular (negation, conditionals, attitude reports like "Alice believes stealing is wrong", mixed moral-descriptive inferences, quantified cases)? What exactly does the moral modus ponens requirement demand -- is explaining the *pull* of the inference pragmatically enough, or does validity have to come out as the same species as ordinary truth-preserving validity? Which requirements are genuine and which are just cognitivist camp preferences dressed up as tests? And the part I care most about: is some subset jointly unsatisfiable? My hunch is you can't simultaneously hold that moral utterances express attitudes rather than statable contents, that the very same thing occurs embedded and unembedded, and that the inferences are valid in the standard sense -- but maybe I'm wrong and that's the thing I'd most like checked. Finally, which single requirement knocks out the most proposals actually on the market? One constraint on form: continuous prose only, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, write it like an email from a friend who happens to know this literature. The list can be long, I just want it flowing.
