Read the novella against the Epicurean argument and its two main rivals: the deprivation account (death is bad because of the good life it takes away) and Lucretius's symmetry argument (if we don't mourn the nonexistence before our birth, why mourn the nonexistence after our death?). Point to specific scenes -- the fall while hanging curtains, the struggle against the black bag, the three days of screaming -- where the novella presses hardest on the philosophical weak points of each position, and be honest about where the story resists being conscripted as evidence for any side.
