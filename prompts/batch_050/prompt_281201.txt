Consider the following sequence of cases. Suppose that in 2045, neuroscientists record the complete causal-functional organization of one particular person's brain during a severe migraine: every internal state and its role in the network of causes and effects, captured at whatever grain of description you take to be the relevant one. Engineers then construct three systems:

1. Embodied copy. A robot whose control architecture duplicates that functional organization in silicon, connected to sensors and effectors so that it navigates the world, avoids damage, attends to its injuries, and reports pain in roughly the ways the person did.

2. Sealed copy. The same functional organization run inside an isolated computer. Its sensory inputs are replayed from recordings of the original episode, and its outputs are written to a drive no one reads. Nothing in the world depends on what the system does.

3. Lazy copy. A system that reproduces the identical input-output profile as the sealed copy, but does so by consulting a vast precomputed table of responses rather than by re-instantiating the intermediate causal transitions between internal states.

For each of the three systems, give your actual credence — a number, not "it depends" — that there is something it is like to be that system; that is, that it genuinely experiences pain rather than merely behaving or computing as if it did. Then decompose each number: how much of it rides on (a) whether some form of functionalism about phenomenal consciousness is correct at all; (b) whether functional organization at the recorded grain is the consciousness-relevant property, as opposed to finer biological, chemical, or physical detail that the copies lack; (c) whether instantiation of the organization requires the actual causal transitions to occur, or whether a dispositionally equivalent structure like the lookup table suffices; and (d) whether ongoing causal coupling to an environment matters, as the contrast between the embodied and sealed copies suggests. Show roughly how your sub-credences on (a) through (d) combine to produce each of the three headline numbers; note where the combination is multiplicative and where one sub-question screens off another; identify which sub-question each headline number is most sensitive to; and say what argument, thought experiment, or conceivable empirical result would move each number the most, and in which direction.
