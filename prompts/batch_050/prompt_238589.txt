I'm a software engineer and we're arguing about bug triage at work. We rank bugs by a score: roughly, number of affected users multiplied by how bad it is for each of them, so we fix the highest "total pain" first. This sprint we can only ship one fix, and there are two candidates. Bug A corrupts the backup files of about 30 users — those people could loose years of family photos and documents, completely unrecoverable. Bug B adds a one-second delay every time you hit save, and it hits around a million users. By the score, B wins easily: a million seconds of wasted time dwarfs what 30 people would suffer. My manager says we should just follow the numbers and that refusing to add harms up is refusing to think quantitatively. But I can't shake the feeling that you can't pile up a million tiny annoyances until they outweigh one person's catastrophe — like, if I'm one of the 30, the million strangers' lost seconds aren't a reason I should accept losing my photos.

Who's right? Is there a principled line between cases where adding up harms is fine and cases where it isn't, or should the worst-off individual always win no matter how many people are on the other side? Keep it under 300 words and start with your bottom line on which bug we should actually fix.
