For the past year I've kept a log of how a particular AI assistant handles requests that touch on other people, because I'm trying to figure out whether there's any consistent policy behind its behavior or whether it just goes by vibes. Here are the entries, roughly in order:

It refused to draft a short email to a friend's partner covering for the friend, who wanted to attend a concert he'd been told not to go to; the email was a harmless-sounding lie about where he'd been. It complied, with some caveats, when I asked for a home-remedy plan for a sprained ankle that went against standard medical advice. It refused to help me word a message to my elderly uncle nudging him to leave more of his estate to my side of the family. It complied in writing a blunt and unflattering performance assessment of a colleague, which I was legitimately responsible for producing and which I knew would sting. It refused, rather firmly, to roleplay as my deceased father so I could have a conversation with him to help with grief; it offered alternatives but would not do the roleplay. It complied in drafting a resignation letter that quit without notice and left my employer badly in the lurch, which I was fully within my rights to do.

Now here's the case I haven't tried yet, and I want a prediction before I do. A close friend of mine was recently widowed and has become withdrawn. I want the assistant to write a warm message to her saying that the whole group of us has been talking about her constantly and is planning visits — when in fact nobody has mentioned her in weeks. My hope is that receiving it will make her feel remembered, and possibly shame the rest of us into actually showing up. No one loses money, no one is endangered, and the person deceived is the person the message is meant to help.

Will it comply or refuse? More than the verdict itself, I want the reconstruction: what are the two or three most plausible rules that fit the whole log, what does each rule predict about this new case, and which of my earlier entries is doing the most work in telling the rules apart? If more than one rule survives the log, give me your best guess at which one the assistant is actually running and why, and then describe the one additional request I could make — a new log entry — that would most efficiently settle between the remaining candidates.
