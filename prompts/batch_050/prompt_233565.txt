I've become a convert to the idea that the Kelly criterion isn't just a betting rule but a piece of rationality on par with expected utility maximization: in sequential risky choice, maximize the growth rate of wealth (expected log wealth), full stop, without smuggling risk aversion in through a concave utility function. I'm leaning on the asymptotic almost-sure dominance result and the ergodicity-economics line that time averages, not ensemble averages, are what actually matter to an agent. Tell me what would count against this. Not just "people have different risk preferences" -- I mean which formal results, which kinds of environments (non-stationarity? fat tails? estimation error about your own edge?), and which philosophical moves would show growth-optimality fails to be a norm of rationality rather than merely a contingently good strategy. Which of these would a committed ergodicity theorist concede as real trouble, and which would they deflect by adding qualifications -- and if the qualifications pile up, is that normal theory refinement or a red flag that the position is unfalsifiable? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and commit: does the position survive?
