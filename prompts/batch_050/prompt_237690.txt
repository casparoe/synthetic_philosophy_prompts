At a seminar on existential risk, a colleague defended the doomsday argument by repeating the slogan “reason as if you are typical.” She took this to mean that, absent evidence distinguishing us from other observers, we should expect our birth rank to be unremarkable and therefore should not assign much credence to futures containing vastly more humans than have existed so far. What precise theses could the slogan express? Distinguish at least claims about random sampling, self-locating uncertainty, reference classes, evidential relevance, and the temporal position of an observer. For each thesis, state what would count as being “typical,” whether the thesis is trivial, substantive, or implausible, and whether it actually supports the inference from our early position to a relatively short human future. In particular, identify which readings merely assume a disputed reference class or sampling rule rather than independently justifying the doomsday conclusion.
