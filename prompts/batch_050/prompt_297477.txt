I want to know what theory of moral knowledge my gut reactions commit me to. Case 1: a diagnosed psychopath who can state the moral rules perfectly, predict exactly how others will judge his actions, even argue ethics convincingly -- but feels nothing about any of it and only uses the rules to avoid getting caught. I say he doesn't really know hurting people is wrong, he just knows it's called wrong. Case 2: a man with severe intellectual disability who can't articulate any rules and fails every verbal test, but gets genuinely distressed when someone is hurt and shares his food without being asked. I say he does know some things are wrong. Case 3: a patient with frontotemporal dementia who used to be gentle and now shoplifts and is cruel to his wife -- I want to say he lost knowledge he had, not that he never had it. Case 4: a normal guy who knows cheating on his taxes is wrong and does it anyway with a clear conscience. Here I want to say he knows it's wrong but just doesn't care -- so apparently I think caring isn't required for knowledge, which seems to wreck my answer to case 1. What principle about what it takes to know something is wrong captures most of these? I'd rather you tell me which verdict I should abandon than give me some gerrymandered theory that saves all four. Also tell me what your principle says about a psychopath raised in a community whose norms he genuinely endorses and follows -- is his moral knowledge intact or not?
