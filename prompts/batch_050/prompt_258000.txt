Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed futarchist -- someone who holds that policy should be chosen by betting on which policies will maximize a retrospectively measurable national welfare metric -- and a critic who holds that democracy's legitimacy and information aggregation cannot be outsourced to speculative markets in this way. Both have read each other's best arguments and are drafting in good faith. Include: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, covering what prediction markets in fact do well, what the empirical record on market thinness and manipulation attempts shows, and what values a political system must serve beyond accuracy; (2) a table of their residual disagreements, each row phrased so that the side it attributes a view to would accept the wording -- I expect at least the following: whether the welfare metric smuggles the value-laden choice of ends back in at a less accountable point, whether market prices reflect information or the wealth-weighted opinions of traders, and whether a failed futarchy could revert to ordinary democracy or would corrode it; (3) for each disagreement, the crux -- the sub-question whose resolution would move at least one party -- and a test, case, or observation both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal, identify which part and explain why.
