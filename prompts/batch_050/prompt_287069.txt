Interpretability researchers routinely say things like: this direction in activation space is the model's representation of the Golden Gate Bridge; this circuit implements indirect-object identification; this probe shows the model represents whether a statement is true. Skeptics reply that a probe decoding a feature does not show the model uses it, that a direction you can steer along may be a crank rather than a concept, and that the "representations" we find are often artifacts of the decoding method we chose. I am tired of reading this debate verdict by verdict. Before I read any more of it, I want the standard of evaluation itself: what must an account of when an internal pattern in a trained network counts as a genuine representation — as opposed to a decoding artifact, a merely convenient description, or a feature of the analyst rather than the model — actually deliver?

Write me that list of desiderata. For each one, give the case or consideration that motivates it: the probe that decodes a feature the model never seems to use; the steering direction that changes behavior in exactly the predicted way; the two incompatible but equally successful decodings of the same activations; the representation that appears under one interpretability method and vanishes under another; the feature that is spread across the whole network rather than localized anywhere. For each desideratum, say whether you take it to be genuinely non-negotiable or a prejudice of one camp that a good theory may legitimately abandon, and defend the classification. Identify any pair of desiderata you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which you would sacrifice and why. Then, briefly, score three stances against your list: the realist view that directions and circuits passing causal-intervention tests are bona fide representations; the methodological skepticism on which interpretability findings are artifacts of the probing setup; and a pluralism on which several incompatible representational schemes can all be correct.

Be committed throughout: where the desiderata conflict, pick a side and give your reasons rather than presenting the menu and leaving the choice to me. Where the existing stances all fail some desideratum you consider non-negotiable, say so plainly.

Deliver the desiderata as a table with these columns: Requirement (one-sentence statement of the desideratum); Motivating case (the concrete situation that forces it); Status (Non-negotiable or Negotiable, with a one-sentence defense); Greatest pressure on (which of the three stances this requirement most threatens, and how). Follow the table with a short prose section on the incompatible pairs and your verdicts, and close with a JSON object named "scoring" whose keys are the three stances, each mapping to an object with fields "desiderata_met" (list of requirement numbers), "desiderata_failed" (list of requirement numbers), and "one_line_verdict" (a single committed sentence).
