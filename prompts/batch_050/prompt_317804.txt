Construct a thought experiment in which Robert Brandom's inferentialism and a standard truth-conditional semantics deliver different verdicts about whether two speakers mean the same thing by a word. The direction I want is the one inferentialists are usually pressed on: the two speakers refer to exactly the same things with the word, and every utterance they actually produce with it gets the same truth-value, yet their dispositions to draw inferences from and to claims involving the word differ enough that an inferentialist must say their words express different concepts, while a truth-conditional theorist says same meaning, different beliefs.

Constraints: no Twin Earth, no translation puzzles, no stipulating that the speakers "mean" different things — the divergence has to be generated by the scenario, not asserted. Use a real, realistic setting (a profession, a craft, a hobby community) and a word such a community would actually use. The difference in inferential dispositions must be systematic and principled, not a random slip or a case of one speaker simply being confused about an empirical fact — or if it does rest on an empirical disagreement, say so and own the consequence. Keep the scenario itself under 200 words.

After the scenario, do the demonstration: state the verdict each theory gives and trace exactly which feature of the case forces it. Then three further things. First, identify the single load-bearing feature of the design, and say what happens to the divergence if you remove it. Second, give the strongest counterattack available to the truth-conditional theorist — presumably some version of the charge that the case confuses difference in meaning with difference in belief, or that individuating concepts by inferential role fragments meaning so finely that no two speakers ever share a concept, which would be a reductio rather than a datum. Say whether your case survives that attack or only postpones it. Third, tell me what the case is actually good for: I want to use it in a graduate seminar handout on whether inferential role semantics can individuate conceptual content without collapsing into holism, so flag any way the scenario quietly presupposes a position on that question rather than leaving it open.

Finally: if the specification as I've given it is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, if any case meeting the constraints can be redescribed by the truth-conditional theorist so as to dissolve the disagreement, or if "systematic and principled but not mere empirical disagreement" turns out to be an incoherent demand — say that plainly at the start instead of producing a case that papers over the problem.
