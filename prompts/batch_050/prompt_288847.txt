My reading is basically this: when Machiavelli says fortune governs about half of what we do and leaves the other half to us, that's not meant as a careful quantitative estimate at all. I think he's being strategic — he understates what virtù can accomplish because his actual point is to shame passive, wait-and-see rulers, and the flood-river metaphor plus the stuff about adapting one's character to the times suggests that really fortune only dominates when there's no preparedness or flexibility, i.e. almost all of the apparent power of fortune is just the visible face of absent or mismatched virtù. On my reading the 50/50 line is almost ironic, or at least heavily deflated by everything around it.
