i'm making a handout for the pragmatics unit of my phil of language course and i want to open it with a poem. write me a poem of four quatrains that is itself a letter of reference for a philosophy grad student on the job market, in the spirit of grice's famous example where the letter damns the candidate by only mentioning things like his lovely handwriting and punctual attendance. heres the constraint: each quatrain should sink the candidate by exploiting a different one of grice's four maxims -- one stanza for quantity, one for quality, one for relation, one for manner -- while never saying anything literally false or literally negative about him. the poem shouldn't name or label the maxims at all; someone who knows their grice should be able to read it and correctly tag which stanza exploits which maxim, and ideally explain why to themselves. the quality one is the tricky one since irony is hard to pull off in a reference letter, so that stanza is where the poem will live or die. rhyme or dont, but it has to read like something an actual professor might plausibly write and sign, all surface politeness while the candidate's prospects quietly bleed out on the page.
