I’m in high school and our philosophy club got into an argument about a view called tranquilism, roughly the idea that what matters is not creating pleasure, excitement, achievements, or satisfied desires, but removing craving, distress, frustration, pain, and other forms of suffering. A perfectly calm person is not missing anything just because they do not feel ecstatic, and a possible person who would be happy is not “lost” merely because they are never created. I don’t want another argument about whether this sounds depressing. I want a spec sheet first: if someone wanted to turn tranquilism into an actual ethical theory for comparing lives, choices, and population policies, what requirements would it have to meet?

Please give the desiderata in a list or table, with the case or consideration motivating each one. For example, it needs to say something about the difference between satisfying a craving and never having it, being calm and being unconscious, preventing suffering and merely distracting someone from it, and relieving existing misery versus creating extra bliss. It also has to handle awkward cases like one hour of torture versus millions of barely noticeable annoyances, a painless death versus continued peaceful life, a person who adapts so well to oppression that they stop consciously minding it, and choosing between making troubled people calm or creating new people who would already be calm. Say which requirements seem non-negotiable for any plausible version, which are just preferred by particular camps, and which pairs might be impossible to satisfy together without weird results.

At the end, briefly test four contenders against the spec: classical hedonism, preference utilitarianism, strict negative utilitarianism, and the strongest version of tranquilism you can construct. Please keep the scoring short; the main thing I care about is the requirements list itself. Also, don’t quietly build in the conclusion by defining tranquility as whatever is good. If the theory needs some unit of badness or some rule for trading off intensity, duration, number of people, risk, and responsibility, make that a seperate explicit requirement.
