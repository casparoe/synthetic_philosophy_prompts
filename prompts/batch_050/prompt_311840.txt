For our school philosophy club zine, write a satirical advice column about the Dennett vs. Caruso-ish debate over just deserts and punishment. An invented student writes in because their friend copied their chemistry assignment, lied about it, and now the school is deciding between suspension, a restorative meeting, and making them redo the unit under supervision.

Have two rival columnists answer the letter: one takes a Dennett-ish compatibilist line that responsibility can be real enough when someone is morally competent and responsive to reasons, while the other takes a Caruso-ish free-will-skeptic line that nobody has basic desert and consequences need forward-looking reasons like prevention, repair, and rehabilitation. Make each one funny by exaggerating an actual awkward feature of their view, but definately don’t turn either into a total strawman. Both should give a concrete recommendation, and each should get at least one good hit in against the other.

About 650–750 words, deadpan, and understandable to students who haven’t read either philosopher. After the column, add a separate short note saying how confident you are that the two portrayals are broadly fair, and identify the one joke or claim you’re least sure is philosophically accurate.
