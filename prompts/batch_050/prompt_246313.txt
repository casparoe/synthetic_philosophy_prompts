A colleague who works on environmental limits has a favourite one-liner. Whenever someone points out that forecasts of population-driven scarcity have failed for two centuries, she replies that Malthus wasn't wrong, just early. I heard it again last week at a workshop, where she used it to shut down a paper arguing that this long track record of failure ought to lower our credence in present-day doom forecasts. I would like some forensic work done on the slogan itself: 'Malthus wasn't wrong, just early.'

Begin by distinguishing the precise claims it could be making, and lay them out as a menu rather than settling on one. Candidates might include: (i) that his specific mechanism — population tending to grow geometrically while food supply grows at best arithmetically — is sound and simply has not yet had time to bite; (ii) the weaker claim that exponential growth of any kind must eventually collide with some finite constraint, even if the binding constraint keeps shifting (arable land, then soil fertility, then energy, then the atmosphere's capacity to absorb carbon); (iii) the methodological claim that a long-run prediction carrying no date cannot be refuted by any finite stretch of contrary evidence, so two hundred years of non-arrival counts for nothing; and (iv) the purely rhetorical claim that repeated short-run failure is compatible with eventual vindication. For each reading, give a verdict: platitude, substantive and defensible, or false. In particular, address whether reading (ii) is trivially true in a finite universe and therefore worthless as a defence of Malthus specifically, or whether it can be given enough content to be worth defending; and whether readings (iii) and (iv) render the theory unfalsifiable — and if they do, whether that is a vice here or merely the correct treatment of asymptotic claims. Then say which reading my colleague needs if the slogan is to do the work it did at the workshop, namely blocking the inference from a track record of failure to reduced credence, and whether any reading that survives scrutiny actually blocks it. Finally, if defending the slogan requires continually redating the reckoning or re-specifying which resource is the real constraint, explain at what point rescue turns into the silent adoption of a new theory, and what a defender would have to concede up front — a date, a stopping condition, a specified constraint — for 'early' to be a claim with content rather than an immunising stratagem.

Please write your answer in British English.
