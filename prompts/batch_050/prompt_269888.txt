(2) On suffering I'm asymmetric: you can't compensate extreme suffering of some beings with mild pleasures for others. Intense agony gets something like lexical priority, which is part of why I take factory farming so seriously.

(3) Expected value maximization is the right decision rule under uncertainty, full stop. No risk aversion, no ambiguity aversion.

(4) But I reject fanaticism: you shouldn't let tiny probabilities of astronomically large outcomes dominate your decisions. That's why Pascal's mugging doesn't work on me.

(5) Given all that, extinction risk reduction is clearly the top priority, because the expected future is enormous and plausibly net positive, so losing it is astronomically bad.

(6) And s-risks are overhyped. The scenarios are so speculative that taking them seriously as a dominant priority is exactly the Pascalian move I reject in (4) -- you'd be letting a vanishingly small probability of an astronomically bad outcome run your whole life.

Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest fix is and what I'd be giving up.
