I'm a high school senior taking an independent study in aesthetics, and for my final paper I want to defend a set of claims about why people enjoy sad art. Before I commit to them, I'd like to know whether they actually hang together. My commitments: (1) the pleasure I get from sad films and novels is genuine, occurring during the experience rather than being mere relief afterward or a side effect of something else; (2) sadness is by its nature unpleasant -- that's part of what distinguishes it from other emotions; (3) in real life I don't want tragic things to happen and would prevent them if I could; (4) when watching fiction, I sometimes actively hope the tragic outcome occurs, and I would judge the work worse if it were rewritten with a happy ending; (5) I would think poorly of someone who took pleasure in watching a real person suffer, yet I see nothing wrong with my own pleasure in (4), and I don't think the fact that the suffering is fictional is by itself enough to explain this asymmetry. Are these jointly tenable? Please identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the tensions by how serious they are, and for each genuine one tell me specifically what I would have to give up or modify to resolve it -- without assuming in advance which commitment I should drop.
