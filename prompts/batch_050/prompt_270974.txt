I can follow the secondary literature, but I have never had to make the idea intelligible to people who have not already bought it. Do not explain the epoché to me; plan how I should explain it in roughly forty minutes. What must be in place first -- I assume the natural attitude, but I am unsure whether to enter through Descartes, through intentionality, or through something else entirely -- and in what order should the suspension of judgment, the turn to the phenomenon as given, and the idea of constitution be introduced, given what depends on what? What single concrete example should carry the session, and what must that example have and lack (I suspect a perceptual case rather than an emotional one, but I cannot say why)? Which misconceptions should I expect from this particular audience -- my guesses are that the epoché is Cartesian doubt about the external world, that it is a meditative technique for emptying the mind of assumptions, and that the reduction is just careful introspection of one's own mental contents -- and at exactly which point in the sequence should each be headed off, rather than corrected after the fact? Finally, what should I openly bracket as beyond this session -- the transcendental ego, eidetic variation, the later revisions -- and what closing question would show me that the idea has landed rather than been filed under skepticism?
