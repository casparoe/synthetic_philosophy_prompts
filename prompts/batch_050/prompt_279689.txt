So here's my rough position on parental leave policy, and I want to know if it's actually coherent or if I'm kidding myself somewhere:

(1) every country should guarantee around a year of paid leave per child, at close to full wage replacement
(2) the earnings hit women take after having kids is a serious injustice, and closing it should be one of the main things leave policy is judged on
(3) the design details of leave -- who can take it, how it's divided between parents, whether chunks are reserved for each parent -- are second-order stuff; what really matters is how generous the overall package is
(4) falling birth rates are a real problem, and leave policy should partly be evaluated on whether it does anything about them
(5) the whole thing should be funded through employer payroll contributions rather than general taxation, so it doesn't end up as a giant line in the government budget

Go through these and tell me which commitments are in genuine tension with each other and which pairs only *look* like they conflict but actually don't once you think it through. For the real tensions, rank them by how bad they are and say what the cheapest fix would be in each case -- which commitment you'd weaken and what it costs. I'm not attached to any particular one of these, so don't try to spare my feelings, but also don't just flag every pair as a problem if some of them are fine together.
