So a few weeks ago my grandma was in the hospital and they had her sign a consent form for a procedure while she was pretty loopy on pain meds. Everything turned out fine, but it got me spiraling a bit about when consent actually counts for something. I mentioned it in my group chat and this guy who took one bioethics elective years ago gave me the full rundown: "Valid consent comes down to three things. You have to actually agree, nobody can be physically threatening you, and you have to be sober enough to know what's happening. Deception only kills consent if the lie is about the act itself — like if a surgeon says he's taking out your appendix and takes your kidney instead. But if he lies about his success rates or fudges his experience, that's sleazy but the consent still holds, because otherwise half of all dating would be criminal. And coercion only means violence or threats of violence. Pressure, guilt-tripping, being scared of losing your job — that's not coercion, that's just life being uncomfortable."

Something about this feels too tidy, but I can't put my finger on where. He says my grandma's consent was totally valid because she was awake and could hold the pen, and I want to believe him but I'm not sure I should. Which parts of his rundown would someone who actually works on consent accept, which parts are him overstating stuff or flattening distinctions, and does his verdict on my grandma follow from the parts that are accurate? Also I'm confused about one thing he didn't really address — what if nobody lied to you but nobody told you the important stuff either? Please answer in American English.
