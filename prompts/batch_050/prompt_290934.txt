So I'm a novelist, currently stuck on a book whose villain (or maybe not villain, that's kind of the question) is a man who manipulates and hurts people without apparently losing a wink of sleep. In trying to write him convincingly I've fallen down the philosophy-of-psychopathy rabbit hole, and everywhere I turn people reach for the same analogy: the psychopath is to moral wrongness as the colorblind person is to red and green. He can learn the traffic-light rules, he can say "that's wrong" the way a colorblind driver says "red means stop," but the actual moral *seeing* just isn't there. People use this analogy to do real work -- to argue that psychopaths don't genuinely *know* their actions are wrong in the way that matters for blame, that punishing them is like punishing someone for being unable to distinguish colors.

I'd like you to audit this analogy properly. Lay out the correspondence table: what plays the role of the retina/cones, what's supposed to be the color experience itself, what's the behavioral rule-learning, and where things like empathy, fear, and the ability to parrot moral vocabulary are supposed to fit in. Then take it apart. Colorblind people don't *seek out* situations that exploit their deficit; psychopaths often do -- does that break the analogy or just complicate it? Colorblindness is total and stable, but plenty of psychopaths seem to selectively "see" moral claims when they're the victim -- is that like colorblindness that switches on and off, and if not, what does that do to the whole picture? Also colorblind people generally wish they could see color; does the psychopath's apparent indifference to his own deficit matter for whether the missing thing is knowledge, caring, or something else? If the analogy breaks, tell me what breaks first and whether the exculpatory conclusion (he doesn't really know it's wrong, so blame is misplaced) was being carried by the analogy the whole time.

I'm not asking you to settle the empirical literature, and please don't invent citations I can't check. Reason from what's well established. I mainly want the conceptual mapping done honestly so I know which features to give my character and which would betray the actual debate. One more thing: tell me how confident you are in the main claims of your answer, and single out the single point you're *least* sure of -- the spot where you think you're most likely just repeating something that sounds right.
