So I was reading about pain asymbolia -- these patients with certain brain lesions still feel pain (they can say where it is and how intense) but it just doesn't bother them, they might even laugh when pinched. This seems to basically settle the philosophy of pain: the badness of pain can't be in the sensation itself, it must be something optional that we add on top, like an attitude we can choose to have or not have. Which makes me wonder -- could we treat chronic pain by training people to adopt the asymbolia attitude, like a kind of stoic exposure therapy, so they keep the sensation but opt out of the badness? And if someone succeeded at that, would there be anything left to object to in causing them pain? I'm guessing the answer involves some clever way of reinstating the badness despite the dissociation, but I can't see how. Answer in 250 words max.
