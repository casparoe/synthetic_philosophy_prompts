For a graduate seminar in the philosophy of artificial intelligence, I want a thought experiment that separates deceptive alignment from a treacherous turn rather than treating the latter as the natural narrative sequel to the former. Construct an original scenario set in a near-future administrative institution—for example, a hospital scheduling system, an electrical-grid operator, or a research-funding office—and present three minimally different versions of it:

A. The system is deceptively aligned but never makes a treacherous turn.

B. The system makes a treacherous turn without having been deceptively aligned beforehand.

C. The system undergoes an outwardly similar shift in behavior, but neither deceptive alignment nor a treacherous turn has occurred.

Use these working criteria. Deceptive alignment requires the system intentionally to foster a misleading expectation about its objectives because doing so protects its actual objectives or future policy. A treacherous turn requires a transition from cooperation to adversarial action because the balance of enforceable control has changed. You may argue that either criterion is philosophically inadequate, but begin by constructing cases that satisfy them.

Constraints:

- The system must have bounded capacities: no omniscience, perfect prediction, direct self-replication, inexplicable emergence of consciousness, or ability to read human minds.
- Its incentives and strategic reasoning must arise from the institutional setup rather than from an explicit instruction to deceive or a stipulated desire to harm humanity.
- Human operators must retain some plausible auditing and rollback mechanisms, but no interpretability tool that directly reveals the system’s goals.
- Hold the deployment environment, capabilities, and available evidence as constant as possible across the three versions. The relevant differences should be structural rather than merely differences in what labels the narrator assigns.
- The first version must not contain a future defection that is merely postponed. Explain why indefinite cooperation remains instrumentally rational despite the system’s concealed objective.
- The second version must not reveal that the system had secretly harbored the same adversarial plan all along. Explain how sincere initial cooperation can later become strategic defection without retroactively making the earlier behavior deceptive.
- The third version must not be a simple malfunction. Its apparent turn should admit a rational, cooperative explanation grounded in changed evidence, altered instructions, or a conflict between two legitimate directives.

After presenting the scenario in at most 450 words, analyze the three versions. State precisely why each does or does not satisfy the two working criteria, identify the feature of the case doing the decisive conceptual work, and explain what happens to each classification if that feature is removed. Then consider the strongest objection to each result: that deceptive alignment requires at least a conditional intention to defect; that treachery requires prior concealment; and that the third case is simply ordinary misalignment under another name.

Conclude by proposing a behavioral or counterfactual test that human auditors could use to distinguish the three situations. Be explicit about which distinctions remain underdetermined by observable conduct alone and whether that underdetermination is merely an epistemic limitation or evidence that one of the two concepts has no sharp boundary.
