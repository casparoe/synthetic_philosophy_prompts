I’m the best man at my brother’s wedding next month and I need a toast of about four or five minutes. He’s a philosopher of time, his bride is an astrophysicist, and they apparently spent their first proper date arguing about whether a supernova in the Andromeda galaxy is already real or still merely future. Roughly a third of the guests will know what a simultaneity slice is, and the other two thirds will mostly be there for the open bar, so it has to work for both groups.

I’d like the toast to use the Rietdijk–Putnam argument as its central image: two observers moving differently can disagree about whether a distant event is present or future, and if reality itself is not supposed to depend on someone’s direction of motion, then at least some future events look no less real than present ones. My brother accepts some cautious version of that conclusion, but he gets very annoyed when people slide from the future is real to everything was inevitable or we could have known what would happen. Please make the speech structurally depend on keeping those three claims apart. Their marriage should come out as something they made together, not as a scene they were doomed to act out, while still taking the block-universe picture seriously rather than reassuring everyone that the future is completely unwritten. Include one gentle joke involving the Andromeda wedding invitations, keep the physics accessible enough for our grandmother, and don’t let it become a seminar with boutonnieres.
