I'm taking a philosophy of religion course, and we've just covered the omnipotence paradox. I have a set of verdicts about what an omnipotent being can and cannot do, and I want to know what principle, if any, unifies them.

My verdicts:
1. God cannot make 2+2 equal 5 or draw a square circle, and this is no threat to his omnipotence: there is no such "task" to fail at.
2. God cannot create a stone too heavy for him to lift, and again this is no threat: a stone an omnipotent being cannot lift is an incoherent description, so nothing doable goes undone.
3. God cannot change the past, and this is no threat either — the past is not the sort of thing that admits of being made otherwise.
4. God can make promises that genuinely bind him — he cannot afterward break them — and this inability is no threat; it reflects his faithfulness rather than any lack of power.
5. God cannot sin or lie, and this is no threat, since such acts would manifest weakness, not power.
6. God cannot create a second omnipotent being, and I think this too is no threat: two omnipotent wills could conflict, which shows the scenario is incoherent.

What worries me is that verdicts 2 and 4 look structurally identical — in each, God brings about something he thereafter cannot overcome or undo — yet I excuse one as incoherent and the other as a perfection. Please state precisely the strongest principle (or a small set of candidates) that captures my verdicts, saying which case motivates each clause; tell me which verdict it must overturn if it cannot save them all; apply it to a case I haven't decided, namely whether God can create creatures whose free choices he cannot control; and finally, tell me where the principle is most likely to fail — what consideration or counterexample would most plausibly force a different verdict.
