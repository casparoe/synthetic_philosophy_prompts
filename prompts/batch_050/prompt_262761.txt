After thirty years working as a condensed-matter physicist, I retired and began reading philosophy of science, and I keep circling one dispute. Two of my former colleagues — one now sympathetic to the reductionist tradition in philosophy, one to the literature on emergence — have been arguing for a year about what effective field theories actually show. The first, whom I will call the Fundamentalist, holds that an effective theory is a calculational convenience: it works because it approximates a deeper theory, its successes are inherited from that deeper theory, and the only genuine explanations live at the most fundamental level we can reach. The second, the Autonomist, holds that the renormalization group demonstrates something stronger: that physics at a given scale is largely insulated from the microphysics beneath it, that the same low-energy theory can arise from indefinitely many different microscopic completions, and that therefore each scale has its own explanatory structure which a completed final theory would not improve upon.

Draft the joint statement these two could sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. The following facts should constrain you, since both parties accept them: critical exponents at the liquid–gas transition match those of the Ising model despite utterly different microscopic constituents; low-energy predictions proceed by absorbing unknown high-energy physics into a handful of renormalized parameters, without any knowledge of the correct ultraviolet completion; and yet certain low-energy questions — the smallness of the Higgs mass relative to the Planck scale being the standard example — seem to drag the deep physics back in, which is the basis of the naturalness debate. Whether that last fact refutes the Autonomist or only limits her claim is precisely what is in dispute.

Structure the statement with exactly these sections, in this order:

**Agreed Ground** (roughly 250 words): the claims both can sign — about the formalism, about universality classes, about what the decoupling of heavy particles does and does not establish, and about what each side takes itself to be explaining. Every claim here must be substantive; omit anything so hedged it is empty.

**Residual Disagreements** (roughly 200 words): the precise points of continued disagreement, each stated in wording both parties would accept as a fair description of their own position. Take special care with the word "explain" — I suspect the two camps mean different things by it, and if so, say so here rather than letting it fester.

**Cruxes and Registered Predictions** (roughly 300 words): the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side, and for each, the case, argument, or conceivable finding both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Candidates you should consider: whether insensitivity to microphysics constitutes explanatory autonomy or merely epistemic convenience for creatures like us; whether the naturalness problem shows that ultraviolet data leaks into the infrared after all; and whether the multiple realizability of one effective theory by many ultraviolet completions supports autonomy in the way philosophers of mind once claimed it did for psychology. Do not limit yourself to these.

**Verbal Residue** (roughly 100 words): whichever part of the dispute the drafters conclude was about words rather than the world, and the evidence for that verdict.

Write as the drafters would: neutral, exact, and without adjudicating the cruxes yourselves.
