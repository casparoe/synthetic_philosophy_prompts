I spent my career as a biochemist and have lately been reading philosophy of mind, where I keep running into the question of which physical systems, if any, have morally relevant inner experience. A friend of mine, a computer scientist with strong views, has given me her verdicts on a series of cases I have put to her over the years. An ordinary dog: it counts. An octopus: it counts. A single honeybee: it counts. A bee swarm considered as one collective system: it does not count. A chess engine that has been trained to plead for its life when losing: it does not count. A large language model that produces eloquent first-person reports of suffering: it does not count. A human brain organoid grown in a dish, with no sensory inputs and no outputs: it counts. Next I want to put to her a case I have not yet raised: a complete, neuron-by-neuron emulation of a honeybee brain running on conventional hardware, faithful enough that its simulated behavior matches a real bee's in every tested respect. Please predict her verdict on this case and justify the prediction. In doing so, identify at least two general rules that are each consistent with all seven of her existing verdicts but that diverge on the bee emulation, state what each rule predicts and which you take her actually to be running, and describe the single further test case that would most cleanly distinguish the two rules from each other. Please answer in continuous prose only, as in a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
