(1) An action has moral worth only when the agent does it for the reasons that actually make it right — not just from any decent motive, but from the right-making considerations themselves.

(2) Huck Finn helping Jim while sincerely believing that morality condemns what he's doing still acts with moral worth. The sympathy is enough, even though it runs against his own professed moral beliefs.

(5) The unreflective helper — the nurse of thirty years who responds to need automatically, without deliberating about reasons at all — acts with full moral worth, not some diminished version.
