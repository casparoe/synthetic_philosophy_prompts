I'm trying to work out what I think pleasure is by going case by case, and I want to know whether there's a principle underneath my verdicts or just a heap of prejudices. The old heterogeneity worry, the one associated with Sidgwick, is that pleasures seem to share no introspectible quality at all — that the pleasure of a warm bath and the pleasure of a good theorem have nothing in common that you could point to in experience. I want to know whether my own reactions commit me to that pessimism or escape it.

Here are my verdicts:

1. The taste of a ripe mango: a pleasure, and the paradigm case — it has a felt quality I could recognize blind.
2. The moment my chronic back pain eases after medication: the strongest pleasure I know, even though nothing positive has been added to my experience — something has just been subtracted.
3. The satisfaction of finishing a difficult proof: a pleasure, but if you asked me to describe the sensation, I would draw a complete blank. There is nothing it feels like, as far as I can tell.
4. A warm bath when I'm already comfortable: a mild pleasure, clearly felt, clearly located.
5. Flow while rock climbing: during it, I'm aware of no pleasure whatsoever; my attention is entirely on the rock. Afterwards I'd still call it one of the most pleasurable things I do, and I'm confident I'm not merely misremembering.
6. Schadenfreude at a rival's public failure: it feels pleasurable, and grudgingly I count it as a pleasure, though I wish I didn't.
7. The masochist's enjoyment of pain: I want to say the pain is genuinely unpleasant for them and yet the episode as a whole is pleasurable — so a pleasure can be partly constituted by an unpleasant sensation.
8. Deep contentment in meditation: barely any phenomenal character at all, but a pleasure.
9. Anticipating a holiday that later gets cancelled: it felt pleasurable at the time, but I'm genuinely torn about whether pleasure that was "of" nothing real still counts.
10. Being tickled: an intense, unmistakable sensation, but not a pleasure — I dislike it throughout and would pay to make it stop.

My tentative principle: pleasures are experiences that, while we have them, we want to continue for their own sake. But I can already see it straining — during flow I want nothing at all, being absorbed; I want the relief in case 2 to continue but only because I want the pain to stay gone; and in case 6 I want the feeling to continue while disavowing that very wanting.
