I'd like your credence on a claim about political psychology, with the reasoning decomposed. The claim: the left–right distinction tracks a real, cross-culturally recurring dimension of human political temperament — something like a stable axis of attitudes toward hierarchy, change, and the scope of moral concern — rather than being a historically contingent coalition label that could just as easily have been carved up differently (so that, say, free trade and secularism ended up on one side and labor rights and nationalism on the other). My own credence is about 0.55; a colleague's is 0.9.

Please decompose the question into at least four sub-questions, including these or better versions of them:

1. The probability that a single dominant psychological dimension of political temperament recurs across most known political cultures, whatever it is called locally.
2. The probability that, conditional on such a dimension existing, the actual issue-content of "left" and "right" is strongly constrained by it, rather than being substantially arbitrary (i.e., that on a rerun of history the sides would mostly end up with the same policy bundles).
3. The probability that the distinction will still organize political conflict fifty years from now, once issues with no obvious historical parent — AI governance, human enhancement, space settlement — dominate the agenda.
4. The probability that what holds each side together internally is shared temperament rather than mutual convenience and shared opponents (i.e., that if the opposing coalition disappeared, each side would stay coherent rather than fragment).

State how you combine the terms into an overall credence, identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and say what evidence would move that term most. Finally, diagnose where my colleague (0.9) and I (0.55) most likely disagree — which sub-question best explains the gap.

Write in American English. Present the decomposition as a table with columns: sub-question, your probability, one-line justification. Then give a short paragraph covering the combination, the sensitivity analysis, and the disagreement diagnosis.
