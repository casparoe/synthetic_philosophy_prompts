write a sonnet in the voice of a man blind from birth whose sight has just been surgically restored, the morning after. he's been sat in front of a table with a cube and a sphere on it and told to say which is which without touching them. the octave should be him taking in light and color for the first time and finding it weirdly useless -- a flood of shapes he has words for but no grip on, like being read a language he's only ever translated. the volta should be the moment he realizes the hard part isnt the seeing, its the question: whether the cube his hands have known for thirty years is the same thing as this angled brightness in front of him. let the couplet stay undecided about whether he guesses, refuses, or asks to touch them first. someone who knows Molyneux's problem should be able to tell excatly where the poem stands on it, but it should still land as a poem for someone whos never heard of it.
