I'm running a session of our local rationalist meetup on the boundary problem in democratic theory — i.e. before you can ask what the people want, who gets to count as the people? — and I want a handout that sorts the main answers into a decision tree rather than the usual laundry list of principles. Please build the tree from the logical space, not from author names: each internal node should be a question someone could honestly answer without knowing the literature (e.g. whether the demos is fixed by the scope of the decision at hand, or whether there has to be a standing people prior to any particular decision), and each leaf should be a named position plus one sentence on its most awkward implication. These views have to show up as leaves: the all-affected-interests principle, the all-coerced or all-subjected principle, national self-determination, the status-quo-plus-accretion view (current membership is provisionally legitimate, grows only by naturalization), and the deflationary verdict that the problem has no principled solution and democracy is just conceptually incomplete here. Phrase at least a few of the nodes using these test cases: Scottish independence (Scotland-only vote or UK-wide?), a downstream country whose river your polity wants to dam, and resident non-citizens who've lived in the country for twenty years. Aim for 7-9 leaves total; if some combination of answers is unoccupied, still put it on the tree with a one-line diagnosis of whether it's empty because the view is incoherent or merely undefended, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate. Start with the tree itself — no preface, no re-explaining the boundary problem back to me.
