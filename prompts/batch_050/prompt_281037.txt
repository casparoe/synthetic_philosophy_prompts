Next month I'm giving a talk in my university's history-and-philosophy colloquium defending a version of the hinge of history idea, and I need to survive the Q&A. My thesis, roughly: I'm NOT claiming we can know we're at the single most pivotal moment ever. I'm claiming something weaker but still spicy -- that because of (a) the realistic possibility of transformative AI this century and (b) the chance that certain values or institutional arrangements get durably locked in afterwards, the expected influence per unit of effort right now is much higher than in a typical historical century, and this is true even under pretty heavy uncertainty. I'll sketch the significance-persistence-contingency way of thinking about it and argue our era scores weirdly high on all three.

The audience: this is the scary part. It's a mixed room. There are a couple of moral philosophers who I expect to go after whether we can even reason about effects centuries out (cluelessness-type worries) rather than the empirical AI stuff. Plus grad students who'll ask whatever. Nobody in the room is an AI person, so I can't lean on timelines arguments.
