I’m doing a short talk at our philosophy-in-the-pub night arguing that the modern Stoic revival has turned the dichotomy of control into a tidy life-hack, and that this quietly cheats: people say only judgements and intentions are up to us, then still treat health, promotions and children behaving as the scoreboard for whether we did Stoicism properly. My invented bit is that lots of us run a hidden third zone between control and no-control: influence-with-credit, where we claim not to crave outcomes but feel morally robbed when effort doesn’t pay. Audience: two CBT therapists, one classics teacher allergic to Instagram Stoicism, and a few knackered self-help readers. Prepare me for the questions I’ll actually get, in their vocab, ordered by likelihood and damage: what to concede, what to hold, a two-minute reply for each, where each reply is soft, and the question I’m hoping nobody asks. Keep it concrete, no reading list.
