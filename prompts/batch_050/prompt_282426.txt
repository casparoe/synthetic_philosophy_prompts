Draft the joint statement that a proponent of the non-individuals view and a defender of weak discernibility could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the metaphysics of identical quantum particles. By the non-individuals view, I mean the position that the permutation invariance of fermionic and bosonic states and the failure of classical counting show that quantum particles are not individuals at all; by weak discernibility, I mean the position that even absolutely indiscernible particles stand in irreflexive physical relations (such as having opposite spin to each other in a singlet state) that suffice to establish their numerical distinctness and hence their individuality. Structure the deliverable in three parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: these should include what both sides accept about the formalism and its empirical success, the status of classical Maxwell-Boltzmann counting, whether any experiment could decide between the two ontologies, what is really going on when experimenters speak of having measured this electron rather than that one, and the fate of the principle of identity of indiscernibles when restricted to non-relational properties. Second, a disagreements table in which each residual dispute is stated in a formulation its own side would accept as accurate, covering at least the charge that irreflexive relations presuppose numerically distinct relata and so cannot ground distinctness without circularity, the question of whether individuality requires a primitive thisness over and above qualitative profile, and the methodological question of whether an ontology can be read off a mathematical formalism at all or whether the underdetermination is itself the datum. For each crux, specify a case or argument both parties accept as bearing on it and record each side's prediction in advance -- for instance, how each camp expects the circularity charge to fare when tested against a small toy model of relationally discerned objects, and how each treats the case of two particles whose wavefunctions never overlap. Third, a short closing section that identifies at least one point the collaborators came to regard as merely verbal, states the terminology each side will use going forward, and flags every occurrence of the words particle, identical, and individual with an explicit gloss, since unqualified use of those terms is itself part of what the collaboration was convened to repair. The statement should be something both camps could put their names to: no vacuous compromises, no formulations slanted toward one side, and no pretending the residual disagreement is smaller than it is.
