Mechanistic interpretability researchers now routinely do the following: they train sparse autoencoders on a language model's internal activations to extract thousands of "features," label each feature with a natural-language gloss by inspecting the inputs that activate it most strongly (e.g., "fires on text about the Golden Gate Bridge"), then demonstrate their labels by artificially amplifying or suppressing a feature and observing that the model's behavior changes in the predicted way (e.g., it starts working the Golden Gate Bridge into unrelated answers). On the strength of this workflow, papers and press releases state flatly that the model "has a representation of" the Golden Gate Bridge, that deception features "exist" in the network, and so on.

What does this practice presuppose about what a representation is? Reconstruct the implicit theory of content: it seems to treat a feature's meaning as fixed jointly by what causes it to activate and what its activation causes, which resembles a causal-informational theory of representation -- but is that the right reconstruction, and does the labeling-by-inspection step smuggle in the researcher's interpretation in a way a purely causal theory would forbid? Then ask the harder question: does the success of steering interventions establish that the model *represents* anything, or only that certain directions in activation space are reliable *handles* on behavior? Could a thoroughgoing instrumentalist -- someone who denies there is any fact of the matter about what the network represents -- license every part of this practice unchanged, treating "feature" talk as a convenient fiction? If so, the practice may be philosophically underdetermined: say precisely where the realism is doing work and where it is idle decoration. Finally, consider one place where the practice might actually *require* realism: when researchers claim to have found "the same" feature in two different models, or to have found a feature for a concept (like deceptive intent) whose causal footprint is contested. Does the cross-model and contested-concept case force commitments the steering demonstrations alone do not?
