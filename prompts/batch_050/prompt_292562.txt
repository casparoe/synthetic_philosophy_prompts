Objections to acausal trade I've already got on my list, so don't repeat these: (1) the enforcement objection -- real trade works through causal channels like repeated interaction, reputation, contracts, and once you strip away all causal contact nothing raises the cost of "defection," so the word trade is doing unearned work; (2) the verification objection -- you can never check whether your counterpart even exists or held up its end, so the practice is observationally indistinguishable from being mugged by your own imagination; (3) the redundancy objection -- once you've adopted the decision theory that makes you predictable to distant reasoners, cooperation just falls out of the theory itself, so "acausal trade" adds no normative force beyond the decision procedure doing all the lifting; (4) the mugging objection -- counterfactual-mugging-style cases suggest the same reasoning commits you to paying out to any agent clever enough to model you, which looks less like trade and more like a general policy of being exploitable. I want a fifth objection that isn't a rewording, special case, or combo of these four. And then tell me straight up whether it actually threatens the idea or just makes it feel icky.
