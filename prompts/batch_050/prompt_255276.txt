A colleague insists that 'AI alignment' names one problem, not a cluster of loosely related safety concerns, and she classifies proposed research projects accordingly. Her verdicts so far: mechanistic interpretability of a large model's internal circuits counts as alignment research; training models to refuse to help with phishing or malware does not ('that is safety policy, not alignment'); studying whether models behave differently when they can tell they are being evaluated does; improving human-feedback pipelines to make assistants more helpful does not ('that is product work'); and formal work on reward tampering by reinforcement-learning agents does. A new project proposes machinery by which a model's deployer and its end users could inspect, contest and override the values its developer baked into it — work squarely on the question of alignment to whom. Predict her verdict, identify the single problem she appears to take alignment to be, name at least one rival conception equally consistent with her track record, and say which further hypothetical project would distinguish them. Please answer in British English.
