The other grants that erasure really is costly but thinks this doesn't settle anything — she should press something like: a demon with a huge blank memory never has to erase, so it can run the Szilard engine cycle for ages and the second law is only "saved" in some asymptotic limit, which seems like a weird way to rescue a law of physics. I want them to actually work through the concrete one-particle setup (box, partition, piston, which side the molecule is on) rather than talk in vague terms about "information", and they should respond to what the other one actually said, not just restate their opening positions. Ideally they end by pinpointing the exact step where they disagree (e.g. whether writing a measurement result into memory is itself thermodynamically free, or whether the entropy cost can always be postponed but never avoided), not by one of them "winning". Around 500-700 words, and dont make the critic a strawman because my TA will definately notice.
