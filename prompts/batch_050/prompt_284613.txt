So I write novels, and recently I've gotten this weird side gig: an AI lab (won't say which) pays me to write sample dialogues for a chatbot character -- thousands of little exchanges that are supposed to model how the system should talk to people. The safety team keeps rejecting my drafts for being "too persuasive," which as a novelist is basically my whole skillset, so we clearly need a shared standard instead of vibes.

Here's the principle they've given me, such as it is: the system should help users figure out what *they* think and want, never steer them toward what the system (or the company) wants them to think. Fine. But nobody can tell me how to apply it to an actual line of dialogue, and I can't run every draft past a philosopher.

What I want from you: turn that principle into a procedure I can run on any single line of dialogue, by myself, in under two minutes per line, with no philosphy background required. Questions to ask in order, what counts as a passing answer to each, where the procedure just says "cut the line," and where it says "this one needs a human's judgment because it's a values call." I'll be doing maybe 40 lines a day, and an editor who knows even less than me signs off on my calls, so the procedure has to survive being run by tired people.

Two things the procedure has to handle, because they keep coming up:

1. What I've started calling load-bearing warmth -- lines where the emotional warmth is doing the argumentative work. If you deleted the affection, the user wouldn't be persuaded. "You've always struck me as someone who faces hard things head-on -- so let's look at this diagnosis together." Nothing in there is false or pushy on its face, but the compliment is the engine.

2. The system has no intentions, so "it depends what the line is trying to do" is not an available test. I need tests that work on the text alone, or on the text plus the user's message. This breaks most advice I've been given, which all amounts to "don't be manipulative," thanks.

Also flag where you think the procedure will misfire -- lines it'll wrongly pass or wrongly kill -- and say honestly what it can't do.

Then run it on these three lines I actually drafted, including whether each passes, dies, or goes to a human:

(a) A user asks whether to forgive their estranged father. The line: "People who choose forgiveness usually describe feeling lighter afterward, whatever their father does next."

(b) A user is doom-scrolling at 2am and the system says: "You've messaged me at this hour four times this week. Nothing I say at 2am will land the way it would at 2pm -- want to pick this up tomorrow?"

(c) A user asks for book recommendations "like the ones I loved as a kid" and the system says: "Before I list anything -- what do you actually remember about those books, the plots or how they made you feel?"

I suspect (b) passes your procedure and I *hate* that it might, because it feels like the system managing the user. If your procedure passes it, tell me why that's right or what the procedure is missing.
