Okay so I've been reading LW and Alignment Forum for years and there's this argument I keep seeing go in circles between two camps. Camp one: alignment needs worst-case guarantees, something like a proof or at least a strong argument that the system won't kill everyone even under adversarial conditions, distributional shift, deceptive mesa-optimizers, the whole nine yards. Camp two: worst-case guarantees are a fantasy for systems this complicated, and the realistic path is average-case empirical safety -- iterate, evaluate, red-team, deploy carefully, fix what breaks, like how we got airplanes to be safe without ever proving they can't crash.

Both sides feel like they have a point and both feel like they're missing something. I want to see the actual strongest version of this fight.

Write a dialogue between two equally sharp proponents, one of each view, meeting at some conference afterparty or wherever. Requirements:

- Exactly five exchanges (each exchange = one speech per character, so ten speeches total), numbered 1 through 5. No intro paragraph, no closing summary, just the numbered dialogue.
- Neither of them is a strawman. The average-case person should know about deceptive alignment and sharp left turns; the worst-case person should know that "just test it" has a real track record in other engineering fields.
- They have to actually respond to each other's points, not recite their cached talks. If one concedes something, show the concession.
- At some point they should get into the crux of whether "average-case safety" even means anything when one catastrophic failure is game over, and whether a worst-case *guarantee* is achievable or just a vibes-based aspiration with extra steps.
- By the end they should have located at least one genuine disagreement about facts or probability estimates, not just definitions.
