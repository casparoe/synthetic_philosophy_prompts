So I've ended up informally advising a small regranting program -- think five or six theory-ish people, one to two years each -- on work around eliciting latent knowledge from AI systems: the whole cluster of problems about getting a model to report what it "knows" when its incentives, training signal, or internal architecture push against honest reporting. I need to hand the committee a prioritized list of open conceptual questions, and I'd rather outsource the first draft of the thinking to you before I embarass myself in the meeting.

Some framing so you know where I'm coming from. I've been toying with a distinction and I want it stress-tested as much as used: call it the difference between read-off failures and construction failures. Read-off failures are cases where the model's internal state already contains something like a straightforward encoding of the true answer, and the problem is "merely" that the output channel is trained to say something else -- deception, sycophancy, sandbagging, whatever. Construction failures are cases where the honest answer isn't sitting anywhere in the network as a retrievable token-shaped thing at all: the knowledge is smeared across the computation, implicit in behavior, or only exists relative to a way of asking, so there's nothing to "read off" even with a perfect lie detector. My hunch is that most of the classic ELK framing implicitly assumes the read-off picture, and that if construction failures are the common case in practice, half the research agenda gets reordered. But I could be wrong about either the distinction or the hunch.

Given that: which open conceptual questions in this area should we actually fund, and in what order? I want a defended agenda, not a survey. For each question you prioritize: why it's upstream of other work (or why people wrongly treat it as upstream), how tractable it is for people who are good at philosophy/theory but can't just run frontier-scale experiments, and what empirical results over the next year or two would move it up or down your list. Explicitly say whether your ordering assumes the read-off picture dominates, and how it changes if construction failures are the real bottleneck -- and if you think my distinction is confused or collapses on inspection, say that and reorder accordingly, because that's useful to me too.

Also tell me which questions you'd deprioritize even though they're popular, on the grounds that their answer wouldn't change much downstream. One more thing: please be careful to flag what's genuinely settled among people who work on this versus what's still contested -- I don't want to walk into the committee presenting one camp's hunch as the consensus view, and I know this field has more unsettled cruxes than it sometimes admits.
