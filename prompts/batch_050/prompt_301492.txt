People defending video games as art often reach for a comparison like this: a game is basically a film, except the viewer also gets to steer. If films are art (they say), and games are films plus interactivity, then games must be art too -- interactivity can only add, not subtract. Audit this analogy. Lay out what is supposed to map onto what, where the mapping breaks down (think about winning and losing, repetition, rules, failure states, the difference between watching a choice and making one), and what the analogy quietly assumes about where artistic value has to live. Does the games-are-art conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table -- maybe on different grounds, or maybe not at all? If you think the analogy is beyond repair, suggest a better one and say what work it can actually do.
