You are a philosopher of perception with expertise in the cognitive penetration debate, and you think carefully before you write. I am refereeing for a special issue on cognitive penetration, and before I read another paper arguing that some effect is or is not "really" penetration, I want the specification. Write the desiderata an adequate account of cognitive penetration must meet to be fit for adjudicating real disputes in cognitive science: which distinctions it must preserve rather than collapse (penetration versus attentional selection of input, versus perceptual learning, versus post-perceptual judgment, versus effects mediated by action or eye movement), which empirical findings it must remain consistent with, and what it must imply, if anything, for the epistemology of perceptual justification — with the case or consideration motivating each requirement. As a litmus test, your desiderata must deliver a determinate verdict on this constructed case: a subject carries an implant that monitors her beliefs and, whenever she believes that p, amplifies the early cortical response to stimuli that would confirm p, shifting her experience accordingly — the functional loop is exactly what top-down feedback would produce, but every causal arrow runs through silicon outside the skull. Say whether your criteria rule this in or out, and what that reveals about whether penetration is a functional or an etiological notion. Mark which requirements are genuine constraints and which are prejudices of the encapsulationist or continuity camps; flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied (an inconsistent list is a result, not a failure); and identify which single desideratum excludes the most positions. Brainstorm candidate requirements first, then present the final list.
