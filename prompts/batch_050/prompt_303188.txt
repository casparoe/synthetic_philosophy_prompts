Write a deadpan satirical op-ed of roughly 650 words, in American English, of the sort that might run on the opinion page of a serious national newspaper. The author is the invented director of an invented policy institute, and the proposal is this: trial by jury should be abolished, and verdicts in criminal cases should instead be decided by popular election. The author's stated grievance is the jury's method of selection -- ordinary citizens chosen essentially at random, with no test of competence, no public record to inspect, and no way to hold them accountable once the verdict is delivered -- and the remedy is the mechanism the country already trusts with its most consequential decisions: campaigns, debates, advertising, polling, and a vote.

The satire must work by transplant, not by invention. Every argument the author gives for electing verdicts should be a genuine, recognizable argument for electing legislators: popular sovereignty, accountability through the next election, the aggregating wisdom of a large electorate (the author may lean on Condorcet's jury theorem in its usual electoral dress), the educative value of campaigning and public deliberation, and the sheer scandal of letting randomly selected, untested, unaccountable strangers decide matters of the highest importance. Do not hand the author arguments no defender of elections would actually make; the humor should come from watching respectable arguments curdle when applied to the courtroom, and a reader who accepts those arguments for legislatures should feel distinctly queasy reading them here.

Specific requirements:

- Fill in the procedure with straight-faced specificity: how the Guilty and Not Guilty campaigns are organized and funded; televised verdict debates; campaign advertising; swing precincts; exit polls; and what becomes of the defendant in the months between indictment and Verdict Day.
- Include exactly one passage in which the author considers, and brusquely dismisses as crankish, the suggestion that legislatures themselves might be filled by lottery -- whether with plain citizens drawn at random or with randomly chosen citizens briefed by experts on the issues before voting. Let the dismissal sit without commentary; do not editorialize on it.
- The author must never wink. No scare quotes around "democracy," no asides to the reader, no acknowledgment that anything is amiss, and do not use the words satire, parody, or modest proposal anywhere in the piece.
- End on the author's sincere-sounding peroration about trusting the judgment of the people.

After the op-ed, append a fenced JSON code block summarizing the piece, with exactly these fields: "target", a string naming the practice or position the satire is aimed at; "transplanted_arguments", an array of objects, each with the fields "argument" (one sentence), "usual_home" (where this argument is normally deployed), and "effect_when_transplanted" (one sentence on why it jars in the courtroom); "deadpan_devices", an array of short strings, each naming one technique the piece uses to keep the irony legible without breaking character; and "word_count", an integer giving the length of the op-ed alone. The entire response, op-ed and JSON alike, should be in American English.
