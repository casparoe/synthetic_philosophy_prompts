I've been turning over a pattern I keep running into, and I'd like to know whether it has an established name and whether anyone has mapped out its edges. Here's the pattern in three instances. First: I replaced my old wind-up kitchen timer with a phone app, and it struck me that these two objects share not a single part, material, or mechanism -- gears and springs on one side, code and a speaker on the other -- and yet they are, in some very real sense, the *same thing*, so that if someone asked "where's the timer?" I could point to either. Second: a game of chess can be played with carved wooden pieces, on a screen, or by two people calling out moves on a long walk with no board at all, and everyone agrees it's the *same game* even though nothing physical carries over. Third: a message -- a birthday greeting, say -- can exist as ink on paper, letters carved in stone, or light on a monitor, and the message itself seems untouched by all these changes of material. The general shape: what something *does* can stay completely fixed while what it's *made of* varies freely, apparently without limit.

I've tried to pin down what this pattern is *not*, and I'd like help there too. It's not just two copies of one object, since copies share their construction. It's not merely that two tools can do the same job in the loose everyday sense -- a hammer and a rock both drive nails, but nobody would call the rock another hammer in the deep way the app seems to be another timer; or maybe they would, and that's part of what I'm asking. It also seems different from translation between languages, where a sentence survives a change of words, because in my cases what survives isn't a text but a *way of working*. So: is there a recognized concept for this -- for one and the same "job" or "organization" being carried out by wholly different materials? If so, how do the people who study it mark it off from the near-misses I've listed? And where do its boundaries run: is the same song played by an orchestra and then whistled in a hallway a case of it? Is a recipe and the cake that follows it?

The reason I care, beyond tidiness, is that I've been thinking about minds. If the pattern is real -- if the job is what matters and the material is interchangeable -- then it seems at least conceivable that something not made of brain-stuff could do whatever brains do, and I gather some people have argued roughly this, though I don't know the details or the objections. If instead the material matters essentially, that avenue closes. So my final question: whatever this pattern is called, does it actually bear on the question of what a mind is, or am I connecting two things that only look related? I'd rather have a careful carving-up of the phenomenon than a bare label.
