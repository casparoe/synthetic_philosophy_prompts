In working through the fifth book of Boethius's Consolation of Philosophy, I have been collecting the main responses to the problem of reconciling divine foreknowledge with human freedom. Here is my list so far:

(1) Foreknowledge without causation: God's knowing in advance that I will do something does not cause me to do it, any more than my watching you act causes you to act; knowledge as such adds no compulsion, so simple foreknowledge leaves freedom intact. (2) The Boethian eternity solution: God is not in time at all but apprehends the whole of history in a single eternal present, so strictly speaking he does not foreknow my choice — he sees it the way I see a present event, and seeing a present event does not necessitate it. (3) The Ockhamist distinction between hard and soft facts about the past: that God believed yesterday that I would act a certain way tomorrow is arguably a soft fact about yesterday, still within my power to affect, so acting otherwise would not require altering a fixed past. (4) Middle knowledge: God knows, prior to creation, what every possible creature would freely do in every possible circumstance, and actualizes a world accordingly; his knowledge of my choice no more constrains it than a friend's perfect prediction of my behavior would. (5) The denial of determinate truth for future contingents: statements about my future free actions are as yet neither true nor false, so there is nothing about them to be known, and omniscience need only extend to what is knowable.

I suspect there is at least one further response to the problem that this list omits. Please supply one. It must be genuinely distinct — not a rewording, special case, or combination of the five above — and it may come from any period or tradition, historical or contemporary, provided you can state it precisely. Present your answer as exactly four numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (i) the response, named and stated; (ii) how it is meant to dissolve the tension between foreknowledge and freedom; (iii) why it does not collapse into any of the five responses on my list; and (iv) your assessment of whether it succeeds.
