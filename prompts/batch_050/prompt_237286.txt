Like many longtime readers of rationalist blogs and forums, I've absorbed the Boltzmann brain problem second-hand: in a universe that persists long enough, thermal or vacuum fluctuations should produce vastly more short-lived brains with fabricated memories than evolved observers, so if I'm a typical observer I should expect to be a fluctuation — an expectation my next second of coherent experience seems to refute. Everyone agrees this is a reductio of *something*; no one seems to agree what. Before reading another proposed fix, I want the spec first. Write the desiderata an adequate response to the Boltzmann brain problem must meet to count as solving it rather than deferring it, with the consideration behind each. In particular, the list should address: whether a fix may rest on contingent cosmology (no eternal inflation, a decaying vacuum, a finite future) or must work regardless of which empirical picture turns out true; whether it must explain why *I* am not a fluctuation, or merely arrange that most observers aren't; whether the reductio itself — that a theory predicting I'm almost certainly a brain undermines my evidence for that theory — is sound, or may be rejected as part of the solution; and whether a fix that makes fluctuating brains rare on average but dominant in some epochs suffices. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific, flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, and identify the single requirement that eliminates the most existing proposals.
