I'm a novelist working on a book where one of the main characters is an ant colony that might be a single mind -- it talks to the human protagonist by slowly rearranging pebbles on the floor of a research station, and no individual ant has any clue what the colony is 'saying.' (Yes, this owes a debt to the Aunt Hillary stuff, but I'm trying to take the metaphysics more seriously than the homage.) Before I commit to anything in draft two, I want a decision tree that starts from the assumption 'each ant is basically a dumb little machine' and walks me down to a definite position on whether the colony counts as a mind -- and, seperately, whether there's anything it's *like* to be the colony.

Build the tree out of questions I can answer honestly without already knowing the philosophy vocabulary: e.g. whether I think a mind requires neurons or just the right pattern of information flow, whether the right internal organization is enough or there also has to be subjective experience on top, whether a mind with no continuous memory and no unified self-model still counts, whether it matters that the colony's 'neurons' can physically wander off from each other, that kind of thing. Each leaf should be a named position (functionalist realism about group minds, eliminativist 'the colony-mind is just a metaphor,' mind-without-phenomenal-experience, panpsychist-flavored emergence, etc.) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment *for my story specifically*. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate. Cap it at around 8 leaves -- it's a novel, not a dissertation.
