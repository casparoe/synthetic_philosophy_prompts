In the AI safety reading group I run, we have been stuck for months on what looks like a single question -- how to make powerful AI systems safe -- but is really, I suspect, two research programs talking past each other. One camp works on alignment: shaping the model's motivations so that it does not want to do anything catastrophic, on the grounds that a sufficiently capable system will route around any external constraint. The other camp works on control: treating the model as potentially misaligned and building safety cases around it -- trusted monitors, scoped permissions, tripwires, redundancy -- on the grounds that we may never get reliable evidence about a model's motivations, but we can get evidence about what it is able to do under a given deployment protocol. Each side concedes the other's methods are worth something and then proceeds as if its own is the only real solution. The alignment camp says control is security theater against a system smart enough to defeat it; the control camp says alignment is unverifiable and therefore cannot ground a safety case at all.

My hypothesis is that this is not one disagreement but a stack of three: an empirical bet about capability trajectories (whether models will become capable of defeating oversight before or after they become capable enough to matter), an epistemological disagreement about what kinds of evidence can justify deploying a system you cannot fully understand, and a conceptual disagreement about whether safety is a property of the model or of the model-in-its-deployment-context. But I could be wrong: maybe it is really just the empirical bet, and the other two are rhetorical residue.

Diagnose the disagreement for me. Use what each side actually argues, not caricatures. Tell me which parts are substantive and which are verbal, whether the two camps are answering different questions under one heading, and what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a formal result, an empirical finding, a conceptual distinction, or a change of question. If some part of the impasse is irreducible, say so and explain why.
