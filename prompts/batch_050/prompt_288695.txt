Plutarch famously said he was writing lives, not histories, and that a throwaway remark or a small joke can reveal more about a person's character than their great battles and sieges. Suppose he turned up today. What do you think he'd make of the raw material modern life hands to biograhers — a person's tweets, group chats, search history, saved playlists? Would he treat this stuff as a goldmine for exactly the kind of character-reading he prided himself on, or would he see a problem with it — say, that it's all surface with no judgement applied, or that people perform differently once they know every casual utterance is on record forever? And would he approve of griefbots (AI chatbots trained on a dead person's messages, so the bereaved can keep talking to them) as a continuation of his own project in the Parallel Lives — keeping exemplary or cautionary figures around so the living can go on learning from them? Where exactly would the analogy break down for him? Please answer in British English.
