For several years I have been collecting instances of a pattern I cannot find a proper name for, and I would like to know whether one exists. Three examples, in increasing order of abstraction. First: a coastal village gets written up in travel pieces as unspoiled; visitors come precisely because it is unspoiled; within a decade it is indistinguishable from every other resort town, and the people who loved it move on to the next unspoiled place. Second: a small online forum develops an unusually patient and substantive culture; its reputation draws newcomers; the regulars who produced that culture drift away because the tone has changed, and the thing that attracted the newcomers no longer exists by the time they arrive. Third: a trail is prized for the solitude it offers, and the more people hear that it offers solitude, the less of it there is.

My question is whether this is simply the tragedy of the commons or something genuinely different. In the textbook commons -- a fishery, a grazing pasture -- the resource is a stock that shrinks with use, but each unit of use is like every other: a fish caught is a fish gone, and the fish that remain are unchanged fish. In my cases, use does not merely subtract from a stock; it alters the character of the good for everyone, and part of what gets consumed is the scarcity itself -- the quiet, the unspoiledness, the smallness of the community. The value depended on other people staying away.

So: is there an established concept for goods whose value partly consists in their not being widely consumed, where consumption degrades the very quality that attracted the consumption? If there is, I want more than the label. Tell me where it sits relative to the tragedy of the commons, to congestion effects, and to goods whose exclusivity is the whole point (where the buyer never cared about the underlying thing at all) -- my cases seem to differ from all three, and I want the differences stated precisely, with boundary cases: does a crowded highway count? A popular national park? An obscure band that signs to a major label? I would also like to know what follows for remedies. Standard commons governance assumes a stock to be preserved and allocates rights over it; does any of that apparatus apply when the "stock" is partly constituted by the absence of other users, so that preserving it means keeping it unknown?

Two requests about how you answer. If I am conflating two or three different phenomena here, or if one of my instances does not actually belong with the others, say so plainly rather than coining or borrowing a label that papers over the difference. And if the honest answer is that nothing established fits, say that, and then propose a term with a definition sharp enough to exclude the near misses.
