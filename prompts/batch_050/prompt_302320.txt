I am putting together the reading list for a graduate seminar on theories of concepts, and I am trying to catalogue the standard objections to prototype theory before we move on to exemplar and theory-theory alternatives. My current list of objections is as follows. (1) The compositionality objection: prototypes do not combine in the right way, so the prototype of 'pet fish' is neither a prototypical pet nor a prototypical fish, which suggests the prototype of a complex concept cannot be derived from the prototypes of its parts. (2) The dissociation objection: typicality ratings and categorization judgments come apart -- people rate penguins as atypical birds yet will insist, without hedging, that a penguin is definitely a bird -- so whatever typicality measures, it is not concept membership. (3) The expertise objection: prototypes vary across individuals and subcultures (a birdwatcher's prototype differs from a child's), but if concepts were prototypes this variation would make communication and shared inference mysteriously difficult. (4) The inference objection: prototype structure does not explain why conceptual inference and categorization have the all-or-nothing character they appear to have in reasoning, definition-seeking, and argument. (5) The empty-shell objection: saying a concept is a prototype amounts to saying it is a weighted list of features, but nothing in the theory constrains which features may appear on the list or where the weights come from, so the theory inherits whatever explains those things and explains nothing itself.

Here is my request. Give me one more objection to prototype theory that is not on this list and is not a restatement, special case, or conjunction of any item on it. In particular, I am not looking for another compositionality example beyond the pet-fish family, another experimental dissociation between typicality and categorization, or another appeal to variability across knowers. Ideally the objection should target a commitment of prototype theory that items (1)–(5) leave untouched. After stating it, assess it honestly: is it a serious problem for the view, and could a sophisticated prototype theorist absorb it with minor adjustments? If it turns out the best remaining objection is weak or covertly collapses into one of the five above, say so and explain why, rather than inventing a spurious sixth objection for the sake of the exercise.
