Just got out of a two-hour argument with a friend and I'm still annoyed, so help me stress-test my side. I was defending Dennett's line on intentionality. The example, roughly: a vending machine has a component whose job, given how it was designed, is to detect quarters -- but ship the machine somewhere that people feed it different coins of the same size and weight, and ask what that inner state is "really" about. Dennett says there's no deeper fact to find; content is just functional role plus design history, and the same goes for us, with natural selection playing the engineer. So-called original intentionality is a myth -- ours is as derived as the machine's. My friend thinks this is obviously backwards: the machine only means anything relative to us, while her thoughts mean things full stop.

What would actually count against my position? Give me the exposure map: which arguments or cases would be decisive versus merely awkward, what defenders of the view themselves concede as real threats (not just what critics say), what it predicts that a Searle-style view doesn't, and whether it's been qualified into unfalsifiability, since "there's no fact of the matter here" can be an answer to everything. Personally I'd be most moved by a case where the view is forced into a content verdict that breaks something else we need content for -- misrepresentation, say, or mental causation. One variant we fought over: swap the fixed sensor for a detector that keeps recalibrating itself to whatever coins circulate locally. Does the content drift with it? I said yes, definately, and my friend said that answer alone shows the view is dead.
