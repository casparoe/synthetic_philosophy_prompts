I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I keep getting stuck on a corner of the Sleeping Beauty / self-locating belief stuff that my philosopher friends hand-wave as just anthropics. Setup I have in mind: Beauty knows that whatever choice she makes on Monday will be copied by every later awakening that is phenomenally identical to this one. So if the coin lands tails and she wakes twice with amnesia, the same decision gets executed twice; if heads, once. Now vary only the bookkeeping, not the world. In version A, a donation is charged once per actual awakening, so choosing give on Monday costs more in the tails world because there are two indistinguishable givers. In version B, the donation is charged once per coin-branch, so the number of awakenings changes nothing except how sure Beauty should be about where she is. Same evidence at the moment of choice, same character, same causal levers, but the rational verdict seems to slide depending on whether you count decision-moments, observer-moments, branches, or copies-that-will-remember-having-decided.

Is there an established name for this exact seam: cases where duplication changes the epistemic weight of an action without changing its causal reach, so thirder vs halfer reasoning, evidential vs causal decision theory, and person/count-based ethics all start pulling apart? I definately don't want a bare label. Tell me the nearest concepts, why none of them quite fit if that's your view, and give the thing a clean definition with a couple of boundary cases: when does an extra amnesic copy count as another chooser, when is it just another place the same choice shows up, and what changes if the copies can leave messages for each other but can't verify them until after the payoff is locked?
