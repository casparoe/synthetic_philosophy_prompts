Write a deadpan press release, roughly 600 words, from a fictional AI lab announcing that its new flagship model has been "certified aligned" ahead of public deployment. The humor must come from the real practices and tensions of prosaic alignment work rather than invented absurdities: RLHF that makes models pleasant rather than honest, sycophancy, red-teaming done by the lab on its own model, alignment evaluations the model may know it is undergoing, the gap between a model that won't help you build a bomb and a model that won't want to. Keep the marketing register perfectly straight throughout — no winking, no disclaimers, no admission that anything is wrong. The irony should be legible purely through euphemism, careful wording, and conspicuous gaps: a reader who follows alignment discourse should be able to reconstruct from the announcement what was tested, what wasn't, and what the lab quietly declined to define. The model itself may be quoted, but only in language that sounds reassuring in the press release and faintly ominous to a safety-minded reader. Aim for a piece where the target is clearly the style of discourse and the optimism of current methods, not any real company — and where a specialist would judge the satire fair.
