So I'm drafting a first-contact novel and my writing-group friend Dae has strong opinions about which alien-contact plots are "actually realistic" under dark forest logic -- you know, the idea that the galaxy is quiet because anyone who makes noise gets killed, so everyone hides or shoots first. His rulings so far:

- A civilization that broadcasts greetings to the stars gets wiped out. Realistic, obviously.
- A civilization that goes totally silent, no emissions at all, survives for ages. Realistic.
- A civ detects a nearby peer civ, roughly equal tech level, and strikes first without even trying to talk. Realistic, he'd say talking can't resolve the suspicion.
- A civ detects a stone-age planet a few light years off and just leaves it alone. Realistic, they're not worth the energy.
- A civ detects a fast-growing civilization on the far side of the galaxy and launches a relativistic strike at it anyway. Realistic.

Here's my plot problem. In my draft, one civilization stumbles on another that's been technologically frozen for like ten thousand years -- they uploaded into virtual paradises, stopped expanding, stopped inventing, their emissions are a faint steady trickle. No growth, no ambitions, apparently no threat to anyone, ever. But they do sit on a juicy star system, and their stasis is only *apparent* -- you can't exactly peek inside to confirm they'll never wake up hungry.

Would Dae call it realistic if my detecting civilization strikes them, or if it leaves them be? More than the yes/no, I want to know what rule his five verdicts above are actually running, because I think at least two or three different rules fit them -- like "shoot anything that could ever outgrow you" versus "shoot whatever competes for your patch of space" versus "shoot anything you can't verify is harmless" -- and they seem to split on my frozen-civ case. Tell me which rule fits him best and why, and give me one more hypothetical verdict I could pitch at him that would settle which rule it is.

Keep it under 350 words, I have a deadline and a short attention span.
