A coworker and I got into it over lunch about whether I should feel bad about my job. I do backend work at a company whose ad-targeting stuff I think is genuinely harmful (not illegal, just bad). He walked me into this argument and I can't find the exit:

(1) You're complicit in some wrongdoing only if your participation makes a difference to it -- i.e., had you not participated, the wrong would have been smaller, or less likely, or wouldn't have happened at all.

(2) If I quit tomorrow they backfill my role within a month and everything I would've built gets built anyway, on roughly the same timeline.

(3) So my working there makes no difference to the wrongdoing.

(4) Therefore I'm not complicit.

That already bugged me, but then he pointed out the same arguement shape covers basically everything: my one vote never swings an election, my personal emissions don't change the climate, and even a getaway driver who knew three other drivers were on call "makes no difference" either. So it seems like nobody is ever complicit in any large-scale harm, as long as each individual contribution is redundant. That excuses literally everyone involved in every collective bad thing, which can't be right.

Which step do I reject? I suspect (1) is the weak link but I also kind of believe it -- if I genuinely can't affect the outcome, in what sense am I "participating" in it at all? If I have to give something up I'd rather keep (2) (it's just empirically true about my situation) and reject whatever saves the idea that complicity is real, but I want to know what that costs. E.g. does dropping (1) mean I'm complicit in every bad thing done by any company I buy products from?
