Every activity I've found online just has students discuss cases where theories disagree, which they treat as a normal "which theory is right?" debate. I want something where they actually *feel* the missing-common-scale problem in their bones before I ever name it.

Design me a structured game or simulation with concrete rules and roles. Rough idea of what I'm hoping for (feel free to restructure): students in small groups each "run" a different moral theory with its own internal scoring currency, and then get forced into a joint decision under uncertainty about which theory is true, so they have to allocate some limited resource without any exchange rate between the currencies. Ideally they should stumble into the classic moves themselves -- just averaging the numbers, doing a parliament-style vote, picking a compromise option nobody endorses -- and discover why each move is fishy, rather than me lecturing about it.

Give me: setup and rules precise enough that I could run it cold, a rough timeline for the 80 minutes, and the debrief -- which moments in the game map onto which parts of the philosophical problem, what will predictably go wrong when I run it (e.g. students just negotiating a social compromise instead of engaging the epistemic problem), and an honest note about which feature of real moral uncertainty the game can't reproduce.
