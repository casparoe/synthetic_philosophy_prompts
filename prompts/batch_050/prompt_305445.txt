So I manage a warehouse team of about thirty people, and corporate just rolled out this whole "people analytics" thing where everyone has individual metrics (pick rates, error rates, idle time) tracked automatically, and bonuses plus write-ups get generated basically off the dashboard with minimal human involvement. At the same time, the same company is pushing a big "culture and values" initiative, like posters about how we're a family and trust and integrity are what make us great. The combination struck me as weird, and then a friend who reads old Chinese philosophy stuff lent me a translation of the Han Feizi and I got completely sucked in, especially the chapter "The Two Handles" where he says rulers should govern purely through punishment and reward and never let anyone else hold those levers.

The part I keep re-reading is this bit from chapter VII (W.K. Liao's translation): "Whenever a minister utters a word, the ruler should in accordance with his word assign him a task to accomplish, and in accordance with the task call the work to account. If the work corresponds with the task, and the task corresponds with the word, he should be rewarded. On the contrary, if the work is not equivalent to the task, and the task not equivalent to the word, he should be punished. Accordingly, any minister whose word is big but whose work is small should be punished. Not that the work is small, but that the work is not equivalent to the name. Again, any minister whose word is small but whose work is big should also be punished. Not that big work is not desirable but that the discrepancy between the work and the name is worse than the accomplishment of the big work." The part about punishing someone for EXCEEDING their target honestly broke my brain a little.

So here's what I want: walk me through what Han Feizi would probably think of my company's setup if he could see it. Some things I'm stuck on: (1) Would he love the automated dashboard since it's pure reward-and-punishment with no favoritism, or would he hate that we've basically handed "the handles" over to an algorithm and to the HR people who designed it, which is exactly the minister-usurps-the-ruler move he warns about? (2) The passage above seems like it would condemn stuff my best workers do all the time, like quietly fixing problems outside their job description or helping a slower coworker (which tanks their personal numbers but helps the warehouse). Is his point that that's actually corrosive, and if so, is he right? And (3) that same passage also seems to be a machine for producing sandbagging and metric-gaming -- my people already set easy goals so they can hit them exactly. Did he have an answer to that, or is this the one place where the whole incentive-scheme falls apart? Also curious where the cheesy "we're a family" values stuff fits in, since I suspect he'd have views on rulers who lean on virtue-talk. Don't just give me "he'd say incentives good" -- I want the tensions he himself would feel.
