For a paper I am drafting on musical expressiveness, I would like your credence in the following proposition: some passages of purely instrumental music are literally sad — that is, 'sad' is a true predicate of the music itself, and not merely shorthand for the music's disposition to make typical listeners sad, nor a loose metaphor. Please decompose your credence rather than giving a bare number. As a starting structure, give me (i) your probability that applying emotion predicates to music requires some non-arousalist vindication, so that resemblance, persona, or contour-style accounts are the live options; (ii) conditional on that, your probability that at least one such account succeeds in grounding genuinely literal predication rather than an elaborate 'as if'; and (iii) whatever further terms you think the calculation needs — for instance, the probability that the literal/metaphorical boundary here is determinate enough for the proposition to have a truth value at all. Give a number and one line of justification for each term, then show how they combine. Finally, tell me which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would shift it most.
