Whenever a new technology kicks off a row — gene drives, novel food additives, geoengineering, frontier AI — someone reaches for the drug approval analogy: we don't let a pharmaceutical company sell a pill until it's passed trials proving it's safe, so why should any other innovation be released first and tested on the public afterwards? Prove safety first, release second. I want you to audit this analogy rather than just nod at it. Give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of the drug, the patient, the trial, the placebo group, the regulator, the side effects — and then tell me where it breaks. The disanalogies I suspect matter most: a drug does nothing until someone swallows it, while plenty of technologies diffuse into the environment or the economy whether or not anyone "takes" them; there's no control group for a technology everyone is exposed to at once; and the drug frame makes delay look free in a way it isn't when the delayed thing is a beneficial technology. Separate the disanalogies that threaten the precautionary conclusion from the ones that are just cosmetic. Does "prove it safe before release" survive the audit, and if the drug frame can't carry it, is there a better analogy for what precaution should actually demand?
