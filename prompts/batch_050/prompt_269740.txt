I run the ethics committee for a hospital network that also operates a patient-facing scheduling app, and before I evaluate any more proposed accounts of consent, I want a specification to judge them against. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of valid consent must meet, given that its purpose is to tell an institution when it may treat someone's agreement as settling what may be done to them. The theory has to get certain cases right, so state each requirement together with the case behind it: it must explain why a surgeon who lies about how many times she has performed an operation, while stating its risks accurately, seems to compromise consent to surgery in a way that her lying about being good company does not; it must deliver a verdict on the courier app's weekly terms screen, which drivers must accept to keep receiving jobs, and say whether repeated acceptance under that background condition can count as consent to each new clause; it must handle fluctuating competence, as when someone with early dementia signs a research protocol on a lucid morning; it must say something about psychiatric advance directives, where a person consents now to treatment their later self will refuse; and it must explain where the line falls for consent to serious bodily harm, given that boxing and tattooing sit on one side of it. Beyond cases, name the distinctions the theory cannot collapse: competence, information, and voluntariness as separate failure modes; consent as an inner state of willingness versus consent as a public act that changes what others are permitted to do; and consent that waives a claim versus consent that grants a permission. State also the consistency constraints, including the threat-offer asymmetry, that a threat can coerce where an offer with identical consequences for refusal cannot, or else that the theory must explain why this asymmetry is an illusion. Then identify any pair of desiderata you judge to be jointly unsatisfiable, since that is the part I most want, and say which single requirement excludes the most accounts currently in circulation. Throughout, commit: where two demands conflict, say which is a genuine desideratum and which is a prejudice of one camp, and give your reasons rather than laying out options for me to choose among. Close by briefly assessing the two main families of view, consent as a mental state and consent as a public performance, against your own list. Do not use headings, bullet points, numbered lists, bold text, or tables; write the whole response as continuous prose, the way you would write a long letter.
