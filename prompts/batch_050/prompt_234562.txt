So here's the situation. I've been running a philosophy of mind reading group and we just spent six weeks on the original vs derived intentionality fight, Searle's Chinese Room on one side, Dennett on the other, and I want a diagnosis because honestly the debate looks frozen. Searle says: the man in the room shuffles symbols by rulebook and plainly understands no Chinese, so running the right program is never sufficient for understanding; syntax doesn't add up to semantics, and intentionality in artifacts is only ever derived from us. Dennett replies that this is an intuition pump that exploits how slow and fragmented the imagined simulation is, and he presses cases like his two-bitser: a coin acceptor built in the US to sort quarters gets shipped abroad and ends up triggered by some local coin of the same size and weight, and asking what its internal state REALLY refers to (quarters? the foreign coin?) has no deeper answer than what we or the environment select for. His claim is that our own intentionality is derived the same way, from evolution instead of engineers, so the original/derived line Searle needs doesn't exist. Meanwhile Searle happily concedes brains are machines, just meat machines with causal powers silicon lacks, which makes it sound like an empirical bet about substrate, except neither side seems to update on any actual neuroscience or AI result, which makes me doubt that reading.

Here's my hypothesis, treat it as a hypothesis: both sides accept the same facts about the case, and the real fight is about whether first-person obviousness ("I can see the man doesn't understand") counts as data at all, or whether understanding is entirely a third-person attributional matter. Maybe it's verbal in a specific way, two senses of 'understand' talking past each other. Or maybe there's a hidden empirical crux.
