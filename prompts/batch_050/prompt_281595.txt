I am a high school student preparing a research presentation on simultaneous invention, using Newton and Leibniz’s independent versions of calculus and Darwin and Wallace’s convergence on natural selection as my main examples. I already understand several standard explanations for why major discoveries sometimes occur almost at once:

1. Common foundations: earlier mathematics, observations, and conceptual problems make the same conclusion available to multiple researchers.
2. Enabling tools and evidence: new instruments, experiments, specimens, or bodies of data make a previously inaccessible discovery possible.
3. Social demand: governments, industries, scientific institutions, or intellectual communities reward solutions to a particular problem.
4. Many-searchers selection: large numbers of people pursue promising problems, but history remembers only those who succeed, making simultaneous successes seem more surprising than they are.
5. Hidden interdependence: supposedly independent researchers may be connected indirectly through correspondence, publications, assistants, teachers, or shared intellectual communities.
6. Convergent intellectual frameworks: researchers educated within the same paradigm ask similar questions and regard similar answers as legitimate.

Propose a seventh explanation that is not merely a rewording, special case, or combination of these. Give it a concise name, explain the underlying mechanism, and distinguish it from each explanation above. It should be plausible without depending on obscure historical claims or citations. Then test it against both Newton–Leibniz and Darwin–Wallace, allowing for the possibility that it explains one case better than the other.

Finally, use your proposed explanation to distinguish two claims: that the discovery itself was likely to be made by someone, and that it was likely to be made by the particular person who received credit. If the same result could have emerged later or through another researcher, explain why the inventor’s identity might still matter—for example, because it affects the result’s formulation, applications, reception, terminology, or institutional influence. End by stating honestly whether your seventh explanation is genuinely illuminating or whether it collapses into one of the six I listed.
