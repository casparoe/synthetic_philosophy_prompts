In a reading group I belong to, two members, Priya and Marcus, have spent two sessions arguing over the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I'd like a neutral adjudication. The setup, as everyone in the group understands it: Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday night and a fair coin is flipped out of her sight. If it lands heads, she is awakened on Monday only; if tails, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday, with her memory of the Monday awakening erased in between, so that no awakening feels distinguishable from any other. She knows all of this in advance. The question is what her credence that the coin landed heads should be upon awakening.

Priya opened with the sampling argument: across many runs of the experiment, two thirds of Beauty's awakenings occur in tails-worlds, so upon awakening she should reason as though she had been drawn at random from her awakenings and set her credence at one third. Marcus replied that Beauty learns nothing on awakening that she did not already know with certainty on Sunday — she knew she would be awakened at least once either way — and that information which is guaranteed regardless of how the coin lands cannot justify updating away from one half. Priya then offered a betting argument: suppose that at each awakening Beauty is offered a bet that wins her £10 if the coin landed heads and costs her £9 if it landed tails. On Marcus's credence of one half the bet has positive expected value, so his position has her accept it every time; yet across repeated runs of the experiment she accepts it once in heads-worlds and twice in tails-worlds, and so reliably loses money, about £4 per experiment on average. Marcus answered that the loss is an artefact of the bet being placed twice in tails-worlds, not a defect in the credence: the bet structure punishes the outcome that generates more awakenings, and no single awakening-agent is guaranteed a loss, so the apparent Dutch book fails. He then went on the offensive with a variant of his own: suppose the coin lands tails and Beauty is awakened a hundred times with erasure between awakenings, rather than twice. On Priya's sampling reasoning, Beauty must be about 100/101 confident in tails upon awakening — near-certain about a coin toss having seen nothing but the inside of the same room, on the strength of a fact she knew before she fell asleep would obtain either way. Priya bit the bullet: in the amplified variant near-certainty is correct, since any other policy would leave her wrong at the overwhelming majority of her awakenings. She also pointed out that an outsider told only that Beauty is awake somewhere in the experiment would conditionalise to one third, and Marcus's position has Beauty assign a different credence from someone with identical relevant evidence. Marcus's last move was a reflection argument: on Sunday night everyone agrees the credence is one half, and Beauty knows exactly what her epistemic situation on Monday will be; if Priya is right, Beauty already knows tonight that her Monday self will be at one third despite receiving no evidence she does not already possess, so either she should shift to one third tonight — which even Priya denies — or the thirder is committed to rational credences drifting through the mere passage of time and scheduled memory management. Priya's rejoinder, briefly, was that principles about trusting one's future self presuppose that the future self is better informed, which anticipated memory loss breaks.

Could you go through the exchange move by move — the sampling argument, the no-new-evidence reply, the betting argument, Marcus's response to it, the hundred-awakenings variant, Priya's bullet-biting and her outsider argument, and finally the reflection argument and rejoinder — and say who is right on each specific point, rather than merely declaring a winner overall? In particular, I'd like to know which move you think is the weakest on each side, what if anything both are missing, and where the disagreement genuinely bottoms out: is it really about whether self-locating information counts as evidence on a par with ordinary evidence, about what credences are for, or about something else entirely?
