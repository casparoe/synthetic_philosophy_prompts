So I've been circling the parental-licensing debate and the kid's-right-to-an-open-future stuff (Feinberg, and LaFollette's old argument that if we screen adoptive parents we should screen biological ones too), and I want to figure out what I actually think, because my gut verdicts don't obviously add up. Here they are:

1. Jehovah's Witness parents refuse a blood transfusion for their six-year-old, who will very likely die without it. The state should override. No hesitation.
2. Parents in a religious community pull their daughter out of school at twelve because girls in their community are expected to marry early and run households. The state should force them to keep her in school.
3. An Amish-style community takes kids out after eighth grade to work the farm and learn the community's way of life. I think this should be allowed, even though it plainly narrows those kids' options.
4. Deaf parents use IVF embryo selection to deliberately have a deaf child because they want the child to share deaf culture. I don't think this should be illegal. It makes me uncomfortable, but not should-be-banned uncomfortable.
5. Parents teach their kids at home that another race is inferior. Vile, but it has to be legal — the state can't police dinner-table talk.
6. School vaccine mandates: fine. Actually removing kids from anti-vax parents: way too far.
7. Rigorous screening of adoptive parents: obviously right. The identical screening imposed on biological parents: deeply wrong, even though the child's interest is the same in both cases. I know this one looks inconsistent, that's kind of the point of including it.
8. Banning smoking in the car with kids in it: fine.

The pair I really can't reconcile is 2 and 3 — in both, adults are closing off a child's future in the name of communal values, and "eighth grade vs. twelve" feels like alot of weight to put on four years.

My tentative principle was: parents may shape a child's life but may not irreversibly close off major ranges of options before the child can choose for herself, plus maybe some harm threshold bolted on. But 4 breaks it (choosing a deaf embryo forecloses hearing, which is a major range of options, yet I still don't want it banned), 8 seems to be about harm rather than options at all, and I can't even tell what the principle says about 7, since licensing isn't about shaping a child you have.

So here's what I want: a principle — or two candidate principles if one won't do it — stated precisely enough that I could apply it myself to new cases. For each clause, tell me which of my verdicts is doing the motivating work. Then tell me what your principle predicts for two cases I haven't listed: parents who raise their kid speaking only a dying minority language, so the kid grows up semi-isolated from the wider society; and parents who conceive a second child partly to be a bone marrow donor for their sick first child, with the donation happening when the donor is five. And be honest at the end about which of my eight verdicts your principle can't save, and which single verdict you'd tell me to drop if I had to drop exactly one.
