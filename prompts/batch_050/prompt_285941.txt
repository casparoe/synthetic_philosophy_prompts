I'm writing a paper that treats the Formula of Universal Law as a test to be engineered rather than a text to be exegeted, and before I defend my own version I want the specification. Write the desiderata that any working reconstruction of Kant's universalizability test must meet: which cases it has to get right (the lying promise, at minimum, and I'd add refusing beneficence), which failure modes it has to be protected against (overly specific maxims that universalize trivially, overly generic ones that fail innocuously, immoral maxims that pass because the agent conveniently exempts himself by description), and which distinctions it must preserve (contradiction in conception versus contradiction in will, testing a maxim versus condemning an action token, the test's output versus its authority). Give the case or pressure motivating each requirement, and say which you take to be non-negotiable and which reflect the commitments of a particular interpretive camp. Then the part I care most about: tell me which pairs of desiderata look jointly unsatisfiable -- I'm suspicious that making the test sensitive enough to catch the egoist and insensitive enough to spare the harmless maxim are in real tension -- and which single requirement eliminates the most reconstructions currently on offer.
