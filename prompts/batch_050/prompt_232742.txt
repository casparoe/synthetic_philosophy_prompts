so i've been lurking on LW and ACX threads for years and there's this analogy that keeps coming up whenever self-deception gets discussed: the conscious mind is like a government's press secretary. the actual decisions get made elsewhere in the bureaucracy, and the press secretary's job is to stand at the podium and give confident explanations it often just invented, and -- this is the part people add -- the secretary doesn't even know it's spinning. it believes its own briefings.

people use this to dismiss the classic puzzle about self-deception, which is that deceiving yourself seems to require simultaneously knowing the truth (so you can hide it) and not knowing it (so the hiding works), which looks impossible. the press secretary move supposedly dissolves the paradox: no single thing both knows and doesn't know, it's just different departments.

i'm not convinced the analogy actually does that work, and i want an audit. give me the explicit mapping first: what plays the role of the president/cabinet, the press pool, the classified documents, the secretary "believing the briefing." then tell me where it breaks down. some specific things i keep chewing on:

- a real press secretary has an audience that is genuinely other people. in the self-deception case, who is the audience? if the "press release" is just for internal consumption, is that still deception in any interesting sense, or have we just redescribed motivated reasoning?
- press secretaries occasionally do find out they were lied to, and then they get angry and resign. the analogy seems to predict self-deceivers should be able to catch themselves in a way they empirically can't, or at least not straightforwardly. is that a disanalogy or a feature?
- does the modular picture actually avoid the paradox, or does it just relocate it -- because someone has to decide which documents get classified and which get briefed, and if that someone "knows," aren't we back to a unified deceiver?

lastly: if the press secretary analogy fails, is there a better one (corporate accounting? a search engine optimizing for engagement rather than accuracy?), and does the surviving picture let genuine self-deception exist, or does it collapse into just "bias" and "compartmentalization"? i suspect the paradox survives in a thinner form but i can't pin down where.
