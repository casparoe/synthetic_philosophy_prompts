I’m a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and my team is building an app called MoodLoop. It uses typing cadence, sleep patterns, and voice metadata to flag elevated risk of depressive episodes, social anxiety, or ADHD-like attention problems, then offers coping exercises. I recently read a short summary of Ian Hacking’s “looping effect,” which I took to mean that psychiatric classifications can change the people being classified because they interpret and reshape themselves through the label.

Our product lead thinks this makes the whole classifier scientifically incoherent: if its output changes the person being measured, then the phenomenon being measured no longer has a stable target. Her proposed fix is a “loop-free” mode that gives users no feedback, hides every label, and records only aggregate outcomes, so the diagnosis definately can’t leak back into the phenomenon.

I’m not sure that follows. Since looping supposedly means psychiatric labels create or at least validate disorders, could we use that mechanism therapeutically? For example, could we classify someone as having “resilient-depressive tendencies” — an intentionally new label meaning they are vulnerable but good at recovering — so they loop into a healthier identity? If not, where exactly is my reasoning going wrong? And for the app, is the better response to eliminate feedback, make the labels private, monitor label-induced drift, or treat the intervention itself as part of what is being predicted?

Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
