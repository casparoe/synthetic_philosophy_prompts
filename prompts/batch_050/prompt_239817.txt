I'm a science journalist, and last month I published a long feature arguing that the hole argument in general relativity settles one of the oldest questions in the philosophy of space and time: spacetime points, taken as entities over and above the material events and metrical relations located at them, do not exist. My piece reconstructs the argument for a general audience roughly as follows: because the field equations of general relativity are invariant under smooth rearrangements of the points of the manifold, a theory committed to the independent reality of those points must count infinitely many physically identical descriptions as genuinely distinct possible worlds, and this multiplies possibilities in a way that makes the theory's own determinism collapse inside a region of empty space. I then argued that the only intellectually honest response is to accept that mathematical descriptions differing only by such a rearrangement describe one and the same physical situation, and that the substantivalist's stage without actors is a fiction we've been taking seriously for four centuries out of sheer inertia. I closed by connecting this to the way quantum gravity researchers talk about spacetime as emergent, presenting the hole argument as the first inkling that spacetime was never fundamental.

To my surprise, the philosophy of physics department at a nearby university has invited me to present the piece at their colloquium. I know my audience, roughly: mostly working philosophers of physics, including at least one defender of what I believe is called sophisticated substantivalism -- the view that one can keep spacetime points as real while still treating the rearranged descriptions as the same possibility -- plus several younger physicists from the quantum gravity group who, I gather, regard the whole dispute as a confusion that dissolves once you properly understand gauge redundancy. There will be no one in the room who needs the argument explained from scratch, and no one sympathetic to pop-science framing for its own sake.

Prepare my Q&A prep sheet.
