For the avoidance of doubt: by the asymmetry argument I mean the claim that the presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure good, while the absence of pain is good even if no one exists to enjoy it, but the absence of pleasure is not bad unless someone exists to be deprived of it -- and that this asymmetry makes coming into existence always a harm, on balance, compared with never existing. Don't quote Benatar verbatim, just work with the idea.

The document should have: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can genuinely sign -- about what the asymmetry is and isn't, about what it would and wouldn't imply for people who already exist, about which common objections on both sides are just bad (I suspect both would agree that "but life is mostly nice for most people" and "so we should all kill ourselves" are both misses, but you tell me); (2) a disagreements table where each residual disagreement is stated precisely and in wording each side would accept for its own position -- my guess is one real crux is whether the asymmetry itself is intuitive or is an artefact of comparing existence with a blank, and another is whether "harm" can apply to someone without a victim in the comparison world, but push back on my guesses if they're wrong; (3) for each crux, what both sides would accept as bearing on it -- an argument form, a thought experiment, an analogy -- with each side's prediction written down in advance.

A few requests about how to do it. Take your time and think carefully before drafting; brainstorm candidate agreed claims first and bin the ones that are vacuous or slanted. If part of the dispute strikes you as verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why, in the document itself.
