As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I’m less interested in whether Goodman’s grue is linguistically natural in the abstract than in how someone distinguishes lawful projectibility from a suspiciously constructed reference class. A friend of mine has the following pattern of verdicts about which generalizations may reasonably be extended to unexamined cases:

- She accepts that unexamined copper wires conduct electricity, given extensive tests of other copper wires.
- She accepts that unexamined emeralds are green, given extensive examination of other emeralds.
- She accepts that samples of a particular enzyme will denature above 80°C, even though none has yet been heated that much, because independent physical and chemical evidence supports the prediction.
- She rejects the Goodmanian claim that all emeralds are grue, where grue means green if first examined before January 1, 2030, and blue otherwise.
- She rejects the inference from many observed white swans to all swans being white.
- She rejects the inference from three silver coins in a jacket pocket to every coin in that pocket being silver, treating the sample as too small and the population as too heterogeneous.

Now consider a new case. A cryogenic archive contains thousands of vials of the same bacterial strain, and every vial tested so far has contained viable bacteria. Independent biochemical evidence strongly establishes that the preservative will remain effective until January 1, 2030, then abruptly decompose, after which the bacteria will rapidly die. Call a vial vivile if it contains viable bacteria when tested before that date and nonviable bacteria when tested afterward.

Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: identify at least three different rules that fit my friend’s earlier verdicts; state what each rule predicts about the claim that every vial in the archive is vivile; give your best prediction of her actual verdict, including your reasoning and rough confidence; describe one further hypothetical verdict that would best discriminate among your candidate rules; and distinguish what she would probably say from what she should say if she were being maximally consistent with her own precedents.
