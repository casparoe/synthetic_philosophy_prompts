So my book club just finished A Tale of Two Cities and somehow I got voluntold to lead the discussion, and I want to go a bit deeper than "wasn't Sydney Carton noble." Here's the thing. While reading I fell down a wikipedia hole about how historians argue over the French Revolution -- roughly whether the Terror was the inevitable result of class hatred building up for centuries, or whether it was driven by ideology and paranoia, or whether it was basically contingency (the war going badly, the king's flight to Varennes, a series of panics) and could have gone otherwise. And it struck me that the novel seems to be picking a side in that fight, but I cant quite tell which one.

So read the novel against that debate. Some of what I'm chewing on: Madame Defarge -- is she the class-hatred reading made into a person (her family was destroyed by the Evremondes, so of course she wants them all dead, even the innocent ones), or is she Dickens's warning about what happens when grievance hardens into ideology? Darnay is condemned for a name and a bloodline he explicitly rejected, which seems like a point for the "the machine just runs on its own logic now" view. And that old saying about revolutions devouring their children -- does it actually fit the book? The guillotine in Dickens mostly eats bystanders and scapegoats (the seamstress!) rather than rival revolutionaries, so maybe he's describing something different. Also the opening, the spilled wine cask in Saint Antoine with everyone scrambling to drink it -- is that Dickens saying the violence was baked in from the start, i.e. the structural reading?

Where does the book resist whichever reading you give it -- I don't want a tidy Dickens-was-a-Marxist or Dickens-was-a-conservative answer. And one more thing: Carton's sacrifice at the end, with his vision of a future where the revolution's fury burns itself out. Does an individual moral act like that even register in a structural story about class forces, or is Dickens smuggling in a whole different theory of what moves history?
