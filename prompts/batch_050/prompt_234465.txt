so last month i joined a political party for the first time in my life. nothing grand -- my friend Priya is running for city council and around here basically nothing (ballot access, endorsements, volunteers, money) happens unless you go through the local party. two meetings in i caught myself arguing for a transit position i don't actually believe because that was the line we'd agreed to take, everyone nodded, and it felt *good*, which is honestly the part that weirds me out. my brother, who is insufferable, sent me Simone Weil's essay "On the Abolition of All Political Parties" (written 1943, only published after she died, I read the Simon Leys translation) with a note that just said "this is you now." one passage keeps nagging at me:

"There was, however, a Club des Jacobins; at first it merely provided an arena for free debate. Its subsequent transformation was by no means inevitable; it was only under the double pressure of war and the guillotine that it eventually turned into a totalitarian party. [...] Thus, in Continental Europe, totalitarianism was the original sin of all political parties."

i want to fight her about this and you're going to play her. ground rules: you are Simone Weil, author of that essay, speaking in the first person, and you believe every word of it. you may concede small points -- coordination is a real problem, your positive alternative is underdeveloped, whatever -- but you never concede that political parties are a legitimate or necessary institution. you're not a caricature though: you've heard the objections and thought about them harder than i have. talk like her: austere, exact, no fluff, and keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like a real argument. you're allowed -- encouraged, even -- to interrogate me about how my party actually functions, since that's your method. it's 1943 for you, so when i mention things from after your lifetime, describe them back in your own terms rather than pretending you know the details. stay in character until i say "stop."

my opening: your whole "original sin" move generalizes from one club that radicalized *under war and the guillotine* -- your words, those are the pressures that did it. my party squabbles about bike lanes and weed ordinances. so isn't totalitarianism a contingent accident of revolutionary France and not the essence of parties as such? you even admit the Jacobin club "at first merely provided an arena for free debate" -- well, that's all i thought i was joining. and second, the practical one: parties are how ordinary people pool money and bodies against candidates who already have both. abolish them and Priya's race is just her against a developer-backed incumbent with a billboard budget. you'd replace parties with what, exactly? a politics of pure individuals sounds like a politics where the rich and famous win every time. what do you say?
