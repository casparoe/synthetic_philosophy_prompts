Please argue that revisionist just war theory cant really stop at rejecting the moral equality of combatants. If unjust combatants are liable to defensive harm because they pose unjust threats, then the same liability logic seems to sweep in lots of civilians who knowingly contribute to an unjust war (munitions workers, arms dealers, taxpayers, maybe even enthusiastic voters), so the combatant/noncombatant distinction collapses along with the equality of combatants. You should anticipate the obvious replies -- e.g. that liability requires some minimum threshold of causal contribution, or that necessity and proportionality constraints keep civilians protected in practice -- and argue that they dont actually do enough work to save the traditional distinction.
