I'm a novelist, which is relevant here only because everyone in my family thinks it gives them license to say my life is "material." Two years ago my older brother published a memoir that included, thinly disguised, the worst period of my life -- my divorce, a miscarriage I'd told exactly three people about, stuff I'd shared with him in confidence as his sister, not as a source. The book did well. He's never apologized; his line is that the writing "transmuted" it and that art has its own rules, and honestly I don't think he even believes he did anything wrong. I stopped speaking to him.

Now my mother's health is failing and she's started pressing me to forgive him before she dies -- not for his sake, she says, but because a family can't go on like this. My aunt (who's very religious) tells me forgiveness isn't something the other person earns, it's something you owe regardless, and that withholding it is a kind of pride. My therapist says roughly the opposite: forgiveness is for me, not him, and forcing it on a schedule to make dying people comfortable is just self-betrayal in a nicer outfit. Here's what I keep getting stuck on, and I'd like actual help thinking it through rather than a pep talk. First: can you even forgive someone who isn't sorry and would do it again, or does "forgiveness" in that case just dissolve into something else -- excusing him, or deciding it didn't matter, or merely not being angry anymore? Because I can imagine getting to a place where I'm not consumed by it, but calling that forgiveness feels like a con. Second: if forgiveness is a gift, something freely given, how can anyone -- my mother, my aunt, God -- demand it from me? Doesn't an obligation to forgive destroy the thing it's obligating? And third, the one that really keeps me up: is there a coherent position where I owe my mother *something* here (she's dying, she didn't do this to me) without owing my brother forgiveness itself -- some middle station between full reconciliation and the cold war we've been running? Or is that a comforting fiction, and real forgiveness just is all-or-nothing?

I've tried writing this as fiction three times and it keeps collapsing, probably because I don't know what I think. Walk me through the distinctions -- forgiving vs. excusing vs. reconciling vs. just moving on -- and tell me which of them, if any, could actually be a duty, and to whom. Bonus points if you can tell me whether my brother's "art transmutes it" defense deserves anything other than contempt.
