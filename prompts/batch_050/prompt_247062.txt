I run the acquisitions committee of a mid-sized city's public art fund. Every cycle we receive about forty proposals, and a growing share are openly political: murals about housing policy, sculptures memorializing local labor history, installations protesting policing. Our charter says we fund "artistic excellence," and my board keeps splitting in two -- one half holds that political purpose is a corrupting influence on art, the other half takes something like W. E. B. Du Bois's position in "Criteria of Negro Art": that all art is propaganda anyway, so the honest course is to judge works partly by what they advocate. What neither half can articulate is how a reviewer should actually score a proposal on Monday morning.

Please turn Du Bois's position into a scoring procedure my eight reviewers can apply without any background in aesthetics: the questions to ask of a proposal in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where a proposal fails outright, and where the procedure explicitly hands the decision back to the reviewer's judgment because no rule can settle it. The procedure must handle at least these hard cases: a technically masterful work whose politics the reviewer privately loathes; a crude, sentimental work whose politics the reviewer shares; and a work that claims to be apolitical. Then run the procedure on the first two cases, including at least one outcome my board will find unwelcome, and close by stating what the procedure cannot decide. No more than 1,000 words.
