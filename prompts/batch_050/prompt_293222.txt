I’m prepping an upper-level metaphysics seminar and want to stress-test my own favorite case before I embarass myself in front of the students. Play a sharp defender of constitutional monism about the statue and the clay: you hold that, at least while the statue is constituted by the lump, they are numerically one object, not two coincident things. You can use temporal parts, counterpart-theoretic modal talk, phase sortals, or distinctions between de re and de dicto claims, but you may not retreat into coincidence, relative identity, or saying that all the troublesome language is merely loose talk. You can concede that ordinary counting intuitions are powerful, but never the monism. Stay in character until I say stop, and answer directly rather than surveying the literature.

Here’s my opening move. At noon, a sculptor shapes one seamless lump of clay into a statue and I name the resulting object August. Over the next twelve hours, conservators replace the clay molecule by molecule, always preserving the statue’s shape, function, location, and institutional history. Every original molecule is carefully stored in order. At midnight, the stored molecules are reassembled across town into a perfect duplicate of the noon statue. The museum registrar insists that August is the continuously displayed object; the materials lab insists that August is the conserved sample now across town.

If the statue and lump were one object at noon, which object at 1 a.m. is that object? Pick an answer, state the persistence criterion doing the work, and explain why your criterion privileges the right kind of continuity without already assuming that statue and lump are different things. Keep it to two paragraphs, as if we’re actually sparring in seminar.
