I've been reading up on the ontology of music for a seminar paper and want to check my working position before I commit to it. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Musical works are abstract objects -- types, not physical things, events, or mental ideas. The Hammerklavier isn't any of its performances or scores.
(2) Works are created by composers, not discovered. Beethoven brought something into existence; he didn't stumble on a pre-existing structure.
(3) One theory should cover both the classical and recorded cases: in a lot of rock and electronic music, the work just is the finished studio track, not a song that the track is one performance of.
(4) Performing a work correctly means being faithful to its score -- but I also want to say a jazz group improvising over the changes is genuinely performing the standard, not just borrowing from it.
(5) Two composers could in principle write note-for-note identical pieces and yet produce two different works, because of differences in context, instrumentation conventions, etc.

Does this package hang together? Go through it pair by pair where needed: which commitments are actually in tension (and how badly), which only look like they conflict but dissolve once you're careful, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me. Rank by severity rather than flagging everything equally. And don't tell me which one I should be most attached to -- I want the audit, not a verdict.
