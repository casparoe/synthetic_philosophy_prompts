Here's something I've been chewing on since an argument with a coworker last week. Take France's 2004 law banning conspicuous religious symbols in public schools (headscarves being the obvious target, whatever the neutral wording said), with the 2010 face veil ban as a follow up. Supporters of both defended them in roughly "our values" language -- laïcité, republicanism, sometimes explicitly Western values or Enlightenment values. And here's the thing: plenty of Americans, who would also describe themselves as defending Western values, looked at the same laws and saw straightforward violations of religious freedom, which they'd put in the very same bucket.

So analyze the headscarf episode as a test case for whether "Western values" is a coherent category at all, and run it in both directions like a proper case analysis.

First direction, category to case: if "Western values" names a real shared inheritance, French defenders and American critics shouldn't be able to read opposite policy conclusions out of it on the same concrete question. Does the episode show the category is empty, or just that it has genuine internal tensions, like liberty of conscience versus secularism as competing strands of the same tradition? And where does laïcité actually fit -- is it a Western value, a specifically French one wearing universal clothes, or arguably anti-Western if religious freedom is the core commitment? Also, who even counts as "the West" for purposes of this argument, given that most of Europe's own practice a century earlier wouldn't look especially liberal on religion or dress?

Second direction, case to category: what does the episode reveal about how the phrase functions? My suspicion is that "Western values" is never doing descriptive work in real arguments -- nobody cites it neutrally, it only shows up as a rhetorical move. If that's right, what exactly is the move doing? Drawing an in-group boundary? Borrowing authority from a consensus that doesn't exist? Retroactively projecting the speaker's current values onto a tradition and calling it inheritance? Test that suspicion against the episode rather than just asserting it.

I don't want a both-sides survey that ends with "it's complicated." Give me a verdict: does "Western values" survive this case as a coherent category (maybe as a family-resemblance cluster with real internal conflicts), or does the headscarf affair expose it as incoherent, and what should we say is going on when politicians invoke it? Defend the verdict against the strongest version of the other side, and be direct about how confident you are in it.
