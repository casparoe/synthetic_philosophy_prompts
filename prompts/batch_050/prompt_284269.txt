I'm trying to sort out my intuitions about creating people, and I want to know what principle they add up to, if any. A couple who never conceives, even though their child would have been perfectly happy, does nothing wrong and harms no one -- there's no one to be harmed. A couple who conceives knowing the child will have a painful degenerative disease does something seriously wrong. But once the happy child exists, her pleasures strike me as a genuine good and her pains a genuine bad, so it's not that I think pleasure just doesn't count. Adding more happy people to an already populated world feels neither good nor bad, maybe faintly good, while adding more miserable people is clearly bad. And here's where I trip up: when I imagine choosing directly between an empty world and a world with millions of happy people, the populated world seems better, even though nobody in the empty world is deprived of anything -- which is exactly the verdict that seemed to do the work in the never-conceiving case. Is there one asymmetry (Benatar-style or otherwise) that captures all of this, or do I secretly need different principles for pairwise world comparisons and for procreation choices? Which of my verdicts is the weakest link? And what does the best candidate principle say about the case I keep going back and forth on: conceiving a child whose life would be about average -- decent pleasures, ordinary pains -- partly for your own sake?
