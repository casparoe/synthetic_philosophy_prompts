I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm drafting a long feature on what biologists actually mean when they say they have explained something. The proximate occasion is a fight I keep having with researchers: molecular biologists tell me that an explanation is a mechanism — entities and activities organized so as to produce the phenomenon, in the style associated with Machamer, Darden, and Craver — while ecologists and systems biologists tell me that some of their best explanations contain no mechanism at all, citing things like the Hodgkin–Huxley equations (which predicted action-potential dynamics beautifully before the ion channels doing the work were identified) and the metabolic models that predict bacterial growth rates from network topology and flux constraints without naming a single interacting part. Rather than referee the fight on instinct, I want the specification first: what must any adequate philosophical account of explanation in the life sciences be able to do?

Write the desiderata list. For each requirement, give the concrete case or result that motivates it — for instance: it must make sense of why finding one more component or one more activity sometimes completes an explanation and sometimes adds nothing; it must distinguish constitutive relevance (a part's being in the mechanism at all) from merely etiological relevance (a cause upstream of it); it must say something principled about levels, since explanations in biology notoriously cross molecular, cellular, and organismal scales; and it must either accommodate mechanism-free explanatory successes like the ones above or bite the bullet and deny that they are explanations. Flag which requirements you take to be non-negotiable for any account and which are really just the housekeeping rules of one camp, and if some subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable — my suspicion is that covering both mechanism-rich and mechanism-free successes while keeping the constitutive/etiological line sharp will turn out to be impossible — say so plainly, since the shape of the impossibility would itself be the most interesting finding in the piece. Then, briefly, score the mechanist account and its two or three most serious rivals (the old covering-law picture, a unificationist view, and an interventionist-causal view) against your list, and identify the single requirement that eliminates the most contenders.

I don't want a survey that leaves every option open; commit. State in your first sentence which account you think an adequate theory of biological explanation most resembles after the desiderata are applied, then give the list and the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. Where the desiderata force an uncomfortable verdict — where a camp's favorite demand turns out to be optional, or where no existing account survives — say which it is and why, rather than hedging between them.
