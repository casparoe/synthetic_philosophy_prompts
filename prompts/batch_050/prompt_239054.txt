I'm a high school junior, and a friend from my philosophy club gave me a puzzle about probability and memory that has been bothering me for weeks, plus some related cases I came up with myself. I have gut verdicts on all of them, and I strongly suspect they don't hang together. I'd like to know what principle, if any, I'm actually committed to. Here are the cases and my verdicts.

(1) On Sunday night I agree to an experiment. While I sleep, the researchers flip a fair coin. If it lands heads, they wake me once, on Monday morning. If it lands tails, they wake me Monday, give me a drug that completely erases my memory of that waking, and wake me again Tuesday. I know all of this in advance. I wake up in the lab. My gut says: the coin was fair, and I've learned nothing — of course I'd be awake right now either way — so the probability of heads is still 1/2.

(2) Same setup, but now a bet is offered at each waking: I win $30 if the coin landed heads, I lose $20 if it landed tails, and the bet is placed again at every waking. On Sunday I work out that tails means paying the $20 twice, so overall the bet is unfavorable and I refuse it in advance. But when I actually wake up, my 1/2 judgment from case (1) tells me the bet favors me, and I feel tempted to take it. That temptation feels wrong. I can't have gotten smarter just by opening my eyes.

(3) A different case. A machine draws either one name from a registry (if a red ball comes up) or a thousand names (if a green ball comes up), and everyone selected is brought to an identical waiting room with no way to tell how many others were selected. My name gets called. Here I'm nearly certain the green ball came up — my being called was guaranteed under green and a thousand-to-one shot under red. This verdict feels rock solid to me.

(4) Back in the lab from case (1): I wake up, and before I can check anything, a nurse tells me it's Monday. Now my gut says heads is about 2/3 likely — under heads, Monday was guaranteed; under tails, Monday was only one of two chances. But if being told it's Monday raises my confidence in heads, then merely waking up must already have carried information, which contradicts my verdict in case (1).

(5) Finally, a variant: everything is as in case (1), except the coin isn't flipped until Monday night, and only if it lands tails will they wake me again on Tuesday. When I wake on Monday, the coin hasn't even been flipped, and it's fair — surely the probability of heads is 1/2. Yet my room, the drug, and my state of mind are exactly as in case (1), where my other intuitions seem to point below 1/2.

Is there one principle for assigning probabilities when I don't know which of several possible "me right now" situations I'm in that captures these verdicts? I suspect at least one of (1), (2), and (4) has to go — tell me which, and why it's that one and not another. I'd also like to know what your principle predicts for a case I haven't worked out: a version where tails means I'm woken on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, with the same memory-erasing drug each night — what should my confidence in heads be at a waking, and does your principle say anything different about the betting situation in that version?
