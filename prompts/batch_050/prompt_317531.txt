My friend took a behavioral econ class last semester and now she won't shut up about the dollar auction. Here's how she explained it to me at a party, as best I can reconstruct:

"An auctioneer holds up a $1 bill. Highest bidder gets the bill, but the runner-up has to pay whatever they bid and gets nothing. Game theorists proved the only rational move is to never bid at all — that's the equilibrium. But when real people play, they always end up bidding over a dollar and the auctioneer walks away with a guaranteed profit no matter what happens. So it's basically a proof that humans are irrational. Specifically it's the sunk cost fallacy in action: once people have money in, they can't bring themselves to quit, so they keep throwing good money after bad. Lesson: never enter an auction of any kind, they're all designed to trap you this way."

This was a fun story but some of it smelled off to me. Like, if nobody should bid, why does anyone bid in the first place — is the very first bid already irrational, or is it only later bids? And the "guaranteed profit" part seems too strong, surely if one person bids 5 cents and everyone else just declines, the auctioneer is out 95 cents? Also I thought the sunk cost fallacy was about letting past costs influence decisions when they shouldn't, but here continuing to bid seems like it actually does reduce your loss at each step, so calling it a fallacy feels like it might be mislabeling the trap.

Can you go through her summary piece by piece and tell me what a game theorist would actually accept, what's an overstatement, and what's just wrong? I especially want to know: does the standard analysis really say "never bid," or is it subtler than that (e.g. what happens with a first bid of 99 cents, or limited budgets)? Is escalation past a dollar driven by sunk-cost psychology or by the forward-looking incentives the rules create, and does that distinction matter? And once the errors are corrected, does her takeaway advice ("never enter any auction") follow from anything true, or is she overgeneralizing from one weird payoff structure?
