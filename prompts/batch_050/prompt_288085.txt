I work nights stocking shelves at a supermarket, which leaves a lot of time for my brain to wander, and I keep getting stuck on one particular wierd thing. I want to know if it's a known thing with a name, or if I've just tied myself in a knot on my own.

Here's the setup. When I stub my toe on a pallet jack, I yell "ow, that hurts." Fine. But when I try to trace what actually made me yell, I get something like: toe hits metal, signals shoot up nerves, brain does stuff, muscles contract, sound comes out. Every step of that chain is physical stuff pushing other physical stuff. So where in that chain does the actual feeling of pain — the hurtyness of it, the part that it's like something to have — do any work? It seems like the words would have come out exactly the same even if the feeling had been different, or absent, or swapped for some totally other feeling. Same with telling my coworker the break room coffee tastes burnt, or that a song sounds sad. The feeling just sort of rides on top and doesn't pull any levers.

But if that's right, something really strange follows that I never see spelled out: my sincere belief that I'm in pain, and my saying "I'm in pain," aren't caused by the pain-feeling at all. They're caused by the nerve-and-brain machinery, which would have produced the same report no matter what the feeling was like. So why do my reports and my feelings match up so reliably? It's like a weather vane that always points the right way even though the wind never touches it. And it gets worse: right now, while thinking this very thought, it feels like my experience is informing my thought — but by my own argument, that "feeling of being informed" isn't doing anything either. It's as if the whole running commentary I have about my own inner life is a radio play performed by machinery, and the actual inner life is stuck in the audience, unable to change the script.

I've heard people throw around the word "epiphenomenalism" but from the summaries I've seen that's just the first half — the claim that experiences don't cause anything. My thing is specifically the ugly aftermath for our own judgments and reports: if the feeling can't cause the report, in what sense is the report even about the feeling? Does this specific knot have a name of its own, as opposed to the general "consciousness doesn't do anything" idea? Also, how is it different from the ordinary fact that people can be wrong about their own feelings, like mislabeling an emotion — because my thing seems deeper, like the reports couldn't even be right in the usual sense, just coincidentally aligned. And if nobody's named it, go ahead and coin a term, but give me a real definition plus some boundary cases: does the same problem hit our judgments about memories and intentions too, or only raw sensations, and what would a creature look like for whom the problem doesn't arise?
