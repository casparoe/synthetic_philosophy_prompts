Couple months ago I got into this long running argument with my coworker about whether its ever ok for the law to ban something just because its wrong, when literally nobody is harmed by it. No victim, no complaints, nothing. She says obviously not, I keep feeling like there's something on the other side even though I can't defend it well. That rabbit hole led me to the Hart-Devlin debate, where as I understand it Devlin argued a society kind of holds together through shared morality so the law can legitimately enforce that morality even against private harmless acts, and Hart pushed back hard that this is confused and dangerous.

I get the broad strokes but whenever I try to figure out what they were ACTUALLY disagreeing about underneath it all, everything I read just picks a side and argues. So here's what I want instead: write the document Hart and Devlin could have co-signed if they'd been forced into one of those adversarial collaborations where opponents have to produce a joint statement. Specifically:

1. A numbered list of claims both would sign. Stuff they actually agree on about what law is for, what needs justifying, what the other side isn't saying (no strawmen allowed in the whole document).
2. A table of the remaining genuine disagreements, each one phrased so that side's own supporter would accept the wording as fair to their view.
3. The cruxes -- the sub-questions where, if you settled one, at least one side would actually have to update. I suspect one crux is empirical (does private immorality really erode social cohesion the way Devlin feared, or is Hart right that society survives moral change fine?) and another is about what even counts as a harm vs mere offense or disgust. But tell me if those are the real cruxes or if I'm off.
4. For each crux, a concrete case or observation both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Like: a consensual private act literally nobody ever finds out about; a practice most of the population finds viscerally revolting but which correlates with no measurable harm; a ban that reduces a practice mostly through social shaming rather than actual enforcement.

And a few honesty requirements: if part of the dispute turns out to be just verbal -- two people using "harm" or "society" or "collapse" differently -- flag which part and say why. At the end, say which side you personally think the joint statement ends up tilting toward if any (joint statements always tilt somewhere), and where your drafting is most likely to have been unfair. And tell me what specific argument or piece of evidence would change your own verdict on which crux actually settles the debate -- like, what result or thought experiment would flip you from Devlin-leaning to Hart-leaning or the other way around.
