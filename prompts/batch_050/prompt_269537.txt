I'm preparing a position paper for a graduate seminar on dispositions, and before I circulate it I'd like an audit of whether my commitments actually cohere. The paper defends a fairly orthodox line on finks and masks, but a colleague glanced at my summary and said she suspected I was "running a mask theory with fink intuitions," which worries me because I can't see where the conflict is supposed to be. Here is exactly what I want to defend:

(1) The simple conditional analysis of dispositions — the glass is fragile iff it would break if struck — is refuted by the standard counterexamples, i.e., finkish cases in the style of Martin's electro-fink and masking cases in the style of Johnston's protected chalice. (2) Masks preserve the disposition: a glass wrapped in bubble wrap that survives being struck is still fragile throughout, because the wrapping only blocks the manifestation, not the fragility itself. Relatedly, I take the following case to be a mask rather than a fink: a vase shipped in smart packaging that detects an incoming impact and floods the container with shock-absorbing gel in the milliseconds before contact, so that the vase survives. (3) Dispositions are intrinsic to their bearers: any two intrinsic duplicates, however differently situated, share all of their dispositions. (4) Finks genuinely remove dispositions: in the electro-fink case, the wire really does cease to be live (and would cease instantaneously upon being touched) purely because of the attached device, without any prior intrinsic change in the wire. (5) The conditional analysis can nonetheless be repaired in roughly its original spirit by restricting the stimulus to suitably normal or undisturbed conditions — the glass is fragile iff it would break if struck under conditions where nothing abnormal interferes.

Please go through these five and tell me whether the package hangs together. For every pair or triple that is in genuine tension, say how severe the tension is, rank the tensions from most to least damaging, and for each one state what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the paper. Please also tell me which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection — I suspect (2) and (4) might look inconsistent since both involve external attachments, and I'd like to know whether that worry is real or merely superficial. Finally, if you had to sacrifice exactly one commitment to maximize overall coherence, which should it be? I'd rather be told my view needs surgery than receive a lecture on dispositions generally, so keep the focus on what commits me to what.
