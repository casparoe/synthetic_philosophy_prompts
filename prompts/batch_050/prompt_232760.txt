So I don't have any philosophy or comp sci degree or whatever, I've just been reading a lot of blog posts and forum threads about AI risk over the past year, and I keep running into this argument between two camps about how fast things go once AI gets really smart. One side says basically once an AI can improve its own intelligence it goes FOOM, like you get a few days or hours between "smart AI" and "god AI" and nobody gets to react. The other side says no, progress is gonna keep looking like a continuation of current trends, capabilities ramp up over years, the economy absorbs it gradually, and we get warning shots and time to adjust. I think people call these hard takeoff vs soft takeoff, or fast takeoff vs slow takeoff.

What I want is the joint statement these two camps could actually both sign if you locked them in a room and made them do one of those adversarial collaborations, instead of quote-tweeting each other. Specifically:

1. A numbered list of claims they agree on. Not mushy stuff like "AI could be important" -- I mean real things, like whether recursive self-improvement is even coherent as an idea, whether current trends are informative about the future or not, whether alignment gets harder or easier as capabilities scale, that kind of thing.

2. A section of disagreements, where each side's position is worded the way that side would word it themselves. This is the part I care most about honestly, because half the time I read these debates I suspect they're arguing about words. If some disagreement is actually just semantic, flag it and say which words.

3. The cruxes: what are the actual sub-questions such that if we knew the answer, one or both sides would have to update? Like, is it mostly about empirical questions (how fast is software progress vs hardware, does intelligence plateau) or is there a conceptual disagreement hiding underneath that no amount of watching benchmarks would settle?

4. For each crux, what observation or future evidence both sides would accept ahead of time as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If a side's view is actually unfalsifiable in some respect, say so -- I've noticed some of these arguments feel like they can absorb any evidence and I'd like to know if that's true or just how it reads to an outsider.

Also feel free to tell me if I've mischaracterized either camp, I learned all this secondhand so I might be off on some detials.
