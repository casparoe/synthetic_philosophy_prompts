So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training beyond some podcasts, and I got into an argument at work I can't stop thinking about. A while back we shipped an ML feature that ended up quietly screwing over a particular group of users — nobody designed it to do that, every individual decision along the way was honestly defensible, and the postmortem literally concluded "organizational failure, no single person at fault." Half my team thinks that's a cop-out: the company did it, the company should be blamed. The other half thinks blaming "the company" is like blaming the weather — only actual humans can be wrongdoers and the rest is just vibes and PR. I want to run a lunch-and-learn on this, and what I need is a decision tree / flowchart that sorts my coworkers into the main positions on whether a company itself can be genuinely blameworthy.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of famous names. So the fork questions come first, each one a concrete case or claim where the positions actually come apart, phrased so people can answer without knowing any jargon (no "do you believe in corporate intentionality?"). Cases you could mine: a trading algorithm no single employee fully understands that causes real damage; a company whose incentive structure reliably turns decent people into jerks while each of them stays individually blameless; the same harmful decision made (a) by unanimous board vote versus (b) emerging from process with no vote ever taken; whether a company's apology can be sincere if nobody inside it actually feels sorry. Eight to twelve questions total. The leaves should cover at least: corporate-blame-as-shorthand-for-blaming-individuals; the view that the company is a real agent in its own right because of its internal decision procedures; the view that the company can be responsible/answerable but not blameworthy because it has no inner life; and a functionalist if-it-walks-like-an-agent option. Include a scoring key mapping answer patterns to leaves, mark which answer combinations end up unoccupied and say briefly why (incoherent vs. merely unpopular), and flag any pairs of positions your tree can't seperate rather than pretending it can.

One constraint from my coworker Priya, who did a philosophy minor and is annoyingly smug about it: she insists the tree has to pull apart "the company should pay / get sanctioned / lose its license" from "the company deserves blame," because apparently those can come apart and mashing them together is how these discussions go in circles. Also there's that old line about corporations having no soul to damn and no body to kick — if it's a decent intuition pump make it the root question, and if it's actually a bad one tell me why before the lunch-and-learn so I don't embarrass myself.
