The author then writes (I'm paraphrasing from memory): "Since the subject could not have known the second dot's color or position before it appeared, the experience of smooth motion must have been constructed after the fact. Dennett used this experiment to show that the brain performs what he called Stalinesque revision: like a show trial, the evidence presented to consciousness is fabricated in advance, with a delay of a few hundred milliseconds during which the brain assembles the motion narrative before it is experienced. The rival hypothesis — that the subject really experiences the stationary first dot and then has the memory instantly rewritten, which Dennett called Orwellian — is refuted by the data, because subjects' reports match the constructed motion rather than the actual stimuli. This proves that conscious experience is a delayed confabulation and that there is no such thing as experiencing anything 'in real time.'" Something about this feels like it overshoots — I had the impression Dennett's point was that neither story can be established, not that one was experimentally confirmed — but I can't articulate where the author goes wrong, and for all I know the author is right. Is this a fair summary of what the color phi phenomenon shows and of Dennett's use of it?
