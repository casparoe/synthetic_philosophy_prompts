I fix bikes as a hobby and I've been chewing on something for months now, and I'm hoping someone can tell me whether the philosophy literature already has a word for it.

Everyone who rides knows the two basic states. When the bike is working, it basically disappears -- you're not riding a bike, you're just going somewhere. And when something actually fails -- chain snaps, tire blows -- the bike suddenly becomes this huge obvious *thing* in front of you that you have to stare at and fiddle with. I know Heidegger wrote about this in Being and Time, the whole ready-to-hand versus present-at-hand thing, where a tool is transparent in use and only becomes an object you *look at* when it breaks. Fine. That part I get.

But there's a third state I keep living in that doesn't seem to be either of these, and I can't find it in anything I've read. Say my rear derailleur starts shifting half a second late. Not broken. I still get everywhere. But now there's this thin slice of my attention permanently parked on it. I'm not inspecting it -- I'm still just riding, the tool is still doing its job -- but it's not invisible anymore either. It's like the tool is glowing faintly at the edge of awareness. Or my mouse at work with a dying battery: it stutters maybe twice an hour, and somehow my whole posture toward it changes, I half-expect every click to fail, and weirdly I think my *clicking itself* gets worse because I'm bracing for it. The tool is functioning, but my relationship to it has been contaminated by the expectation of breakdown.

Heidegger's breakdown stuff all seems to be about actual failure -- the hammer breaks and then the whole workshop lights up. But in my cases nothing has failed yet. The anticipation alone is doing the work, and it produces this hybrid mode that is neither smooth use nor inspection. It's also not just "worry" in the ordinary psychological sense, because it changes how the activity itself unfolds -- the riding, the clicking.

So my questions: does this anticipatory, half-withdrawn mode of tool-use have an established name, in Heidegger scholarship or phenomenology of technology or anywhere else? If it's somewhere in the literature I've missed, tell me how it differs from plain breakdown and from ordinary inattention. And if it genuinely doesn't have one, I'd love a coined term with an actual definition -- what it is, what it isn't, and where the boundary cases are. For instance: does it count if the tool never ends up failing at all? What about when I'm wrong and the tool was fine the whole time -- was I still in this state, or was I just anxious? Please don't just hand me a label; I want the carving.
