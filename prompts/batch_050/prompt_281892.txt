I teach a grad seminar on well-being, and after last week's session two of my students, Marta and Dev, kept arguing in the hallway about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. I stayed out of it, but I've been turning their exchange over since and I'd like a referee. Marta opened by saying hedonism founders on the fact that pleasures have nothing in common: the pleasure of a hot shower, the pleasure of finally seeing why a proof works, the pleasure of hearing a friend got the job -- introspect them and there's no shared ingredient, so "pleasure is the good" is either false or empty. Dev said that's too quick: the *objects* differ but the feeling is the common element, a felt hedonic tone that comes in degrees and attaches to wildly different sources, the way different songs can share a key. Marta said she'd introspected and found no such tone -- no quale present in both the backrub and the proof -- and what actually unifies pleasures is that we *welcome* them: pleasure is an attitude, being pleased about something, not a sensation. She added that the attitudinal view handles cases the felt-quality view can't, like being pleased for months that your reputation survived some scandal without feeling anything most of that time. Dev pushed back on two fronts: first, that "welcoming" seems to overgenerate -- you can grimly accept something, or want something you take to be bad for you, like the addict who wants the fix; second, that defining pleasure by what we welcome or seek is circular, since what is liking if not a kind of felt pro-attitude? Marta's last move was to say there's no circle, because liking is just a functional profile -- a state that tends to sustain itself, that you'd pursue, that you regret losing -- and Dev answered that once pleasure is just a causal role, you've lost the very thing that made hedonism plausible, since nothing about a causal role is obviously *good*. Then Dev had to catch a bus.

So, adjudicate. Go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point -- is there plausibly a shared hedonic tone across diverse pleasures, does the attitude theory really handle "standing" pleasures better, does welcoming overgenerate, is the circle real or is the functional-role reply adequate, and does the functional move really cost hedonism its plausibility? Then say who's more right overall, or whether the dispute dissolves into two theories that only look like rivals. Continuous prose only, please -- no headings, no bullets, no bold, just write it like a long email to a colleauge. And at the end, tell me what would change your verdict: which consideration, if it turned out to hold, would flip your judgment on the tone-versus-attitude question, and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to break down.
