Two of my colleagues in our philosophy of physics reading group — a committed presentist and a committed eternalist — have spent most of the academic year arguing about the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe, and I have volunteered to referee. Rather than commission another round of dueling papers, I have asked them to produce a joint statement in the style of an adversarial collaboration, and I would like you to draft it on their behalf.

For context: the presentist holds that only present events exist, where "present" tracks some observer-independent division of spacetime; the eternalist holds that all spacetime events exist tenselessly and that past, present, and future mark no objective division. The argument under dispute runs, in outline: for any two sufficiently separated events, one inertial frame judges them simultaneous while others judge them in either temporal order; so if what exists is determined by what is present, then what exists becomes frame-dependent; and since special relativity privileges no inertial frame, the only consistent view is that all events exist equally. The presentist's countermoves over the year have included: treating simultaneity as partly a matter of convention and so denying that frames carve reality at its joints; proposing that becoming is local, defined relative to each spacetime point or its past light cone rather than to a global now; and suggesting that quantum nonlocality, or the foliation structure of some cosmological theory, might yet single out a physically preferred slicing of spacetime. The eternalist replies that each of these either abandons presentism's motivating intuition or buys a "now" at the price of something relativity gives us no reason to believe in.

The document should contain four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both parties would sign: what special relativity actually establishes, what the argument does and does not assume, and what would distinguish a metaphysical conclusion from a mere artifact of coordinate choice. Second, a table of the remaining disagreements, each formulated so that its own side would accept the wording as a fair statement of its position. Third, the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party; I suspect one is the conventionality of simultaneity and another is whether "frame-dependent" must mean "not real," but let the record rather than my suspicions determine the list. Fourth, for each crux, a case, theorem, or physical theory that both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's verdict or prediction recorded in advance, so that future argument can be checked against it. If some portion of their dispute turns out on inspection to be verbal, the statement should identify that portion explicitly and explain why.
