so i spent the last three weekends setting up a "smart" home system and somewhere around 2am on sunday, after it helpfully unlocked the front door because the vacuum reported low battery and that was somehow one of a trillion things causally downstream of nothing, i fell down a wikipedia hole and landed on the frame problem. roughly: any action has infinitely many consequences and infinitely many non-consequences, and an agent somehow has to figure out which of those are worth thinking about without checking them all. brains do this effortlessly. my vacuum does not do this.

write me a short story (1000-1500 words) that actually dramatizes the frame problem. a few constraints so it doesn't turn into generic robot fiction:

- not a misaligned-AI story and not a paperclip maximizer story. in those the agent has bad goals; i want the problem to be inference, not values. the agent's goals can be totally fine and even admirable -- the disaster (or comedy) should come from it being unable to tell which of the endless consequences of its actions matter, or which things stay the same when it acts.
- ideally show both failure modes at some point: computing too much (paralyzed while it verifies that opening the fridge doesn't change the number of moons jupiter has) and computing too little (does the sensible-seeming thing, chaos ensues).
- the agent should be genuinely smart in every other respect. the whole horror of the problem is that raw intelligence doesn't solve it.
- whatever embodiment you like -- household robot, ship's computer, a golem, a literal-minded genie -- but if you go the genie route, be careful: careful-what-you-wish-for stories are usually about sloppy wording of goals, which is a different problem. the genie's issue has to be the inferences, not the wording of the wish.
- nobody in the story should say "the frame problem" or name-drop philosophers. it should be legible purely from what happens.
- end on something unresolved rather than a tidy fix -- i don't actually believe there's a tidy fix, and if there is one my vacuum hasn't found it.
