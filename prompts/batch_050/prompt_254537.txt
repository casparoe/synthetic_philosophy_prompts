A friend and I came up with this after working through Sartre for a reading group and it seems to prove way too much, but we can't find where it breaks. (1) For Sartre, consciousness is freedom: whatever my situation is, I am free with respect to how I take it up, because the meaning my situation has for me is conferred by my projects rather than given by the bare facts -- the same rock is an obstacle for the climber and a landmark for the shepherd. (2) If the meaning of my situation comes from my choice of projects, then no situation is intolerable in itself: what makes chains unbearable is that I have projects -- comfort, dignity, a future -- which the chains frustrate. (3) So whenever someone suffers under their circumstances, they could in principle stop suffering by abandoning or revising the projects rather than the circumstances. (4) If a person can always relieve their own suffering through a free internal shift of this kind, then their continued suffering is, in the end, their own doing -- suffering you could drop but don't is suffering you're choosing. (5) So talk of oppression always involves bad faith on the part of the oppressed: they pin their suffering on the world precisely to hide from themselves the freedom (3) says they have. (6) Therefore there is no such thing as oppression, only bad faith about freedom, and the slave who blames the master is in bad faith just like Sartre's waiter who overplays his role. The conclusion is obviously insane -- slavery is real and it is the master's fault -- but each step looks like something Sartre either says outright or is committed to, and honestly (4) also sounds like the obvious way to read his claim that we are responsible for everything that happens to us, even the war. Which step should we actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If forced to pick, I'd rather keep (1) and (2) than save anything downstream of them, but I'd like to know whether the damage is a conflation between two senses of 'freedom', a bad move about what counts as 'choosable', or something else entirely -- and whether Sartre himself has the resources to block the argument or whether his admirers have to fix it for him.
