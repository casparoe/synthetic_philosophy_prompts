So the frog's inner state means "fly" and not "small dark moving dot" because snapping at flies is what the detection mechanism got selected for, even though the mechanism can't tell the difference. I find the view really attractive, honestly, maybe because it smells like something an engineer would invent: content as a design spec, reference as a fitness function. But before I let myself get too attached, I want to stress-test it.

So: act as a philosopher of mind who's sympathetic to naturalizing mental content in general but not a true believer in any one theory. Think carefully, brainstorm a bit before you settle on anything, and then give me a map of what would count *against* teleosemantics as I've described it. I already know the greatest hits (Swampman-style cases, the disjunction problem, the indeterminacy about whether the frog's state is about flies or fly-shaped-things), so don't just list them — for each candidate defeater, tell me whether a committed teleosemanticist would actually concede it as damaging or wave it off as a feature that looks like a bug, and rank everything from fatal to merely awkward. I definately also want the defeaters that don't usually make it into intro summaries: consequences the view is stuck with that turn out to be unacceptable, cases it can't describe, ways it might secretly collapse into some rival theory of content.
