Since my company began routing parts of its client correspondence through a large language model, my colleagues and I have fallen into a recurring dispute: when the system produces something inappropriate, is it best understood as a goal-directed agent that reasoned badly, or as a simulator that happened to be playing an inappropriate character? To settle what is actually at stake in this disagreement, please design a thought experiment that would empirically discriminate between the two pictures. Specifications: (1) the probe must be implementable through an ordinary chat interface, with no access to the model's weights, activations, or training data; (2) the two pictures must yield genuinely divergent behavioral predictions, not merely different labels for the same output; (3) the divergence must arise from the models' actual structure — training on text, conditioning on a conversational context — rather than from a failure mode the simulator picture would predict just as readily. After presenting the scenario, show the reasoning for each picture: what it predicts, why, which feature of the setup does the discriminating work, and whether removing that feature collapses the probe back into something both pictures explain equally well. Conclude with the strongest objection available to someone holding a third, deflationary view — that the model is a mere pattern-completer to which neither "agent" nor "simulator" adds predictive content — and assess whether your experiment survives it.
