I work in type theory and functional programming, and I am completely fluent in that vocabulary: simply typed and dependently typed lambda calculus, function application and beta-reduction, currying, judgments versus terms, the distinction between an object language and the operations by which its terms are formed. I have only a secondhand grasp of metaphysics, and I keep hearing about Bradley's regress from Appearance and Reality (1893, the early chapters on primary and secondary qualities and on relation and quality) without being able to tell whether it is a deep problem or an artifact of a bad formalism. So here is what I want.

Bradley's argument, as I understand it, runs roughly like this. Take his stock example of a lump of sugar that is white and sweet. The complex is not just the qualities piled together, so we seem to need relations holding among them. But if the relation is a further constituent alongside its relata, then we need a second relation to connect the first relation to the relata, and a third to connect the second, and so on without end; if instead the relation is not a constituent but somehow does the relating on its own, then it is doing work that nothing in the inventory of constituents explains, and Bradley concludes that no finite arrangement of terms and relations can constitute a genuine unity, which is part of what drives him toward the Absolute.

Please restate this argument, step by step, in my vocabulary, as precisely as you can. Relata as terms of given types; relations presumably as function terms of type A → B → Prop, or as terms of some relation type; the regress as what happens if you insist that the relating itself be represented by a term, so that application has to be mediated by another term, ad infinitum. Be explicit about where the argument bites: is Bradley effectively demanding that the metalinguistic operation of application be internalized as an object-language term, and is the regress just the observation that this demand can never be satisfied?

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about. Which moves in Bradley's argument translate cleanly into type-theoretic terms? Which translate only with distortion — for instance, is treating a relation as a function term already a distortion of what he meant by a relation? And which parts have no expression at all in my vocabulary: I suspect his positive conclusion (that unity exists only in an undifferentiated Absolute that absorbs all relations) is inexpressible as any type or term, but tell me whether I am right.

Finally, the verdict. Does the type-theoretic rendering vindicate Bradley, refute him, or dissolve him? Candidate readings I can see: (a) Bradley is right that unity cannot be assembled from constituents, and type theory concedes this by taking application and judgment as primitive and unexplained — it presupposes exactly what he wanted explained; (b) the regress shows only that his demand was malformed, since asking for a term that does what application does is a category error, and there was never a problem about unity; (c) Frege's notion of an unsaturated or incomplete function expression is an attempt to thread this needle, and I want to know whether it survives translation into lambda calculus better than Bradley's own formulation. Tell me which of these you endorse and why, and say explicitly whether the residue you identified is a defect of my framework or a sign that part of the original problem was less coherent than it looked.
