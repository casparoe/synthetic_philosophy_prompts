In 1928, Dirac's relativistic wave equation for the electron turned out to have negative-energy solutions. Rather than discard them, Dirac eventually interpreted them as holes in an infinite "sea" of filled negative-energy states; such a hole would behave as a positively charged particle with the electron's mass, and Anderson's 1932 cloud-chamber discovery of the positron seemed to vindicate the mathematics. Analyze this episode through the explanatory version of the indispensability argument for mathematical realism -- the claim that we ought to believe in mathematical objects because mathematics is indispensable to our best scientific explanations of physical phenomena. In particular: (i) Does Dirac's derivation of the positron show mathematics playing a genuinely explanatory role, or only a predictive and heuristic one, and does the explanatory version of the argument even get a grip on a case whose glory is a successful prediction? (ii) Dirac's physical interpretation of the mathematics -- the infinite sea, the positron as a hole -- is rejected in quantum field theory, where the positron is a particle in its own right, yet the mathematics that generated the prediction was retained wholesale. If confirmational holism says that empirical success confirms the whole theoretical package, mathematics included, what does this dissociation between the retained mathematics and the discarded interpretation do to the holist premise? (iii) Reach a verdict on whether the episode, on balance, supports or undermines the explanatory indispensability argument. A conditional verdict (for instance, that it vindicates a broadly Quinean indispensability claim but not the explanatory refinement) is acceptable if defended, but do not end undecided. Keep the analysis disciplined about the mapping -- what exactly the mathematics contributed, what was confirmed, and what was later stripped away -- and say explicitly where the indispensability argument is simply silent about the case rather than forcing it to speak.
