I just had a long and honestly kind of annoying argument with a friend about whether the simulation argument should make us less “fanatical” about the far future. By fanatical I mean being willing to accept a tiny probability of success in exchange for an astronomically large payoff, like slightly reducing the chance of a future catastrophe that could affect trillions of lives.

Please compare these two positions we got stuck on:

**Position 1: The Simulation Discount.** If most minds like ours eventually exist inside simulations, then we should think we’re probably simulated. That makes extreme far-future plans less compelling: our world might be temporary, sealed off from the “basement” universe, periodically restarted, or subject to intervention by whoever runs it. So even if our choices seem to have enormous future consequences locally, the probability that those consequences persist or matter at the deepest level may be tiny.

**Position 2: The Agency Conservation View.** Being simulated wouldn’t make our experiences, choices, or victims any less real. A simulated person can suffer just as much as a non-simulated one, and a simulated civilization can still contain morally relevant futures. Also, if our simulation was created to learn something about the simulators’ own history or decisions, our behavior might carry information about—or even influence—what happens outside the simulation. Simulation uncertainty could therefore increase the stakes instead of shrinking them.

Compare the assumptions behind both views, especially about:

- whether simulated minds count as much as physical ones
- whether moral importance depends on being in the “base” reality
- how likely ancestor simulations, entertainment simulations, scientific simulations, and dead-end simulations are
- whether our decisions could correlate with decisions made outside the simulation
- whether uncertainty should lower expected value or merely make the calculation harder

Use concrete examples rather than staying at a purely abstract level. In particular, describe one scenario where the Simulation Discount is clearly the more sensible view, and one where it leads to an obviously bad conclusion. Also discuss whether someone could reject full-blown fanaticism while still treating the far future as overwhelmingly important.

Finally, say which position you find stronger overall. Don’t just split the difference unless the difference genuinely deserves splitting. My friend was very confident but I think he was definately skipping a few steps.
