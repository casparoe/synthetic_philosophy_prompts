I run a philosophy-of-science reading group whose members are working researchers — two physicists, a molecular biologist, and three machine-learning engineers — with no background in philosophy of mind. Next month we take up Dennett on the intentional stance and his essay Real Patterns, and I have a single two-hour session to get them from zero to a serious discussion. Plan the session for me: not the explanation itself, but its architecture.

Specifically, I need the following. First, the dependency structure: the three stances (physical, design, intentional) in the order I should introduce them, with the reason each one presupposes or fails to presuppose its predecessor. My own view is that the intentional stance cannot be taught first, because treating a system as approximately rational only makes sense against a prior grip on what the system is for — but tell me whether the dependency really runs that way and what concretely breaks if I reverse the order. Second, the carrying example: I want one system that survives redescription at all three levels and that I can keep returning to once we reach the patterns question. Candidates I have considered are a chess engine, a thermostat, and Conway's Game of Life. Tell me which carries the most weight, what such an example must have and must lack, and at what point in the arc each rejected candidate fails. Third, the misconception map: name the five or six confusions this particular audience will predictably form — I expect at minimum that the stances get heard as pretending, that the design stance gets heard as intelligent design, and that real patterns gets heard as lossy compression, which we already have — and specify exactly where in the sequence each must be defused, since several become harder to remove if allowed to sit through the wrong section. On the last of these: the machine-learning engineers will reach for minimum description length within five minutes, and I need a decision on whether to use that hook or resist it, and why. Fourth, what I should openly decline to cover at this level — the indeterminacy-of-interpretation background, the comparison with Davidson, Fodor-style critiques, or whatever else you judge — with a one-line reason for each exclusion. Finally, give me one question to pose in the last ten minutes whose answer would reveal whether the group has grasped the mild-realist claim about patterns or merely learned the vocabulary.

Begin your response with the plan's central structural claim in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of this request.
