I've been following the discussion about whether LLM "hallucinations" are really bullshit in Harry Frankfurt's sense, as opposed to lies or just plain errors, and it feels totally stalled to me. You've got one camp that says: Frankfurt defined bullshit as speech that's indifferent to the truth of what's said, and LLMs are the purest case of that indifference ever built, since there's no truth-tracking in there at all, just next-token prediction. Another camp says: hold on, Frankfurtian bullshit requires a speaker with a certain attitude -- indifference is itself a mental state, a stance toward truth -- and a system with no attitudes at all can't be indifferent, it can only misfire, so "hallucination" is just a fancy error term and calling it bullshit is a category mistake. And then a third group that wants to say it's sometimes lying because some models trained with RLHF have learned to produce outputs that score well even when their training signal punished them for the underlying behavior, which looks kind of like an incentive to deceive.

Everyone cites the same three or four papers, everyone agrees on what Frankfurt said, and nobody has budged in two years. My working hypothesis is that this isn't one disagreement but at least two stacked on top of each other: a verbal fight about whether "bullshit" requires an occurrent attitude, and underneath it a real empirical disagreement about what kinds of states (if any) a trained LLM actually has, where neither side can agree on what would even count as evidence about that. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real philosophical disagreement here that could actually be settled.
