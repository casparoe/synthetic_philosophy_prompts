**Exam question:** Define acausal trade. Explain how it differs from ordinary cooperation sustained by repeated interaction, reputation, or enforcement, and state the conditions that must hold for an acausal trade to go through. Then assess whether acausal trade is possible between agents located in one and the same universe. Illustrate with an example.

**Student's answer:**

Acausal trade is cooperation between agents who cannot interact causally — no signals, no shared institutions, no possibility of enforcement — and who nevertheless each take an action that benefits the other because each can predict what the other's decision procedure will output. Instead of reciprocity over time, the mechanism is a kind of subjunctive dependence: each agent reasons that the other is modeling them, and selects its action partly on the basis of what that modeling will conclude. For example, two advanced civilizations might arise in causally disconnected regions, each able to cheaply produce something the other values enormously; each produces the good for the other because each predicts that the other's reasoning mirrors this same calculation.

This differs from ordinary cooperation in that there is no iteration, no threat of punishment, and no communication channel by which trust could be built; the entire "agreement" consists in the correlation between the two decision procedures.

For the mutual prediction to be reliable enough to sustain trade, the two agents must be identical or near-identical copies, since only then can each treat its own reasoning as evidence about the other's; this is why acausal trade is essentially Hofstadter's superrationality applied to exchange. Given such symmetry, cooperation is effectively guaranteed: because each agent's decision procedure logically determines the other's output, defection is not a live option for either party.

Finally, acausal trade is impossible within a single universe. Agents in the same universe share a causal past, so they could in principle signal one another, which means any apparent acausal trade between them would collapse into an ordinary, causally mediated one. Strictly speaking, acausal trade can only occur between agents in separate universes, for instance across branches of a multiverse.
