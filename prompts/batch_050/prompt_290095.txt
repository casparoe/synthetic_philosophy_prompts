The enhanced indispensability argument runs roughly as follows: some empirical phenomena admit explanations in which mathematics does the explanatory work — for instance, the fact that periodical cicadas emerge on prime-numbered life cycles (13 or 17 years) is explained by the number-theoretic fact that primes minimize overlap with the cycles of predators and competitors, or the fact that honeycombs are hexagonal is explained by the theorem that hexagons tile the plane with minimal perimeter. If we ought to believe in the entities posited by our best explanations, and these explanations commit us to mathematical objects, then we ought to be platonists.

I'd like your actual credence that this argument (or some modest strengthening of it) gives us good reason to accept mathematical platonism — a single number, not a verdict of "contested." Then decompose it: (i) the probability that cases like cicada primes or the honeycomb are genuinely explanatory rather than merely representational or predictive; (ii) the probability that, conditional on their being explanatory, the explanation is distinctively mathematical rather than fully recoverable by a nominalized counterpart; (iii) the probability that explanatory indispensability even transmits ontological commitment (i.e., that the Quinean confirmational picture the argument relies on is sound); and (iv) any other term you think is load-bearing. Give a number and one line of justification for each, show how they combine, and identify which term your final credence is most sensitive to — i.e., which sub-question, if resolved against you, would most force you to revise the total.
