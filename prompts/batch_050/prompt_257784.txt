I’m prepping a philosophy-of-physics podcast with an author who defends “sophisticated substantivalism” as the right response to the hole argument. Her basic line is that spacetime points are real, but that two general-relativistic models related by a hole diffeomorphism aren’t genuinely different possible worlds: once you strip away coordinate choices and independently stipulated point-identities, the metric and matter relations fix all the physical facts. She thinks this preserves substantivalism without accepting radical indeterminism.

Draft 9–11 interview questions that actually press on this position rather than letting her give the usual overview. The sequence should start by making her explain the hole argument without blurring active diffeomorphisms into mere coordinate transformations, then move into the harder stuff: why denying haecceitistic differences doesn’t just turn her view into relationism; what exactly makes point identity “extrinsic” or metric-dependent; whether Leibniz equivalence is doing metaphysical work or merely stating a symmetry; how her view handles the apparently deterministic evolution of everything outside the hole; and whether boundary data should be allowed to fix the identities of points inside it. Include at least one question about whether the hole argument is really about determinism, general covariance, or our tendency to mistake mathematical surplus structure for physical possibilities, and one about what changes, if anything, when we move from classical general relativity toward quantum gravity.

For each question, give the answer she’s most likely to give and then a follow-up that challenges that answer. I’d like the follow-ups to get progressively harder to deflect with slogans like “that difference isn’t observable” or “you’re mistaking representations for realities.” End with a question that forces her to name the biggest cost of her view or describe what would make her abandon sophisticated substantivalism. Don’t ask for readings, citations, or biographical background.
