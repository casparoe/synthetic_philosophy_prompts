A friend and I got into a surprisingly heated argument after a near-miss on the highway last month, and I've been unable to settle it to my own satisfaction. A truck had begun drifting into our lane; the horn sounded, I swerved, and afterwards I had a vivid sense that I had been watching the truck in my side mirror for a second or two before I reacted. But here's the puzzle: I certainly wasn't *aware* of watching it at the time. So which happened? Did I genuinely see the truck in the mirror all along, with the experience arriving "in consciousness" only once the horn snapped me to attention? Or did my brain, in the moment of panic, fabricate the mirror-image and stitch it into my memory with an earlier timestamp, so that it now *feels* as though I saw it before I reacted, even though no such experience occurred at the time? My friend insists these are obviously two different things and that one of them must be the truth; I can't see how any possible evidence could separate them, since every report I could make — even to myself — comes after the fact.

This isn't an isolated case. I sometimes read a word wrong the first time through a sentence, someone corrects me, and then my memory of having read it gets quietly overwritten: I can no longer recover what it was like to see the wrong word, and it retroactively seems as though I read the right one from the start. Again the same fork presents itself — a real experience later revised, or a non-experience later invented — and again I can't imagine a test that would adjudicate it. I want to be clear about what I'm not asking. I'm not asking about déjà vu, and I'm not asking about clinical confabulation or ordinary memory distortion, because the puzzle isn't that my memory is inaccurate; it's that I'm unsure whether there was an experience at the earlier time for the memory to be accurate or inaccurate *about*. Nor is it quite the phenomenon from those attention experiments where people fail to notice a change in front of them — there the experience presumably happened and simply wasn't noticed, whereas my puzzle is precisely about whether "it happened but wasn't noticed" is even a coherent alternative to "it never happened at all."

So: is there an established name for this fork, or a literature organized around it? If there are named positions on either side — roughly, the "you experienced it and the record was revised" view versus the "the record is the only place it ever existed" view — I'd like to know what they're called and how their defenders think the choice between them could be made empirically rather than by stipulation. And if the prevailing view is that the fork is not a real fork at all, that on fine enough timescales the question has no answer, I'd like to understand what follows from that: does it merely show that consciousness is experimentally elusive, or does it undercut the seemingly obvious assumption that every experience has a definite time at which it entered awareness? Please don't just hand me a label — I want the carving: what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, which nearby concepts it has to be distinguished from, and where its edges are.
