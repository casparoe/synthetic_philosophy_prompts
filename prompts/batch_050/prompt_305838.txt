The exam question was: "In 'Where Am I?', Dennett imagines having his brain surgically removed and kept alive in a vat, while his body is operated remotely by radio link. What question is he using this fantasy to investigate, and what is his answer to it? Explain why neither 'I am where my brain is' nor 'I am where my body is' fully satisfies him."

Here's the student's answer:

"Daniel Dennett's 'Where Am I?', from his 1978 collection Brainstorms, uses a science-fiction scenario to ask where the self is physically located. In the story, Dennett's brain is removed and kept functioning in a vat of nutrients while his body, which he names Hamlet, is controlled through radio transmitters, with the brain itself nicknamed Yorick. The puzzle is that his point of view seems to travel: he expects to find himself in the vat with his brain, but he keeps experiencing the world from where his body is.

Dennett's conclusion is that the self is located wherever the brain is, since the brain is what does the thinking. The story shows this because even when the body is destroyed, the person survives as long as the brain does. So the answer to 'where am I?' is 'in the vat,' and the confusion the story generates just comes from the fact that our sense organs are attached to our bodies.

This is similar to Putnam's brain in a vat argument, where Dennett's point is that we can never be certain we are not envatted brains being fed fake experiences. The thought experiment also supports the psychological continuity theory of personal identity: since the person survives the body swap, what makes you you is the continuity of your memories and psychology rather than your body. Overall, Dennett uses the story to argue against Descartes' idea of an inner theater where the self sits and watches experience, which connects to his later multiple drafts model of consciousness."
