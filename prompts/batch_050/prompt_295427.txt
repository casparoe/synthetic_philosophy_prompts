I would like your actual credence, as a number, on the following proposition about quantum gravity: the smooth four-dimensional spacetime of general relativity is not fundamental — that is, in the correct theory of quantum gravity, spatiotemporal notions (metric, distance, locality, perhaps dimension itself) appear only in an approximate, derived description of some underlying structure that is not itself spatiotemporal. I am deliberately not asking which candidate theory is right; I am asking about the shared commitment that string theory, loop quantum gravity, causal set theory, and much of the holography literature seem to hold in common.

Please do not answer with a survey of positions or a shrug about underdetermination. I want a number, and I want the reasoning that produces it laid bare. Specifically, decompose your credence into the sub-questions it depends on and assign a rough probability to each, with a line or two of justification. The terms I would expect to see include at least the following, though you should add or split terms if you think the decomposition demands it: (i) the probability that the evidence from AdS/CFT and related holographic results — where a spatial dimension demonstrably emerges from a non-gravitational theory — generalises beyond anti-de Sitter boundary conditions to universes with cosmologies like ours; (ii) the probability that the black hole information problem and recent Page curve computations genuinely bear on the ontology of spacetime, rather than being purely quantum-information-theoretic results compatible with a fundamental spacetime; (iii) the probability that the near-universal convergence of research programmes on a non-fundamental classical spacetime reflects evidential pressure rather than sociological selection — after all, 'emergence' is also the only obvious way to reconcile quantum theory with gravity that anyone has found, which could make the convergence an artefact of a lack of alternatives; (iv) the probability that the question is even determinate — that 'fundamental versus emergent' has a fact of the matter here, rather than dissolving into rival formalisations of inter-theoretic dependence once a mature theory exists. Note that term (iv) is a probability that the other three are well-posed, so think carefully about how it enters the combination.

Then combine the terms, state your final credence, identify the single term your number is most sensitive to, and tell me what evidence or theoretical development would move that term most. For calibration: my own credence is about 0.6, and a colleague of mine puts hers at 0.9. If you can, say where you think that disagreement most likely lives within your decomposition.

Please write your answer in British English.
