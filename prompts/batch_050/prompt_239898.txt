At my reading group (mostly retired engineers plus one psychiatrist, so arguments are guaranteed) we got onto mental illness last week. Someone said what people always say in these discussions: depression is an illness just like diabetes, nobody blames a diabetic for needing insulin, so nobody should blame a depressed person for needing antidepressants. The psychiatrist nodded along and said this is the standard anti-stigma line and that it works. I kept quiet, because I'd just started reading Thomas Szasz, who as far as I can tell argued that "mental illness" is a metaphor that got promoted to a diagnosis: minds aren't the kind of thing that can literally be sick, only brains and bodies can, and absent some bodily lesion, calling a pattern of behavior a disease is a moral judgment wearing a lab coat.

Now, I spent forty years in experimental physics measuring actual physical quantities, so I know roughly what diabetes is. Beta cells stop producing insulin, blood glucose climbs, you can put a number on it with a cheap meter from the pharmacy, and the treatment replaces exactly the thing that's missing. When I try to build the correspondence table for the analogy I get: pancreas ↔ brain, insulin ↔ serotonin (or whichever neurotransmitter is in fashion), glucose reading ↔ symptom checklist. That last pair looks to me like its doing all the heavy lifting while being by far the weakest link in the chain.

Can you audit the analogy properly? Give me the mapping first, then seperate the disanalogies that actually matter for the anti-stigma conclusion from the ones that are just pedantic. My hunch is that "nobody gets committed against their will for high blood sugar" matters enormously and "insulin is a hormone and SSRIs aren't" matters not at all, but I could be wrong about either. What does the analogy quietly smuggle in about causation, about responsibility, and about who gets to overrule the patient's own account of their life? And the question I actually care about: does the decent conclusion — that mentally ill people deserve the same sympathy and social support as physically ill people — need the analogy at all, or can it stand on its own once the disanalogies are laid out? If the diabetes comparison is carrying more weight than it can bear, is there a better analogy waiting in the wings, or should we do what Szasz presumably wanted and drop the medical framing entirely, and then find some other route to the anti-stigma conclusion?
