For a graduate seminar I teach on philosophy of mind, I want the specification rather than the verdict. Hofstadter's strange-loop account of the self — the 'I' as a self-referential symbol that a sufficiently complex symbol system inevitably comes to contain, and that somehow loops back to be causally central to the system's behavior — is routinely either embraced as deep or dismissed as an evocative metaphor. Write the list of desiderata that would have to be fixed in advance of that dispute: what must a theory of selfhood actually do for the strange-loop account to count as a substantive answer to the problem of the self rather than a redescription of it?

Give me each requirement with the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which are non-negotiable for any theory of selfhood and which are demands of particular camps. The requirements I most want to see argued through are these, though you should add any I have missed:

1. The theory must explain why the self-symbol, if it is merely one symbol among millions, gets to be causally privileged — how a representational structure inside a physical system can bear on what that system does without violating causal closure at the physical level, or else must explain why downward causation talk is legitimate here.
2. It must supply a principled criterion distinguishing systems that have a self from systems that merely represent one — a novel narrated in the first person, a chatbot that says 'I', a thermostat with a feedback loop — since self-reference alone appears in all of them.
3. It must handle the pathology cases a theory of self cannot dodge: dense amnesia, advanced dementia, split-brain patients, depersonalization. In each case the account must say whether a self is present, diminished, multiplied, or gone, and the answers must not be ad hoc.
4. It must say something about the felt givenness of experience — why there is something it is like to be the loop — or explicitly argue that this demand is a confusion the theory is entitled to refuse.
5. It must respect whatever is true in the phenomenological distinction between a minimal self (the bare subject of the present moment) and a narrative self (the temporally extended character), rather than collapsing them.

Then: say whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, since if so the list itself is the interesting result. Identify the single requirement that poses the greatest threat to the strange-loop account specifically, and defend your choice. Close with a brief note — two or three sentences each, no more — on how the strange-loop view, narrative theories, and illusionism about the self fare against your list, keeping the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard.
