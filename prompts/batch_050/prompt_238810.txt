Constraints:

- Nobody in the story is irrational or misinformed. The divergence shouldn't depend on anyone making a mistake.
- The scenario itself should be one paragraph.

Then show me the case works. State what the frequentist says about the probability in question and why, and what the propensity theorist says and why. Say where a subjectivist could land on each side without changing anything about the setup. After that, tell me which feature of the scenario is doing all the work, what happens to each verdict if you remove that feature, and what the best line of attack is for a frequentist who wants to stop the case counting against them. If the case only works by quietly assuming propensities exist rather than making the frequentist's position genuinely uncomfortable, say so -- I'd rather know now than have a student point it out at me.
