"Explain what is meant by the procreation asymmetry: the claim that there is a morally relevant difference between (a) failing to bring into existence a person who would have had a good life and (b) bringing into existence a person who would have a bad life. Set out the four comparisons between existing and never existing that are usually taken to generate the asymmetry. Then consider the Seed Vault: you are the last caretaker of a vault of viable embryos. Pressing Button G would grow an embryo into a person with a flourishing life; pressing Button M would grow one into a person whose life is constant suffering. Most people judge that pressing M would be seriously wrong, while simply declining to press G is at most mildly regrettable and plausibly not wrong at all. Does the asymmetry vindicate these intuitions? Assess one objection to the asymmetry that arises from the case."

And here's the student's answer, word for word:

"The procreation asymmetry is the claim, first formulated by Derek Parfit and later developed by David Benatar, that the absence of pain and the absence of pleasure must be evaluated differently when we consider people who never exist. The standard formulation has four parts: (1) the presence of pain is bad; (2) the presence of pleasure is good; (3) the absence of pain is good even if there is nobody around to enjoy that absence; (4) the absence of pleasure is not bad unless there is somebody who is deprived of it. Applied to procreation: if I bring a miserable person into existence, the pain they suffer is straightforwardly bad (by 1); but if I decline to bring a happy person into existence, the pleasure they miss out on is not bad (by 4), because there is no one who is deprived of it. So pressing Button M is seriously wrong whilst merely not pressing Button G is not wrong, and the asymmetry therefore vindicates the standard intuitions about the Seed Vault.

One might object that our reaction to the vault shows we do after all think it is bad when happy people are not created — otherwise why would anyone feel even mild regret at not pressing G? The defender of the asymmetry can reply that this regret attaches to people who exist: the caretaker, would-be parents, or a society that would have valued new members, none of whom are the merely possible child. Since nobody is deprived, the regret does not track anything genuinely bad for the uncreated person, and the objection fails.

It also follows from the asymmetry that there is no moral reason to procreate whatsoever. Since failing to create a happy person is not bad, and creating a happy person is at best morally neutral in itself, procreation can never be morally favoured, and anyone who thinks they have a reason to have children is simply mistaken."
