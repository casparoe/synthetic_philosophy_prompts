I want to build a decision tree for this forum that sorts people by what they actually believe about infinite ethics, rather than by which summary of Bostrom's paper they last nodded along to. Assume everyone starting the tree is a vanilla total consequentialist for finite cases and has no problem with large finite numbers; the tree should find where they get off the train as the numbers become infinite. Each branch node should be a concrete case or question someone can answer without knowing the jargon: infinitely many people at barely-positive welfare versus finitely many at excellent welfare; two outcomes that each contain infinite total welfare but where one keeps getting ahead of the other after some point; whether a finite improvement to an already-infinite good registers as an improvement at all; whether you're willing to discount by time, by space, or by causal distance to make the sums finite; whether a prospect with unbounded upside and tiny probability (a St. Petersburg-style gamble) should be accepted at any finite cost. Leaves should include: some discounting view that restores finite arithmetic; an overtaking or eventual-dominance extension of totalism; bounded-utility consequentialism; lexical or trumping views where some goods outrank any amount of others; satisficing or threshold views that decline to rank everything; the finitist move of denying that infinite outcomes can actually obtain, making the problem moot; and the pessimistic leaf that accepts paralysis and treats infinite cases as genuinely undecidable. Please build the tree from the logical space of possible answers rather than from a list of named positions, note any combination of answers that looks unoccupied and say briefly whether that's because it's incoherent or merely undefended, and flag any pair of leaves your questions can't actually separate. Aim for five to seven branch nodes and around seven leaves.
