I'd like a piece of satirical journalism in the house style of The Onion: a punchy headline followed by 450–600 words of deadpan mock reporting. The story: the lending committee of Meridian Regional Bank has been called into emergency session after its annual fairness audit failed on mathematical rather than procedural grounds. The bank's credit model is calibrated within each demographic group and equalizes approval rates across them, yet produces unequal false-positive rates — and a junior analyst has circulated a two-page proof that, whenever underlying default rates differ between groups, no informative score-based model can satisfy calibration, equalized odds, and predictive parity at the same time, meaning at least one of the audit's three criteria must be dropped no matter what anyone does. The comedy should arise from the committee treating a theorem as a morale or compliance problem: members propose fixes that each quietly sacrifice one criterion while announcing the trilemma "resolved" (per-group thresholds that break calibration; reweighted training data that shifts but does not erase the base-rate gap; a human-in-the-loop review stage, as though the humans were exempt from the arithmetic; a motion to redefine "false positive" for audit purposes; a proposal to audit the model's "intentions" instead). Include at least one quote from the bank's chief ethics officer, who attributes the failure to insufficient stakeholder engagement, and one from the junior analyst, who has been uninvited from the follow-up meeting and now works on something called the Culture Committee. A reader versed in the algorithmic-fairness literature should recognize that you understand the impossibility results — without your citing any paper, theorem, or researcher by name — while a reader who isn't should still grasp why the committee is doomed and find it funny. Keep the irony straight-faced, the banking details plausible, and never break the fictional frame to editorialize. As the very last line, state the piece's satirical thesis in a single sentence, beginning "Summary:".
