I'm a novelist, not a political theorist, and I'm trying to sort the major characters in a novel into left, right, and genuinely hard-to-place without turning them into cardboard factions. The setting is fictional, but the ideologies are close enough to ours that I want the labels to feel earned rather than just vibes. Here are my gut verdicts:

- Mara wants low taxes, weak unions, strong property rights, and very little redistribution. She also thinks some people are naturally suited to rule and others to follow. Clearly right.
- Dev wants national healthcare, strong unions, high taxes on the rich, and worker ownership in large firms. But he also wants church authority protected, divorce discouraged, and sexual media censored. I still call him left, because the economic egalitarianism is the center of his politics, though this one feels shakier the longer I look at it.
- Anouk wants to abolish private ownership of major industry, national borders, prisons, gender roles, and most forms of representative government, replacing them with small democratic councils. Clearly left, even though she hates the existing state.
- Cassian is an old-fashioned monarchist. He thinks the crown owes every loyal subject food, housing, and basic security, but he rejects democracy, equal citizenship, and the idea that commoners have any claim to rule. I call him right despite the welfare guarantees.
- Petr wants to nationalize the banks and utilities, raise workers' wages, and guarantee jobs, but only for members of the dominant ethnic group. He wants strict borders and legal preferences for that group. I call him right despite the nationalization and pro-worker talk.
- Lio wants open borders, drug legalization, same-sex marriage, no inherited ranks, and no state religion, but also nearly unregulated markets and no redistributive welfare state. I instinctively call him right, because he'd rather accept large inequalities than give collective institutions power over private choices.
- Sera lives under an already democratic, materially egalitarian society and wants to preserve its institutions exactly as they are against reformers on both sides. I don't think this makes her right just because she's conservative in the dictionary sense.
- Ivo wants to halt economic growth, protect local customs and religious festivals, break up huge corporations, and replace them with cooperatives. He is suspicious of both centralized state power and individualistic consumer culture. He feels genuinely mixed, not merely like someone scoring four left points and four right points.
- Vale wants technological acceleration to dissolve nations, families, private property, and eventually biological differences into some radically equal posthuman collective. Despite sounding nothing like an ordinary progressive, I still read the project as left.

Is there one coherent principle behind these verdicts? I suspect policies like markets, welfare, tradition, or state power aren't basic enough, since several characters use the same policy for different ends. Maybe the real axis concerns whether hierarchy is natural, necessary, or corrigible, but I can already see that struggling with Lio and Dev.

Give me the best precise principle, or two rival principles if one won't do the work. For each clause, say which case forces it. Please also test the principle against new characters: a secular free-market egalitarian, a democratic nationalist who supports redistribution but restricts immigration, and a feminist theocrat who rejects capitalism. If one of my verdicts has to go, identify it and explain why. And be explicit about what would change your mind -- especially which case, distinction, or historical example would most likely undermine your proposed principle.
