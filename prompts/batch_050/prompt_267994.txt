I'm in a phil of language seminar and we've spent three weeks on metaphor, and I honestly can't tell what the fight between Davidson and the conceptual metaphor people (Lakoff, Johnson, that whole tradition) is even about anymore. Davidson says a metaphor means exactly what it would mean literally and that's it -- all the interesting stuff is something it makes you notice or do, not something it says. The CMT folks respond with piles of evidence that we systematically think of, say, arguments in terms of war or time in terms of money, as if that refutes him. But as far as I can tell Davidson could just shrug and say sure, that's all the causal/cognitive effects he was talking about, nothing about meaning. And the CMT folks could shrug back and say they never claimed to be doing Davidson-style semantics in the first place.

So my question: has this debate just been stalled for 40 years because the two sides are answering different questions -- one about what metaphorical utterances mean, one about how metaphor structures thought -- while both using the word "meaning"? Or is there a real substantive disagreement underneath, and if so where exactly does it live? Give me a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, based on what each side actually commits to, and tell me what would count as progress -- is there some finding or distinction that would settle it, or should we expect it to just keep going?
