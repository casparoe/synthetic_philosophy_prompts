Lately I've been reading about Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes -- the hard core, the protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, the negative and positive heuristics, and the distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts -- and I keep having the feeling that he's describing a startup, just one that runs on decades instead of quarters.

So: translate Lakatos's account into the vocabulary I actually run my company on, as faithfully as you can. Specifically, I want mappings for: the hard core and why Lakatos forbids tinkering with it; the protective belt as the layer that absorbs refutation; the positive heuristic as a plan for how to grow rather than what to do when you fail; a progressive problem shift (each move predicts something new and some of it gets confirmed) versus a degenerating one (each move only patches the last anomaly); and Lakatos's refusal to give any mechanical rule for when a programme must be abandoned -- his point that a degenerating programme can legitimately be kept alive and can even stage a comeback.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about. Which pieces translate cleanly? Which ones only survive with distortion -- my suspicion is that novel prediction has no real analogue in my world, since an A/B test confirms what I built rather than what I foresaw, but maybe I'm wrong and there's a stricter counterpart I should be imposing on myself. And which parts of Lakatos have no expression in startup vocabulary at all -- I'm guessing truth and empirical content are the casualties, since my framework's ultimate arbiter is survival and revenue, not correspondence to reality. For each piece of residue, say whether it shows my vocabulary is missing something a founder should have, or whether it shows Lakatos's criterion was doing less work than it appears.

Two applied questions to close. First: my board wants a written kill criterion -- a list of experimental results that would commit us to abandoning the hard core rather than pivoting the belt. State, in Lakatosian terms, whether that demand is coherent, and what the honest version of it would look like. Second: Goodhart's law -- a measure becoming a target and ceasing to be a good measure -- feels like it should slot into this translation somewhere; tell me where, or tell me it's a different animal entirely.

Finally, be straight with me about epistemic standing: say how confident you are in the fidelity of each major mapping, and single out the single point of the translation you are least sure of, explaining why that one is the weakest joint. Around 1,000 words.
