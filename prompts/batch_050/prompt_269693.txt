The chapter summarizes the psychology of concepts as follows: "Research has established that concepts are prototypes: your concept of bird is a mental list of the features typical birds share — feathers, flight, song — and you classify something as a bird by checking whether it possesses enough of them. Because a prototype stores only the average and discards the individual instances it was abstracted from, the view has one obvious, well-known failure: it predicts that every bird should seem equally bird-like, and it is frankly embarrassed by the finding that people treat robins as more typical than penguins. The same experiments show the theory's reach, though, since people will even rate 3 as a better odd number than 447, which proves that mathematical concepts are prototypes too. The main rival, the theory theory, holds that concepts are miniature scientific theories; but theories are explicit, consciously accessible bodies of belief, so that view cannot explain the split-second, unreflective classifications of young children, and the field has largely abandoned it."
