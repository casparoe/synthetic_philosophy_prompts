I'm designing the final exam for an upper-level philosophy of biology seminar, and I need one essay question on biological individuality that cannot be answered by reciting the textbook taxonomy of the debate (bottlenecks, germ-soma separation, superorganisms, holobionts). The question should be answerable in about 75 minutes and should force students to actually assess a position rather than survey the literature. Here is the framing I'd like the question to use. Suppose a colleague in the biology department proposes the following criterion: an entity is an organism just in case the fitness interests of its parts are aligned by a bottlenecked life cycle plus internal policing mechanisms (germline sequestration, uniparental transmission of symbionts, suppression of cheaters). Call this the alignment thesis. Its defender claims it explains why you count as one individual while a coral holobiont or a biofilm does not. Its critic objects that the thesis confuses the mechanisms that produce individuality with individuality itself, and mishandles clear cases.

The essay question should ask students to evaluate the alignment thesis, with these required moves: (a) state the thesis precisely enough that it generates testable predictions about particular cases; (b) test it against at least three of the following: a siphonophore colony, a Dictyostelium fruiting body formed from a chimeric aggregate, the aphid–Buchnera partnership, a clonal aspen stand of genetically identical ramets, and a honeybee colony; (c) diagnose whether the failures they find, if any, push toward pluralism about individuality (e.g., distinguishing evolutionary from physiological or metabolic individuals), toward a matter-of-degree view in which organismality tracks the cooperation-to-conflict ratio, or toward treating the bottleneck as an explanation of individuality rather than a criterion of it; and (d) say what theoretical work the bottleneck does if it turns out to be neither necessary nor sufficient. The intended crux I want the question to expose: chimeric slime mold fruiting bodies suggest individuality without a bottleneck; the siphonophore suggests that a bottleneck shared by colony and zooids cannot by itself fix which level counts as the individual; and the honeybee colony forces students to either bite the superorganism bullet or say what besides alignment (contiguity? physiological integration?) does the excluding.

Please deliver: (1) the exam question itself, worded as students will see it, including any permissible scaffolding but without tipping the answer; (2) a model answer of roughly 600 words written at the level of a strong student who has done the reading; and (3) a marking scheme out of 20 marks, built around five must-hit points with the marks each carries, a one-line note for each on how candidates typically get it wrong (e.g., treating the cases as illustrations rather than as tests, conflating high relatedness with a bottleneck, or describing all three resolutions without endorsing one), and finally an explicit statement of what separates an A answer from a B answer -- I want that distinction to turn on a specific intellectual move, not on polish or coverage.
