So I'm a junior and my school has this elective called Philosophy Through Film, and last week we had this whole unit on sad movies and why anyone would voluntarily watch them. My teacher said something offhand like "obviously nobody actually enjoys the sadness itself, you enjoy the movie despite it" and my friend Marcus went home and came back with an argument that he says proves the teacher wrong, but the argument also seems to prove something insane, and now we've been arguing about it at lunch for three days.

Here's his argument, cleaned up a bit. (1) To enjoy a tragedy as a tragedy, you have to be genuinely moved by the suffering in it — if the characters' pain left you totally cold, nobody would say you enjoyed the sad parts, you'd say you missed the point. (2) If you're genuinely moved by the suffering and you'd be disappointed if the suffering were removed from the story (which you would — nobody wants the version where nothing bad happens), then the sadness is part of what you're enjoying, not something you put up with for the sake of other stuff. (3) Your emotional response doesn't care whether suffering is fictional or real — the tears are real tears, your heart rate goes up the same way, there's no special "fiction filter" in the brain that switches the emotion off. (4) So enjoying a tragedy just IS taking pleasure in suffering. (5) And if suffering itself is what you're taking pleasure in, then the only thing stopping you from enjoying real suffering is practical stuff (consequences, guilt, not being allowed), not anything about your actual tastes. (6) So everyone who loves sad movies is basically the kind of person who would enjoy watching real people suffer if they could get away with it.

(6) is obviously deranged. Marcus's mom cries at literally every sad movie and she also fosters rescue dogs, she is not secretly a monster. Also the argument seems to prove way too much — by the same logic, everyone who likes horror movies wants to actually be chased by a murderer, and everyone who plays shooter games wants to shoot real people, and nobody believes that. But when I try to tell Marcus which step is wrong he just asks me "ok but WHICH ONE" and honestly I can't pin it down. My gut says something sneaky happens around (3) or (5), like the fact that you KNOW it's a movie is doing more work than he's admitting, but he says that's cope. One thing: I really don't want to solve it by saying "the sadness isn't real sadness, it's just pretend," because that feels like cheating and also false to my experience — I genuinely bawled during that movie we watched. So which step do I reject, what does rejecting it cost me, and is there a version where I get to say the sadness is real AND enjoyable AND it doesn't make me a bad person? Or is one of those three gotta go?
