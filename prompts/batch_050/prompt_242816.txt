So I recently got into this situation that's been making me obsess over Kavka's toxin puzzle, of all things. Short version: I signed up for one of those commitment contracts (money gets donated to a cause I despise if I don't hit my goal), and a friend pointed out that the smart move, on one reading, is to fully intend to do the thing while quietly knowing that future-me will weigh things up fresh when the time comes. And I realized my actual position is basically: you cannot sincerely intend to do something you already know you won't do when the moment arrives. Like, intention isn't just a mental state you can adopt at will, it's partly constituted by your expectations about your own future behavior. If I'm dead certain I'll chicken out, then whatever I'm doing now isn't intending, it's just... performing an intention, or wishing loudly.

That's the position. What would count against it?

I want this mapped out properly, not just the standard moves from the puzzle itself. Some things I'm wondering about:

- Are there real cases where people plainly intend something while being quite sure they won't do it? Like addicts making genuine resolutions, or soldiers, or whatever. Would actual examples move the needle, or can I always redescribe them as "not really intending"?
- What does the view predict about weaker cases -- where I'm not certain I'll fail, just pretty doubtful? If intention requires expected follow-through, does it come in degrees in a way that matches how we actually talk?
- Could a behavioral or neuroscientific result count against me here? Like if intending turned out to be a cleanly identifiable state that's present even when people predictably don't act?
- Is my view actually just definitional and therefore unfalsifiable? If any apparent counterexample gets absorbed by saying "then it wasn't a real intention," have I insulated myself from everything, and is that bad for a view about a concept like intention or is that just how conceptual claims work?

I'd like the potential defeaters ranked from "would genuinely sink this" to "awkward but survivable," and for each one, note whether someone holding my position would themselves concede it counts or would dismiss it as changing the subject. Also curious what the strongest version of the opposing intuition even is -- the person who says sure, I can intend right now to drink it, full stop, even knowing I won't. What are they noticing that I'm not?
