I'm putting together the final for my second-year early modern course and I want one essay question on Hume's Of Miracles (Enquiry, section X) that students can't answer by reciting the greatest-hits summary -- miracle = violation of a law of nature, uniform experience beats testimony, done. So I want the question built around a case Hume never had to think about: suppose a particle detector in a well-run lab registers an event that current theory says is impossible, and the 'testimony' for it is the machine readout plus the lab notebook plus a replication attempt, not a human witness at all. The question should force them to decide whether Hume's maxim is really about testimony as such or about weighing any evidence against any other, and whether his two strands come apart here -- the part 1 argument from uniform experience versus the part 2 points about how actual witnesses behave (love of the marvellous, ignorant and barbarous nations, rival religions cancelling out), most of which don't obviously apply to a detector. I'd also like the question to make them deal with the awkward fact that the readout's reliability itself rests on the very theory the event is supposed to refute.

Deliverables: (1) the question worded exactly as it would appear on the exam, answerable in about 75 minutes; (2) a model answer of roughly 600 words; (3) a marking scheme out of 20 built around five must-hit points with the marks each carries, plus one line per point on the specific way students typically botch it -- e.g. treating Hume as arguing that miracles are impossible rather than that testimony can't establish one, or lumping the Indian prince and the frost in with the miracle cases when Hume explicitly says that event was not contrary to the prince's experience, just not conformable to it. The scheme should make clear what an A answer does that a B answer doesn't -- I'm expecting the B answer to get the maxim right but never notice the circularity problem with the instrument, so the A/B line should live somewhere near there. Dont just say 'more detail gets more marks', tell me the actual moves.
