So if two agents are fully rational, they must have the same prior -- if their priors genuinely differed, at least one of them would be basing theirs on nothing, which is irrational. (That's the common prior assumption as a requirement of rationality.)

(2) The only legitimate source of differing beliefs between rational agents is differing information. So two rational people with a common prior who end up disagreeing are just conditioning that prior on different evidence -- they sit in different cells of their information partitions, so to speak.

(3) Aumann showed that if two agents with a common prior have posteriors about some event that are common knowledge between them, those posteriors are identical. You can't "agree to disagree."

(4) So whenever two rational agents both know they're both rational, and they exchange their views openly (making them common knowledge), their views must converge.

(5) But people disagree constantly -- about politics, religion, economics, whether some movie is good -- and they do this openly, while knowing the other person knows their view, and often while sincerely regarding each other as smart and well-informed.

(6) Combining (4) and (5): essentially every actual disagreement involves either someone being irrational, or someone not actually believing the other person is rational, or the views not really being common knowledge. And since these escape hatches are pretty extreme, basically all human disagreement is a giant pool of irrationality.

(7) It gets worse. By the same logic (the no-trade stuff), whenever two rational people with a common prior consider a bet, each should realize the other's willingness to bet is evidence, and after iterating this, no mutually acceptable bet exists. So every speculative trade, every sports bet between friends, every short seller on earth involves irrationality on at least one side, guaranteed.

(8) That last conclusion is absurd, or at least it's way stronger than anything I'm willing to sign up for. Markets clear every day, and plenty of disagreements seem like exactly the kind you could have between two rational people.
