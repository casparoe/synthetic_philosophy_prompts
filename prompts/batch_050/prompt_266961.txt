Write a parody etiquette column — the genteel, unflappable register of a Miss Manners-style advice page — for the society of donors, lobbyists, and elected officials, answering three or four readers' letters in about 650 words. The columnist's rules of politeness must map exactly onto real American campaign-finance doctrine, so that good manners and legal compliance are the same thing. Suggested material: a letter asking whether it is improper to hand a senator a check while discussing pending legislation (the columnist's horror at discussing the two in the same breath should encode the quid pro quo standard — the only true obscenity — while noting that the same check and the same conversation on separate afternoons are perfectly gracious); a letter from a billionaire asking how much generosity is too much (no limit, provided the money flows through an "independent" committee and he and the candidate observe the social rule of never speaking to one another, which the columnist treats as the height of refinement); a letter asking how one may properly communicate one's wishes to the independent committee one is forbidden to coordinate with (the columnist recommends announcing them publicly, on a website, where anyone might read them); and, if useful, a note on which advertisements are tasteful (the vulgarity of uttering words like "vote for" or "defeat" outright, versus the decorum of merely remarking that a candidate hates puppies). The satire must exaggerate real features of the doctrine — the contribution/expenditure distinction, the narrowing of "corruption" to explicit quid pro quo, the coordination firewall and its evasion, the express-advocacy line — rather than invent absurdities, so that a campaign-finance lawyer laughs in recognition and finds the portrait fair. Keep it deadpan throughout: the columnist must never notice anything amiss, and the reductio should land entirely through her serene confidence that this is simply how civilized people behave. No real persons, living or dead, may appear; invent the letter-writers.
