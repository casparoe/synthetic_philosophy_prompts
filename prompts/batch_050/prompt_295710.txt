so I just had this stupidly long argument with a friend (like 2 hours at a bar, got way too heated lol) about whether "moral compromise" between people with genuinely different values is actually a real thing or just a polite word for everyone selling out a little. neither of us budged and now I want to see the argument done properly by two people who actually know what they're doing.

Write a dialogue between two characters, Mira and Theo, who run a small grantmaking fund together. The fund has exactly enough money to fully fund one of two projects per year:

- Project A: a malaria prevention program in sub-Saharan Africa
- Project B: a sanctuary and legal advocacy program for farm animals

Mira is a thoroughgoing human-welfarist: she thinks animal suffering counts for something but so little compared to human death and disease that funding B would be, by her lights, a serious moral error. Theo is a total utilitarian about suffering who thinks factory farming dwarfs everything else in sheer quantity of preventable misery, so funding A would be, by his lights, nearly throwing the money away. Neither thinks the other's cause is worthless exactly, but each thinks the other's project is a genuinely bad use of the money.

Theo proposes a compromise: alternate years, or split each year's budget 50/50, and he frames it as gains from trade, like two countries specializing and exchanging — each of them "concedes" something they disvalue in order to get more of what they value than they'd get from a coin flip or a fight. Mira is the skeptic: she thinks this trade analogy papers over something real, because in ordinary trade both parties end up better off by their own lights, whereas here each of them is being asked to actively fund what they consider a moral mistake, and "half of a wrong" isn't obviously better than a fair lottery where you never have to dirty your own hands.

Requirements for the dialogue:

1. Both of them have to be genuinely sharp. Do NOT make Mira a sanctimonious deontologist cartoon or Theo a spreadsheet-brained utilitarian cartoon. Mira's skepticism should be principled, and Theo should actually understand the objection he's responding to.
2. They must respond to each other's actual arguments, not talk past each other. At least twice, one of them should concede a specific sub-point ("ok, fair, but...") before pushing back.
3. At some point Theo has to deal head-on with the strongest version of Mira's worry: that the compromise has a kind of "moral remainder" — that both of them walk away having done something they think is wrong, and calling it efficient doesn't dissolve that.
4. At some point Mira has to deal head-on with the strongest version of Theo's reply: that refusing to trade isn't purity but just leaving joint value on the table, and that her "clean hands" are bought at the price of more actual suffering.
5. The dialogue shouldn't end with one of them converting. It should end somewhere more interesting than agreement — e.g., them locating the exact point where their disagreement actually lives, or realizing they were using "compromise" to mean two different things. Your choice, but earn it.
6. Keep it naturalistic — these are two tired colleagues, not debate robots. Interruptions, half-finished sentences, the occasional jab are all fine.
