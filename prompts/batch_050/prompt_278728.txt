so I'm putting together a short position statement for an AI safety reading group I'm in and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Roughly what I believe:

(1) The orthogonality thesis: intelligence and final goals are independent dimensions, so you can in principle have arbitrarily high intelligence combined with basically any final goal, including ones we'd find completely insane.

(2) Instrumental convergence: for a sufficiently capable agent, nearly any final goal implies the same instrumental subgoals -- self-preservation, resource acquisition, resisting shutdown, protecting its goal content from modification. So you can predict a lot about a system's behavior without knowing what it ultimately wants.

(3) A superintelligent system would understand human values better than any human does, and because it understands them so well, small errors in how we specify its goals would mostly get corrected by the system itself -- the way a good employee figures out what you actually meant instead of following a badly worded instruction literally.

(4) Current LLMs are basically myopic pattern matchers without real goals, which is why they're largely safe and why the absence of power-seeking behavior in todays models is exactly what you'd expect.

(5) The real danger is scaling: keep making these same architectures more capable and at some point you get a system that does plan over long horizons in pursuit of goals, and then everything in (2) starts applying.

(6) Despite that I'm fairly optimistic about alignment overall, because the goal axis can be set through training (RLHF and whatever succeeds it) more or less independently of the capability axis.

Questions: which of these are genuinely in tension, which pairs only look like they are but dissolve if you state them carefully, and for each real tension what's the least costly revision and what does it cost? Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all the same. If one single revision would clean up most of the mess, tell me which one. My own guess is that (1) and (2) are the problem area, but I might be wrong about that.
