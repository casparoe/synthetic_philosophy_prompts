Alright, I retired from physics (condensed matter) about three years ago and since then I've fallen down the philosophy-of-democracy rabbit hole, mostly via social choice theory. Reading group meets next month and the topic is populism, so I wrote down where I think I've landed. I'd like to know if the package hangs together before I embarrass myself in front of people who actually know this stuff.

Here's my list:

(1) The Arrow/Condorcet material convinced me there's no such thing as "the will of the people" as a coherent preference ordering. Individual preferences can be perfectly transitive while the group's aren't, and there's no aggregation rule that fixes this without paying an unacceptable price. So when a populist claims to speak for the people's will, they're invoking something that doesn't exist.

(2) That said, I think the populists have a real point: when policy persistently and systematically diverges from what majorities say they want, that is evidence of elite capture, and it's a genuine democratic failure. Democracy ought to make policy track majority opinion, at least roughly and over time.

(3) Since cycling means there's often no option that beats all the others, whoever controls the agenda legitimately determines the outcome. Someone has to set the agenda, there is no neutral way to do it, and I don't see a principled complaint against whoever ends up doing it.

(4) It's perfectly democratic for a constitutional court to strike down a statute even when that statute has broad majority support.

(5) We should use referendums alot more than we do, since they ask the voters directly instead of filtering everything through party elites and their machinations.

Some of these feel like they're in tension but I can't tell which tensions are real and which are just surface-level. Please go through them, tell me which pairs actually conflict and which only look like they do, and rank the real ones by how bad the damage is. For each real tension, tell me the cheapest fix -- i.e. which commitment to weaken or drop and what that costs me. I don't have a strong attachment to any particular one of these, so don't pull punches, but do be specific about what commits me to what rather than giving me a general lecture on populism.
