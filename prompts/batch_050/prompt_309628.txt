So my roommate and I got into it over the Catalonia stuff a while back and he hit me with this argument that I still can't shake, because the conclusion is obviously nuts but every step sounds reasonable when he says it. Here it is as best I remember:

(1) A government is only legitimate over people who consent to be governed by it -- that's the whole idea behind "governments derive their just powers from the consent of the governed," and if you deny it you're basically saying rule by force is fine.
(2) Consent that can't be withdrawn isn't really consent. A club you can join but never leave isn't a voluntary association, it's a trap.
(3) So any group of people that no longer consents to its government must have the right to leave it, i.e. to secede.
(4) What counts as "a group of people" can't be decided by the very government they want to leave -- that would let the government veto every secession by defining the relevant group as the whole country, which makes the right empty.
(5) So the group gets to define itself: any collection of people with a shared identity or shared interests who vote to leave, get to leave.
(6) But then: my street could hold a vote, decide we share an identity (we have a group chat and a strong opinon about the new parking rules), and secede from the city to stop paying municipal taxes while still using the roads. Same for the richest neighborhood in every country. Same for any company town. Secession all the way down until the state is just whoever didn't bother opting out.
(7) That's absurd, so something in (1)-(5) has to give. He says it's (1) and consent theory is dead. I really don't want to give up (1) -- it feels like the one thing political philosophy actually got right.

Where does this actually go wrong? Walk me through which premise you'd reject and what rejecting it costs -- like, if I give up (4) instead, am I just handing every central government a veto, and is that maybe fine? If I give up (2), is there any real difference between "consent" and "subjection"? And if the answer is something like "secession rights only apply to groups with some special status" (oppressed nations, historic peoples, whatever), tell me what that status could be that includes Catalonia or Quebec but excludes my street, without just being circular. Also curious whether the standard fixes here end up amounting to "consent matters but only for big enough groups," and whether that's a principled position or just a pragmatic patch pretending to be one.
