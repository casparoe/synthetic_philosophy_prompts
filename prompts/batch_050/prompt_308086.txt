Something I keep running into at work and in my family, and I want to know if philosophers actually have a name for it. I manage a small team, and one of my people, Maya, started staying an extra hour most days to help close up, even though nobody asked her to and it's not in her contract. For months everyone was grateful, it was clearly above and beyond. But somewhere along the way it flipped: now if she leaves at five people are visibly anoyed, and last week our director told her she was "leaving the team in the lurch" by going home on time. The same extra hour that used to earn her praise now, when skipped, earns her blame. Same thing with my aunt who hosted every holiday for fifteen years and then, the one year she said she wanted a break, got treated like she'd committed some kind of betrayal. And a friend of mine who always drove everyone around and is now basically not allowed to drink at parties because he's "the driver."

So the pattern is: an act starts out clearly optional and praiseworthy, but if you do it reliably enough it silently gets reclassified as required — and skipping it becomes an actual wrong, not just a dissapointment. A friend who took an ethics class told me the word "supererogation" covers the above-and-beyond part, but as far as I can tell that's about the act itself, not about this conversion process where generosity quietly hardens into obligation and the earlier free gifts get retroactively redescribed as installments on a debt. Is there an established term or literature for the conversion specifically? And if there isn't, can you help me pin the thing down properly: what exactly is doing the work here (expectations? reliance? some fairness intuition about what peers can demand of each other?), how is it different from breaking a promise (Maya never promised anything) and from an ordinary shift in social norms, and where are the edges — does it happen after one act or only a pattern, does it matter whether the beneficiaries came to depend on it, and can the person ever reset things without being blamed? I'm not going to be satisfied with just a label, I want to understand what the phenomenon actually is.
