Is there a name for this? I got into it with my sister over the holidays about whose memory of some childhood thing is right, and at one point she goes, look, I know my memory is good, I can think of tons of times I remembered something and it turned out to be exactly how I remembered it. And I said that's cheating, you're checking your memory against your memory, and she said so what, the times all line up. The annoying thing is I couldn't say exactly what was wrong with it, because it's not a circular argument in the textbook way -- she's not just going "my memory is reliable because my memory is reliable," she's pointing at this whole pile of cases where everything coheres. But every single item in the pile got there using the very faculty on trial.

Once I noticed it I started seeing it everywhere. The fuel gauge in my truck: if I wanted to convince myself it works, I'd note that it drops as I drive, jumps when I fill up, never reads full right after it read empty -- but all of that is me looking at the gauge. A guy I work with insists he's a great judge of character and his evidence is that the people he vouched for turned out great, which he knows because he keeps tabs on them, i.e. judges them. Even scientists seem to do a version when they calibrate one instrument against a second one that was itself calibrated against a third, and somewhere the chain loops back. So my questions: does this pattern -- a source or faculty vouching for itself through its own track record -- have an established name, and is it one thing or several? How is it different from plain begging the question, because it honestly feels like it should count for *something* (a gauge that "agrees with itself" ten thousand times seems better off than one you checked once, but if the check is worthless, ten thousand worthless checks shouldn't add up to anything). And if it's worthless, what's the non-cheating way anyone ever establishes that a basic faculty like memory or perception works at all? I'd rather not just get a label -- I want to know where the edges of the concept are, what's genuinely inside it and what only looks like it (is my sister's move the same as the fuel gauge one?), and whether philosophers think there's any legitimate version of it.
