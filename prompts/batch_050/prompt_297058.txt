I cover science for a general-interest magazine and am writing a long feature on the spread of machine-learning methods into a field I will call molecular epidemiology. The generational divide is stark: senior figures tell me the new work is not real science because it predicts without explaining, while the younger researchers say their critics simply cannot see what the models are doing. Kuhn's framework seems tailor-made for this. If paradigms are incommensurable, then the two sides are not really disagreeing — they are talking past each other by structural necessity — and the senior objections are not arguments to be answered but symptoms of a paradigm in decline, fated to be resolved by retirements rather than rebuttals. My editor and I are therefore inclined to write the piece without adjudicating the methodological dispute at all, treating the old guard's criticisms as sociologically interesting but epistemically weightless. Before we commit, I want the inference stress-tested. Does Kuhn's incommensurability thesis actually license treating cross-paradigm criticism as contentless in this way, given that both camps here share journals, datasets, funding panels and much of their vocabulary? Does his account of scientific revolutions entail that argument across the divide is futile? And if the framing survives scrutiny, how should I read the fact that the younger researchers still publish careful rebuttals in the establishment journals — is that merely the rhetoric of conversion? Please identify anything in the reasoning above that misstates Kuhn, since the architecture of the whole piece depends on it.
