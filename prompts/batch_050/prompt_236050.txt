I'm preparing a handout for a graduate reading group in quantum foundations: a decision tree that sorts a reader from the starting commitment "I accept standard quantum mechanics — unitary evolution plus the Born rule — and I agree there is a genuine measurement problem" to a named position on the status of the wavefunction. The leaves must include Everettian many-worlds, GRW-style objective collapse, Bohmian mechanics, QBism, relational quantum mechanics, and a psi-epistemic view in the ontological-models sense (the kind illustrated by toy models where distinct quantum states correspond to overlapping distributions over hidden states). Phrase every branch node as a question the reader can answer without knowing which label it leads to — e.g., questions about what, if anything, physically changes when a measurement outcome appears; whether two agents may correctly assign different pure states to the same system; whether collapse is a physical process or an update of information; and which classical desideratum (a single observer-independent outcome, measurement independence, locality) the reader is least willing to surrender. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first lay out the questions and the answer combinations they permit, then locate the actual positions, and for each unoccupied combination note whether it is empty because incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Explicitly flag any leaves your tree cannot separate, and address at least these two hard cases: (a) Bohmian readers who treat the wavefunction as a real physical field versus as a law-like or nomological entity, and (b) Everett versus GRW, both of which take the wavefunction as real — show exactly where they split. Conclude with one worked example path per leaf, so a reader can check the tree against a familiar route to each position.
