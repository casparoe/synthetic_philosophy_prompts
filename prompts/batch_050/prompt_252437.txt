My roommate heard a podcast and now keeps throwing the phrase consequentialist cluelessness at me during dinner. His version, roughly, is: 'Because every action ripples into the indefinite future, a consequentialist can never know whether donating to charity, eating lunch, or stepping aside for a stranger will, centuries later, cause slightly more or less value; therefore all actions are morally tied unless we secretly smuggle in common sense. Expected value talk makes this worse, because a microscopic chance of an astronomically huge future effect always swamps the near-term reasons normal people care about, so the view either collapses into paralysis or into fanaticism about the far future. Rule-consequentialism and virtue ethics dodge this completely because they can just follow stable rules and stop calculating.' He says this is basically a refutation of trying to do good by forecasting consequences, though when pressed he admits maybe there is a difference between not knowing the exact outcome and having literally no reason to prefer one option over another.

I’m inclined to think he is half-right and half-making a sophomore move, but I can’t say exactly where the move is.
