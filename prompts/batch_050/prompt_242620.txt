A retired professor who gave a guest lecture in my philosophy of language seminar handed out notes glossing Fitch's knowability result as follows: 'The verificationist holds that every truth is in principle knowable. Fitch showed that, given two assumptions no theory of knowledge can do without — that knowledge distributes over conjunction and that only truths can be known — this thesis collapses into the claim that every truth is in fact known. Since that claim is obviously false, verificationism about truth, and with it Dummett-style anti-realism, stands refuted. The proof is a few lines of pure logic and goes through in any respectable logical system, so there is no logical escape route; the only way out is to deny that knowledge is factive.'

The lecture was confident and the professor is reputable, so my inclination is to trust the summary, but I am uneasy: verificationists did not simply abandon their view after 1963, which makes me suspect they had some reply that the notes are not mentioning.
