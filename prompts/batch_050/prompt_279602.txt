Two of my coursemates had a proper row in the pub last night and I'd like a referee.

Some background: there's a politician in the news at the moment who keeps saying that he alone speaks for 'the will of the people', and that parliament, the courts, the media etc. are just obstacles between him and that will. Tom said this is exactly the sort of thing Rousseau rules out, and read us this bit from The Social Contract (Book II, ch. 1, Cole translation) off his phone:

"I hold then that Sovereignty, being nothing less than the exercise of the general will, can never be alienated, and that the Sovereign, who is no less than a collective being, cannot be represented except by himself: the power indeed may be transmitted, but not the will."

Tom's reading: you can hand a leader power but never your will, so anyone claiming to *embody* the people's will isn't just being arrogant, they're saying something that's literally impossible on Rousseau's view. The whole populist pitch is a category error.

Priya pushed back on two fronts. First, she said the passage is about sovereignty not being alienable in principle, i.e. it's a claim about what legitimate authority is, not a description of what can happen -- obviously a leader *can* go around claiming to speak for the people, and Rousseau's point doesn't stop voters rallying behind him, it just labels it illegitimate. Second, she pointed out that Rousseau himself says, a page later, that the commands of rulers can pass for general wills so long as the sovereign people doesn't oppose them, and that "universal silence is taken to imply the consent of the people" -- so a leader with an acquiescent, supportive public could be channelling the general will after all, and Tom's got it backwards.

Tom replied that the silence bit only applies where the people remain 'free to oppose' and simply don't, which he says is nothing like a leader actively claiming to *be* the people's voice. Priya said that distinction does no real work.

Who's right about which points? And is either of them missing something about what Rousseau actually means by will being untransmittable?
