Quick ask: I'm prepping a discussion group on the "violence is declining" debate (the Pinker-ish claim that war, homicide and so on have trended down over the centuries, plus all the pushback against it), and I want a decision tree I can hand out that takes someone from "I don't know whether violence has declined, or even whether the question is well-posed" to a named position.

Build the tree from the actual fault lines of the debate, not from a list of famous names. The questions I want doing the sorting work:

- whether "less violence" should be measured per capita or in absolute terms, and -- the bit I really want you to wrestle with -- what the right denominator even is when comparing across eras (deaths per 100,000 people? per person-year at risk? per opportunity for conflict?), given that world population has multiplied many times over
- whether structural violence counts (harm done through institutions, preventable deprivation, that sort of thing) or only direct, deliberate violence
- whether the statistics of war are fat-tailed enough that a long stretch without a very big war tells you almost nothing about whether the underlying risk has actually dropped
- whether cross-era comparisons are even commensurable, or whether "violence" is too theory-laden a notion for there to be a fact of the matter about its long-run trend
- whether a fall in observed deaths settles anything when the capacity for violence (weapons stockpiles, destructive potential per war) hasn't gone away

Each node should be phrased as a question someone could honestly answer without already knowing which position it lands them on. For the leaves: use the real position names where they exist (decline theorist, statistical sceptic, definitional expansionist, constructivist about the whole question, etc.), and where a combination of answers is coherent but nobody seems to defend it, coin a name and say in a sentence why the leaf is empty -- is the view incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? If two leaves can't really be seperated by your tree, flag that openly rather than papering over it.

Audience is a mixed undergrad/postgrad philosophy of science seminar, so aim for roughly 5-8 leaves and keep each node's question short and concrete.

Please write it in British English. At the end, add a short note on where the tree is most likely to mislead: which sorting question you'd scrap first if someone pushed back on it, and what kind of objection would force you to restructure the whole thing rather than just tweak a branch.
