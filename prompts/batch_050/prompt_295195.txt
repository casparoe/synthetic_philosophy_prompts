I am preparing a one-page flowchart to help new members of an institutional review board figure out what they actually believe about when randomizing patients between treatments is ethically permissible. Please construct a decision tree whose root is the shared premise that a proposed trial assigns patients between treatments A and B, and whose leaves are the main positions on the role of uncertainty in research ethics: theoretical equipoise, clinical equipoise, the simple uncertainty principle, and the view that the equipoise requirement misconstrues research ethics and should be replaced by a net-risk or non-exploitation test, plus at least one unoccupied position that your questions make available but that nobody seems to defend. Each branch node should be a single question a physician could answer without knowing the labels — for instance, whether what matters is the enrolling doctor's own state of mind or the distribution of judgment across the expert community, whether genuine disagreement within that community suffices or the evidence must be roughly balanced, and whether the permissibility condition must also function as a stopping rule once data begin to accumulate. Anchor every node in at least one of three cases: (i) a comparative-effectiveness trial of two standard-of-care chemotherapy regimens; (ii) a placebo-controlled trial of a new antidepressant where an effective treatment already exists; (iii) a trial in which the enrolling surgeon privately believes A is superior while the field remains split. For each leaf, give the position's name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment — ideally, what it implies about case (iii). Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot in principle separate, and for each unoccupied combination of answers say briefly whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.
