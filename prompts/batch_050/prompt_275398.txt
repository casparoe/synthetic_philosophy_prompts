So I keep finding myself leaning on the strategy-stealing assumption when I argue with people about AI risk -- roughly the idea that for any plan a misaligned AI could use to grab power or resources, an aligned AI (or humans with an aligned AI's help) could in principle run the same plan at least as well, so being "the good guys" isn't a systematic handicap. The name comes from that old result in combinatorial game theory where you show the second player can't be in a winning position because they could just steal the first player's winning strategy, but obviously the AI version is way less airtight.

What would count against it? I want a genuine map of where this assumption is exposed, not just the one-line "well what if the other AI is smarter" reply. Some things I'm curious about:

- Are there strategies that only work if you lack scruples -- deception, blackmail, threats you actually have to be willing to carry out, burning resources the aligned side can't afford to burn -- and does the assumption quietly fail there, or can a constrained agent fake or substitute for all of them? Which specific asymmetry would be fatal vs merely awkward?
- The assumption seems to smuggle in conditions like "the aligned AI is comparably capable" and "it acts in time." How much capability gap or delay breaks it, and would its defenders concede that as a real defeater or just redescribe it as "well then the assumption didn't apply"?
- What does the assumption actually predict that its denial doesn't? Like, what observable feature of the world would tell me which side is right?
- Has it been qualified into unfalsifiability? Every time I poke at it, someone retreats to "approximately true" or "true given enough lead time," and I'm no longer sure what would even settle the question. Is that a problem for this kind of assumption, or is that just what a reasonable heuristic looks like?

Rank the defeaters from "this would kill it outright" to "this is annoying but survivable," and be honest if you think the whole thing is more of a vibes-based hope than a checkable claim.
