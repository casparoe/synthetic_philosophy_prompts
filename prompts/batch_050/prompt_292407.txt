I got into a dumb late-night argument about the old sphex wasp example (the digger wasp that drags its paralyzed cricket to the burrow, pops in to check, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches while it's inside, drags it back and does the whole check again, over and over, like a tape rewinding). My roommate's position is that the wasp is basically a machine and we're basically wasps with better PR. I think there's a real line but I can't state it, so here's me trying to triangulate it from my gut verdicts on cases. The wasp's reset loop: sphexish, no question. A chess engine repeating the same losing move in a position that differs only by one pawn being one square over: sphexish. A GPS that routes you into a closed road and then, when you drive past the turn it wanted, just recalculates the same closed road from your new position: sphexish. But a jazz musician who leans on the same licks every solo: not sphexish, just has a style. My grandpa telling the same story every holiday, beat for beat: not sphexish, though it's uncomfortably close to the professor who gives literally the same lecture for twenty years, which I do want to call sphexish-adjacent. A colleague who, whenever you refute his argument, rephrases the same argument slightly and presents it as a response: sphexish, even though he's technically generating novel sentences. Someone with OCD checking the stove forty times: I don't want to say sphexish, but I can't articulate why the wasp's forty rechecks are and these aren't. And a habit like always ordering the same thing at a restaurant: not sphexish, even though it's a rigid trigger-response pattern if anything is.

So what principle, if any, sorts these the way I want? My first instinct was "flexibility under perturbation" -- sphexishness is revealed when the world pushes back and the system can't adjust. But that convicts the OCD case and the grandpa, and it acquits the GPS if the engineers add a road-closure database, which feels like it misses something, since the GPS still has no idea what it's doing. My second instinct was "the system must represent what it's doing and why" -- but that risks convicting the wasp and acquitting my refutation-recycling colleague, who definitely has representations and is definitely looping. Maybe the line isn't flexibility vs. rigidity but something about which level of the behavior is rigid (fixed goal, flexible means = fine; fixed means, or a goal that survives its own reasons = sphexish?). I'd like a candidate principle stated precisely enough that I can poke it, plus an honest list of which of my verdicts it forces me to drop.

Then the two cases I actually care about, where I'm stuck. First: a current large language model, which is flexible in some sense (paraphrases, adapts to context) but will confidently repeat a wrong answer if you push on it, and arguably never adjusts the deeper pattern no matter how the conversation perturbs it. Second: me, driving my commute on autopilot, arriving with no memory of the drive -- but if a kid runs into the road I snap into full alertness instantly. Is that flexible responsiveness layered on top of a sphexish substrate, and does the snapping-out save it? And the thing underneath all this: my roommate says antisphexishness can't be what free will is anyway, since a thermostat that adapted creatively would still just be a fancier machine. Is he right that flexibility is at best necessary and nowhere near sufficient for freedom -- and if so, is there anything a behavior could show, from the outside, that would settle whether something is sphexish all the way down?
