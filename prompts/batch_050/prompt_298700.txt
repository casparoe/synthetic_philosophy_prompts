I recently defended my dissertation proposal, and next month I'm presenting its central chapter to our department's ethics workshop, whose audience I know well: it includes two committed egalitarians in the spirit of Larry Temkin who think inequality is impersonally bad even when it's bad for no one, a hard-nosed classical utilitarian who thinks both prioritarianism and egalitarianism are just arithmetic with a guilty conscience, and a Scanlonian contractualist who suspects the whole debate rests on an aggregation framework we should abandon. My talk defends prioritarianism against the leveling-down objection, but via a specific route: I concede that leveling down can make things better in one respect (equality) while arguing that it cannot make things better all things considered, because betterness must be anchored in what is good or bad for persons. On my view, a state of affairs in which everyone is worse off cannot be better than one in which everyone is better off, since there is no one for whom the leveled-down state is better, and I treat this person-affecting constraint as non-negotiable rather than as one value among others.

Prepare me for the question period. Give me the objections in roughly the order I'll receive them and from whom, phrased as each of these audience members would actually phrase them — the Temkinians will press me with cases of natural inequality across isolated populations who can never interact, and with the charge that my constraint collapses into a slogan when applied to future-generations cases; the utilitarian will accuse me of smuggling in a distributive intuition I can't ground; the contractualist will ask why I'm talking about states of affairs at all. For each objection, indicate how damaging it is to my overall position, what I should concede graciously and what I should hold, and sketch a reply I can deliver in under two minutes. Finally, tell me which objection I'm most hoping nobody raises — I suspect it's something about what my person-affecting constraint implies when the leveled-down world contains better-off people who simply don't exist in the unequal one — and help me prepare an honest answer rather than a dodge.
