I'm designing a one-page flowchart for an undergraduate aesthetics course, meant to sort students by their considered verdicts on cases rather than by which label sounds right. Write a decision tree that starts from four concrete cases every respondent must first take a position on: (1) a MIDI score that has never been performed, (2) a cover version that changes the instrumentation but keeps every pitch and rhythm, (3) a studio album assembled entirely by editing, such that no performance of the whole album ever occurred, and (4) a piece whose original instrumentation is lost, now played on modern equivalents. Each branch node should be a question a student could answer without knowing any theory's name (e.g., whether the work is identical to its sound structure, whether the circumstances of its composition are part of what it is, whether a recording can itself be the work rather than a document of one), and each leaf a position in the ontology of musical works: pure sonic Platonism, the contextualist view that works are structures-indicated-at-a-time-and-place, the nominalist view that the work just is the set of its correct performances or copies, the view that in the recording tradition the track itself is the work of art, and the view that a performance is essential for any work to exist at all. Note explicitly which leaf, if any, cannot be reached without further questions about works for electronic media, and flag any two leaves your tree sorts identically. Where a branch combination corresponds to no defended position, say so and give one sentence on whether that corner of the logical space is empty because it's incoherent or merely unoccupied.
