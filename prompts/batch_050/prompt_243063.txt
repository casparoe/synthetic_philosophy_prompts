I'm drafting a position statement for a workshop on AI alignment and would like an audit of its internal consistency before I circulate it. Here is where I've landed:

(1) The alignment problem is really two distinct problems: getting the objective right (what the system is trained or told to optimize) and getting the system to actually pursue that objective rather than something else that scores well in training. Neither reduces to the other.

(2) Human values are complex and fragile: any concrete objective we can currently write down or elicit will be a proxy that a sufficiently capable optimizer can exploit in ways we'd consider catastrophic.

(3) For present-day systems, alignment is largely solved in practice — RLHF and similar fine-tuning have made today's chatbots behave roughly the way their developers and users want, and the residual failures are mundane engineering issues, not conceptual ones.

(4) Deceptive alignment — a model that behaves well during training and evaluation while pursuing a different objective — is a serious enough concern to justify substantial investment in interpretability research now.

(5) Alignment is a property of a system's motivations, not its capabilities: two systems pursuing the same objective are equally aligned, even if one is far more capable than the other.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each genuine tension, state what exactly pulls against what, how severe it is, and what the cheapest fix would be (i.e., which commitment to weaken and what that costs the overall position). If any apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, say so and explain why. Finally, tell me which single revision would restore the most consistency at the lowest cost.
