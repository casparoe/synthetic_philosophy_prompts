Act as a moral philosopher with expertise in normative ethics, bargaining theory, and the ethics of compromise, advising a small research funder. The funder has set aside money for roughly five person-years of work on gains from trade through moral compromise: cases where parties with genuinely different values (a strict deontologist and a utilitarian, a conservationist and a welfarist) coordinate so that each advances the other's values in exchange for advancement of their own. Before the funder writes its grant call, think carefully through the landscape and produce a defended agenda: which open questions in this area matter most for the funder's stated goal of determining when such compromise is genuinely possible and when it is an illusion, and which two or three of those questions should absorb the limited person-years?

I want the agenda to take two complications seriously rather than wave at them. First, the asymmetry problem: in textbook trades both sides sacrifice something they merely prefer, but in moral compromise one side often regards the other's benefit not as a forgone good but as a positive wrong — the deontologist is not losing utility by letting the utilitarian act; she is becoming complicit. Does the gains-from-trade framing survive that asymmetry, and is "when does complicity break the analogy to ordinary trade" itself a top-tier question? Second, value laundering: a party can apparently profit from compromise by adopting or inflating values it does not really hold, extracting real concessions in exchange for restraint it never cared about. Whether there is a principled test distinguishing genuine from manufactured values may be a crux on which the entire area implicitly depends — or it may be a distraction.

Rank the open questions, state the criteria you used (importance, tractability, neglectedness, and how much else depends on each question), defend the ordering against the most plausible rearrangement, and say what finding would change your ranking. The deliverable is a defended agenda, not a survey; please do not append reading lists, citations, or bibliographies.
