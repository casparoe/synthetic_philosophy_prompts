I’ve been reading LessWrong and adjacent blogs for years, so my default frame is map versus territory: classifications are tools for prediction, not things reality hands us labeled in advance. But the natural kinds debate seems to have been stuck for decades on whether the sciences really carve nature at its joints.

As I understand the rough camps:

- Realists say successful sciences discover objective divisions in causal structure. Chemical elements are the showcase case: atomic number supports tons of inductions, and alternative groupings look clearly worse.
- Pluralists or conventionalists say classification is always relative to our purposes. Biology has multiple species concepts, medicine can classify diseases by cause, symptoms, prognosis, or treatment response, and none of these is obviously the one true taxonomy.
- Then there are compromise views where kinds are real but fuzzy: clusters of properties that reliably hang together because of shared causal mechanisms, even without sharp essences.

My suspicion is that the debate has stalled because natural is doing at least three different jobs: clustering without arbitrary researcher choices, supporting prediction and intervention across contexts, and licensing a stronger metaphysical claim that reality itself has preferred categories. Those can come apart. A category can be useful for many purposes without being metaphysically special, and a category can track a real causal mechanism while having a boundary drawn by convention.

My current pet framing is compression: a classification is more natural when it gives an unusually good lossy compression of the world for a wide range of questions. On that view, element is highly natural because it compresses chemistry well across many contexts, while psychiatric diagnosis is less natural because different questions reward different groupings. But maybe this just relocates the dissagreement into arguments over which questions and contexts should count.

Diagnose the debate rather than giving me a survey or a final verdict. Are the sides disagreeing about empirical facts, metaphysics, standards of explanatory success, or just using natural differently? Are they answering different questions under one heading? Please engage the strongest version of each camp, explain where the compression framing helps and where it smuggles in pluralism, and say what would count as progress: a formal result, a better distinction, domain-specific evidence, or an admission that the original question is ill-posed.
