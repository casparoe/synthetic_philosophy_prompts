I'm pretty sold on risk-weighted expected utility theory (Buchak-style REU) as an account of rational choice under risk -- locating risk attitudes in how an agent weights the chances of the worst outcomes, rather than in the shape of the utility function, seems like exactly the right way to handle Allais-style cases, and I like that it makes ordinary risk aversion potentially rational rather than a bias to be explained away. But I want to stress-test the view before committing. Act as a decision theorist who is sympathetic to REU but not a true believer, and take your time: brainstorm the full space of objections first, then organize your answer.

What would count against REU? Specifically, I'd like: (1) an assessment of which standard objections are genuine defeaters and which dissolve once the theory is stated carefully -- the double-counting worry (utility curvature already captures some risk attitudes, so risk-weighting over-counts), the violation of betweenness, the permissiveness worry (some choice of risk function rationalizes nearly any preferences, so the theory lacks bite), and trouble with sequential choice and plan adoption; (2) thoughts on two worries I've been chewing on that I haven't seen fully worked out -- first, whether REU faces a self-application problem, since the choice between EU and REU is itself made under normative uncertainty, and it's unclear whether applying REU to its own adoption vindicates the theory or makes it look self-serving and arbitrary; second, suppose it were proven that an REU agent must either reject the reduction of compound lotteries or accept dynamically inconsistent preferences -- which horn should the REU theorist grab, and is either actually fatal? (3) the discriminating predictions: what behavior would REU predict that EU-with-diminishing-marginal-utility can't accommodate, so that empirical evidence could actually arbitrate?

Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a committed REU theorist would concede as real problems versus dismiss as misunderstandings. If you conclude the theory has been insulated against refutation, say whether that's a vice for a normative theory of this kind.
