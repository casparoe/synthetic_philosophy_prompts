You are a teaching assistant grading undergraduate exam scripts for a course on paradoxes and the philosophy of the infinite. Below is one exam question, followed by a student's answer. Grade the answer out of 10, and provide the specific comments that justify your grade: identify what the answer gets right, what it gets wrong or leaves underdeveloped, and where exactly each credit-worthy or credit-losing step occurs. Point-by-point assessment is expected rather than a general impression.

Exam question:

A lamp is fitted with a single button that toggles it between on and off. At t = 0 the lamp is switched on; at t = 1/2 it is switched off; at t = 3/4 on; at t = 7/8 off; and so on, with one switching act at each time 1 − 1/2ⁿ. Is the lamp on or off at t = 1? Explain the apparent paradox, and assess whether it shows that completing infinitely many acts in a finite time is logically impossible. In your answer, distinguish carefully between what the description of the task determines and what it leaves open.

Student's answer:

"The paradox is generated by the symmetry of the switching pattern. The lamp cannot be on at t = 1, because for every time at which it is switched on there is a later time, still before t = 1, at which it is switched off; and it cannot be off at t = 1, for the parallel reason. Yet once t = 1 arrives, every switching act has been carried out, and the lamp must presumably be in one state or the other. Since neither state is privileged, Thomson's conclusion is that completing infinitely many acts in a finite span is logically impossible.

This conclusion can be resisted by attending to the mathematics of the sequence. The switching times are given by the geometric series 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + …, which converges to exactly 1. Because the sequence of times converges, the switching process as a whole has a limit, and the lamp must settle into whichever state is the limit of its successive states. The puzzle about t = 1 is therefore really a question about what that limit-state is.

As Benacerraf pointed out in response to Thomson, the lamp is on at t = 1: once all the switchings are completed, the lamp is simply left in the state that results, and nothing in the description forces it to be off. The appearance of paradox arises only because the original description is incomplete about what happens at the limit, not because the task itself is contradictory. A supertask is merely an infinite sequence of acts performed within a finite interval, and since we can consistently describe the lamp as on at t = 1, such tasks are logically possible after all. The moral is that one must not infer an impossibility in the task from a gap in its description."
