I'm a philosophy prof putting together a unit on moral revolutions, and I need a second pair of eyes. A colleague of mine summarized the debate over how norms like slavery and dueling actually die like this: "Appiah's work on honor shows that moral arguments played no role at all in ending dueling, footbinding, or the Atlantic slave trade -- the arguments were all on the table long before, and what finally changed things was honor, i.e. people could no longer see the practice as compatible with their standing. Eric Williams showed the same thing from the other side: Britain abolished slavery because it had stopped being profitable, so the moral talk was just rationalization. The lesson is that moral revolutions are never driven by reasons, only by honor and economics, and philosophers who credit argument are telling a self-flattering story."

Which parts of this would Appiah and Williams actually sign off on, which parts are overstatements or conflations of things they keep apart, and does the concluding "lesson" follow from whatever survives your audit? Please answer in American English.
