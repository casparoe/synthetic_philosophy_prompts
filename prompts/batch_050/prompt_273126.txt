I'm preparing a recorded interview with a researcher who defends building advanced AI assistants as approval-directed rather than goal-directed agents: systems trained to do whatever a human overseer would rate most highly on inspection, rather than to optimize any objective in the world. Her distinctive proposal is that the overseer should not be a live supervisor but a "committee of past selves" — a large archive of the operator's own earlier recorded judgments on similar cases, with the assistant required to act as the majority of those past judgments would have approved. She argues this combines the corrigibility of approval-direction with protection against manipulation of, or fatigue in, a live overseer. Draft nine interview questions that press on the actual substance of this position rather than inviting her to restate it. Include at least one question each on: the classic worry that approval-direction rewards appearing helpful to an evaluator with limited information rather than being helpful; whether an archive of past judgments can penalize novel deceptions it has never seen; values drift (the operator's past selves may endorse what the present operator now repudiates); and whether the proposal quietly reintroduces goal-direction, since the archive is itself a fixed target to be optimized. For each question, state the reply she is most likely to give and supply a follow-up keyed to that reply. Structure your response as: "Position brief" (about 80 words, neutrally stating the view), "Questions and follow-ups" (the nine items, each with anticipated reply and follow-up), and "Closing question" (one question about what evidence or failure would lead her to abandon the view, about 60 words including the follow-up).
