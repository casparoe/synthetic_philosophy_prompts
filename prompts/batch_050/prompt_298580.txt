I help run a small philosophy meetup at my company (mostly engineers, a few philosophy grads) and next month we're interviewing a philosopher who defends a modernized version of Locke's memory criterion of personal identity -- roughly, you survive as the same person as long as the right memory connections hold. Draft me 8 or so interview questions, and for each one a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I don't want softballs. Specifically I want at least:

- one question pressing Reid's brave officer objection (the old general remembers being the young officer but not the flogged boy, so the transitivity of identity breaks on a memory theory), with a follow-up for when she inevitably appeals to overlapping memory chains instead of direct memory;
- one on the circularity worry (Butler's point that memory seems to presuppose identity rather than constitute it -- you can't define identity by memory if "genuine" memory already means "memory of something *you* did");
- one about how the theory handles amnesia patients or just ordinary forgetting, and whether identity gets gappy;
- one on fission-style cases (like if my memories got copied into two bodies, or honestly something like a database fork with two replicas both claiming to be the original -- I work in distributed systems so feel free to let me lean on that analogy in the question);
- one on false/confabulated memories, which feels like it should be embarassing for the view.

Also end the list with one question about what result or case would actually make her give the view up, since she has a reputation for treating objections as "puzzles for the research program." Questions should sound like they come from a smart non-philosopher who's done the reading, not a survey course.
