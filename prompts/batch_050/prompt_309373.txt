I'm a retired physiologist (40 years of muscle and nerve work, now I read philosophy on the porch, go figure) and I've gotten stuck on the literature around pain asymbolia -- patients who say they feel pain, can locate and describe it, but insist it doesn't bother them and they dont mind it at all. Before I embarrass myself at the local reading group I want someone to audit where I've landed. Here's my position, roughly:

1. The badness of pain is exhausted by how bad it feels to the subject. If a sensation feels fine to the person having it -- no suffering, no distress, nothing -- then nothing bad is happening to them in that moment, full stop. There's no badness floating free of the experience.

2. The asymbolia patients genuinely feel pain. Not some ersatz sensation, not pressure or warmth mislabeled -- the very same sensation I feel when I burn my hand on the stove, just with the hurting stripped off. The injury-detection part intact, the suffering part gone.

3. It follows from 1 and 2 that their condition is only instrumentally bad. The tragedy is downstream: they chew their tongues, they don't notice fractures, they get infections they never feel. In the moment of the pain itself, nothing bad is going on.

4. On welfare generally I'm basically a hedonist: how well a life goes comes down to the balance of good feeling over bad feeling across it.

5. And yet: if someone offered me a drug before an operation that would put me in exactly the asymbolic state -- I'd feel all of it, "the same" sensation, just wouldn't mind -- the offer strikes me as horrifying, not as a free lunch. Same for the patients: even one who by luck avoids all injury seems to me to be living a worse life than the rest of us, like something essential has been amputated.

So: does this set hang together? Which pairs are in real tension and which only look like they are (I have a feeling 1 and 2 bother people more than they should)? Please rank the tensions by how serious they are rather than treating them all as equally fatal, and tell me for each what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. And don't just tell me which commitment to abandon -- I want to see the structure of the problem first, because I genuinely can't tell which of these I'd give up.
