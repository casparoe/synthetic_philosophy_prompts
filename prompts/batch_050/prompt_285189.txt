Me and my friend Dana have been arguing for like a year about when the "age of humans" actually started — as in, when Earth stopped being just Earth and became Earth-with-us running things. Neither of us are scientists, we just watch alot of documentaries and read articles, but Dana has super firm opinions, and she insists this has a real answer, it's not just something a committee of geologists gets to vote on.

Her verdicts so far:

- Early farming, people clearing forests and planting crops thousands of years ago: no. Her line is that plenty of animals rearrange their surroundings, and it was patchy anyway, some places farmed and others didn't for ages.
- The thing where, after Europeans arrived in the Americas, so many people died that forests grew back over huge areas and you can apparently see carbon dioxide dip in ice cores around 1610: yes, that counts. First time the whole planet flinched because of something humans did, all at once.
- The industrial revolution, coal and smoke and steam engines: yes. That's when we started actually steering the climate instead of just leaving dents in it.
- The radioactive dust from atom bomb tests, which apparently left a thin layer you can find in lake mud and ice all over the world from the 1940s-50s: no. She says that's just fallout, contamination from a few dozen explosions, not the planet itself doing anything different.

Now the case we're stuck on: microplastics. Tiny plastic bits are turning up in deep ocean sediment, arctic ice, lake beds, basically everywhere, starting from around the 1950s, and they'll be sitting in the rock record for who knows how long. I think she has to say this counts because it's a worldwide layer in the ground, way more so than the bomb dust she waved off. She says she hasn't decided yet.

So: what rule is she actually running in those four verdicts? I can think of at least two rules that fit all four of them and they give opposite answers on the plastic. Tell me what each rule predicts, which one you think is really hers and why, and which of her four old cases the plastic is most like. And if her verdicts genuinely underdetermine the plastic case, tell me what would settle which rule she's on — like some future case (we clean all the plastic up? we deliberately bury a worldwide layer of time capsules as a monument?) where the rules would clearly split. Also, does her "a committee can't just vote on it" stance even make sense if she can't say what her own rule is?
