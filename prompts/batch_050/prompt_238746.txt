Is there a name for this? I bake bread for a living and I keep running into the same weird pattern. For a couple years I'd toss a free roll in the bag for regulars at my market stall. At first people were delighted. After a while nobody even said thanks, it was just part of the deal, and the month flour prices spiked and I stopped, two customers were genuinely angry at me, like I'd short-changed them. Meanwhile the stall next to mine has never given away a crumb and nobody holds it against her. Same thing with my sister: she's hosted every family Christmas for a decade, and the one year she said she wanted to travel instead, half the family acted like she'd betrayed them. My brother, who has hosted exactly zero Christmases in his life, gets totaly off the hook. And there's a flip side: when that same brother once showed up with a bottle of wine, everyone treated him like a hero.

So the pattern is: do extra good long enough and it stops counting as extra, it becomes your floor, and dropping back to what everyone else does all along gets treated like a wrong you've committed. And on the other end, doing nothing for ages seems to make a tiny gesture count double. Its like the scoreboard quietly re-zeroes itself around your track record.

Is there an established term for this, in ethics or psychology or anywhere? I don't think it's just "taking someone for granted" -- that's about gratitude fading, and this is about the standard for *blame* moving, specifically in a way that punishes you for having been good. If there's a real concept I'd love the name, but I don't want just a label: tell me what the closest concepts are, how this thing differs from each, and whether the free-roll side and the brother-getting-praised side are one phenomenon or two. And if there's genuinely no name, coin one, define it, and tell me where it stops applying -- like does it still count if the person promised the extra thing up front, or if they're paid for it, or only if the extra was voluntary the whole time?
