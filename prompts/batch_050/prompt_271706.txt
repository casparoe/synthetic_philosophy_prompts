I'd like to know what principle, if any, lies behind my gut verdicts about which systems have minds. An individual ant: no mind worth speaking of. A human brain: obviously a mind. Aunt Hillary, Hofstadter's ant colony in which colony-level dynamics implement symbol manipulation while each ant is a mere signal carrier: I find myself saying she genuinely thinks, even though no ant does. The US economy, which is also a massively parallel system of signal-passing agents: no mind. A population the size of China's, each person simulating one neuron of a particular human brain over radio links: I lean toward yes, a mind, though less confidently than with Aunt Hillary. A lookup table that stores the correct response for every possible conversation it could have: no. A beehive: no, though I can't say why it should differ from the ant colony. A large corporation with well-organized internal information flows: no, I think, but my reasons feel thin. Is there a single account of what makes a system minded that delivers most of these verdicts, and which of my verdicts does it force me to abandon? In particular, what should such an account say about the beehive and the corporation, the two cases I keep flip-flopping on?
