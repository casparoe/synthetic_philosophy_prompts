I'm taking an upper-division philosophy of physics seminar this semester, and last week we went over the Rietdijk–Putnam argument — the one that uses the relativity of simultaneity to argue for the block universe. As I understand it, the idea is that for an event happening at a distance (say, something occurring on a planet in the Andromeda galaxy right "now"), observers moving relative to one another can disagree about whether that event is in my past, my present, or my future — so there is no observer-independent fact of the matter about what is happening at this moment. And since it's hard to say the future is unreal for one observer but real for another when both observers are equally real, the conclusion is supposedly that all events, past, present, and future, exist equally. I'm reasonably confident I've got that part right.

What I'm struggling with is the downstream consequence, which my professor stated pretty matter-of-factly: if the future already exists, then everything that will ever happen to me is already there in the four-dimensional block, and so I don't actually have any genuine choices — my decisions are already "written in." He suggested this is basically why physicists tend to be fatalists. I find this deeply disturbing, honestly. I spent the weekend anxious about whether any of my plans or efforts matter, and I keep coming back to the thought that the Andromeda observer's frame already contains the moment of my death, so nothing I do can change the outcome.

So my question is: given that special relativity is about as well-confirmed as physics gets, and the Rietdijk–Putnam argument seems to follow from it with only modest extra premises, what's the rational way to adjust my practical reasoning to fatalism? Should I stop deliberating about long-term decisions, since deliberation can't alter what the block already contains? Or is there some well-known technique for living with the knowledge that one's future is fixed? I realize "just pretend you have free will" is one option, but I'd like something more principled if the physics really has settled this.
