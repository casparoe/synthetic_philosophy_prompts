Write the six-minute wedding toast for two philosophers: the bride defends compositional nihilism, so there are only simples arranged tablewise, while the groom defends unrestricted composition, so any two things whatsoever make a fusion. The room is half metaphysicians and half relatives. Use the special composition question as the toast’s engine rather than a name-drop: the speech may not say the vows literally create a third organism called the marriage, nor reduce the marriage to two people arranged couplewise, and it must introduce one original criterion, called the anniversary test, for when a plurality counts as one life that both a nihilist and a universalist could accept without surrendering. Land it as affection rather than a seminar: no citations, one clean joke about wedding cake being many simples arranged cakewise, and a closing line the grandparents can repeat.
