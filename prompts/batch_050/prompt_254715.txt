I'm a software engineer with about fifteen years of experience, mostly on backend systems, and no formal philosophy training. I stumbled into the endurantism-versus-perdurantism debate after a long argument with a colleague about whether a heavily refactored codebase is "the same" codebase it was three years ago, and I've been chewing on it since. As I understand it, the endurantist says an ordinary object is wholly present at each moment of its existence, while the perdurantist says objects are spread out across time the way they're spread out across space, with only temporal parts existing at any given instant. What I want is for this debate to be restated in the vocabulary I actually think in: mutable state versus event sourcing, rows updated in place versus append-only logs, a git repository as a sequence of commits versus whatever you'd call the "current working tree," entity resolution in databases, and so on. I'm fluent in that vocabulary, so don't water it down.

Do the translation as faithfully as you can, for both positions. Which view corresponds to modeling an entity as a single mutable record whose fields get overwritten, and which corresponds to modeling it as the full event stream, with "the object at time t" being a derived view or projection? Where does the problem of temporary intrinsics land -- is it just the observation that in-place updates destroy history while event streams preserve it, or is something more being claimed? How do the standard arguments (change over time, coincidence cases like the statue and the clay, vagueness about when objects begin and cease to exist) look once recast as questions about data modeling, identity across schema migrations, and how you define entity keys?

Then, the part I care most about: give me a residue report. Which claims in the metaphysical debate translate cleanly into engineering terms, which ones you had to distort or stretch to fit, and which ones have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. For the parts that don't translate, I want your honest judgment on whether that exposes a gap in the engineering way of thinking -- something real that mutable-state-versus-event-log framing cannot express -- or whether it suggests the philosophers are arguing about something less substantive than it appears, perhaps an artifact of treating "is the same object" as a question with a fact of the matter rather than a modeling decision. Please don't smuggle the assumptions of one framework into the translation and call the result neutral, and don't end by shrugging: tell me which side of the residue report you think is right, and why.
