My uncle died last spring and left his house to my cousins, and at the shiva (of all places) I got into an argument with an old friend about inheritance that I keep losing, and it's driving me crazy because every step he takes seems fine and the conclusion seems insane. Here's his argument, as best I can reconstruct it. (1) A society has equal opportunity only if no child's prospects in life are shaped by factors that are arbitrary from that child's own point of view -- things they did nothing to earn or deserve. (2) Whether your parents are rich is the paradigm case of something arbitrary from the child's point of view. (3) So equal opportunity requires that wealth not be transmissible downward: a 100% inheritance tax, or something with the same effect. (4) But everything else parents give their kids is just as arbitrary from the kids perspective: being read to at night, a stable home, the neighborhood, the connections, the dinner-table vocabulary, even the genes. (5) So equal opportunity requires neutralizing all of that too, which means you can't really let families raise their own children -- you get the Plato setup where kids are raised in common and don't know who their parents are. (6) That's monstrous and nobody should accept it. (7) So (1) is false, and equal opportunity doesn't actually require anything about inheritance at all -- taxing estates is just envy dressed up.

The really annoying part is that I want to keep (1). It feels like the whole point of talking about equal opportunity in the first place. And I want to keep some serious estate tax too, since I can't see how you look at dynastic wealth compounding over generations and call the resulting race fair. But every time I try to say that taxing an inheritance is legitimate while bedtime stories are off-limits, he asks what the difference is from the kid's standpoint, since the kid earned neither one, and I genuinely don't have an answer. So: which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? If the answer is that (1) needs to be weakened, I'd like the weakened version spelled out precisely enough that it still condemns aristocracy but doesn't condemn parenting -- if that's even a coherent thing to ask for.
