You are an expert in welfare economics and institutional decision theory advising a policy review board. Think carefully: before committing to an answer, enumerate every decision rule that is consistent with the board's full record, and only then turn to the new case.

I sit on a hospital network's reform board, which may approve only changes it judges to make no one worse off. Our record so far:

1. Approved an optional subsidized mindfulness program.
2. Approved replacing brand-name supplies with equally effective generics, with the savings returned to staff as a bonus.
3. Rejected mandatory flu vaccination for all staff, on the grounds that already-immune staff would bear a small risk with no offsetting benefit, even though total infections network-wide would have fallen.
4. Approved, after a unanimous staff vote, replacing seniority-based parking with a lottery giving everyone an equal chance at the best spots; a transit stipend ensured even senior staff were at least as well off in expectation.
5. Rejected a centralization of on-call scheduling that was projected to lower every individual's expected burden, because the projections rested on a speculative staffing model the board did not trust.

The new proposal: a mandatory hospital-wide scheduling algorithm with strong evidence, from several comparable hospitals, that it reduces expected on-call hours for every individual staff member. It works only if adoption is universal. A staff vote showed 90% support; the dissenters are coerced. In any given month some staff will in fact draw slightly worse schedules than under the old system, though everyone's expected burden falls.

Predict the board's ruling and defend the prediction. Which precedent is the closest analog? If the record genuinely underdetermines the outcome, say so, state each candidate rule (for example: ex post Pareto only, individual consent required for any risk imposition, ex ante Pareto suffices, or majority vote plus ex ante gains), give each rule's verdict, and describe the further case that would best distinguish the two most plausible rules.
