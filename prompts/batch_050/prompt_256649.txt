Alright, some background first. I'm a retired physicist (forty years, mostly condensed matter) who's recently fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole, and my reading group has been stuck for months on the Sleeping Beauty problem. You know the setup: fair coin flipped Sunday; if heads she wakes Monday only, if tails she wakes Monday and again Tuesday with her memory of the Monday waking erased; the question is what her credence in heads should be on waking.

Being me, I didn't argue, I simulated. Python, a million runs, and when I counted up the awakenings the coin was heads on a third of them, which I thought settled it for the thirders. But the halfer in our group (a retired accountant, stubborn as granite) pointed out that per experiment the coin comes up heads half the time, and said my simulation just counts awakenings instead of runs. He called that a category error. Its been six months and the annoying thing is we both seem to be right about our own number.

Here's my hypothesis, carried over from my old life: this smells like simultaneity before Einstein. Two camps using one word — credence — for what might be two different things, and the fix is to demand an operational definition. Credence-as-betting-behavior plausibly gives you a third (especially since Beauty gets offered the bet twice on the tails branch), credence-as-something-else gives you a half, and once everyone says which they mean the dispute evaporates. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real question left standing once you've fixed what credence is *for* — something about whether waking up counts as getting evidence when you knew in advance you'd wake up either way.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? A verbal dispute hiding behind a technical term? Bedrock intuitions about self-location that neither side can argue for?
