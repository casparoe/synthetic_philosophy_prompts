I'm a third-year philosophy student in a seminar on consciousness, and next week I'm presenting a paper arguing that Daniel Dennett's coffee-tasters thought experiment -- the one about Chase and Sanborn, the two tasters who come to dislike a coffee they once loved, where one blames his changed qualia and the other blames his changed standards -- fails to show that there is no fact of the matter about qualia. My thesis is that Dennett's argument quietly runs together two very different claims: that we often cannot tell whether our experience changed or our reactions to it did, and that there is nothing to be right or wrong about here at all. I argue the first is true and unthreatening, while the second only follows if you assume that facts about experience must be introspectively accessible to count as facts -- a verificationist premise I claim Dennett needs but never defends in the essay. I concede that the case does show first-person authority over one's own qualia is weaker than naive realists assume.

My audience: about fifteen classmates who have all read the essay, a professor who is openly sympathetic to Dennett-style functionalism, two students who I know lean toward panpsychism and will think my defense of qualia doesn't go nearly far enough, and one sharp student who keeps raising inverted-spectrum cases in every session regardless of topic. None of them are specialists; the professor is.

Prepare me for the Q&A. Give me the six to eight objections I will actually get from this specific room -- not generic objections to qualia realism -- ordered by how much damage each would do to my thesis if I answer it badly. Phrase each the way the person raising it would phrase it (the functionalist professor will not put things the way the panpsychists will). For each: my two-to-three-sentence reply, and a candid note on where that reply is weakest. Flag which objections attack my verificationism charge specifically and which attack the realist position I'm defending, since those need different handling. Tell me what I should concede immediately to look honest rather than cornered. End with the question I'm hoping nobody asks, and my best available answer to it anyway.

Maximum 900 words.
