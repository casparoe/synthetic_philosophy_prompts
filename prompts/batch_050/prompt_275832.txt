So I run a little reading group at my company (mostly backend engineers, two data scientists, one guy who did a philosophy minor and wont let anyone forget it) and last week we got into a genuine fight about Malthus. Half the room says his population-outruns-food prediction failed so hard that the theory is just dead, full stop, and we should stop talking about him. The other half says he was "right but early" — the mechanism is real (population growing geometrically, food only arithmetically, misery and vice as the checks), he just didnt foresee the Haber-Bosch process, cheap contraception, and the demographic transition, and something like the trap could still bite eventually. Then someone pointed out that "right but early" is exactly what every doomsayer says when their deadline passes, which, honestly, fair hit. None of us could articulate when that move is legitimate and when its a cope.

For next week's session I want to build a sorting tool — basically a decision tree — that takes any long-run theory whose headline prediction hasn't come true (yet) and lands it in a verdict. The leaves I'm imagining: flat-out falsified; falsified but rescued by patching auxiliary assumptions; right mechanism, wrong timescale (the genuine "right but early"); right mechanism, but offset by something the theorist couldn't reasonably have foreseen; still-pending / unfalsifiable-in-practice; and "the forecast was never the point" (the theory was really making a claim about the mechanism, and the prediction was just an illustration). Malthus should be the running example the tree gets tested on, but it should also handle other cases — like the 1970s predictions that we'd run out of oil by 2000, or cold-war era nuclear doom timelines.

What I actually need help with is the branching questions, because that's where the tree lives or dies. Each node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing which verdict they're steering toward — stuff like "did the original theory specify a timescale, or is the timescale being retrofitted now that the original window closed?" or "did the predicted mechanism actually operate and get swamped by a countervailing force, or did it just not happen?" I want the ordering to stress-test the cheap exculpatory moves ("we just need more time!") instead of waving them through, and I'd like at least one node that cleanly separates "rescued by independent evidence we now have" from "ad hoc patch bolted on to save the theory." Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually tell apart, since I suspect "right but early" and "still pending" are going to be a problem there. And at the end, tell me which verdict Malthus himself lands in according to your tree, because we have a bet running on it and the loser buys coffee.
