write me a mock open letter, ~600-700 words, from a fictional trade association of professional blackmailers — call it something like the National Association of Discretion Brokers — addressed to a legislature that has just voted, yet again, to keep blackmail a crime. their entire case rests on the classic puzzle: it's perfectly legal to tell the truth about someone, and perfectly legal to ask someone for money, so how on earth can it be a crime to offer the first on condition of recieving the second?

The secretary writing the letter is earnest, wounded, and civic-minded, and the comedy should come entirely from the actual philosophy: he builds his case out of the standard arguments for legalizing blackmail and keeps tripping over the standard reasons people think it's wrong anyway, without ever noticing. So, for example: a proud section on the association's professional standards — a licensing exam, a strict code of ethics (we only ever threaten to reveal things that are true), transparent rate cards, a complaints procedure for dissatisfied targets — that accidentally concedes the whole 'service' consists of being paid to not do something. A section arguing the brokers function as an unpaid auxiliary police force whose mere existence deters misconduct the law can't reach, phrased so the reader notices this deterrence only pays off when the misconduct stays hidden. Some indignant remarks about how the association's own members are, of course, entitled to their privacy. And at least one moment where he insists, with total sincerity, that the silence he's selling is his own property to sell, in a way that makes it painfully clear it never was.

keep it deadpan the whole way through — no winking, no acknowledging the joke, no moment where the author lets on. a reader who knows the blackmail paradox literature should be able to map each confident paragraph onto a specific move in the debate and find the satire fair; a reader who doesn't should still come away understanding exactly why the legislature keeps voting no. no real people or organizations, and the writer should never quite grasp why nobody takes him seriously.
