My friend does population genetics and I've been poking at whether her notion of fitness is the propensity kind (fitness = expected reproductive output, not actual output) or something sneakier, because I've read enough LW-adjacent stuff to know the propensity move is supposed to dissolve the "survival of the fittest is a tautology" objection. Her verdicts so far:

- Two beetles, identical genomes; one gets crushed by a falling branch before breeding, the other leaves five larvae. She says they're equally fit -- the crushed one just got unlucky.
- Strain A reliably leaves 2 offspring per generation; strain B leaves 8 or 0 with even odds (so average 4), every individual drawing from the same lottery each generation. She says B is fitter.
- An allele that makes its carrier die saving three siblings: she says it can spread, so the carrier's fitness "isn't lower in the relevant sense."
- A mutant that roughly doubles lifetime offspring but only by delaying breeding so generation length triples: she hesistated and said fitness is about invasion success, not raw offspring count.

New case for her: strain C leaves 6 offspring in wet years and 0 in dry years, with wet/dry years coming at random, so lineages persist across many generations of both; strain D leaves a steady 2 every year. Averaged across the climate distribution C's per-individual expected output beats D's. Predict her verdict, tell me which rule she's actually running (arithmetic-mean propensity? geometric-mean growth-rate maximizer? inclusive-fitness bookkeeping?), say which earlier verdicts pin down what and which underdetermine, and name the one hypothetical that would settle between the surviving rules. Also: does her actual rule still defuse the tautology objection, or does one of the candidate rules quietly collapse back into "fitness is whatever spreads"?
