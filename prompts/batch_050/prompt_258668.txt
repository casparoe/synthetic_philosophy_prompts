Write a deadpan parody as a new-hire compliance handbook for a mid-sized consultancy that has decided to run office politics on nuclear deterrence logic. Venue: glossy HR onboarding PDF that somehow got approved by Legal. About 800-1000 words, formatted as numbered modules with quiz questions and an incident-report appendix. Target: the way deterrence talk slides from sober caution into a whole lifestyle: credibility, reputation, costly signals, doomsday devices as recurring calendar invites, escalation ladders, tripwires, mutually assured cc-ing. Exaggerate real features people will recognize: the stability-instability paradox, where everyone feels safe under the big umbrella so they get reckless about tiny Slack wars; the commitment problem, where revenge has to look automatic even if it is obviously ruinous; first-strike versus second-strike capacity, but for deleting shared drives and surviving performance review season; and brinkmanship as reply-all chicken where both sides insist they cannot back down. Invent one office doctrine, like the Department of Credibility, and keep it straight-faced enough that a careless reader might forward it. No real leaders, no current countries, no citations, and dont lean on niche history. Make the jokes land even if the reader only knows half the vocab, but smuggle in precise distinctions between deterrence by denial and deterrence by punishment, and between deterring someone and compelling them. End with a mock ethics disclaimer that accidentally admits the system makes small disasters rational. The humor should come from the logic, not random wackiness, and there should be exactly one suspicious typo somewhere in module three.
