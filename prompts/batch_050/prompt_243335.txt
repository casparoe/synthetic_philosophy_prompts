I'm applying for a one-semester undergraduate research grant in political theory and need a proposal of about two pages, on chapter 25 of Machiavelli's The Prince (I'm working from the Marriott translation). Everyone in my seminar stops at the river metaphor and the fortune-is-a-woman line, but the passage I want to build the project around comes right before those:

"Changes in estate also issue from this, for if, to one who governs himself with caution and patience, times and affairs converge in such a way that his administration is successful, his fortune is made; but if times and affairs change, he is ruined if he does not change his course of action. But a man is not often found sufficiently circumspect to know how to accommodate himself to the change, both because he cannot deviate from what nature inclines him to, and also because, having always prospered by acting in one way, he cannot be persuaded that it is well to leave it; and, therefore, the cautious man, when it is time to turn adventurous, does not know how to do it, hence he is ruined; but had he changed his conduct with the times fortune would not have changed."

My half-formed thought: this "match your disposition to the spirit of the times" doctrine might be unfollowable in principle, and Machiavelli half-admits it. If character is fixed by nature and habit, as the passage says, then the advice can't be followed by the very person it's aimed at, and if it's really advice to someone else (pick agents whose temperaments fit the moment, or hedge across dispositions) it becomes a different doctrine entirely. On top of that, unless "the spirit of the times" can be identified in advance rather than in hindsight, the thesis only ever explains success after the fact, since we never hear about the impetuous men whose times never came. And it seems to sit awkwardly with the river passage earlier in the same chapter, where virtù builds defences in fair weather that hold whatever the times bring — one picture says prepare so fortune can't hurt you, the other says no fixed preparation exists.
