I review marketing and fundraising materials for a mid-sized nonprofit — email appeals, donation page defaults, the little urgency banners, the works. We sign off on maybe forty pieces a week, I get about ten minutes per item, and leadership wants "consistent standards" without me calling a committee meeting every time. Someone told me the rough test from the philosophy literature on this: a tactic counts as manipulation rather than persuasion if it gets people to act by going around their capacity to weigh reasons instead of engaging it — and that one decent check is whether the person would still go along if they knew exactly which levers were being pulled on them. Turn that into a procedure a reviewer can actually run: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a passing answer at each step, where the procedure should just veto something, and where it has to hand the call back to a human because no test settles it. Then run it on three cases from my world: a donation page that pre-checks the monthly-giving box, an appeal letter telling one family's story in detail before asking for money, and a banner saying only 3 spots left when the spots are artificial. At least one of these should come out in a way I won't like, i.e. the procedure should condemn something we currently do. Be honest about which steps are real tests and which are judgment calls wearing a procedure's clothes, and tell me what would change your mind — what kind of counterexample or consideration would show the bypass test is the wrong thing to build the procedure around in the first place. No headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables please, just continuous prose like a long email.
