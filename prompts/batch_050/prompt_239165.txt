I'm a software engineer and recently got pulled into genetics because my kid was flagged for a variant on a newborn screening panel, and since then I keep running into the same analogy from every direction: DNA is a code. The genome is written in a four-letter alphabet, genes are instructions, transcription is like running the program, a mutation is a typo. The genetic counselor literally leaned on it to reassure us -- the variant is a single misspelling, and the cell has proofreading machinery, so most typos get caught or don't matter. Meanwhile a buddy of mine who did some philosophy of biology in college says the code talk is a metaphor that does real damage: nothing is reading the code, "information" in DNA is just causation dressed up in computer words, and calling a mutation a typo presupposes there's a correct text, which quietly smuggles in a designer.

I want an audit, not a pep talk. Give me the correspondence table first: what actually plays the role of the alphabet, the syntax, the semantics, the reader or compiler, the program, and (if anything) the programmer? Then tell me where it breaks. I gather the genetic code is arbitrary -- the same amino acids could have been assigned to different triplets -- which some people cite as proof it's a genuine code, but there's no separate interpreter the way there's a CPU, the cell is somehow both text and reader, and whether a given stretch "means" anything depends on the rest of the machinery, splicing, regulation, all that. Also which features the analogy quietly imports that DNA lacks -- I suspect determinism is one, since everyone hears "it's in her genes" as "it's fixed."

Finally: do the conclusions that were actually being drawn survive? Specifically the counselor's typo-reassurance, the determinism my family keeps slipping into, and my friend's claim that code talk is design smuggled in disguise. And if the code analogy can't carry the load, is there a better one -- recipe, musical score, something else -- or is that asking for trouble too?
