Can you tell me whether a set of views I've ended up with on the hole argument and gauge freedom actually hang together? Here's where I'm at:

(1) Leibniz equivalence is right: two spacetime models related by a diffeomorphism that drags the metric and matter fields around inside some region (the 'hole') describe one and the same physical possibility. Spacetime points have no primitive identity over and above the fields defined at them.

(2) Because of (1), I don't think the hole argument shows general relativity is indeterministic in any troubling sense. Once you identify diffeomorphic models, the allegedly many futures collapse into one.

(3) I'm still a substantivalist, though. Spacetime is a genuine substance, not merely a web of spatiotemporal relations between bodies -- I just hold the 'sophisticated' version of substantivalism that's compatible with (1).

(4) In electromagnetism I'm a realist about the vector potential itself, not just about gauge-invariant functions of it. The Aharonov-Bohm effect clinches this for me: electrons pick up a measurable phase shift while travelling through regions where the magnetic field is zero, and I don't see how to explain that without the potential being physcially there, doing the work.

(5) At bottom I think the hole argument and gauge freedom in EM are the same point in different clothing: both tell you that to extract the physical content of a theory you have to mod out by a transformation group.

So does the package cohere? Give me the tensions you find, ordered from most to least serious, and for each one say which commitment I'd have to drop or soften and roughly what that would cost me. And if any pair of these looks like it conflicts but doesn't really once you look closely, flag that too -- I don't want to give something up over a merely apparent problem.
