As I understand the tetralemma, Nāgārjuna takes each target concept — causation, motion, the self — through the four limbs (it exists; it does not exist; it both exists and does not exist; it neither exists nor does not exist) and settles on the third, with emptiness being the name for this licensed contradiction in things. If that is right, Madhyamaka looks like a form of dialetheism: its adherents accept true contradictions about perfectly ordinary things like pots, fires, and persons. My question is how the tradition contains the damage. Once contradictions about mundane objects are on the table, what stops the licensed contradictions from spreading until anything whatsoever can be affirmed — what keeps a Madhyamika from concluding both that charity is good and that it is worthless, or that the fire is lit and that it is not?
