I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. Please use that vocabulary, precisely, for the following.

Hofstadter's MU-puzzle uses a tiny formal system with alphabet {M, I, U}, one axiom (the string MI), and four rules: (1) any string ending in I may have a U appended; (2) from Mx you may produce Mxx, doubling whatever follows the M; (3) any occurrence of III may be replaced by U; (4) any occurrence of UU may be deleted. The puzzle is to derive MU. It can't be done, and the proof is an invariant argument: the count of I's is preserved mod 3 (rules 1 and 4 don't change it, rule 2 doubles it, rule 3 subtracts 3), it starts at 1, and MU has 0. Hofstadter's point is that this fact is invisible from inside the system -- a derivation search runs forever without ever concluding the goal is unreachable -- and that seeing it requires what he calls jumping out of the system: reasoning about the rules rather than with them. He builds on this to argue that minds are distinguished by exactly this capacity to step back from the rules they're operating under, and connects it to Gödel-style results: any fixed formalization can be transcended by a reasoner who can survey it from outside.
