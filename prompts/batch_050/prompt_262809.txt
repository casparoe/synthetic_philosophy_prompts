I'm preparing the final exam for my upper-level undergraduate course on population ethics, and I need one essay question on the nonidentity problem that students cannot answer by reciting Parfit's catalogue of cases. Here is the scenario the question should be built around:

A public health authority offers couples who carry a heritable mitochondrial disorder two free interventions, but a couple may take only one. Option A is a two-year course of treatment that must be completed before conception: a child conceived afterward will be entirely healthy, though of course a numerically different child from any who would otherwise have been born. Option B is forgoing treatment and conceiving now: the resulting child will have the disorder, which reduces lifelong well-being substantially but leaves life clearly worth living. The exam question should then introduce a comparator: the same authority also funds neonatal surgery that cures a numerically identical disorder in already-born infants. Students must explain why "the Option B child has not been harmed, since otherwise she would not exist" is at least superficially compelling, assess whether the strongest available repair is an impersonal one (e.g., a same-number quality principle) or a person-affecting one (e.g., harm understood de dicto, or a complaint-based account), and then use the neonatal comparator to test their preferred repair: does it vindicate the intuition that Options A and the surgery are equally mandatory, or does it force an asymmetry, and is that asymmetry defensible?

Please write: (1) the exam question itself, phrased as it would appear on the paper, answerable in 60 minutes; (2) a model answer of roughly 600 words; and (3) a marking scheme out of 20 built on five must-hit points, stating the marks each carries and the one-line way weak candidates typically botch each -- including at least one point that separates an A answer (which must diagnose why the counterfactual comparative account of harm generates the problem, not merely report that it does) from a B answer.
