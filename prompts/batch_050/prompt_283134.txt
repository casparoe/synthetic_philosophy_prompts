I'm presenting at a behavioral economics brownbag next month and my claim is that money pump arguments don't actually show that cyclic preferences (A>B>C>A) are irrational. My line is that the pump only works if the agent makes each trade myopically, without factoring in what she can see coming, and once she does factor it in the pump stops pumping -- so what the argument really shows is that cycles + naive choice policy is exploitable, not that the preferences themselves violate rationality. The audience is mostly experimental/behavioral economists who take revealed preference pretty seriously, plus one or two decision theorists, and none of them are going to be sympathetic to what they'll hear as philosophers overcomplicating an obvious point. Draft my Q&A prep: the six or seven objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how much trouble they'll cause me, phrased the way an economist would actually put it, each with a two-minute reply and a note about where the reply is weakest. Also include the objection I'm hoping nobody raises.
