Right, some background first. I spent the best part of forty years as an experimental physicist (condensed matter, mostly) and retired about five years ago, at wich point I finally had time to read the philosophy I'd been nodding along to at conferences. What hooked me was structural realism — the idea that when scientific theories get overthrown, the mathematics tends to survive even when the picture of the world doesn't, Fresnel's equations carrying over into Maxwell's theory being the famous case. I now co-host a tiny podcast for the emeritus crowd at my old department, and next month's guest is a philosopher of science (I'd rather not name her here) who has defended ontic structural realism for years — the stronger version where structure isn't just all we can know, it's all there is.

I'd like eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. No softballs about how she got into philosophy, please. I want the set to press where I gather the literature actually presses:

- The cherry-picking charge: that we only identify the "retained structure" with hindsight, so cases like Fresnel-to-Maxwell look more impressive than they really are.
- Newman's objection, at least as I understand it: a bare structural claim is nearly empty, because any old collection of things with the right number of members can be arranged to exhibit a given structure. Her likely reply, I'd guess, is that the structure has to be genuinely realised in the world rather than merely mathematically instantiated — but then hasn't the non-structural content crept back in? I want a follow-up that forces her to confront that.
- The "relations without relata" business. When I pressed a philosopher friend on this over lunch he said the objects are just nodes in the structure, which strikes me as either incoherent or ordinary realism wearing a false moustache. I'd like a question that makes her pick one.
- Whether the motivation for dropping objects depends on how quantum mechanics shakes out — if a hidden-variables interpretation wins one day, does the case for ontic structural realism evaporate, or does it stand on its own?
- And something on what evidence could actually count against the view, because at the moment I honestly can't see what would, wich worries me.
