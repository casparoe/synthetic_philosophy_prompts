I'm a senior at my high school and I chair the student government's five-member constitution committee. Last spring our student body elected a new president with about 60% of the vote on a platform of ending gridlock, and since September she has: replaced three of the five election commissioners with volunteers from her own campaign (the charter permits presidential appointments, though by convention they were nonpartisan); persuaded the council to make the student newspaper's budget subject to a fresh vote each semester, then voted to cut it after a series of editorials criticizing her; and announced that the two-term limit won't apply to her because her first months in office came by appointment, not election. Every move was arguably within the letter of the charter. Our charter also contains an anti-entrenchment clause, added years ago and never used, which lets this committee declare a pattern of conduct anti-democratic entrenchment and thereby trigger a school-wide vote of confidence. We vote Thursday on whether to issue that declaration, rule on each act separately instead, or do nothing.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate to the committee beforehand. It should give a clear bottom-line recommendation with a rationale, propose criteria that distinguish genuine democratic backsliding from ordinary hardball politics and from policies the losing side simply dislikes, and address who has the standing to make that call -- an unelected student committee, the faculty advisor, or only the student body at the next election. Two of our members campaigned against the president, so the opinion also has to confront the objection that backsliding is just a partisan label the minority applies to a majority it resents, and it should give the strongest version of her defense (electoral mandate, procedural legality, the cost of an unprecedented escalation) before answering it.
