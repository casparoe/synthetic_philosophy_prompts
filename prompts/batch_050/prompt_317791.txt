I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I've been asked to run a 60-minute public session at an ideas festival on the theme "Can you be rational at every step and irrational overall?" About 30 attendees, no assumed background, general-audience adults. I want to build the session around a live game that makes participants feel the money-pump argument from the inside: each person privately ranks three prizes (say, a nicer pen, a bookstore voucher, a box of good chocolates), and then a roving "trader" (me, plus volunteers) offers them a sequence of one-for-one swaps, each swap looking like a small improvement or a fair trade by their own stated rankings, until anyone with cyclic preferences has walked in a circle and paid a token for the privilege. Please design the activity: exact rules, materials, a token-based payoff scheme that needs no real money, how to handle participants whose preferences are transitive (they should still have something at stake), and a script for how the trading rounds should unfold. Then give me a debrief plan that maps what happened onto the philosophical issues -- the argument that rational preferences must be transitive, the reply that preferences can legitimately change between trades so the sequence is not one choice, and the question of whether the pump shows irrationality or merely vulnerability to exploitation. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (participants who smell the trick and refuse to trade, ties in rankings, people who just like trading) and how to handle each, and be frank about which feature of the philosophical problem the game cannot reproduce. American English, no more than 750 words total.
