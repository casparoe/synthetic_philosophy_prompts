So I drive a delivery van and listen to a lot of philosophy podcasts, and awhile back I heard the one with the drowning kid in the shallow pond, where you'd obviously ruin your shoes to save her so why not donate the money instead. It got in my head and I've been arguing with my wife about it for months, and I've started writing down my actual gut verdicts on when morality is allowed to demand something from you, and I want to know if there's a real principle underneath or if I'm just a mess. Here they are. My brother works in software, makes great money, gives maybe 5% to charity: I think morality can fairly demand 20% from him, nice car and all, that's not "too much." Donating a kidney to a stranger: beautiful if you do it, but nobody owes it, demanding it would be insane. Last year our town flooded and the one nurse on the volunteer crew worked three weeks straight while her own basement filled with water, and everyone, me included, treated that as what she owed, not some bonus saint act -- and honestly the cost to her was probably worse than a kidney. My friend Maya skipped a relief deployment she'd signed up for to go to her sister's wedding, and I think that was completely fine even though the deployment clearly did more good. And here's the pair that keeps me up: I think a billionaire keeping a third yacht while kids starve is obscene and the yacht is absolutely demandable, but my neighbor is a retired librarian who gives basically nothing and keeps her weekly hairdresser appointment, and I don't think anyone can take that from her -- even though her haircut money, pooled with enough other people's, saves lives same as the yacht money.

So what's the principle? Is it about how much you have left over, or a percentage thing, or is it that the nurse owed it because of her role and Maya didn't owe the deployment because her sister counts for something? I want the principle stated precisely enough that it tells me what to think about cases I haven't listed -- like, is my brother required to give up his season tickets, am I required to spend my Saturdays at the food bank instead of fishing? And be honest with me about which of my verdicts the principle just can't save. I suspect the kidney and the nurse can't both survive whatever rule also spares the librarian, and I'd rather know which one I have to drop than get some mushy answer that pretends they all fit.
