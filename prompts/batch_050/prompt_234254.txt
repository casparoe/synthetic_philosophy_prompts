Eight years ago I used most of my savings to buy a struggling small hotel in a declining coastal town, and since then I have poured in nearly all my money and time. The hotel has never turned a profit, the town's tourist trade shrinks every year, and I am exhausted enough that I am seriously considering selling for whatever I can get. My financial adviser tells me the only rational question is what the hotel will cost and earn from this point forward; everything I have already spent is gone either way and should play no role whatsoever in my decision.

Two things keep me from following her advice. First, last month I signed a contract with a regional travel cooperative: beginning in fourteen months they will bring tour groups to the town, with my hotel as their designated lodging — but the deal is void if I am not still operating when the program starts. Second, my daughter turned down a place at a university in the city in order to help me run the hotel, relying on my promise that I would see it through, and that decision cannot be undone if I quit now.

Is my adviser right that sticking with the hotel would be irrational? If not, which of my reasons for continuing are legitimate, and which amount to irrationally honoring what I have already lost? And even if I do continue, is there any sense in which the lost years and money should still matter to the decision or to how I ought to feel about it?
