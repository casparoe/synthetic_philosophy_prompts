I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm fact-checking a draft review essay we've received about Steven Pinker's The Better Angels of Our Nature and the broader debate over whether violence has declined. The reviewer summarizes the book's thesis as follows, and I want to know how much of this I can let stand:

"Pinker argues that violence has declined over the course of human history, and he proves this by showing that deaths from war and murder have fallen steadily since the Enlightenment. Archaeological evidence from non-state societies is largely irrelevant to his argument, since his claim is about the modern era: beginning in the seventeenth century, the rise of the sovereign state monopolized force, and violence declined as a direct result. He calls the period after 1945 the Long Peace because interstate wars ceased entirely, and he attributes this to nuclear deterrence. Since measured violence keeps falling, he predicts the trend will continue, and he concludes that human nature itself has become less violent -- people today are constitutionally less aggressive than their ancestors. Critics who point to the world wars or to ongoing atrocities are therefore simply mistaken about the facts, refuted by the data."

I have a general sense that the book is more hedged than this and that the per-capita versus absolute-numbers issue matters a lot here, but I don't know the argument well enough to mark up the paragraph sentence by sentence. Could you go through it and separate what is a fair statement of the book's claims from what distorts them? Specifically: which claims Pinker actually makes and in what form; where the summary overstates, conflates distinct claims, or substitutes a stronger thesis for a weaker one; whether anything in it corresponds to a real position Pinker explicitly disavows; and whether the reviewer's framing of the critics as factually refuted survives a correct statement of the thesis. I'd also like your judgment on the underlying methodological question the summary glosses over: if the claim is that the world has become less violent, what exactly should be measured -- deaths per capita, absolute deaths, the frequency of violent acts, or the prevalence of practices like slavery and corporal punishment -- and does the verdict depend on the choice in a way the summary conceals?
