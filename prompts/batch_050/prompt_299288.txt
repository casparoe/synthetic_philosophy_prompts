Right, so I don't have any background in philosophy, but I got a bit obsessed with something I stumbled across — apparently a philosopher called G. E. Moore once tried to prove the external world exists by just holding up one hand, then the other, and basically saying: look, here's a hand, and here's another, so there are at least two things outside my mind, job done. People have been arguing ever since about whether that's brilliant or completely missing the point of what the sceptic was asking, and honestly I still can't decide if it's a proper proof or just very confident cheek.

Anyway, I want you to write me a villanelle about it. The form seems weirdly perfect for this: two lines keep coming back throughout the poem, and then they finally appear side by side in the last stanza. So make your two refrain lines be the two hands — one refrain for each hand he raises. As the poem goes round, they should keep getting doubted in different ways (am I dreaming, how do you actually know you've got hands, doesn't the sceptic win on a technicality), and then when the two refrains finally meet in the closing quatrain it should land as the conclusion of the proof — the two premises coming together so the world outside your head walks through the door, so to speak.

A few requests: keep it in proper villanelle shape (five tercets then a quatrain, the alternating refrains in the right places, rhymes where they should be). Make the argument inside it accurate enough that someone who actually knows Moore's proof would nod along — including the bit where he freely admits he can't prove the premises and just insists he knows them anyway, which I gather is kind of the whole move. Don't name-drop loads of other philosophers, keep it about the hands and the sceptic. And I'd like it to work as a poem rather than an essay chopped into lines — if it could be both correct and actually moving, that'd be great. I know that's asking alot.
