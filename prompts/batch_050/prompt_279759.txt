Write a satirical FAQ published on the website of an invented consultancy, the Cosmic Prospects Bureau, whose advisers help scrupulously rational clients respond correctly to Pascal's mugging and its natural extensions. The target of the satire is naive expected-value maximisation under arbitrarily large promised payoffs, not any named person; the Bureau's anonymous advisers are the sincere but hopeless proponents of the view being ironised.

The piece should run to about 800 words, be written in British English, and take the form of eight to ten numbered questions and answers in the Bureau's blandly reassuring corporate voice. The questions should be the ones a troubled client would actually bring: whether to hand over a wallet to a stranger who claims to administer a simulated universe; whether a mugger who specifies his offer in precise detail (the exact number of saved lives, the mechanism, the timescale) deserves more than one who is vague; how to apportion one's salary across several competing muggers with incompatible utopias; whether one may apply a discount for the mugger's evident untrustworthiness without, as the Bureau puts it, 'smuggling one's intuitions in through the back of the calculation'; and what to make of a mugger who offers nothing but merely threatens.

The humour must arise from the Bureau taking its own decision theory entirely seriously: the answers should begin sensibly and then, by impeccable internal logic, arrive somewhere the reader recoils from, so that the piece works as a reductio rather than as mere mockery. A reader acquainted with the literature on fanaticism, credibility discounts and the leverage problem should recognise that the author understands what is being satirised and ideally judge the satire fair; a reader who is not should still find it funny. The irony should remain deadpan throughout, with no winking asides, though one answer may quietly register a strain — a hesitation, an uncharacteristically short reply — before the corporate composure reasserts itself. Close with a final question in which the Bureau addresses, with complete sincerity, the rumour that its advisers do not in practice hand over their own wallets.
