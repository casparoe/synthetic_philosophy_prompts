For a philosophy of science reading group, I've been asked to write down my working commitments on causation so the others can stress-test them, and I'd like an audit before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm an interventionist. To say that C causes E just is to say that some possible manipulation of C would make a difference to E; I don't think we need anything metaphysically thicker than that, and I'm suspicious of talk of "production" or "oomph" over and above patterns of difference-making.

(2) I think an analysis of causation only earns its keep if it ultimately cashes causal talk out in non-causal terms. A theory that smuggles causal notions into its own definiens may be a useful map of the concept, but it has failed at the job of analysis.

(3) Absences can be causes. The gardener's not watering the plant caused its death, and any theory that cannot deliver that verdict is a theory of something other than our ordinary causal talk.

(4) In early preemption cases — Assassin A poisons the coffee while Assassin B stands ready to poison it if A hesitates — B's non-action is no cause of the death, though B's readiness clearly matters to our causal judgments in some way I haven't pinned down.

(5) Causation is transitive: if C causes D and D causes E, then C causes E, and the apparent counterexamples people trade in must be misdiagnosed.

(6) Causal facts are objective features of the world. Whether C causes E does not depend on which variables some investigator happens to have chosen to write down.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and for each real tension what is the cheapest revision and what does it cost? Please order the tensions by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad, and note that I don't already know where the problem is, so I need the tracing of what commits me to what, not just a verdict.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — one sentence naming which pair or pairs are the real trouble — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
