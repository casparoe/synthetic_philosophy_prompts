I'm a second-year undergraduate taking a combined philosophy of mind and cognitive science course, and something I keep noticing in everyday behavior has started to feel philosophically important, but I don't know whether the literature has already carved it out. Here's the pattern. My housemate cooks the same pasta dish several nights a week, and she is genuinely good at it: she adjusts for the pan, the heat, how much pasta is left in the packet. But if you interrupt her partway through -- ask her a question while she's salting the water, say -- she doesn't pause and resume. She goes back to the beginning of the whole sequence: gets the pot out again, checks the cupboard, runs through steps she's plainly already done. The same thing happens to me when I'm reading a difficult text and lose focus: I don't re-find my place, I restart the page from the top. And our lab demonstrator described a technician who, when one measurement in a long protocol failed, would redo the entire preparation rather than correct the failed step, even when the redo cost an hour and the correction would have cost a minute.

What strikes me about these cases is that the behavior looks flexible at one level and rigid at another. Within the sequence, everything is adaptive and intelligent -- that's why it doesn't look like a simple reflex. But the moment the sequence is disturbed, the person seems to have no internal record of where in the task they are, only a trigger for running the whole thing again. The flexibility never extends to the structure of the routine itself; it only ever operates inside it. I want to say the competence is somehow nested inside a larger incompetence, and that the restart-on-interruption is the tell that reveals the nesting.

Does this configuration have an established name? I suspect it's adjacent to things like fixed action patterns in ethology and to the psychology of habit and action slips, but both of those seem to be about rigid or automatically captured behavior, whereas the phenomenon I'm interested in is specifically about locally flexible, intelligent-looking behavior that turns out to be anchored to a loop it cannot revise or resume mid-course. If there is a recognized concept here, I'd like to know what it's called and how it differs from those neighbors; if the literature only circles it without naming this exact thing, I'd be grateful for a coined term with a careful definition, including what would count as a boundary case -- for instance, is the person who can resume but only after laboriously reconstructing their place an instance of the phenomenon or already free of it?
