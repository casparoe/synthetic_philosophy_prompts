I have no formal training in philosophy or the history of mathematics, but I have spent the last year reading Hobbes on my own, mostly the parts of De Corpore where he talks about geometry and whatever I could find about his long fight with the mathematician John Wallis. I have been keeping a running list of Hobbes's distinctive claims about geometry, because I want to understand why a man who was plainly wrong about the technical questions remained so sure he was right. My list so far:

1. Geometry is his model of what science should be: it is the one field where people reach conclusions they cannot wriggle out of, and he thought civil philosophy could be made just as certain, partly on the grounds that we can only fully know what we ourselves have made -- we construct geometrical figures, and we construct the commonwealth.
2. His kinematic account of geometrical objects: a line is produced by the motion of a point, a surface by the motion of a line, and so on, so that geometry is grounded in the motion of bodies rather than in abstract entities. This is how he folds geometry into his materialism.
3. His complaint that Euclid's definitions are inadequate because they merely tell you what a thing is called, not how it is generated -- a real definition, on his view, has to give the cause or construction of the thing defined.
4. His refusal to accept unextended points or breadthless lines: since geometry is about bodies, every point, however small, has some magnitude when considered as the mark of a moved body.
5. The episode he is now infamous for: his repeated claims to have squared the circle, published and defended across decades, and the decades-long quarrel with Wallis that followed, in which Hobbes kept producing new supposed refutations rather than conceding.

Here is my request. Give me one more genuinely distinctive Hobbesian claim about geometry -- a doctrine or episode -- that is not on this list. It must not be a rewording, special case, or corollary of anything above: if it is just item 2 or item 4 restated in different words, I do not want it. Explain how it connects to, or sits in tension with, the items already listed, and give me your honest assessment of whether it helps answer my underlying question -- whether Hobbes's geometry was a coherent (if failed) research program or just stubbornness dressed up as method.

Finally, please tell me how confident you are in the attribution and substance of what you add, and single out the one point in your answer you are least sure of, so I know where to double-check.
