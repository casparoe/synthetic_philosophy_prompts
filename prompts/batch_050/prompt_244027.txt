I am deciding whether to accept a place on a long-distance evacuation mission and would like philosophical rather than medical, legal, or practical advice. The only way to reach the ship is a transport booth. The booth scans my body and brain in enough detail to reproduce their complete organization, converts the scanned body into raw material, transmits the specification to the ship, and assembles a body there from stored matter. The person who steps out will have all my memories, intentions, habits, attachments, and character traits, and will firmly believe that he is me.

The transport company offers two protocols. Under the standard protocol, my body on Earth is destroyed as soon as the scan is complete. Under its new verification protocol, my Earth body is instead kept unconscious for 48 hours. Only after the person on the ship wakes, passes extensive physical and psychological tests, and reports an unbroken sense of being me is the Earth body painlessly disassembled. During those 48 hours, the two people could in principle be awakened and speak with one another by video, although their experiences would begin diverging immediately after the scan. The company presents this option as reassuring because it ensures that no defective replica is accepted.

Assume that the technology has an excellent safety record, that cost and convenience are irrelevant, and that my aim is not to produce an admirable successor or preserve my projects for someone else. I want to know whether I will still exist on the ship.

Should the verification protocol make me more willing to use the booth? Please analyze separately:

1. Whether having all my memories, concerns, intentions, and character traits is enough for the person on the ship to be me.
2. Whether the decisive loss occurs when the scan is made or only when the Earth body is destroyed.
3. Whether the temporary coexistence of the Earth person and the ship person strengthens or undermines either one’s claim to be me.
4. Whether my concern should be the all-or-nothing question of who is me, or instead the preservation of what ordinarily makes continued existence matter.
5. If the awakened Earth person demands that the process be cancelled after the ship person has awakened, whether the company should treat that demand as my decision.

Give a definite recommendation, explain where your reasoning is most vulnerable, and identify the smallest change to the procedure that would alter your verdict.
