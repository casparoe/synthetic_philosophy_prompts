I work on reverse-engineering trained machine-learning models, and I keep running into a situation I don't have a word for. I suspect it isn't specific to my field, so I'm asking here.

Here is the pattern, in two instances. First: my team studies a trained image model. One colleague insists the network stores what it knows in particular individual neurons -- she points to a unit that fires for wheel-like shapes. Another colleague says that framing is an artifact of how we happened to label the units, and that the network really stores things in directions spread across many neurons at once, no one of which means anything alone. Both of them can predict everything the network will ever do. Yet they give different answers to a question that seems factual: does this network contain a representation of a wheel, or doesn't it? They aren't disagreeing about what the model will output. They're disagreeing about what is inside it.

Second: a hobbyist friend reimplemented an old arcade game by watching it play, without ever seeing the code. His version matches the original on every input anyone has ever tried. He claims his version "has a lives counter"; someone else notes that you could equally describe his program as storing a single number from which remaining lives are computed on demand, and that the original was surely written the first way. Same behavior, forever; different answers about whether a lives counter is a thing the program contains.

What strikes me is that in both cases the choice of description sometimes does practical work -- my wheel-direction colleague can edit the model's behavior more cleanly than my wheel-neuron colleague, which she takes as evidence her inventory of the network's contents is the real one, not just the convenient one. I'm not sure that follows, but I'm also not sure it doesn't.

Is there an established name for this specific problem -- empirically equivalent descriptions that agree on everything observable yet disagree about what exists inside a system, and the question of whether any fact settles between them? I've seen neighboring ideas mentioned: the underdetermination of theories by data, and Quine's claim about translation being indeterminate. But as I understand them, the first concerns competing theories of what will happen, and the second concerns what a speaker's words mean; neither seems to be exactly about competing inventories of a mechanism's parts. If I'm wrong about that, explain where the boundaries of those concepts actually lie and which one mine falls inside. If my thing fits nothing established, say so plainly and propose a name with a definition and some boundary cases. A bare label without the carving won't help me.
