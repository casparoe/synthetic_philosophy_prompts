I run a regional ambulance service. One veteran dispatcher can often tell from a caller’s voice that a patient is deteriorating before the vital signs do; audits show her hunches are right about as often as our new triage algorithm, which issues the same alerts but also supplies a checklist of reasons. We can fund either extra staffing so she can act on hunches immediately, or an integration that makes crews follow the algorithm only when its stated reasons are present. In Plato’s Meno, Socrates compares correct opinions to the statues of Daedalus: valuable while they remain, but liable to run away unless tied down by an account of why they are true. Treat the dispatcher’s hunch and the algorithm’s alert as the two things Socrates is comparing. Which deserves more authority in life-and-death decisions, and what policy would recognize the extra value of being tied down without fetishizing explanations that might be post hoc?
