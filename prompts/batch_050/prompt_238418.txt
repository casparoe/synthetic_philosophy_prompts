ok so here's the situation. I'm a novelist, finishing a big multi-generational book that runs roughly 1870 to 1970, one family, wars and pogroms and a couple of genuinely hopeful decades in between. Every draft I write, my early readers split into two camps: the ones who say the book finally "means" something because it ends on a note of hard-won progress, and the ones who say I've smuggled in a fairy tale, that history doesn't arc anywhere and I'm just describing where the shrapnel happened to land. I can't write the final chapters until I know what I'd even be committing to either way.

So before I read another page of anyone's actual philosophy of history, I want the spec first. Write the desiderata: what would a theory have to be able to do to count as an adequate account of whether history has a direction (toward justice, toward anything)? Not the theories themselves yet -- the requirements list. Each requirement with the case or consideration that motivates it.

Some things I suspect have to be on the list, tell me if I'm wrong: it has to handle the fact that some specific things really did get better (abolition, smallpox) without that licensing the whole arc; it has to survive contact with the 20th century, which is where every cheerful 19th century version went to die; it has to distinguish between history having a direction and us just being able to tell a story about it afterward, since storytelling is literally my job and I know how easy it is; and it has to say something about who or what is doing the bending -- blind mechanism, human effort, divine plan -- and whether the theory even works if the answer is "nobody."

Flag which requirements you think are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's hobbyhorse (religious providence people vs progress liberals vs the permanent pessimists). And the part I most want: if some subset of these desiderata can't be jointly satisfied -- like, if demanding both "explains real moral progress" and "doesn't depend on a cosmic backstop" turns out to be incoherent -- say so plainly, because that would basically settle my ending for me. Then, only briefly, how a providential view, a secular moral-progress view, and a pure contingency view do against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria. I'll do my own scoring, I have a book to finish.
