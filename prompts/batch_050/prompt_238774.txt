I run a small philosophy-of-mind reading group that occasionally lands actual guests, and next month we're interviewing a philosopher who defends the multiple-drafts view of consciousness -- the idea that there's no further fact about exactly when an experience occurred, only which narrative about it ends up winning out in the brain. The classic test case is the color phi illusion: two differently colored dots flash in different spots, and people insist they saw one dot move and change color partway through, even though the second dot only appears at the end. Draft me about eight interview questions that press on the details instead of letting him give the overview yet again. Specifically I want at least one question on the two ways of explaining the illusion -- did you really experience the color change and then misremember it, or did the brain wait and construct the whole sequence only after the second dot arrived -- and wether he really wants to bite the bullet that there's no fact of the matter which one happened; one question about extremely short timescales, like a few hundred milliseconds, where denying a fact of the matter about timing seems craziest; one on how he responds to people who say they can just tell the difference between having seen something and having inferred it; and one on whether the view is falsifiable at all, or whether it's more like refusing to play the timing game. For each question, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give, because he's very good at sliding past the hard part with a joke.
