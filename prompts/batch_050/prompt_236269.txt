I'm in a legislation course and we keep getting into fights about textualism vs purposivism, and I realized I don't actually know what *I* think, so let me just list my gut verdicts on some hypotheticals and you tell me what theory of interpretation I apparently hold, if any.

Case 1: a town ordinance says "no vehicles are permitted in the botanical garden." An ambulance drives in to reach someone having a heart attack. Not a violation, obviously. Case 2: same ordinance, and a collector wants to display his vintage car as the centerpiece of a flower show, pushed in by hand, engine never on. I say that IS a violation -- it's exactly the kind of thing the word covers and the council can grant exceptions if it wants, that's not my job as a judge. Case 3: a statute adds five years to a robbery sentence if the defendant "carries a weapon during the offense." The defendant had a disassembled rifle in a locked box in the trunk of the getaway car. I say no enhancement, that's not carrying in any real sense. Case 4: a law requires wills to be signed "at the foot of the document." A will signed at the top, clearly intended, witnessed, everyone agrees it's authentic. My gut says it's invalid anyway -- formalities are formalities, the whole point is you follow them exactly. Case 5: a city code bans "sleeping in public parks between midnight and 6am." A nurse on a night shift dozes off on a bench during her break at 2am. I don't think she should be convicted, even though she was, strictly speaking, asleep in a park at 2am. Case 6: a regulation says employers must give a 30-minute break for any shift "exceeding six hours." A shift of exactly six hours gets no break, and I say that's right -- the line is arbitrary but it IS the line, and pretending six means six-plus is just rewriting the rule.

So: what principle, if any, captures this set? Cases 1 and 5 look purposivist, 2, 4 and 6 look textualist, 3 could go either way, and I can't tell whether I'm tracking something real (like where the burden of absurdity has to be, or whether the provision is a formality vs a prohibition) or just being inconsistent depending on how sympathetic the defendant is. If there's a principled line that saves most of these, state it precisely and tell me which verdict, if any, it forces me to give up. And then apply it to a case I keep flip-flopping on: the same town bans "the use of electronic devices during council meetings," and a councilmember reads the meeting agenda off her laptop while a speaker is at the podium. Violation or not?
