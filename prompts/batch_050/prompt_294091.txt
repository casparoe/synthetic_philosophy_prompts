Is there a name for this? I keep circling around a certain kind of person and I can't find the concept for them.

Say someone has figured out — correctly, let's grant — what morality requires, and organises their whole life around it. The thing I keep noticing is that the better they get at it, the more they have to lie. Not about facts. About why they do things.

Example one: they visit their depressed friend every week, like clockwork. If you asked why, the honest answer would be something like "because regular visits to unhappy people in my circle is roughly the best use of my Tuesday evenings" — but saying that out loud would hurt the friend, so they say "because I missed you." And over time the performance has to go deeper than words, because people can tell. So they work on themselves until they actually feel the missing, on schedule. The maximally moral person ends up running a permanent covert opperation on their own inner life, manufacturing spontaneous affection, and ideally hiding the manufacturing from themselves too.

Example two: a parent who, on impartial grounds, half-thinks their kid's college fund should go to malaria nets instead, decides to keep the fund — but realizes that if the kid ever sensed even a flicker of hesitation it would be poison. So they don't just refrain from mentioning it; they train themselves to not hesitate, to make the partial love seamless from the inside.

Here's the thing: it's not hypocrisy, because the actions are all good, arguably maximally good. It's not ordinary insincerity either, because it might be exactly what morality demands of them. It's more like morality, followed all the way down, ends up requiring you to counterfeit the very motives morality is supposed to consist in.

I know there's a famous line of thought that a perfectly moral person would be a cramped, unappealing kind of person — but as I understand it, that's about what the saint misses out on: hobbies, humor, a life of their own. My thing is different, or at least narrower. It's about what sainthood does to the saint's motives — the way perfect morality seems to force a split between what actually moves you and what justifies you, and then demands you plaster over the split so well that even you can't see the seam.

So: does this configuration have a name? If it does, don't just give me the label — I want to know how it differs from the nearby things it could be confused with (the split-motive problem, the "don't deliberate morally about your friends" literature, plain old alienation), and where its edges are. Does it count if the person fakes the motive but feels no inner split? Does it count if they try to manufacture the feeling and fail? And if there's genuinely no established term for it, what would you call it, and what's the crispest definition you can offer?
