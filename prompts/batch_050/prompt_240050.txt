In two weeks I'm presenting a paper to our department's metaphysics work-in-progress group defending a Humean, best-systems view of laws of nature, and I want to survive the Q&A. My thesis, briefly: everything the anti-Humean wants from the idea that laws "govern" — supporting counterfactuals, grounding explanations, marking the difference between nomic and accidental regularities — is delivered by the best-systems account once you accept that governance is a matter of the role the best system plays for rational inquirers (how we should set our credences, what counts as explaining rather than redescribing), not a matter of laws metaphysically pushing the mosaic around. The extra primitive necessity posited by governing-law views does no predictive or explanatory work beyond this, and the demand that the Humean explain why the mosaic is regular at all is a demand we should just refuse, since any answer would itself bottom out somewhere unexplained.

The audience is about fifteen people. Two are committed dispositional essentialists who think powers do the governing work for free once you have the right ontology of properties. There's a postdoc who defends governing laws in roughly the Dretske-Tooley-Armstrong style with nomic necessitation as a sui generis relation. There's a Lewisian sympathizer who will attack me from inside the Humean camp on the pragmatics of "best" — whose standards of simplicity and strength, fixed how, without circularity given that our standards are themselves shaped by the actual laws. And there are two or three philosophers of physics who mostly care whether best-systems thinking can handle symmetry principles and other apparently meta-level laws, and whether "the mosaic" is even a coherent notion once you take spacetime and quantum entanglement seriously.

Prep me for the question period. I want the objections in roughly the order they will actually come, phrased the way those specific people would phrase them, with a flag for which ones are merely annoying and which could sink the paper if I fumble them. For each: what I should concede cheerfully up front, what I should hold, and a two-or-three-sentence reply I can deliver off the cuff, plus a note on where my reply is weakest so I know which fights to shorten. Include the classic moves I should expect — the circularity charge about standards, the worry that on my view a world containing a single particle has trivial or gerrymandered laws, the complaint that I've replaced governance with epistemology and so haven't defended Humeanism but changed the subject — but also tell me which objection I'm secretly hoping nobody raises, and give me the most honest version of it I can handle. One thing I specifically need: the essentialists will say my view can't explain why properties behave the same way across all possible worlds, and I need to know whether "that's a demand for explanation we reject" is a reply or a retreat.
