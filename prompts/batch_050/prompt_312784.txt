I am taking an upper-level epistemology seminar on Wittgenstein’s On Certainty, and I asked a classmate to explain the contemporary idea of hinge epistemology to me. Their summary was this: Wittgenstein thinks claims such as I have two hands, there is an external world, and the earth existed long before I was born are foundational propositions whose truth we know with certainty because we cannot seriously doubt them. Moore’s mistake, on this account, was not to appeal to such propositions but to treat his confidence in them as fallible. The hinges are instead psychologically indubitable beliefs: they serve as the fixed premises from which our empirical beliefs are ultimately inferred, and doubting one would make the whole language game collapse. My classmate also said that the river-bed metaphor means that some propositions have this foundational status permanently and independently of context, even though the system of hinges belongs to a social practice rather than to an individual mind.

Something about this reconstruction seems partly right and partly foundationalist in a way Wittgenstein may have been resisting. Please assess its fairness in detail. Which elements would most hinge epistemologists accept, and which conflate the enabling role of hinges with ordinary knowledge, inference, or subjective conviction? In your answer, distinguish a proposition’s functioning as a hinge from its being an empirical hypothesis; psychological inability to doubt from a certainty internal to a practice; and the claim that doubt requires a stable background from the stronger claim that every particular hinge is immune to revision. Could Moore’s example about having two hands be a hinge in one context but an empirical claim in another? And does the river-bed image really imply that hinges are timeless, or does it allow them to shift without making inquiry impossible? Finally, say whether the conclusion that radical scepticism is simply incoherent follows from the corrected account, or whether that conclusion requires a further argument.
