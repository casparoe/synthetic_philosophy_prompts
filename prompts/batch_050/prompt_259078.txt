I'm drafting a short position statement for a graduate reading group on population ethics and cosmology, and before I circulate it I want to know whether my commitments are jointly tenable. Here is where I've landed. (1) I hold a totalist welfarist axiology: outcomes are ranked by the sum total of welfare they contain, and more welfare is better regardless of who enjoys it. (2) I reject discounting welfare by temporal or spatial distance; a flourishing life at the edge of the observable universe counts for exactly as much as one next door, and a life a billion years from now counts as much as one today. (3) I assign non-negligible credence to the hypothesis that the universe is spatially, and perhaps temporally, infinite and contains infinitely many people, and I want an ethics that does not collapse conditional on that hypothesis. (4) I think the right response to infinitarian paralysis is the counterfactual-difference approach: rather than attempting to compare the total value of infinite outcomes directly, compare actions by the difference they make to what unfolds in their causal downstream, treating the vast inaccessible remainder as a fixed background; on this view, in expectation our options rank just fine. (5) I am broadly sympathetic to longtermism: because the future may be enormous, interventions with small probabilities of affecting astronomical numbers of future beings can rightly dominate mundane near-term goods in our deliberation. (6) I refuse to pay Pascal's mugger, and I regard any decision theory that counsels paying as thereby disqualified.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the literature. Which pairs of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and how severe is each tension? Which pairs appear to conflict but do not, once one is careful about what each claim actually says? For each genuine tension, state what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me. And if you had to select a single revision that restores the most consistency for the least collateral damage to the rest of the view, say which one it is. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as all equally fatal, and be concrete about the mechanisms by which the commitments collide rather than merely asserting that they do.
