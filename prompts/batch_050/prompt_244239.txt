I help edit a small humor magazine aimed at tech workers, and I want to commission a piece I can also use as a writing sample. The discourse around AI products has settled into a strange pattern: every launch video promises an "agent" that plans, acts, remembers, and pursues goals on your behalf, while every terms-of-service document insists the very same system is a mere "tool" whose outputs you alone are responsible for. Agency is the selling point; toolhood is the legal alibi. I find this double bookkeeping ripe for satire, but everything I have drafted so far comes out either as an angry op-ed or as a generic joke about robot uprisings, neither of which is what the magazine wants.

Write the piece for me: the launch-day product page for a fictional company's fictional AI system, consisting of a press-release-style introduction followed by a short FAQ — you may invent the company name, the product name, and the marquee features. Roughly 700 to 900 words, in a deadpan corporate-marketing voice throughout, with no winking at the reader. The target is the tool/agent double standard just described, plus the surrounding habits of the discourse: autonomy benchmarks touted in the blog post and disclaimed in the fine print, the "human in the loop" who is plainly a rubber stamp, safety language that quietly presupposes the system has intentions while liability language flatly denies it. The humor should come from exaggerating features that genuinely exist in this discourse rather than inventing implausible ones, so that a reader who has followed the tool-AI-versus-agent-AI debate recognizes the satire as fair, while a reader who hasn't still enjoys the page as pure corporate absurdity. Ideally the copy should slide along the tool–agent spectrum — hammer at one end, colleague at the other — without ever admitting it has moved, and the contradiction should be visible to the reader without ever being stated.

After the piece, include a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "targets" (an array of the specific real habits of AI marketing and safety discourse being satirized, one short phrase each); "exaggerations" (an array pairing each target with a one-sentence description of how the piece inflates it); "liberties" (an array noting any places where the piece leans on a contested reading of the tool/agent debate or distorts the actual state of the discourse for comic effect — write an empty array if there are none, but I would rather you be honest than flattering); "confidence" (your confidence, high/medium/low plus one sentence of justification, that a specialist would judge the satire accurate to its target rather than a caricature of a caricature); and "least_certain_element" (the single part of the piece you are least sure lands fairly, and why). Write everything, including the JSON string values, in American English.
