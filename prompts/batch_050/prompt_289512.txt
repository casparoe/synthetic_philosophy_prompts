I'm in a reading group on criminal responsibility and we keep circling around one line. Every time psychopathy comes up someone says some version of "they know right from wrong, they just don't care" -- last week it got said about a case where the defendant ran a fraud over two years, lied fluently to everyone including his own family, showed no remorse at sentencing, but could explain perfectly well why what he did was illegal and condemned. The phrase was used to close the question of whether he was criminally responsible, and then, weirdly, also to close the separate question of whether anything was even morally wrong with him -- as if "he knew, he just didn't care" settled both at once.

But what is "knowing right from wrong" actually claiming here? Can you lay out the different precise readings of that sentence, from weakest to strongest? Some candidates I can gesture at: he can recite which acts society punishes; he can predict which acts other people will condemn; he can pass the kind of test where you're asked whether something would still be wrong if the teacher or the law said it was okay (the rule-by-authority cases versus the hitting-someone cases); he genuinely believes that his victims' interests count for something, as opposed to merely knowing that everyone else talks as if they do; he grasps moral demands as applying to him even when he won't get caught; and then something about *feeling* it -- guilt, empathy, aversion -- rather than just believing it. For each reading: is it trivial, substantive and defensible, or actually false of the psychopathic profile as it's usually described? And which readings do the two uses of the slogan each need -- the legal use (is he responsible, or does anything like the insanity defense touch him) and the moral use (does he deserve the same blame as anyone, or is some excuse lurking)? My hunch is the slogan survives by borrowing the verdict that only the strong readings justify from evidence that only establishes the weak ones, but I can't pin down where exactly the slide happens, and I'd like the menu of readings laid out so I can bring it to the group.
