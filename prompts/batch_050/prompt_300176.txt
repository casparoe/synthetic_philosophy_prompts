"The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem states that any voting rule that selects a single winner from at least two alternatives, and that is defined for every possible profile of individual preference orderings over those alternatives, is either dictatorial or manipulable. The theorem is essentially a corollary of Arrow's impossibility theorem: since Arrow showed that no aggregation procedure can jointly satisfy his fairness conditions, Gibbard and Satterthwaite established that strategic manipulation is simply the same impossibility appearing in another guise. The significance for democratic design is accordingly severe. The theorem shows that every real-world electoral system is always open to profitable manipulation, so rational voters have no reason ever to vote sincerely, and election outcomes therefore never reflect the electorate's true preferences. At most, institutional designers can choose which voters get opportunities to misrepresent themselves."
