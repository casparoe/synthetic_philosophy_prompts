Design a thought experiment that isolates the Meno problem: the question of why knowledge should be more valuable than mere true belief. The scenario must satisfy three constraints. First, it should involve two agents who hold the same true belief about the same practical matter (say, the way to reach a destination), where only one of them knows. Second, the agents must be matched for present practical success: both act on the belief and both succeed equally well, so the value of knowledge cannot be cashed out as superior reliability on this occasion. Third, the difference may not be generated simply by the mere true believer being likely to go wrong on future occasions, since that relocates the case to the stability of belief rather than the value of knowledge itself. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each constraint: state precisely what the knower has that the true believer lacks, and where the extra value shows up in the case — for instance, in credit owed to the agent for the success, in the appropriateness of others relying on their judgment, or somewhere else. Then confront the central worry directly: if a true belief already delivers everything one wants from it, adding whatever turns it into knowledge may seem like valuing a good cup of coffee more because the machine that produced it is reliable, when the coffee is just as good either way. Explain why your case survives this objection, or concede which specification it forces you to relax. Conclude by saying what would change your mind: which consideration or counterexample would lead you to conclude that knowledge has no value beyond true belief with the same practical payoff, or where your argument for its extra value is most likely to fail.
