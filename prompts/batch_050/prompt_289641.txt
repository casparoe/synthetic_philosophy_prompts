Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the foundations of the Everett interpretation: the decision-theoretic camp, associated with Deutsch and Wallace, which holds that a rational agent who accepts unitary quantum mechanics must apportion credence over branches according to the Born-rule weights, and the incoherence camp, which holds that probability talk has no purchase when every outcome occurs on some branch and that the derivation only appears to work by smuggling probabilistic assumptions into its rationality axioms. Structure the statement in four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides sign: what the formalism delivers, what decoherence does and does not accomplish, and what the derivation establishes conditional on its axioms being probability-free. Second, a disagreements table in which each side's position is stated in words that side would itself accept, without caricature. Third, a list of cruxes — sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp — for instance, whether axioms like diachronic consistency or branching indifference are normatively neutral or already encode the Born rule. Fourth, a record of agreed cases with each side's verdict registered in advance: an Everettian agent repeatedly offered even-money bets on outcomes in branches of weight one-hundredth, and a stipulated scenario in which a civilization observes, over ten thousand trials, frequencies that persistently diverge from the Born weights. Where you judge that a portion of the dispute is verbal — say, disagreement about what 'probability' must mean to count as such — identify that portion and argue for the verdict.
