My uncle is always sending me video essays, and the latest one was about Freud and religion. I haven't read The Future of an Illusion myself, but the guy in the video summarizes Freud's argument like this, and I want to know how much of it I should trust:

"Freud's whole case is that religion is a mental illness. He literally calls it a universal neurosis, which means religious believers are psychologically sick even if they seem fine. He traces belief in God to wish-fulfillment: we're terrified of death and helpless against nature, so as children of the species we invented an all-powerful father in the sky to protect us. And here's the kicker -- once you've shown that a belief comes from a wish, you've shown it's false. That's how you expose a delusion, and Freud explicitly treats 'illusion' as just another word for delusion. So without needing any fancy metaphysical arguments, Freud basically disproved God's existence: the belief fails the test because of where it comes from. His prediction was that religion would die out within a few generations as science advances, and anyone who's still religious after being shown all this is just being willfully childish."

A few things ping my radar. I vaguely remember hearing that Freud actually made a big deal out of distinguishing illusions from delusions, and that an illusion for him isn't necessarly false -- if that's right it seems to wreck the middle of the summary. Also the "disproved God" bit feels too strong for a careful writer. But the uncle insists the video is accurate and that I'm just being squeamish because the conclusion is harsh.

So, sentence by sentence: which parts would someone who actually knows Freud accept, which are common simplifications, and which are just wrong? In particular: what did Freud mean by calling religion an illusion, and does showing a belief originates in a wish do the argumentative work the video says it does? What did Freud actually claim about whether God's existence can be proven or disproven? And when he called religion a universal neurosis, did he mean individual believers are sick? Finally, if I fix the summary to only the accurate parts, does the video's bottom line -- that religion stands refuted and persisting in it is irrational -- still follow?
