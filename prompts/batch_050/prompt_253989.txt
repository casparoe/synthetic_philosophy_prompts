I'm a senior software engineer at a company that makes a voice assistant for households, and I'm leading the team responsible for how the assistant behaves when the people in a home want conflicting things from it. I've run into a design question that feels like more than a design question, and I'd like help thinking it through.

The concrete situation: one assistant serves everyone in a household under a single account, and conflicts are constant. A parent asks it to enforce a no-screens-after-nine rule; the teenager asks it to play a video at ten. One partner wants it to keep reordering the usual groceries; the other is trying to change the family's eating habits. A visiting grandparent asks it to settle an argument about politics in a way that will clearly leave one family member feeling the device has sided against them. Nothing here is illegal or dangerous — it's just ordinary people wanting incompatible things from a shared tool.

My product manager's position is that we should stay out of it entirely: the assistant is an appliance, not a member of the household, and appliances don't take sides. His proposed policy is that the assistant simply serves whoever is currently speaking, within limits set by whoever pays for the account. He calls this "neutral."

Something about this bothers me, but I'm struggling to articulate the objection precisely. Serving the current speaker seems to quietly favor whoever is loudest or most present; deferring to the account owner seems to quietly favor whoever pays; and refusing to ever push back seems to quietly favor whoever's preferences are already winning in the household. But I can't tell whether that means his policy smuggles in value judgments while pretending not to — or whether every possible policy does this, in which case "neutrality" is just a way of hiding a choice rather than avoiding one.

Could you help me with the underlying structure here? Is genuine neutrality between conflicting users a coherent design goal at all, or is any default already a substantive stance? And if some stance is unavoidable, what considerations should govern whose interests the assistant weights, and how — the person speaking, the person paying, the people affected, the majority? I'd rather understand the conceptual landscape than get generic product advice.
