so there's this guy in a politics discord i'm in (spun off from an ACX open thread, mostly LW-adjacent people) who insists on classifying every goverment past and present using Spinoza's scheme from chapter 17 of the Theologico-Political Treatise. his whole thing is that passage where Spinoza lays out democracy as the arrangement where the entire multitude, as one body, keeps the sovereign right to do everything it has the power to do -- as opposed to that right sitting with one guy (monarchy) or with a select few (aristocracy). he treats it less like a history-of-philosophy trivia fact and more like a classification algorithm he can run on anything. he calls it "the only non-cringe taxonomy of regimes".

his verdicts so far, on stuff people have thrown at him:

1. classical Athens (assembly of all citizens decides war, laws, everything; officials picked by lot): democracy, "obviously, the reference implementation"
2. renaissance Venice (hereditary Great Council of patrician families elects the doge and runs everything): aristocracy, "textbook"
3. Louis XIV's France: monarchy, "duh"
4. modern-day Norway: aristocracy. his reasoning: a few hundred people hold all state affairs for fixed four-year terms, everyone else just gets to pick which few. he calls it "an aristocracy that re-interviews for its own job"
5. a housing co-op someone described where a 7-person board is drawn by lot every month from the whole membership, and any member who collects ten signatures can force a full-member vote that can override or sack the board at any time: democracy
6. the same co-op but the board is elected for an irrevocable five-year term and members can only wait it out: aristocracy

now here's the new case, which someone (ok, me) just posted and which has him apparently stumped for once: a charter city running on liquid democracy. every resident can vote directly on every measure through an app, any resident can delegate their vote to anyone else, and delegations are revocable instantly, one tap. also, 2% of residents signing a petition forces any decision to a binding full referendum. in practice though, about 90% of residents have delegated to the same roughly thirty super-delegates for a decade straight, those thirty draft basically every proposal, control the agenda, negotiate with each other behind the scenes, and almost nothing gets forced to referendum because their blocs pre-decide everything.

so: predict his verdict -- monarchy, aristocracy, or democracy, by his lights. tell me which underlying rule you think he's actually been running this whole time, since i can see at least two different principles that fit verdicts 1-6 and they split on this case (something like "follow where the sovereign right actually lives, including who can override and recall" vs something like "democracy means no standing ruling class, everyone genuinely shares in governing"). and tell me which earlier case i *should* have asked him about that would have pinned down his rule before i sprung the charter city on him.
