I'm writing a column about why financial markets work at all, given that famous result that rational, common-prior traders basically shouldn't want to trade with each other (if you're offering to sell me something, what do you know that I don't?). So I need help sorting the usual escape routes from these no-trade results.

Here's my list of candidate explanations for why real markets hum along anyway:

1. Liquidity and portfolio-rebalancing needs (people trade because they need cash or to reduce risk, not because of info)
2. Heterogeneous priors -- people genuinely start from different beliefs
3. Overconfidence -- everyone thinks their own analysis is better than the other side's
4. Noise traders / behavioral types who just trade for no good reason
5. Costly information acquisition (the Grossman-Stiglitz flavor: prices can't be fully informative or nobody gets paid for gathering info, so markets need some "noise" to function)
6. Different utility for the same information -- your private signal means something different to you than to me because our situations differ

Could you sort these into tiers by how *deep* the explanation is -- meaning, how much it tells us something interesting about knowledge, information, and beliefs versus just being a behavioral patch or an accounting trick? A sentence or two per item on why it lands where it does, and I'd love it if you flagged any cases where two items are secretly the same explanation wearing different hats.

One more thing. In the piece I'm toying with a distinction between two kinds of trading motive that I haven't seen named anywhere, so I made up terms: "information-harvesting" trading (you trade to profit from info you already have) versus "information-testing" trading (you partly trade *in order to find out* what the market knows, e.g. to see how the price reacts to your order). Does that distinction actually carve anything real, or does it collapse into the standard stuff on my list? Roughly where would it slot into your tiers as an explantion for trading volume?
