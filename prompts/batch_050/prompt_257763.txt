Bit of backstory: my mum's side of the family is Hungarian, and every time we're over there we end up arguing about politics at the dinner table. My uncle's line is always the same -- look, we have elections, the opposition runs in them, newspapers slag off the government every day, judges still sit in courtrooms, so how can you call this authoritarian? And honestly I struggle to answer him, because when I say "democratic backsliding" he just shrugs and says that's a fancy way of saying "my side keeps losing". What I've noticed, though, is a specific pattern that I can't find a good word for. Nothing ever gets *abolished*. Instead, one institution at a time -- the electoral commission, the media regulator, the courts, whoever draws constituency boundaries -- gets staffed with loyalists or nudged through some small, perfectly legal rule change, each one individually defensible, each one too boring for anyone abroad to notice. The elections still happen! They genuinely happen. But the pitch gets tilted a couple more degrees each cycle, and by the time you add up ten years of two-degree tilts the opposition is playing uphill on a cliff face, yet any individual step you point at looks hysterical to complain about.

So: is there a name for this precise thing? I don't mean a coup, obviously, and I don't think it's quite what people mean by "backsliding" either, since that word seems to cover everything from actual coups to a bit of gerrymandering -- it's a label for the *outcome*, whereas I'm after the *method*: formally legal, incremental, incumbent-driven, never crossing a line you could point a court at, and specifically designed so that each individual move is deniable ("other countries redraw boundaries all the time!"). I've seen people throw around "competitive authoritarianism" but when I looked it up it seemed to describe a whole regime type rather than the technique, and someone else said "abuse of law" which feels closer but also weirdly broad. Is there an established concept in political science for this exact mode of hollowing-out -- and if the nearest terms all miss the target slightly, can you tell me how the thing I'm describing differs from each of them, and what you'd call it? Please don't just hand me a label: I want to know where the edges of the concept are, e.g. does it count if the changes are later reversed, does it count if voters genuinely approve of each step, does it stop being this thing once the opposition stops being allowed to win at all.
