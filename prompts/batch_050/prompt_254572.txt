I am preparing a seminar session on the philosophy of selfhood and film, and I want to use Christopher Nolan's Memento as the main text, read through Thomas Metzinger's self-model theory of subjectivity. You should answer as a philosopher of mind with genuine expertise in self-model theory and a practiced hand at film interpretation; think carefully, and be faithful in both directions -- do not reduce the film to an illustration of the theory, and do not bend the theory to fit the film. No need for citations or bibliographies, and discuss scenes without quoting dialogue; my students will have just watched the film, so you can rely on their knowledge of its plot and major scenes.

Here is the angle I want. Metzinger's core machinery, as I take it, is this: the brain constructs a phenomenal self-model -- an integrated, transparent representation of the system as a whole -- and transparency means we do not experience the model as a model; we simply take ourselves to be in direct contact with ourselves and the world. Crucially, Metzinger also distinguishes minimal, moment-to-moment phenomenal selfhood from the richer autobiographical or narrative self built up over time. Leonard Shelby, after his injury, retains the first but cannot update the second: he has a perfectly intact first-person perspective, yet his diachronic identity must be outsourced to tattoos, Polaroids, and annotated notes. Two further ideas I want tested against the film, neither of which (so far as I know) is in the literature, so treat them as proposals to evaluate rather than established notions. First, call Leonard's archive of inscriptions an opaque self-model: a self-model whose representational, editable character is fully visible to its owner, in contrast to the transparent self-model ordinary subjects enjoy. Does the film show that an opaque self-model is even psychologically possible, or does it show that Leonard treats his notes as transparently true at the moment of reading, whatever he knows in the abstract? Second, call his deliberate manipulation of his own future self -- planting false information that his future self will read as fact, as he does when he sets Teddy up as his next John G. -- the Teddy gambit: a recursive act in which a system models its own future transparency and exploits it. I want to know whether Metzinger's framework can even accommodate knowing self-model manipulation of this kind without generating a regress, and what the gambit implies for the claim that we are systems that mistake themselves for the content of their self-models.

Please structure your response in the following sections, in this order:

1. Brainstorm (~150 words): a rough bullet sketch of the key scenes, the key theoretical moves, and the points of friction, before you commit to a reading.
2. The reading in outline (~150 words): the overall interpretation in a nutshell, stated as a thesis my students could argue with.
3. Where the self-model lens illuminates the film (~350 words): the scenes and structural features (including the reversed chronology itself, and what it does to the viewer's own epistemic position) that the theory genuinely explains.
4. Where the film resists the theory (~300 words): I especially want you to press on a mismatch I suspect -- that the film is fundamentally about diachronic, memory-based personal identity in a roughly Lockean spirit, whereas Metzinger's minimal phenomenal selfhood is synchronic and would survive total amnesia intact. If Leonard still has a phenomenal self-model, does the film's tragedy even register in Metzinger's terms, or does the theory have to say the horror is located elsewhere than where the film locates it?
5. The opaque self-model and the Teddy gambit (~300 words): an assessment of my two proposals, including the regress worry and what the Sammy Jankis storyline -- with its suggestion that Leonard's own memories may be confabulated or transposed -- does to each.
6. What the film adds that argument alone cannot (~200 words): end by saying honestly whether Memento contributes anything to the philosophical debate about self-modeling, or whether it is merely a vivid illustration of positions that already existed.
