Here's roughly what I believe:

1. Velleman is basically right: loving someone is a response to their value. Love is an appraisal of the beloved as valuable, which means love can be rational or irrational the way beliefs can be justified or not — if the person is genuinely worthless, loving them is a mistake, and you'd have reason to get out of it.

2. Trading up is not a thing. If I really love my girlfriend, then the fact that some other woman is smarter, funnier and kinder gives me literally zero reason to switch, and if I felt the pull of that it would just show I never loved her properly in the first place.

3. On the direction of explanation, Frankfurt is right: I don't love her because the stuff about her is precious, the stuff is precious because I love her. Her terrible taste in music and our dumb running jokes only matter because they're hers.

4. Love isn't voluntary — you can't just decide at will to stop loving someone — but people can still be blameworthy for their loves, like a parent who simply never comes to love their kid.

5. Shared history is itself a reason. The fact that we've been through things together gives me a reason to keep loving her that a total stranger with identical qualities wouldn't have. This is the Kolodny bit I find convincing.

6. If she had a brain injury and her personality changed completely — the wit, the warmth, all of it gone — I don't think I'd thereby have a reason to leave. Staying would be the loving thing to do, walking away would be a betrayal.

What I want is an audit, not a general essay about love.
