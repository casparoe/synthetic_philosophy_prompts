I've been asked to contribute a written position statement to an interdisciplinary workshop on consciousness science, and before I circulate it I'd like a consistency check on the core commitments, because I've assembled them from different corners of the literature over several years and I'm no longer sure they're all mutually friendly. Here is the set. (1) Phenomenal consciousness overflows cognitive access: there is more in experience than can be reported, remembered, or used in reasoning, and the Sperling partial-report paradigm is good evidence for this. (2) Global workspace theory is essentially correct as an account of access consciousness: a representation becomes accessible by being broadcast for widespread consumption. (3) Moral standing tracks phenomenal rather than access consciousness; a system can matter morally, and can suffer, even if nothing in it can report or deliberate about its states, which is why I think the standing of nonverbal animals and simple organoids turns on phenomenology alone. (4) The change blindness and inattentional blindness results are robust, and they show that naive introspection dramatically overestimates how much people experience. (5) Unattended experience is nonetheless rich and determinate: the unreported background of a scene is experienced in specific detail, not as vague gist. (6) Introspective reports, gathered carefully under controlled conditions, remain our principal evidential window into phenomenal consciousness.

Please audit this set rather than explain it. Identify which pairs of commitments genuinely pull against each other (distinguishing these from pairs that merely look incompatible), order the real tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to give to resolve it and what that concession would cost the overall position. I want a committed verdict, not a menu: name the single revision you would make if you were me, and defend that choice against the obvious runner-up. One constraint on scope: don't just tell me that some individual commitment is controversial in the field -- I'm aware each is contested -- tell me where the set as a whole fails to cohere. Keep the whole reply under 500 words.
