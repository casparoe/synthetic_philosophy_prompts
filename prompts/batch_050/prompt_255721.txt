So a while back I stumbled across this thing called the dollar auction and I genuinely can't stop thinking about it. Quick version in case its not familiar: you auction off a single dollar bill, highest bidder wins it like normal, but the nasty twist is the second-highest bidder ALSO has to pay whatever they bid and gets nothing. So say I'm at 90 cents and you're at 95 — I'm now looking at paying 90 cents for literally nothing, so obviously I go to a dollar. Then you're about to lose 95 cents, so you bid $1.05, because losing five cents beats losing ninety-five. And on and on. Apparently when people actually play this, the dollar regularly sells for multiple dollars, both bidders end up worse off, and the only winner is whoever ran the auction.

The reason it's stuck in my head is I'm in the middle of something with my neighbour that is starting to smell exactly like this. Short version: dispute over where the boundary line runs between our places, there were surveyors, then letters from lawyers, now it's heading toward court. I'm in for about four grand so far, he's spent similar, and the actual strip of land is worth a few hundred bucks at most. Neither of us can back down now, because backing down means admitting everything spent so far was wasted. And once I saw the pattern there I started seeing it everywhere — the car I kept repairing long past the point of sense, a business partnership I stayed in two years too long because of what I'd already put into it.

Please describe the most important implications of the dollar auction idea for how I handle the neighbour situation, and more generally for catching myself earlier next time. Stuff like: how do you tell the difference between a dollar auction and a situation where pushing through really is the right call? Is "not throwing good money after bad" a rule with exceptions, and how would you spot them? Is quitting late actually worse than quitting early, or does it just feel worse? And does the money already spent count for anything at all when deciding what to do next, or is the right move to pretend it never happened?
