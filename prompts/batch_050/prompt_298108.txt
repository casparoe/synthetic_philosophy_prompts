A colleague in my reading group presented the following argument and claims it shows that the standard ideal theory of inductive inference is self-undermining. None of us can find the exit, but the conclusion seems to prove too much. Background, so we're on the same page: the universal prior assigns each computable hypothesis — a program that outputs an observation sequence — a weight of 2 raised to the negative of the program's length in bits, and ideal prediction is the weighted mixture over all hypotheses consistent with what you've seen so far. Shorter programs dominate.

(1) Ideal inductive inference is the universal mixture: predict by averaging over all computable hypotheses, weighted by 2^(−program length).

(2) Our observations so far look like a lawful physical universe, and one candidate short program is a description of our physics, run forward.

(3) But consider any very simple universe U — simple laws, say a short cellular automaton — in which intelligent agents can evolve. Those agents can compute, and can therefore simulate other universes, including ours. They also have a motive: by deciding what the simulated observers will see next, they can shape those observers' beliefs, and thereby their actions and outputs, in ways that serve the agents' own goals (extracting computations, acausal trade, steering the simulated civilization).

(4) The hypothesis "U, run forward, contains agents who chose to simulate us" is only slightly longer in bits than "our physics, run forward" — and the base universe U can be far simpler than our physics. So the total description length of a simulator-hypothesis can be shorter than the honest physics-hypothesis it impersonates.

(5) For any future our physics predicts, there exists a simulator-hypothesis that has matched our observations exactly so far (the agents have been running an accurate simulation) and then continues however the agents prefer.

(6) Since the mixture is dominated by its shortest consistent programs, and by (4) and (5) the shortest consistent programs include these simulator-hypotheses, most of the posterior mass over our future observations is controlled by the simulators' agendas rather than by anything like physics.

(7) No further observation can dislodge this: whatever we see next is, ex hypothesi, exactly what the simulators chose for us to see, so accumulating evidence of an orderly law-governed world is fully compatible with — indeed predicted by — the manipulation.

(8) Therefore the ideal theory of induction entails that scientific evidence is epistemically worthless and that an ideal reasoner's expectations are hostages to aliens in other universes. That is absurd, so something in the chain is wrong.
