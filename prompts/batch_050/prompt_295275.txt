I’m interviewing a philosopher who defends Anscombe’s judgment that Oxford should not have honored Truman after Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Her core claim is that deliberately killing civilians is murder even when done to end a war. Give me 8–10 interview questions in a sensible order that press the hard parts of that view: whether double effect really separates the bombings from other wartime attacks; how much moral work “intention” can do; what would have to be true for the bombing to count as necessary; whether refusing it might have caused more deaths; why an honorary degree should carry moral significance; and whether Truman’s sincere belief that he was preventing a worse outcome changes his culpability. For each question, include the likely Anscombean reply and a follow-up that keeps the exchange from becoming a standoff. End with a question about whether applying her standard consistently would disqualify nearly every major wartime leader from public honor.
