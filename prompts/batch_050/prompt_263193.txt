I'm helping a small EA-aligned grantmaker allocate three years of conceptual philosophy funding aimed at evaluating tranquilism — the view that what ultimately has value is the absence of craving and suffering, rather than the presence of pleasure, satisfied preferences, or anything else positive. Which open questions about tranquilism should this funder prioritize, and in what order? For each question you prioritize, tell me: why it is upstream of the others, in the sense of which downstream verdicts in population ethics or practical cause-prioritization actually hinge on settling it; how tractable it is for philosophers using existing tools; and what finding would move it down the list. Commit to a single ordering and defend it — I don't want a menu of options to choose among. State your weighting criteria explicitly (importance, tractability, neglectedness, dependency structure), and name at least one candidate question you'd cut entirely because settling it wouldn't change what anyone should do.
