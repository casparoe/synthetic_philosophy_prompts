hey, so I'm prepping for a grad reading group on population ethics (we just did Nozick, Parfit's Reasons and Persons is up next week) and I want to sanity-check my position before I present it and get dunked on. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a prioritarian, not a straight utilitarian: welfare has diminishing marginal moral weight, so benefits to the worse-off count for more, even when the total numbers come out slightly lower. (2) I've never been moved by the utility monster. Moral theories don't owe us the "right" answer about beings that couldn't exist; a creature that converts resources into welfare at some absurd rate is a philosopher's fantasy, and I'd rather have a theory that works for beings like us than one contorted to handle impossible cases. (3) I do think the repugnant conclusion is a bullet no one should bite -- any theory that prefers a vast population of lives barely worth living over a smaller flourishing one is disqualified on that basis alone. (4) I think inequality in welfare is bad in itself, not just instrumentally: two distributions with the same total can differ morally just in how the welfare is spread. (5) I find the separateness-of-persons objection to aggregation overblown. We aggregate costs and benefits across times within a single life without apology -- that's just prudence -- and I dont see why the boundaries between persons should be morally deeper than the boundaries between tuesdays. Aggregation across persons stands or falls with aggregation within lives.

When I sketch this for people, someone always says (1) and (2) are in tension, which honestly surprises me. What I want is an actual audit rather than vibes: which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and for the real ones, how deep does the problem go? For each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Please rank the tensions by severity instead of treating every pair as equally damning, and end by telling me which single revision would clean up the most. Also, please answer in American English.
