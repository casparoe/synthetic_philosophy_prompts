I have spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, and I am fluent in the vocabulary of economics, reinforcement learning, and sequential decision theory: states, observations, actions, transition kernels, reward functions, policies, feasibility constraints, bounded computation, and generalization. Please do not simplify those terms.

Restate the equality of what debate among welfare, resources, and capabilities in the vocabulary of a constrained Markov decision process, treating each person as an agent embedded in an environment. Translate the three positions as precisely as possible: welfare as something like reward or return, resources as endowments and constraints, and capabilities as opportunities available under a policy. Where a straightforward translation is misleading, revise it rather than forcing the fit.

For the capability view, distinguish at least three candidate translations:

1. the raw set of actions formally available to the agent;
2. the set of outcomes reachable by some feasible policy;
3. the set of outcomes attainable by the best policy the agent could actually discover and execute given bounded time, compute, information, and cognitive ability.

Say which translation best preserves the distinction between having an opportunity and actually exercising it. In particular, if an outcome is reachable in the formal MDP but only through a policy no human-like agent could realistically find, does the agent genuinely possess the corresponding capability?

Then give me a residue report:

- Which parts of the debate translate cleanly into decision-theoretic language?
- Which parts survive only with distortion—for example, because scalar reward smuggles in a particular theory of welfare, or because feasibility does not capture social and bodily conversion factors?
- Which parts have no natural counterpart in an MDP at all—for example, adaptive preferences, expensive tastes, interpersonal comparison, responsibility, self-respect, or what a person has reason to value?

Do not let the formal vocabulary quietly settle the philosophical dispute. If a modeling choice privileges welfarism, resourcism, or the capability approach, flag it. Conclude with your verdict on whether the untranslated residue exposes a limitation of decision theory or reveals that part of the original debate is less precise than it appears.
