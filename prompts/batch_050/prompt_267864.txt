I sit on the program committee of a small chamber-music society, and after a note I wrote in our season announcement spiraled into a long argument on the members' forum, I've been trying to write down what I actually believe about what musical works are. Before I circulate the statement, I want to know whether the position is jointly tenable. Here is where I've landed: (1) Musical works are abstract types — no score, performance, or recording is the work itself; these are all just vehicles or instances of it. (2) Works are discovered, not created: a composer uncovers a structure that existed timelessly beforehand, and it follows that, say, Beethoven's Ninth would never have existed had Beethoven died in 1803. (3) A work is nothing over and above its sound structure: any two performances that sound exactly alike — same pitches, rhythms, dynamics, timbres — are equally complete instances of the same work, regardless of what instruments or historical setting produced them. (4) Historically informed performance matters for authenticity: a Bach cantata played on period instruments is a more authentic presentation of Bach's work than a modern-instrument performance, and not merely because it happens to sound different — even if a modern ensemble could reproduce the period sound exactly, something essential to the work would still be missing. (5) In rock and studio pop, by contrast, the work simply is the recording: there is no independent work of which the classic studio track is a performance; the artists make the work in the studio and the finished master is it. (6) A cover version is a performance of the very work the original artist gave us — that is exactly what distinguishes a cover from a new composition that borrows material.

Please audit this set rather than lecture me on musical ontology generally. Tell me which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, ordered from most to least severe, and for each genuine tension state what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Also tell me which pairs merely look like they conflict but dissolve once the relevant distinctions are drawn, and why. I should note that I have no preferred candidate for rejection — I want the audit to decide for me. So, finally: if I could revise only one commitment, which single change would clean up the most of the position, and what would I be giving up?
