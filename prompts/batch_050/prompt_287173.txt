A mate of mine who's doing a physics master's keeps telling me my gut reactions to branching scenarios are incoherent, so I want to lay them all out and find out what principle, if any, I'm actually committed to. The setup throughout: I know Everett is true (grant me that), I'm about to undergo some measurement or splitting process, and I report how confident I feel, before entering, about what I'll observe. Case one: a spin measurement that decoheres into an up-branch and a down-branch with equal Born weights, 1/2 each. I'm 50/50, no problem. Case two: same thing but the weights are 3/4 and 1/4. I find myself about 75% confident I'll see up, and that feels just as natural. Case three is where it gets awkward: a rigged device produces one branch of weight 0.91 where I see red, and ninety branches of weight 0.001 each where I see blue — all of them real, decohered, containing a fully conscious me. My honest gut says I'm almost certainly going to see blue, because there are ninety of me seeing it and only one seeing red. My mate says this flatly contradicts case two, and I can see why she'd say that, but the feeling is stubborn. Case four: I've already looked and seen spin-up, and afterwards the lab runs some irrelevant process that splits my branch into a thousand sub-branches differing in some microscopic detail. My confidence that I saw up stays at 100% — splitting after the fact shouldn't touch credences that were already settled. Case five: two branches of equal weight, but in one of them a copy machine makes a duplicate of me inside the branch, so there are two of me seeing B and one seeing A. I still feel 50/50 — the extra copy within a branch doesn't seem to matter, though I realise this sits oddly with case three if my real principle is "count the observers". Case six: a branch of weight one in a billion where something horrible happens to me. In planning my life I want to just ignore it, but saying my credence is literally zero feels wrong too, since the poor sod in that branch is as real as I am.

So: is there one principle that captures most of these — some rule about how self-locating credence should distribute over branches — and what exactly does it say? If it has to sacrifice one of my verdicts, tell me which one and why that one rather than another. I'd also like to know what the principle predicts for two cases I haven't listed: first, a measurement where decoherence is gradual and smeared out so there's no clean fact of the matter about how many branches there even are (does the principle still say anything determinate there?), and second, whether it gives me any reason to expect, over a long run of repeated 3/4–1/4 experiments, that my frequencies should come out near 75/25 rather than 50/50 — because my mate says that's the whole ballgame and I don't really see why. If the answer is that my case-three intuition has to go, I'd also appreciate a story about why it feels so compelling if it's wrong, since I suspect it'll survive whatever argument you give me.
