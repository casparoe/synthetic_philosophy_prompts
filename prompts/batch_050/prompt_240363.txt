I run a discussion group at a sixth-form college -- about 14 students, mix of 17- and 18-year-olds, we've got one 75-minute session. We've been circling around Indian philosophy and next week I want to do something on the Jain idea of anekāntavāda, the view that reality is many-sided and that ordinary assertions only ever capture one aspect of a thing, which is why the Jains thought you should hedge claims with "in a certain respect" rather than just flatly asserting them.

Design me an activity where they *feel* why someone would end up holding this, rather than just hearing it described. What I have in mind roughly: something where different participants end up with genuinely conflicting but apparently accurate accounts of the same thing, they try to settle who's right, and the resolution they keep reaching for -- one complete standpoint-neutral description -- turns out to be elusive or weirdly unilluminating. They should bump into this themselves before I name anything. I especially don't want the activity collapsing into lazy relativism ("everyone's entitled to their opinion!!") because that's exactly what the Jain view *isn't* -- the Jains were realists who thought the thing really does have all these aspects, and that the mistake is one-sided assertion, not belief in a mind-independent world. So the activity needs to somehow make unqualified flat-out assertion feel like the failure mode, not disagreement itself.

Give me: the setup and materials (nothing that needs buying, we have a classroom and whatever's in it), the rules, a rough timeline for the 75 minutes, and then the debrief -- which moments in the activity map onto which bits of the view (the many-sidedness, the respect-relative predication, the idea that one-sidedness is a kind of intellectual fault). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time -- I suspect the clever ones will try to reconstruct the "objective" full picture and feel smug about it -- and how to handle that. And be honest about which feature of the actual doctrine the activity can't reproduce and I'll just have to lecture on: I assume the metaphysical bit (that reality has *infinitely* many aspects, not just more than we happened to sample in 45 minutes) is hard to stage.
