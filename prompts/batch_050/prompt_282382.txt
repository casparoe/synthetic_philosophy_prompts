Write an essay on whether a rational agent can coherently assign probabilities to her own future actions while she is still deliberating about what to do. I spent my career in the natural sciences, where I became accustomed to the thought that a measuring device embedded in the system it measures faces special difficulties: the act of prediction can feed back into what is predicted. I am struck that deliberation may pose an analogous problem. If I am trying to decide whether to accept a job offer, it seems I can forecast what I will do only by treating myself as an object governed by habits and causes; yet deliberation seems to require that I treat the outcome as genuinely open, as up to me rather than as a fact about myself waiting to be discovered. Some philosophers conclude that deliberation and self-prediction are incompatible stances, so that the question "what is the probability that I will take the job?" is somehow ill-posed or idle for the deliberator. Others insist that since I plainly can have well-founded beliefs about my future conduct (I know I will not burn the house down tonight), probabilities for my own actions must be assignable, and any tension must be explained away rather than indulged.

Your essay should reconstruct the strongest version of each side: the argument that deliberation crowds out prediction, and the argument that self-prediction is as legitimate as any other prediction. Consider, among other things, whether the alleged tension dissolves if we distinguish the standpoint of deliberation from the standpoint of prediction; whether assigning a probability to one's own choice risks making the choice self-confirming or self-defeating, and whether that matters; and whether there are practical contexts (planning, promising, advising oneself as one would advise another) in which one stance must give way to the other. Do not end on a shrug. Reach a definite verdict on whether a deliberating agent can rationally assign probabilities to her own choices, state precisely the conditions under which your answer holds, and justify it against the strongest objection you can formulate.
