I'm in a graduate seminar on normative ethics, and last week we reached Williams's integrity objection. The room quickly split along a familiar line: everyone granted that Williams lands a real blow on act-consequentialism, since the theory seems to require an agent to treat her own deepest commitments -- her marriage, her life's work, her identity-constituting projects -- as merely one source of impersonal value among others, to be set aside the moment the arithmetic says so. But most of my classmates then concluded that the objection is parochial: it tells against crude maximizing views, they said, and nothing more. One friend argued at length that Scanlonian contractualism in particular is immune, because a principle forbidding anyone from ever prioritizing her own projects could be reasonably rejected, so agents retain space for partial commitments as a matter of contractualist right. The prerogative is built in, she insisted, so the alienation never arises.

Please argue that this comfortable division of the territory is mistaken: that if the integrity objection succeeds against act-consequentialism at all, it also succeeds against Scanlon's contractualism, and that the reasonable-rejection reply my friend offered relocates rather than answers the problem. In developing the argument, you will need to say precisely what the objection's operative mechanism is -- whether it is a complaint about deliberative psychology, about the structure of justification the theory demands, or about the relationship between an agent and her ground projects -- and then show that the same mechanism is triggered when an agent must stand ready to justify her commitment-laden choices to others on terms no one could reasonably reject. Address the strongest version of the escape route directly: explain why being permitted by impartial principles to favor one's own projects is not the same as being related to those projects in the way Williams thinks integrity requires, and why a permission issued by an impartial procedure may itself be, in the relevant sense, one thought too many. If you think the argument only goes through against some formulations of contractualism and not others, say which, and diagnose what the residual disagreement reveals about what the integrity objection was really objecting to all along.
