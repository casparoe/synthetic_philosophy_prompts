My flatmate is taking a philosophy module and now believes he has refuted infinity. It's about Thomson's lamp: a lamp that's on after one minute, off after another half minute, on after another quarter, and so on, so infinitely many flicks happen before the two-minute mark. His argument goes:

(1) A process is only genuinely possible if a full description of how it unfolds settles what things are like when it ends. The run-up to the finish has to force the finish — otherwise you haven't described a task, you've described a gap.
(2) Take the lamp's entire infinite history of flicks before the two-minute mark. That history is just as compatible with appending "and at two minutes the lamp is on" as with appending "and at two minutes the lamp is off." There is no last flick for the final state to inherit from, so neither ending contradicts anything that came before.
(3) So the flicking history doesn't force any final state.
(4) So by (1), the lamp story describes something impossible — not a task at all, just a gap dressed up as one.
(5) Nothing in (1)–(4) depends on lamps or switches, so it goes through for any infinite sequence of steps squeezed into a finite stretch of time.
(6) So no such process is possible, period.

Here's why I can't just accept this. An idealized bouncing ball where every bounce takes half as long as the previous one performs infinitely many bounces and then lies still, and physics textbooks seem totally unbothered by this. But the bare list of bounces doesn't "force" the stillness either — you could tack on "and at the end it's sitting on the ceiling" without contradicting a single bounce. So by his lights the ball can never stop, and presumably Achilles never catches the tortoise either. That feels like alot to sacrifice for a lamp.

Where does the argument actually break, and what do I lose by breaking it there? If multiple steps are suspect, tell me which one is cheapest to give up. For the record, what I'd most like to keep: the ball really does stop, races really do get finished, and the lamp story is still defective somehow — tell me straight if I can't have all three at once.
