Two friends of mine got into a surprisingly heated argument last weekend and it's still bugging me, so I want a referee. Context: my friend Marta just moved in with her boyfriend, and it turns out he's kind of controlling -- nothing illegal, but he sulks for days if she sees friends without telling him first, checks her phone sometimes, and she now pre-clears her plans with him to keep the peace. Over drinks, Marta insisted she's "still totally free -- nobody locks me in, I can technically do whatever I want, I just choose not to deal with the drama." Our other friend Dev pushed back hard, and they went back and forth like this:

(1) Dev said freedom isn't just about unlocked doors -- a slave with a kind master who never interferes is still unfree, because everything he does, he does at the master's mercy, and Marta is in the same structural position: she acts freely only so long as her boyfriend happens to permit it. (2) Marta said that can't be right, because on that view anyone who could *in principle* be interfered with is unfree, and since literally everyone is vulnerable to interference from someone, the word "free" loses all meaning -- her boyfriend's sulkiness is just a cost she weighs, like an expensive flight, and we don't say expensive flights make you unfree to travel. (3) Dev replied that a sulk isn't just a "cost" like a high price, because a price is impersonal while a sulk is one person's arbitrary will being imposed on another, and freedom in the relevant sense is precisely about not having your choices depend on another person's whim, even if that whim stays dormant. (4) Marta's counter was that Dev is smuggling in moralized language: "domination" or whatever sounds bad, but her actual day-to-day options are identical to what they'd be if she were single and just hated conflict, and you can't call two situations with identical options different in freedom without freedom becoming a disguised judgment about relationships being icky.

Neither of them would budge and honestly I could see both sides by the end. Can you go through the four moves one by one and say who's right at each step and why? And on the overall question -- does Marta's situation actually reduce her freedom, or just change her incentives -- I'd like your verdict, plus one concrete variation of the scenario that would flip your answer, so I can tell whether you two would disagree or whether you'd just be drawing the line in a different place.
