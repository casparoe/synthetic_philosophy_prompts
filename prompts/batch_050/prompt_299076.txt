Does this thing have a name, or is it just the dark forest wearing a hat? Here's the configuration I keep coming back to.

The usual dark forest story (the Three-Body books version) goes: everyone stays quiet because somewhere out there is a hunter, and given chains of suspicion and the impossibility of really checking, you can't afford to find out. Fine. But I keep noticing a weaker configuration that produces the same total silence with zero hunters. Each actor reasons something like: most other civilizations are probably harmless, I'd even say 99.9% of them, but if I'm wrong about one, that's extinction, and there's no second round — no near misses to learn from, no iteration, my first mistake is the last data point I ever get. Meanwhile hiding costs me almost nothing. So I hide. And if everyone runs that calculation, you get a silent sky in a universe where nobody was ever going to hurt anybody. The silence is generated by the asymmetry of the payoffs alone, not by any actual malice anywhere.

I notice the same shape in mundane places: the dev who won't list maintainers' real names on a small open source project not because they think anyone specific is stalking them but because why open that door; a friend who won't put a "nothing in this car" sign up OR leave the doors unlocked, just parks with zero signals either way. Downside unbounded-ish, upside trivial, no repeats. Hide.

So, questions. (1) Does this have an established name, or is it treated as just a special case of the dark forest / precautionary principle? It feels distinct from both — the dark forest as usually told needs at least the credible possibility of predators doing the work, and the precautionary principle is about acting under uncertainty generally, not this specific one-shot asymmetric-payoff silence. But maybe I'm wrong and it collapses into one of them. (2) Does the one-shot-ness do the essential work? If contact were survivable and repeatable — an iterated game instead of a single bet — does the silence equilibrium dissolve, and does that connect it to why cooperation emerges in repeated prisoner's dilemmas but not one-shot ones? (3) Does my version make different predictions than the standard one? A dark forest with real hunters should leave wreckage, destroyed worlds, evidence of strikes. A pure risk-aversion forest should be quiet AND clean. So is my version actually weaker as a Fermi paradox answer, or is it stronger precisely because it needs less?

Don't just hand me a label if one exists — I want the carving: what exactly this phenomenon is, where its edges are, and which neighboring concepts it isn't. And if there genuinely is no name for it, say so and propose one with a definition sharp enough that I can tell what falls outside it.
