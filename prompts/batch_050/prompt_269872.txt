I've been obsesed with the dark forest hypothesis since reading Liu Cixin, and I want to stress-test it against the actual METI / Active SETI controversy — the fact that real humans have repeatedly, deliberately broadcast messages at other stars (Arecibo in 1974, a bunch of beamed transmissions since), while a vocal chunk of the SETI-adjacent community, plus figures like Stephen Hawking, have argued this is reckless and we should at most listen quietly.

So: analyze the METI debate as a live test of the dark forest hypothesis. Concretely:

(a) Spell out the assumptions the dark forest logic actually needs — the chain of suspicion, exponential technological growth, finite cosmic resources, and the impossibility of verifying intentions across light-years of delay — and for each one, show how it maps onto an argument people really make for or against broadcasting. Which assumptions are doing the heavy lifting, and which could you drop without the conclusion changing?

(b) The hypothesis predicts that rational civilizations go quiet. So what do you do with the fact that we've been leaking radio for decades and actively shouting on top of that? Does the "we're already noisy, the stealth ship has sailed" objection actually defeat dark forest reasoning, or does it just relocate the decision point (e.g., from "should we transmit" to "should we transmit directionally, loudly, or at all going forward")?

(c) Where does the model break? Which parts of the real debate does it illuminate well, and which parts does it simply not have the machinery for — I'm thinking of things like the timescales involved, the fact that a beamed message is one-way with no reply commitment, and the political question of who on Earth gets to speak for the species without anyone's consent.

Finally, the meta-question: does this real-world episode count as evidence against the dark forest, or is the hypothesis structured so that we'd never get disconfirming evidence until it was too late? And what does that near-unfalsifiability do to its standing as a candidate explanation of the Fermi paradox — is it a hypothesis at all, or more like a prior?
