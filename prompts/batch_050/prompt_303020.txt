I'm a PhD student in my fourth year, and for the past eighteen months I've been stuck in a pattern I can no longer explain to myself. Every night I close my laptop genuinely resolved to start writing at eight the next morning, and this resolution feels completely stable -- I can see, clearly and calmly, that a morning of drafting is worth far more than whatever I'd do instead. But at eight the next day, "just one hour" of email or news always seems obviously harmless, and the day dissolves. What unsettles me is that my judgment doesn't merely weaken; it reverses. The very same trade-off that looks indefensible at midnight looks defensible at 8 a.m., and I only recognize the reversal afterwards. A friend tells me I don't really know, in the moment, what I ought to do; another insists I know perfectly well and simply fail to act. Meanwhile, the only intervention that has ever worked is a program that locks me out of the internet until noon -- which seems to settle the matter in nobody's favor, since it treats me as someone whose present judgment can't be trusted even though it feels lucid.

While trying to think this through, I came across a passage in Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics (Book VII, in the Ross translation) where he raises almost exactly this puzzle about the incontinent person: "if he acts by reason of ignorance, what is the manner of his ignorance? For that the man who behaves incontinently does not, before he gets into this state, think he ought to act so, is evident." Aristotle's question is not whether such a person is ignorant, but in what way -- what precisely drops out, or goes quiet, at the moment of action.

So here is what I'd like from you. First, give a committed diagnosis of my case: which "manner of ignorance," if any, fits someone whose preferences systematically reverse as the moment of action approaches -- do I lose hold of the general principle, fail to apply it to this particular morning, possess the knowledge only in the way a sleeper possesses it, or am I not ignorant at all, in which case Aristotle's framework misclassifies me? Second, based on that diagnosis, give me definite advice about whether devices like my internet lock are a rational response to my situation or a way of avoiding the real problem -- pick a side rather than presenting both. Please keep the whole answer under 350 words.
