My roommate and I have been having the same argument on and off for over a year and it's gone stale, so I want to try something different. The fight is about whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler. He's the great man guy: no Hitler, no Holocaust, maybe no second world war at all, because the particular radicalism, the timing of the war gamble, and the decision to murder Europe's Jews flowed from one man's obsessions, nerve, and grip on his party, and a movement led by a Strasser or a conservative-military takeover of the sort people were scheming about in 1932 gives you a nasty authoritarian Germany but not that Germany. I'm the structuralist: given the Versailles grievance, the depression, a coalition system that couldn't govern, elites who despised democracy, and eliminationist-adjacent antisemitism that was already in the water, Germany in the thirties gets some revanchist authoritarian regime under almost any leader, and Hitler was more the vehicle than the driver -- the forces picked him as much as he shaped them. We keep talking past each other because we never separated what we actually agree on from where we really diverge.

So write the document we'd produce if we ran this as an honest adversarial collaboration instead of an argument. I want the claims both of us can sign: presumably things about Weimar's structural crisis and pre-existing antisemitism on my side, but also things on his side, like the fact that specific outcomes (the exact timing and target of the invasion of the Soviet Union, say, or which intra-party faction won out in 1934) genuinely did hinge on individual decisions. Then state our remaining disagreements in exact terms, each phrased so that the person holding it would accept the wording of his own position, not a strawman. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us. I suspect one crux is methodological -- what could possibly count as evidence for or against a historical counterfactual at all, since we can't rerun the thirties -- and another is replaceability, i.e. what a Nazi or authoritarian Germany under different leadership most plausibly does about the Jews and about war. For each crux, name the historical evidence or comparison we would both accept as bearing on it -- I'm thinking of cases like Austria, Hungary, and Romania, where fascist or authoritarian antisemitic movements existed without any Hitler figure, and the behavior of the conservative elites who handed power over in 1933 -- and write down in advance what each of us predicts that evidence shows, so neither of us gets to move the goalposts after.

Two more things. First, each of us should state what we would count as evidence against our own view, since if neither of us can name anything the collaboration is fake. Second, if you conclude that part of our disagreement is verbal rather than substantive -- for instance that we're trading on different readings of "caused" or that "without Hitler" is ambiguous between removing the man in 1919, in 1933, and in 1939 with different results each time -- say exactly which part and why. Format-wise, please write the whole thing as continuous prose, like a long email or memo we'd both sign at the bottom: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. It can be as long as it needs to be, but the structure has to come through in the writing itself rather than in formatting.
