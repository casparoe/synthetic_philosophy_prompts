Having recently sold my company, I have set aside €10 million to donate over my lifetime, and I have become preoccupied with the "hinge of history" hypothesis: the idea that we may be living at the most influential period in human history, because decisions made now could lock in humanity's long-run trajectory (for better or worse) through technologies like advanced AI. I need to turn my thinking on this into a concrete allocation. Please assign percentages of my fund, summing to 100, across three strategies: (i) work on near-term existential risks such as misaligned AI or engineered pandemics, which presupposes that this century is the hinge; (ii) institution- and capacity-building that preserves option value, which presupposes the true hinge lies further in the future; and (iii) robustly effective near-term causes like global health and poverty reduction, whose value does not depend on the hinge hypothesis at all. Give the numbers as decimals (e.g., 0.45/0.35/0.20) with your reasoning, and for each strategy state the single piece of evidence that would most change its share. Then rate the hinge hypothesis itself, as applied to the present century, on a 0–1 plausibility scale, addressing the objection that people in every era have believed their own time to be uniquely pivotal.
