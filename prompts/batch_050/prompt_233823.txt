Retired physicist here, late convert to philosophy of religion, and I keep running into a gap I don't have vocabulary for. Suppose God exists and is omniscient. Everyone seems to agree he knows all the necessary truths (2+2=4, no married bachelors) and also knows the contingent future (what I will in fact have for breakfast). But there seems to be a third thing: he would know what I *would freely choose* in circumstances that never actually occur -- what Peter would have done if the rooster hadn't crowed, what I'd have done if I'd been offered the job in 1987. And here's the odd part: this knowledge can't be grounded in what God has decided to bring about (these circumstances never get brought about) and it can't be grounded in necessity (I could have chosen otherwise), yet supposedly he still knows it, and on some views he uses it in deciding which world to actualize in the first place. Does this middle sort of knowledge -- contingent, not up to God, but known prior to any decision of his -- have an established name? How is it supposed to differ from plain foreknowledge of the future, and what grounds it if neither necessity nor divine decree does? If there's no accepted term, coin one and mark its edges clearly.
