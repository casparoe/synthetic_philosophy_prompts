Here's my opening move: your principle is either circular or useless. Which interests count as "affected" by a decision depends on what gets decided — whether the dam affects the town downstream depends on whether it's built, and whether it's built depends on who got to vote on it. So you need the demos already fixed before the principle can tell you who belongs in it, which means it can't answer the boundary question at all. And if you dodge that by saying everyone possibly affected by any possible decision gets a say, then every municipal zoning dispute becomes a world referendum, since American interest rate decisions "affect" farmers in Brazil. Either the principle presupposes the very boundary it's supposed to draw, or it draws a boundary nobody could accept. Which horn are you taking?
