I’m putting together a discussion question for a philosophy of mind reading group, and we kept getting stuck on the Aunt Hillary-style question of whether an ant colony could literally have a mind. Imagine a synthetic colony of 50 million ant-like robots. Each unit has only a simple processor and no understanding of the colony’s overall plans, but together their signaling and feedback loops reproduce the relevant causal organization of an awake human cortex for one hour: there is global information integration, recurrent processing, memory, attention-like selection, self-modeling, and coordinated behavior. The units are spread across a warehouse and communicate at the same speeds as neurons do, and replacing any small subset doesn’t disrupt the overall process. The colony-level system behaves as if it is experiencing and thinking about the world, even though no individual ant-bot does.

Act like a careful philosopher of mind who is also comfortable assigning rough probabilities. What is your actual credence that this colony has one unified phenomenal consciousness—that there is something it is like to be the colony as a whole, over and above whatever experiences the individual ant-bots may have? Don’t just say that it depends on your theory of consciousness. Give me a number, then decompose it: how much depends on substrate independence, how much on whether functional organization is sufficient for consciousness, how much on the criteria for counting this as one subject rather than many interacting systems, and how much on our uncertainty about what phenomenal unity requires. Include rough probabilities for those sub-questions, explain how they combine, and identify which one your final credence is most sensitive to. Also give a comparison credence for an ordinary biological ant colony with much less integrated organization.

Please think carefully and briefly brainstorm rival answers before committing. Separate what is reasonably settled or widely accepted from what remains genuinely contested, and don’t present a contested theory of consciousness as if it were the expert consensus. Finally, say what empirical discovery or philosophical argument would move your number the most, and in which direction.
