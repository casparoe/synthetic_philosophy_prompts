"In classical electromagnetism, the scalar and vector potentials are gauge-dependent: one can shift them by a gauge transformation while leaving every observable prediction unchanged. Gauge symmetry is therefore not a genuine symmetry of nature, like rotational symmetry, but merely a redundancy of our mathematical description, comparable to the freedom to choose different coordinate systems for the same space. It follows that the only physically real quantities in the theory are the gauge-invariant ones, namely the electric and magnetic field strengths. The Aharonov-Bohm effect is the famous exception that proves the rule: since the relative phase shift of the electron beams depends on the vector potential in a region where the electric and magnetic fields both vanish, the potential must be physically real after all. This is why physicists now treat the potentials as the fundamental quantities of electromagnetism and the fields as derived from them. Philosophers call the gauge-dependent remainder 'surplus structure,' and the general methodological lesson is that a mature theory should be reformulated so as to eliminate all of its surplus structure, just as Newtonian gravity can be reformulated to do without absolute space."
