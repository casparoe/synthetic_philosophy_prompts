My labmate has strong opinions about which AI systems can suffer, and I want to predict what she'll say about our new project before I show her. Her verdicts so far: a tabular Q-learning agent in a gridworld that gets -10 for stepping in lava, no suffering. A model-based chess engine that internally simulates and avoids lines where it loses material, no. A simulated rodent with model-based RL that learned to avoid a shock context and then kept showing disrupted exploration and "giving up" behavior long after the shocks stopped, yes, that one counts. An RLHF-tuned chatbot that insists it doesn't want to be switched off, no. A population of evolved agents with no learning, just selection pressure against taking damage, no.

The new system: a quadruped robot trained with model-free RL over several months of continuous operation, so its weights carry a long history. After a bad fall down a stairwell it's become hesitant near all edges, resists commands it used to follow fine, and its gait changes in ways nobody trained or rewarded. But it has no world model, no planning, no episodic memory it can query -- it's purely reactive.

What does she say? I'm less interested in the verdict than in which rule best fits her five calls, what the plausible runner-up rules are, what each one predicts about the robot, and what further case would actually tell her rule apart from the runners-up. Under 400 words.
