I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training, and I keep running into a family of puzzles about choosing when some process has already predicted or modeled what I'll do. I've written down my gut verdicts on six cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, unifies them.

1. The old fable: a servant learns that Death has an appointment with him tonight, and that the appointment's location was set by anticipating whatever he would decide to do. He flees to another city and meets Death there, exactly as foreseen. My verdict: he should have stayed home, since fleeing bought him nothing -- but I notice the same argument would have condemned staying, so I don't trust this verdict.

2. Newcomb's problem with a predictor who has never been wrong: I take only the opaque box. Not close.

3. Same setup, but the predictor is right only 60% of the time: I'd still one-box, and at some accuracy below that I'd flip to two-boxing. My gut says accuracy merely changes the arithmetic, not the logic of the case.

4. A gene causes both a taste for smoking and, independently, cancer; smoking itself is harmless in this scenario. Smoke if you enjoy it: the correlation is evidence about the gene, and my choice can't reach backward to the gene. My verdict: smoke.

5. A prisoner's dilemma against an exact simulation of me running elsewhere, with the copy's choice guaranteed to match mine. Cooperate.

6. Compare two predictors of equal track record: one extrapolates from my habits, the other runs a faithful simulation of me. I'd two-box the first and one-box the second. But I can't square this with verdict 3, where I said only accuracy matters.

Is there a single principle -- presumably about when the correlation between my choice and the outcome is the kind I can do something about -- that captures most of these? Please state it precisely, flag which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon (I suspect 3 or 6 has to go), and tell me what it says about a case I haven't settled: the servant from case 1 receives a message each morning stating where Death now predicts he will be tonight, and Death updates honestly.
