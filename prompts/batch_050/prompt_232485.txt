I run a second-year epistemology seminar (16 students, one 75-minute meeting). I want a staged activity that makes the surprise exam paradox happen in the room rather than just get described: the group should first watch backward induction apparently dissolve my announcement ("there will be exactly one quiz during this session's rounds, and on the morning of the quiz you will not know it comes that day"), and then discover that despite the argument having gone through, the quiz can still genuinely surprise them.

Please design the activity with the following features:

1. A concrete ruleset: the session is divided into numbered rounds standing in for days, I pre-commit to a quiz day in a way students can verify afterward (sealed envelope or similar), and students register predictions or wagers each round with a points scheme that needs no money but gives them real incentive to reason, not guess.

2. Two distinct phases. In the first, the announcement is made in full and the group may deliberate aloud before the rounds begin. In the second (run later in the same meeting), the announcement is weakened in a specified way — you choose how — so that one premise of the backward induction is removed rather than merely doubted, letting the debrief isolate which premise was doing the work.

3. A plan for the predictable failure mode: students who already know the paradox will predict the final round precisely because the argument excludes it, turning the paradox into a coordination game about my psychology. Tell me how to keep this meta-level from collapsing the exercise (or how to exploit it if it happens).

4. A debrief mapping specific moments in play onto the steps of the eliminative argument, the epistemic principles it assumes (e.g., closure, iterated knowledge), and the difference between being unable to know the day and being unable to rationally believe it.

5. An honest statement of what the activity cannot reproduce — the paradox involves real days and genuine retention of knowledge across them, and a compressed simulation will lose something; say what.
