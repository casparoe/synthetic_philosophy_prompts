I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I've been thinking about the Sleeping Beauty problem. Quick recap of the standard setup as I understand it: Beauty is woken on Monday if a fair coin lands heads, and on both Monday and Tuesday (with her memory of the first awakening erased) if it lands tails; the question is her credence in heads upon waking. I've devised a variant that I think pulls apart two things the original conflates. Before the coin flip, Beauty must pre-commit to a single price at which she will accept, at every awakening, a bet paying €100 if the coin landed heads; on tails her acceptance is enforced at both awakenings, so she effectively buys the bet twice. Once the experiment begins she cannot revisit the commitment.

Write a dialogue between a committed thirder and a committed halfer arguing about what price Beauty should pre-commit to, and about what the gap (if any) between her optimal pre-committed price and her credence at an awakening shows. Both should be equally sharp: each should construct a modification of my variant designed to make the other's answer look absurd, and each should respond to the other's modification directly rather than restate their opening position. At some point they should address whether their dispute is really about what Beauty should believe or only about how she should bet, and whether a Dutch book argument settles anything here. The dialogue should end with them isolating, in their own terms, the single premise on which they still disagree.
