Write a parody user manual for a vending machine, maybe 500-600 words, clearly ghostwritten by a philosopher who has strong views about original vs derived intentionality. So: the manual keeps nervously insisting that the machine does not actually recognize quarters, want correct change, or believe anything about your selection -- it just behaves as if it does, and any quarter-detecting intentionality in the building is derived from the designers, the operators, and you, the user. Include sections like "Getting Started," "What To Do If The Machine 'Thinks' You Gave It A Dime" (scare quotes doing a lot of work), a troubleshooting table, and an FAQ where one question -- something like "ok but where did MY intentionality come from then" -- gets a suspiciously evasive answer. The jokes should come from the actual Searle vs Dennett fight (thermostats, as-if intentionality, the regress about who lends meaning to whom), so that someone who knows the two-bitser debate laughs and someone who doesn't still gets a readable manual. Deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader.
