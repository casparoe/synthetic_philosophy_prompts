I run a monthly discussion evening at my local library -- about a dozen adults, no philosophy background, ninety minutes. We've been circling the question of whether a government may redistribute wealth that people accumulated through entirely voluntary transactions, and I want them to feel the force of the libertarian complaint before we argue about it. Design a game or simulation, playable with poker chips and small donated prizes, in which the group starts from a distribution they themselves agree is fair, then trades freely under rules they consent to, and ends up somewhere unequal -- at which point I propose we redistribute back to the starting pattern, and ideally some of them spontaneously object that this is taking what is legitimately theirs. I want the objection to arise from their own experience, not from anything I say, and nobody should hear the name Nozick until the debrief.

Two complications I specifically want built in, because our group keeps talking past each other on them. First, include at least one round where a trade between two willing participants foreseeably worsens the position of bystanders who were never consulted -- I want to see whether the legitimacy of the outcome survives that, or whether my players quietly abandon the principle they just defended. Second, end the debrief by mapping each phase of the game onto the distinction between how holdings were acquired, how they were transferred, and what to do when one of those steps was unjust -- and tell me which part of that framework the game cannot actually reproduce and I will have to supply in words. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this (I suspect someone will refuse to trade or someone will treat the chips as meaningless), and how to handle it. Finally, be self-critical about the design itself: tell me which feature of the activity is doing the most questionable work -- where it might be manufacturing the intuition rather than revealing it -- and what observation during a playtest would convince you the activity is failing at its purpose.
