Exam question:

(a) State van Fraassen's Reflection principle as precisely as you can.

(b) Explain the relationship between Reflection and updating by conditionalization. Is one a special case of the other, does one entail the other, and why?

(c) Suppose an agent knows that tonight, while she sleeps, she will be administered a drug that will lower her credence in H tomorrow morning, without her acquiring any new evidence bearing on H. Does Reflection require her to lower her credence in H now? Defend your answer.

Student's answer:

(a) Reflection is a diachronic constraint on how an agent's credences ought to evolve over time. It says that an agent's current credence in a proposition A, conditional on her credence in A at some later time t being r, should itself be r: Cr(A | Cr_t(A) = r) = r, for every value r the agent regards as a possible future opinion. Intuitively, you should defer now to the opinion of your better-informed future self.

(b) Conditionalization is the synchronic norm that an agent's credences at a time must be probabilistically coherent, and Reflection extends that norm across time. Van Fraassen's central result is that an agent who plans to update by conditionalization thereby satisfies Reflection: if future credences are produced by conditionalizing on the evidence that arrives, then one's current credence equals one's expectation of one's future credences, so the required conditional credences come out right. Conversely, the principal argument for Reflection is that violating it exposes an agent to a diachronic Dutch book: van Fraassen showed that book can be made against any agent whose credences fail the principle, which is why rationality requires complying with it.

(c) No. Reflection is motivated by deference to a future self who is epistemically better placed, typically because she has received more evidence. Where the agent instead expects her future self to be impaired, as with the drug, that motivation lapses, and the principle is standardly understood as restricted to situations in which the anticipated future opinion arises through learning. She may therefore rationally hold her credence at 0.8 now while fully expecting it to be 0.3 tomorrow.
