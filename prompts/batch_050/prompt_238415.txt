For a 75-minute undergraduate seminar of 16 students, design a structured activity that lets participants experience the puzzle of self-deception before it is named. The activity should create a genuine incentive to avoid an unwelcome truth while preserving enough ambiguity for students to steer their own evidence-gathering, so that some later insist they “really thought” the convenient thing while others describe strategic ignorance instead. Include rules, materials, any roles or payoff structure, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that separates three possibilities: holding contradictory beliefs, intentionally causing oneself to believe falsely, and merely remaining selectively ignorant. Do not begin with a lecture or use the term “self-deception” until after the reveal. Also explain what will predictably go wrong the first time the activity is run, how to prevent students from treating it as a lie-detector exercise, and which feature of philosophical self-deception the activity cannot reproduce.
