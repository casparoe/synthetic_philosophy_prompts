I'm in the third year of a politics degree and we've just spent two seminars on luck egalitarianism, which I mostly enjoyed. A mate of mine who did the module last year lent me his old revision notes, and he summarises the view like this: "Luck egalitarianism is the idea that inequalities are unjust when they result from luck rather than choice. So if you're born with a disability, or into a poor family, or your business fails because of a flood, society owes you compensation, because none of that was your fault. But if you blow your wages gambling or refuse to work, you get nothing, because that's on you. Dworkin came up with the distinction between brute luck and option luck to sort cases into these two piles, and the whole point of the view is to achieve equality of outcomes by levelling down where necessary. Critics say this is too harsh on people who make bad choices -- like the reckless driver who crashes without insurance -- and luck egalitarians basically have no answer to this, which is why the view has largely been abandoned. Also, for it to work the government would need to establish exactly why each person ended up worse off, which everyone agrees is impossible in practice."
