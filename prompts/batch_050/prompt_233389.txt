I got into rational choice theory through some youtube videos and a couple of pop-science books, and I keep circling around the "money pump" argument. I don't have any formal training in this, so before I embarrass myself in an online argument I want to know if the position I've landed on actually holds together. Here's what I currently believe:

1. Cyclical preferences can be fully rational. Concrete example: I'm picking between three holiday cottages, and I genuinely prefer A to B (nearer the beach), B to C (way cheaper), and C to A (much quieter). None of these are mistakes or me being confused, I've checked them each a dozen times and they all feel rock solid.

2. The money pump argument doesn't refute cyclical preferences, because the pump only works if you keep agreeing to trade, and nobody has to keep trading. At some point I just say no and walk away. The argument assumes I'd act like a machine that accepts every offer, and I'm not.

3. Whether a particular choice is rational should be settled by the options actually on the table at that moment. Turning down a trade you genuinely prefer, just because of deals someone might offer you later, is letting hypotheticals boss you around, and that seems irrational to me or at least not something rationality requires.

4. Relatedly: whether I'm rational can't depend on what offers other people decide to make me. Otherwise someone could make me irrational just by showing up with the right sequence of deals, and that's backwards.

5. If someone can see in advance that following their whole plan will leave them worse off by their own lights than just doing nothing, and they follow through anyway, that's irrational.

6. It can be perfectly rational to pay money to take options away from your future self — leaving your credit card at home, using those apps that lock your phone at night, that kind of thing.

So: does this hang together? Tell me which of these are actually in tension with each other and which only look like they are, and rank the real tensions by how bad they are. For each one, tell me what I'd have to give up to fix it and what that costs. If there's one revision that cleans up the most damage, point me at it. Also, where the people who actually work on this stuff disagree among themselves, say so — don't present one side of a live debate as if it were the settled view.
