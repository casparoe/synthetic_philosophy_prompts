My opening challenge: the two-factor story seems to confuse explaining an attitude with classifying it. In Capgras, someone may insist their spouse has been replaced, search for clues, and feel estranged, yet never call the police, change legal arrangements, or otherwise act as though a kidnapping really happened. Someone with Cotard may say they are dead while still eating lunch and avoiding traffic. If the delusional content is compartmentalized from action in this way, why call it a belief at all, rather than an obsessive imagining or felt reality? Worse, saying a belief-evaluation deficit explains why the person believes it looks circular: the fixation is treated as evidence of belief, and the belief is then explained by whatever causes fixation. So what would make you abandon the belief reading instead of just adding one more constrain?
