ok so I'm meant to be presenting my own view at our ethics reading group next week (topic is supererogation) and before I embarrass myself I want to check the thing actually hangs together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Some acts are supererogatory -- genuinely good to do, but not morally required. My stock example is donating a kidney to a stranger: heroic if you do it, but nobody's done anything wrong by keeping both kidneys.
2. The old demandingness objection to utilitarianism is a good objection. Any theory that says I'm obliged to give away most of my salary to effective charities, forever, has gone wrong somewhere.
3. What you morally should do just is what you have most moral reason to do. If you've got decisive moral reason to do something then it's required, and if you've got decisive moral reason against it it's wrong. I honestly can't see what "should" could mean if it comes apart from "most reason".
4. The reasons in favour of the kidney donation are moral reasons. What speaks for it is the stranger's need, not anything about my own projects or relationships.
5. Moral saints really are morally better people than the rest of us, and we should admire them rather than find them faintly annoying.
6. Blame is only ever apt for wrongdoing. You can't properly blame someone for merely declining to be a hero.
