As a retired natural scientist newly studying philosophy of biology, I am interested in the distinction between explaining a trait as an adaptation and explaining it as a spandrel. Give one carefully developed, biologically plausible example—not the familiar cases of the human chin, the redness of blood, or the dome of San Marco—in which a trait is widespread, regular, and even correlated with reproductive success, yet may still be a developmental by-product rather than something directly selected for. Specify the organism, trait, and hypothetical developmental mechanism; present the strongest adaptationist explanation; offer a rival spandrel or exaptation explanation; and explain what evidence could discriminate between them without treating present-day usefulness as decisive. Conclude by discussing what the case reveals about the limits of inferring evolutionary history from current function.
