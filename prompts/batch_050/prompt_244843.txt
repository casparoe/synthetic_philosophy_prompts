Having spent my career as an experimental physicist, I am accustomed to existence proofs that come with an apparatus attached, so the axiom of choice has been a source of genuine perplexity since I began reading philosophy of mathematics in retirement. I would like a decision tree that sorts a mathematically literate reader from the starting point "I understand the statement of the axiom of choice and have seen it used in proofs" to a named position on its status: Platonist acceptance, formalist acceptance, constructivist rejection, and at least one position that retains weaker choice principles (countable or dependent choice) while denying the full axiom. Each branch node should be a question the reader can answer from their own commitments rather than from knowledge of the labels — for instance, what they make of the Banach–Tarski decomposition, whether a proof of existence must in principle exhibit its object, and whether the law of excluded middle applies to undecidable statements about infinite sets. Where two named positions land on the same leaf, please flag that the tree cannot separate them and say what further question would; and where a combination of answers corresponds to no position anyone holds, note briefly whether that is because the combination is incoherent or merely unoccupied. I would like the tree to distinguish what a position says about the truth of the axiom from what it says about the legitimacy of proofs that use it, since I suspect these are often conflated.
