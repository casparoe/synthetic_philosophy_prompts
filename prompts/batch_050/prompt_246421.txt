quick ethics question from a real situation, plus a passage I can't stop chewing on.

My coworker Priya pulled me aside yesterday and asked me straight up whether the company is planning layoffs in our department, because she's about to sign a lease on a more expensive apartment. Here's the thing: I'm a team lead, and last month I sat in on a planning meeting where layoffs in our department were on the table. Nothing is final and I'm not supposed to share any of it. So I told her: "I'm not aware of any confirmed layoffs." Which is literally true! Nothing is confirmed. But obviously I said it so she'd walk away thinking there's nothing to worry about, and she visibly relaxed and thanked me.

So then last night I fell down a wikipedia hole and ended up reading Augustine's On Lying (as you do), and this part got to me:

"he may say a false thing and yet not lie, if he thinks it to be so as he says although it be not so; and, that he may say a true thing, and yet lie, if he thinks it to be false and utters it for true, although in reality it be so as he utters it. For from the sense of his own mind, not from the verity or falsity of the things themselves, is he to be judged to lie or not to lie."

His test seems to be entirely about what's in your head and what you're trying to do, not whether the sentence checks out. So on his view, did I lie to Priya? I uttered a true sentence and believed every word of it, but the entire point was to plant a false belief in her. Does that count as the double-hearted thing he describes, or fall outside it? And here's what really bugs me: if Augustine thinks my true-but-slippery answer is morally the same as a flat-out lie, then it seems like I may as well have just lied, which surely can't be his conclusion. What does his framework actually leave open for me? Just refusing to answer feels like it tips her off too, since if there were nothing brewing I'd have simply said no. Would he say that refusing to answer is itself a kind of utterance, given that he talks about lying "in words, or by signs of whatever kind"? What should I have done, and should I go back and say something to her now?
