The tree must reach at least these leaves: epistemicism, supervaluationism, a three-valued truth-gap view, a degree-theoretic (fuzzy) view, and a contextualist view that relocates cutoffs across conversational contexts. Then present the whole thing as a table with exactly these columns: (1) Position name; (2) Answers to each branching question, listed in order; (3) The single claim that most sharply distinguishes it from its nearest neighbor on the tree; (4) Its most awkward commitment regarding higher-order vagueness (i.e., the apparent absence of a sharp boundary between the clearly tall and the borderline tall); (5) One concrete everyday case that puts pressure on it. After the table, add a short section listing any coherent combination of answers that no named position occupies, with one sentence each on whether that slot is empty because the view would be incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.
