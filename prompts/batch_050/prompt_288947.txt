Here's my opening move. Imagine a state, call it Veridia, that scores perfectly on all eight of your desiderata. Its statutes are general, published, prospective, crystal clear, mutually consistent, possible to obey, stable, and officials enforce exactly what's on the books, nothing more and nothing less. But the laws themselves are monstrous: they run an efficient, fully predictable system of expropriation and forced labor aimed at a disfavored minority, and everyone in Veridia knows exactly where they stand. Your framework seems forced to say Veridia's system is fully law, and even morally better for being so legality-minded. But that strikes me as perverse, because the clarity and reliability of the system is precisely what makes the oppression so effective -- a sloppier, more arbitrary Veridia would be easier to hide from, evade, and resist. So: does legality retain moral value even when it makes evil work better? And if you want to claim that a regime this wicked must somewhere fail one of the eight desiderata, tell me wich one and why -- without smuggling in substantive moral judgments under the label of procedure.
