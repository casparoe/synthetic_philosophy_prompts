ok so i'm prepping to run a reading group on moral responsibility and the Frankfurt literature is a swamp -- everyone seems to grant the counterexample and then build a totally different theory on top of the wreckage. before i assign anything i want the spec, not the theories. what does an account of moral responsibility actually have to deliver, given that Frankfurt-style cases exist?

here's what i mean by deliver. lay out the desiderata: which cases it has to give a definite verdict on, which distinctions it can't collapse, which moves are off-limits. the cases i can think of, in rough order of how much trouble they cause:

1. the classic setup -- an agent does something bad on their own, but a counterfactual intervener stood ready to force their hand and never had to step in. everyone says the agent's responsible anyway; does a theory just get to bank that intuition, or does it owe an explanation of why the unavailable alternative doesnt matter?
2. prior-sign variants, where the intervener watches for some tell that precedes the decision. this is the one where people start fighting over whether the tell is deterministic (in which case the case sneaks in compatibilism) or indeterministic (in which case theres a tiny window of alternative left)
3. following on that: the flicker question. if the only alternative open to the agent is some thin thing -- the tell itself, or starting-to-hesitate -- is that enough to ground responsibility? i.e. does the alternative have to be *robust* in some sense, and can anyone actually define robust without circularity
4. a case i made up and havent seen discussed: someone who *deliberately* engineers their own inability to do otherwise -- like signing a binding commitment device, or taking a pill that removes the temptation -- specifically so that at the moment of action they couldnt have done otherwise. standard Frankfurt cases have an external intervener; here the intervener is your past self. does responsibility get inherited from the earlier free choice, and if so, does the theory need a whole tracing apparatus, and does that apparatus smuggle alternatives back in through the back door?

for each desideratum give me the consideration that makes it a genuine requirement vs. a prejudice of one camp (compatibilists demanding X, libertarians demanding Y). also: which distinctions cant be collapsed -- i suspect responsibility vs. free will is one (the cases might show something about blame while showing nothing about freedom), and blame vs. accountability vs. attributability might be another but i'm not sure.

two specific things i want flagged: first, which single desideratum eliminates the most live positions, and second -- the part i care about most -- whether some pair of requirements on the list is jointly unsatisfiable, e.g. responsibility requires alternatives robust enough to ground blame AND the Frankfurt intuition stands. if the list itself turns out to be incoherent thats a result, tell me.
