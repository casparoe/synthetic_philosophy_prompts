I have to submit a grade plus written comments for one of the entries, and I'd like your assessment before I commit, because the last thing I need is to be the journalist who misgrades the philosophy essay.

Here's the question the students were given:

"In On Bullshit, Harry Frankfurt distinguishes the liar from the bullshitter by their relation to the truth: the liar must track the truth in order to conceal it, while the bullshitter simply does not care whether what he says is true. When a large language model produces a confident falsehood, is it lying, bullshitting in Frankfurt's sense, or merely in error? Defend your answer against at least one objection."

And here's the student's answer (anonymized):

"Frankfurt's liar and bullshitter are distinguished by their attitudes toward truth. The liar knows the truth and deliberately hides it; the bullshitter is indifferent to whether his claims are true, caring only about the impression he creates. Bullshit, unlike lies, need not deceive anyone, but Frankfurt is clear that it is always false, since someone telling the truth is ipso facto not bullshitting.

A language model cannot lie. Lying requires believing the truth and intending to conceal it, and a model has neither beliefs nor intentions. Nor can it bullshit: bullshitting is defined by the speaker's indifference, which is an attitude a model cannot have. Confident falsehoods from LLMs are therefore best classed as errors, like a miscalibrated thermometer that reads 41°C in the rain. The model is not trying to do anything; it is malfunctioning.

One might object that 'indifference to truth' describes the model's training perfectly, since it is optimized to produce plausible text rather than true text. But a process is not an attitude, so the objection fails.

If anything here is bullshit, it is the marketing: claims that these systems 'understand' and 'reason' are made by people who do not much care whether they are true."

Please give me your response as exactly five numbered points, no intro and no wrap-up: the grade first, then the specific comments that justify it, each point a sentence or two.
