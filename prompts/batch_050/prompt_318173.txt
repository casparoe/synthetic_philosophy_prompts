So a bit of background first: my wife and I are doing IVF and last week we recieved our genetic screening results. We've got five viable embryos, all chromosomally normal, and the clinic gave us this whole report — polygenic risk scores for stuff like heart disease, one embryo carries a variant tied to a modestly higher depression risk, one's predicted to end up a bit shorter, that kind of thing. We have to choose which one to transfer and it turns out I'm completely stuck. Not medically stuck. Philosophically stuck. Everyone around us talks like obviously you pick "the healthiest one" and when I ask why it gets vague fast.

So here's what I want: argue with me. Play someone who genuinely holds the view I keep running into — that prospective parents have a real moral obligation, not just permission, to select from the embryos they could have the one expected to have the best life. Give this person real intellectual resources: not a strawman eugenicist, not a caricature, someone who has thought about the objections and can concede small points (predictions are noisy, parents get some discretion, the science is early) while never giving up the core claim. Stay in character until I say stop. Keep your replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like an actual conversation and not a seminar, and don't lecture me or recite a reading list at me.

My opening move: I honestly don't understand who this obligation is supposed to be to. Say we pick embryo 3 over embryo 1. The kid born from embryo 3 can hardly complain we wronged them by picking 3 — if we'd picked 1, they just wouldn't exist, there'd be a different kid instead. And embryo 1 isn't made worse off either, it just never becomes anyone. So "do it for the child's sake" seems to collapse into... what, making the world contain a bit more wellbeing in the abstract? And if that's all it is, I don't see why that's MY job as a parent rather than some impersonal accounting I never signed up for. Parents have obligations to their actual kids, not to a spreadsheet of possible people. What am I missing?
