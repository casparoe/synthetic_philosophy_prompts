I am trying to get clear on the bootstrapping problem in epistemology before committing to a theory of justification, and I want a specification rather than another tour of the literature. Work from the following case, which is mine rather than the standard gas gauge: Mara buys a consumer weather station whose display shows, alongside each forecast, the station's own estimated accuracy for that forecast. Each morning she believes the forecast on the basis of the display, and each evening she notes that the displayed accuracy figure was high. After a month, on that basis alone, she concludes that the station is a reliable forecaster and thereafter treats its forecasts as near-authoritative. She has never once checked a forecast against what actually happened.

Write the desiderata that any adequate response to the bootstrapping problem must meet. I want to know which inferences it must condemn and which it must protect (some epistemic circularity, such as using reasoning to check reasoning, seems unavoidable, so a response that condemns all of it has overgenerated); which cases it must deliver the intuitively right verdict on (the actually-reliable source, the cross-checking of two instruments of the same design against two of independent designs, and third-person versus first-person assessments of the same reasoning); and which structural commitments of a theory of justification it forces into the open, such as closure of justification under known deduction, the status of basic sources, and the difference between being justified in believing something and being justified in believing one's source reliable. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it and your judgment on whether it is negotiable. State explicitly whether easy knowledge and bootstrapping are one desideratum or two, since some responses treat the defect as lying in the premises and others as lying in the inference to reliability. Flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, say which one you would give up, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidate theories. Then, briefly, score three contenders against the list: externalist reliabilism (including the version that simply accepts bootstrapping as legitimate), dogmatism about perceptual justification combined with a failure-of-warrant-transmission diagnosis, and an evidentialist view on which justification for trusting a source must ultimately come from somewhere other than the source itself.

Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly eight items: items 1 through 6 each state one desideratum in no more than three sentences, including the motivating case and whether it is negotiable; item 7 identifies the pair of desiderata in genuine tension, which you would sacrifice, and which requirement excludes the most candidates; item 8 gives the three-way scoring.
