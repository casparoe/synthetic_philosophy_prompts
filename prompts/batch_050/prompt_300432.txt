I work as a backend engineer at an edtech startup, and we have this pipeline that auto-generates short "fun fact" sentences and quiz prompts for a kids' science app from a knowledge graph. The sentences come out as bare generics -- no "all", no "most", just stuff like "ducks lay eggs", "sharks have skeletons made of cartilage", "mosquitoes carry malaria." Before each release (we ship roughly 400 new sentences a month) a small team of content reviewers approves or rejects them. The reviewers are smart but they're not linguists or philosophers, they get maybe 10 minutes per sentence batch, and right now they're just going by vibes, which means the same kind of sentence gets approved by one reviewer and bounced by another.

The fights keep landing in the same places, and here's my problem: the obvious tests don't work. "Ducks lay eggs" is fine even though male ducks don't, and ducklings don't, and honestly a good chunk of adult females aren't laying right now either. "Mosquitoes carry malaria" is fine even though the vast majority of mosquitos never carry anything -- but if the rule is "a tiny dangerous minority is enough," then "sharks attack swimmers" should pass too, and it feels different, like it would scare kids for no good reason. Meanwhile "books are paper rectangles" is arguably true of most books and it's still garbage as a fact sentence. And we've stayed away from anything about groups of people entirely, but I want the procedure to tell us whether that's right, because "pigeons carry disease" somehow feels different from the same sentence about a nationality and I can't articulate why in a way I could put in a style guide.

So: write me an actual decision procedure the review team can run per sentence. The questions in order, what counts as a passing answer to each, where the procedure hard-stops with a reject, and where it escalates to me or the head of content because it's a values call and not a facts call. Tell me which distinctions I have to keep even if reviewers find them annoying, and which ones I can collapse without breaking anything. Be honest about where the procedure will misfire.

Then run it on these five, showing the work at each step: "ducks lay eggs", "mosquitoes carry malaria", "sharks attack swimmers", "sea turtles mistake plastic bags for jellyfish", and "lions are the kings of the jungle" (a reviewer keeps approving this last one and it drives me nuts -- lions don't even live in jungles, but kids love it). Include at least one case where your procedure gives an answer I won't like, and tell me whether I should trust the procedure or my dislike.
