So I help run the residents association for my apartment block, and we've landed in a weird situation I need a memo for. A company that runs a bunch of servers moved into the commercial unit next door, and they made us this offer: every month they pay the building $3,500 toward our maintenance fund, and in exchange we let them turn their cooling fans up by one notch. Here's the thing -- each notch is so small that nobody can actually hear the difference. We did a test, you sit in the courtyard and they flick between notch 47 and notch 48 and nobody can tell which is which, not even the guy on the ground floor who's closest to them.

The problem is the notches add up. Two years in, some of us swear the courtyard is noticeably louder than it used to be, but here's what makes my head hurt: it never got worse in any single month. Every vote we took was the same vote -- take $3,500 for a change nobody can perceive, or refuse and get nothing. Refusing always seemed kinda pointless? But now we're on this track where if we keep voting the same way for five more years the courtyard will presumably be unusable, even though at no point will any one vote have made it worse.

One of the other committee members, Dave, says the answer is obvious: just keep taking the money and stop the month it actually starts to bother us. I've tried explaining that it will never "start" bothering us in any particular month, that's the whole trap, but he just says if it never bothers us then there's no problem and if it does we stop, and honestly I can't fully say where his argument goes wrong even though I feel sure it does.

Anyway. Draft me the memo I'm circulating before Thursday's meeting. It needs an actual bottom line -- what policy we should adopt going forward, not just vibes -- and it has to take Dave's position seriously because he's not stupid and half the committee agrees with him. If you think he's right and we should just keep voting month to month, fine, but then explain why the five-years-from-now outcome doesn't count against that. And if you think we should lock in some kind of cap or stop the rolling votes entirely, explain how that's not just irrationally leaving free money on the table every single month forever.

Oh and keep it short, nobody on this commitee reads anything longer than a page.
