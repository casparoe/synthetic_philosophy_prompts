Imagine that tomorrow a reputable logician publishes a proof that ZFC is inconsistent, and after a year of scrutiny the mathematical community reluctantly agrees that the proof is correct, though the derivation is long and no one has yet pinpointed where things go wrong. Conditional on this scenario, rank the following axioms and axiom schemas by how likely it is that they play an essential role in generating the contradiction: Power Set, Separation, Replacement, Infinity, Choice, and Foundation. For each, assign a rough probability (percentages need not sum to 100, since several axioms may be jointly involved) and explain briefly what makes it suspect or safe. Then, still conditional on the scenario, rank three reactions the mathematical community might adopt by how reasonable you find each: (a) retreating to a weaker system such as Zermelo set theory or something predicative, (b) retaining ZFC but with restrictions on the implicated axioms tailored ad hoc to block the known proof, and (c) adopting a paraconsistent logic so that the inconsistency can be tolerated without collapse. Justify your rankings in terms of what mathematics actually needs from its foundations, not merely in terms of which option preserves the most theorems.
