I am drafting a short statement of my views on scientific progress for a realism workshop, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is where I have landed on truthlikeness:

(a) Verisimilitude is a semantic notion, not an epistemic one. Whether one theory is closer to the truth than another is a matter of how their contents relate to the world, not of our evidence; two theories can differ in truthlikeness even if we could never in principle tell which is closer.

(b) Progress in mature science consists in increasing verisimilitude, and this is something we can rationally estimate from evidence. Our judgments about which of two theories is closer to the truth track the actual ordering, fallibly but reliably.

(c) Content-based definitions of truthlikeness — measuring it by a theory's true versus false consequences — are beyond repair after the well-known collapse results for such accounts. The viable framework is the likeness approach: represent the possible states of the subject matter in a structured space, define a distance between states, and measure a theory's truthlikeness by how close the states it permits are to the actual state.

(d) The choice of metric on that state space is not a free parameter. For a well-posed domain there is a privileged distance function, fixed by the structure of the subject matter itself rather than by our descriptive convenience.

(e) Truthlikeness comparisons must be representation-invariant. If theory A is closer to the truth than theory B, that verdict cannot reverse merely because both are re-expressed in a different but equally adequate vocabulary.

(f) Some concrete verdicts I am not willing to give up: Newtonian mechanics is closer to the truth than Aristotelian physics; general relativity is closer than Newtonian mechanics; and these are not shorthand for comparative predictive accuracy — they are claims about how the world is.

Please go through this set and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what exactly generates it, and what the cheapest repair would be, including what that repair costs. For each apparent tension, show why it dissolves. Then rank the tensions by severity and tell me which single revision would restore the most coherence overall. I am not asking for a survey of positions in the literature — I want to know whether my package, as stated, hangs together.
