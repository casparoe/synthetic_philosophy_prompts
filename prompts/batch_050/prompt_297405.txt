was arguing with a friend about when a legend tips over into being history, and he came up with a criterion i cant quite refute: a legend becomes history the moment archaeologists locate the physical setting it names. so on his view the Trojan War became (at least partly) history once Hisarlik was dug up and accepted as Troy, Arthur stays legend since nobody agrees where Camelot was, and the Exodus stays legend until someone finds a plausible Late Bronze Age Israelite camp trail in the Sinai. is he right? part of me wants to say yes -- it seems perverse to claim the excavation of Troy tells us nothing about whether the Iliad counts as history -- but part of me suspects the criterion conflates two different things, and that if i could say exactly which two things, i'd see why finding a city doesnt settle whether the war happened, let alone whether it happened anything like the way Homer tells it. so: what exactly is wrong (or right) about the "find the place" criterion, what should replace it, and how do the three cases come out sorted once you apply the better test? i'd rather have the general rule than a verdict on each case, but i suspect you cant give one without the other
