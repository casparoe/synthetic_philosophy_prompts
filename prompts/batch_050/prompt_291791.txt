'In 2016, several groups of researchers independently proved that it is mathematically impossible for any risk assessment algorithm to be fair. Specifically, they showed that an algorithm cannot simultaneously be calibrated — meaning its risk scores mean the same thing across demographic groups — and also have equal false positive and false negative rates for those groups. This result applies to tools like COMPAS, which judges use in bail and sentencing decisions, and it means such tools are necessarily biased no matter how carefully they're designed. The only escape from the theorem would be a perfectly accurate predictor, which is impossible in practice, so the conclusion generalizes: there is no such thing as a fair algorithm, only tradeoffs between different kinds of unfairness. Any company claiming their algorithm is "fair" is either confused or marketing.'
