So I'm writing a novel, and the spine of it is a deal between two communities who have to share a valley and basically disagree about everything that matters. Not in a "they want different stuff" way, in a "they think different things are *sacred*" way. I've gotten really into the idea that two sides with clashing values can sometimes both come out ahead, morally speaking, by trading -- each one concedes something it thinks is wrong in exchange for the other side conceding something it thinks is *more* wrong, so by each side's own lights the world gets better. Gains from trade, but the currency is moral compromise.

The problem is I keep coming up with the same handful of shapes for these deals, and my characters are starting to feel like they're re-enacting the same negotiation with the labels swapped. Here's what I've already got:

1. The charity swap. Two donors, one thinks animal suffering is the whole ballgame, the other thinks it's global poverty. Each agrees to give to the *other's* cause on condition the favor's returned, and total giving goes up so much that both prefer the outcome to just donating their own way. Nice but bloodless, and my editor already flagged it as "a parable, not a scene."

2. Straight logrolling on legislation. Each faction gets its bill passed on the issue it cares most about by voting for the other's bill on the issue it cares less about. Works fine but it's the oldest trick in politics and everyone sees it coming.

3. The split-the-difference policy. Both sides sign onto one package neither of them likes, because each weights the parts differently -- side A hates clause one but loves clause two, side B feels exactly the reverse. This is my workhorse and honestly it's getting worn out.

4. Taking turns. The two communities alternate whose rules govern the shared space -- your sabbath one week, mine the next, your food rules on your days. This one's decent dramatically but I've used it twice already.

What I need is one more *kind* of deal -- a different mechanism, not a reskin of the above with different props. It has to be a genuine moral trade where both sides think they've won by their own values, not just a prudential standoff or a bribe. And it has to be something I can actually dramatize between people, not between spreadsheets. If your example turns out to secretly be one of my four in a trenchcoat, tell me so rather than letting me embaress myself in chapter nine.
