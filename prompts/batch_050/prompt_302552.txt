Exam question: Explain the hole argument as presented by Earman and Norton. What is the argument intended to show, and how does it bear on the debate between substantivalists and relationalists about spacetime?

Student's answer: The hole argument begins with a model of general relativity consisting of a manifold M equipped with a metric g satisfying Einstein's field equations. Consider a diffeomorphism that is the identity everywhere outside some region H (the "hole") but nontrivial inside it. Because Einstein's equations are generally covariant, the dragged-along metric also solves them. The two models agree everywhere outside H yet assign different metric properties to points inside H. If the substantivalist is right that manifold points exist independently of the metric, then these are two genuinely distinct physical possibilities that coincide throughout the past, so the substantivalist must accept a radical indeterminism: the laws cannot determine what happens inside the hole. Earman and Norton conclude that spacetime points do not exist independently of their metrical relations, which is to say that relationalism is correct and substantivalism is refuted. The argument is specific to general relativity, since it depends on the general covariance of Einstein's field equations; in pre-relativistic theories with a fixed background geometry, no analogous construction is possible. A common response, Leibniz equivalence, holds that the two diffeomorphic models describe one and the same physical situation, so that no genuine indeterminism arises.
