I've spent the past year in a reading group on moral responsibility, and I've settled on a position I want stress-tested rather than applauded. Like most people, I accept that Frankfurt-style cases refute the principle of alternative possibilities in its original form: if Black would have intervened had Jones shown any sign of choosing otherwise, but never actually intervenes because Jones chooses on his own, then Jones seems responsible despite lacking alternatives. The standard countermoves don't impress me. The flicker-of-freedom objection points to leftovers -- Jones could have begun to choose otherwise, the telltale sign could have appeared -- but these strike me as too thin to ground responsibility. And the dilemma objection, which says that Frankfurt cases either presuppose determinism (and so beg the question against the incompatibilist) or leave a residual alternative (and so fail against PAP), seems to me handled by the more sophisticated buffer and prior-sign constructions, where the intervener's cue is some involuntary precursor that doesn't determine the decision. So my position is this: the flickers exist, but they're morally irrelevant, and what Frankfurt cases really refute is a robustified version of PAP -- the claim that responsibility requires alternatives that are substantial enough to matter morally. I take this to be a genuine rescue of something PAP-like, not a surrender: the incompatibilist intuition survives in disciplined form.

What I want is a map of my exposure. What would have to be true for this robustified-PAP position to be false or worthless? Consider every kind of pressure, not just the ones I know: cases the view cannot describe (say, where an agent seems responsible precisely because of an alternative that is, by my lights, non-robust); arguments that the robustness condition cannot be spelled out without circularity -- defining "robust" as whatever responsibility happens to require, so that the principle becomes unfalsifiable; the charge that the rescue is Pyrrhic, since if flickers don't matter then perhaps alternatives never mattered at all and the real lesson of Frankfurt cases is that responsibility is an actual-sequence affair, leaving my weakened PAP as a hollow concession no incompatibilist should want; and any tension with adjacent commitments, such as how tracing handles responsibility for actions flowing from earlier free choices, or whether blameworthiness and moral worth diverge on the relevance of alternatives.

Please rank the candidate defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each one say whether a defender of my position would themselves concede it as counting against the view or would dismiss it as a misunderstanding. Then take a stand: identify the single most damaging objection, give your verdict on whether the robustified-PAP rescue survives it, and, if it doesn't, say what you think I should hold instead and why that position is better rather than merely different. I'm not looking for a balanced survey of the literature's options; I want a committed assessment of where my position actually breaks, delivered by someone willing to be wrong.
