Write a satirical crisis-communications memo, around 600 words, from an interstellar public-relations consultancy retained by a civilization that has just been detected by a neighbor. The consultants take the dark forest hypothesis as settled client reality and advise with the bland confidence of corporate PR: whether to answer the incoming greeting, how to handle the leak that the neighbor has noticed being noticed, messaging strategy around the chain of suspicion, and crisis planning for a relativistic strike that may already be on its way. The humor has to come from applying the hypothesis's own machinery correctly -- communication as threat disclosure, the technological-explosion asymmetry, silence as the only defensible posture -- so that a reader who knows the reasoning laughs because the advice follows, not because it's a caricature. Never have the memo state the standard objections (detection is not intent; the hypothesis assumes the hostility it claims to deduce; silence is itself a signal); let the consultants' recommendations imply them instead. If you think the premise misreads the hypothesis -- for instance, that its logic wouldn't actually generate this advice -- say so plainly rather than writing the memo anyway. After the memo, add a table with three columns: "Memo element," "Feature of the hypothesis it exaggerates," and "Objection the joke quietly smuggles in," covering the memo's main jokes.
