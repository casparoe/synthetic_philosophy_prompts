ok so some context first: I've been reading LW and the EA forum for like ten years, I donate to longtermist stuff, and I'm currently about to quit my job and retrain for AI policy. Before I do something that expensive I want to stress-test the belief cluster thats actually doing the motivating, because I suspect I assembled it piecemeal from different blog posts whose authors would hate each other. Roughly, here's what I believe:

1. We might plausibly be at the hinge of history. Not as a vibe — the actual claim that this century has a decent shot at being the most influential period ever, mostly because of AI. I'd put maybe 10-20% on the long-run future getting substantially locked in by what happens with AI over the next few decades.

2. I'm big on the outside view. Basically everyone who ever believed they lived at a uniquely pivotal moment was wrong, priors against being special should be strong, and when my inside view tells me I'm special I should mostly discount it.

3. Expected value is the right frame for big decisions, and small probabilities of enormous stakes can legitimately dominate. Pascal's mugging doesn't scare me much; I think the answer is better priors, not abandoning EV.

4. I'm basically clueless about the long-run effects of anything I do. The future is chaotic, effects wash out, signs flip, and anyone claiming to know how their actions today land in the year 3000 is overconfident. Maybe with a carve-out for a small number of robust levers, AI being the obvious candidate.

5. Practically, I only want to work on things with feedback loops. If a field can't tell me within a few years whether Im making progress, that's a strong reason to stay out — thats how you get a decade of impressive-sounding work that was worthless.

So: which of these are actually in tension and which only look like they are? The 1-vs-2 thing is the fight everyone talks about, but I've held all five for years without feeling crazy, so either I've silently resolved it or the real problem lives somewhere else in the set. I genuinely don't know which. Order the tensions by how much damage they do, and for each say what the least painful revision is and what it costs.
