"Rawls's original position is a thought experiment in which representatives of citizens choose principles of justice behind a veil of ignorance. The veil strips the parties of all knowledge that could bias the choice: they do not know their class, wealth, natural talents, race or sex, or their conception of the good, nor do they know anything about the society they will live in, including its level of development or the general facts of human psychology and economics. Because no one can tailor principles to their own advantage, the choice is fair, which is why Rawls calls the view justice as fairness.

The parties are rational and mutually disinterested, and they know they are choosing principles for a well-ordered society whose requirements everyone will have to comply with. Rawls argues that under these conditions of radical uncertainty the parties will reason according to the maximin rule: choose the option whose worst outcome is the best of the worst outcomes. Maximin is appropriate, he says, when three conditions hold: the parties have no reasonable basis for estimating probabilities, the chooser cares little about gains above the minimum, and the alternatives contain outcomes that are intolerable. Rawls, following Harsanyi, holds that the parties should assume it is equally probable that they will turn out to be any particular person in the society, which is what makes the worst-off position the salient one.

Applying maximin, the parties will not gamble on average utility, even with a floor, because the floor might be low and averaging could in principle sanction severe losses for some if the gains to others are large enough. Instead they choose the two principles: equal basic liberties for all, and social and economic inequalities arranged to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged and attached to positions open to all under fair equality of opportunity. The strains of commitment reinforce this: a party who agreed to average utility and then found himself at the bottom might be unable to honor the agreement, whereas the two principles are stable because everyone can affirm them whatever happens."
