Okay so I've been going around in circles on counterfactual mugging type problems and I want to figure out what principle my gut is actually following, if there is one. Here are my verdicts, don't laugh:

1. Classic setup. Omega flips a fair coin, tells me the deal in advance: heads it gives me a million, tails it asks me for $100. It comes up tails, Omega asks. I pay. No hesitation, this one feels clear.

2. Exact same setup except Omega is only right about my decision 90% of the time, not infallible. Now my gut says don't pay. Which is weird, right? The structure of the deal is identical, only the epistemics changed, and 90% is still really good. But something about "it might just be wrong about me" makes my hand stay in my pocket.

3. XOR blackmail. Omega sends the letter, termites or payment but not both. I don't pay. Feels totally different from case 1 even though on the surface it's also "Omega predicted something about me and now I'm being asked for money." I think the difference is that in case 1 the branch where I'm not asked is great for me, and in this one the branch where I don't get the letter is... not something I bought by paying? I'm not sure how to say it.

4. Parfit's hitchhiker situation -- I'm rescued from the desert on the strength of a promise to pay later, and once I'm safe nobody can make me pay. I pay. And I notice this feels like case 1 even though the benefit here actually happened rather than counterfactually happened, so whatever principle I have apparently doesn't care about that distinction.

5. Counterfactual mugging again but this time the heads outcome isn't a million for me, it's Omega giving a million to a complete stranger, and tails I pay $100. Gut says don't pay. So apparently the good thing in the other branch has to be good *for me*? Which feels a bit selfish for a principle but there it is.

And the one I keep flip-flopping on: case 1 again, but after the coin lands tails and before I decide, Omega offers to show me the recording of the flip. I already know it's tails from being asked, so the recording tells me nothing new... and yet I have this nagging feeling that if I'd watched the flip happen with my own eyes, paying would feel like throwing money away, and that this feeling shouldn't make a difference but maybe does.

So: is there one principle that delivers verdicts 1 through 5, ideally also settling the recording case, and if not, which of my verdicts is the one that has to go? I'd rather get a clean principle that overturns one of my intuitions than a baroque one that saves all of them, but I want the tradeoff made explicit either way. Also tell me what the principle says about at least one case I didn't list -- maybe one where the sizes of the gains and losses are wildly asymmetric.

Two more things. First, if any of my cases is underspecified in a way that actually matters (like maybe case 2 changes depending on what exactly Omega told me in advance, or the XOR one depending on whether I get the letter in both branches), say so and answer for the version you think is the interesting one, rather than just picking a reading silently. And second, if my verdicts are just flatly inconsistent in a way that means no non-joke principle exists, or if the whole way I've framed this has a confused premise baked in, tell me that straight instead of politely reverse-engineering a principle to flatter me.
