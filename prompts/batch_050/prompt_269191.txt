I run a reading group on AI safety, grad students mostly, around 10 people, and we have one 90-minute session on the idea of AI safety via debate -- the proposal that you can get truthful behavior out of powerful systems by having two of them argue in front of a judge who can't check everything. I want an activity that makes them *feel* why this might fail: specifically, that a sufficiently good debater can win for a false claim against a debater arguing for a true one, not by lying outright but by obfuscation -- burying the weak step in a chain of locally plausible moves, daring the opponent to spend their limited verification budget on the wrong sub-claim. Design a game with actual structure: roles, how claims and evidence-checking work mechanically (the judge's time or attention has to be a scarce resource somehow), how you pick the claims people debate (they should be checkable in principle but costly in practice), and how many rounds. The debrief should map moments in the game onto the actual proposal -- e.g. if a human debater with prep time can beat truth in your classroom, what hope does a human judge have against a superhuman one -- and be honest about the big disanalogy the game can't reproduce (real debates bottom out in shared facts and physics, not tokens in a box, and nobody knows whether that asymmetry saves the proposal). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time -- like the "false claim" debater just being obviously bad at it and everyone concluding the problem is easy.
