I'm a first-year PhD student and my department has an internal pot of money for six-month projects (nothing huge, enough to buy out teaching), and the application is due next month. I want to pitch something on collective minds, and my starting point is Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary from Gödel, Escher, Bach -- the conscious ant colony that can hold a conversation even though no individual ant understands anything. Standard functionalism says a colony could have beliefs if it has states that play the right causal roles, and people seem happy to leave it there. But here's my half-formed worry. One common mark of a genuine belief is that it can be *wrong*: a representational state that can't misrepresent arguably isn't representing at all. Individual ants misrepresent constantly (follow a stale pheromone trail, attack a nestmate after a weird smell gets on them). The interesting question is whether a *colony* can. Take ant mills, where army ants get stuck following each other in a circle until they die. Is that the colony falsely believing there's food thataway, i.e. a collective hallucination? Or is it just a mechanical breakdown with no more content than a jammed printer? I suspect there's pressure towards the second answer, because whatever the colony ends up doing tends to get redescribed as what it was "trying" to do all along, which would make colony-beliefs unfalsifiable and therefore cheap. If that's right, functionalism about group minds has a problem nobody has quite pinned down.

Turn this into a two-page research proposal. I need: the question stated precisely enough that it has an answer; why the obvious existing positions (functionalism about collective intentionality, eliminativism about group minds) don't already settle it; what I'd actually do across the six months, in rough order; and what a result would look like -- including what would count as the project failing, e.g. if it turns out colony-level misrepresentation is either trivially possible or trivially impossible, either way the proposal should say what I'd conclude from that. Please keep it at a level where a non-specialist grants committee can follow it, don't include any citations or a bibliography (I'll add those myself), and write the whole thing in British English.
