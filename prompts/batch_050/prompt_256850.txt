I am preparing a position paper for my graduate seminar on decision-making under normative ignorance, and before I commit these views to print I want a consistency audit. Here are my considered commitments:

(1) Structural parity: uncertainty about which first-order moral theory is correct should be handled with the same formal machinery as ordinary empirical uncertainty. I assign credences to theories and maximize expected choiceworthiness, where choiceworthiness is a theory-relative measure of how well an option fares by that theory's lights.

(2) Equal voice in normalization: before aggregating across theories, each theory's choiceworthiness scale should be normalized using the gap between the best and worst options available in the choice at hand, so that no theory dominates the aggregate merely because it happens to attach enormous magnitude to this decision.

(3) Menu-independence: if a new option is added to the feasible set, and every theory under consideration ranks it strictly below the options I was previously deliberating between, then which of the original options I ought to choose should be unaffected by its presence.

(4) Anti-fanaticism: it should not turn out that a theory to which I assign very small credence — say, under two percent — controls my decision simply because its stakes are vast. My deliberation should be driven by theories I take seriously.

(5) Pluralism about constraints: I give nontrivial credence, around 0.3, to a deontological theory under which certain act-types are prohibited regardless of consequences, and I expect my framework to honor that theory's verdicts in the cases where it applies, rather than flattening them into cost terms to be traded away.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which are only apparently in tension, and what is the least costly revision that restores consistency? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than flagging every pair; part of what I am asking for is discrimination, including an explicit verdict on at least one pair that looks problematic but is not. Do not assume I know where the problem lies, and do not assume I have a preferred candidate for revision.

Present the audit as a table with the following columns: Commitments involved (by number); Type (genuine or apparent); Severity rank (1 = most severe; write n/a for apparent tensions); What would have to give; Cost of that revision. After the table, add at most two short paragraphs: one explaining the single most severe tension and why it is more serious than the others, and one stating your recommended revision and what I lose by accepting it.
