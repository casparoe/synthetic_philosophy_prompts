So I moderate a small discord where we argue about philosophy, and there's one fight that comes back every couple months like clockwork: whether stuff like money, gender, race, borders etc. is "just a social construct" and what that even means. The frustrating part is nobody in this argument actually disagrees about any facts. Everyone agrees money is just paper and ledger entries that only work because we all go along with it. Everyone agrees there are biological differences between people and also that the way we group people into races doesn't line up cleanly with genetics. Everyone agrees that if humanity vanished tomorrow there'd be no more debts or national borders but there would still be, like, rocks and DNA. And yet the same two camps reform every time: one side says "so these things aren't real, they're made up, we could abolish them by collective decision," the other side says "try telling someone with a mortgage that money isn't real, these things are as real as anything." Nothing moves. It's been two years.

My pet theory is that the whole fight is secretly about what the word "real" is allowed to mean — one camp reserves it for stuff that exists without us, the other camp counts anything with reliable causal powers (a dollar really buys you bread, a border really gets you detained). So maybe it's purely verbal and I should just make everyone taboo the word "real" and the fight evaporates. But I'm not sure, because when I floated that, people got MORE angry, which made me suspect the real disagreement is downstream: it's about whether calling something constructed is an argument for changing it. Like, camp one thinks "made up" implies "up for renegotiation," camp two thinks plenty of made-up things are load-bearing and you'd be insane to knock them out casually. That's not about the word "real" at all, that's a disagreement about what follows from something's origin story.

Anyway here's my homemade test case I keep using and nobody bites on it: imagine a village that sorts everyone by earlobe type (attached vs. hanging) and it genuinely runs their whole society — who can hold office, who eats first, who marries who. Is earlobe-type-status "real"? It's grounded in an actual physical difference, the sorting is clearly invented, and the effects on people's lives are undeniable. If your answer to that case differs from your answer about money, what's doing the work? So: what kind of disagreement is my discord actually having — verbal, factual, about what counts as evidence, or two different questions under one banner? Give me an actual diagnosis with reasons, use what each side would actually say, and tell me what would count as settling it (a distinction? an experiment? giving up?), or if it's genuinely unsettleable, why. Don't just give me a survey of positions, I already live in the survey.
