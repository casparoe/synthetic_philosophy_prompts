So I've been teaching a seminar this semester on the evolution of cooperation, and I realised while prepping the final lecture that my own taxonomy of mechanisms is probably stale. Here's the list I've been giving students for how cooperation can get off the ground and stabilize in populations of self-interested agents (think one-shot or finitely repeated prisoner's dilemmas, not just conveniently infinitely repeated ones where folk theorems do all the work): (1) kin selection / inclusive fitness -- cooperate because your partner shares your genes; (2) direct reciprocity -- tit-for-tat and friends, where cooperation pays because you'll meet again and defections get punished; (3) indirect reciprocity -- reputations, where you cooperate with those who have been good to others and third parties observe and update; (4) spatial or network reciprocity -- cooperators cluster on a lattice or graph and mostly interact with each other, walling off defectors; (5) group or multilevel selection -- groups with more cooperators outcompete groups with fewer, even if defectors win within each group; (6) partner choice / biological markets -- agents can refuse to interact, so cooperators get picked as partners and defectors get shunned out of the market entirely; and (7) green beards -- a gene tags its carriers with a recognizable marker and directs cooperation specifically toward other carriers.

Here's my problem: I'm fairly sure this list (basically Nowak's five mechanisms plus the standard add-ons) covers everything in the textbook canon, and I want to give the students something to actually chew on rather than regurgitate. So: propose one further mechanism by which cooperation could evolve and remain stable that is NOT on this list and not a variant, special case, or recombination of anything on it. In particular, it shouldn't just be reputation wearing a different hat, or clustering implemented by a slightly different spatial structure, or partner choice redescribed at the group level -- I want a genuinely different causal route from self-interested dynamics to stable cooperation. It's fine (even encouraged) if the mechanism is speculative or only works under unusual conditions; I'm asking for conceptual novelty, not an established result.

After you present it, do two things. First, explain precisely how it differs from each of the seven mechanisms above -- the danger is smuggling in kinship or repetition or assortment under a new name, so be honest about where it comes closest to one of the listed items and why it nonetheless isn't that item. Second, give me your candid assessment of whether it could actually work: what conditions it needs, whether those conditions are realistic anywhere in nature or human society, and whether it would survive invasion by defectors. If you suspect it's broken, say so and say where it breaks. I'd rather have a flawed-but-genuinely-new idea with an honest postmortem than a polished retread of tit-for-tat. At the very end, state the name you'd give your proposed mechanism (invent a short label if it doesn't have one) in the form \answer{...}.
