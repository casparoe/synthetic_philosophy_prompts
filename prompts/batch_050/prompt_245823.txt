My philosophy of mathematics seminar spent six weeks on Lakatos's Proofs and Refutations, and the room split into two camps that have been talking past each other ever since. The first camp — call them the quasi-empiricists — takes the dialogue's lesson to be that mathematics grows the way the empirical sciences do: bold conjectures (Euler's V − E + F = 2 for polyhedra being the canonical case), attempted proofs, counterexamples that force revision either of the proof's hidden lemmas or of the concepts themselves, and no final certainty anywhere, only well-tested conjectures. The second camp — call them the infallibilists — replies that everything in that history is psychology and pedagogy: genuine mathematics consists of formal derivations from explicit axioms, informal proofs are at best heuristics pointing toward real ones, and the so-called refutations of the Euler conjecture merely show that the protagonists had not yet fixed what they meant by polyhedron — a verbal mess, not a counterexample to anything mathematical.

Both camps have now agreed, under some duress, to an adversarial collaboration, and I have been deputized to draft the joint statement. What I need is the document itself: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can honestly sign — I expect these to include something about the historical record of accepted proofs later found to contain gaps, the epistemic difference between informal and mechanically checkable proofs, and the fact that central mathematical concepts (polyhedron, continuity, integral) have changed under pressure from problem cases, but please find formulations slanted toward neither camp; (2) a table of their remaining disagreements, each stated twice, in wording each side would accept as a fair statement of its own position; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move someone. For each crux, give a concrete case, argument, or foreseeable development that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

Candidate cruxes the two camps themselves floated, which you may use, refine, or replace: whether a discipline in which every accepted proof was mechanically verified would still experience refutations or only failed conjectures and revised definitions (the quasi-empiricists predict the dialectic of proofs and refutations migrates to the choice of axioms and definitions; the infallibilists predict that mathematics proper becomes refutation-free and the rest is pre-formal heuristics); whether the counterexamples in the Euler saga — the hollow cube, the picture-frame solid, the star polyhedra — are mathematical refutations or merely changes of subject; and whether our warrant for unsurveyable results like the classification of finite simple groups or the four-colour theorem differs in kind from our warrant for a short transparent proof, and what that difference, if any, shows about the epistemology of mathematics. Finally, if on inspection part of this dispute turns out to be verbal — I have a suspicion the word proof is doing double duty throughout — the document should say so explicitly and identify exactly which part.
