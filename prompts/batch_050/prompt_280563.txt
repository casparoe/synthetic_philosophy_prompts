I've spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, and my working vocabulary comes mostly from there: incentives, equilibria, selection pressures, Goodhart's law, legibility in the Seeing Like a State sense, multipolar traps, principal-agent problems, and the general move of explaining social phenomena by asking who wants what and what gets optimized for. I've recently been reading Foucault on power, knowledge, and discipline, and I keep having the feeling that I could absorb most of it into that vocabulary -- but I distrust this feeling, because it's exactly the feeling that precedes a bad translation.

So: please restate Foucault's core claims in the rationalist vocabulary I just described, and then give me an honest residue report. The claims I have in mind are roughly these: that modern power works less by repression and prohibition than by producing behaviors, categories, and ultimately kinds of people; that the human sciences are not neutral descriptions that happen to be used by the powerful but are themselves technologies of power, since to classify and measure people is already to act on them; that discipline operates through surveillance, normalization, and the examination rather than through spectacular violence; that the Panopticon matters as a diagram of a society in which people internalize the gaze and police themselves; and that power is not a thing some agent holds but something exercised throughout the social field, without a single center or author.

Take these one by one. For each, do three things. First, give the best translation you can into incentives, selection effects, information asymmetries, and equilibrium analysis -- e.g., is the internalized-gaze point just the claim that credible continuous monitoring changes the payoff matrix so that enforcement becomes nearly free? Second, say what you had to distort to make the translation work: for instance, my framework assumes agents with pre-existing preferences that incentives operate on, and I suspect Foucault's claim that power constitutes the subject strains against exactly that assumption. Third, say what, if anything, has no expression in my vocabulary at all.

Then the residue report proper. Where translation fails, tell me whether the failure indicts my framework -- i.e., there is a real phenomenon the incentive-and-equilibrium lens structurally cannot see -- or whether it indicts Foucault, i.e., the untranslatable remainder is where the vagueness lives and a rationalist reconstruction is a kind of laundering that makes the claims sound more precise than they are. I'd also like to know whether the disagreement is actually empirical in disguise: if Foucault's defenders and my framework disagree about whether subjects' preferences are endogenous to the systems that govern them, is there anything that would settle that, or does each side just describe the same phenomena in terms that make its own ontology look mandatory?

Play the translation straight rather than charitable-in-my-direction. If the upshot is that my vocabulary is a special case of his, or vice versa, say so and argue it.
