I'm putting together a unit for my graduate seminar in formal epistemology on what I take to be an undersold problem: how an ideally rational agent should revise her credences upon learning a conditional fact, as opposed to a categorical one. The anchor case is van Fraassen's soldier who is equally likely to be in any of three regions and then receives a message constraining a conditional probability (roughly: given that she is in one army's territory, her being in a particular subdivision of it has probability three quarters), with the puzzle that seemingly principled update rules disagree about whether the message should leave her unconditional credences in the territories untouched, shift probability away from the territory the conditional is about, or redistribute it in still subtler ways. Last year the unit collapsed into a shouting match between two students, one insisting that any adequate rule must be a species of conditioning on the right proposition, the other that the message is a genuine constraint and must be handled by constrained optimization over credence functions, and I realized I had no neutral standard by which to adjudicate. So: I want the standard itself as the deliverable.

Write the list of desiderata that a general theory of updating on learned conditionals must meet in order to count as adequate, with the motivating case or consideration behind each. I want the list to cover, at minimum: the requirement of collapsing to ordinary Bayesian conditionalization when the conditional information is in fact learnable by conditionalizing on some proposition (and a careful treatment of whether the soldier's message is or is not so learnable, i.e., the adverbial-versus-propositional reading issue); behavior under symmetry and relabeling of regions; whether learning a conditional probability may ever properly change the unconditional probability of its antecedent, and if so under what further conditions; consistency with plausible reflection-style and diachronic coherence constraints; and extensibility to cases where the message itself is uncertain, iterated, or concerns nested conditionals. For each desideratum, state the case that does the work of motivating it -- feel free to construct variants on the soldier scenario, including ones I haven't thought of, where a candidate rule gives a verdict that is intuitively damning or intuitively correct.

Crucially, mark which requirements you take to be non-negotiable for any theory worthy of the name and which are better understood as partisan commitments of particular camps (the strict conditionalizers, the minimum-relative-entropy optimizers, those who treat conditional information as merely ordinal or comparative). And do the thing I most want: tell me whether some subset of the desiderata on your list is provably or plausibly jointly unsatisfiable -- if the field's disagreement is really a trilemma among individually compelling requirements, the list itself is the result, and I want that stated sharply rather than papered over. A brief closing scorecard of the main existing approaches against the list is welcome but keep it subordinate; the criteria and their motivating cases are where the thinking is, and my students can do the scoring themselves in class.
