So I've been going down a rabbit hole on Bertrand's paradox and I want a diagnosis of why the debate is stuck, not another walkthrough of the chord problem itself. Quick setup so we're on the same page: Bertrand, in his Calcul des probabilités back in 1889, asks for the probability that a chord of a circle chosen at random is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle, and then shows you can get 1/2, 1/3, or 1/4 depending on how you cash out what "random" means (random endpoints on the circumference, random distance from the center, random midpoint in the disk). His own takeaway, at least as I read him, is basically that the question as posed never picked out a unique distribution, so the clashing answers just expose that the problem was underspecified, not that probability talk is broken.

Fast forward a century and the literature seems split into two camps that haven't moved in like fifty years. Jaynes argued the chord problem actually IS well-posed once you take the physical setup seriously, because invariance under translation, rotation, and scale pins down the 1/2 answer uniquely, and alot of people treat that as the solution, full stop. On the other side you have the pessimists (van Fraassen and company) who think Bertrand buried the principle of indifference for good, and that any appeal to symmetry or invariance is just smuggling in empirical information that a principle of pure ignorance was supposed to make unneccessary. Everyone in this fight knows everyone else's arguments cold, and yet nobody has budged an inch.

My hypothesis, which you should feel free to demolish: this only looks like one debate. The Jaynes camp is really asking something like "what probability assignment does this physical situation warrant," while the indifference skeptics are asking "what follows from ignorance alone," and since no actual reasoner is ever in a state of total ignorance, the two sides are answering different questions under the shared label of Bertrand's paradox. Which would explain the stall, since nothing ever settles cross-question disputes.

So tell me: what kind of disagreement is this really? Verbal, substantive, different questions under one name, or bedrock clashing intuitions about what probability even is? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here — a formal result, a new distinction, a changed question, or should we honestly expect nothing?
