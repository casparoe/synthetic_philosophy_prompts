I work as a software engineer, and over the past year a few colleagues of mine have gotten seriously into effective altruism and animal welfare. Last week, over lunch, one of them was explaining why he donates to charities working on insect welfare rather than, say, farmed chickens or wild mammals. When I pushed back -- surely the evidence that insects are sentient is much weaker than for vertebrates -- he shrugged and said, "When the numbers are large enough, a small probability still wins." He used the same line again when I asked how small a probability could be before it stops mattering: apparently there are quintillions of insects alive at any time, so even if the chance they're sentient is one in a thousand, the arithmetic supposedly isn't close. The sentence clearly did a lot of work for him: it ended the discussion about the strength of the evidence, and it justified a fairly radical donation choice.

My problem is that I can't tell what that one-liner actually claims, and as an engineer I'm suspicious of sentences that seem to cash out a contested judgment as pure multiplication. Could you lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise claims that "when the numbers are large enough, a small probability still wins" could be making? For example, I can imagine it being a trivial remark about expected value arithmetic, a substantive claim that expected value is the correct decision rule under deep moral uncertainty, a claim about what kind of thing the "probability of insect sentience" even is (a credence? a frequency? something that could in principle be driven to zero?), or a disguised empirical claim that the insect population really is large enough to dominate any plausible comparison. For each reading, please say whether it's a platitude, a defensible but controversial position, or something actually false -- and flag any readings that fall apart under the standard worries about fanaticism or Pascalian reasoning that I vaguely remember from a philosophy podcast.

Finally, and most importantly: which of these readings, if any, would actually support the use my colleague put the slogan to -- namely, concluding that the evidence question doesn't matter much and that insect welfare should swamp donations to vertebrate causes? I'd like to go back to him with a sharper version of the disagreement rather than just a gut feeling that you can't multiply your way out of an open empirical question.
