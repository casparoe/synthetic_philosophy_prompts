I am preparing a chapter for a volume on the aesthetics of interactive media, and I keep encountering an analogy that I have also used myself in lectures: a playthrough of a video game is like a performance of a musical work. The designer corresponds to the composer, the game's rules and mechanics to the score, the player to the performer, and the individual playthrough to the performance on a given evening. In the manuscript I am reviewing, this analogy is made to do three distinct jobs. First, it is used to argue that games are art in just the way music is art, so that the "are games art?" debate should be considered settled by transitivity. Second, it is used to argue that highly skilled or inventive play deserves the kind of aesthetic appreciation we extend to a great pianist's interpretation, and hence that esports and challenge runs are misclassified if we treat them as mere sport or spectacle. Third, it is used to argue that game criticism should model itself on music criticism: close readings of particular playthroughs as "interpretations" of the work, rather than reviews of the artifact as a consumer product. Please audit the analogy. Give me the correspondence table explicitly, including what plays the role of the score's notation, the performer's technical apparatus, the audience, and the interpretive choices a performer makes within the notes as written. Then identify where the mapping breaks down, and -- this is the part I care most about -- separate the disanalogies that actually matter for each of the three argumentative uses from the ones that are merely decorative. I am particularly unsure what to do with the fact that a score fully determines what counts as a correct rendering while a game's rules instead generate a possibility space the player partly authors, and with the fact that what is aesthetically interesting in play seems to be the player's decisions rather than anything audible or visible to a spectator. Finally: for each of the three conclusions, say whether it survives once the analogy's support is withdrawn (perhaps on independent grounds), or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole way; and if some better comparison -- jazz improvisation, theatrical performance, chess, cooking from a recipe -- does more honest work for any of the three, say which and why.
