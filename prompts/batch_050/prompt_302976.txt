So last month I got a decent bonus at work and decided to finally donate a chunk of it, and this somehow turned into a huge argument with my friend Dani, who is very into expected value reasoning. She thinks I should give the money to some org working on lowering the odds of human extinction, because even if the chance they make a diference is tiny, the stakes are so astronomical that the expected value crushes everything else. I keep going back and forth, and along the way I've noticed my gut is weirdly inconsistent about small-probability-huge-stakes stuff in general. My verdicts, for what they're worth:

- The classic mugging setup (stranger says hand over $50 or they'll do something with astronomically bad consequences, trust them, the math checks out): absolutely not, I'd feel like a mark.
- Home insurance where the premium costs a bit more than the actuarial value of the coverage: obviously buy it, I sleep better.
- Flu shot: tiny reduction in risk, near-zero cost, obviously do it.
- My aunt was offered an experimental treatment with maybe a 2% chance of working and it seemed completely right to try. 2% isn't nothing when the alternative is certain.
- Skipping a flight because planes sometimes crash: ridiculous, I'd never.
- Buying a lottery ticket for fun: fine, but nobody should pretend the math is on their side.
- Bednets (pretty confident they save actual lives) vs the extinction-risk org (Dani says maybe one in a million chance of mattering, but the payoff is literally the entire future): gut says bednets, then I feel guilty, because by my own aunt logic tiny chances of huge outcomes should count.

The pair I can't reconcile is the aunt case and the donation case. If 2% of one life is worth chasing, why isn't one-in-a-million of everything worth chasing? And the mugger and the extinction org look structurally identical to me, which bothers me even more.

Is there a principle that captures most of these? My best guess is something about whether the probability estimate is trustworthy (actuarial tables are real, Dani's one-in-a-million is a vibe), but that doesn't quite handle the mugger, since I'd refuse him even if he seemed weirdly credible. Maybe it's about whether taking the risk seriously would make normal life impossible (that's the plane one). Anyway: give me the best principle, or a couple of candidates, tell me which of my verdicts it has to throw out, and what it says about the thing I'm actually stuck on, which is cryonics. Dani brought that up too, it costs real money, and nobody can even state the odds.
