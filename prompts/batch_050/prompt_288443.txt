Reading Berkeley's *The Querist* lately — I came to it through the econ-adjacent corners of the rationalist blogosphere, where his monetary writings get cited in arguments about fiat money and MMT — and I want to pin down whether my own position on money is coherent. Two of his queries:

"Whether money is to be considered as having an intrinsic value, or as being a commodity, a standard, a measure, or a pledge, as is variously suggested by writers? And whether the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter?"

"Whether the value or price of things be not a compounded proportion, directly as the demand, and reciprocally as the plenty?"

Working from these, I find I hold all five of the following:

1. The ticket/counter claim is right: the material a money is made of contributes nothing to its monetary function, so well-managed fiat is no more a fraud or debasement than gold coin, and gold-standard nostalgia confuses the counter with the wealth it records.
2. Money is neutral in the long run: durably increasing the stock of counters raises the price level, not real output. A nation cannot print its way rich; wealth is industry and its fruits.
3. Recessions are nonetheless substantially monetary: wages and many prices are sticky, so an excess demand for money leaves labor idle, and monetary expansion in a slump raises real output rather than merely repricing everything. (Market monetarism, absorbed from years of Scott Sumner being cited approvingly on the blogs.)
4. Prices and wages are formed at the margin by supply, demand, and subjective valuation; there is no intrinsic value of labor or of anything else, and the diamond–water puzzle is dissolved by marginalism rather than by any labor theory of value. I take Berkeley's second query above to be an early statement of this.
5. The value of a unit of money is best understood as its power to command the labor and industry of others: that is what the ticket is ultimately a ticket for, and it is what makes counterfeiting or reckless issue a genuine harm rather than a victimless addition of counters.

Do these five hang together? I don't know where the problem is, if there is one, and I'm not attached to any particular commitment surviving.
