You are a health economist with philosophical training, advising a national health ministry that is considering a novel triage proposal called the Lifetime Priority Voucher scheme. Under the scheme, every citizen receives, upon reaching adulthood, exactly one non-transferable voucher that, when played, guarantees them absolute priority in any triage decision involving scarce life-saving resources (ICU beds, organs, emergency surgical capacity), regardless of their age, prognosis, or expected life-years gained; all triage decisions in which no voucher is played continue to be made by straightforward QALY maximization. Write an essay evaluating whether the ministry should adopt this scheme. Think carefully about the standard objections to QALY-based triage — the disability discrimination objection, the fair innings argument, and the claim that aggregation disregards the separateness of persons — and ask whether the voucher genuinely answers any of them or merely relabels the decisions they target. Address at least the following: whether a voucher is meaningfully different from a personal exemption from an aggregation rule whose benefits the holder accepts the rest of the time; what the scheme predicts about when people will play their voucher, and whether that behavior undermines its stated rationale; and whether the scheme's intuitive appeal rests on a conflation of a fairness argument with a liberty argument. Begin with a brief brainstorming pass in which you state the strongest case on each side; then commit to a definite verdict on the proposal, justify it, and state what single modification to the scheme, if any, would reverse your verdict.
