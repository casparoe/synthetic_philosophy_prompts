Me and my friend have been fighting about the Epicurean take on death for weeks now. I'm basically with Epicurus: death can't be bad FOR you, because while you're alive it hasn't happened, and once it happens there's no you left to be harmed. He's a deprivationist, he thinks death is bad because it steals all the good experiences you would've had. We keep going in circles so write the joint statement we'd end up with if we actually did one of those adversarial collaborations.

To keep it concrete, use the test case we made up: the memory pill. A pill that painlessly erases your last two years, forever. Everyone agrees taking it would be a terrible loss, but you'd never once experience missing anything. I say the pill is bad because of what your life is like after (confusion, having to relearn your friendships, etc.), not because of the erased chunk itself. He says the erased chunk is exactly the loss, so something can be bad for you without you ever feeling it.

Format it like this, in order:
1. "Stuff we both sign" — numbered agreed claims, ~150 words (e.g. death can definately be bad for other people even if not for you, fear of death might be irrational even if death is bad, that kind of thing)
2. "Disagreements table" — each disagreement worded so each of us would accept our own side's phrasing
3. "Cruxes" — ~100 words each. I suspect one is whether "bad for you" has to be experienced by you at some point, another is the symmetry thing (nobody mourns not being born in 1800, so why mourn not being alive in 2200). For each crux give a case we both agree bears on it and what each side predicts about it.
4. "Where we're just talking past each other" — ~80 words on which part of the fight is only about words.
