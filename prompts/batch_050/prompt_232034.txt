Question: Davidson famously denies that metaphors have a special metaphorical meaning. Explain his view, and contrast it with conceptual metaphor theory (Lakoff and Johnson). Which side do you find more convincing, and why?

Student's answer: Davidson argues that a metaphor means only what its words mean literally; there is no second, hidden metaphorical meaning the hearer has to decode. When Romeo says Juliet is the sun, the sentence just means that Juliet is the sun, which is plainly false, and its power lies in making us notice things about Juliet rather than in conveying some further proposition. In this his account is basically Gricean: the literal meaning is the input, and the hearer then computes an implicature to work out what the speaker really meant. Lakoff and Johnson, by contrast, hold that metaphor is not just a matter of language but of thought: we understand abstract target domains by mapping them onto concrete source domains, as in ARGUMENT IS WAR, so metaphorical mappings structure our everyday concepts. I find Lakoff and Johnson more convincing, because Davidson cannot explain why some metaphors are apt and others fall flat, whereas a mapping account can.
