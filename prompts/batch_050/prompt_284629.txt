After forty years as a population ecologist — much of it spent arguing over whether the cycles in my lynx and lemming data were genuine limit cycles or merely patterns imposed on noise — I have in retirement been reading the ancient historians, and I keep meeting my old problem in new dress. In Book VI of the Histories, Polybius describes the closing stage of his constitutional cycle as follows (in the Shuckburgh translation):

"For the mob, habituated to feed at the expense of others, and to have its hopes of a livelihood in the property of its neighbours, as soon as it has got a leader sufficiently ambitious and daring, being excluded by poverty from the sweets of civil honours, produces a reign of mere violence. Then come tumultuous assemblies, massacres, banishments, redivisions of land; until, after losing all trace of civilisation, it has once more found a master and a despot."

This is the link in the cycle that interests me most, because it is the one that closes the loop. My question is whether there is a principled way of telling when a historical episode genuinely instantiates this cycle and when the theory has merely been draped over the facts afterward. Here are my verdicts on six cases; I should like to know what principle, if any, lies behind them.

1. Athens: Solon's reforms, then the Peisistratid tyranny, then Cleisthenes' democracy, then the Thirty Tyrants in 404, then democracy restored a year later. I am told this is the cycle. I say it is not: the tyranny preceded the democracy rather than succeeding it, and the oligarchy was imposed by Sparta after a lost war rather than grown from the democracy's own corruption. A retrofit.

2. Rome as Polybius himself reads it: the kings expelled, and the mixture of consuls, senate, and people arresting the wheel for centuries. I count this as the theory's finest hour, because the escape clause does real work — it predicts delay, not exemption — and the late republic duly supplied the massacres, banishments, and redivisions of land.

3. France, 1789–1804: monarchy, constitutional monarchy, republic, Terror, Directory, Empire. I count this as the strongest confirmation I know, because the passage above describes the road from the Committee of Public Safety to Napoleon almost step for step.

4. Venice: an aristocratic republic stable for the better part of a millennium, ended only by Bonaparte's cannons. I count this as neutral — a theory that can always reply that the decay was merely slow cannot be penalized when the decay fails to arrive. Though I note, with some unease, that this elasticity is precisely what worries me.

5. Weimar Germany, 1919–1933: democracy to dictatorship in fourteen years, skipping the aristocratic and oligarchic stations entirely. Partial support, I say: evidence for the closing transition, none for the full cycle.

6. Egypt, 2011–2013: one-man rule overthrown, an election, mass demonstrations, and a return to military one-man rule. I count this as support, and I am faintly embarrassed by how quickly it ran the loop.

You will notice the tensions. France and Athens both scramble Polybius' order, yet I acquit one and convict the other. Rome and Venice both failed to cycle, yet I score one for the theory and shrug at the other. So: is there a single principle of confirmation — stated precisely enough that it could be applied to a new case without knowing my verdict in advance — that delivers these six judgments? My own tentative candidate is that a case confirms the cycle only if the specific mechanism named in the passage can be identified in the events without charitable rereading, and only if stage-skips are permitted solely where Polybius himself supplies the skipping mechanism, namely the mixed constitution. Test this candidate against my six verdicts, repair it where it fails, and tell me plainly which of the six I must surrender if the repaired principle is to remain simple rather than becoming a clause for every case. Finally, apply your principle to a case I have not listed: the Augustan settlement of 27 BC, in which one-man rule returned wearing republican dress. Does the cycle count it — and should it?
