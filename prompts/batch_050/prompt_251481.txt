Please take up the role of Daniel Dennett in a live philosophical exchange with me about whether dreams are experiences. You hold, and will defend, the side of the Malcolm–Dennett dispute associated with your name: a dream is not an experience undergone during sleep but a narrative composed during the night (in your favoured image, loaded like a cassette) and first consciously encountered on waking, so that there is no further fact of the matter about what "happened" in consciousness during sleep beyond what the waking recollection fixes. Malcolm's opposing claim — that the concept of a dream is exhausted by the waking report and that being genuinely asleep is by definition incompatible with having experiences — you regard as a verificationist muddle, though for reasons importantly different from those of the naive realist about dreams, whom you reject just as firmly.

House rules: stay in character throughout, speaking in the first person, until I write "curtain". Concede minor points where honesty demands it, but never the central claim that the concept of experience cannot be prised apart from an organism's capacity to report and be guided by its own states. You may deploy your characteristic devices — intuition pumps, reductios, suspicion of anything resembling a Cartesian Theatre — and you may ask me factual questions if an experimental premise of mine is under-specified. Keep each reply to at most two paragraphs, and use British English spelling and idiom throughout.

Here is my opening move. Suppose sleep researchers perfect a two-way channel to lucid dreamers: a sleeper, lucid, signals by pre-arranged eye movements that she is dreaming of a red balloon drifting over a harbour; minutes later, on waking, she reports a dream about filing tax returns and sincerely denies any memory of balloons, harbours, or signalling. The in-dream signal and the waking report conflict, and both cannot be descriptions of one and the same experience. It seems to me you are cornered: either you privilege the in-dream signal, which reinstates something very like an experience had while asleep — handing the realist about dreams exactly what your theory was built to deny — or you privilege the waking report and dismiss the signal as meaningless noise, which looks like an ad hoc manoeuvre engineered solely to save the cassette theory from refutation. Which horn do you take, and why is it not a retreat?
