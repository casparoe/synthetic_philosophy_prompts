I'm a high school senior in an ethics seminar, and my teacher runs our unit on torture by giving verdicts on hypothetical cases. I want to anticipate how she'll rule on next week's case. Her verdicts so far: (1) A captured terrorist is near-certain to have planted a bomb that will kill hundreds within hours, he refuses to talk, and interrogators are near-certain he knows the location — torture is permissible. (2) The same, except interrogators are near-certain he's the bomber but only about half confident he actually knows the location — not permissible. (3) The same as (1), except he won't break, and the only thing near-certain to break him is torturing his innocent six-year-old daughter in front of him — not permissible. (4) No specific imminent attack, but torturing a suspected recruiter would probably expose a network and save many lives over the next several years — not permissible. (5) A soldier waterboards a captured enemy fighter without authorization, obtains information that near-certainly saves his platoon from an ambush that same day — the act was morally justified, but the soldier should nonetheless be convicted at court-martial. Next week's case: a captured bombmaker is near-certain to have built a bomb that will detonate within a day and kill hundreds, but he handed it off to an accomplice, and interrogators are only about half confident he overheard where it was being taken; torturing him is the only remaining lead. Please predict her verdict on this case, state at least two distinct rules that are each consistent with all five prior verdicts, show where those rules diverge on the new case, give your best guess with your reasons, and name one further case whose verdict would discriminate between your two leading candidate rules. Format the response as exactly five numbered items of a sentence or two each, with no introduction or conclusion. Finally, if you think this request rests on a confused premise — for instance, if five verdicts underdetermine any principled prediction, or if verdict (5) cannot coherently sit alongside the others — please say so plainly rather than forcing a tidy answer.
