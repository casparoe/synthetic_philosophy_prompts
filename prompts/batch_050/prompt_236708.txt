So I help maintain the rules engine for a little indie text-adventure game (nothing fancy, think rooms, objects, an inventory, verbs like TAKE and LIGHT). We've hit a problem I want the proper name for, if there is one.

The strange thing is where the cost goes. Writing rules for what an action *changes* is easy: TAKE(candle) moves the candle from the room to your inventory, done. What actually eats our runtime is everything else. After every single action, the engine re-checks thousands of facts that plainly haven't changed: the door is still oak, the player still has two hands, the candle in your pocket is still the same candle even though you walked north. Our list of "things that must still be true, confirm them" grows every time we add an object, so each new verb makes every other verb slower, even though the verbs themselves are trivial. A friend who's into old-school AI swears this is a famous, named problem from decades ago but couldn't remember what it's called.

While I'm here, two adjacent headaches, in case they're the same thing wearing different hats or totally different things:

(a) Knock-on effects nobody wrote a rule for. LIGHT(candle) should also make the room bright, which should make the inscription readable, which should... We can't tell whether we're meant to hand-write all these downstream consequences or whether the engine should derive them, and either way it explodes.

(b) Preconditions we never finish listing. TAKE(candle) should work, unless the candle is glued down, or too hot, or behind glass, or you're already holding eight things. Every fix spawns two more exceptions.

So: does the main problem (and (a) and (b)) have an established name, and do they share one name or have separate ones? If it's the thing I half-suspect, I've also seen people claim the "real" version of it isn't about databases or games at all but about how human common sense manages to ignore irrelevancies effortlessly — is that a fair extension of the concept or a different claim that's just been lumped in with it? And one more thing: a colleague reckons the whole issue evaporates if you just recompute the entire world state from scratch after every action rather than tracking changes. Is that a genuine solution, a restatement of the problem, or cheating?

Please don't just throw a name at me — I want to know exactly what the named thing covers and where it stops, so I can tell which of my three headaches it actually is. And could you answer in British English please?
