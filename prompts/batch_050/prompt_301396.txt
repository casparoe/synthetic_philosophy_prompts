One just stays on the town council and votes present alot. I keep wanting to write scenes where the reader has to decide who counts as implicated, and I can't do that well without knowing what a theory of complicity would even have to settle.

So: before I read any actual moral philosophy on this, give me the spec. What would an adequate theory of complicity -- of participating in wrongdoing you didn't cause and maybe couldn't have stopped -- have to deliver? The cases it has to get right (the getaway driver who knew vs the one who didn't ask, the employee whose contribution is real but tiny and fully replaceable, the bystander vs the participant, collective stuff where no individual's act made a difference), the distinctions it's not allowed to collapse (contributing vs enabling vs benefiting vs just failing to prevent? intending the bad outcome vs foreseeing it vs being negligently oblivious?), and whatever facts about causation or responsibility from elsewhere in philosophy it has to stay consistent with.
