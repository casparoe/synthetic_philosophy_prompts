Is there a name for this? I keep noticing a kind of "because" that doesn't seem to be the ordinary causal because. The cake is a birthday cake because of what we intend to do with it. The remark counts as rude because of what it conventionally signals. The set whose only member is Socrates exists because Socrates exists, and not the other way around. In none of these does one event happen and then *cause* the other. It's more like one thing makes another the case without any pushing or producing going on, and it can hold between things that exist at the exact same time, or between things where causation would make no sense at all, like numbers.

Here's the part I can't find anyone talking about, though. Sometimes this making-the-case seems to run in a loop. The committee has authority because its members defer to it, but the members defer to it because it's the committee with the authority. Each link in that circle feels perfectly fine on its own, and honestly the loop doesn't feel broken to me either, yet the little philosophy I've read insists these chains of what-depends-on-what have to bottom out somewhere. So: does the non-causal because itself have an established name, and how is it different from causation on one side and from plain logical entailment on the other, since it seems to sit between them? And does the loop case fall under the same concept, or does it break it? If it breaks it, what exactly has to give -- the one-wayness, or something else? I'd rather have the carving than just a label.
