In my philosophy of mind seminar, whenever a student asks whether the Turing test bears on machine consciousness, the instructor cuts the discussion short with a stock line: "The Turing test is a behavioral test, so it tells us nothing about consciousness." This is clearly meant as a conversation-stopper, but the more I think about it, the more it seems to blur together several quite different claims. Please lay out, as a numbered list, the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing — from the near-trivial (the test's criterion is defined in terms of conversational behavior rather than inner states) to the radical (behavioral indistinguishability carries no evidential weight whatsoever regarding phenomenal consciousness) — and assess each one: is it trivial, substantive and defensible, or false? Finally, say which readings would actually support the instructor's dismissive use of the line, what further premises those readings would need, and whether any defensible reading gets him the conclusion he wants.
