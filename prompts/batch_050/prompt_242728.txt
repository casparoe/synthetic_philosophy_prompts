So this came up because my roommate keeps saying stuff like "I have to pick up my sister from the airport" and I know for a fact he doesn't have a sister (turns out it's his cousin, long story), and we ended up in a dumb hour-long argument about whether his sentence was false, or fine, or whether I "accommodated" the sister into existence by not objecting. So now I want to settle it properly for my reading group: write a "which theory of presupposition do you actually hold?" quiz.

Eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete utterance (e.g. "Have you stopped smoking?", "The king of France is bald" said today, "I regret telling her the truth", "Even Bob passed the exam", someone saying "we had pizza tonight too" when nobody in the conversation had mentioned pizza, and "If I have kids, my kids will be bilingual" said by someone with no kids). Each question should give two or three answer options describing what you'd say is going on -- not asking "do you believe in semantic presupposition?" directly.

Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least these positions: the semantic view where presuppositions are entailments and their failure wrecks truth value, Stalnaker-style pragmatic presupposition where the requirement is on the common ground, Lewis-style accommodation/scorekeeping where cooperative hearers just quietly update the record, and a deflationary view where presupposition "triggers" are just conventionalized implicatures that can be cancelled. Note explicitly in the key if there are pairs of positions the quiz can't really separate (I suspect Stalnaker and Lewis are hard to pull apart), and mention any answer combination that lands in an unoccupied spot in the logical space rather than pretending every pattern is a real position. Keep the quiz itself answerable by someone who has never heard these names.
