So I'm a community representative on our local hospital's research ethics committee (I'm a high school teacher, no medical background, I basically got voluntold through a parent council thing but I've ended up really into it). We review proposals for clinical trials and I've been keeping notes on how the committee has voted over my two years, and I want to figure out if we even have a coherent policy or if we just go on vibes.

Here's my notes on past decisions. We approved a trial randomizing cancer patients between two standard chemo regimens where the evidence in the literature is genuinely split down the middle. We approved a trial randomizing kids with asthma between two common inhalers, even though one was newer with less long-term data, as long as parents consented. We rejected a trial randomizing patients between surgery and a less invasive procedure, and the minutes say the deciding factor was that the lead surgeon running it clearly believed surgery was better and said so in interviews, even though expert opinion nationally was divided. We rejected a placebo-controlled antidepressant trial where an effective standard treatment exists and patients on placebo might deteriorate. But we approved a placebo-controlled trial for mild seasonal allergies where being on placebo just meant itchy eyes for a few weeks. And we approved, with extra monitoring conditions, a trial of a cheap generic vs the expensive brand-name drug even though some of our own doctors thought the generic was probably worse.

Now we've got a new one and I want to predict how we should vote if we're being consistent with ourselves. The proposal randomizes patients between two different timings of the same standard treatment (morning vs evening dosing). The extra risk to patients is basically nil either way -- everyone gets the real drug, same total dose. But the lead researcher's cover letter makes it pretty obvious she personally thinks evening dosing is better and is running the trial to prove it. Meanwhile the wider medical community seems genuinely split on the timing question.

Which way should we vote to stay consistent with our track record, which of the earlier cases is the closest analog, and does our track record actually settle this or does it underdetermine it? If it underdetermines, spell out what principle we'd effectively be adopting with a yes vote vs a no vote, and tell me what hypothetical case would have distinguished those principles from each other. Also curious whether our past decisions are even defensible as a set or whether some of them contradict each other and I should just say so at the next meeting.
