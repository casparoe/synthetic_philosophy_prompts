I'm a novelist, so forgive the slightly unhinged framing, but I need help keeping a character consistent. There's a mentor in my book who has been riffing all through it in a loose Hofstadter-ish way: self as pattern, strange loop, a person echoing in other heads, but not magic soul-stuff. I don't want scholarship about him, I want to predict what this mentor would say in the next scene.

His verdicts so far:

- Destructive scan into one perfect copy on Mars, memories and quirks intact: he says the person survives, "as much as anyone survives breakfast."
- Same scan but two copies are made: he says both continuers are real, but it is nonsense to ask which one is secretly the original; the single "I" does not ride along like a parcel.
- A devoted fan reads all of a dead author's letters and can imitate the style perfectly: he says some pattern persists, but only as an influence, not the author.
- A widow who can finish her husband's sentences, keeps his projects going, and now laughs like him when tired: he says "part of him really is still here," but only part.
- A grandson genetically identical-ish and raised to resemble grandpa, but no memories, habits, loves, or grudges transmitted: he says no persistence worth the name.
- A notebook containing every fact about a dead man, consulted by nobody: he says that's a map in a drawer, not a person.
- A beloved professor dies and three students independently carry pieces: one has his generosity, one his argumentative style, one his research obsession, none talks to the others: he says there are embers, not a fire.

New case for the next chapter: the protagonist dies. Beforehand she splits her life deliberately among five friends. One gets her private journals, one her sense of humor and grudges, one her unfinished novel-in-progress and obligations to it, one her habits of mercy and apology, one the role of asking "what would Ana do now?" and vetoing cheap answers. Alone, each is fragmentary. But once a week they meet, argue, sometimes cry, and after a year the group reliably reaches the decisions Ana would have reached, including a few that surprise each member individually and feel to them like her correcting them from inside the room. No single brain contains her. The system only works when all five show up and keep faithful to her.

What verdict should my mentor give if he's being consistent with himself: does Ana persist as a distributed self across the five friends, or is this still just five mourners doing an excellent seance? Please don't just vote yes/no. Lay out the rules that could fit his earlier calls, say which rule you think he's actually been running, what that rule predicts here, what the strongest rival rules predict instead, and which earlier verdict most constrains the answer. Then give me one extra thought experiment that would best discriminate between the top two interpretations. Also: tell me how confident you are in your prediction, and explicitly flag the single point you're least sure about. ty in advace.
