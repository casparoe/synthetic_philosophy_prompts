Explain the phenomenon of imaginative resistance using the following case. Readers readily accept, on the authority of a fantasy novel’s narrator, that speaking one word turns water into wine. Yet if the same narrator adds that an aesthetically elegant ritual makes killing an innocent child morally obligatory, many readers refuse to imagine that this is true in the story, despite accepting the narrator as infallible about the fictional world. Explain why this asymmetry is puzzling, assess whether the resistance is best understood as a failure of imagination, a refusal to believe the narrator, or an inability to assign stable fictional content to the claim, and explain how the diagnosis changes if the moral claim is made only by a fallible character within the story. Lead with your bottom line, and answer in no more than 450 words.
