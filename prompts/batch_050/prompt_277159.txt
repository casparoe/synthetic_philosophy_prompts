I'm a science and ideas journalist working on a column and I keep coming back too an old thought experiment: Hofstadter's careenium, where tons of tiny interacting marbles (simmballs) cluster into bigger units (simms) that shove each other around in ways that track symbol-like regularities. Thought experiment for the piece: suppose we actually built one, real marbles, every trajectory tracked, and the simms play out some pattern. Now consider two interventions: (a) we nudge a single simmball off course, and (b) we "nudge a simm", i.e. we displace all of its constituent simmballs at once in exactly the way the high-level description would dictate. Both change the outcome.

Rate each of the following claims a philosopher might make on a scale from 1 (totally defensible) to 5 (nonsense), with one or two sentences of justification each:

1. Only simmballs do any real pushing; the simm-level story is causally inert.
2. Simms cause things too, because interventions defined at the simm level are just as legitimate as simmball-level ones.
3. Simm-level causal talk is fine but strictly redundant: every such claim can be replaced by a simmball-level claim with zero loss.
4. Redundancy isn't the point; what matters is which level supports better counterfactuals and generalizations, and here that's the simm level.
5. The question dissolves once you see that "cause" just means "supports interventions", which both levels do.

Then tell me which claim you'd put in the headline and why. Max 350 words.
