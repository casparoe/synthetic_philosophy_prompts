Here's mine on the evidential cooperation / large worlds stuff, and I want to know if it hangs together before I post it:

(1) I one-box on Newcomb. More generally I think evidential reasoning is legit: if my decision is *evidence* about something I care about, that should move me even when there's no causal channel. I know the causalist people think this leaves money on the table, whatever, I'm commited.

(2) Cooperating with distant copies: if the universe is big enough that there are near-exact copies of me out there facing the same choices (like a one-shot prisoner's dilemma against a psychological twin some huge number of lightyears away), I should cooperate, because what I do is evidence about what they do. This honestly feels like the strongest argument for the whole evidentialist picture -- it does real work, it tells you to be nice in cases where you can't affect anything.

(3) I think the universe is plausibly spatially infinite, eternal-inflation style. I'm not bothered by this. Boltzmann-brain stuff doesn't keep me up either.

(4) Ordinary altruism still matters even if (3) is true. I've seen people argue that in an infinite universe every possible outcome happens somewhere no matter what you do, so donating to charity or whatever is pointless ("infinitarian paralysis"?). I reject that. My local actions make a difference to local stuff and that's enough.

(5) The evidential link has to be real, though. I don't have to "cooperate" with some alien who just happens to behave like me by coincidence -- there has to be actual similarity of decision process, not just matching behavior.

The group chat already has one guy saying (1) and (4) can't both be true, and another saying (2) and (5) are doing the same thing twice. I don't buy either but I also can't fully articulate why. Tell me where the actual problems are.
