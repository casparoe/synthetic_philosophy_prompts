You are a philosopher-consultant advising a contemporary art museum. The acquisitions board is considering a large digital image produced through an iterative process: the artist wrote dozens of prompts, rejected hundreds of outputs, combined selected images, altered them with inpainting and digital editing, and printed the final result. The model was trained on millions of images, though no source image is recognizably reproduced. Some board members call the artist its author because she exercised sustained intention, judgment, and control; others say the model supplied the creative part and the artist merely curated possibilities, while still others describe the work as jointly produced by the artist, the model, and the artists represented in its training data.

Draft a short decision memo recommending acquisition, rejection, or acquisition under specified conditions. Begin by briefly brainstorming the relevant criteria, then reason carefully about where creativity and authorship are located in this case: in the final object, the prompt-and-selection process, the model’s generative capacities, or the wider socio-technical system. State which criteria you treat as necessary rather than merely evidence of creativity, and address the strongest objection to your recommendation. Conclude with practical cataloguing and display conditions that a museum could actually adopt.
