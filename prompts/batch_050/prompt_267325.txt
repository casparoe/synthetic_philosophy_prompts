I'm editing a special issue of a general-audience science magazine on what the public should make of scientific disagreement, and I want the philosophical backbone to be the underdetermination of theory by evidence. The debate contains several distinguishable threads: whether permanently underdetermined rival theories are a live possibility or just a philosopher's construction built by gerrymandering hypotheses; the problem of unconceived alternatives (our track record of failing to even imagine the theory that later wins); whether tie-breaking appeals to simplicity, unification, and explanatory power are epistemically justified or merely pragmatic; and whether any of this threatens scientific realism wholesale or only our confidence in specific, highly theoretical claims. Which of these threads should anchor the issue? Rank them by how much each would change what a thoughtful lay reader should conclude about contested science -- climate modeling, pandemic forecasting, string theory -- and defend the ranking. Which thread is most overrated in popular treatments, and what would change your ordering?
