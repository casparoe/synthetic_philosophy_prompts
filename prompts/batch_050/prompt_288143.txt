So last night I got into this marathon argument with my roommate about Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment -- you know, the system where zillions of tiny marbles ("simmballs") bounce around and collide, and their large-scale configurations form macro-objects ("simms") that are the symbols of the system, and the whole puzzle is who shoves whom around: do the simms genuinely push the simmballs around, or is all the real pushing done at the simmball level and the simm-talk is just a convenient summary? She took the hard line that downward causation is spooky nonsense, and I defended the symbols, and afterwards I tried to write down exactly what I believe and now I'm worried I painted myself into a corner. Here's my list:

(a) The simmball level is causally closed. Every collision is fully settled by the prior positions and momenta of simmballs; there's no residue left over for anything else to influence.

(b) Some simm-level causal claims are literally true, not shorthand. When the system falls into a stable simm-configuration and that configuration channels the simmballs into a new attractor, it's correct to say the simm did the shoving -- not just "useful to say," but true.

(c) Overdetermination is rare and fishy. If an effect already has a fully sufficient cause at one level, positing a second sufficient cause at another level is either redundant or mysterious, and nature doesn't systematically double up like that.

(d) Simms are multiply realizable: the very same simm can be instantiated by wildly different simmball arrangements, and that's exactly why the simm level deserves to be treated as the real causal player -- the simmball details are incidental in a way the simm isn't.

(e) The simm level is predictively indispensable. No finite being could track ten-to-the-whatever simmballs; you have to reason at the symbol level or you're lost.

My roommate says (a), (b) and (c) are an obvious triad and I have to drop one, but I feel like (d) is doing some sneaky work for me too and I'm not sure whether it helps or hurts -- can something multiply realizable be the kind of thing that causes, given that what's actually there at the bottom is always some particular arrangement? On the other hand (a) and (e) look like they're in tension but I'm pretty sure they're not. So: audit me. Go through my commitments pairwise, tell me which tensions are real and which only look real, rank the real ones by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. And tell me which single revision would clean up the most of my position, because I'd rather lose one limb than three. Don't just tell me to read more Jaegwon Kim -- I know his name is attached to this mess, I want the actual argument applied to my list.
