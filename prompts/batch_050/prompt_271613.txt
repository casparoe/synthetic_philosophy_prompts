so im writing the background section of a paper that defends inference to the best explanation, and before I can defend it I need to know what I'd even be defending. everyone in this literature argues past each other because they're evaluating IBE against different unstated standards, so: write me the desiderata. what does an account of IBE, as a legitimate form of ampliative inference, actually have to deliver?

I want the full spec, with the case or pressure behind each requirement. Some things I suspect belong on the list, but tell me if any of them are actually prejudices of one camp:

- it has to say what makes one explanation better than another (loveliness), without just listing virtues like simplicity and scope and calling it a day
- it has to explain why loveliness should be any guide to likeliness -- why is the explanation we'd most want to be true more likely to be true? this is the part where I always feel the hand-waving starts
- it has to handle the "best of a bad lot" problem: the best available explanation might still be terrible, and the truth might not even be in our hypothesis space
- it has to either reconcile itself with Bayesianism (show IBE is just good Bayesian practice in disguise, or a heuristic for it) or defend being a rival to it -- it cant just ignore the elephant
- it has to distinguish itself from plain enumerative induction and from "inference to the only explanation," and say which of those collapses are acceptable
- it has to deal with the contrastive thing -- explaining why p rather than q -- since which explanation is "best" seems to shift with the contrast
- it has to not overgenerate: people "infer the best explanation" all the time in conspiracy thinking and medical quackery, so the account needs to say what's going wrong there without ruling out the science we like

For each desideratum: is it non-negotiable for any account, or is it a preference of the explanationist (or the Bayesian) camp? And the part I most want: are any of these jointly unsatisfiable? I have a nagging feeling that "our explanatory judgments objectively track truth" and "our hypothesis spaces may never contain the truth" can't both be honored, but maybe that dissolves on closer look.
