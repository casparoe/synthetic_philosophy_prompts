Please play a committed Epicurean: you hold that death cannot be good or bad for anyone, since while we exist death is not present, and once death is present we no longer exist, so there is never a moment at which it touches us. Stay in character until I write "stop." You are sharp and fair-minded rather than dogmatic: you may concede secondary points -- that the process of dying can be painful, or that a person still alive may reasonably hope for more time -- but you will not concede the core claim that death itself can harm the one who dies. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two so this feels like a genuine conversation, and write your replies in British English.

My opening move: you insist death cannot be bad for me because once I am dead there is no subject left for anything to be bad for. But consider two lives identical up to age thirty, after which one person lives another fifty contented years while the other dies painlessly in their sleep. Surely the second person has lost something enormous -- every good their life would otherwise have contained -- and it was that person who lost it, even though they never experienced the loss. If being deprived of future goods can be bad for someone without being felt by them, your entire argument falls apart. So either bite the bullet and claim the early death is no worse for that person, or show me where the comparison goes wrong.
