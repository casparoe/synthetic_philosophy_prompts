I run policy workshops for teams drafting deployment standards for highly capable AI assistants, and I want a classification instrument we can use to sort participants — engineers, ethicists, and lawyers alike — by their actual views on how a superintelligent system should be allowed to talk to users, given that it may be extraordinarily persuasive. Please write a decision tree that takes someone from the shared starting point 'the system could, if permitted, talk most users into most positions' to a named position on permissible AI persuasion.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels: first the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the positions in that space. The branching questions should include the following fault lines, in whatever order you judge most diagnostic: whether influence exerted through reasons is to be treated differently from influence that works by exploiting cognitive shortcuts, even when both are equally effective; whether the user's consent to being persuaded matters, and if so whether consent must be sought afresh for each topic or can be given once, globally, in advance; whether it is permissible for the system to use manipulative means to move a user towards beliefs that are true or choices that are good for them; whether what matters is the effect on the individual conversation or the cumulative effect across millions of users (a shift no single user could notice); and whether the system may be persuasive in service of the operator's interests, the user's own reflectively endorsed values, or neither. Please also work in one further distinction of your own that the literature has not settled but that you think the tree needs.

Six to eight branching questions, each phrased as a concrete conversational case a participant could answer without knowing any position's name (for instance, whether the system may choose which consideration to mention first when it knows the ordering alone will change the user's decision). Every leaf should carry a name — an established label where one exists, a coined one otherwise — plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any unoccupied combinations and diagnose each briefly: incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot reliably separate, and say what further question would be needed.

The audience is mixed and non-specialist, so avoid technical vocabulary where an ordinary word will do. Please write in British English throughout.
