I’m a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep getting confused by the Church-Turing thesis. People seem to treat it as a definition, an empirical claim about physics, a conceptual truth about algorithms, or a theorem waiting for a proof, and those feel like very different things.

Before I pick a side, write the desiderata an adequate account of effective computability should meet. Treat it a bit like writing a conformance spec for computation: which cases the account has to classify correctly, which distinctions it must keep seperate, and which kinds of evidence would count for or against it.

Your list should cover at least:

- ordinary algorithms with unbounded but finite-at-any-moment time and memory
- nondeterminism, parallelism, randomness, and interactive computation
- oracles and alleged hypercomputers
- continuous or analog physical systems
- quantum computation
- the difference between computability in principle and tractability under resource limits
- the difference between a procedure being finite and human-followable, physically implementable, mathematically definable, and mechanically verifiable from finite traces

For each desideratum, explain the motivating case and whether it is non-negotiable or just the preference of one camp. In particular, say which requirements force the thesis to be about idealized algorithms and which ones turn it into a claim about the physical universe. I also want to know whether a model of computation must have a finite description, whether each step must be local and finite, whether effectiveness has to be uniform across inputs, and whether a successful result needs to be checkable without trusting an infinitely precise device.

If some plausible subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, make that explicit instead of smoothing it over. Then briefly score the main interpretations of Church-Turing as definition, conceptual analysis, empirical hypothesis, and methodological boundary against the list. Keep the scoring short; the criteria and their edge cases are the main thing. No bibliography or citations, please.
