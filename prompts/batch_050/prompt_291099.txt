So I'm a philosophy PhD student (epistemology) and I'm about a year into writing a dissertation chapter on the regress problem. My committee keeps telling me to "state my criteria for a satisfactory solution" before I defend any position, and I keep dodging it because honestly I don't have a good list. So: write me the desiderata a theory of the structure of epistemic justification has to meet to count as actually solving the regress problem, not just renaming it. I mean the full spec -- what foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism are each trying to deliver, formulated neutrally enough that it isn't rigged in favor of any of them from the start.

For each requirement give me the case or consideration behind it -- e.g. why a theory has to explain how justification can *transmit* along a chain and not just sit at each link, why it has to handle cases where my evidence base is finite but my belief-forming dispositions aren't, why it can't just stipulate that some beliefs are "basic" without saying what makes them justification-conferring rather than merely unjustified-but-excused, why it has to say something about whether the structure is supposed to be psychologically real in actual believers or just an abstract rational reconstruction (this is the one that trips people up in my lit review). Also whether it must avoid circularity outright or just vicious circularity, and what the difference even is.

Then the part I actually care about: tell me which requirements are genuine constraints on ANY solution and which are just the preferred axioms of one camp dressed up as neutral ground (I'm pretty sure "no infinite chains" is the latter, but argue me out of it if you think I'm wrong). And if some subset of the genuine requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly and say which one you'd cut -- that would basically BE my thesis result if it's true.

One thing to keep in mind as the purpose constraint: I need this theory to be usable for thinking about justification in cases where the "believer" isn't a normal adult human -- think very young kids, or cases of long chains of testimony where no single person holds more than a few links. If a desideratum only makes sense for ideally reflective agents, flag that.

Don't hedge. Commit: for each item say whether it's non-negotiable, camp-relative, or fake, and give the reason. If you think one of the three standard positions is just dead on arrival against your own list, say which and why. I'd rather argue with a wrong opinionated answer than get a careful menu of options.
