Two people in my set theory reading group have been arguing about Skolem's paradox for a whole semester and our instructor finally told them to do an adversarial collaboration instead of derailing every session. Camp A says the paradox is basically an artifact: first-order ZFC has countable models (assuming it's consistent) because first-order logic can't pin down the power set, and inside such a model the sentence "the reals are uncountable" is true because the model simply doesn't contain a bijection from omega to its reals -- but that bijection exists outside the model, and there's an absolute, model-independent fact of the matter that the real numbers really are uncountable. Camp B says that's exactly the kind of complacency the paradox should cure: all we ever have is proofs from some axioms in some theory, "the actual universe of sets" is doing no explanatory work, and uncountability is only ever relative to a model, so Camp A's "external bijection" just relocates the question to a bigger model that has the same problem.
