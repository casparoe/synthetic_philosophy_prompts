I'm presenting my epistemology profile in a grad seminar next week and want a consistency audit before people start poking at it. Here's where I've landed:

1. Dogmatism about perception: my perceptual experiences justify my beliefs about the external world immediately. I don't need any antecedent evidence that my senses are reliable before those experiences can do justificatory work.
2. Closure: if I know p and competently deduce q from p, I know q, or at least I'm in a position to.
3. Bootstrapping is bogus. Take the gas gauge case: someone with no prior reason to trust the gauge reads it, believes the tank is full, notes the gauge said F and the tank was indeed full, iterates, and concludes the gauge is reliable. I say no knowledge of reliablity gets generated that way.
4. But I'm a Moorean about skepticism: seeing my hands gives me knowledge that I have hands, and from there I can deduce that I'm not a handless brain in a vat -- and that deduction gives me genuine knowledge that I'm not, not just a dialectical rebuttal I can wave at the skeptic.
5. Rule-circularity is fine: the only track record we could ever have for induction is itself inductive, and I think that still amounts to a real justification for relying on induction.
6. I'm an internalist: justification depends only on what's accessible from the subject's own perspective.

Do these hang together? Go through them and tell me which pairs or triples are in genuine tension and how severe each is, which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single change cleans up the most. Please rank rather than flagging everything as equally problematic.
