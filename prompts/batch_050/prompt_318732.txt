I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy working on animal ethics and food systems, and I'm running a reading group where two of my members have been deadlocked all term. One is a fairly orthodox welfarist of the effective altruism flavour: she thinks the single worst thing an ordinary consumer does is eat chicken and eggs, because chickens are so small that a given number of calories requires vastly more individual lives and far more suffering-days than beef, so anyone who swaps beef for chicken is making things dramatically worse (the small-animal replacement problem). The other defends a version of the "least harm" argument: he thinks plant agriculture kills staggering numbers of wild field animals (rodents, birds, insects) through ploughing, harvesting and pesticides, and that a diet of pasture-raised beef might kill fewer sentient individuals per calorie than a diet of grains and pulses.

They've finally agreed to stop arguing and do a proper adversarial collaboration. Draft the joint statement they should produce. I want:

1. A numbered list of claims both can sign — e.g. about per-calorie vs per-animal accounting, about how little we actually know about field-animal mortality, about whether pasture land supports more or fewer wild animals than cropland — but only claims they genuinely both accept, not watered-down mush.
2. The precise points of disagreement, each phrased so that the side it describes would accept the wording as fair. I suspect at least these are in there: whether the replaceability/"logic of the larder" move is legitimate (i.e. whether a farmed animal that wouldn't otherwise exist counts as a benefit), whether deaths of wild animals in crop production are morally on a par with deliberate slaughter or more like side-effects we tolerate everywhere else, and whether wild animals' lives in these landscapes are net positive at all (if a field mouse's life is mostly hardship, does killing it harm it in the same way?).
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where settling them would actually move one of them — and for each crux a concrete piece of evidence or a case both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction about it written down in advance. For instance, I think one crux is empirical (roughly how many sentient animals die per hectare under different farming regimes, and whether anyone's estimates are trustworthy) and one is conceptual (whether foreseen-but-unintended deaths should be weighted differently at all).

If you think any part of their dispute is really about words rather than substance, flag it and say why. Please keep it in British English, and definately keep it neutral — I don't want either of them accusing me of rigging it.
