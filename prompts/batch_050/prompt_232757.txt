I'm on the seven-person board of a housing co-op (48 units, I've been on the board two years) and we're stuck in a situation that feels philosophically weird, not just politically messy. Background: we're deciding whether to convert the south lot into either more parking or a shared garden with some accessible raised beds, it's been contentious for months. Two weeks ago, board member A sent an all-residents email saying that if the garden option passes she will resign from the board and withdraw her family from the co-op's volunteer maintenance rota, which honestly would hurt us since she runs the finances. Not to be outdone, board member B then announced at the open meeting that if the parking option passes HE will resign and also that he'll campaign to recall anyone who votes for it. Now both of them say their hands are tied -- A literally said "I can't walk it back now or no one will ever take me seriously again," and B said almost the same thing word for word. So we've gone from a decision about a lot to a decision about whose threat blinks first, and I notice that whoever had committed SECOND got to pick the stronger threat, which seems to reward escalation. Three other board members now want to just postpone the vote indefinitely because any outcome "destroys" someone, which strikes me as letting the threats win.

At our next meeting I'm supposed to present a written opinion on a proposed standing rule, drafted by our secretary, that reads roughly: statements made outside a duly called meeting in which a member conditions their future service, cooperation, or compliance on the outcome of a vote shall be treated as procedurally inadmissable -- the board deliberates as though the statement had not been made, and the vote proceeds on the merits of the question alone. There's also a live question of whether, if we adopt the rule, it applies retroactively to A's and B's existing ultimatums or only going forward. Please draft the opinion I'd circulate: a clear recommendation (adopt or not, retroactive or not, and what we do about the garden/parking vote specifically), the reasoning behind it, and an honest engagement with the strongest objection, which I think is A's point that being able to put something real on the line is how ordinary members signal that an issue matters deeply to them, and that a board which refuses to hear such statements is refusing information about how much people care. I keep going back and forth on this -- on one hand her email does tell me something true about her, on the other hand if we let that count, aren't we just awarding the decision to whoever is willing to escalate fastest and most publicly, and won't every future dispute become two people racing to lock themselves in first?
