I'm a PhD student in ethics working on the moral bioenhancement debate associated with Persson and Savulescu. Their claim, roughly, is that liberal democracies face existential threats rooted in human moral shortcomings -- climate inaction, catastrophic weapons, failures of large-scale cooperation -- and that we may therefore need to enhance moral motivation by biomedical means, perhaps even without universal consent. Their critics respond that such interventions would violate freedom, that "moral enhancement" smuggles in contested normative content under a technical label, and that the proposal rests on a crude picture of moral psychology in which virtue is a dial to be turned. Well over a decade in, the exchanges seem to me to have settled into a fixed repertoire: catastrophe arguments on one side, freedom objections on the other, with nobody moving.

My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not really about biomedical intervention at all. I suspect the two sides operate with different conceptions of what moral improvement consists in: the proponents treat it as a change in stable motivational dispositions (less aggression, more empathy) that drugs or genetic interventions could in principle produce, while the critics take moral improvement to be essentially a matter of reasons-responsiveness -- better judgment rather than better impulses -- which no intervention can bring about because it must arise through the agent's own deliberation. If that is right, the catastrophe argument and the freedom objection mostly pass each other, and the debate's apparent stakes are misplaced.

Is this diagnosis correct? Engage with what the two sides actually argue and locate where the disagreement really lives: in competing accounts of moral psychology, in the value of freedom, or in empirical conjectures about what biomedical means could plausibly achieve. Given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress -- a conceptual distinction that splits "moral enhancement" into separate notions, an empirical result about the plasticity of moral dispositions, or a concession one side is structurally unable to make. I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the literature.
