I teach an upper-level undergraduate course in metaphysics, and next term I am devoting two weeks to origin essentialism -- roughly, the Kripke-inspired thesis that a person could not have existed without originating from the very sperm and egg they in fact originated from. I want students to stress-test the thesis against reproductive scenarios it was not designed for, and I would like your help calibrating the difficulty of the cases before I build the unit.

Consider the following six candidate cases, each of which puts pressure on the thesis in a different way:

1. Same sperm, different egg: could you have existed had your father conceived a child with a different woman?
2. Same egg, different sperm: could you have existed had a different sperm from the same ejaculate fertilized your mother's egg?
3. Mitochondrial donation: a child conceived via spindle transfer, so that the nuclear DNA comes from the intended parents but the mitochondrial DNA comes from a third-party donor. If the intended child is numerically identical to a child conceived without the donation, the gamete-origins criterion seems too coarse; if not, it seems too fine.
4. In vitro gametogenesis: an egg grown in a laboratory from one parent's skin cells, fertilized by the other parent's sperm. The resulting child has no egg-origin in the ordinary sense at all.
5. Cryopreservation delay: the very sperm and egg that in fact produced you are instead frozen and united forty years later. Origins are held fixed; timing, upbringing, and everything downstream change.
6. Monozygotic twinning: a single zygote splits shortly after fertilization, producing twins. If origins are essential and the twins share one origin, either they were once one individual (which raises puzzles about fission and identity) or origin essentialism must say something subtler about when the essential origin is fixed.

Please do two things. First, rank the six cases from most damaging to least damaging for the strong version of origin essentialism, giving a paragraph of reasoning for each placement. In your reasoning, attend to whether the case attacks the necessity of origins, the sufficiency of origins, or instead the vagueness of what counts as the origin in the first place, since these are different failures and I want students to keep them apart. Second, tell me which single case you would choose as the centerpiece counterexample for the unit and why, bearing in mind that my students will have read the standard material on necessity of origins but will not have encountered any of these scenarios before, and that I want maximum dialectical leverage against the thesis rather than maximum biological novelty.
