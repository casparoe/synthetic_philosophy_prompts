So there's this handout that circulates in my Hume reading group -- nobody remembers who wrote it -- and it summarizes Hume on causation like this:

"For Hume, causation is nothing but regular succession. When we say A caused B, all we mean is that A-type events have always been followed by B-type events. Hume proved that there is no necessary connection between cause and effect -- what we call necessity is just a feeling of expectation in our own minds, projected onto a world that contains no real causal powers whatsoever. That's why the 'New Hume' interpreters (Galen Strawson and that crowd) who claim Hume actually believed in objective necessary connections we merely can't know about are just wishful revisionists: read plainly, Hume is a regularity theorist and a skeptic about causal power, full stop."

This has been bugging me because it sounds confident and mostly matches what I remember from the Enquiry, but the last bit feels like it's rigging the game somehow. Can you audit it? Specifically:

1. Which parts are a fair statement of Hume's view, and which parts smuggle in a contested interpretive verdict while presenting it as what Hume just says? I have a feeling "proved that there is no necessary connection" does more work than Hume's actual argument does, but I'm not sure what the weaker correct version would be.
2. Does the handout even get the regularity theory itself right? I vaguely recall Hume offering two definitions of "cause" that don't obviously say the same thing, and this summary seems to only have one of them.
3. What would the New Hume people actually object to here, and what would the traditional regularity-reading people say back? Don't just referee in favor of whichever side is currently fashionable -- I want to know whether the handout's "read plainly" move is legitimate or whether it quietly assumes the very thing the two sides disagree about.
4. Where does this leave the ordinary reader: if I accept the corrected version, what should I actually believe Hume thought about whether the world contains causal powers?

Please answer in American English.
