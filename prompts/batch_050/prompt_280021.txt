So I'm in this reading group on formal epistemology and we've spent three sessions on the Sleeping Beauty problem and I'm genuinely stuck on why nobody budges. Quick recap in case it helps: Beauty is put to sleep, a fair coin is flipped, if heads she's woken once (Monday), if tails she's woken twice (Monday and Tuesday) with her memory of the first waking erased, and whenever she's awake she can't tell which waking it is. The thirder says her credence in heads on waking should be 1/3, the halfer says it stays 1/2 because she's learnt nothing new -- she knew she'd be woken either way. Both arguments have been on the table since the early 2000s, everyone in my group understands both perfectly well, and literally nobody has changed their mind. That seems philosophically interesting in itself and I'd like a diagnosis rather than another pass at adjudicating it.

My own hypothesis, which you should feel free to demolish, is that the two sides are tacitly using credence for different jobs: thirders are thinking about what betting odds are fair given how many times you'll be offered the bet, halfers are thinking about what your evidence supports, and the apparent disagreement evaporates once you say which job you mean. Relatedly, maybe the whole thing turns on whether an awakening you fully expected counts as evidence at all. Oh and my flatmate proposed a variant that I think might discriminate between diagnoses: same setup, but on tails the Tuesday waking happens with a slight headache induced by the drug and the Monday one doesn't, so the two tails-wakings are phenomenally distinguishable -- though on any given waking Beauty can't tell whether the absence of a headache means it's Monday-tails or heads, since she'd have no headache in the heads case either. Does that kind of internal difference between the wakings change anything, and does what it changes depend on your diagnosis?
