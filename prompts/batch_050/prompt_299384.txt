"Basically there are two standards people propose for calling an AI system safe. A worst-case guarantee means you've mathematically proven the model can never do anything catastrophically harmful, no matter what input it gets -- it's like formally verifying a compiler. An average-case guarantee means the system is safe on typical inputs, i.e. on average over the deployment distribution, so rare catastrophic failures get averaged away and the system can still count as 'safe' even if it occasionally does something terrible. Since averaging lets disasters hide in the mean, worst-case guarantees are the only honest safety standard, and anyone who settles for average-case guarantees is implicitly okay with the occasional catastrophe. But since worst-case guarantees are provably impossible for learned neural networks, alignment research aiming at them is a waste of time, and the practical alternative is extensive red-teaming before deployment, which is how you actually get average-case guarantees in practice."
