I am taking a philosophy of physics seminar this term, and for my final essay I want to assess whether Everettian quantum mechanics has an adequate account of probability. Rather than surveying the existing proposals, I want to first pin down the standard they should be judged against. So: write the list of requirements that any derivation or justification of the Born rule within a no-collapse, purely unitary interpretation must satisfy in order to count as having solved the probability problem, with the consideration motivating each requirement.

Please make sure the list covers at least the following fault lines, and feel free to add ones I have missed. First, the theory must address two distinct problems and say which it is solving: the incoherence problem (what probability could even mean when every outcome with nonzero amplitude occurs on some branch) and the quantitative problem (why the squared amplitude, specifically, rather than branch counting or some other power of the amplitude). Second, any derivation from rationality principles must state its axioms explicitly, and the axioms must be independently motivated rather than reverse-engineered to yield the answer; ideally the derivation should pinpoint exactly which assumption rules out rival measures. Third, the account must not presuppose that branches come in a well-defined integer number, since decoherence gives only an approximate, fuzzy branching structure. Fourth, it must explain why it is rational to weight one's future successors by the proposed measure, including the treatment of branches with tiny but nonzero measure. Fifth, it must not secretly modify the dynamics or smuggle in additional structure, since then it is no longer Everett but a different theory.

Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp (for instance, whether the account must deliver genuine single-case chances or merely justify rational credences). Most importantly: if any subset of the requirements on the list is jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly and identify which requirement has to give. Skip scoring the existing proposals against the list; I will do that myself.
