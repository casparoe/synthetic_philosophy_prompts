I'm a philosophy PhD student and I somehow ended up as head moderator of a fairly big online philosophy forum (long story). The paradox of tolerance gets thrown at me constantly, from both directions: ban the intolerant or the forum stops being a tolerant place, and banning people for their views is itself the intolerance the paradox warns about. I'm tired of relitigating it from scratch everytime. Turn the paradox into an actual moderation procedure I can run on a reported post or thread: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a satisfying answer to each, where the procedure just outputs remove or keep, and where it has to punt to a human because the call is about values rather than facts. Please be careful about which distinctions survive the translation into a checklist -- intolerance of ideas vs of people, refusing to platform someone vs punishing them for speech, whether the person being complained about is themselves abiding by the norms they demand from others -- and be honest about which of these are too fuzzy to operationalize. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) a long, polite, heavily footnoted post arguing that some group shouldn't be allowed to immigrate; (2) a sarcastic pile-on mocking a new member's genuinely bad argument; (3) a user who meticulously follows every rule on-site while encouraging people elsewhere to harass one of our members. At least one of these should come out in a way I'll find uncomfortable, and tell me which step of the procedure produced it.
