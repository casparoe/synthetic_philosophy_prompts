The half-baked idea: satisficing is usually sold as the realistic alternative to maximizing -- you set a threshold, you take the first option that clears it, you stop searching. But Simon-style aspiration levels aren't fixed, they adapt while you search. Find three good apartments in a row and your threshold creeps up; a dry spell and it sinks. My suspicion is that this creates a problem nobody quite states cleanly: if your threshold is always adjusting to the options you've seen so far, then "stop when something clears the bar" starts to look suspiciously like "keep going until the search stops paying off", which is just maximizing with search costs wearing a trench coat. So the proposal question would be something like: is there a version of satisficing with adaptive aspiration that stays genuinely distinct from cost-sensitive maximizing, and if not, does satisficing survive as anything more than a heuristic label?

Write the proposal: state the question precisely so it could actually have an answer, explain why the usual framing (satisficing as maximizing plus deliberation costs) leaves my version of the problem open rather than dissolving it, lay out what I'd actually do over the semester (which formalisms or thought experiments I'd use, in roughly what order), what the possible conclusions are and what would decide between them, and what would count as the project failing. Also include an honest bit about what could make this too big for one semester and what I'd cut first.
