1. battle deaths in state-based conflicts per 100,000 people per year
2. homicide rate (per capita)
3. one-sided violence -- massacres, pogroms, genocides -- per capita
4. absolute number of violent deaths worldwide, ignoring population growth entirely
5. "structural violence": preventable deaths from poverty, treatable disease etc., counted as violence because someone could in principle have stopped them
6. cruelty-without-death stuff: torture prevalence, corporal and capital punishment, slavery-like practices
7. tail risk: the estimated probability in a given year of a war that kills more than, say, 1% of the world's population, even if no such war is currently happening
8. deaths in colonial conquest and frontier expansion, which often get left out of the standard war datasets because nobody was keeping tidy records on the losing side

What I want from you: assign each component a weight between 0 and 1 (they should sum to 1, and yes, 0 is allowed -- if you think a component is junk, say so and bin it), with a sentence or two of justification for each. My suspicion is that the per-capita vs absolute choice swamps everything else and the rest is fiddling at the margins -- am I right about that, or does something else (the frontier data problem? the tail-risk component?) matter just as much? And lastly, in one paragraph: given that the answer visibly depends on the weights, is "is the world becoming less violent?" the kind of question that has a fact of the matter at all, or is it really several questions wearing a trenchcoat?
