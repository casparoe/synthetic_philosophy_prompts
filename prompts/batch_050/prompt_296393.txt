"Zorn's lemma says that if you have a partially ordered set in which every nonempty chain has an upper bound, then there is a maximal element — the largest element of the set, the one nothing exceeds. Intuitively it is just transfinite induction repackaged: instead of climbing through the ordinals one step at a time, you check the chain condition once and get the conclusion for free. Zorn's lemma is a consequence of the axiom of choice, but it is strictly weaker than choice itself — the full strength of the axiom is needed for the well-ordering theorem, whereas Zorn's lemma alone does not suffice to prove it. That is why working mathematicians prefer to cite Zorn's lemma rather than the axiom of choice: it lets them get their maximal ideals and vector space bases while assuming less. One caveat: the maximal element the lemma hands you need not be unique, and the proof gives you no procedure for finding it."
