I’m a high school student and our debate club has asked everyone to write a one-page statement of their views on blame and hypocrisy, since our next topic is whether public figures lose the right to call out misconduct they’ve engaged in themselves. Before I present mine, I want to know whether my positions actually hang together. Here’s roughly where I stand: (1) whether blame is appropriate depends on what the person being blamed did — the wrongness of an act doesn’t change depending on who points it out; (2) all the same, when a hypocrite calls me out for something they do themselves, “you’re one to talk” is a legitimate response, and their criticism genuinely loses some of its force; (3) pretty much everyone is hypocritical at some point, and that fact on its own doesn’t disqualify anyone from making moral criticisms — if it did, moral discussion would just collapse; (4) blame from complete strangers with no connection to the situation is out of place even when everything they say is accurate, because it isn’t their business. Are these four jointly consistent? I don’t want a lecture on hypocrisy in general — I want an audit of this specific set.

Please structure the response in four sections, in this order: (a) “The verdict in brief,” two or three sentences saying whether the set survives as-is; (b) “Genuine tensions,” roughly a paragraph each, ordered from most to least severe, each stating exactly which commitments conflict and what would have to give; (c) “Apparent tensions,” a short paragraph on any pair that looks inconsistent but dissolves on closer inspection, with the distinction that dissolves it; (d) “The cheapest revision,” one paragraph recommending the single change that restores the most consistency at the lowest cost to what I clearly care about.
