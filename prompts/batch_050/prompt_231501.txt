i'm a phd student in moral psychology and i'm meant to be writing a chapter on akrasia, so naturally i am procrastinating by commissioning poetry. i want a villanelle about hyperbolic discounting. the constraint: the two refrains should be the same resolution (the classic "i'll start tomorrow" kind) but voiced from two different points in time -- once from far off, where the big delayed reward clearly wins, and once up close, where the small immediate one suddenly outweighs it. the form should do the philosophical work here: villanelle refrains keep coming back, and i want each return to land differently because the tempting option has gotten temporally closer, so the preference flips even though the person has learned nothing new. that's the part i most need the poem to get right -- the reversal is driven purely by proximity in time, not by new information, which is exactly what makes it a puzzle for the picture of agents as rationally weighing outcomes. in the closing quatrain the two refrains should finally sit side by side and it should be obvious that the "future self" whose interests kept getting discounted was the same person all along. a good poem first, but accurate enough that my commitee wouldn't roll their eyes.
