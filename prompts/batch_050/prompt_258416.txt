I somehow ended up chairing our town's commemoration review committee (volunteer thing, five of us, none of us historians) and it's quietly eating my life. We handle requests to pull down statues, rename streets and schools, remove plaques, that kind of thing — maybe a dozen a year, some trivial, some that pack the town hall with furious people on both sides. Council technically signs off on our recommendations but in practice they rubber-stamp whatever we send them, so it's really on us. Right now each case gets decided by whoever argues loudest at the meeting, and I want to replace that with an actual procedure we run the same way every time. We have one evening meeting per case, basically no budget, and the option to hire a local historian for a few hours if we can justify it.

I've been chewing on this on my own and have some rough distinctions I want baked in, though tell me if any of them are bad. One: there's a difference between *honoring* someone and just recording that they existed — a heroic statue in the square is a different kind of speech than a name on a map that nobody thinks about. Two: it matters whether the awful thing is what the person is commemorated *for*, or a side thing. Three: it matters whether people can avoid the commemoration or it's forced on them daily — a plaque in a museum versus the street you live on or the school your kid goes to. Four: the dead can't be harmed, so the real stakes are for living people, but I'm genuinely torn on how to weigh residents who feel insulted against the "you're erasing history" crowd.

So here's the ask. Write me a procedure we could actually run in a single evening per case: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the process can just end early with "keep it," where it has to punt to a public consultation or the hired historian instead of letting us wing it, and mark explicitly the steps where judgment can't be eliminated and the committee just has to vote. Then run it on three cases off our actual docket: (a) renaming the street named after the mill owner who basically founded the town and endowed the hospital, but who in 1912 brought in private security that shot striking workers; (b) the equestrian statue in the main square of a national founding figure, honored for the independence stuff, but who personally enslaved people and signed off on some brutal frontier policies; (c) a primary school named after a beloved 1960s local teacher who, it turns out, wrote a crankish newspaper column full of ugly stuff about immigrants. Flag anywhere the procedure spits out an answer you think I won't like — my gut says rename the street, and I suspect a decent procedure might not let me, so I want that friction made explicit rather than hidden. And say what the procedure can't handle at all.

Last thing, and I really want this part: after the procedure and the worked cases, tell me which step you're least confident in and what argument or consideration would make you restructure it. Like if someone convinced you that what the original namers intended doesn't matter at all, does the whole thing collapse or just one step? I'd rather know where it's fragile before I present it than find out in front of a packed town hall.
