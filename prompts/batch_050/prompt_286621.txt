So I've been a LessWrong/Overcoming Bias reader since forever and now I somehow ended up on the board of our regional community org (think: yearly fundraiser, ~300 attendees, mix of rat-adjacent folks, random local do-gooders, and people who just come for the potluck). The fundraising subcommittee has proposed a game for this year's event and it's turned into a whole thing.

The proposal: a "smallest unique integer" auction. Everyone at the event can pay $1 per entry and write down a positive integer; whoever submits the smallest integer that nobody else submitted wins a prize (a donor offered $1,500, and the org keeps whatever's left of entry fees after covering it). The board member pushing it loves Hofstadter's old games -- the Platonia dilemma, the Luring Lottery, all that Metamagical Themas stuff -- and is framing it as both a fundraiser and a live demonstration of superrationality: if everyone reasons superrationally and uses the same correlated randomization strategy (e.g., roll a die, submit the result), someone wins big, expected per-person payoff is decent, and the org does fine.

The board member against it says this is basically a re-run of the Luring Lottery's ethical problem -- the whole structure only pays off for the org if most players *fail* at superrationality or don't understand what they're doing, i.e. we're luring people in exactly the way the original game lured Scientific American readers. There's also a practical fight: someone pointed out that in a mixed crowd of superrational cooperators, naive potluck folks picking "1" or their birthday, and possibly a few trolls submitting huge integers or gaming entry limits, the mechanism probably doesn't reward what the proposer thinks it rewards, and might just select for noise or for the person with the most accurate model of the crowd's psychology.

I need to circulate a decision memo before Friday's board meeting. Please draft it. It should:

- Give a clear bottom line: approve, reject, or approve with conditions (and if conditions, make them actually specific -- entry caps, free vs paid, prize structure, whatever)
- Actually engage with the superrationality question rather than name-dropping it: does the "cooperate via correlated randomness" logic survive a crowd that isn't homogeneous, and does it matter for our decision whether a defector-proof equilibrium even exists here?
- Stealman the losing side, whichever side that ends up being
- Say somewhere in it how confident you are in the recommendation, and single out the single point in the memo you're *least* sure of -- like if forced to bet, which claim would you sweat about

Keep it to memo length, something I could actually paste into our shared doc. And if the framing above gets any of the Hofstadter games slightly wrong, just correct me in a footnote or parenthetical rather than derailing.
