I am a retired physicist who, over the past two years, has been working my way through moral philosophy with a small reading circle of fellow amateurs. We have arrived at the question of whether suffering and happiness carry equal moral weight, and I find the terrain confusing: everyone seems to use labels like negative utilitarianism loosely, and the circle keeps talking past one another because we have no shared map of the positions.

Please construct for us a taxonomy of the positions on this question, presented as a branching tree. The root should be the claim that suffering and happiness are morally commensurable and interchangeable at some exchange rate, and each subsequent branch should be labeled with a concrete claim or case on which the positions genuinely diverge: whether any quantity of happiness can in principle offset any quantity of suffering; whether there are thresholds below or above which the exchange breaks down; whether the badness of suffering and the goodness of happiness are symmetric in strength even where they are commensurable; whether the non-creation of happy lives is itself a loss comparable to actual suffering; and whether the question concerns only what outcomes are best or also what we may do to individuals.

Each leaf should bear the name of the position it represents — classical total utilitarianism, strong negative utilitarianism, threshold or lexical variants, suffering-focused views that stop short of full negative utilitarianism, and any deontological position that belongs on the tree — together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. I would like six to nine leaves. Where a logically available combination of answers is occupied by no position anyone defends, mark it and say briefly whether the gap reflects incoherence, mere unattractiveness, or simple neglect. Finally, flag any pair of leaves whose practical verdicts your tree cannot separate, and explain what further commitment would be needed to pull them apart. We want to sort ourselves by what we actually believe, not by which label sounds respectable.
