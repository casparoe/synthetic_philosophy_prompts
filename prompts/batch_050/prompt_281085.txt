So I run a board game night at our community centre, usually 12 to 16 people, mixed ages, and I get maybe 90 minutes of their actual attention if I'm lucky. I fell down a youtube rabbit hole about the prisoner's dillema and that tournament where the super simple strategy beat all the fancy math ones, and I want to build a game that makes people *feel* why being nice-but-not-a-pushover wins, instead of just telling them.

Design me the game. Some requirements, because I've been burned before:

- Last time I tried something like this I announced "okay, ten rounds" and the second round ten came around everyone stabbed each other, and afterwards people said they saw it coming from like round seven. So the ending has to be unpredictable somehow. Give me the actual mechanic for that, not just "make it vague."
- I want a phase where players can refuse to keep playing with someone and go find a new partner, because I have this hunch that cooperation works totally differently when you can just walk away from jerks. Is the hunch right? How should that phase work?
- Points system that doesn't use real money but still stings a little. Maybe the overall winner gets first pick of the snack table, idk, you tell me.
- One thing the videos never explained: what happens when people *misread* each other. Someone means to cooperate but it comes out wrong, or you think someone screwed you over and they actually didn't. Real life is full of that. Should the game build in a misunderstanding mechanic, and what does it do to which strategy comes out on top?
- Then a debrief plan: map what actually happened in the room onto one-shot vs repeated games, tell me when to finally say the words "prisoner's dilemma," what will definately go wrong the first time I run it, and one honest thing the game can't capture about cooperation in the real world.

No lecture up front though. They're there to play games, not get schooled.
