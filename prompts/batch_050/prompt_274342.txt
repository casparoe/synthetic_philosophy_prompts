So next week I'm giving a talk at my local EA/rationalist meetup and I'd like help prepping for the Q&A. My thesis goes one step past the standard unilateralist's curse argument: I want to claim the curse operates *inside* single organizations, not just among independent actors. My pitch is that in any lab or startup with, say, 30 people who can each unilaterally trigger something hard to reverse (publishing a result, shipping a feature, emailing a journalist, open-sourcing weights), the effective decision-maker is whoever has the most optimistic private estimate, and the larger the org the worse the selection effect gets. So I propose a fix: a norm that anyone about to take an irreversible action has to get explicit sign-off from the two colleagues they think are *most skeptical* of the action, instead of the usual announce-and-proceed-if-nobody-objects.

The audience: maybe 15 people. A handful of longtermists who know the original curse argument well and will spot any sloppy restatement of it. Two people with actual decision theory backgrounds who will immediately ask about the case where everyone is fully rational and knows about the curse, since then silence becomes informative and I've heard the effect can weaken or even flip. Several startup-engineer types with strong move-fast priors who think norms like mine are how organizations calcify and die. And one guy who thinks the curse is basically a fully general argument for never doing anything, and will say so.

Give me the objections roughly in the order they'll come, phrased how each of those people would actually phrase them, with my best reply to each, what I should concede versus hold, and where my replies are weakest. Some things I especially want pressure-tested: does my two-most-skeptical-signoff fix actually dissolve the selection effect or just relocate who counts as the pivotal agent? Is the curse symmetric between action and inaction, i.e., is "not acting" itself a unilateral decision when anyone could have raised the alarm and didn't? And what's the objection I should most hope nobody asks, the one where my talk is genuinely thin? Also flag anywhere my "internal" extension diverges from the original argument in a way that changes the logic rather than just the setting.
