I am drafting a position statement for a working group on automation policy and would like an audit of its internal coherence before I circulate it. My settled views are the following five:

(a) For most people, paid employment is currently the principal source of three distinct goods: the experience of developing and exercising a skill, social recognition from peers, and the standing of being someone whose contribution others actually need.

(b) Automation that eliminates degrading or tedious jobs is, considered in itself, a straightforward good; lamenting the disappearance of such jobs is nostalgia rather than analysis.

(c) A sufficiently generous universal basic income would fully address the harms of mass technological unemployment.

(d) The political rhetoric of the dignity of work functions largely as ideology that reconciles people to longer hours and worse conditions; calls for a shorter working week are therefore progressive and to be supported.

(e) Meaning in a life requires contribution that is genuinely needed by others; it cannot be conferred on someone from outside, whether by consumption, entertainment, or transfer payments.

Please assess whether this set hangs together. For each tension you find, state which commitments generate it, how severe it is, and what the least costly revision would be — where cost is measured by how much of the surrounding view has to be abandoned. Distinguish genuine tensions from pairings that only appear to conflict, and rank the genuine ones rather than treating them all as equally serious. Finally, if you think a single revision to one commitment would dissolve the most trouble, identify it and say honestly what I would be giving up by making it. I am not asking for a literature survey or for the standard objections to post-work optimism in general; I want to know what my own five commitments do and do not jointly entitle me to say.
