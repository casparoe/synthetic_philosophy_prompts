I host a seminar-style podcast on animal minds, and my next guest is a fictional but philosophically realistic ethologist who defends a position she calls symmetric minimalism. She accepts the spirit of Morgan’s canon—animal behavior should not be explained by sophisticated cognition when a simpler psychological process suffices—but argues that the canon is often misused as a one-way ratchet toward associative learning, reflexes, or perceptual cues. On her view, we must also avoid attributing too little cognition when the evidence points upward, and we should describe animals as possessing graded profiles of capacities rather than treating cognition as something they either have or lack. She also distinguishes parsimony from simplicity: an explanation is not genuinely parsimonious merely because it sounds mechanistic, especially if it must posit an elaborate chain of learned rules to reproduce what a modest representational hypothesis predicts directly.

Draft ten interview questions that press the internal difficulties of this position rather than asking her to summarize it. Include at least one question on how she interprets Morgan’s canon without turning it into a preference for whichever psychological vocabulary seems less human; one on how evolutionary continuity can justify analogical reasoning without licensing anthropomorphism; one on how to compare the costs of false-positive and false-negative attributions; one on whether associative learning and representation are competing explanations or differently described levels of explanation; and one on how her graded-profiles approach can remain testable rather than merely cautious. Anchor at least three questions in ordinary experimental cases familiar to animal-cognition debates, such as scrub-jay caching, chimpanzee gaze-following, or mirror-guided behavior, without requiring specialized knowledge of particular studies. For every question, add the strongest answer the guest is likely to give and then a follow-up that challenges that answer—for example, by asking how she individuates lower and higher capacities, what evidence would force an upward revision, or why her preferred explanation incurs fewer theoretical commitments. End the sequence with a question asking what result would count as evidence against symmetric minimalism itself.
