So I've talked myself into illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — roughly the view that there's no genuine "what it's like" glow inside, just brains that represent themselves as having ineffable qualitative states, and the hard problem dissolves once you've explained why we're disposed to believe and say that kind of thing about ourselves. My problem is I can't tell anymore whether this view is actually risking anything. So: what would count against it? I don't want the usual "but the illusion would itself have to be experienced!" complaint unless you can turn it into something with real bite. I want a map of the view's exposure — which introspective or psychophysical findings, which results about how introspective reports are generated, which failures of the "meta-problem" research programme (i.e. actually explaining, mechanistically, why people say they're conscious) would hurt it, and where. Please rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones a committed illusionist would concede as trouble versus shrug off with another qualification — and if your verdict is that the view is so insulated that basically nothing could disconfirm it, say so, and tell me whether that's a dealbreaker or just the price of being a deflationary theory.
