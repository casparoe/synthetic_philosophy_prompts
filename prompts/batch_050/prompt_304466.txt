I'm drafting a statement of where I stand on the cosmological argument for a philosophy reading group, and I'd like a consistency check before I present it. My commitments: (1) whatever begins to exist has a cause; (2) an actually infinite series of past events is impossible, so the universe began to exist; (3) every contingent fact has an explanation — no brute facts, which is why even an infinite series of contingent things would still need an explanation as a whole; (4) the regress of explanations has to terminate in something that exists necessarily rather than contingently; (5) God's necessity exempts him from needing any further explanation — asking what explains God is a category error; (6) God is timeless and changeless, and the first cause must be personal, because only a free agent could explain how a temporal universe arises from a timeless cause. Which of these are actually in tension with each other, and which pairs only look like they are? For each real tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be, and rank them by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. If one single revision would clean up the most problems, flag it.
