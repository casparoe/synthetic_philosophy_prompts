Construct a thought experiment in which moral rightness and divine approval are correlated because both are effects of a common cause, rather than because either explains the other. The scenario should be realistic enough to clarify the Euthyphro dilemma, should avoid stipulating that God is identical to goodness itself, and should make divine approval genuinely reliable rather than infallible by definition. In one paragraph, present the case; then explain what each horn of the dilemma says about it, whether the common-cause structure escapes the dilemma or merely pushes it back one step, and which feature of the case is doing the philosophical work. Conclude with the strongest objection a divine command theorist could raise against the analogy.
