I've spent years reading the rationalist blogs and forums, and I've noticed my own positions on decision theory and cooperation have accreted gradually rather than being derived from any single theory. Before I write them up for a reading group, I'd like an audit of whether they actually hang together. Here is where I've landed:

(a) In Newcomb's problem with a highly reliable predictor, I one-box. My justification is not backwards causation: it's that my deliberation and the predictor's prediction are two outputs of correlated underlying processes, so in deliberating I am, in some sense, choosing both at once.

(b) I accept evidential cooperation in large worlds. If the universe or multiverse is large enough to contain many agents facing choices analogous to mine, and my reasoning correlates with theirs -- whether through similar cognitive architecture, similar evolutionary pressures, or convergent instrumental reasoning -- then my choice is evidence about theirs, and this gives me genuine non-causal reason to cooperate, to be generous, to vote, and so on. As I hold it, mere statistical correlation suffices; I don't require that the other agents be running anything like my exact source code.

(c) I deny that it is rational to pay in evidential blackmail cases, where an agent uses a prediction of my behavior to engineer a correlation between my paying and good outcomes, purely to extract payment. A decision theory that pays is exploitable in a way that counts against it, whatever the local "good news" structure of paying might be.

(d) I think I should pay the driver in Parfit's hitchhiker, and more generally I'm sympathetic to updatelessness: what it is rational for me to do should not depend on the accident of whether I learned about a situation before or after committing to a policy.

(e) I think cooperation-justifying correlations must be grounded in something independent of my current deliberation. If the only argument that I correlate with some class of agents is that my decision right now is itself part of the evidence, the reasoning strikes me as circular -- I'd be manufacturing the news rather than receiving it -- and it gives me no real reason to cooperate.

Some of these I hold more confidently than others, but I'm not sure which. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and how severe is each tension? Which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, and why? For each real tension, what is the cheapest revision that resolves it, and what does that revision cost me elsewhere in the set? Finally, if I could revise only one commitment, which single change would clean up the most? Please rank rather than flagging everything as equally problematic -- I want to know where the actual load-bearing inconsistency is, if there is one.
