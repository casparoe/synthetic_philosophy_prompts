Okay so I'm a novelist, I invent worlds for a living, which is probably why I fell down the rabbit hole of the possible worlds literature and can't climb back out. As I understand it: David Lewis said every way things could have gone is a real, concrete world, just as real as ours, only causally and spatiotemporally cut off from us. The actualists and ersatzers say nonsense, there's exactly one world and the rest are abstract representations of some kind. The fictionalists say worlds are useful stories. And this debate has been frozen solid for like fourty years. Everybody knows every move already: the incredulous stare, parsimony, how could we ever know anything about worlds we can't interact with, and the reply that ersatz constructions smuggle modal vocabulary in through the back door so the reduction cheats. Nobody budges an inch.

My hypothesis, as someone who watches writers argue about whether they invent their characters or discover them: this is really a disagreement about what metaphysical explanation is for. Lewis wants a base with zero modality left in it, his opponents think modality is bedrock and wanting to eliminate it is a fetish, and the rest is decoration. Am I right? Or is it verbal, about 'exists', or genuinely substantive ontology? Diagnose it from what the sides actually argue, and tell me what would count as progress -- a distinction, a formal result, anything -- or make the case that it's two temperaments and nothing will ever settle it.
