I'm writing an essay for my business ethics module and I'm stuck on how my seminar leader thinks about corporate blame. Her verdicts in class so far: a pharma company where mid-level scientists buried bad trial results and the board genuinely never saw them -- she says the company itself is blameworthy, not just the scientists. A charity whose treasurer secretly siphoned donations into her own account, against everything the charity stood for -- she says the charity is not blameworthy, only the treasurer. A delivery firm whose routing algorithm systematically sent drivers through a poor neighbourhood, even though no individual employee ever intended that or even noticed -- she says blameworthy. And a company where the board explicitly voted to dump waste in a river -- blameworthy, obviously.

Next week we're doing a case where a supermarket chain's safety procedures were completely standard for the industry, nobody violated any policy, but a fire in one of their warehouses killed two workers because of a risk that was well documented in the trade press. Predict her verdict on whether the chain itself is blameworthy. More importantly: what rule is she running across the earlier cases, what other rules fit them equally well but would give a different verdict here, and what extra case would you ask her about to settle which rule she's actually using?
