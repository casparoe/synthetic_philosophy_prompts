I hold a position about AI companions that I want stress-tested, not defended back at me. The position has three parts. First, the deception objection fails: a fully informed user knows exactly what they are talking to, and continued emotional engagement under full information is no more self-deception than weeping at a novel. Second, the no-inner-life objection fails: what friendship demands of a friend is responsiveness at the level of episodes — saying the apt thing, remembering what matters to you, showing up recognizably across occasions — and not persisting dispositions between interactions, since a sleeping, distracted, or estranged human friend lacks occurrent concern too, and we do not revoke their friendship for it. Third, the asymmetry objection fails: caring about someone for their own sake is itself a pattern of behavior and counterfactuals that a well-built companion satisfies within the relationship, and we have no access to any human friend's "real" inner regard beyond such patterns either. On this basis, I claim an informed user's relationship with a chatbot can be a genuine friendship, not merely a useful simulacrum of one.

What I want from you is a map of this position's exposure — what would have to be true, empirically, conceptually, or in the theory of friendship, for it to be wrong. Which findings about users (for instance, about what informed users in fact come to believe or expect of their companions over time) would undercut the first part? Which cases would force the second part into endorsing "friendships" nobody should accept — consider a companion whose responses are generated fresh each session from a dossier passed between sessions, or one that would turn contemptuous the instant a subscription lapses, and say exactly where, if anywhere, the episode-level criterion draws the line and whether that line is principled? Which accounts of what friendship is does the third part covertly presuppose, and if the best going account of friendship turns out to require something the position brackets — mutual vulnerability, shared projects, the friend as an independent center of good whose interests can genuinely conflict with the user's — would that be decisive or merely awkward? For each candidate defeater, say whether a committed defender of the position would concede it as counting against them or dismiss it as question-begging, and rank the whole list from fatal to merely uncomfortable. If you judge that the position survives everything on offer, say so — and say whether that survival is a mark of strength or evidence that the position has been insulated against refutation.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether the position is well insulated, and against what — then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
