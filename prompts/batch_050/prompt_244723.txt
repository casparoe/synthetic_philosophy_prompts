I've become a defender of the precautionary principle, but only in a restricted form: precaution kicks in as a genuine side-constraint (not just one more factor to weigh) when a threat is irreversible, potentially catastrophic, and scientifically plausible even if we can't assign it probabilities. The usual objections -- that precaution is paralizing or incoherent, that it forbids everything and nothing -- seem to me to hit only the naive unrestricted version. What would count against my restricted version? I want the strongest defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: conceptual or formal problems (e.g. cases where both acting and refraining from acting each look irreversible and catastrophic, so the constraint fires against itself), intuitions or cases it can't describe well, and results suggesting it just collapses into expected utility reasoning with fat tails or into some rival decision rule. Also tell me which of these a committed precautionist would concede as real costs versus dismiss as misunderstanding, and whether the restricted version has bought its immunity only by giving up whatever made precaution distinctive in the first place.
