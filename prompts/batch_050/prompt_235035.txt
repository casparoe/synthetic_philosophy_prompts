After a long argument with a friend yesterday evening, I have been talked into, or at least talked around to defending, the strong version of the claim that technical artifacts can embody political properties independently of their users' intentions — roughly, that the design of a bridge, an algorithm, or a filing system can itself enforce, permit, or foreclose forms of life, and not merely serve as a neutral instrument for someone's politics. To keep the discussion precise, my friend and I stipulated the following as the strongest defensible version: an artifact has politics only if (a) its politics survive counterfactually across a wide range of user intentions, and (b) no feasible redesign at comparable cost would remove them, so that the politics are load-bearing rather than incidental.

You are a philosopher of technology with expertise in the social construction of technology and its critics. Think carefully, and before answering, briefly brainstorm the distinct ways this position could be defeated. Then give me a map of its exposure: what would count against the stipulated strong thesis? I want, first, the observations, cases, and arguments that would be genuinely decisive against it — including what defenders of the thesis would themselves have to concede as counting against it, not only what social constructivists say — and second, those that would merely be awkward. In particular: would it matter if every canonical example turned out to be better explained by the economic and legal systems surrounding the artifact rather than the artifact's material form? Would the thesis be refuted, or only narrowed, if it turned out that all political effects trace to the artifact's *systematic misalignment with some users' bodies or resources* rather than to anything intrinsic to design? And does condition (b) smuggle in the conclusion, since what counts as "comparable cost" is itself politically set? Finally, tell me honestly whether the strong thesis, as stipulated, risks anything at all — whether its defenders can absorb any counterexample by redescribing intentions as built into design — and if so, whether that unfalsifiability is fatal to it or just par for the course for claims of this generality.
