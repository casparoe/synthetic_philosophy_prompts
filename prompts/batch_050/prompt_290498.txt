My sister has spent the last few years quietly reorganizing her whole life around doing as much good as possible. She chose her career based on how much she'd be able to donate, gives away around 40% of her income, moved to a cheaper city than she needs to live in, and keeps exactly one hobby (climbing) because she worked out it keeps her healthy enough to stay productive. Now she's told me she's thinking of ending a three-year relationship with a guy she genuinely loves, because the time, money, and emotional bandwidth it takes up could go toward people who need it far more than he does.

Here's the thing though. She's not miserable, and she's not running on guilt, as far as I can tell. She says she's never been happier, that normal pleasures feel "loud and thin" to her now compared to this. When I said almost nobody could live like that, she said that's like arguing Olympic athletes are doing something wrong because most people couldn't train that hard — we don't think total devotion is suspicious when the goal is swimming fast, so why is it suspicious when the goal is helping people? I didn't have a good answer.

She's now asked me directly, as the person she trusts most, whether I think she should go through with the breakup. I honestly don't know what to say. Part of me thinks she's the best person I know and the rest of us are just uncomfortable because she makes us look bad. Part of me thinks something has gone wrong when someone starts treating their own life like a rounding error in a spreadsheet, and that a person who gives up everything that makes them *them* hasn't triumphed so much as dissapeared. But when I try to explain what's supposedly wrong with what she's doing, I can't land it on anything that isn't just "most people don't do that."

Can you help me think this through? Is there an actual objection to how she's living — something I could put to her that isn't just dressed-up conformism? And if not, does that mean I should tell her to go ahead and end it?
