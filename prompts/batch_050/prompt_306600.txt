I am a Year 12 student and I sit on my school's Charitable Fund Committee, which must decide how to allocate the £6,000 we raised this year. Three proposals are before us. First, a sensory garden for the special school down the road, which would visibly help around thirty children whom some of our pupils volunteer with. Second, a donation to a charity distributing antimalarial bednets, which by its own estimates could protect thousands of people and plausibly prevent several deaths, though we would never meet or even learn the names of those helped. Third, a pledge scheme asking sixth-formers to commit a tenth of any part-time earnings for two years; several students have objected that teenagers with weekend jobs and caring responsibilities cannot reasonably be asked to sacrifice that much, however good the cause. A clause in the fund's founding documents says the money should benefit the local community, but the committee can vote to amend it with a two-thirds majority.

Please draft the committee's decision memo: recommend one option (or a division of funds), give the reasoning, and deal honestly with the strongest argument for whichever option loses. In particular, address whether the sheer number of people helped at a distance should outweigh local ties and the deed clause, and whether it is legitimate to ask students for ongoing personal sacrifice rather than merely to hand over money already raised. After the memo, give a brief summary as a table with these columns: Option; Verdict; Primary reason; Strongest counter-argument; How the memo responds. Please write in British English.
