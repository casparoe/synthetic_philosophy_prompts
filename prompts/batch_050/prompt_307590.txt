I run a fortnightly reading group for machine-learning engineers. Everyone in it is fluent in the standard reinforcement-learning formalism -- an agent sends actions into an environment and receives observations and rewards back -- but next week I have forty minutes to introduce embedded agency: the observation that a real agent is made of the same stuff as the world it acts in, so it must model a world that contains itself, can be modified by the world, can be inspected by smarter things in it, and cannot in principle hold a complete internal copy of its own surroundings. Don't give me the talk; give me the plan for the talk. What has to be established before the central problem can even be stated -- I'm assuming the audience first has to notice the Cartesian boundary their usual formalism builds in, but tell me if something else has to come earlier -- and in what order should the pieces go so that the difficulties (self-modelling, self-modification, being out-computed by an environment that can read your code) feel like consequences of one move rather than a list of puzzles? Choose the one carrying example to build the session around -- something like a program playing a game against an opponent who can read its source, or an agent whose world-model must depict the hardware it runs on -- and say what it must contain, what it must leave out, and why it beats the obvious alternatives. Anticipate the misconceptions this particular audience will form, and place each at the point in the sequence where it should be headed off: at minimum, that this is just embodiment or robotics, that a POMDP already handles it via partial observability, that the fix is to bolt on a self-model module trained on the agent's own behaviour, and that it's merely the frame problem rebranded. Give me one question I can pose at the end whose answer would show the group has grasped why the standard picture fails rather than merely memorised the failure modes, and tell me what I should explicitly refuse to cover at this level (logical uncertainty, infra-Bayesian machinery) and how to say so without sounding evasive. Please answer in British English.
