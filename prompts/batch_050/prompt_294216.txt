I am a novelist, and across several manuscripts I keep running up against the same structural phenomenon; I suspect logicians or philosophers of self-reference have already catalogued it, and I would like to know whether it has an established name. Let me describe it by instances rather than abstractly. In one draft of mine, a character becomes aware that she is a character and addresses her author directly, and I noticed that the narrative voice conveying her address remains perfectly intact: someone still has to narrate the confrontation, and that someone never steps into the scene to be confronted in turn. In the famous Escher print of two hands drawing each other into being, the same thing occurs: the artist's own hand, the one holding the real pencil, is nowhere in the picture, and it is the only hand that was never drawn by anything. And in casual discussions of the simulation hypothesis, I observe that people readily imagine we might be simulated, yet they picture the simulators as comfortably un-simulated; some level always gets to stand outside the regress it generates. The pattern, then: whenever representational levels fold back upon themselves, exactly one level seems exempt — it performs the folding and is never itself folded.

Please act as a scholar expert in self-reference, formal logic, and narrative theory. Think carefully, and before answering, brainstorm at least four candidate concepts from any relevant field (logic, metamathematics, narratology, philosophy of mind) that might name or approximate this phenomenon. Then give me your response as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion. The items should cover: the nearest established concepts and how each differs from what I have described; which fits best and why; whether the exempt level is a genuine necessity in such structures or merely a contingent feature of how humans tell stories and frame arguments; if no existing term fits exactly, a coined term with a precise definition and at least two boundary cases that fall outside it; and finally, what the phenomenon implies for a novelist attempting to write a story in which no level is exempt at all.
