I sit on the ethics committee of a mid-size professional association for civil engineers (about 8,000 members, we run continuing ed and handle complaints). We've got a live case and I've been asked to draft our recomendation memo to the full board.

The facts: one of our members, a senior structural engineer at a state contractor, was on a team reviewing bridge inspection protocols. After a meeting where the group agreed to flag certain inspection shortcuts "for further study," she copied about 40 pages of internal draft reports and sent them to a journalist. Maybe two thirds of it documented genuinely worrying stuff — inspection intervals being quietly stretched on bridges already rated in poor condition. The rest was routine internal email, some of it embarrassing to named colleagues but not evidence of any wrongdoing. She never used our association's complaint channel or the state DOT hotline. Her defense is that both are captured: our association president sits on the contractor's advisory board, and she claims a colleague who complained internally two years ago got frozen out of projects (she says she has emails showing this, though we haven't seen them). The state's position is that nothing has collapsed and all inspections met the letter of federal minimums.

The committee has to recommend one of: reprimand, censure, expulsion, or no sanction. The board is leaning toward some sanction because members are furious about the leaked internal emails; I'm not sure that's the right thing to hang it on.

Draft the memo. I want:

1. A clear bottom-line recommendation the board can vote on.
2. A reusable framework — criteria for when breaching professional loyalty to colleagues and the association is justified vs. when it's just betrayal — that can handle the wrinkle that she disclosed way more than she needed to. Does over-broad disclosure change the verdict on the disclosure that actually mattered, or should we treat them as two separate acts?
3. An honest treatment of the strongest case for sanctioning her. I think it's something like: if members can't trust the association with draft reports and candid internal debate, internal review dies, and the public ends up worse off than it would be with slower but real oversight. Steelman that properly, then answer it.
4. How much weight to give her "the channels are captured" claim given we can't fully verify the retaliation story — does the burden shift to us to show our complaint channel actually works?

Keep it under 900 words, real memo format, no throat-clearing.
