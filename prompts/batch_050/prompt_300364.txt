You are a metaphysician specializing in the literature on dispositions, preparing a specification for a graduate seminar. Write the list of requirements an adequate theory of dispositions must meet, with the case or consideration motivating each requirement. At minimum, address how the theory must handle the following families of problem cases: (1) finks — objects that possess a disposition only because a mechanism that would remove it is never triggered, as with the live wire connected to a device that would render it dead the instant a conductor touched it; (2) masks — objects that genuinely have a disposition whose manifestation is suppressed by an external arrangement, as with the fragile glass that survives being dropped because it is wrapped in protective packaging; (3) antidotes — cases where the characteristic stimulus occurs and the causal process toward manifestation begins but is interrupted partway, so the disposition seems present throughout despite the absence of manifestation; and (4) mimics — objects that lack a disposition but would display its characteristic manifestation anyway due to some extrinsic factor, which a theory must distinguish from genuinely disposed objects without collapsing the distinction between finks and masks. For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable for any theory of dispositions or merely a preference of a particular camp — for example, whether the theory may remain neutral on whether dispositions require a categorical basis, or whether taking a stand there is itself a desideratum. If some subset of these plausible requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, any straightforward conditional analysis of disposition ascriptions together with the verdict that finked and masked objects genuinely possess their dispositions — identify this explicitly and explain which requirement should give way; that is the part I most care about. Before writing the final list, briefly brainstorm the space of problem cases to check that nothing central is missing, and think carefully about which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate theories. Conclude by stating what would change your mind: which counterexample or consideration would lead you to drop, weaken, or add a requirement, and where the list is most likely to be mistaken.
