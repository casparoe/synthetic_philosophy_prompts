Four months ago I underwent general anesthesia for the first time, and I have not quite stopped thinking about the gap: five hours in which, so far as I can tell, there was no one home, after which someone woke up and smoothly took over my life. In the aftermath I picked up a popular book on the philosophy of selfhood, which leans heavily on Descartes. Its chapter on the Meditations summarizes his achievement like this: 'Descartes shows that the self is the one thing that cannot be doubted away. Since doubting is itself a kind of thinking, anyone who doubts his own existence thereby confirms it: I think, therefore I am. From this single inference Descartes establishes that what I am, essentially, is an immaterial thinking substance, distinct from my body, and that this substance is the persisting subject of all my experiences -- the same I throughout my life. With the self thus secured as an indubitable foundation, everything else I clearly and distinctly perceive can in principle be rebuilt upon it, and skepticism is answered at its root.' The author is a respected science writer, not a philosopher, and the passage is doing real work in his book: he later argues that each of us possesses a Cartesian core that survives every interruption, including sleep, coma, and anesthesia. My question is whether the summary is fair to Descartes, quite apart from the book's larger project. Please go through it claim by claim and separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification a careful scholar would tolerate in a popular work, and what Descartes himself explicitly denied or took pains to qualify -- I have in mind, for instance, things I have heard about whether the cogito is really an inference, and about how much of my temporal existence it actually vouches for. Then tell me whether the author's comforting conclusion, that the cogito guarantees a single persisting self across gaps like my five missing hours, follows from anything Descartes actually established, or whether it depends on exactly the parts of the summary that do not survive scrutiny.
