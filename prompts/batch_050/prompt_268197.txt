Ok so I need an essay written. Last night my friend Dani and I got into a huge argument about cultural appropriation in art -- it started because a painter we both know, whose whole aesthetic is basicly lifted from West African textile patterns, is up for this prestigious residency, and I said she shouldn't get it and Dani said that's insane -- and we talked in circles for like three hours and neither of us budged. I want the essay to settle it, or at least settle it better than we did.

Dani's position: all art is borrowing. Picasso took from African masks, rock music took from the blues, nobody owns a style or a palette or a rhythm, and the only genuine wrongs in this area are specific and narrow -- copying an identifiable living artist's actual work, or using sacred or closed ceremonial material that a community has explicitly asked outsiders to leave alone. Everything beyond that, she says, is moralized gatekeeping that would, if taken seriously, freeze artistic influence entirely, since influence never flows in tidy permissioned channels. My position: there's a further harm that doesn't depend on copying anything specific or on anyone feeling disrespected. Roughly -- when a well-positioned artist popularizes a style developed by a marginalized community, mainstream audiences start treating the borrower as the reference point, and the originators end up looking like imitators of their own tradition. They lose authorship of the thing they made, at least in the audience's eyes, and that costs them credit, opportunities, and eventually the standing to keep defining what their own tradition is. (I called this "credit displacement" at like 1am, I'm sure someone's formulated it better but that's the gist.)

Write an essay that adjudicates this properly. Steelman Dani, don't strawman her -- her "all art is borrowing" point is genuinely strong and needs a real answer, not a dismissal. Then evaluate my credit-displacement account on its merits: does it actually pick out something wrongful, and does it draw the line between appropriation and ordinary influence better than the standard candidate accounts (offense/disrespect, violation of sacred practices, straightforward economic harm)? Engage the obvious objections to me: audiences aren't obligated to track origins; influence runs in every direction; arguably the harm is the audience's ignorance rather than anything the artist did; and my account might condemn even respectful, fully-credited borrowing whenever the borrower happens to get famous enough, which seems wrong. Then reach an actual verdict: is appropriation a real moral category, and if so, what criterion marks the line between legitimate borrowing and wrongful appropriation? Please don't end with "it's complicated and depends on context" -- I want a defended conclusion I can bring back to Dani next time this comes up. Which it definately will.
