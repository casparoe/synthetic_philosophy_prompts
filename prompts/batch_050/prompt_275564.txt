I'm a high school senior taking physics this year, and our unit on special relativity has started an argument among my friends that I keep losing. From what I understand, simultaneity is relative: two events that happen at the same time in one frame of reference happen at different times in another, and there is no frame-independent "now" stretching across the universe. My friend claims this settles the old philosophical debate between presentism (only the present moment exists) and eternalism (the past, present, and future all exist) in favor of eternalism. His reasoning is that if what counts as "now" depends on the observer's motion, then everything must exist all at once, and the apparent flow of time is just an illusion produced by our consciousness. He says anyone still defending presentism is basically refusing to accept established science.

If that's right, I have some follow-up questions I can't get straight answers to. First, if the future already exists, why do physicists still do calculations about it — why predict an eclipse instead of just checking what's already out there? Second, does eternalism mean everything is fated? If next Tuesday already exists with whatever I'll end up doing, it seems like my deliberating about it is pointless, since the outcome is already sitting there. Third, if eternalism is the settled scientific answer, what would it even mean for an experiment to decide between presentism and eternalism? Is there some observation that could still overturn eternalism, or has that ship sailed? My friend says no experiment is needed because relativity already did the work, but that feels too fast to me.

Please answer these, but if my questions (or my friend's argument) rest on a mistake, I'd rather you correct the mistake than answer on my terms. In particular, I'd like you to clearly separate what is genuinely established by physics from what remains contested among physicists and philosophers who actually work on time — I don't want a disputed metaphysical position presented to me as settled science, and I also don't want to be told that something obvious to everyone is "still an open question" if it isn't.
