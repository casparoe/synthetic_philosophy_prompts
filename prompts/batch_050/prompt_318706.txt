So here's a thing that's been bugging me. I'm a novelist, and for the last year or so I've been in this little group with some other writers and a couple of game designer friends, and we keep circling the same fight: whether the fact that a game lets the player make choices is a genuinely new kind of aesthetic achievement or whether it's actually a problem for the work. One side (mostly the designers, plus one poet) says that agency is the whole point, that a game where your decisions matter gives you something no novel can, a kind of beauty that lives in what you do rather than what you're shown. The other side (me included, honestly) keeps saying that the more control you hand the player, the more you lose the thing that makes a work a work -- the shaped, authored, this-and-not-that quality. The designers respond that we've just never played the right games, we respond that they've never noticed what a real ending does, and round we go. Nobody has conceded a single point in twelve months.

My hypothesis, which I offer very tentatively, is that we're not actually disagreeing about games at all but about what authorship is for -- like, one side thinks the artist's job is to build a possibility space and the other thinks it's to make choices on the audience's behalf, and neither of us can defend those commitments with anything but taste. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real empirical disagreement buried in there about what players actually experience, or a merely verbal one where 'art' is doing all the work.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Diagnose it using what each side would actually say, don't just referee. And given your diagnosis, tell me what, if anything, would move it -- a distinction, an example someone would have to concede, a different question we should be asking -- or argue that nothing would and that's fine.

One format request: please write your answer as continuous prose, like an email or a letter. No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables -- I find all that stuff makes me skim instead of read.
