I’ve been arguing with a friend from my philosophy of physics reading group about what people ought to mean by nonlocal in Bell-type situations. Her verdicts on our toy cases so far are: two boxes whose outputs were entirely preprogrammed in their common past, with measurement settings chosen independently, are local; an ideal loophole-free Bell experiment with entangled particles and independently chosen settings is nonlocal; PR-box-style correlations, which violate a Bell inequality more strongly than quantum mechanics while still forbidding signalling, are nonlocal; a retrocausal model in which the later measurement settings constrain the earlier shared state is not nonlocal; a superdeterministic model in which the settings and source state are correlated through their common initial conditions is not nonlocal; and a model containing an actual faster-than-light causal connection between the two wings is nonlocal.

Now I’ve given her a stranger case. Two devices violate a Bell inequality. Their settings are genuinely independent of the source, and there is no retrocausal dependence of the source on the settings. There is also no faster-than-light causal process: changing a local fact about one device never produces a change in the other, and every ordinary causal influence remains within the light cones. Nevertheless, the fundamental law does not assign separate outcome-fixing states to the two devices. It directly assigns joint outcomes to the pair as one indivisible system, and the statistics cannot be recovered from local states at each wing together with facts about their common past. Predict whether my friend will call this case nonlocal. Lay out at least three rules that fit her earlier verdicts—such as a Bell-structural rule, a causal-influence rule, and a nonseparability rule—and say what each implies about the new case. Give your best guess about which rule she is actually using and why. Finally, describe one further case that would distinguish the two most plausible rules, especially a case on which Bell-nonlocality and mere nonseparability might come apart.
