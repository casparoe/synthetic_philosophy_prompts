Since retiring from a career in ecology, I have been spending more time on moral philosophy, and I recently resolved to bring my diet in line with the thought that animal suffering matters. I keep a rough spreadsheet of what I eat, and I had assumed the arithmetic would be simple: fewer animals harmed, better outcome. Instead I have tied myself in knots, and I would like advice on how to reason through it.

Here is my difficulty. My butcher tells me I should switch from chicken to beef, since one steer provides as much meat as dozens of chickens, so eating beef kills far fewer individual animals per pound. That seems right as far as it goes. But I spent forty years studying grassland ecosystems, and I know what a hectare of cattle pasture is: it is land from which a great many wild animals have been excluded — rodents, ground-nesting birds, insects — creatures whose lives, as I observed them, involve a great deal of predation, starvation, and exposure, and which may on balance not be worth living at all. So perhaps displacing them is a benefit rather than a cost, which would seem to favor pasture-raised beef even more strongly. Yet the same reasoning unsettles me: if keeping land clear of wild animals is good because wild lives are miserable, then clearing more land for farming would be good, which feels like a reductio rather than a conclusion. Meanwhile a vegetarian neighbor points out that my lentils and bread are not innocent either, since cultivating crops kills field animals wholesale, so the comparison is really between different ways of killing, not between killing and not killing. And a philosopher friend added, unhelpfully, that if I stopped eating chicken the chickens would simply never exist, and it is not obvious that this is bad for them — which may be true but makes my spreadsheet useless, since I have no idea whether to count an animal that is never born as a cost, a benefit, or nothing at all.

What I want is not a vague assurance that it is complicated. I would like you to tell me which of these considerations is actually load-bearing for the decision and which are distractions, and to give me a usable way of weighing animal lives I end against animal lives I prevent or fail to bring about. Please be candid about how confident you are in each step of the reasoning, and single out the single point in your answer where you are least sure you are right.
