I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've become preoccupied with the question of whether the brain is a computer. I'm planning to spend roughly a year of evenings on a self-directed study project, ending in a long essay, and I want to spend that year on the open question whose resolution would most clarify the whole debate rather than on the question that's merely most famous. Before I lay out my specific ask, let me explain the conceptual frame I'm bringing from engineering, because I want the prioritization to engage with it rather than ignore it.

In my day job there's a sharp difference between emulating a system and simulating it. An emulator of a chip reproduces its input-output behavior by actually performing the same abstract operations, so that anything the original could do -- including running its bugs -- the emulator does too. A simulation of, say, turbulent airflow reproduces behavior to some resolution but nobody thinks the simulated air is wet. The interesting cases are in between, and they tend to involve what I think of as substrate leak: places where the supposedly irrelevant implementation details turn out to be load-bearing for the higher-level behavior, like floating-point rounding, cache timing, or thermal throttling showing up in the output of software that was supposed to abstract all that away. When a system has substrate leak, emulation has to reach down into the physics, and at the limit the distinction between emulation and simulation collapses.

So my working suspicion is that the brain-as-computer question reduces, in large part, to a question about substrate leak: how far down into the neural, chemical, or even physical details you have to reach before the remaining abstraction still captures everything that matters about the mind. If the mind is like software on a clean virtual machine, functionalism is basically right and minds are portable across substrates. If it's riddled with substrate leak all the way down, the computational claim becomes either trivially true in some thin descriptive sense or just false.
