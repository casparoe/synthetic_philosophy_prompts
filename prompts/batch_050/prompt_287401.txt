Act as a professional philosopher and research supervisor with expertise in value pluralism, rational choice, and moral disagreement. I am a software engineer with no formal philosophical training, and I am preparing a short proposal for a four-month independent research project. My interest arose while helping several nonprofits allocate donated engineering time: each organisation evaluated the same projects according to a different mixture of values, such as expected benefit, fairness to existing beneficiaries, urgency, and respect for prior commitments. A weighted score could identify the best overall project, but the more interesting cases were those in which each side preferred a compromise to simply imposing its own ranking. I want to investigate a phenomenon I provisionally call hollow moral compromise: an agreement that appears to generate gains from trade because every value receives some satisfaction, yet leaves each value so fragmented or tokenistically served that nobody’s core concern is genuinely advanced.

Please turn this half-formed idea into a polished one-page research proposal. Begin by thinking carefully about the strongest formulation of the central question; then state it precisely, explain why familiar appeals to Pareto improvement, weighted aggregation, or splitting the difference do not settle it, and describe the conceptual framework the project would develop. In particular, I would like the project to distinguish hollow compromises from at least three alternatives: genuine moral gains from trade, principled refusal to compromise where a value has a non-negotiable threshold, and compromises that are instrumentally sensible despite leaving a significant moral residue. Set out a plausible four-month method—perhaps clarifying the idea, testing it against carefully constructed allocation cases, identifying the conditions under which compromise has value, and deriving practical criteria for recognising hollow compromises. Explain what the final deliverable would be, what would count as success or failure, and why the project is tractable without undertaking new empirical research. Do not provide a bibliography, literature review, or citations; refer to relevant positions only in general terms, as I shall supply any references myself. Use clear, formal British English and make the proposal persuasive to a reviewer who is philosophically literate but unfamiliar with this particular problem.
