So I've basically been a dark forest believer for a while now — not just in the "cool idea from the Three-Body books" sense, but as my actual best-guess explanation for the Great Silence, and the reason I think METI/active SETI is genuinely reckless. What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Some things I'm unsure how to weight: would observing an obviously un-hidden civilisation that's survived for millennia actually falsify it, or would a defender just shrug and say that civ is naive and doomed? Does the apparent absence of past strikes — no suspiciously destroyed stars, no relativistic-weapon debris fields — count against it, or does the theory predict strikes should be untraceable anyway? Are there game-theoretic results that would break it, e.g. showing that hiding is impossible at interstellar scales, or that first-strike advantage collapses once detection is symmetric?
