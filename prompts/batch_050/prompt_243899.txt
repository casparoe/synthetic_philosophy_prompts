I've recently become persuaded that deliberation and prediction are incompatible: while I'm genuinely deliberating about what to do, I cannot assign a probability to what I'll choose, because treating my choice as a settled fact awaiting prediction would preempt the very question my deliberation is meant to settle. That much seems right to me. But it appears to have a consequence nobody warned me about, and I'd like to verify the inference before reorganizing my affairs around it.

If I cannot coherently hold credences about my own choices, then my ordinary planning seems to rest on an incoherent foundation. Whenever I estimate the chance that I'll stick to my budget next quarter, finish the manuscript by spring, or still want this career in five years, I am presumably attempting the forbidden move of forecasting myself. Worse, my long-term partner and closest friends appear better epistemically positioned with respect to my conduct than I am: they can treat me as an object of prediction in exactly the way I, qua deliberator, cannot. So my tentative conclusion is that rational self-management requires outsourcing the forecasting of my own behavior. Before committing to deadlines, savings goals, or promises, I should poll the people who know me best about what they predict I'll do, and plan around their estimates rather than around my own intentions, which — if the argument is sound — give me no probabilistic traction at all.

Two questions, then. First, have I drawn the inference correctly? Does the point about deliberation crowding out prediction really undermine first-person planning in general, or am I sliding somewhere — and if so, where exactly? Second, on the assumption that the outsourcing scheme is sound, how should I weight my informants' predictions against one another, and what is the rational response when their unanimous forecast flatly contradicts a sincere present intention — when I fully intend to finish the book, say, and everyone who knows me predicts I won't? Should I simply decline to make the commitment in the first place?
