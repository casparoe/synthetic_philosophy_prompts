“Lewis begins with Stalnaker’s Thesis: for an indicative conditional A → C, P(A → C) = P(C | A) whenever P(A) > 0. If this equality must hold for every ordinary probability function over a fixed language in which A → C expresses a proposition, the thesis may be applied not only to P but also to probability functions obtained from P by conditionalizing on C and on ¬C. Under standard assumptions, this yields P(A → C) = P(C), which, when combined with the original thesis, makes A and C probabilistically independent in non-extreme cases. Since that cannot hold across all credence functions, Stalnaker’s Thesis is incompatible with any truth-conditional semantics for indicative conditionals.

The proof assumes conditionalization as the relevant rule for revising beliefs, so an agent who updates by imaging rather than conditionalization is outside its scope. Likewise, context-sensitive, dynamic, and expressivist accounts automatically avoid triviality because they deny that a conditional expresses one fixed proposition across different credal contexts. The result also leaves the Ramsey Test untouched, since that test specifies a procedure for accepting a conditional rather than a semantic equation concerning its probability.”
