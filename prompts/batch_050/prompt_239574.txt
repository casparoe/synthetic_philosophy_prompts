I returned to the Catholic Church a few years ago and, wanting to understand the theology I had grown up with, worked my way into the Molinist–Thomist dispute over grace, foreknowledge, and freedom. What strikes me is how little it has moved since the Dominican–Jesuit controversies of the late sixteenth century: the Thomists press the grounding objection -- nothing makes counterfactuals of creaturely freedom true prior to the divine decree, so middle knowledge has no object -- and the Molinists reply that such counterfactuals need no truthmaker beyond what the agent would freely do, or that the demand for grounding already assumes the Thomist picture of causality. Each side seems to rest on a commitment the other cannot share: for the Thomists, the universality and primacy of divine causation; for the Molinists, libertarian freedom as a condition of praise and blame. So my question is diagnostic rather than adjudicative. Is this a substantive metaphysical disagreement about what exists, a clash of bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for non-circularly, or a disagreement about what explanation requires that has been mislabeled as a disagreement about God? Support your diagnosis with what each side actually claims, and tell me what would count as progress -- a distinction between kinds of grounding, a formal result about counterfactuals, one side conceding a premise -- or whether, on your diagnosis, no progress should be expected and why.
