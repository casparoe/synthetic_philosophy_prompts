I went along with it, and then a couple weeks ago she asked me point blank if she was dying and I said no, you'll be fine. I've been chewing on it ever since.

My brother-in-law, who did a year of seminary and brings this stuff up at every family dinner, told me Augustine wrote a whole treatise against lying and sent me this summery from some Catholic ethics site:

"St. Augustine held that lying is always wrong, in every circumstance, because speech exists to communicate what is in the mind, and a lie deliberately misuses speech by asserting the opposite of what one thinks. Since every lie corrupts language in the same way, Augustine regarded all lies as equally grave — a polite fib and a malicious slander are the same offense in God's eyes. This is why he concluded that if murderers come to your door asking where your friend is hiding, you must tell them, since lying to them would be no less sinful than any other lie."

The murderers-at-the-door thing is basically my situation with the volume turned up, so it got my attention. But parts of this smell off. Did Augustine really think every lie is equally bad? That seems cartoonishly rigid even for a strict guy. And does "never lie" really mean you have to answer the murderers — couldn't you just stay silent and let them draw their own conclusions? I suspect the site is flattening him into a caricature but I don't know the texts well enough to say where.
