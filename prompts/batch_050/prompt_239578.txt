After forty years in experimental biology, I have lately been reading about the alignment problem in artificial intelligence, and I keep encountering the same argumentative pattern: someone proposes that a system should serve human values, a critic produces one awkward case (the person's preferences are manipulated, or the majority wants something monstrous, or future generations are left out), and the proposal is amended or abandoned. What I have not seen stated anywhere is the specification itself. So: write the desiderata that a theory of whose values an aligned AI ought to serve must meet in order to count as adequate -- the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the constraints it cannot violate -- with the motivating consideration behind each. I would expect the list to address at least: raw versus laundered or adaptive preferences; aggregation when values conflict across persons; the standing of future people and of non-human sentience; and the liberal demand that a powerful system not simply impose one contested conception of the good. For each desideratum, indicate whether you take it to be a genuine requirement or the prejudice of a particular camp (social-choice theorists, utilitarians, contractualists, democratic theorists), flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied -- that is the part I most want to see -- and identify which single requirement eliminates the most contenders in the current literature. A brief closing scorecard of the main approaches against the list is welcome but secondary; the criteria are the deliverable.
