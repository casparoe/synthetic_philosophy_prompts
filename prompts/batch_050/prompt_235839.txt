So I'm a PhD student in epistemology and this semester I'm TAing for a professor who runs her phil of religion seminar almost entirely through cases, and she almost never states her general view -- she just gives verdicts and lets us triangulate. I have a bet going with another TA about what she'd say on the last case of the term, and I want to settle it before Friday. Her verdicts so far: Clifford's shipowner, who stifles his doubts and talks himself into sincerely believing the ship is seaworthy -- impermissible, obviously. The mountaineer from James's essay who has to believe she can make the leap or she won't -- permissible, and she was pretty emphatic about it. Believing in a new friend's good character on thin evidence, where withholding trust would poison the friendship before it starts -- permissible. A startup founder who believes her company will beat the base rates, partly because despair would make her slack off and sink it for sure -- permissible. A fan who believes his team will win the final on no particular evidence -- impermissible, she said it's not even a hard case. And someone who comes to believe an accused stranger is guilty based on gossip, in a high-profile trial -- impermissible.

Here's the last case: someone raised in a religious tradition, for whom the question is genuinely alive, finds the evidence on both sides exactly inconclusive, and is deciding whether to believe in God -- where, unlike the mountaineer and the founder cases, the believing doesn't do anything to make it true, it just organizes their whole life. The stakes are huge and they can't really sit on the fence forever. My co-TA thinks she'll say permissible because she keeps citing the genuine-option machinery approvingly (option is live, forced, momentous, evidence can't decide). I think her actual track record is narrower than James's official test: every permissibility verdict she's given is a case where the belief either helps make itself true or is partly constitutive of the good at stake, and the religion case fails that. Who's more likely right? Tell me which rule you think she's actually running, what the main alternative rules are that also fit all six verdicts, and -- this is the part I care most about -- what case I could pose to her that would actually discriminate between the leading candidates, since I suspect the religion case itself might underdetermine it. Also, where you lean on claims about what James or Clifford actually argued versus later reconstructions, please flag what's settled scholarship and what's genuinely contested, rather than presenting some contested reading of James as the received view.
