Two blokes from my reading group had a proper row last week and I've been volunteered to settle it, so here goes. We'd been talking about that old Hofstadter picture of the mind — the 'careenium': an enormous baize table covered in zillions of tiny 'simmballs' bouncing around, whose jostling clumps into big slow 'simms', and the simms knocking into one another is what thinking is, at the level where you'd talk about symbols and ideas. The fight was about whether the symbols do any actual pushing, or whether it's simmballs all the way down.

Dan's line: simmballs all the way down. Every collision is fixed by little-ball physics; the symbol-level story ('the thought of Vienna triggered the memory of the cake') is just a handy compression for creatures like us who can't track 10^14 balls. Compare: nobody thinks 'the pressure' is an extra thing shoving the piston over and above what the molecules do. So symbols are real as patterns but causally idle.

Maya said that proves too much, because by that logic hurricanes don't knock houses down either — only molecules do — and genes don't cause eye colour. And she kept pressing this test: if the symbol had been different, the simmball trajectories would have been different. Think of Paris instead of Vienna and totally different little balls go flying. Counterfactual dependence like that just IS causation, she says, so symbols push the substrate around.

Dan's comeback: actually try running that counterfactual. You can't swap the Vienna-symbol for the Paris-symbol without swapping a squillion simmballs — there's no scenario where the symbol changes while every simmball stays put, because the symbol just is the simmballs. So her counterfactual is ill-defined, or rather it's secretly a counterfactual about simmballs wearing a symbol costume. Whereas 'if this one molecule had been elsewhere' is perfectly coherent while all the gas-level properties stay fixed. Asymmetry: low-level interventions are clean, high-level ones piggyback.

Maya granted that intervening on a symbol has to go through simmballs, but said that's true of every higher-level cause ever — you can't change the hurricane without moving molecules either — so Dan is really denying downward causation wholesale, fine, but then the only causation in the universe sits at the very bottom, and here's her trump card: the simmballs themselves are just a cartoon for neurons, which are a cartoon for molecules, which are a cartoon for quantum fields. So by his own lights his precious simmballs don't push anything around either; only quantum fields do, and maybe not even them if they supervene on something deeper. The position eats itself.

Dan said he'd happily bite that bullet: causation lives at the fundamental level, everything above it is explanation rather than causation, and explanation is allowed to be many-levelled without anything spooky going on. He accused Maya of sliding between 'cause' and 'explain', and said talk of the hurricane causing the damage is harmless loose talk precisely because it bottoms out in molecules.

Maya's last move (this is where it got loud): if the symbol level tracks real, reliable regularities — symbol S1 being followed by S2 far more reliably than any simmball-level description we could actually compute would reveal — then it's carving nature at a joint the physics-level story misses. A Laplacean demon wouldn't need it, granted, but we aren't demons, and extra predictive grip isn't fake just because a demon could do without it. Dan said predictive usefulness still isn't causal oomph. Maya said there's no 'oomph' anywhere, not even at the bottom — physics only gives you regularities too. And round they went.

So, who's right about which bit? Specifically: (1) is Dan's 'your counterfactual is ill-defined' move any good, or does it prove too much like Maya says? (2) does Maya's trump card — simmballs are cartoons all the way down — actually land, or can Dan be a fundamentalist about causation without incoherence? (3) is the explanation-vs-causation distinction Dan leans on doing genuine work here, or is it just relabelling? And overall, who's more nearly right? Also, is there anything BOTH of them are missing? I've got a vague sense that Hofstadter himself thought the answer was a genuine yes — symbols really do push simmballs around — and that this wasn't meant to be spooky, but neither of them could say how that's supposed to work without magic. If you can, spell that out too.
