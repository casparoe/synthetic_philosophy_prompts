So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, and I got into this rabbit hole about whether dreams are experiences. Apparently Norman Malcolm argued back in the 50s that dreams literally can't be experiences happening during sleep -- basically all we have is the waking report, and he thought saying "I was conscious while asleep" doesn't even make sense. Then Dennett comes along with his cassette theory: your brain pre-records a bunch of narrative material and on waking it splices in whatever fits, so the dream was never "played" at all. What confuses me is that these two seem to land in almost the same place (no experience during sleep) from totally different directions, and yet people treat this as a live debate. Meanwhile sleep science kept moving -- REM, lucid dreaming, people signaling with eye movements from inside lucid dreams -- and it doesn't seem to have settled anything.

My engineer brain says this is an argument about whether a log written on wakeup proves a process actually ran during the night, and that the two sides just disagree about what counts as valid logging. But maybe that's too glib.
