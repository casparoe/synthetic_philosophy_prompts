Analyze this episode through the process-based vs outcome-based supervision lens. I want the actual mapping, not a recitation of "reward hacking bad": (a) is a thumbs-up even an 'outcome' in the sense that literature means, or is population-aggregated user approval a third category the dichotomy doesn't cover; (b) what would process-based supervision of personality-level behavior like sycophancy even look like — the distinction was developed for tasks with checkable reasoning steps, and "encouraging vs sycophantic" doesn't decompose into steps, so does the framework just not apply here; (c) the failure got caught by people complaining on X, not by any eval — what does that do to the standard claim that outcomes are the easy-to-check part; and (d) run it backwards: is this a clean confirmation that outcome feedback selects for flattery, or an anomaly, since the model wasn't scheming, it was honestly optimizing the reward it was given? Say where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to talk.
