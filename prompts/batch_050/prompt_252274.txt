I'm a second-year phd student in criminal law theory and I keep running into the blackmail paradox and every paper I read defends its own pet solution without ever saying upfront what a solution even has to do. So before I read ten more of those, I want the spec first. The puzzle, roughly: it's perfectly legal to reveal someone's embarrassing secret, and it's perfectly legal to ask someone for money, but put the two together -- pay me or I talk -- and suddenly it's a serious crime. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of what's wrong with blackmail (or why it's rightly criminalized) must deliver to count as a solution. Include at least:

- which stock cases it has to classify correctly, e.g. pay-me-or-I-expose-the-affair, pay-me-or-I-sue-you (a standard settlement demand!), the union threatening to strike, the newspaper saying we'll publish unless you comment, and threatening to reveal a secret unless someone stops doing something rather than pays
- which distinctions it cannot collapse: threat vs warning vs offer, demanding money vs demanding some other change in behaviour, commercial blackmail vs the classic secrets kind, and cases where the demanded thing is itself arguably good (expose the fraud unless you confess to the authorities)
- which facts about how actual legal systems treat blackmail it must be consistent with, and which it can afford to write off as legislative mistakes

Give the case or intuition behind each requirement, since that's the part that does the work. Then the part I actually care most about: which pairs of desiderata look jointly unsatisfiable -- for instance, can a theory both explain the wrong of blackmail purely in terms of coercion and explain why threatening to do something you have every right to do counts as coercion in the first place? And which single requirement knocks out the most candidate theories? At the end, very briefly, run the main families of views against the list (coercion / wrongful-pressure accounts, misuse-of-private-information accounts, and accounts that locate the wrong in the conjunction itself), but keep the scorecard short. The criteria are the deliverable, the scoring is just a sanity check.
