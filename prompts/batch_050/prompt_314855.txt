I cover science and tech policy for a magazine, and after reporting trips on digital privacy in Berlin, Washington, and (once, slightly nervously) Shenzhen, there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I don't have a word for. In Berlin, my friends got their building blurred on Street View and treat the state as the thing you hide from — they'll hand a US corporation their whole life but tell the tax office the bare minimum. In DC it's inverted: everyone demands transparency from the government while streaming their location to apps without a second thought. And in China what struck me most was people *showing off* their trust scores, like a credit rating crossed with a fitness streak — not hiding from the system at all but competing to be maximally visible to it.

For a piece I'm drafting I went back to Bentham's Panopticon letters from the 1790s, and the mechanism there (paraphrasing, forgive me) is that the watched can never tell whether anyone is actually in the watchtower, so they have to behave as if watched at every moment. The Chinese setup seems to run on the opposite fuel: it works because everyone knows exactly when and how they're being measured, and the result is something you display. It's not the "privacy paradox," because these people aren't professing concern and then betraying it. It's not sousveillance either, since nobody's watching back. So: is there an established name for this configuration — surveillance that runs on voluntary, even proud, self-display, where what you conceal calibrates to who the watcher is? And how does it carve against the panopticon idea and against the German and American cases: are those three the same phenomenon with different watchers, or genuinely different things? I'm mostly after the carving, not a bare label — what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are, and if nothing established quite fits, what you'd call it and why.

Give me the answer as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no introduction and no wrap-up. Also tell me how confident you are in the identification overall, and flag which of the five points you're least sure of.
