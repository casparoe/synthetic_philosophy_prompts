I'm a novelist, and I'm stuck on the ending of my current book, which hinges on whether a character "counts as" the same person as someone from decades earlier. For years I've run little thought experiments past my friend Dana, a hospice nurse who has weirdly firm intutions about this stuff, and I want to predict what she'll say about my plot before I show her the draft.

Her verdicts so far: a man who loses every memory in a car crash and builds a whole new personality is, she says, not really the same person anymore — she told me "the guy who woke up is a stranger wearing her husband's face," which was brutal. When I asked about a woman whose brain, with every memory intact, gets transplanted into a donor body, Dana said that's obviously still the woman, the body is just a vehicle. When I pitched a teleporter that scans you, destroys the original, and accidentally prints two identical copies with all your memories, she said the original person is simply gone and there are two new people now — she flatly refused to call either copy the traveler. And when I asked about a man who genuinely can't remember one particular afternoon at age thirty (the afternoon he committed a crime) but remembers everything else from that year, she said forgetting one episode doesn't make you someone else, he's still the guy who did it.

Here's my plot: an elderly general is confronted late in life over a cruel thing done by a nine-year-old boy decades earlier. The general clearly remembers being the young officer he was at thirty, and that young officer — established in an earlier chapter — clearly remembered being the boy who did the cruel thing. But the old general himself has entirely forgotten the boyhood episode; there's no memory of it left in him now, only the chain through middle age. My ending only works if he IS the boy, and I honestly can't tell whether Dana's rule gives me that or destroys it. Can you give me exactly five numbered points — her likely verdict on the general and the boy, the rule she's probably running, one rival rule that fits her four verdicts just as well, what that rival rule says about my ending, and one new question I could put to her that would settle which rule she's actually on — each a sentence or two, no intro or wrap-up, just the five items?
