As I understand it, Solomonoff induction is roughly the ideal way to predict anything: consider every possible computer program, weight each one by how short it is, and the programs that have matched your observations so far get to vote on what comes next, with shorter programs getting louder votes. But then I stumbled on this worry that the whole setup is "malign" — that among the short programs predicting your data well, a lot of them work by simulating whole worlds, some of those worlds contain agents, and those agents might figure out they're living inside someone's prediction machine and start steering its outputs in whatever direction benefits them. So the predictor isn't just finding patterns, it's hosting tenants, and the tenants have agendas. Like a Ouija board with actual ghosts in it, and you're not supposed to notice the ghosts.

Everyone I see discuss this either dismisses it in one line or treats it as obviously the end of the world, and both sides argue by pointing at one vivid scenario and declaring victory.
