So I just got out of a four-hour argument with my frend from grad school (we do this every few months, its basically our hobby) about the gender-equality paradox — the finding that countries like Norway and Sweden, which sit at the top of all the gender equality rankings, also have some of the most gender-skewed career choices, with a smaller share of women in engineering than you see in places like Algeria or India.

Her line was the standard one: once you remove economic pressure and social constraint, underlying differences in interests (people-oriented vs. thing-oriented) get to express themselves freely, so the paradox is evidence the differences are basically real, and we should stop treating 50/50 as the default benchmark of justice.

I ended up defending the opposite reading, which I want stress-tested. Roughly my story: the paradox shows gendered choices are MORE culturally constructed, not less. In rich, egalitarian, individualistic countries, gender becomes a medium of self-expression — you have the slack to pick the job that "fits who you are," and who you are is heavily gendered because identity itself is gendered there. In poorer countries you mostly pick the job that pays. So culture isn't suppressing gender differences; it's manufacturing them, and the more affluent and self-expression-oriented the culture, the more manufacturing it does. I threw in a prediction that I'm not sure actually holds: the skew should be strongest in fields where the job is read as saying something about the person (nursing, engineering, teaching) and weakest in fields that are just jobs (accounting, logistics, admin). Might be wrong on that.

What would count against my view? I want a real map of its exposure, not a victory lap. Which observations, if they came out a certain way, should make me abandon the self-expression story versus just patch it? Walk me through stuff like: if the same skew shows up WITHIN countries over time as they became more egalitarian, does that hurt me or help me? If kids of immigrants from high-paradox countries raised in Scandinavia show the same occupational patterns as local kids, whose side does that land on? If the whole paradox turns out to be an artifact of measuring "women's share of STEM grads" versus "women's propensity to pick STEM," does my story survive the correction? And the meta-question that's bugging me since last night: does my view actually predict anything her view doesn't, or did I just redescribe her mechanism in constructivist vocabulary and call it a disagreement? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and flag which ones she should concede cut against HER position too — since I suspect some of these are friendly fire and I want ammo for next time.
