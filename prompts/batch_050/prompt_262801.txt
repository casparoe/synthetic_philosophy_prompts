"Quine's famous gavagai thought experiment shows that when a field linguist hears a native speaker say 'gavagai' while a rabbit runs by, nothing about the speaker's behavior can determine whether they mean 'rabbit,' 'undetached rabbit part,' 'rabbit stage,' or something else entirely. Since all the observable evidence is equally compatible with all these hypotheses, the linguist can never figure out what the speaker actually means by their words. Quine concluded that there is no fact of the matter about what anyone's words refer to -- meaning is indeterminate all the way down, so strictly speaking translation is impossible, and for that matter nobody ever really knows what anyone else means. This is basically an extension of the general point that scientific theories are always underdetermined by data: just as any body of evidence fits multiple theories, any body of linguistic behavior fits multiple translations, and Quine saw these as essentially the same phenomenon. That's why Quine is often read as a kind of meaning skeptic who showed that the ordinary notion of meaning is bankrupt."
