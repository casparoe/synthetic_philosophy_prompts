So a friend and I were arguing about my whole "ethical eating" setup and she said it doesn't add up, so I want an outside audit. Here's what I currently believe:

1. I'm roughly a consequentialist about this stuff: animal suffering counts the same wherever it happens, farm or forest, and more of it is worse, full stop.
2. I eat beef but not chicken. My reasoning is that per calorie, cattle farming kills maybe one animal where chicken farming kills dozens, so beef is the clearly better choice.
3. I think wild animal suffering is probably astronomical in aggregate (most wild animals die young and horribly) and that it's one of the most morally serious facts there is.
4. But I'm against actually intervening in nature to reduce it. Reengineering ecosystems, suppressing predators, that kind of thing strikes me as hubristic and near-certain to backfire, so we should leave nature alone.
5. I'm fine with crop farming even though combine harvesters and plowing kill tons of field mice, voles, ground-nesting birds etc. Those deaths are unintended side effects, whereas slaughtering a cow is deliberate killing, and that difference matters morally.
6. I think insects probably don't have much going on experientially, so I don't lose sleep over, say, pesticide use or stepping on ants.

Go through these and tell me which pairs actually conflict and how badly, and which ones only look like they conflict but turn out fine on closer inspection. For each real tension, tell me what the cheapest fix is -- which commitment I'd have to weaken or drop and what it would cost me. And then tell me which single revision would make the whole set hang together best. I'm not attached to any particular one of these so be brutal. Also, fair warning, I will push back if you just flag everything as contradictory, I want to know which tensions are load-bearing.
