I'm a second-year grad student and I have to defend my take on spacetime at our phil physics reading group next week, so I want to stress-test my commitments before people start poking at them. Quick context on the issue: the hole argument. Take any model of GR, apply a diffeomorphism that rearranges things only inside some small empty region, and you get a mathematically distinct model that agrees with the first one everywhere outside that region. If those two models describe genuinely different physical situations, then everything outside the hole fails to settle what happens inside it. Hopefully I haven't garbled that.

Here's where I've landed:

1. Scientific realism. We should read our best physics at face value. If GR treats spacetime as a dynamical thing, then spacetime is a dynamical thing. I'm not interested in instrumentalist dodges where the math is just a prediction machine.

2. Manifold substantivalism. Spacetime points exist in their own right, over and above the matter and fields located at them. Part of why I find this compelling is that the metric is itself a dynamical player in GR — it carries energy (gravitational waves), it's acted on by matter and acts back. That sure sounds like a substance, not a bookkeeping device.

3. GR is deterministic, full stop. The initial value problem is well-posed: fix the data on a Cauchy surface and the physics everywhere is fixed. This is one of the crown jewels of the theory and I'm not surrendering it because of a philosopher's thought experiment.

4. Differences that consist only in which individual plays which role are still real differences. If the universe could have been exactly as it is except with you and me swapping all our properties, those are two genuinely distinct possible worlds. Same goes for spacetime points.

5. The hole argument is overhyped. Physicists ignored it for decades and got on fine. It's a puzzle about how we represent spacetime, not a discovery about physics itself.

So: does this hang together? Which pairs (or triples) are in genuine tension, and how bad is each — rank them by severity rather than telling me they're all equally problematic. For each real tension, tell me the cheapest fix and what it costs. Also flag any pairings that look tense but actually dissolve once you're careful, because I have a sneaking suspiscion at least one of my worries is misplaced. And if there's a single revision that cleans up the most mess, name it. Don't give me a survey of every position in the literature — audit MY set and tell me what it commits me to.
