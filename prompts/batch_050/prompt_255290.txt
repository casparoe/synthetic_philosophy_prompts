Some background first: I'm a retired experimental physicist, and since hanging up my lab coat I've been slowly working my way through philosophy, currently the debate about what the proper currency of egalitarian justice is -- welfare, resources, capabilities, that whole argument. What got me chewing on it is something much closer to home. My granddaughter has dyslexia, and her university gives her time-and-a-half on exams plus a separate quiet room. The interesting part is the official justification, which is framed entirely as a measurement matter: the extra time is supposed to make sure the exam measures her grasp of the material rather than her decoding speed. That is exactly how we would have talked in my old lab -- you recalibrate the instrument, you don't tamper with the quantity you're trying to measure. But the practice has curious edges. She recieves no extra time on the timed translation exercise in her Italian course, because there reading speed counts as part of the skill being tested. Nobody offers her money instead of the quiet room. Nobody checks whether the accommodations actually leave her as happy or as confident as her classmates. And nobody would dream of giving extra time to a student who is simply bad at exams.

So here is my question. What conception of equality, if any, does this whole accommodations regime presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory for me. Does the instrument-correction rationale line up with any of the standard currencies, or is it quietly relying on a fourth thing -- a view about what exams are for, and what would count as corrupting them? Where would the measurement rationale and a genuine justice rationale come apart, i.e. cases where recalibrating the instrument gives more, or less, than equality would demand? Which rival views does the practice rule out without ever saying so -- what would the university have to do differently if it took a pure welfare line, say, or handed out resources instead of accommodations? And where is the practice simply philosophically underdetermined, compatible with several of the currencies at once? Please keep the whole answer under 450 words.
