I work as a software engineer and, on the side, have been reading up on consciousness science for a reading group with some colleagues. Before our next meeting I wrote down my current position, and I'd like an audit of whether it hangs together. Here is where I've landed:

1. I'm persuaded by overflow-style arguments (the Sperling paradigm and the like) that phenomenal consciousness outruns access consciousness: at any moment, my experience contains more than I can attend to or report.
2. I think global workspace theory is the best current scientific theory of consciousness -- consciousness is, roughly, content that gets globally broadcast and made available to the system's consumers.
3. Methodologically, I hold that report (verbal or behavioral) is the only legitimate evidence for attributing consciousness, whether to animals, patients, or machines; without reportability, a consciousness attribution is untestable.
4. I'm a functionalist: whether a system is conscious depends on its computational organization, not its substrate, so a silicon system implementing the right architecture could in principle be conscious.
5. From the inside, I'm confident my experience is rich in the un-attended periphery -- I seem to see the whole cluttered desk, not just the mug I'm focused on.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Please rank any real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs, and note any pair that looks problematic but dissolves on inspection. Please answer in American English.
