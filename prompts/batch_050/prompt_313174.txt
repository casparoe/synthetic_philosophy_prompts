I've covered physics and philosophy of science for a general-interest magazine for about a decade, and I'm drafting a long essay defending a particular stance on the interpretation of quantum mechanics. Before I commit it to print, I want to audit my own position for internal consistency. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I'm broadly an Everettian. The universal quantum state evolves unitarily at all times; collapse is the unnecessary addition to an otherwise clean formalism, and the many-worlds picture is just what you get when you refuse to add it.

(2) The Born rule must not be postulated either. I take the probabilities to be derivable from the unitary formalism plus constraints on rational belief — roughly the decision-theoretic approach — so that someone who refuses to weight branches by their squared amplitudes is making an epistemic error, not entertaining different physics.

(3) I reject Bohmian mechanics as ad hoc. It posits particle configurations and an equilibrium condition that no experiment will ever reveal, and I hold that a theory earns belief in its extra structure only when that structure does explanatory work we could in principle check.

(4) Decoherence explains the emergence of the classical world from the quantum one — why we get stable, definite-seeming records in a preferred basis — though I grant that by itself it does not pick out a unique outcome.

(5) Bell's theorem, together with the experimental violations of the Bell inequalities, rules out local theories; and one of the things I find attractive about Everett is that it restores locality.

My worry is that this package doesn't hang together, and I'd rather find out from you than from my referees or my commenters. So: which of these commitments pull against each other, and which pairs are only apparently in tension and dissolve once you're careful about what each thesis actually says? Please order the genuine tensions by severity. For each one, tell me exactly what would have to give, what the cheapest repair is, and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the position — including whether it forces me to concede anything to the Bohmians or the collapse theorists that I'd rather not concede. For any apparent tension, show me the disambiguation that makes it go away, since I'll want that paragraph for the essay. Don't assume you know which commitment I'd sacrifice; I'm asking you to trace the dependencies, not to guess my loyalties.
