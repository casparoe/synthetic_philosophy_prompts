So I help run the film series at our local library and this month we screened Amadeus, which I'd seen years ago but had forgotten half of. Afterwards we got into this long argument in the lobby about the Salieri problem -- the movie more or less paints him as a mediocre schemer who poisons Mozart out of envy, and as far as I can tell the historical Salieri was a respected composer who got along fine with Mozart and even taught his kid. One guy in our group said it doesn't matter at all because they're both dead two hundred years and it's a great film, and someone else pushed back hard and said the fact that the real Salieri can't defend himself is exactly why it matters more, not less.

So here's what I'd actually like: read Amadeus through the philosophical question of what, if anything, we owe the real dead when we dramatize them. Take seriously the strongest versions of both sides from our lobby fight -- the idea that the dead have no interests left to harm so slandering Salieri is a victimless transformation in service of art, versus the idea that reputation is one of the few things that survives us and a hugely popular film is uniquely positioned to bury the real person under the fictional one. But don't just map the debate onto the film mechanically. The film itself seems to complicate things in ways I found interesting: it's framed as the old Salieri's confession, so it's arguably a story about envy and mediocrity wearing historical costumes rather than a claim about what actually happend, and nobody involved ever pretended it was a documentary. Does that framing let it off the hook, or does the "based on real people" veneer make it worse by borrowing credibility it hasn't earned? And where does the film genuinely resist the ethical reading -- where would I be distorting the movie to score a philosophical point?

End by saying whether Amadeus, as a specific case, adds anything to the general debate about historical fiction and the dead that the abstract arguments alone don't get you. Write it in American English, and no need for any citations or bibliography -- I just want the argument.
