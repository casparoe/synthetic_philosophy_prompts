I'm a journalist working on a longform essay about self-reference, and I want the analytical core of the piece to be a worked case rather than a tour of examples. The case I've chosen is the crisis that Gottlob Frege's Grundgesetze der Arithmetik underwent between 1893 and 1903. Here are the facts as I'll present them to readers, and you may treat them as stipulated for the analysis: Frege spent decades trying to derive arithmetic from pure logic alone. The second volume of his system rested in part on a principle (his Basic Law V) that effectively assigned to every concept an object -- its extension -- that could itself fall under concepts, so that the logical machinery could range over the very things the machinery had generated. In 1902, shortly before the second volume appeared, Bertrand Russell wrote to Frege pointing out that the system licenses the formation of the extension of the concept extension that does not fall under itself, and that asking whether this extension falls under itself yields a contradiction from either answer. Frege added an appendix conceding that the foundations of his project had been shaken, and the program of reducing arithmetic to his logic collapsed in its original form. The subsequent repairs -- Russell's own theory of types, and later axiomatic set theories that restricted which collections count as sets -- deliberately rebuilt the barrier Frege had let fall. And then, in 1931, Kurt Gödel showed that even carefully stratified formal systems of arithmetic can be made to speak about their own provability, via a systematic coding of symbols and proofs as numbers, and that this controlled self-reference yields sentences the system can neither prove nor disprove.

The theoretical lens I want applied is the tangled hierarchy and the inviolate level, in roughly the sense Douglas Hofstadter develops in Gödel, Escher, Bach using Escher's Drawing Hands as the emblem: a hierarchy is tangled when something at one level turns out to manipulate, represent, or constitute the level above or below it, so that the ranking of levels loops back on itself; an inviolate level is one that the tangle can never reach -- the level of the artist, the paper, the rules of the game -- which stands outside the loop and is what makes the loop coherent rather than merely contradictory. In Drawing Hands, the two hands draw each other, but the lithograph and Escher are untouchable.

Here is what I need from you. First, map the apparatus onto the episode with discipline: identify precisely what plays the role of the levels in Frege's system (concepts versus objects versus extensions, or however you carve it), state exactly which move tangled them, and explain why this tangle was lethal rather than fertile -- why Russell's construction detonates the system while Escher's tangle merely delights. Second, treat the repairs as engineering responses: in what sense do type theory and restricted set comprehension re-establish an inviolate level, and did they actually succeed, or did they merely relocate the tangle? Address Gödel here specifically: his arithmetization looks like a deliberate, licensed crossing of the level between a formal system and talk about that system, executed inside the system itself. Is Gödel's construction a counterexample to the claim that the repairs restored inviolate levels, or is it a demonstration that a level-crossing can be safe provided it runs in only one direction (the system can absorb facts about itself, but cannot be redefined by them)? Say which reading you find more defensible and why. Third, run the analysis in the other direction: what does this episode do to the theory? The Drawing Hands framing celebrates tangles as the source of meaning and mind, but here the tangle was pure destruction, and the creative construction (Gödel's) was achieved by the most careful level-management imaginable. Does the theory actually possess the resources to distinguish generative tangles from destructive ones, or is that distinction a gap in it? And if the inviolate level in this case turned out to be, not a level inside mathematics, but the mathematicians standing outside it choosing which principles to keep, does that vindicate the theory or show that it was never really about formal systems at all? Finally, be explicit about where the framework falls silent: identify at least one feature of the episode that the tangled-hierarchy lens cannot illuminate without being forced, and say what a better-suited framework would capture instead.
