I am a PhD student in political science writing a dissertation on participation in mass atrocity, and I keep returning to the Browning–Goldhagen dispute of the 1990s over Reserve Police Battalion 101. Browning, drawing heavily on Milgram-style situational psychology, argued that ordinary middle-aged German men, not selected for fanaticism, became killers through conformity, deference to authority, peer pressure, and gradual habituation. Goldhagen replied that the battalion's behavior is explained by a specifically German eliminationist antisemitism that made killing Jews intelligible and even voluntary, pointing to the same archival record: the offer to opt out, the cruelty beyond orders, the killings continued without supervision. Three decades later the framing of the debate seems frozen — situation versus disposition — and each new case study in the perpetrator literature gets filed under one side or the other without moving anyone. My own hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis, is that the two sides were partly answering different questions: Browning asked why the men who participated did so, while Goldhagen asked why Germany produced a genocidal project at all, and that the level-of-analysis gap let each treat the other's evidence as compatible with their own account. But I am not confident this fully explains the persistence, since the two did directly contest the same episodes, such as the initial opportunity to withdraw from the Józefów massacre. Please diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is: verbal, empirical but underdetermined by shared archives, a clash between historical and psychological standards of evidence, or a dispute about which variation needs explaining (between nations, between units, or between individuals within one unit). Then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress — for instance, whether individual-level variation in participation within the battalion is the decisive test, whether later work on other perpetrator units has already quietly resolved it, or whether the debate should simply be dissolved.
