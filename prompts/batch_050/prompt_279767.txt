Last time I taught this it fell completely flat: I explained the trilemma on the whiteboard, students nodded, and it was obvious nobody actually felt why "every belief needs a reason" generates a real problem rather than just an annoying game for pedants. So I want to design an in-class activity or game, runnable in about 50-60 minutes, where the students run headfirst into the regress themselves BEFORE anyone names it. My rough seed idea: some kind of structured "justification chain" game where students have to defend claims (maybe real small-stakes ones, like the group deciding something they actually care about, so the justifications matter to them) and the rules force them to keep supplying reasons -- so that they personally experience the moment where you either circle back to something you already said, hit bedrock and just... stop, or keep going forever. Ideally the game should have rounds or rule variants so that all three responses to the regress each get their moment where they seem attractive, and their costs get felt too (e.g. foundationalism should feel dogmatic at some point, coherentism should feel like cheating, infinitism should feel paralyzing). Start with a one-sentence pitch of the activity as your first line, then the details -- no preamble, no restating my question back at me.
