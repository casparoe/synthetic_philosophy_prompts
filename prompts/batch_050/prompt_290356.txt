I am looking for the established term, if there is one, for an intrapersonal analogue of the objection that one person’s gain cannot simply cancel another person’s loss. In one case, an hour of agony at age twenty is justified by greater pleasure at age sixty. In another, an irreversible harm is imposed now for opportunities available decades later, even though the later stage of the person will remember little of the earlier one and care about very different things. The question is not whether prudence permits future discounting, whether the later outcome is impersonally better, or whether the later stage is literally someone else. It is whether the later benefit can compensate the earlier stage when no single momentary subject experiences both. Is there an accepted label for this “separateness within a life” position, or is it merely an illicit transfer of the interpersonal case? Please distinguish it from time-slice egoism, time-relative interests, and reductionism about personal identity, and specify the boundary: how much psychological continuity is needed before ordinary prudential balancing becomes legitimate?
