I'm putting together a seminar unit on the ethics of risk and I keep going back and forth between two views, so I'd like you to settle it for me by writing the joint statement the two camps could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. Camp one is the consent-first view: imposing a non-trivial risk of harm on someone without their consent is pro tanto wrongful, even if the risk never materializes and nobody ends up hurt -- so the reckless driver whose gamble came off still wronged the pedestrians. Camp two is the expected-harm view: risk imposition is only wrong insofar as it worsens expected outcomes, so a risk that harms no one and was justified ex ante by sufficient benefit isn't wrong at all, and "wronging someone by endangering them" is really just wronging them by exposing them to a bad gamble that could have been better chosen.

Two complications I want the statement to grapple with rather than gesture at. First, basically all social activity -- driving, building, flying, emitting -- imposes small risks on strangers without their consent, so the consent-first side seems to court paralysis; how do both sides handle the de minimis / threshold question, and is there a formulation of a threshold that neither side has to treat as ad hoc? Second, risks of risks: a policy might create a 10% chance of later subjecting someone to a 50% risk. Does the consent-first camp need consent for the higher-order risk too (and if so, does the regress stop anywhere), and can the expected-harm camp just multiply the probabilities without losing something it cares about?

Format it as a numbered list of agreed claims, then a disagreements section where each side's position is stated in words that side would actually accept, then the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp -- and for each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal (e.g. both sides actually agree on which acts are permissible and only disagree about what to call the wrong-making feature), say so explicitly.
