I've been thinking about whether our past selves should get to bind our future selves, in the context of advance directives for medical care, and I keep going back and forth. Here are my gut verdicts on some cases, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, unifies them.

(1) At 35, healthy and clearheaded, a woman signs a directive saying that if she is ever in a permanent coma with no realistic hope of recovery, she should not be kept on life support. At 60 she is in exactly that state and never updated the document. I think it should be honored.

(2) A man repeatedly says throughout his life, including in a formal directive, that he would rather die than live with advanced dementia. He now has advanced dementia, no longer remembers the directive, and by all appearances is content -- he enjoys meals, music, and visits, and resists nothing. I think it would be monstrous to withhold ordinary care or hasten his death on the strength of the document.

(3) A woman with early dementia, still lucid in many respects, tells her doctor she retracts the no-resuscitation directive she signed five years earlier. Her adult children say she was already impaired when she retracted it and that the original document should stand. My gut says the retraction should win, but I'm uneasy, because if it wins, I don't see why a still-later, deeper-in-dementia version of her couldn't "retract" again.

(4) A 25-year-old in the grip of a severe depressive episode writes a detailed refusal of all future life-sustaining treatment. He recovers, never tears it up, and years later is brought in unconscious after an accident. I think the directive should carry no weight, even though it was written just as formally as the one in case (1).

Is there a single principle about when the earlier self's word should govern that delivers all four verdicts, or does something have to give? If a principle can't save all of them, please say which verdict you'd sacrifice and why, and what your favored principle predicts for a case I haven't listed -- for instance, a dementia patient who verbally asks for comfort care only, contradicting her directive demanding everything be done. I'd also ask that you state how confident you are in your conclusions and single out the claim you are least sure of.
