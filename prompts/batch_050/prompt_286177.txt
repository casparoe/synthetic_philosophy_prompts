So for my civics/social studies elective we have to write a "consistency check" on our own worldview and I figured I'd get a second opinion first. Here's roughly where I'm at on the whole success thing:

(1) Jantelagen — that Scandinavian "don't think you're better than anyone" norm — and tall poppy syndrome in general are toxic. Cutting people down just for standing out is envy, and it makes everyone hide what they're good at.
(2) The American Dream is basically true and worth teaching to kids: anyone can make it with talent and hard work, thats the whole point of a fair society.
(3) Meritocracy talk is mostly a myth — success is way more about luck (where you're born, who your parents know, right place right time) than anyone admits.
(4) We should celebrate successful people way more than we do — hold up founders, athletes, top students as role models and make a big deal of them.
(5) My uncle who got rich off selling his app at exactly the right moment fully deserves every cent, I'd say it to his face.

Which of these actually clash, which only look like they do, and which are fine together? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say what I'd have to give up to fix it. Then tell me the single cheapest revision that makes the whole set consistent — don't just say "it depends on definitions," actually pick one.
