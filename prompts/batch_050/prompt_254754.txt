ok so I was rereading some stuff on levels of description and built this argument that I think proves way too much but I can't find the crack. Setup first so we're on the same page: picture a system with two descriptions. Down below there's the substrate -- tiny parts banging into each other, call them the smal balls, each one mindless, each collision fully determined by physics. Up above there's a coarse-grained description where certain stable configurations of smal balls count as symbols, and the symbols seem to push each other around in meaningful ways, like one thought leading to another. The claim people like to make is that BOTH levels are real and the top level genuinely does causal work.

Now the argument:

(1) Every event in the system is completely fixed by events at the bottom level -- the trajectory of each smal ball is determined by the other smal balls plus physics, full stop.
(2) If the bottom-level story leaves no gaps, then there is no causal work left over for anything else to do. Whatever happens happens whether or not the symbol-level description is true of the system.
(3) So the symbols don't cause anything. They're just a pattern we project onto the churn -- a useful shorthand at best, like saying "the storm headed north" when really it's just air molecules.
(4) But my own thinking sure *seems* like symbols doing work: the thought that it's raining seems to cause the thought to grab an umbrella, and the umbrella-grabbing thought seems to cause my arm to move.
(5) Worse, the argument doesn't stop at thoughts. The same premises show neurons don't cause anything (the physics does), that aspirin doesn't relieve headaches (the molecules do), and that my keystrokes right now aren't caused by me wanting to make this point.
(6) So either (4) and (5) are all illusions and basically nothing above quarks ever causes anything, or one of (1)-(3) has to go. I refuse to accept the first disjunct.

Which step do I reject? I have a soft spot for (1) since it just seems like what physics says, so I'd rather lose (2) or (3), but I want honesty not comfort. For the step you pick: say exactly why it looked airtight, what rejecting it commits me to (am I stuck with causes that violate physics? double causation? magic?), and whether the fix also rescues the aspirin and the neurons or only the thoughts.
