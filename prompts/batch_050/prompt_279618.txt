Daniel Goldhagen replied that the killers were not ordinary men at all but ordinary Germans, products of a uniquely German culture of eliminationist antisemitism, so deep-rooted and peculiar to Germany that the perpetrators killed eagerly and believed they were doing right. Browning's view won the scholarly debate because it was better supported by the battalion's records, while Goldhagen's book only sold because it told people what they wanted to hear; so the episode shows that situational explanations beat cultural explanations of atrocity in general."
