I'm a senior in high school and I run our philosophy club's small interview series, where we invite a local academic and I moderate in front of maybe thirty students. Next month's guest is a law professor who defends Joseph Raz's conception of the rule of law: that it is a purely formal and instrumental ideal, on a par with the sharpness of a knife -- it makes law good at doing whatever law does, including evil, and so a wicked legal system can fully comply with it. I'd like you to draft eight to ten interview questions.

The problem is that my first draft of questions was all softball, and the guest will see them coming. I want questions that press where the view actually has to pay a price. Please include at least one question on each of the following: whether a legal system built for racial persecution could really satisfy the formal criteria, and if so whether it's misleading to keep calling compliance an achievement worth the name; Lon Fuller's claim that his eight desiderata carry an inner morality, so that fully respecting them is harder to reconcile with evil law than Raz allows; Jeremy Waldron's complaint that Raz's list says a lot about rules but almost nothing about courts, hearings, and the chance to argue your side, and whether leaving out the procedural, argumentative side of law guts the ideal; and what Raz's view says about cases where judges have punished people under retroactive or strained law in the name of justice, as with prosecutions after a regime falls -- whether the rule of law is just one value among others there, and what that admission costs.

For each question, give me the follow-up keyed to the reply the guest will most plausibly give, since he'll have ready answers to the first move in each exchange. My audience is mostly students who haven't read Raz, so each question needs a one-sentence setup that makes the stakes intelligible without dumbing it down.

I also don't want a menu. Please commit to an order for the questions that builds a single line of attack, tell me which of these fronts you judge most damaging to Raz's position and why, and -- if after working through it you think the view actually survives all of this -- say so plainly and tell me which objection you'd concede is weaker than it looks.
