I run a graduate reading group in formal epistemology, and two of my regulars have been circling each other all year. One defends an objective Bayesianism in the Jaynesian tradition: for a given state of knowledge, there is a rationally mandated prior, and the maximum entropy principle -- pick the distribution of greatest Shannon entropy consistent with your constraints -- is the canonical way to find it. The other is a subjective Bayesian who grants that rationality requires coherence under updating but denies that any prior is more rational than another consistent one; on her view, maxent at best repackages subjective choices as objective ones, because you must still choose the hypothesis space, the constraints, and the underlying measure or parametrization relative to which entropy is maximized. They have agreed to write a joint statement, and I want you to draft it for them.

The document should have four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both parties can sign: what the maxent formalism provably delivers given fixed constraints and a fixed reference measure, where it reduces to a uniform distribution, what its known failures of language-invariance look like in the Bertrand-style cases, and which theorems about coherence and updating neither side disputes. Second, a disagreements section in which each position is stated in wording its own defender would accept -- no straw formulations -- covering at least whether partial ignorance singles out a unique rational credence function, whether the choice of parametrization in maxent is an objective matter (for instance via transformation-group invariance considerations) or a disguised subjective input, and whether maxent-style constraint-imposition is genuinely different from ordinary conditionalization on evidence. Third, a cruxes section: for each disagreement, a concrete case or result both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Include at least these two: a dice factory known only to produce dice with mean roll 3.8, where the objectivist prescribes the maxent distribution on that single moment constraint -- what does the subjectivist say is left out, and what would each predict about long-run frequencies if such dice were actually manufactured? And a cube factory whose output has side length between 1 and 2 cm, where asking for the probability that volume exceeds 3 cubic centimeters yields different answers under uniformity in side versus uniformity in volume -- have each side state exactly how it handles the ambiguity and what would count as resolving it.

Fourth, close the statement with two things: an honest accounting of which parts of their dispute, if any, are verbal rather than substantive (I suspect the phrase state of ignorance is doing equivocal work for them, but let the document decide), and, for each party, a description of an argument, theorem, or empirical pattern that would move them -- for example, what would convince the subjectivist that some priors are rationally privileged, and what would convince the objectivist that every apparently objective maxent solution smuggles in a choice the principle cannot itself justify. Where no such mover exists for one side, the document should say so plainly rather than inventing one.
