Design me an in-class activity where the students' own behavior during the session ends up varying with some trivial situational factor — time pressure, a cheap mood prime, whether someone's watching, whatever you think works — so that when I put the results on the board, they discover their choices tracked the situation way more than any stable "character" they thought they brought into the room. Constraints: it has to run in a normal classroom with zero budget (points, tokens, or self-reported dice rolls are fine, no real money), the manipulation has to stay covert until the debreif, and it has to produce actual visible data — per-condition numbers I can write up and compare, not just vibes. Give me: the setup and what to tell them up front (i.e. the cover story), the exact rules and payoff scheme, a rough minute-by-minute timeline, and then a debrief script that maps what just happened onto the situationist critique — including where the virtue ethicists push back (cross-situational consistency, the "these are just college sophomores doing trivial tasks" objection, etc.) so they see the critique has real teeth but isn't an instant knockout.

Also: tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of the actual research the activity can't reproduce and that I'll just have to lecture on.
