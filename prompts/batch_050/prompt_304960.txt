About eight years ago I lent my younger brother a decent chunk of money, around four grand, to help him "get back on his feet." Turned out a lot of it went to betting apps and I never saw most of it again. It caused a huge fight, my parents took sides, and it basically poisoned family gatherings for two years. After all that I made myself a hard rule: I do not lend money to family, period, no exceptions. And honestly the rule has been great for me. A cousin asked once, an uncle asked another time, I just said sorry, it's my rule, and there was no agonizing, no guilt spiral, done. I started thinking rules like this are just a smart way to live, you figure out the lesson once and then you never have to re-litigate it every time.

Now my sister has asked me for a loan and its messing with my head. Her situation is nothing like my brother's. Her kid needs an expensive dental procedure that insurance won't fully cover, she's always been careful with money, and she even offered to sign something and pay interest. Every detail feels different. But my best friend, who watched the whole brother thing go down, says the moment I start making exceptions I've got no rule at all, just vibes, and I'll talk myself into bad decisions exactly like I did last time. His line is that a rule you override whenever it feels wrong isn't a rule, it's just you doing whatever you felt like doing anyway and dressing it up.

So here's what I keep going around in circles on. When I look at my sister's case and think "this is different," am I being thoughtful and fair, or am I just rationalizing because she's the sibling I like more? If I say yes to her, was it then wrong of me to flatly refuse my cousin three years ago without even hearing him out? And more generally I guess: is living by firm rules the honest, consistent way to be, since people are great at fooling themselves case by case, or is following a rule even when the situation in front of you clearly calls for something else just a way of avoiding actually thinking? I want to figure out which of these two instincts is the better one to trust, because I have a feeling the answer matters way beyond this one loan.
