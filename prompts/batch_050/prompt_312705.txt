so i run the philosophy society at my uni and we've been circling the hard problem of consciousness for like two years now, and I've started to suspect the debate isn't actually a debate anymore. every term it's the same loop: someone presents the knowledge argument or the zombie conceivability thing, the physicalists say conceivability proves nothing and point at all the neuroscience correlations, the dualists say correlation isn't identity, the illusionist in the corner says there's no phenomenal consciousness to explain, just introspective models, and the panpsychist asks why the physicalists get to assume matter is experientially dead in the first place. nobody updates. nobody even seems to update *privately* -- the people i know who've changed their minds on this changed them in year one and have been parked ever since.

what i want is a diagnosis, not another verdict on who's right. is this actually one disagreement? my tentative hypothesis (feel free to demolish it) is that the camps don't even share a referent for the word "phenomenal consciousness" -- the illusionist is denying the existence of a thing the dualist experiences as undeniable, and they're not disagreeing about a property of a shared target, they're talking past each other about which target exists. sort of like how a debate about whether atlantis was rich isn't a debate about atlantis's economy if one side thinks atlantis never existed. but maybe that's too quick, because both sides at least agree on the *functional* facts (attention, reportability, integration), so there IS common ground somewhere -- the question is whether that common ground is enough to make it a genuine disagreement with a resolution, or whether the residual dispute after you fix all the functional facts is a clash of bedrock intuitions that no argument could in principle move.

also worth addressing: if the disagreement is really about what counts as data -- first-person phenomenal seemings vs third-person measurements -- does that make it an epistemological dispute wearing a metaphysical costume? and is there any conceivable empirical result (a perfect neural correlate? a consciousness-meter?) that would settle it, or would each camp just redescribe the result in their own terms, which would itself be evidence for my "no shared referent" hypothesis?

concrete examples of what each side actually says are very welcome, and if your diagnosis implies the question itself is ill-posed, i want to hear that too rather than a diplomatic both-sides answer.
