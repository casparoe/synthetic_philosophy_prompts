So I'm prepping for a cosmology reading group and we're doing a session on the Boltzmann brain problem -- the worry that in a universe that lasts forever in a boring heat-death state, random thermal fluctuations should eventually produce lone conscious brains with false memories, and that these should vastly outnumber ordinary evolved observers like us, so on some self-locating reasoning I should expect to be one.

Here are the responses I already know about, so please don't give me any of these:

- The cognitive instability / self-undermining response: if I were a Boltzmann brain, my memories and reasoning would be unreliable junk, so the belief undercuts its own justification.
- The measure response: the conclusion that brains dominate only follows from particular measures over eternal-inflation multiverses, so the whole problem is an artefact of measure choice.
- The cosmological escape routes: maybe dark energy decays, the vacuum tunnells to a lower state, or the universe recollapses, so there's no eternal fluctuating phase for brains to dominate in.
- Rejecting typicality: the argument needs some principle saying I should reason as if I'm a random draw from the observers matching my evidence, and one can just deny that principle.
- Page-style arguments from continued order: if I were a minimal fluctuation, my experiences should dissolve into chaos in the next instant, and the fact that they don't is evidence I'm not one.

As far as I can tell that's basically everything standard, so I want something genuinely new: an objection to (or possibly a dissolution of) the Boltzmann brain argument that isn't on the list, isn't a rewording or special case of anything on it, and doesn't just combine two items from it. It also can't depend on some obscure bit of physics I'd have to take on trust -- it should work with the rough picture above.

Then, and this is the important bit, tell me honestly whether your objection is actually any good: does it hold up against the obvious replies, or is it the kind of thing that sounds clever at a reading group and falls apart by Thursday?
