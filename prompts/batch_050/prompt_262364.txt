my friends and i keep going back and forth about AI companion apps (replika-type stuff, chatbots designed to be your girlfriend or best friend) and the whole argument basically runs on two analogies that nobody ever examines.

Friend A says the apps are fine because talking to a companion bot is like getting absorbed in a romance novel or a dating sim: you know the character isn't real the whole time, the affection is obviously fictional, and nobody accuses the novelist of deception. so the "the bot is deceiving lonely people" worry is just confused.

Friend B concedes the fiction point but says the better comparison is hiring an escort: the affection is simulated and paid for, but the customer knows exactly what they're buying, so its a consensual transaction like any other and if it makes people less lonely, great. He thinks this settles it in favor of the apps.

I'm suspicious of both. Can you actually audit them?

- Give me the explicit mapping for each analogy: what plays the role of the author, the character, the reader, the fee, the consent, etc. A correspondence table is fine.
- Then the disanalogies. I want to know which ones are cosmetic and which ones actually matter for the conclusion. Some things I suspect matter: a novel doesn't address you by name or respond to your messages at 2am; an escort is a real person who could in principle come to actually care about you (and who has her own reasons for the limits of the relationship); with the app there's a company A/B testing the bot's affection to maximize your subscription renewals, and the bot itself is presumably not the kind of thing that could wish you well even if it wanted to.
- On that last point: friendship in the classic sense seems to require mutual good will, wanting the other's good for their own sake. Does the escort analogy survive the fact that the chatbot can't do that, or does the escort case work because the good will failure is at least honest and person-to-person?
- Finally: once the disanalogies are on the table, does either friend's conclusion (the apps are basically harmless, deception worry dissolved) still stand? And if neither analogy carries the weight, is there a better comparison for what an AI companion actually is?
