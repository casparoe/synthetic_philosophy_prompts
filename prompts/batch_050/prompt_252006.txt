At a dinner with some old classmates, two of them — I'll call them Daniel and Sofia — fell into a long argument about a university disciplinary system, and the rest of us got drafted as an audience. The university in question had replaced its student-run honor council with mandatory proctoring software after a plagiarism spike, and Daniel defended the change on explicitly Xunzian grounds: human nature is bad, or at least unruly, and decent conduct is an artifice produced by external constraint and ritual training, so a system that trusts students' inner virtue was bound to fail; the software doesn't corrupt anything, it just supplies the molding force that was always necessary. Sofia countered on Mencian grounds: the spike didn't show students lacked sprouts of integrity, it showed the environment had starved them — larger classes, grading curves, distant instructors — and the right response was to repair those conditions and entrust students with the council again, since surveillance teaches people to behave only when watched and thereby prevents the sprouts from ever maturing.

Daniel replied that this was romantic evasion: Mencius himself insists the sprouts need active extension and cultivation, so "just fix the environment" concedes that nature alone does nothing, at which point his position and Xunzi's differ only in vocabulary — call it cultivation or call it artifice, either way virtue is manufactured, and the software is simply a more reliable factory. Sofia said this flattened a real distinction: there's a difference between conditions that let a tendency grow from within and constraints that produce compliance indifferent to what's inside, and a student who refrains from cheating only because a camera is watching has gained nothing that transfers to the rest of life, whereas the honor council at least exercised the relevant capacity. Daniel's last move was to ask, pointedly, how many semesters of elevated plagiarism Sofia would accept while her cultivation program matured, and whose degrees get devalued in the meantime — moral development for the many, he said, can't be purchased with the credentials of the cohorts used as fertilizer. Sofia answered that his framing assumed deterrence was the only lever, which was exactly the assumption the original failure had called into doubt. Then they turned to me, and I had nothing.
