Theres a long-running standoff in consciousness science between higher-order and first-order theorists: is a perceptual state conscious because some separate higher-order state (roughly, prefrontal) represents it, or is being conscious just a feature of the first-order representation itself (posterior, recurrent, take your pick)? What gets me is that both sides now cite basically the same evidence — no-report paradigms, prefrontal lesion and stimulation studies, decoding work — and draw opposite conclusions from it, and the debate has barely moved in twenty years despite several adversarial collaborations designed to settle it. My working suspicion is that this isn't really an empirical disagreement but a definitional one: higher-order people seem to be operationalising consciousness as availability for report and rational access, first-order people as phenomenal feel, so each side's decisive finding is the other side's confound. Is that diagnosis right, or is there a genuine empirical or formal question buried underneath (e.g. what prefrontal activity is actually doing, whether overflow cases are coherent)?
