So I'm a backend engineer (10 yrs, distributed systems, zero philosophy training) and next month I'm giving one of our internal "big ideas" talks at work. My talk is about whether AI systems like the ones we build could ever deserve moral consideration -- not whether they're smart, whether it could ever be wrong to treat them as pure tools.

The whole talk is built around a chunk of Descartes' Discourse on Method (Part V, 1637) that I stumbled on. He imagines machines that look and act exactly like us, and says there'd still be two dead giveaways they're not really one of us: first, they could never use words flexibly to genuinely declare their thoughts the way even a cognitively impaired human can, and second, any machine can only do the specific things its parts were arranged for, whereas human reason is (in his words, roughly) a universal instrument that works in all kinds of situations. So his bet is basically: conversation plus general-purpose adaptability is the line between a real someone and a fancy automaton.

My thesis: Descartes accidentally wrote the spec we all now use, his two tests are exactly what everyone points at with LLMs, and that if a system ever actually passed both tests in the strong sense he meant -- not benchmark-passing, but the real flexible thing -- then refusing it any moral standing just because of what it's made of is pure substrate prejudice. I basically argue Descartes got the tests right and the conclusion wrong.

The audience is the problem: ~40 engineers, mostly backend/infra, a handful of ML people, one ex-biologist, nobody with philosophy background. This crowd's religion is "it's just matrix multiplication" and "you're anthropomorphizing the autocomplete." They are smart, bored by hype, and will absolutely smell it if I hand-wave.

Also tell me where Descartes himself is doing more work in my argument than I realize -- like, am I just assuming his tests track moral status rather than intelligence, because typing it out now I'm not sure that's the same thing. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
