I work with causal models and am fluent in the vocabulary of directed acyclic graphs, counterfactuals, interventions, mediators, selection effects, and transportability. Restate Nietzsche’s genealogical challenge to morality in that vocabulary as precisely as you can.

To prevent the translation from collapsing several different claims into one, distinguish three questions:

1. **The origin estimand:** which historical and psychological conditions generated the moral vocabulary and dispositions Nietzsche discusses?
2. **The persistence estimand:** which mechanisms preserve those dispositions after the original conditions have changed or disappeared?
3. **The warrant estimand:** what follows, if anything, for the authority of a moral commitment once its origins and persistence mechanisms have been identified?

Translate slave revolt, ressentiment, the ascetic ideal, internalisation, and bad conscience into this framework. For each, say whether it is best modelled as an exposure, mediator, moderator, latent disposition, selection mechanism, or something else. Where Nietzsche’s types of person resist treatment as well-defined populations, or where the relevant intervention is not even hypothetically manipulable, say so rather than forcing the concept into a causal diagram.

The most important part is the residue report. Separate:

- what translates cleanly;
- what translates only by distortion;
- what has no adequate expression in the causal-inference vocabulary.

In particular, consider whether the evaluative force of genealogy survives translation. Does showing a value’s contingent and psychologically compromising origin merely risk the genetic fallacy, or can it undermine the value’s authority without amounting to a formal refutation? If causal vocabulary cannot capture that operation, explain whether this is a limitation of the vocabulary or evidence that the original argument depends on rhetoric, interpretation, or evaluative judgement rather than causal explanation.
