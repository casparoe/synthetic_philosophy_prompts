Last month I agreed to look after my neighbour's aquarium for a fortnight while she was abroad. The arrangement was that I would go in every two or three days to feed the fish and check the equipment. In the event I went round only once, right at the start; work consumed me, and the promise simply slipped my mind. When she returned, half the fish were dead. A leak in the filter had slowly drained and fouled the tank — a fault which, on her account, had probably begun before she left, and which a regular visitor might have caught in time, though even she concedes that by the second week the damage may have been irreversible whatever anyone did.

A mutual friend also had a key and knew about the arrangement; he never went round either, though unlike me he had never promised to.

My neighbour holds me responsible, and I am inclined to think she is right, but I keep getting tangled when I try to say exactly why. Three things pull in different directions. First, I did nothing — literally nothing — and it feels odd to say that an absence of action is what killed the fish, as though my staying at home were an event in the world on a par with the leak. Secondly, if the tank was doomed by the second week regardless, then my staying away may have changed nothing at all, and it is strange to be blamed for an outcome one could not have prevented. Thirdly, my friend's inaction seems to be exactly as much a 'cause' of the deaths as mine, yet nobody blames him, and I cannot articulate the difference beyond pointing to the promise.

Can you help me think this through? Did my failure to act play any part in bringing the outcome about, given that the outcome may in any case have been guaranteed by the filter fault? And if it did not, does that dissolve the blame — or is my neighbour's complaint better understood as resting on something other than causation, and if so, is that a stronger or a weaker ground for her resentment?
