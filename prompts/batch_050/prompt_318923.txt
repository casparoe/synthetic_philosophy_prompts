I am writing an essay that draws on Rousseau's critique of the theater to argue against public subsidies for the performing arts, and I have hit an apparent difficulty that I need help resolving before I can proceed. In his Letter to d'Alembert, prompted by the suggestion that Geneva establish a playhouse, Rousseau condemns the theater as an institution that corrupts the morals of a people. Yet Rousseau himself composed a successful opera, Le Devin du village, that was performed before the French king, and he wrote a comedy, Narcisse, in whose preface he directly confronted the charge that his own literary work was inconsistent with his published attacks on the arts. As far as I can see, either he was a hypocrite whose strictures need not be taken seriously, or his argument must be more complicated than the flat prohibition my essay attributes to him — and if it is more complicated, I have been unable to reconstruct it in a form that still yields a general case against the performing arts. Was Rousseau simply inconsistent, or does his critique, properly stated, distinguish among cases my essay is running together? Please reconstruct his actual argument about what the theater does and to whom it does it, explain how his own operatic and dramatic work stands relative to that argument, and tell me honestly whether his position, once correctly understood, lends my essay's conclusion any real support or undercuts it. I would like the answer in continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
