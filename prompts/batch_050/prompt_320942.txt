A reading group has spent a term on Lewis Carroll's 1895 dialogue between the Tortoise and Achilles, and two factions have emerged. The regresses hold that the Tortoise's challenge is genuine: since no rule of inference, however stated, can compel its own application, the justification of deduction must bottom out in something that is not itself a further premise or rule — a practice, an intuition, or a pragmatic vindication — and anyone who denies this has missed the point. The dissolvers hold that the regress is an artifact of a confusion: the Tortoise treats modus ponens as a missing premise when it is a rule governing passage between premises and conclusion, and once that distinction is respected no further justification is needed, nor coherently demanded. Draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Format the reply as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the claims both camps accept about what the dialogue shows and does not show; (2) the residual disagreement, stated in wording each side would endorse for its own position; (3) the crux — the sub-question whose resolution would move at least one side; (4) a case or argument both agree bears on that crux (such as whether a reasoner who sincerely assents to all premises yet withholds the conclusion is making a logical error or none), with each side's prediction recorded; (5) any part of the dispute they conclude was merely verbal, and why.
