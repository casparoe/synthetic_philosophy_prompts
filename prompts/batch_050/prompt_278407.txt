I work in forecast aggregation, and I keep running into a pattern I cannot find an established name for. I would like to know whether one exists, and if not, how best to carve the phenomenon off from its neighbors.

The pattern is this: the value of adding one more person's judgment to a pool depends entirely on how much that person already shares with the pool, and I have three cases that I think are the same phenomenon but might not be. First: fifty analysts each estimate a quantity from raw data they gathered separately; the pooled estimate beats the best individual, and a fifty-first analyst still improves it. Second: fifty analysts estimate the same quantity, but every one of them worked from the same two industry reports; the pool barely beats a single analyst, and the fifty-first adds nothing. Third: fifty analysts estimate independently, then see each other's figures and revise; after revision the pool is more confident and less accurate, and the revised estimates cluster around whatever the most senior person said first.

Here is why I am dissatisfied with the vocabulary I have. It is not groupthink: in the second case there is no cohesion, no pressure, no dissent being suppressed — nobody ever speaks to anyone. It does not seem to be what people mean by herding or cascades either, since in the second case all the estimates are produced simultaneously and nobody is imitating anyone. The "wisdom of crowds" literature lists independence as a condition and then, as far as I can tell, never names the failure mode when the condition fails. I have also been told there is a classical result — eighteenth century, I believe — that a majority vote becomes arbitrarily reliable as you add voters who are each slightly better than chance, and I take it my second case is exactly where that result's fine print bites. But I do not know what the fine print actually assumes, or what the thing that breaks it is called.

So: is there an established term for the information-overlap itself, as opposed to the social dynamics that sometimes produce it? How does it relate to that classical voting result, and is my third case genuinely the same phenomenon as my second, or a different one that merely looks similar? If no existing term carves at the right joint, what would you call it, and where would you draw its boundaries?
