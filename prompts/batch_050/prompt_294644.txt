every time the ai art thing comes up in my studio critique group, the same two analogies get deployed. person a says ai images are obviously art because photography went through the exact same thing: painters called it a soulless machine that just copies the world, said the camera does all the work, and now photos hang in the met. give prompting fifty years and we'll feel silly for doubting it. person b fires back that prompting midjourney isn't like taking a photo at all, it's like commissioning a portrait: describing what you want to a painter in detail has never made anyone the painter, it makes you the client, and nobody puts the client's name on the canvas.

audit both analogies for me. for each one, give me the correspondence table -- what plays the role of the camera, the film, the photographer's choice of moment and framing, the subject sitting in front of the lens -- and then where the mapping actually breaks. i'm especially interested in what each analogy quietly assumes about where creativity is located: in choosing? in executing? in curating from a pile of outputs? and one disanalogy i never see addressed: the camera points at the actual world, the model points at other people's images. does that matter for the argument or is it a red herring.

my working suspicion is that the photography analogy, even if fully granted, only shows that ai images *can be* art, while person a uses it as if it settles that the prompter is *the artist* -- and that the commissioning analogy proves too much in the other direction, since it would apparently make a film director the client of her own cinematographer. tell me whether that suspicion holds up, and if you think there's a better third analogy (i've heard "the prompter is like an art director" and "like someone fishing"), say what features it would need to have to actually settle anything rather than just relocate the disagreement.
