I'm a journalist who covers AI and the philosophy of technology, and I'm stuck on a conceptual knot I keep hitting while reporting a piece on "low-impact" AI agents -- systems designed to pursue their goals while avoiding side effects on the world. I've read about the main proposals, things like penalizing an agent for reducing the attainability of other goals (attainable utility preservation) or for moving the world away from states it could easily have reached (relative reachability). The pitch is always that we can get safety-ish behavior without specifying everything humans care about -- you don't need a full utility function over world-states, just a measure of how much the agent changed things. I want to explain to my readers whether this pitch even makes sense conceptually, and I keep going back and forth on it.

Here's where I get confused. Every impact measure seems to need a baseline -- impact *relative to what?* -- and the choice looks like it smuggles values back in. If the baseline is "the world as it would have been without the agent," then an agent standing next to a vase that's about to be knocked over by a gust of wind gets penalized for *saving* the vase, because it deviated from the vase-breaking baseline. So the designer has to decide which changes count, and that decision looks suspiciously like specifying value after all. Meanwhile there's an offsetting worry I picked up from one researcher: couldn't an agent cause a huge change and then "undo" it to game the measure -- flood a factory floor and then drain it, say -- or freeze the whole environment in place to guarantee nothing ever deviates from the baseline, which would be catastrophically high-impact by any normal standard while scoring as zero impact?

So my question, which I'd like explained carefully rather than just answered yes or no: is there any coherent notion of "side effect" or "impact" that doesn't presuppose a prior specification of which features of the world matter? Walk me through the strongest case that there is -- maybe something about causal influence, or about preserving optionality being genuinely value-neutral -- and the strongest case that the whole project is circular, since "options worth preserving" or "changes that count" can only be individuated relative to some value-laden description of the world. And if the project is partly circular, is that fatal, or is there a version of the idea where you only need a thin, widely-agreed notion of value (say, that keeping humans alive and empowered matters) rather than a full one? I'm trying to figure out whether the low-impact agenda is a real research direction or a placeholder for the hard problem it claims to avoid, and I want the piece to get this right rather than just vibing.
