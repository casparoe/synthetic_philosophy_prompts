So I follow a couple of law-and-tech blogs and a subreddit where the question of legal personhood for AI keeps coming up, and honestly the debate feels completely stuck. Every thread goes the same way: one side says hey, we already made corporations legal persons and nobody thinks a corporation can suffer or vote or feel guilty, so why is it crazy to do the same for a sufficiently autonomous AI? The other side says the analogy fails because a corporation always has humans inside it -- a board, shareholders, actual people whose decisions are the company's decisions -- whereas an AI system is, well, the thing itself, with no one home behind it. And then they just repeat this at each other forever. I've also seen people bring up that rivers in New Zealand got legal personhood and that admiralty law lets you literally sue a ship, which the pro side treats as more proof that personhood is just a practical legal tool you hand out when convenient, while the anti side waves it off as irrelevant because the river and the ship have human guardians standing behind them.

My hunch, which might be totally off, is that the two camps aren't actually disagreeing about the same question. One is asking something like "would it be *useful* for the legal system to have a person-shaped slot to put AI into, e.g. for liability and contracts," and the other is asking "does an AI *deserve* the status of a person," and these are such different questions that no argument on one side can ever touch the other. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real factual disagreement hiding underneath -- like a bet about whether future AI systems will be autonomous enough that liability gaps genuinely appear, in which case the stalemate is just people pretending to argue philosophy while actually disagreeing about an engineering forecast.

So what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. What kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it verbal (two meanings of "person" doing all the work), is it a hidden empirical bet, is it about which precedents count as analogous (corporation vs. river vs. ship -- who gets to pick the right comparison class and on what grounds?), or is it bedrock intuitions about what personhood is *for* that neither side can argue for without going in circles? Use what each side actually says, including the corporation analogy and the "no one home" response, and then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a distinction that would dissolve part of it, a real-world development that would settle part of it, or a reason to think nothing will ever move it and we should just stop asking the question in this form.
