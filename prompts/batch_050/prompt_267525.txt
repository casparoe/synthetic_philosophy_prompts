I am a doctoral student in financial economics, and for a workshop on the epistemology of markets I have been asked to circulate a one-page statement of my own commitments so the group can stress-test it. I would like the stress test done here first. My position, as I currently hold it:

(a) In deep, liquid markets, the price is the best single estimator available to me: when I need a probability or a valuation, I defer to the market over my own impressions and over any individual expert.

(b) Prices can only aggregate information because gathering information is costly and its gatherers are compensated; perfectly efficient markets are impossible (the Grossman–Stiglitz argument), and the rents earned by informed traders are the engine of the price discovery that the rest of us free-ride on.

(c) In my own portfolio I hold several individual positions chosen on the basis of my own research, and ex ante I expect these to outperform the index net of costs, taxes, and my time.

(d) I accept the no-trade logic (Aumann; Milgrom–Stokey): under common priors and mutual knowledge of rationality, a counterparty's willingness to trade with me is itself evidence against the trade, and "who is on the other side, and what do they know?" is a question I ought to ask before every transaction.

(e) When one of my positions moves sharply against me in the absence of public news, I treat the move itself as information that better-informed traders know something I do not, and I update accordingly, often by exiting.

(f) I advise everyone else — family, friends, students — to hold low-cost index funds and to abstain from stock-picking entirely.

Please audit this set for joint tenability rather than lecturing me on any single item. Identify the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to give and what the revision would cost. Distinguish these from pairs that merely look inconsistent but dissolve on inspection — I suspect at least one apparent conflict is actually my position working as intended. Finally, tell me which single revision would restore the most consistency for the least theoretical damage. Please note, in doing (e) and (d) justice, whether anything in my framework tells me how to read a price move that might have been caused by a noise trader rather than by information.
