HOMEWORK QUESTION:

Consider the following setup. A two-dimensional grid of tiny interacting particles (call them "simms") updates according to simple local rules -- each simm's next state depends only on its current state and the states of its immediate neighbors. It turns out that when you zoom out, stable large-scale patterns form: blobs made of millions of simms that move around, merge, bounce off each other, and so on (call these blobs "simmballs"). The blob-level dynamics can be described by its own elegant laws, and these laws let you predict the system's behavior far more compactly than tracking every simm. Some researchers claim that the simmballs genuinely push the simms around: the blob, as a blob, exerts downward causal influence on its own parts. Others say that's confused -- all the real pushing happens simm-to-simm at the micro level, and the blob-level laws are just useful descriptions.

Write 2-3 pages taking a position on whether the simmballs have genuine causal efficacy. Your answer should (a) state the strongest version of the "only the micro level causes anything" view, (b) state the best case for simmball-level causation, and (c) defend a verdict.

STUDENT'S ANSWER:

The strongest version of the micro-only view is the causal exclusion argument, associated with Jaegwon Kim in the philosophy of mind. The argument runs: every micro-level event has a sufficient micro-level cause, since the simms' update rules are complete and deterministic -- nothing at the simm level is left open. If the simmball pattern is also supposed to cause the simms' behavior, then either the effect is caused twice over, or the macro cause is doing work the micro cause already does. Kim concludes from this that higher-level properties like simmballhood are epiphenomenal: real, maybe, but causally inert, like shadows of the real causal processes.

The best case for simmball causation starts with multiple realizability. The same blob-level law -- say, that two simmballs of equal size repel at close range -- can hold across completely different micro-configurations, and even across different simm-rule-sets that happen to generate the same blob dynamics. Because the macro law is multiply realizable, the micro-level description is too fine-grained to capture it; it misses the generalization. Since the micro description can't explain everything the macro description explains, macro causation must be real.

The exclusion argument's overdetermination horn also fails, I think. If both the simmball and the simms suffice for the outcome, that's just causal overdetermination, like two assassins shooting the same victim at once, and overdetermination is philosophically respectable -- nobody denies both bullets caused the death.

My verdict: the simmballs do have genuine causal efficacy. The exclusion argument shows that macro causation can't compete with micro causation, but it doesn't need to compete -- it overdetermines alongside it, and its reality is confirmed by the fact that macro-level laws capture generalizations invisible at the micro level.
