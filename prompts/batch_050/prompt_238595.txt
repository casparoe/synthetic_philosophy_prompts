So there's a national election in three weeks and for the first time in my life I'm genuinely torn about whether to vote at all. Both candidates with a real shot strike me as bad -- one clearly worse, but the "lesser evil" has promised things I think are seriously immoral. My brother says abstaining is a dereliction of duty, my roommate says voting for the lesser evil still makes you complicit in whatever he does, and my cousin in a safe seat votes third party every time "to send a message." I've realized I don't even know what questions a theory of voting ethics is supposed to answer.

Before I go read Brennan or whoever, write me the spec: what would a theory of the ethics of voting actually have to deliver to count as adequate? The cases it has to give a verdict on (mine, but also the safe-seat expressive voter, the badly uninformed voter, the person whose single vote can't possibly swing anything), the distinctions it can't collapse (duty to vote vs duty to vote well? expressing an attitude vs producing an outcome?), and what it owes us on complicity. Give the motivating case behind each requirement. Then commit: which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's talking point, and if some can't be jointly satisfied -- I'm guessing the complicity constraint and the lesser-evil constraint pull apart -- say which one you'd drop and why. I want an actual position, not a menu.
