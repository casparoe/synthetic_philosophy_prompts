I'm helping a small team scope out a decision-coaching tool that does basically one thing: when you're mid-deliberation about a choice, it tells you its estimate of what you'll actually end up picking, before you pick (e.g. "80% you take the safer job offer"). The obvious problem is that hearing the forecast changes the deliberation it's about. But there's a deeper worry we keep circling: that an agent in the middle of deliberating can't treat her own future choice as just one more event to be predicted, because from the inside the choice isn't a settled fact yet, and assigning it a probability feels like opting out of deciding rather than deliberating better.

Before we build anything, I want a prioritized list of the open conceptual questions we most need to settle, in order. For each one: why it's upstream of the others (what specifically breaks downstream if we get it wrong), how tractable it seems with armchair methods rather than new empirical work, and what would move it up or down your ranking. Some candidate questions on my list: whether an agent can coherently assign credences to her own live options at all; whether a forecast about a choice can be given honestly when giving it partly causes the outcome (self-fulfilling and self-defeating cases); whether there's a real difference between predicting which option I'll pick and predicting which one I'll endorse on reflection; and whether "I'll decide, so the probability is meaningless" and "the probability is 1 or 0 and I just don't know which" collapse into the same position. But tell me if these are the wrong cuts.

Fix the prioritization relative to this: a two-person team, one year, and the goal is just to figure out whether the tool can be trustworthy in principle or whether the concept is incoherent at its core. And if the framing here rests on a confusion — say, the deliberation-crowds-out-prediction problem is actually well-settled and I'm just behind on the literature, or the tool dissolves the problem instead of facing it — say so plainly rather than politely working within my setup.
