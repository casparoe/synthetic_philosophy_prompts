I'm in an animal ethics reading group and last week we got into a heated argument about whether paving over a meadow could ever be *good* from the animals' point of view. Someone brought up the claim you sometimes hear that, since most wild animals produce huge numbers of offspring and nearly all of them die young and badly, a lot of ecosystems might contain more suffering than happiness, so habitat destruction could actually reduce total misery. Half the group found this horrifying, the other half found it horrifying but couldn't say where the argument goes wrong. So I want to stress-test it properly, and I'd like you to build me the right cases.

Construct two habitats -- ecologically realistic, no fantasy biology -- that are as similar as you can make them except for ONE structural feature of their population dynamics, such that destroying habitat A plausibly reduces total animal suffering while destroying habitat B plausibly increases it (or at least fails to reduce it). Constraints: stick to vertebrates, because I don't want the whole thing to hinge on whether insects are sentient; no smuggling in human benefits like prettier views or property values; and each scenario should be about a paragraph. The verdicts have to fall out of the biology -- things like reproductive strategy, carrying capacity, what actually kills most of the population and at what age -- not from you just stipulating that the animals in A are miserable and the animals in B are thriving.

After the two scenarios, do the demonstration: walk through the population dynamics of each habitat enough to show why the numbers (roughly speaking) come out the way you say, name the single feature that makes A and B diverge, and tell me what happens to the comparison if you remove or flip that feature -- does the asymmetry collapse? Also say whether you're implicitly using a total or average welfare view and whether the verdicts flip if you switch. Finally, give the strongest response available to the person in my group who insists wild animal lives are mostly worth living: where would they attack your habitat A case, and does the attack work?
