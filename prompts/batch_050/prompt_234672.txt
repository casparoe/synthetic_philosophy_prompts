Two people in my grad stats seminar (we're psych students, not geneticists, fwiw) got into it after class and it went in circles for like an hour, so I want an outside ruling. The argument, as best I can reconstruct it:

Mara: twin studies put the heritability of IQ in adults somewhere around .5 to .8, so when two populations differ in average IQ, genetics is the leading explanation for the gap.

Dev: that's the textbook mistake. Heritability measures variation within a population, full stop. It says nothing about why two populations differ on average.

Mara: I said "leading," not "proven." It's not a deductive proof, fine, but it has to be some evidence. If almost all the variation you can observe is genetic, the genetic story for the gap is where the burden of proof starts, not where it ends.

Dev: Lewontin's seed example kills this. Take genetically variable seeds, split them randomly into two plots, one with good soil and one deliberately starved. Within each plot, differences are nearly 100% heritable; between plots, the gap is 100% environmental. High within-group heritability is fully compatible with a between-group gap that's entirely environmental.

Mara: that's a rigged toy case built on a huge environmental manipulation. Real human populations aren't seperated by anything like a plot of deliberately starved soil, so the example shows what's logically possible, not what's plausible. And notice it cuts against you too: it shows heritability is a property of a trait-in-a-range-of-environments, not of the trait itself, so your own ".5 to .8" isn't the fixed fact you're treating it as.

Dev: fine, but even granting all that, high heritability at least means the trait is hard to move environmentally. That's the practical upshot.

Mara: height is at least as heritable as IQ and average height climbed 10+ cm in a bunch of countries over the 20th century. Heritability isn't immutability.

Dev: that moved the mean. It didn't change who's tall relative to whom. Rank order is the heritable part, and nobody has shown you can reshuffle that with environment.

At which point they both declared victory and left. Who's right about which specific point here -- the between-group inference, whether the seed example settles anything, the malleability exchange, the rank-order move -- and what's the thing both of them are missing?
