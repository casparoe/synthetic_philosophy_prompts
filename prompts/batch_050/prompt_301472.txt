Ok so in history class we keep getting taught that big events were basically inevitable — like WW1 would've happened anyway because of alliances and nationalism and all that, even if Franz Ferdinand's driver hadn't taken that wrong turn. But honestly I think that's hindsight talking. My position: history is chaotic in the technical sense, tiny random stuff (a wrong turn, a storm, someone getting sick at the wrong time, for want of a nail etc) genuinely redirects everything, and the "deep forces" story is mostly a narrative we slap on afterwards.

What would count against my view? I want the actual strongest evidence and arguments, ranked by how decisive they'd be — not just "historians disagree." Like, are there cases where a huge event got repeatedly "attempted" by chance and kept almost happening until it did, which would suggest the big forces win in the end? Are there patterns (similar revolutions in similar conditions across different countries, say) that contingency can't explain? What would the inevitability side have to predict that my side doesn't, and vice versa? And honestly, is "it's all contingent" even falsifiable, or can I always just say the big forces were *also* produced by earlier lucky accidents? If my view has basically no exposure, tell me, and whether that's actually a problem for a view about history.
