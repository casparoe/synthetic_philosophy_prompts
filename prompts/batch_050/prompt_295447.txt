So here's the thing that bugs me whenever I read the units-of-selection literature: everybody says the gene's-eye view and multilevel/group selection are just two ways of doing the bookkeeping, mathematically intertranslatable, pick whichever is convenient. But that claim always gets demonstrated on the simplest possible altruism models, and I want to know if it actually survives contact with something messier.

Construct for me a biological scenario — plausible organisms, plausible ecology, nothing a field biologist couldn't in principle go out and measure — in which the two perspectives genuinely come apart. I don't mean they describe the same outcome in different vocabularies; I mean they generate different predictions about which trait evolves, so that at least one of them has to be patched, restricted, or quietly given up. No hypothetical physics, no magic, and the actual mechanics have to be on the table: fitness components, population structure, how groups form and dissolve, the assortment mechanism, all of it.

After the scenario, do the accounting from both sides. Show me how the gene-level calculation runs, show me how the group-level calculation runs (Price-equation style or however you like), and pinpoint exactly where they diverge. Then tell me which structural feature of the case is doing the work — the assortment mechanism, how between-group variance gets maintained, non-additive interactions within groups, whatever it turns out to be — and what happens to the divergence if you remove that feature.

Finally, and this is the part I actually care about: tell me what a defender of the equivalence thesis would say to reabsorb the case. If their patch amounts to smuggling group structure into the "genic" model (defining the relevant genic fitnesses as functions of group context, say), does the equivalence survive as a substantive empirical claim, or only as a tautology that any dynamical system can be redescribed at any level if you're allowed to make the lower-level parameters context-sensitive enough? I'm trying to figure out whether the equivalence claim is a theorem or a promissory note, so don't let the case stipulate its verdicts — derive them.
