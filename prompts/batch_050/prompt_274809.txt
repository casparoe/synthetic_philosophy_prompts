Next month I am presenting at a workshop on solar geoengineering governance. My thesis: because stratospheric aerosol injection is cheap enough that a single mid-sized state could deploy it on its own, the "unilateralist's curse" applies — when many actors each hold a noisy private estimate of whether an action is wise, the actor with the most optimistic estimate is the one who acts, so initiatives open to any one party are undertaken more often than even the average actor's own judgment would warrant. I argue that governance should therefore aim less at prohibition and more at multiplying veto points and forcing shared assessment before deployment. The audience is mostly international-relations scholars and climate scientists, several openly skeptical of decision-theoretic arguments, plus two environmental ethicists.
