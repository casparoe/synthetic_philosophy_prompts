I am drafting a novel whose plot turns on forgiveness: one character insists that his sister is morally required to forgive the man who wronged her, although the man has never apologised; another replies that forgiveness which costs nothing and demands nothing is worth nothing. Before I write their scenes, I want to know what I should be asking of a philosophical account of forgiveness. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory must deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. It should say which situations a theory has to make sense of — the unrepentant or dead wrongdoer, the victim who forgives too readily, forgiveness of oneself — and which distinctions it cannot collapse, such as forgiving versus excusing, condoning, forgetting, and reconciling. Mark which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable and which look like the predilections of one camp, and say plainly if you suspect some pair cannot be jointly satisfied; that tension would serve the novel better than a tidy theory. Then, briefly, how the main contenders — forgiveness as forswearing resentment, and forgiveness as a gift that may be given unconditionally — fare against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria; I shall do the dramatising myself.
