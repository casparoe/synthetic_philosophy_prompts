I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science preparing a departmental colloquium on whether neural networks genuinely have representations. My thesis is a form of qualified representational realism: an activation pattern counts as a representation only when it plays a stable, intervention-supported role in producing successful behaviour and remains identifiable under an explicit equivalence class of decodings that preserve the same task-relevant counterfactuals. On my view, high probe accuracy alone does not establish representation, but neither does the absence of a uniquely privileged decoding show that representational claims are merely instrumental. I will argue that this position avoids both uncritical feature realism and the deflationary claim that interpretability only describes useful human summaries.

The audience will be mixed. Several philosophers of mind and science favour causal or mechanistic accounts of representation and will worry about indeterminacy, multiple realisability, and whether my equivalence classes smuggle in the very content they are meant to identify. Two machine-learning researchers working on mechanistic interpretability will be more concerned about probing artefacts, distributed representations, feature splitting, and whether interventions on activation directions establish anything beyond behavioural dependence. A computational neuroscientist will ask whether my criteria illuminate representational explanation in biological systems or simply impose a philosopher’s test that practicing scientists neither use nor need.

Prepare me for the question period rather than writing a general defence. Give me roughly eight objections I am actually likely to receive, ordered by the damage each could do if answered badly. For each objection:

- phrase it in the vocabulary its likely questioner would use;
- identify the strongest version rather than a caricature;
- distinguish whether it targets my metaphysics of representation, my epistemic criteria, or the proposed equivalence relation;
- state what I should concede immediately and what I should refuse to concede;
- provide a reply I could deliver in about two minutes;
- flag where that reply is weakest and what follow-up it invites.

Please also identify any objection I am likely to underestimate because it comes from a different disciplinary vocabulary, and explain how I should redirect it without sounding dismissive. End with the question I should most hope nobody asks, why my position is especially exposed there, and the most honest response available. Do not provide a bibliography or rely on named literature; I need an argument-focused prep sheet. Please write the answer in British English.
