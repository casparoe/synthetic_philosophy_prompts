So a few months ago my cousin got a two-year prison sentence for a burglary, and it kind of threw me into reading about punishment and free will, because I realised I had no actual view on what the sentence was supposed to accomplish. I've now read Dennett and Caruso's Just Deserts (the 2021 dialogue book) and our little reading group is split down the middle: half of us are with Dennett that we can keep moral responsibility and desert in a naturalistic, compatibilist form, and half are with Caruso that nobody deserves punishment in the basic sense and that his public health-quarantine model is the honest way forward. The arguments keep going in circles, mostly I think because each side accuses the other of smuggling something in.

So here's what I want: draft the joint statement these two camps would produce if they ran an actual adversarial collaboration, as if Dennett's camp and Caruso's camp each got to veto the wording. Concretely I want (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can genuinely sign -- things about determinism and its irrelevance or otherwise, about what incapacitation and deterrence can justify on anyone's view, about which retributive practices both agree are indefensible; (2) the remaining disagreements stated precisely, each in wording the side holding it would accept as fair -- I suspect the big one is whether Dennett's evolved, "competence-based" responsibility is real desert or just a useful fiction dressed up as one, but there's probably also a disagreement about whether the quarantine model really avoids treating people as means; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where if you settled them at least one side would have to budge; and (4) for each crux, a case or finding both sides agree is relevant -- brain tumour cases, psychopathy, whatever works -- with each side's prediction about how the case shakes out recorded in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal (e.g. both sides using "desert" differently), say which part and why rather than pretending it's substantive.

Format-wise, a short header, then the agreed claims, then the disagreements as a table or list, then cruxes with the dueling predictions. Please write it in British English, and keep the tone like an actual document two grudging academics would co-sign, not a book report on their debate.
