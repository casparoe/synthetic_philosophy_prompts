My opening move: your argument proves too much, and it proves it symetrically. If "what names the color is not what names the shape" gets you from the premises to a white horse not being a horse, then the very same step, run the other way, shows a white horse is not white -- the shape-name is not the color-name either. So your reasoning makes the white horse neither horse nor white, and you've dissolved the very thing the sentence was about. Why doesn't the paradox eat itself?
