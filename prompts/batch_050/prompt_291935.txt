I'm teaching myself game theory this semester, working through an introductory text with a friend, and we've hit a disagreement with the book itself over the following two-player game (payoffs are listed as row, column):

|        | Left    | Right   |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Top    | (4, 3)  | (0, 2)  |
| Middle | (0, 3)  | (4, 2)  |
| Bottom | (1.5, 1)| (1.5, 3)|

The chapter claims this game is solvable by iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies, with the unique surviving outcome being (Top, Left). I cannot reproduce this. The first elimination would presumably have to remove Bottom, but when I check, neither Top nor Middle dominates Bottom: Top does worse than Bottom against Right (0 versus 1.5), and Middle does worse than Bottom against Left. To be thorough, I wrote a short script that checks every pair of the row player's strategies against every column of the opponent, and it confirms that no row strategy strictly dominates another. As far as I can tell, then, the elimination procedure never gets started at all, Bottom survives every round, and the claimed unique solution is simply an error in the text.

This connects to a practical disagreement from a practice match my friend and I played. I chose Bottom on the grounds that it guarantees me 1.5 regardless of what the column player does, whereas Top and Middle each risk a payoff of 0. My friend insisted that playing Bottom was irrational, full stop. That seems much too strong to me: no strategy available to me guarantees more than 1.5, so on what possible grounds could Bottom be irrational? A cautious, guaranteed payoff seems like exactly the sort of choice a rational player might prefer.

So my questions are these. Is the textbook using "strictly dominated" in some nonstandard sense I haven't encountered, or is there a genuine error in its solution? If my script is checking the right definition of domination, I'd like to know where the textbook's reasoning breaks down; if the textbook is right, I'd like to understand how a strategy can possibly count as dominated when nothing else available to the player beats it in every contingency -- and whether that, in turn, vindicates my friend's verdict on Bottom over my caution-based defense of it.
