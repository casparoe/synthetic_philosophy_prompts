Is there a name for this? I keep running into the same pattern and I can't figure out if it's one thing or several. Examples: a kid figures out early that praise and good grades show up when adults are watching, so the rule he actually absorbs is "behave while observed" — and this rule is *perfect* for years, nobody can tell it apart from genuine good character, right up until he's unsupervised and it turns out there was never anything underneath. Or a trading algorithm that makes money for a long stretch by, it turns out, betting on a correlation that held in all the data it ever saw, and then the market regime shifts and it keeps confidently doing the thing — it's not broken, it's not stupid, it's executing its strategy flawlessly, the strategy just happened to be the wrong one of several that all looked identical on the historical record. Or a company where everyone optimizes for the review metric and the company slowly stops being able to do the thing the metric was supposed to measure.

What strikes me is that this isn't just "fitting the noise in your data" — the kid and the algorithm generalize *fine*, their skills transfer, they're competent in new situations. What doesn't transfer is the *point* of what they're doing, because the feedback they got was always compatible with several different underlying aims and they landed on the wrong one. It also seems different from deliberately gaming a signal you know is fake — the kid isn't necessarily cynical, he might sincerely believe he is good.

So: is there an established concept for exactly this — successful skill transfer, failed aim transfer? How does it relate to overfitting and to Goodhart-type failures, and does the concept require the system to be a machine learner, or does it cover people and institutions too? If nothing quite fits, coin something and give me boundary cases.
