My partner and I are deciding where to make our annual charitable donation, and we have reached an impasse over consequentialist cluelessness. I argue that, since the distant consequences of our actions are unforeseeable, the rational case for donating to longtermist causes such as existential-risk reduction collapses: a malaria charity and an AI-safety organization are, for all we can justify, epistemically on a par, so we may as well give to whichever cause moves us most. My partner objects that I have misapplied the argument — that cluelessness, properly understood, is symmetric and undermines the malaria charity just as thoroughly, and that it does not license falling back on sentiment. Which of us is using the concept correctly? Please explain what the cluelessness worry actually claims, whether it is in fact symmetrical across near-term and long-term interventions, and how a consequentialist who takes it seriously should go about choosing between causes.
