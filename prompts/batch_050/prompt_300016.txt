You are a moral philosopher with expertise in the aggregation debate in normative ethics. I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on whether the numbers should count in rescue choices, in the tradition opened by Taurek's argument that it can be permissible to save one stranger rather than five when you cannot save both groups. Please design a decision tree that takes a student from the shared starting point -- sometimes it is permissible to save the one instead of the five -- to a named position. Think carefully before drafting: first sketch the logical space (the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they allow), then locate the actual positions within it, rather than working backward from a list of names.

Requirements:

- Every branch node must be phrased as a concrete rescue case or a claim about one, answerable by a student who has never heard the labels. Use at least these cases: the standard one-versus-five choice between strangers; a variant in which the lone person is the rescuer's friend; a variant in which the five would be spared only a modest burden while the one would die; and a variant in which the smaller group is contained in the larger (you can save A alone, or A and B together).
- The leaves must include: straightforward aggregationism (always save the greater number); Taurek's non-aggregationism; a weighted-lottery view on which each person receives a chance proportional to what is at stake for them; a Scanlon-style contractualist view on which numbers break ties but do not otherwise aggregate; and at least one threshold or hybrid view on which numbers count only when the stakes on each side are comparable or the numerical difference is large.
- Include at least one leaf that nobody in the debate occupies, and diagnose in one sentence why (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored).
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and state the further question that would separate them.
- Close by identifying the single branch node whose answer you expect to be most contested in seminar, and briefly why.

Write in American English. Do not include citations or a bibliography.
