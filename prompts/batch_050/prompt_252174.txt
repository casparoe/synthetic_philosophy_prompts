After a long argument with a friend who thinks the gender-equality paradox vindicates innate occupational preferences, I am presenting a contrary thesis to an interdisciplinary seminar: greater economic and political gender equality can increase measured gendered career sorting through “constraint substitution”—material necessity becomes less decisive, while gender norms operate more strongly through identity, status, expectations of caring, and ideas about what a meaningful life looks like. My conclusion is that the pattern tells us little about nature until we know how freedom changes the costs and meanings attached to different choices. The audience consists of labor economists, social psychologists, feminist political theorists, and two evolutionary psychologists. Prepare my Q&A: give the objections in roughly the order this audience will raise them, phrased in each group’s own idiom; identify which are strongest; say what I should concede, what I should defend, and where my replies remain weak; and include the question I most hope nobody asks.
