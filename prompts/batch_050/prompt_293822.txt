I just spent two hours arguing with a freind about strict criminal liability. She thinks punishment without mens rea is always unjust; I’m less sure, especially for heavily regulated activities where proving someone’s state of mind would be nearly impossible. Before we continue, write the desiderata for a theory of when strict liability can be justified.

Use this test case: a pharmacy rule makes a pharmacist criminally liable whenever a dispensed bottle has the wrong dosage instructions, even if the pharmacist reasonably relied on a state-verified database and no amount of care would have caught the error. A good theory should say what, if anything, could justify liability there, and where it becomes merely using the pharmacist as a convenient liability shield.

Please use these sections, in order:

The specification — 8–10 numbered requirements, with the case or consideration behind each. Include distinctions the theory cannot collapse: blame versus harm prevention, criminal punishment versus a regulatory penalty, negligence versus strict liability, and proof of fault versus substantive guilt.

Hard constraints versus camp preferences — 2 short paragraphs identifying which requirements seem non-negotiable and which mainly reflect retributivist, deterrent, or administrative priorities.

The joint strain — 1 paragraph on any desiderata that look difficult or impossible to satisfy together, especially fair notice and individual desert alongside efficient enforcement.

Quick check — 3 bullets testing the classic regulatory-offense defense, my friend’s blanket opposition, and the pharmacy rule against your list. Keep it focused on the criteria rather than hunting down authorities.
