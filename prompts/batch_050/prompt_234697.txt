So I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm drafting a feature about how health systems decide which treatments to fund -- QALYs, rationing, that whole world -- and somewhere in the reporting I fell down an internet rabbit hole about whether vast numbers of tiny harms can ever add up to something terrible. There's this thought experiment people argue about where you pick between one person being horribly tortured for decades or an astronomical number of people each getting a single dust speck in the eye, momentarily annoying and nothing more. I realised while drafting that my own working instincts are a bit of a jumble, and before I interview a philosopher for the piece I'd like to know if my position actually hangs together or whether I'm about to embarrass myself. Here's roughly what I believe:

(1) In the speck case, I take the specks. Torturing one person is monstrous, and I genuinely don't think any number of dust specks changes that -- it's not a matter of how many zeroes you write down. (2) At the same time, I'm broadly comfortable with how bodies like NICE allocate NHS money: a treatment that gives millions of people a small amount of extra healthy life can and should win out over one that prevents a much worse outcome for a handful of people. That's just sane policy, and I've written approvingly about it. (3) I think there are real thresholds of seriousness -- torture, or a child's death, sit in a different moral category from headaches and stubbed toes, and it's a confusion to put them on one sliding scale. (4) I think everyone's suffering matters equally -- a unit of pain counts the same whoever it lands on, rich or poor, stranger or neighbour. (5) And when the harms are the same kind, numbers plainly count: other things equal you save the five rather than the one, and anyone who denies that is being precious.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do at first glance -- I have a suspicion at least one apparent conflict here dissolves if you think about it properly, and I'd rather you sorted that out than flagged everything. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would actually have to give. And if one single revision would do the most work in making this consistent, name it, but don't assume I already know which commitment I'm least attached to, because I honestly don't. I'm filing to a London desk, so do the whole thing in British English please.
