(2) Anyone who benefits from a convention's existence is obligated to do their part in sustaining it -- that's just fair play, and free-riding on everyone else's compliance is wrong even when no one is directly harmed.
(3) Everyone physically present in a place benefits from the general practice of convention-following there (orderly queues, predictable traffic, servers being able to pay rent), whether they like the specific conventions or not.
(4) So everyone, including visitors who are only around for a weekend, is obligated to follow local conventions whenever locals would expect them to.
(5) It also doesn't matter whether the convention is good: even a wasteful or annoying convention only does its coordinating work because people comply, so the obligation to comply survives the judgment that a different rule would be better.

(7) That's absurd, so at least one step above is wrong, but each one looks like common sense on its own.

Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up actually cost me?
