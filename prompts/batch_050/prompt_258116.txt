So I recently came accross this idea in AI alignment discussions, sometimes called the strategy-stealing assumption. As I understand it, the thought goes roughly like this: imagine a "good" AI that's trying to do what humans actually want, and it's competing against a misaligned AI that's trying to accumulate power for its own ends. People usually worry about the misaligned one being a bit more capable. But the claim is that this doesn't matter so much, because the good AI can just *steal* the misaligned one's strategy -- whatever effective power-grabbing moves the misaligned AI would make, the good AI could in principle make the same moves and then put the resulting power to good ends instead. On top of that, the misaligned AI supposedly has extra handicaps (it has to hide its real goals, it can't openly coordinate with humans, etc.), while the good AI can cooperate openly with people and with other good AIs. So the conclusion is that a small capability gap is fine.

Assuming I've got the idea roughly right (and correct me if I've mangled it): what are the most important implications of this for how much we should actually worry about AI development races? Like, if strategy-stealing basically works, does it follow that safety-focused labs shouldn't bother staying at the capability frontier? And conversely, if the assumption fails, what does that imply about whether slowdowns or pauses would even help, since a race dynamic might just resume later?

One more thing: please don't just hand me a confident verdict. Tell me which specific argument or consideration would flip your answer the other way, and where you think this whole line of reasoning is most likely to break down. I'm especially suspicious that there are hidden assumptions doing real work here -- e.g. about whether "strategies" are actually transferable between agents with different goals, or about whether the good side can coordinate well enough to pull the theft off -- so poke at those.
