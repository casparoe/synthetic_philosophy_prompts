So I keep running into this in comment threads (most recently in a discussion about whether it could ever be wrong to torture NPCs in games, after that study where people felt bad about it): someone drops "it's just a game" and acts like that ends the conversation. Like, I raised the point that if we ever get NPCs running on architectures complex enough that you should have even a small credence in there being something-it-is-like to be them, then mass-murdering them for fun starts looking at least a little bit bad in expectation, and the reply was literally "it's just a game, none of it is real."

But the more I turn it over the less I know what claim that's even supposed to be. It could mean like five different things:

- that the characters aren't sentient, full stop, so nothing done to them can matter
- that even IF they were sentient somehow, the suffering isn't "real" suffering because it's instantiated in a simulation (which seems to just be denying substrate independence without arguing for it?)
- that the player isn't really performing the action depicted, so no character-level harm reflects on them
- that engaging with fictional suffering is harmless to the player's own moral character (a virtue ethics reading?)
- or maybe just a social move, like "you're overthinking entertainment, stop being weird"

Anyway. What I want: lay out the precise claims "it's just a game" could be making in this kind of exchange -- like, actually cash each one out as a thesis with premises -- but then don't just hand me a menu and shrug. Commit to a position. Tell me which reading is the strongest, which ones are doing the work people *think* they're doing, and give me your actual verdict on whether any version of the slogan defeats the argument that sufficiently sophisticated NPCs could warrant some moral consideration. I'd rather get a definite answer I can push back on than a tasteful survey of options. If the strongest reading turns out to depend on some empirical or philosophical premise that's contested, say so and say which side of that contest you'd bet on and why.
