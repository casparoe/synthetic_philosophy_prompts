For a philosophy reading group, I have written down my current commitments on dialetheism and would like an audit of whether they form a coherent package:

1. Some sentences are both true and false, but not every sentence is true; genuine gluts are confined to semantic paradoxes, with no true contradictions in ordinary empirical discourse.
2. The Liar sentence is both true and false rather than truth-valueless, context-shifting, or merely undecidable.
3. Truth is fully transparent: for every sentence A, A is intersubstitutable with the claim that A is true, and the unrestricted T-schema holds even in self-referential cases.
4. The correct consequence relation is paraconsistent and non-explosive, but it coincides with classical logic in any domain in which no contradiction is true.
5. This local recovery of classical logic must follow from logic itself, not from adding a separate premise asserting that the domain is consistent.
6. Curry-style sentences must be blocked without restricting self-reference, the T-schema, modus ponens, or contraction.
7. Validity is monotonic necessary preservation of truth, with a both-true-and-false premise counting as true for preservation purposes.

Please identify which commitments genuinely conflict and which only appear to, ranking the tensions by severity rather than treating every pair as equally problematic. For each genuine conflict, state the smallest revision that removes it and what that revision costs. Then identify the single revision that would produce the most coherent overall package. Finally, say what argument or technical result would change your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
