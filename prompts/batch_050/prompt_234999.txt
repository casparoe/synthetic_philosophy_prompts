Van Fraassen's Reflection principle says, roughly, that your current credence in a proposition, conditional on learning that your future credence in it will be r, should itself be r: P_t(A | P_{t'}(A) = r). The standard motivations are a diachronic Dutch book argument and the thought that a rational agent should defer to her future, better-informed self the way she would defer to an expert. I'd like a counterexample built to order — and the usual suspects are banned.

Construct a single scenario in which an agent should *knowingly* violate Reflection while satisfying all of the following constraints:

1. The agent is fully rational now and expects to be fully rational later. No drugs, no onset of bias, no brainwashing, no anticipated irrationality of any kind.
2. No memory loss and no tampering with her evidence or reasoning. Her future credence is formed by conditionalizing on honestly acquired evidence at least as informative as what she has now.
3. No self-locating uncertainty — no Sleeping Beauty setups, no duplicates, no amnesia — and no self-referential announcements of the "you will believe this very announcement" variety.
4. A concrete, realistic setting: a physician, an intelligence analyst, an engineer — someone whose actual job is updating on evidence. The person who informs her of her future credence must be an ordinary informant whose announcement she can reason about, not an oracle with logically impossible powers.
5. Explicit numbers throughout: the prior, the possible future evidence, likelihoods, the possible future credence values, and exactly how the informant comes to know and report the future credence.

The violation should fall out of the structure of how the agent comes to learn her future credence, not out of any defect in her or in her future self; if it is merely stipulated, the case fails.

After the scenario, run the verification: (a) compute both sides of the Reflection equation and show they disagree; (b) identify precisely which premise of the standard defenses — the diachronic Dutch book, or the defer-to-your-better-informed-future-self intuition — the case exploits, and why that premise fails here; (c) explain why the violation does not convict the agent of irrationality — in particular, what she should say when offered the Dutch book; (d) state the strongest countermove available to a defender of Reflection (for instance, restricting the principle to agents who will conditionalize on everything they learn, or re-describing what the announcement really teaches her), and assess whether that move saves the principle only at the cost of rendering it trivial. If the arithmetic cannot be made to work under these constraints, say so and identify which constraint binds.
