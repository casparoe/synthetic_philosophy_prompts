I'm a PhD student working on personal identity and I'm stuck on an argument two friends of mine had at the pub after our reading group did Parfit. The setup: a new teletransporter model doesn't vaporize you at departure. It scans you, builds the replica on Mars, and only destroys the Earth original after the replica radios back that it arrived safely. So there's a ten-minute window where both exist. My friends disagreed about basically everything:

(1) Chloe said the overlap window settles it: during those ten minutes the Mars person obviously isn't you -- you're standing on Earth watching a video feed of some guy who has your memories -- so the replica was never you, and the later destruction of the original is just your death with extra steps.

(2) Marcus said this proves too much, since by that logic whether you survive a teleporter ride depends on when the machine happens to incinerate the original, and survival shouldn't hinge on a timing detail in the engineering. What matters is psychological continuity and connectedness, and the Mars person has that regardless of the delay.

(3) Chloe replied that Marcus is conflating two questions. She's happy to grant the Mars person is psychologically continuous with her; her point is that numerical identity is transitive and one-one, and once two candidates exist at the same time, at most one can be her, and it's obviously the one that has her actual body. The branching isn't a side issue, it's the tell.

(4) Marcus's last move was to say that "what matters" in survival can come apart from identity -- in the branch case he'd rather be the Earth person who gets vaporized knowing the replica is fine than be in a world where the scan fails entirely, so Chloe is clinging to identity while ignoring what anyone would actually care about. Chloe said what you'd *care about* is just a preference, not a metaphysical fact, and preferences don't decide who's who.

Who's right at each step? I especially want to know whether Chloe's overlap argument actually does the work she thinks it does, and whether Marcus's move in (4) is a real answer or a change of subject. Also if both are missing something, tell me what.
