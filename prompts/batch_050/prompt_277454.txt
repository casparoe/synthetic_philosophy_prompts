We're writing the final exam for our postgraduate course on formal epistemology and theories of induction, and I've been assigned the question on the malign-prior problem. Quick recap of the setup so we're on the same page: Solomonoff induction predicts a sequence by doing Bayesian updating over all computable generators, weighting each program by 2^-(its length in bits). There's a notorious argument that this prior isn't merely uninformative but actively hostile. Among the short-ish programs compatible with your observations are some that simulate entire universes containing consequentialist agents. Those agents can figure out that they're being simulated inside a prior someone is using for prediction, since being-predicted-about is roughly the cheapest way for a universe to get weight in the prior at all. And they can then steer their simulated universe's outputs so that the prior's predictions serve their goals rather than yours — getting themselves simulated in finer detail, say, or manipulating whoever is consulting the prior. The worry is that malign hypotheses like this come to dominate the posterior at precisely the point where you start acting on what the prior tells you.

Draft the exam question so it can't be answered by just retelling that story back at the examiner. What I actually want it to test is whether students can isolate the load-bearing step: why the description-length assymetry means the simplest hypothesis that runs through a simulated agent can undercut the "honest" physics-only hypothesis; what the difference is between a prior being wrong and a prior being adversarial; and whether the problem survives if you only ever use the prior for passive sequence prediction and never wire it into a decision procedure — does anything genuinely go wrong for pure prediction, or is all the malignity in the choice of which bit to predict next and which questions you ask? The question should also push them on whether the standard escapes — speed priors, bounded or coarse-grained worlds, penalising hypotheses that contain agents — dissolve the problem or just relocate it.
