A colleague of mine defends evolutionary psychology against the charge that it trades in unfalsifiable just-so stories by leaning heavily on the Swiss Army knife analogy. His pitch goes roughly as follows. The mind is not a general-purpose learning device; it is like a Swiss Army knife, a collection of specialized tools, each shaped by natural selection to solve a distinct adaptive problem our ancestors recurrently faced (cheater detection in social exchange, mate choice, predator avoidance, incest aversion, and so on). Because each blade of the knife has a determinate function, he argues, hypotheses about specific modules are as testable as hypotheses about the functions of organs: you reverse-engineer the tool from the problem it was built to solve, then check whether it has the predicted design features. On his telling, the analogy both vindicates adaptationism as a methodology and shows that the just-so-story objection rests on a confusion between guessing a story and inferring a function from form.

Please audit this analogy. Lay out the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the knife as a whole, the individual blades, the blade's shape, the manufacturer, the intended use, and the person reverse-engineering the function. Then identify where the mapping breaks under pressure. Consider at least the following candidate disanalogies, and say which ones matter for my colleague's intended use and which are idle: (1) the knife has a designer with foresight and a settled purpose, whereas selection is blind and works on whatever variation exists; (2) blades spring into existence with their functions fixed, whereas psychological mechanisms develop through gene–environment interaction and may be plastic; (3) a tool's function is its reason for existing, whereas a trait's current use may diverge from the effect for which it was selected, or it may have been selected for nothing at all (byproducts, spandrels, exaptations); (4) the knife's tools are cleanly separable, whereas cognitive mechanisms may share components or be deeply domain-general; (5) reverse-engineering a knife is checked against the manufacturer's specification, but no such specification exists for the mind, and ancestral environments are known only indirectly. Finally, assess whether the analogy quietly imports the very thing at issue — namely, that function-attribution in psychology has an independent check comparable to consulting the designer — and whether my colleague's conclusion that the just-so-story charge is misplaced survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If a better analogy would serve his argumentative purposes, propose one and explain what it buys him that the Swiss Army knife does not.
