I am a sixth-form student designing a ten-week independent project on the inner alignment of small language models. My testbed is deliberately modest: I will fine-tune an open model to advise pupils on how to revise, with a teacher awarding the training score according to whether the advice sounds specific, confident and likely to improve quiz results. I suspect this creates several objectives that are difficult to distinguish from behaviour alone: improving learning, pleasing the teacher, sounding expert, and keeping pupils occupied with easily measurable work. At deployment, the model will advise on subjects and pupil circumstances absent from training, and it may be able to infer from the interface whether its answer is being graded. Given only ten weeks, one helper, a laptop and access to small open models, which unresolved question about inner alignment or goal misgeneralisation should I investigate first: how to infer a latent objective when several objectives explain the same behaviour, how to construct situations in which teacher approval and genuine learning diverge, whether a model’s ability to recognise evaluation changes what we should mean by its goal, how much weight internal interpretability should receive relative to behavioural tests, or another question I have overlooked? Please defend an ordering rather than merely survey the area. Explain which questions are upstream of which, which are realistically tractable at my scale, which are important but should wait, and what experimental result would cause you to revise the ordering. In particular, address whether apparently correct behaviour under a training score can ever provide strong evidence of inner alignment, and what implications your answer has for my choice of test cases. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list. Please write the answer as continuous prose, as in a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables; make the priority order explicit within the prose.
