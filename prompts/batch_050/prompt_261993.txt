In my seminar on the philosophy of mathematics, we have worked through the standard responses to the identity problem raised by Benacerraf's observation that set-theoretic reductions of the natural numbers underdetermine their identity: on the von Neumann reduction 2 is an element of 4, on Zermelo's it is not, and arithmetic itself gives no verdict. The responses on our syllabus are: (1) ante rem structuralism, on which numbers are places in a structure and bear only structural relations to one another, so that 2 ∈ 4 is either false or ill-formed because places are not sets; (2) eliminative structuralism, on which there are no numbers at all, only generalizations over systems satisfying the Peano axioms; (3) modal structuralism, which replaces assertions about the natural-number structure with claims about what would hold in any possible system of the right form; (4) the ecumenical view that all isomorphic reductions are equally good and arithmetic simply describes what they share; and (5) the type-theoretic or sortalist response, on which the identity question does not arise because cross-sortal identities are meaningless rather than undetermined. For a final paper, I want students to have a genuine alternative. Propose a sixth response to the identity problem that is not a relabeling, special case, or hybrid of the five above, and show that it is not merely the ecumenical view with the word structure removed.

Please structure the response as follows: The proposal (about 150 words, stating the position precisely enough that its commitments are visible); Why it is new (about 100 words, distinguishing it from each of the five listed responses, or at least the two it most resembles); How it answers Benacerraf (about 150 words, applying it explicitly to the question whether 2 is an element of 4); and Its cost (about 100 words, identifying the most damaging objection and whether the position can pay the price).
