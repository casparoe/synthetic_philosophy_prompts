Last weekend a friend and I got into a long argument about whether her decision to stop flying was morally serious or just expensive self-flagellation, and I ended up defending a cluster of views I've held for years without ever checking whether they're actually compatible. Since we reached no agreement, I'd like the set audited. My commitments: (1) On climate and factory farming, the inefficacy objection is essentially correct — my individual consumption choices (one flight, one chicken purchase) make no morally significant difference to total harm, because markets and emissions don't register a single marginal unit. (2) Nevertheless, voting in large national elections is genuinely worth doing and perhaps morally required, because even a minuscule probability of being pivotal, multiplied by the enormous stakes of an election, yields substantial expected value. (3) Complicity- and guilt-based arguments leave me cold; what matters morally is consequences, not whether my hands are clean or what my purchase symbolizes. (4) I fully support collective political remedies — carbon taxes, meat taxes, regulation — and I don't think there's anything hypocritical about advocating such policies while personally flying and eating meat, since the whole problem is that unilateral restraint is pointless and only coordination works. (5) I do think boycotts of genuinely odious companies can be worth joining, and I'd judge someone harshly for, say, dumping motor oil into a stream, even though one person's oil is a drop in the watershed.

Please treat this as an audit, not a lecture. Tell me which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, since I suspect at least one apparent conflict here dissolves once you're careful about what the inefficacy objection actually targets. For each real tension, say how severe it is, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs me — keeping in mind that in the argument with my friend I was most confident about (1) and (4), though I don't want to pre-commit to giving anything up in particular. I'm especially interested in whether the reasoning I'd naturally offer for (2) can be kept from generalizing back onto (1), and whether (5) can be squared with (3) without quietly smuggling in a complicity-based view. Order the tensions by severity, and finish by identifying the single revision that would clean up the most of the set.
