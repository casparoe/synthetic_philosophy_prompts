Memory-care units for people with advanced dementia are frequently built around systematic, benevolent deception. Corridors are designed so that the doors to staff areas and exits are disguised as murals, bookcases, or blank walls, while the route into the garden is made visually prominent; some facilities install a fake bus stop so that a resident determined to "go home" will sit down to wait for a bus that never comes, and a caregiver can sit beside her and gently redirect her; staff are trained in validation-style approaches, entering the resident's frame of reality rather than correcting it, so that a resident who asks for her long-dead husband is asked what he was like, not informed that he died twenty years ago. Meanwhile, the same health-care institutions treat informed consent as nearly sacrosanct for competent patients and regard deceiving a patient about her condition, prognosis, or circumstances as a grave wrong. What theory of well-being, and of the value of truth, does the memory-care practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view: is it a form of hedonism on which only felt experience matters, a restricted desire theory on which frustrated-but-unknown preferences no longer count, or something else? Then consider the practice against the intuition Nozick's experience machine is usually taken to establish -- that we want to live in contact with reality, not merely to have the experiences of doing so. Those of us who refuse the machine for ourselves are often untroubled by building gentle, permanent machines of this kind for the demented. What does the asymmetry track? Is the implicit claim that truth has merely instrumental value, which lapses when truth can no longer serve the resident's projects; that reality-contact is a welfare good only for beings capable of narrative selfhood; or that the severely demented have crossed some threshold of moral status? Be careful to distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with: could a defender of an objective-list theory of well-being license the fake bus stop, for instance by treating the prevention of terror and the preservation of dignity as objective goods that outweigh an objective good of truth? And what would have to change in the design and staffing of these facilities if we instead presupposed a view on which being systematically deceived is a welfare harm even when the deception is never discovered and causes no distress -- would the whole architecture become impermissible, or only some elements of it?
