Need a consistency check on some commitments I've accumulated about infinite populations, mostly from thinking about the longtermist stuff. I've adopted these one at a time over a couple years and never stress-tested the whole set at once. Here it is:

1. Betterness between worlds is transitive, and any two worlds are in principle rankable. I don't want any "incomparable" escape hatch.
2. Strong Pareto with no exceptions: if world A is at least as good as world B for every sentient being, and strictly better for at least one, then A is better simpliciter. Shouldn't matter if there are a hundred beings or infinitely many.
3. Identity- and location-blindness: if you take a world and just reshuffle which being sits at which spacetime point with which welfare level, you haven't changed how good the world is at all. Morality can't care about the difference between me-here-you-there and you-here-me-there.
4. For finite populations, straight total utilitarianism: more total welfare, better world.
5. One extra genuinely happy person always makes a world better, even a world that already contains infinitely many happy people.
6. On the decision side: maximize expected value, and don't zero out outcomes just because they're infinitely good or bad. If an act has even a tiny chance of an infinitely valuable payoff, that has to count in the calculation.

Do these hang together? I want an actual audit, not a lecture: list the tensions in order of severity, and for each one say exactly which commitments collide and what the cheapest repair is (which commitment gets amputated, and what it costs me). Also tell me which apparent tensions are fake — pairs that look like they collide but dont on a second look. And don't just gesture at "infinity is weird," I want to know which specific commitment is doing the damage in each case.
