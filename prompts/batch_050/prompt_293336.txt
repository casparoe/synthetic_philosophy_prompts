Me and a friend just spent two hours argueing about whether it’s okay to fog an apartment for bedbugs. I think the insects’ possible suffering should matter at least a little; he thinks pest control is basically just property maintenance. Every time we used a label like humane, necessary, or speciesist, we realized we meant something different.

Make a decision tree for sorting ethical positions on insect pest control. Assume at the start that people have some legitimate interest in preventing disease, crop loss, or serious infestations. Each node should be a concrete question someone could answer without already knowing philosophical jargon, and each branch should be labeled with the claim that separates it from the neighboring branch.

The tree has to include these fault lines somewhere:

1. whether insect suffering counts for its own sake;
2. what we should do when we’re unsure a particular species can suffer;
3. whether killing is a further harm beyond making death painful;
4. whether a slower or more expensive method is required if it causes much less pain;
5. whether prevention is ethically better than extermination even if both work;
6. whether ecological damage can outweigh the interests of individual insects.

The leaves should be positions, not just verdicts about one case. Include conventional permissivism, cautious sentientism, suffering-minimizing pest control, anti-killing preventionism, and ecological holism, using plainer labels if those are too loaded.
