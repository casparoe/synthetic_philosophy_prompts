Hey, I'm in year 12 and I'm on our school council and something properly confusing happened at our meeting last week, and now the teacher who runs our philosophy club says it would make a good essay topic, so I need an essay written about it. Here's the situation.

The council (7 of us) had to decide whether the summer fair can go ahead on the school field in the last week of term. School rules say it can only happen if BOTH of these are true:

1. It won't eat into exam revision time in any serious way
2. The field will be back to normal in time for sports day

We voted on each condition seperately. Condition 1 passed 4-3. Condition 2 passed 4-3. So by the rules, fair's on, yeah? But then someone (fine, it was me) asked to just vote straight up or down on "should the fair happen" and that LOST 2-5. Turns out only 2 people actually thought both conditions held. The ones who said yes to condition 1 were mostly different people from the ones who said yes to condition 2, and one lad who thought both conditions failed still happily voted on each one.

Now everyone's arguing. The chair wants to go with the condition-by-condition result because "we followed the rules as written". The head, when she heard, said she'd only accept a revote if we vote directly on the actual question, because "you can't hide behind bookkeeping when most of the council plainly doesn't want the fair". Then someone else proposed a third option: vote directly on the question, but if you vote no you have to say which condition you think fails, and if you accept that both conditions pass, your no vote gets chucked out. And the head made one more point that's been doing my head in: whoever gets to pick the "conditions" and how they're worded seems able to decide the result before anyone even votes, which feels kind of rigged.

So the essay: which way of aggregating the votes is actually the right/fair one here — voting on the conditions one at a time, voting directly on the outcome, or that third "justify your no" scheme? Weigh up the strongest arguments for each (e.g. the conditions vote tracks the reasons the rule itself cares about, but the direct vote respects what members think overall), deal properly with the framing objection, and then reach an actual verdict on what the council should do AND, more generally, on when (if ever) condition-by-condition voting should beat a straight vote. Please don't just sit on the fence, my teacher says we have to commit to a position.

Roughly 900-1200 words. And can you write it in British English please, my teacher marks us down for American spellings. Thanks!!
