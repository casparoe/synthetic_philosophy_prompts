Every few months someone in our metaethics reading group brings up evolutionary debunking arguments and we rerun the exact same session. Street's Darwinian dilemma gets stated, Joyce's version, then the realists reply that the evolutionary explanation is itself a third-factor account, or that forming true moral beliefs contributed to fitness precisely because they were true, or that the debunker is begging the question by calling the process off-track without saying what an on-track process would even look like. Someone mentions Enoch's pre-established harmony point, someone distinguishes global from local debunking, someone accuses the debunkers of quietly assuming a non-naturalist epistemology, and the hour is up. As far as I can tell nothing in this literature has actually moved in years -- the moves are all pre-registered at this point and both sides know the whole script.

My working hypothesis: this was never really a disagreement about evolution. The biology is basically common ground. It's a disagreement about what epistemic defeat requires. The debunkers think showing that a belief-forming process is insensitive to the truth of the domain is enough to defeat the beliefs, full stop, no further showing needed. The realists think you only get defeat if you can also show the beliefs are false, or at least that there's no available route by which they could've been truth-tracking, and the evolutionary story on its own shows neither. If that's right, the debunking literature is a proxy war between two general epistemologies of defeat, and nobody's going to win it on specifically moral terrain.

Is that the right diagnosis? If yes, what would actually settle it -- a general theory of defeat? (good luck, that debate looks equally stalled) If no, tell me where the real disagreement sits instead. Candidate alternatives I've considered: maybe it's about whether the realist owes a positive account of how moral beliefs could be truth-tracking, and whether the third-factor responses are such an account or just a promisary note. Maybe it's about the default epistemic status of intuitions -- the debunker treats them as needing vindication, the realist treats them as innocent until defeated, and the evolutionary story only bites if you already accept the first frame. Or maybe it's partly verbal -- 'explains' and 'defeats' doing different work on each side.

What I want is a diagnosis with an argument behind it, not a tour of the literature, plus what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction someone should draw, a specific argument one side owes, an empirical input that would matter, or a reason to think this is bedrock and both sides should honestly just stop publishing on it.
