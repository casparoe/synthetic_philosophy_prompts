Draft the joint statement that a committed populist and a committed liberal constitutionalist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By populist I mean someone who holds that there's a genuine, morally authoritative will of the people that corrupt or self-dealing elites regularly frustrate; by constitutionalist I mean someone who thinks "the will of the people" is at best a fiction manufactured by whichever aggregation procedure you happen to use, and that majority sentiment carries no special moral weight on its own.

The document should have: a numbered section of claims both sides will actually sign (about what elections do and don't measure, about policy-opinion gaps, about when insulating decisions from public opinion is defensible, etc.); then a disagreements table where each row states the disagreement in wording both sides accept and each side's position is phrased so that side would endorse its own description; then the cruxes. I'd guess one crux is whether majority preferences that are contradictory or unstable across framings (cycling, agenda-dependence, that kind of thing) can still add up to "a will" in any sense that matters, and another is the durable-majority case: if 60% of a country wants the same thing, consistently, for twenty years, does that change the moral picture versus a fleeting 51% on a single day? For each crux give a concrete case or thought experiment both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about how it should come out written down in advance.

Finally, have them flag any disagreement that turned out to be merely verbal -- my suspicion is that some of the fight is just about what "the people" refers to, but I could be wrong about which parts.
