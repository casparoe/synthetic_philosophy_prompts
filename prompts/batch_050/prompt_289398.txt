I am a PhD student in behavioural ecology preparing a qualifying-exam seminar on signalling theory, and I want to turn the seminar's recurring argument into a usable classification instrument. The argument always starts from the same passage in Chapter VIII ("Principles of Sexual Selection") of Darwin's The Descent of Man, and Selection in Relation to Sex (1871):

"We are, however, here concerned only with sexual selection. This depends on the advantage which certain individuals have over others of the same sex and species solely in respect of reproduction."

The hinge of the seminar's disagreement is the phrase "solely in respect of reproduction": Darwin secures the advantage by bracketing whatever the ornament costs its bearer in survival terms, and the entire modern debate can be read as a dispute over what to do with the costs he brackets. Please build me a decision tree whose root question operationalizes exactly that bracketed remainder — is the display's non-reproductive cost the very mechanism that keeps the signal honest, is honesty enforced without cost by physical constraint, or does the display persist despite cost for reasons that make no reference to honesty at all?

The tree should classify the explanations that empirical studies actually offer for extravagant male displays. Mandatory leaves: the Zahavian handicap (honesty enforced by condition-dependent strategic cost), the index or constraint signal (honesty enforced by an unfakeable production constraint, cost incidental), the Fisherian runaway (preference and trait locked in self-reinforcing coevolution), sensory exploitation (the display parasitizes a pre-existing receiver bias), and the direct-benefits account (the display advertises resources or services rather than genetic quality). If you think conventional signals stabilized by social punishment or retaliation deserve a sixth leaf, add it; if you think that view collapses into one of the five, say which and why.

Requirements. First, build the tree from the logical space, not from the names: each internal node must pose a question answerable from observable or measurable features of the display system (condition-dependence of expression, existence of a production constraint, phylogenetic ordering of preference and trait, whether the signal conveys anything beyond mating eligibility, and so on) — never "is this a handicap?" Second, run five test cases through the finished tree and land each at a leaf: the peacock's train, eyespan in stalk-eyed flies, the bowerbird's bower (an off-body display, which I suspect is the hardest case), advertisement-call pitch in anurans, and roaring rate in red deer. Third, identify any answer combinations no position occupies and diagnose each as incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored. Fourth, flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate from display phenotype alone (I expect Fisherian runaway versus sensory exploitation to be one such pair) and specify the additional evidence — e.g., a preference phylogeny or a measured cost function — that would separate them.

Please return your answer as a single JSON object with these fields: "passage_reading" (string; how you interpret the quoted sentence and what root question it licenses); "nodes" (array of objects with "id", "question", "answers" — each answer an object with "answer_text" and "next", where "next" is a node id or leaf id); "leaves" (array of objects with "leaf_id", "position", "defining_commitment", and "case_verdicts", an object mapping each of the five test cases to the leaf's verdict in one sentence); "unoccupied_combinations" (array of objects with "answers" and "diagnosis"); "inseparable_pairs" (array of objects with "pair", "reason", and "separating_test"). Keep the tree to 10–14 nodes and 5–7 leaves.
