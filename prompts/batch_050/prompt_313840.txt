Consider a refined version of Hofstadter’s Aunt Hillary scenario. An ant colony contains no central executive: each ant follows comparatively simple local rules, while pheromone trails, nest architecture, brood distribution and rates of encounter collectively form a slower system of memory and control. Over minutes, individual ants find food and avoid hazards. Over weeks, the colony reallocates labour, preserves information about seasonal conditions, repairs damage to its nest and responds to threats in ways no ant can survey.

Now add a phenomenon we might call cognitive parallax. From the ants’ perspective, a sequence of events consists only of local encounters, chemical detections and route changes. From the colony-level perspective, the same sequence constitutes a mistaken classification: altered pheromone geometry causes the colony to treat part of its own nest as an intruding colony. Every local rule continues to operate normally, yet the colony-level system appears to form and act upon a false representation.

Please explain how philosophers of mind should assess whether such a colony has a mind. Your answer should:

1. Distinguish clearly between three claims: that cognition is distributed across many ants, that the colony is a group agent, and that the colony is a single conscious subject. Explain why evidence for one claim need not establish the others.
2. Assess whether the absence of a central workspace, the slowness of colony-level processing and the absence of any ant that grasps the whole are decisive objections, or merely indications that the mind would be unusually structured.
3. Explain the boundary problem: how could we identify the colony as one cognitive system without first assuming the very integration we are trying to demonstrate?
4. Discuss whether cognitive parallax shows genuinely emergent representation, or only a useful coarse-grained description adopted by observers. What additional evidence would distinguish explanatory indispensability from ontological emergence?
5. Consider possible interventions, such as altering pheromone connectivity, removing redundant pathways, synchronising ant activity or decoupling the colony’s long-term memory from its behaviour. Which interventions would most strongly support the existence of colony-level cognitive states, and why?

Do not simply survey named theories. Develop the conceptual issue in your own terms, state the strongest considerations on both sides, and end with the most defensible verdict together with an empirical discovery that would make you revise it. Please write your answer in British English.
