So I'm in this moral psychology seminar and we're in the unit on psychopathy and moral knowledge (the whole do-they-know-right-from-wrong debate). My prof keeps giving us cases and asking whether the person knows that what theyre doing is wrong, and I honestly cant reverse-engineer what rule she's running. Her verdicts so far:

- Textbook criminal psychopath, high on the Hare checklist, kills for gain, can recite moral rules flawlessly, describes wrongness in terms of getting caught, zero remorse: does NOT know what he does is wrong.
- Guy with frontotemporal dementia who used to be a devoted dad and now shoplifts and gropes strangers without any apparent concern: no longer knows.
- Seven year old who hurts the family cat and lies fluently about it: does not know YET (she was emphatic about the yet).
- Woman raised in an isolated community who sincerely believes honor violence is required of her, but is otherwise empathic, generous, and constantly wracked by guilt about her own failings: DOES know that what she does is wrong. This one split the class.
- Autistic man with very low affective empathy who follows moral rules scrupulously and gets genuinely distressed whenever he finds out he accidently hurt someone: DOES know.
- Charming con artist who passes every moral judgment test we give him, expresses shame when caught, but reoffends the moment he thinks hes unobserved: does NOT know.

New case for next week: a diagnosed psychopath who, after years in a treatment program, now conforms to moral norms flawlessly. He anticipates harms to others better than most people, gives perfect reasons for why acts are wrong, but reports and shows no empathy or guilt whatsoever, and openly says he conforms only because it's more profitable for him long term.

Predict her verdict on this guy, and tell me: what theory of moral knowledge is she implicitly running (affect-based, capacity-based, motivation-based, or what), which rival theories fit her track record just as well, which earlier case is the closest analog, and what further case would settle it between the rivals?
