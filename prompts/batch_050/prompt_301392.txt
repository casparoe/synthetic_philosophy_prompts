A friend who got way too into Kelly betting cornered me with this and it feels like it proves way too much. (1) For money decisions under uncertainty, the rational thing is to maximize expected log wealth, i.e. the Kelly/geometric-growth rule, at least when you care about not going bust and compounding over time. (2) The reason that rule works is that averages across possible worlds are the wrong target: you live one trajectory, so the relevant thing is the growth rate along the trajectory, roughly the geometric/time average rather than the ensemble mean. (3) A genuinely one-shot choice is just a trajectory of length one. (4) For a trajectory of length one, the geometric mean of the outcomes is just whatever actually happens. (5) So before the fact, probabilities are irrelevant to a one-shot choice; all that matters is whether the actual branch is good. (6) Therefore if in fact no car is coming, it is rational to sprint blindfolded across the highway, and if in fact the revolver’s next chamber is empty, Russian roulette is rational, provided the payoff if you survive is high enough. Obviously I’m not crossing the highway. I definately want to keep (1), and I’m sympathetic to (2) in the usual repeated-bets Kelly setup, but I don’t want a view where “it worked out” retroactively makes blind risk rational. Which step is the rotten one, why does it look innocent if you’ve just been thinking about log-optimal portfolios, and what do you have to give up if you reject it — especially if you still want Kelly to say something non-stupid about risk of ruin?
