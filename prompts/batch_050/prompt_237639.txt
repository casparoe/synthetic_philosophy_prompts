I've gone full Epicurean on death: dying is bad for the people left behind, sure, but it's not bad for *you*. My whole case rests on the mirror arguement from Book III of Lucretius's On the Nature of Things, in William Ellery Leonard's translation:

"Look back:
Nothing to us was all fore-passed eld
Of time the eternal, ere we had a birth.
And Nature holds this like a mirror up
Of time-to-be when we are dead and gone.
And what is there so horrible appears?
Now what is there so sad about it all?
Is't not serener far than any sleep?"

As I read him, this isn't just therapy against fear, it's an actual proof that the two blanks bracketing your life are on a par: since nobody experiences their late birth as having been a deprivation, nobody should experience their early death as one either. I'm even willing to bite the bullet this forces: given two people with equally good lives up to the moment of death, dying at 30 isn't worse *for the person dying* than dying at 80 — extra years are more of the same good, not a greater good you're robbed of.
