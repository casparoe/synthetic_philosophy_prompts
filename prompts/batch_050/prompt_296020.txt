I'm in a bioethics seminar and we just did the unit on why most of Europe bans or heavily restricts commercial surrogacy and egg donation while the US basically has a market for both. I can't figure out what I actually think, so here are my gut verdicts on cases -- tell me what principle (if any) unifies them.

- A woman carries a baby for her infertile sister, no payment beyond expenses: totally fine.
- A US agency matches a couple with a surrogate who's paid ~$50k, both sides have lawyers: also fine, honestly.
- Paying a woman $8k for eggs, which involves hormone injections and a retrieval procedure: fine.
- Selling a kidney for $50k: not fine, even with informed consent and good medical care.
- Selling plasma twice a week for $40 a pop: fine, and it bugs me that this sits next to the kidney one.
- A surrogacy contract that's legally enforceable against the surrogate herself, i.e. she can be forced to hand over the baby or sued for damages if she changes her mind: not fine, even though the paid arrangement itself is fine.
- A wealthy American couple paying a woman in a much poorer country a sum that's life-changing for her but would be exploitative-sounding in dollars: I keep flip-flopping, but my gut says not fine, which seems inconsistent with my verdict on the domestic case.

Is there a coherent principle that gets most of these -- something about what's being sold vs. what the seller keeps, or about which risks money can't compensate for? If no single principle gets all of them, which of my verdicts should I drop, and what would the resulting view say about a case I haven't listed, like paying people to enroll in risky clinical trials?
