So for context: longtime LW/EA forum reader here, and the whole "moral trade" / gains-from-trade-across-value-systems cluster of ideas has been rattling around my head since forever. The classic setup is two people with different cause priorities who both do better by cooperating (like the vegan and the meat-eater who each give up something the other cares more about, or two donors who swap donations), and then there's the fancier stuff about maximizing expected choiceworthiness under moral uncertainty, parliamentary models of your own values, etc. But I've noticed that when this comes up in comment sections people talk completely past each other, because "should you compromise with a value system you think is wrong" secretly bundles like five different questions. One person hears it as "is integrity a side constraint," another hears it as "can you even compare value across theories," another hears "does moral uncertainty give you reasons at all."

So here's what I want: a decision tree (or sorting quiz, whichever you think works better, but I'm leaning tree) that takes someone from the starting position "I think my own values are probably not the one true morality, and I sometimes have opportunities to cooperate with people whose values differ from mine" to a named-ish position on moral compromise. The leaves should include at least: the naive gains-from-trade view (compromise is great, treat it like any other trade), expected-choiceworthiness maximization, the parliamentary/bargaining-internal view, the "just act on your own values, moral uncertainty doesn't change first-order obligations" position (favoritism, I think people call it?), the integrity-constraint view where some trades are corrosive even if mutually beneficial, and a deflationary leaf where inter-theoretic comparison is just impossible so the whole question dissolves. Each branch node should be a concrete question someone can answer without knowing the literature — e.g. a case like "your friend thinks cause X is 100x more important than your cause, you think the reverse, do you split your donations?" or "would you trade away something you consider a rights violation if the other side gives up something you weight heavier?" — not "do you believe in intertheoretic comparability?" Phrase the nodes so the tree sorts by revealed intuitions about cases, not by which label sounds smart.

Two more asks. First, build the tree from the logical space where possible: if some combination of answers is unoccupied (like "uncertainty gives reasons + comparison is possible + but you should never trade"), note that it's empty and say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just underexplored. And flag pairs of leaves your tree can't really separate. Second, please be honest about epistemic status: distinguish what's basically settled in the small literature on this (if anything) from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a contested move — like the analogy between moral uncertainty and empirical uncertainty — as if it's the accepted framework. I'd rather get "nobody agrees on this part" than a false consensus.
