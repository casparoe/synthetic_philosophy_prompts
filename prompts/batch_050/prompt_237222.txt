I am a novelist in the early stages of planning a book, and I need a thought experiment constructed to specifications — one I intend to expand, later, into the spine of the plot. The book concerns a small community reckoning with its own past, and the intellectual scaffolding I want is drawn from Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation, where he distinguishes three ways a person or culture may relate to history. In the monumental mode, the past is a gallery of great exemplars whose existence proves that greatness is possible and spurs the present to emulate it. In the antiquarian mode, the past is an inheritance to be preserved, catalogued, and revered simply because it is ours — the mode of the loyal custodian. In the critical mode, the past is hauled before a tribunal and condemned, so that the living may free themselves from what they inherited. Nietzsche's claim is that each mode serves life in the right measure and injures it in the wrong one. What I want from you is a scenario in which that claim can be watched happening.

Construct a single fictional case — one community, one fixed body of historical fact — in which all three modes are brought to bear on the same past, and in which each mode observably turns from use to disadvantage without the facts themselves changing. The specifications:

1. Realism. Set the case in a plausible society of the last two centuries, with facts an ordinary community could actually have: a founding event, a revered ancestor, an old wrong. No prophetic revelations, no fabricated documents surfacing at convenient moments, no device that does the philosophical work for you.
2. Isolation. The historical record must remain exactly the same throughout; only the mode of engagement with it may vary. If a turn in the story depends on someone learning something new, the case has failed — the point is that the same past nourishes or poisons depending on how it is held.
3. Generated, not stipulated. For each mode, show the mechanism of the flip: what the mode initially gives the community (courage, continuity, liberation) and the precise way the selfsame activity, carried further, begins to damage the living (exemplars that crush rather than inspire, preservation that mummifies, judgment that leaves nothing standing). A case that merely announces that monumental history has "gone too far" stipulates the verdict rather than deriving it; for my purposes that counts as cheating, since I need a plot whose turns follow from the structure of the case, not from authorial decree.
4. One character, two modes. Include at least one character who inhabits two different modes at different times, and whose trajectory shows what it costs to move between them.

After the scenario (which should be no more than four hundred words), supply the demonstration: a mode-by-mode account of the verdict each delivers at each stage and why, following the structure of Nietzsche's argument rather than my summary of it; an honest identification of the single design feature of your case that does most of the work, and of what happens to the case if that feature is removed; and finally, the strongest objection a careful reader of Nietzsche could raise — for instance, that the disadvantages you depict belong to the dose rather than the mode, so that your scenario shows only that people misuse history, not that history's modes carry their antidotes within them.
