Is there a name for this thing I keep noticing? My uncle is deep into a particular political scene, and the standard story would be that he never encounters the other side -- but that's just false. He watches the mainstream news constantly, he reads op-eds from people he disagrees with, he can recite their arguments better than I can. The weird part is what the exposure *does*. Every time he runs into an opposing argument, it gets processed through this ready-made story about why the person is making it -- they're chasing ratings, they got captured by their university, they have to say that to keep their job. And here's the kicker: the more opposing arguments he hears, the *more* confident he gets, because each one is another data point confirming the story about why those people talk the way they do. He's not in an information desert at all. He's drowning in the other side's content and it only makes him stronger in his views. I've seen the same pattern in a running-shoe forum I'm on, of all places, where people keep trying out the rival brand's shoes specifically so they can come back and report on why the rival's fans only like them for tribal reasons.

People tell me "that's an echo chamber" but I've also seen echo chamber used for just... not hearing the other side, which is the opposite of his situation, he's practically mainlining the other side. And it's not exactly that he thinks outsiders are liars or idiots either -- he often grants they're sincere, he just thinks sincerity is explained by something other than the argument being right, and the explanation is unfalsifiable from the inside because any evidence against it is just more of the thing being explained. So: is there an established concept for this specific mechanism, where engagement with disagreement is itself the engine of conviction? If the literature's closest notions only cover part of it, tell me which parts they cover and where my uncle's case falls outside them -- I don't want just a label, I want the carveing. Also please say how confident you are in the identification and flag the single point you're least sure about.
