So I write for the science desk at a general-audience magazine, mostly covering AI, and I'm deep into a feature on interpretability research. Here's the situation I keep hitting: researchers will say things like "the fine-tuned model uses the same mechanism as the base model, just repurposed" or "these two independently trained networks converged on the same circuit," or conversely "this model looks behaviorally identical but it's doing something different under the hood, which is why the anomaly detector has to catch it." Everyone talks as if there's a fact of the matter about when two mechanisms are *the same mechanism*, and nobody has ever given me the criteria. I want the criteria.

Write me the desiderata list: what would a theory of "same mechanism" (for learned systems specifically — neural nets, not clocks or carburetors) have to get right before I'd be justified putting it in print? To keep you honest, here's the terrain the list has to cover, based on what the people I interview actually fight about:

- Two networks trained from different random seeds that seem to have found strikingly similar features. Same mechanism, or just same job done differently?
- One network, before and after fine-tuning on a new task, where the behavior on the old task barely changes.
- A network where you permute a bunch of neurons (or rotate a basis) and literally nothing about the function changes. Is that "the same mechanism"? If your theory says yes automatically, it feels like it collapses mechanism-identity into function-identity; if it says no automatically, it feels like it counts relabelings as real differences.
- Cases where part of the mechanism is shared and part isn't — partial overlap, not all-or-nothing.
- The constraint that the theory has to be *checkable by humans with finite compute*, since the whole point of anomaly detection is that you don't get to just know the answer.

For each desideratum, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, not just the label. And the part I most want: tell me whether some of these requirements pull against each other — e.g. I have a suspicion that demanding invariance under basis changes and demanding that the mechanism be *localizable* in particular weights can't both be fully satisfied once you take seriously that features can be spread across neurons in superposition, but I want that worked out properly, not vibes.

Format it like this, roughly:

**The full spec** — the requirements list with the motivating case for each; this should be the bulk of the piece, maybe 500-600 words.
**Negotiable vs. non-negotiable** — which desiderata any adequate theory must meet, and which are really just preferences of one research camp; ~150 words.
**Where the list fights itself** — any jointly unsatisfiable subsets, and what you'd have to relax to escape; ~200 words.
**The load-bearing requirement** — the single desideratum that knocks out the most criteria people actually use in practice, and why; ~100 words.
**Bottom line** — two or three sentences I could basically lift into the article.

Don't name-drop specific papers or give me citations, I'll do the sourcing myself. Casual but precise is the register.
