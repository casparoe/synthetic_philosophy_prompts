I was arguing with friends after a moral philosophy seminar about the torture-versus-dust-specks problem, and I still think the aggregation side is basically forced. Put aside the cartoonishly large numbers: if one person undergoing severe torture is worse than one person getting a dust speck in the eye, and a dust speck is still a genuine harm, then there must be some number of dust-speck sufferers whose total badness exceeds the torture, just as enough grains of sand eventually outweigh a boulder. So if a totalist denies that conclusion, aren’t they either smuggling in a lexical threshold while pretending to aggregate, or else admitting that tiny harms were never morally real in the first place? I want the strongest diagnosis of where this reasoning equivocates: between intensity and extent, between harm to one subject and harm distributed across many subjects, between finite and merely astronomical quantities, or somewhere else.
