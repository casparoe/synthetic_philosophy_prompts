A friend and I have been litigating the British Raj for like a year now. She's basically on the Niall Ferguson side -- empire as an early globalization project, railways, common law, parliamentary institutions, the English language, and on net a decent deal for a subcontinent that would've been fragmented and warring otherwise. I'm basically on the Shashi Tharoor side -- two centuries of extraction dressed up as civilizing mission, a deliberately deindustrialized India, famines made far worse by policy, and the railways built to drain the country faster not to develop it.

We keep talking past each other, so we've agreed to try an adversarial collaboration instead. Draft the joint statement we should come out of it with. Concretely I want:

1. A numbered list of claims we can BOTH sign. Not mushy stuff like "the Raj was complicated" -- actual substantive claims, e.g. about what happened to India's share of the world economy, about the famines, about who the railways primarily served, about what pre-colonial India was actually like. The test is that a careful Ferguson-ite and a careful Tharoor-ite would both go "yes, fine" to each one.

2. A disagreements section where each remaining dispute is stated in a single sentence both of us would accept as a fair statement of what we're disagreeing ABOUT, and then each side's position phrased so that side would sign off on the wording. No strawmen, no loaded verbs.

3. The cruxes. My guess is the big ones are: (a) the counterfactual -- what does India's trajectory look like in 1947 with no Raj, and is that even a coherent question given how much of "India" the Raj created; (b) whether intent matters to the balance sheet, i.e. does it change the verdict that the railways/telegraphs/legal system were built for extraction if they turned out useful later; (c) how you weigh a large pile of famine deaths against diffuse institutional legacies that are hard to quantify; (d) whether the whole "balance sheet" framing is a mistake, since some goods and harms might just not be commensurable. Feel free to add cruxes I've missed or merge ones that are secretly the same.

4. For each crux, at least one concrete piece of evidence or case that BOTH sides agree bears on it -- a specific famine policy, a specific trade statistic, the post-independence growth record compared to some non-colonized country, whatever -- and then what each side predicts or how each side reads it. The point is that the evidence has to be something we both accept as relevant before we start interpreting it.

Also, at the end, be honest about which of our disagreements are actually empirical, which are normative (how to weigh things), and which are just us using words differently -- I suspect at least one of our running fights is purely verbal and neither of us has noticed.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section, then the disagreements (a table is fine), then the cruxes with their evidence, then the empirical/normative/verbal sorting. Write it like a real document we'd both sign, not like an essay about the debate.
