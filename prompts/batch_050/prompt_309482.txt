ok so i'm on the review team for a citizen-science biosignature project -- we get weird samples from analog field sites and have to decide which ones trigger the full we-might-have-found-life protocol -- and before i read one more paper proposing yet another definition of life i want the spec written down first. not the definitions themselves. the requirements a definition has to meet to be usable by us.

so write the desiderata: what any candidate definition of life must deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. the cases it has to give a verdict on (or give a principled reason for refusing a verdict) should include at least: a dessicated tardigrade that's shown no measurable metabolism for a decade, a mule, a human red blood cell (no DNA at all, no division, clearly ours), a virus, a candle flame (grows, consumes fuel, responds to its environment, can even reproduce if you split it), a self-copying chemical cycle in a hydrothermal vent that predates anything cellular, and a digital organism in an artificial-life simulation that genuinely evolves. and the distinctions it can't collapse: being alive as a property of a particular thing vs life as a historical lineage or a phenomenon; living vs merely life-like; and the operational definition we use to detect stuff vs the definition of what life actually is -- if those come apart, say which one the desiderata should target.

for each desideratum tell me whether it's genuinely non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp, and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. i have a feeling that demanding clean necessary-and-sufficient checklist conditions and demanding the definition handle the lineage cases pull in opposite directions, but i want to see it argued rather than asserted. that joint-unsatisfiability section is honestly the part i care most about.

then: which single requirement disqualifies the most popular contenders in one move (i'm guessing the mule kills every reproduction-based definition outright, but tell me if i'm wrong), and a quick pass on how the main families do against the list -- metabolism-first accounts, the NASA-style working definition about self-sustaining chemical systems capable of darwinian evolution, autopoiesis, and the thermodynamic or entropy-flux accounts. keep the scoring brief, the criteria are where the thinking is.

fix the purpose as: good enough to make actual keep / investigate / reject calls on ambiguous samples, not good enough to settle the metaphysics. any desideratum that only matters for the metaphysics version should be marked as such rather than smuggled in as universal.

give me the main list as a table with columns: desideratum | motivating case | why it's a requirement | non-negotiable or camp preference | which definition families it knocks out. then the conflicts in a second smaller table: pair of desiderata | why they conflict | which one you'd drop and why. no reading lists, no citations, no bibliography.
