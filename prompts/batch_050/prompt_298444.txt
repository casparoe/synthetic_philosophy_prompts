In six weeks I will lead the final meeting of a reading group of professionals -- mostly lawyers and physicians, no philosophical training -- on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. I have ninety minutes. Plan how I should present the argument rather than presenting it to me.

Specify the order in which the components must be introduced and why each depends on what came before: the distinction between everyday responsibility and the "ultimate" responsibility Strawson targets; the premise that one is responsible for what one does only insofar as one is responsible for how one is, mentally; the demand this generates for self-determination of one's own nature; and the regress that follows. A decision I need defended: whether to stage the regress as literally infinite or as terminating in one's unchosen endowment (genes, upbringing, circumstance), since the group will press hard on which it is. I want to introduce the argument without any mention of determinism, because two members are committed libertarians who will otherwise file it as the determinism debate they already have opinions about; the plan should indicate the precise point at which I reveal that the argument is meant to work even if determinism is false, and how to make that landing rather than let it feel like a bait-and-switch.

Choose the single carrying example on which the whole regress will be run -- say what it must contain and what it must scrupulously lack (a dramatic crime, a sympathetic protagonist, controversial politics) and why a trivial choice may serve better than a weighty one.

Map the misconceptions I should expect, in the order they will surface, and mark exactly where in the sequence each is headed off: that this is determinism in disguise; that one shapes one's character through earlier choices (I want the group to generate the regress themselves here, not be handed it); that causa sui is so obviously impossible that the argument proves nothing interesting; and that Strawson is therefore recommending we stop blaming and punishing anyone.

Finally: one closing question that reveals whether they grasped the either-way structure or merely refiled the argument as determinism, and one thing -- the compatibilist replies, the impossibilist literature, or otherwise -- that I should explicitly decline to cover at this level.
