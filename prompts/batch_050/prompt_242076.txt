Question: Using Zorn's lemma, prove that every vector space has a basis. Your proof should (i) specify the partially ordered set to which Zorn's lemma is applied, (ii) verify that every chain in it has an upper bound, and (iii) explain why the maximal element so obtained is a basis.

Student's answer:

Let V be a vector space and let P be the collection of all linearly independent subsets of V, partially ordered by inclusion. P is nonempty because the empty set is linearly independent.

Now let C be any chain in P and put U = ⋃C. Then U is an upper bound for C: clearly every member of C is a subset of U, and U is itself linearly independent, since linear independence is preserved under taking unions. Hence U ∈ P, and Zorn's lemma applies.

By Zorn's lemma, P has a greatest element B. To see that B spans V, suppose some vector v lay outside the span of B. Then B ∪ {v} would be a linearly independent set strictly larger than B, contradicting the maximality of B. So every element of V is a linear combination of elements of B, and B, being both linearly independent and spanning, is a basis. Moreover, since Zorn's lemma selects the greatest element of P, this basis is unique.
