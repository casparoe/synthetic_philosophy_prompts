My reading group has spent the term on mind uploading, and next week I'm presenting on gradual replacement scenarios specifically -- the idea that if you swap a person's neurons for functionally identical artificial components one at a time, slowly enough, the person plausibly survives, and so destructive scanning is no worse than this and uploading is fine. I've been collecting the thought experiments that bear on this, and so far I have: (1) Chalmers's fading and dancing qualia cases, where silicon chips replace neurons gradually and either consciousness fades or flips in ways that seem absurd given functional preservation; (2) Moravec's neuron-by-neuron transfer, where a surgeon replaces each neuron while the patient stays awake and reports continuity of experience throughout; (3) overnight nanobot replacement, where you go to sleep biological and wake up synthetic with no noticed change; (4) Parfit's teletransportation with destructive scanning, included because gradualists argue it is not relevantly worse than slow replacement; and (5) the classic brain-as-Ship-of-Theseus case, where the replacement is so slow that at every moment there is more than enough overlap of parts and psychology to count as the same continuing person.
