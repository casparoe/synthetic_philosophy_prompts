I'm running a 75-minute session of an upper-undergraduate philosophy of language seminar (16 students, split into four groups of four). Design a structured activity that lets students feel the force of Davidson's radical interpretation from the inside. The setup should have each group play the role of a field linguist confronting a speaker whose language is entirely unknown to them, working only from a curated sequence of the speaker's utterances paired with observable circumstances -- build this on invented materials rather than any real language, so no group has an unfair head start and the materials themselves can be engineered. Two constraints on the design: first, the materials should be constructed so that assigning the speaker mostly-true beliefs is the only strategy that yields any interpretation at all, so that students discover the principle of charity as a working necessity rather than receiving it as a doctrine; second, partway through, reveal to two of the four groups (without telling the others) that their speaker's utterances were generated partly at random, so that different groups end up committed to different verdicts about whether their interpretation was ever tracking anything -- and let that asymmetry surface naturally in the debrief when groups compare notes. Please specify the utterance materials (or a precise recipe for generating them), roles within each group, a minute-by-minute timeline, and the deliverable each group produces. The debrief should map specific moments of the activity onto Davidson's commitments (charity as constitutive of interpretation, the impossibility of massive error, and the threat of indeterminacy when multiple translations fit the evidence equally well), anticipate what will predictably go wrong -- e.g., groups smuggling in English syntax, or treating the random-utterance twist as mere cheating rather than as a live question about when interpretation fails -- and say frankly which feature of radical interpretation the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied by discussion afterward.
