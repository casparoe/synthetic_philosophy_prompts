So I'm an engineering manager at a mid-size software company, and my CEO just gave me a pot of money — about $120k — to spend on my team of 8 however I want, as long as I can justify the choice. I've been going back and forth for two weeks and I keep going in circles, so I'm hoping someone can help me think straight.

Basically three options are on the table:

1. Merit-weighted bonuses. The money goes to whoever had the most impact this year. If what you care about is the total benefit to the team, this clearly wins — the money does the most good this way, the people who carried us get rewarded, and overall morale probably goes up the most. But two or three people get almost nothing, and they'll know exactly why.

2. A flat equal bonus for everyone. Nobody gets left out, it's "fair" in the simple sense. But it's obviously worse in total terms — a flat $15k barely registers for the senior folks, and a couple people who killed themselves this year will feel pretty slighted.

3. A mix: a decent flat amount for everyone, a modest merit component on top, plus a chunk spent on stuff the whole team shares (better equipment, a real offsite). The weird thing about this option is that it's never the BEST under any single way of looking at the problem, but it's also never bad — everyone ends up clearly better off than today whether you care about totals, or about the people at the bottom, or about treating everyone identically.

Here's what's bugging me. Whenever I sit down and try to commit to one principle — "maximize total benefit" or "protect the worst off" or "treat everyone the same" — option 3 loses to one of the other two. Every framework I try ranks it second at best. And yet it's the only option that comes out fine no matter wich principle turns out to be the right one, and honestly I have no idea which principle is right. I'm starting to suspect nobody really does.

So what should I do? Is it rational to pick option 3 purely because it's the safe choice under every standard, even though it tops out under none of them? Or is the responsible thing to just pick the principle I can argue for best and follow it where it leads, even if that means some people get burned if I chose wrong?

One extra wrinkle: the team is split too. The senior folks want #1, the junior folks want #2. Does it matter that the actual people affected disagree about which standard is right? If every one of them would take option 3 over me deadlocking and returning the money, does that count for something all by itself — or is leaning on that just me dodging the hard question?
