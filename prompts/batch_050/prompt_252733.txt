So my working position is that life should be defined functionally: a thing is alive iff it actively keeps itself in a low-entropy, out-of-equilibrium state by taking in energy and matter and using them to maintain and repair its own organization. Metabolism-plus-self-maintenance, roughly. Reproduction, on my view, is a downstream trick that some living systems happen to use, not part of the definition -- which lets me count mules, sterile worker ants, and hypothetical immortal non-reproducing organisms as alive without apology.

What would count against this? I want a map of its exposure, not just a list of the usual counterexamples. Specifically:

1. Which cases does it genuinely fail to describe, and how bad is each failure? The obvious stress tests: viruses, dormant bacterial spores and tardigrades in cryptobiosis (alive while metabolizing nothing?), fire and candle flames (self-maintaining, far from equilibrium, yet presumably not alive), a single enzyme in a cell-free system, and wet-lab minimal synthetic cells. For each, say whether it forces a real revision of the view or whether the view can bite the bullet comfortably.

2. What observations or experimental results would actually move me? E.g., if someone built a self-maintaining far-from-equilibrium chemical system that everyone agrees is just chemistry and not alive, is that a defeater for my criterion or does my criterion just absorb it by declaring it alive? Distinguish defeaters that would kill the view from ones that would merely make it awkward or revisionary about common usage.

3. What would the defenders of this kind of view themselves concede as counting against it -- as opposed to what critics of it say? I suspect committed defenders have quietly moved the goalposts at least once (e.g., adding requirements about information, heredity, or individuality), and I'd like the honest version of that history.

4. Has the position been qualified into something unfalsifiable? If every apparent counterexample gets absorbed by reclassifying the case as alive or as not-really-self-maintaining, is that a vice, or is it just what a stipulative definition looks like and fine for the purposes I'd actually use it for (astrobiology and origin-of-life research, mostly)?

Also: my view's main rival is the evolution-based definition (life = a system capable of Darwinian evolution). Say what my view predicts or permits that the rival doesn't, and where the rival cleanly wins, because if there's a case where the rival is obviously better and my view has no independent motivation, that's probably the most decisive thing you could tell me.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence -- the single most serious threat to the position -- and then give the reasoning, sorted roughly by how decisive each consideration would be. No preamble, no restating the question back to me.
