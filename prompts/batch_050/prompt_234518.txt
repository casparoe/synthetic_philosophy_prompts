so I write a science-and-ideas column and I'm working up a piece on consciousness, and I keep hitting the same move from the people I interview. Consciousness, they say, is like the desktop of a computer: the folders and trash can and little document icons aren't what's really going on inside the machine, they're a simplified cartoon the system throws up so you can get things done without ever touching the actual circuitry. And the introspective sense that my experience of red, or my feeling of wanting coffee, is just directly given and self-evidently real -- that's supposedly like a user insisting the folder icon really is a little manila folder inside the laptop. The stronger version of the argument is that this licenses skepticism about phenomenal consciousness itself: if the interface is a fiction, maybe the vivid inner show is one too, or at least nothing like what it seems.

Audit the analogy for me. Give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the icons, the files, the operating system, the user sitting at the screen, the engineers who designed the interface. Then tell me where it breaks -- and specifically which breaks matter for the conclusion and which are just cosmetic. Two places I suspect trouble but can't nail down: the computer case has a user who is genuinely separate from the system, whereas in the brain the 'user' of the interface would have to be the system itself, and I don't know if that's a fatal disanalogy or the whole point; and the desktop was deliberately designed by someone with goals, while whatever simplification consciousness is came out of evolution, which may or may not matter. Also: does the analogy quietly assume there's a faithful 'real file' version of the world that the icon distorts, and is that assumption doing work the arguers haven't earned? Finally, once the disanalogies are on the table, does the strong conclusion (phenomenal experience is a kind of useful fiction) survive, or only the weak one (introspection is unreliable about mechanism)? If there's a better analogy for the same conclusion, or a better conclusion for the same analogy, say so. American English please, and dont feel the need to be diplomatic about either side.
