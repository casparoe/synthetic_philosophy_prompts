So a few of us in my department's philosophy of biology reading group have this running joke where we each had to write down our "official position" on what counts as an organism, and mine is getting pushback and I want a real audit before I defend it at our next session. For context, I'm not a biologist, I've just read around a lot and these are the views I've landed on. Here they are:

(1) What makes something an organism is functional and metabolic integration -- the parts are mutually dependent and work as one coordinated physiological whole. Shared genetics is NOT the criterion, and neither is "what selection acts on," which I think is a separate question. (2) Because of that, I say a siphonophore like the Portuguese man o' war is a single organism, not a colony of organisms -- the zooids can't survive on their own and are as integrated as my organs are. (3) I also think an ant colony is a genuine superorganism, an organism at the colony level, because only the queen reproduces, the workers are like somatic cells, and natural selection operates at the level of the whole colony. (4) But I think the whole "holobiont" thing is overhyped -- me plus my gut microbiome is not one organism, and neither is an aphid plus its Buchnera bacteria, even though the aphid literally can't make essential amino acids without them. Those are just intimate partnerships between two organisms. (5) And mitochondria are just parts of me, full stop, not symbionts, regardless of the fact that they descend from free-living bacteria and still carry there own genome.

One friend says (3) and (4) can't both stand but won't say why, another says the real problem is between (1) and (2), which I don't see at all. So: which pairs of these are actually in tension, and which only look like they are until you think about them? Please rank the tensions by how serious they are rather than just listing everything, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be -- like, does dropping one claim fix two problems, or do I need to qualify the criterion in (1)? Also curious whether treating organismhood as a matter of degree rescues the whole set or just papers over the contradiction.
