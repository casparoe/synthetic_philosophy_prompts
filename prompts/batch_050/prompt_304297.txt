I co-teach a med school ethics elective with a clinician friend, about 14 students, and we've got one 80-minute session on the ethics of randomized trials. Design an activity that makes them feel why equipoise is so hard to hold onto -- not just hear it defined. Here's roughly the shape I have in mind, but tell me if it works and fix what doesn't: I want them to actually run a mini trial. Some students are investigators enrolling patients, a couple are the data monitoring committee watching results accumulate, maybe one or two are outside treating physicians with a strong hunch from their own practice. Give them a real outcome-generating mechanism -- cards, dice, whatever -- so that arm A genuinely starts to look better than arm B round by round, but noisly, and after each round the group has to vote on whether to randomize the next patient or stop the trial. The moment I want them to hit on their own: there's a stretch of rounds where everyone at the table privately believes one arm is winning, the stats haven't caught up yet, and they keep sending patients to the worse arm anyway. And a second beat: the doc with the hunch lost equipoise on round one, but the wider community hasn't -- so whose uncertainty gets to count, the individual's or the profession's?

Give me: full rules, the roles and what info each role is allowed to see, the outcome mechanism, a timeline for the 80 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the individual-vs-community equipoise distinction (which I won't name until the debrief). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (someone will want to peek at the deck, someone will argue the mechanism is rigged, the vote will get steamrolled, whatever you foresee) and how to handle each, plus an honest note on what the activity can't reproduce -- real trials don't let anyone watch the noise this cleanly, patients aren't cards, stopping rules are pre-registered, etc.

Start with the activity itself -- setup and rules first, no preamble about what equipoise is or why the topic matters. I already know why it matters.
