Two of my colleagues — a dialetheist in the tradition of Graham Priest, who holds that certain sentences (the liar above all) are both true and false, and a defender of classical logic, who treats explosion as valid and the paradoxes as calling for a consistent restriction of naive truth — have agreed to stop talking past each other and produce a joint statement. Draft that document for them. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both parties can sign, covering at minimum what the liar paradox genuinely shows, that trivialism is unacceptable to both, that classical reasoning is fine within consistent domains, and what each side's formal machinery actually delivers; (2) their residual disagreements, each phrased so that the side it describes would accept the wording — including whether the dialetheist can coherently express the thought that some sentences are only false rather than true as well, and whether the classical logician's favored repair (hierarchy, truth-value gaps, or a revision rule) solves the paradox or merely relocates it; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party, and for each crux an argument, case, or formal result both agree bears on it — candidates include Curry's paradox, the classical-recapture problem, and whether any serious empirical theory has ever required a true contradiction — with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and (4) wherever the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, an explicit identification of which part and why. Write in American English.
