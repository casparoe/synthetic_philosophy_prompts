For a decision theory seminar, I want the specification before the solutions. Write the desiderata a decision procedure must meet to count as an adequate response to Pascal's mugging -- the situation where an agent is offered an astronomically large payoff conditional on an event of minuscule probability, and naive expected value says pay up. Give the case or consideration behind each requirement: which situations the theory must get right (the mugging itself, fair lotteries, insurance, low-probability medical treatments, funding work on small-probability catastrophic risks); which distinctions it may not collapse (between discounting tiny probabilities and capping large payoffs; between rejecting the offer and refusing to think about it; between a one-shot refusal and a policy that survives iteration); which structural properties it must retain (dominance reasoning, transitivity, resistance to sequential exploitation). Mark which desiderata are genuine constraints and which are the prejudices of a particular camp, flag any subset you believe cannot be jointly satisfied and say exactly what must be given up, and identify the single desideratum that excludes the most contenders (unbounded expected utility, probability cutoffs, bounded utility, lexicographic thresholds, risk-weighted alternatives). Weight the answer toward the criteria; keep the scorecard brief.
