I run operations for a regional pest-control company — about forty technicians, several thousand service visits a year across restaurants, schools, warehouses, and homes. Our industry's code of ethics says we should minimize animal suffering, but nobody has ever turned that into something a technician can actually use on site. I want a decision procedure a tech can run in about five minutes when scoping a job, one that takes the possibility of insect suffering seriously without demanding certainty about insect sentience.

The procedure should be built from the considerations the ethics literature actually offers, translated into operational form: how to reason under moral uncertainty about sentience (expected-disvalue reasoning rather than all-or-nothing verdicts on whether insects matter); how control methods differ in the duration and intensity of the suffering they plausibly cause (instant mechanical kill, neurotoxic sprays, desiccant dusts, glue traps, slow starvation); and — this is the part I most want done carefully — the replacement effect: lethal removal opens niche space that conspecifics recolonize, so killing may reduce no suffering at all over a season, whereas exclusion, sanitation, and habitat modification change the equilibrium population. The procedure should specify the questions the technician asks in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, at what point a method is ruled out regardless of client preference, and where it stops and hands the decision to a supervisor because the trade-off (say, insect suffering against a child's allergy or a restaurant's health code) is a values call rather than a technical one.

Then run the procedure on three cases: a German cockroach infestation in a restaurant kitchen, a wasp nest above a school playground entrance, and a warehouse client paying for a standing monthly preventive spray with no active infestation. At least one should come out in a way a conventional pest-control company will not like — say so explicitly if none do. Close with what the procedure cannot settle: which numbers it cannot supply (sentience probabilities, exchange rates between insect suffering and human health) and how we should handle those gaps without letting them quietly decide everything.
