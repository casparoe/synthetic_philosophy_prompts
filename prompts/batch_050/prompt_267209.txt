So I'm a third-year PhD student working on the metaphysics of propositions, and my cohort runs this end-of-semester thing where everyone has to present their dissertation chapter in a "non-standard format" (last year someone did a puppet show about grounding, it was a whole thing). My chapter is on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and I've decided to do a villanelle, because the form seems almost too perfect for the content and I want to actually exploit that instead of just writing 19 lines that namecheck Bradley.

Here's what I mean, and this is the part I need you to get right. The regress says: if a proposition is just a list of constituents, say a and F-ness, then a list isn't a proposition -- you need something that unifies them, call it a relation R. But now you've got three items (a, F-ness, R) and no more unity than before, so you need another relation to relate R to the original two, and so on forever. The two horns are basically: either the unifier is just one more constituent and the whole thing falls apart into a heap again, or you posit an actual infinity of relations, which nobody wants to sign their name to. I want the two refrains of the villanelle to BE the two horns -- one refrain for "add a relation and you just get a longer list," one for the infinite-chain horn -- so that the villanelle's machinery, where the refrains keep coming back no matter how the tercets try to escape them, enacts the way the regress keeps reasserting itself every time you think you've dissolved it. And then in the final quatrain, where the two refrains have to appear back to back, I want that to land as the moment of facing the actual choice: the poem should end by gesturing at the standard escape route (the unifier is not another object or constituent at all -- it's sui generis, unsaturated, whatever metaphor you like) without pretending that escape is free, since saying the copula is special is arguably just naming the mystery.

A few constraints: please write in American English, keep to the actual villanelle form (five tercets, final quatrain, proper refrain scheme), and no archaic thee/thou stuff, I want it readable out loud. Someone who knows the literature should be able to tell which refrain is which horn and why the form is doing philosophical work; someone who doesn't should still get a decent poem out of it. Also maybe nothing too jokey -- it's going in front of my committee, and one of them wrote a book on facts.
