So I'm drafting an essay on democratic backsliding and I keep running into the same two images, from both sides basically. Everyone says Hungary, Turkey, maybe the US depending who you ask, is a slow-motion coup -- the coup is still happening, it's just wearing a suit and getting reelected. And the other one is the boiling frog: each individual norm violation is small enough that the public never jumps, and by the time the water's actually hot the courts and the media are already captured.

Both are doing argumentative work, not just decoration. The slow-motion coup line is used to license the conclusion that what's happening isn't normal politics and emergency-style responses are justified. The frog line is used to explain why voters don't revolt and to warn that "it still feels democratic" is no evidence that it is.

Audit both for me. Give me the actual correspondence tables -- what plays the role of the coup plotters, the seized TV station, the moment of takeover; what plays the role of the frog, the burner, the heat, and the jumping reflex. Then where each mapping breaks. Some things that bug me already: a coup has a clear point where the constitution stops binding, but backsliding cases often keep using constitutional procedures the whole way through, sometimes even amending them legally. A frog supposedly can't feel the temperature gradient, but voters, judges, and opposition parties can and do notice, and sometimes they turn the heat back down -- which either breaks the analogy or just means the frog occasionally wins. Also both images seem to smuggle in a single intentional agent turning the dial, and I'm not sure backsliding always has one.

Which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusions each image is being used to support, and which are just pedantic? And does either conclusion survive once you state the disanalogies out loud -- is "slow-motion coup" anything more than a way of saying "I think this counts as authoritarianism" without arguing for it? If there's a better analogy for what these cases actually look like (termite damage to a house? rule-bending that eventually breaks the game?), sketch it and say what it gets right that the other two dont.
