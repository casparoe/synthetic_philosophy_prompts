Here's something that's been bugging me about the paradox of tragedy. Construct a thought experiment with two versions of the same sad artwork -- could be a weepie film, a sad country song, whatever, but something a normal person would actually consume, not gallery art -- where the two versions are engineered to split two families of theories. Family one: the suffering is a cost we pay to get goods like insight, beauty, or catharsis, and the pleasure outweighs the pain. Family two: the sadness itself is valued as part of the experience, not just paid for. Constraints: the two versions can't differ in overall artistic quality or in how much insight they deliver, otherwise family one can just say the sadder version is better art and the case settles nothing; no appeal to "the sad ending is narratively required" for the same reason; scenario in one paragraph. After the scenario, walk through what each family says a person should prefer and why, identify which feature of the case is doing the discriminating work, and tell me how the family that comes out looking worse could attack the case -- e.g., by denying that you can really strip the sadness out while keeping the insight, since the sadness might be the vehicle of it. Say whether you think that defense succeeds.
