For a one-off workshop on escalation of commitment (25 undergraduates, 75 minutes), design a structured activity in which participants actually get trapped in a losing course of action rather than merely learning that such traps exist. The complication: the workshop opens with a short lecture on the dollar auction and how it works, so everyone in the room already knows the classic setup, its dynamics, and its point. Running the auction itself is therefore out, and so is anything participants will recognize as a reskinning of it within the first few minutes. Design something with rules and real (small) stakes in which people who can describe entrapment perfectly well still find themselves investing past the point where they intended to stop, and in which their foreknowledge is, if anything, part of why they stay in. Specify the setup, the rules, and the timeline; give the debrief, mapping specific moments of the activity onto the components of escalation (sunk costs, the shift from trying to win to trying not to lose, face-saving, the sense that quitting now wastes everything invested so far); say what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run and how to handle it; and close with an honest note on which feature of real-world escalation the activity cannot reproduce. Write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way one would write a long email to a colleague: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
