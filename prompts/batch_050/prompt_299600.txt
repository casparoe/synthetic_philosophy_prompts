For a Hume reading group I've been asked to write down where I've landed on the causation stuff so the others can poke holes in it. Here it is: (1) I basicly buy the New Hume reading — Hume himself believed there are real necessary connections or powers in the objects, and his scepticism is only about our access to them. (2) The copy principle is doing real work in his argument: no impression, no idea, and that's exactly what puts the idea of necessary connection in trouble. (3) The two definitions of cause Hume offers are meant to be coextensive, picking out the same relation from two different angles rather than two different relations. (4) The only impression anywhere in the vicinity is the internal one — the felt determination of the mind as it passes from the idea of cause to the idea of effect; there is no impression of any connection between the objects themselves. (5) The fact that I can always conceive the cause existing without the effect shows only that causal knowledge can't be a priori, and says nothing against there being real connections out there.

Which of these are actually in tension and which just look like it? Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most — including whether the standard moves people make here (relative ideas, the definitions as reference-fixers rather than real definitions) genuinely save the package or just relocate the problem.
