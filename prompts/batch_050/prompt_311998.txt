Ok so I need advice about a mess that's blown up on my department's hiring committee and I can't tell who's being reasonable.

There's three of us on the committee (me plus two colleagues) and we all agreed beforehand on the rule for hiring: we make an offer only if the candidate is strong on research AND strong on teaching. If they fail either one, no offer. Seemed simple enough.

So we interview this candidate, and then we vote. But one colleague suggested we vote on each criterion separately first, to keep things transparent, and then vote on the actual offer. Here's how it went:

- I thought: strong on research, weak on teaching, so I voted no on the offer.
- Colleague A thought: weak on research, strong on teaching, so she also voted no on the offer.
- Colleague B thought: strong on both, so he voted yes on the offer.

Now here's the problem. If you count the criterion votes, a majority of us (me and B) said the candidate IS strong on research, and a majority (A and B) said she IS strong on teaching. So by the rule we all agreed to, the committee "believes" she passes both criteria... which means we should make the offer. But when we voted on the actual offer, it went down 2-to-1. Only B voted to hire.

Colleague A says the final vote is what counts, end of story — we're three individual judges and a majority of judges said don't hire. Colleague B says that's absurd, because it means the committee is acting against its own stated findings: we've officially recorded that she's strong in both areas and then rejected her for... what, exactly? He wants to send the offer. He also pointed out (annoyingly, I think) that if we'd only voted on the criteria and then just applied our rule mechanically, we'd be hiring her, so the "real" disagreement is about procedure, not about the candidate at all.

I got a third suggestion from a friend in another department who said we should scrap both votes and just talk until we reach consensus on the offer itself. But that seems to just bury the problem, because our disagreement about the criteria is genuine — I really do think her teaching is weak and A really does think her research is weak.

So: what should we actually do? Is one of these ways of combining our judgments more legitimate than the others, or is it all just arbitrary? And does it matter for your answer that the rule itself ("hire iff strong on both") is one we all genuinely endorsed, rather than something imposed on us? I'd also like to know whether this is a one-off headache or whether it tells us something deeper about whether groups can even have coherent views in the first place. Don't just split the difference — tell me which colleague is right.
