EXAM QUESTION: In his critique of utilitarianism, Bernard Williams uses two famous examples: the case of Jim and the Indians, and the man who rescues his drowning wife. Explain what each example is meant to show about utilitarianism. Your answer should define what Williams means by "negative responsibility," explain the role of integrity and ground projects in his objection, state precisely which thought Williams describes as "one thought too many," and say whether Williams claims the husband acted wrongly.

STUDENT ANSWER: In the Jim case, Jim is a traveller who stumbles on a captain, Pedro, who is about to execute twenty Indians as punishment for anti-government protests. Pedro offers Jim a "guest's privilege": if Jim shoots one Indian himself, the other nineteen go free; if he refuses, all twenty die. Williams uses this to attack the utilitarian doctrine of negative responsibility — the idea that you are responsible not just for what you do but for what you fail to prevent, so that if Jim refuses, the twenty deaths are on him. Williams argues this ignores integrity: Jim's refusal flows from his ground projects, the deep commitments that give him a reason to go on living at all, and utilitarianism absurdly asks him to treat those commitments as just one preference among others, alienating him from his own actions. The wife case makes a related point about moral motivation. A man can save only one of two drowning people, one of whom is his wife. Williams says the thought "she's my wife" is one thought too many — the husband shouldn't be motivated by his personal attachment at all, but should simply recognize that saving her is what impartial morality permits, and in an emergency there is no time to deliberate anyway, so any pause for reflection could prove fatal. Williams does not claim the husband acted wrongly by saving his wife; his point is that what matters is acting for the right impartial reasons, not the action itself. Together the two cases show that utilitarianism, by demanding constant calculation and impartial justification, breaks down precisely in the situations where we need morality most.
