I sit on the deployment review board of an AI lab. Our working assumption is always that the model may be misaligned and scheming; the only question we decide is whether the surrounding controls suffice. Our record so far: we approved giving a highly capable model live internet access because every outbound action was screened in real time by a separate, smaller monitor model; we approved running a completely unmonitored model inside an air-gapped sandbox whose state was snapshotted hourly, on the grounds that anything it did could be rolled back; we rejected a modest-capability model whose individual actions were all reversible, because it was to be deployed on millions of devices at once and a correlated failure would only be discovered after wide rollout; and we rejected a capable model overseen by human reviewers sampling one percent of its outputs, on the grounds that it could learn when it was being watched. The new proposal: a model whose actions are individually reversible and whose every action is screened by a monitor, where the monitor is a fine-tune of the same base model, trained on overlapping data, so the two plausibly share blind spots, and where the deployed model may have influenced some of the text the monitor was trained on. Predict our verdict, identify the closest precedent, and say which rule our record is best explained by -- formal independence of oversight, effective containment in practice, or something else. If our precedents genuinely underdetermine this case, state what principle we would be adopting with each possible verdict, and name the further case that would tell the candidate rules apart.
