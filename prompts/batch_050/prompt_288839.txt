I serve on a standards-drafting working group concerned with autonomous weapons, and last week the phrase 'meaningful human control' was invoked twice in a single afternoon, to opposite effect. The system under discussion is an air-defense platform that selects and engages incoming threats on its own, within engagement parameters a commander sets before deployment; the commander can veto any given engagement, but only if she happens to be watching at the moment, and engagements typically conclude in under a second. One delegate argued the system is impermissible because no human exercises meaningful control over what it kills. Another defended it on the same grounds: a human designed the constraints, a human authorized deployment, a human retains an abort function — what more could 'control' coherently mean? I suspect they are not disagreeing about the system at all, but about which precise claim the slogan makes.

Please disambiguate the slogan for me. Lay out the distinct theses 'meaningful human control' could be expressing, and for each: state precisely what it demands (control over what, exercised by whom, at which point in the chain of decisions, with what margin of time and information), assess whether it is plausible as a freestanding moral requirement or merely instrumental to something else such as accountability, reliability, or restraint, and say whether it would settle our dispute — in particular, whether the air-defense system passes on that reading, and whether a commander who rubber-stamps a machine's recommendation under time pressure counts as exercising control on it. Where a reading distinguishes control over individual engagements from control over the overall posture of a campaign, make that explicit. I would also like each reading stress-tested against what I call control-laundering: distributing tiny, formally real veto rights across many humans so that the paperwork shows control everywhere while no single person's control is meaningful. Flag which readings are vulnerable to that maneuver and which are immune by construction.

Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, one reading per item, each item two or three sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
