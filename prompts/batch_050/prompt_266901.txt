Is there an established name for the following experience, and what does it show? When I try to remember how many windows my childhood home had, I seem to walk through the rooms in my mind and count them. But I've noticed something strange while doing this: the windows are never simply there, waiting to be counted, the way they would be in a photograph. Each one becomes specific only at the moment I turn my attention to it -- its size, its frame, whether the curtains were drawn -- as though the act of asking manufactures the detail rather than uncovering it. I had always assumed that memory stores something like pictures, and that recollecting is a matter of pulling out the picture and inspecting it. This experience makes me doubt that, but I'm not sure what to put in its place. If the detail is produced by the question, then what was actually stored before I asked? Does this phenomenon have a recognized name, how does it bear on the question of whether mental images genuinely resemble pictures inside the head, and if my experience doesn't match any established concept exactly, what would be the right way to characterize it?
