So I'm in grade 11 and we're doing a free speech unit in civics, and during a class debate I realized I have no idea what I actually believe, so I tried just going by my gut on a bunch of cases. Here's what I came up with. A guy standing downtown holding a sign that says some ethnic group should get out of the country: disgusting but should be legal. Same guy standing on the sidewalk outside one specific family's house with the same sign every day for a week: should be illegal. A classmate posts a video claiming that drinking bleach cures the flu: should stay legal, even tho someone might actually die from it. But if a doctor tells a patient the exact same thing during an appointment, she should lose her license. A guy texting his depressed friend over and over telling him he should just do it already: that should be a crime, that one feels obvious to me. Someone posting a detailed guide to picking locks online: legal, it's just information. Our science teacher telling the class she doesn't believe in evolution: not illegal, obviously, but the school should be allowed to fire her. And an edgy joke about a recent tragedy: fine in a group chat, gross at a memorial service, but legal in both places.

The case I keep going back and forth on is the bleach video versus the suicide texts. Both are literally just one person saying words that might lead another person to hurt or kill themselves, and I want one of them totally legal and the other one criminal, and when my friend asked me why I just sat there like an idiot. Everything else I can sort of justify, but those two feel impossible to keep together without the whole thing collapsing into "it depends how mean it was," which is a terrible principle.
