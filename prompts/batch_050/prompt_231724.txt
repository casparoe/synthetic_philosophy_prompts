I'm roasting my mentor at his retirement party next month. He's spent thirty years defending Galen Strawson's Basic Argument -- roughly: you act the way you do because of the way you are, so to be truly responsible for your actions you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, but to be responsible for that you'd have had to have chosen it, which means already being some way you didn't choose, and so on back until the whole thing collapses. His conclusion: nobody is ever ultimately responsible for anything, good or bad. Here's the problem. A roast runs on mock blame, and this man has spent his career arguing blame never lands, so presumably not even the affectionate kind. Write the five-minute roast. Half the room are philosophers who will notice instantly if I smuggle in genuine desert; the other half are admin staff and family who just want it funny and warm. I need to blame him -- for his unreadable handwriting, his terrible puns, the decade of unpaid favors he did everyone -- strictly in terms he'd accept, and ideally make the impossibility of roasting him part of the joke itself.
