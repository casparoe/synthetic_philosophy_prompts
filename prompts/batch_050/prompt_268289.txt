Is there an actual name for this thing, or does everyone just talk around it? I've noticed that whenever some region wants to break away -- Quebec, Scotland, Catalonia, wherever -- both sides solemnly agree that "the people should decide," but then it turns out "the people" just means whoever happens to live inside a particular line on a map, and funnily enough each side insists on exactly the line where they'd win. The separatists want the vote region-wide because that's where they're the majority; hold it country-wide and they lose, hold it county-by-county and chunks of their own territory stay behind. And when one of those chunks says fine, we'll hold our own vote and stick with the old country (which I gather actually happened with some communities up north during the Quebec votes), suddenly the independence people sound exactly like the central goverment they've been fighting -- sacred borders, indivisible territory, the lot. So it looks like the real decision is taken one step before the decision everyone argues about, when someone picks the boundary, and you can't settle that by voting because any vote about it would need its own boundary first, which someone also has to pick. Does this trap have a name and a literature, and how does it differ from gerrymandering, which sounds similar but feels off to me since there at least the electorate stays the same and only the districts move? And does the whole idea of self-determination just quietly assume the "self" is already defined, i.e. skip the entire hard part? Don't just hand me a label, I want to know where the edges of the thing are -- what counts as an instance and what doesn't.
