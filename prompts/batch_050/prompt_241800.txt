My partner's grandmother died last year and her family has been debating whether to build one of those chatbots trained on her texts and emails, which sent me down a rabbit hole. For a discussion group I've been writing down my working positions on "griefbots" and the digital dead, and before I share them I want a stress test. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The dead cannot be harmed or wronged -- once someone's gone there's no subject left whose interests could be set back, so anything done after their death can only be good or bad for the living. (2) Using a griefbot is perfectly fine for a bereaved person who finds it comforting; it's basically an interactive scrapbook or a photo album that talks, and how people grieve is their own business. (3) It's genuinely wrong -- not just tacky -- when a company resurrects a dead actor's face and voice to sell products without their prior consent; there's a real disrespect there that wouldn't disappear even if audiences loved the ad. (4) A griefbot that confidently invents political opinions or confessions the person never expressed is doing something wrong, because it misrepresents who that person was. (5) If the deceased explicitly asked not to be simulated, going ahead anyway would be wrong -- but only because of the distress and fallout for the living family, not because it wrongs the dead person, consistent with (1).

Please audit this set: tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are, rank the genuine tensions by how serious they are, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Lead with your overall verdict in the first sentence (hangs together or not, and where the biggest problem is), then go through the pairs -- no preamble or restating my list back to me. If one single revision would clean up the most, flag it at the end.
