We did a unit on whether an ant colony could have a mind, the whole Aunt Hillary idea where the colony seems to have goals and memories even though each ant is basically following tiny local rules. I got put in charge of the final question and I'm kind of stuck, can you write it?

Level: smart high schoolers or intro undergrads. 45 minute essay, around 600 words, 20 marks total.

I want one essay question built around this case: Colony A is a normal ant colony. Over a year, every ant in Colony B is gradually replaced by a tiny robot ant that follows the exact same pheromone-response rules, so from the outside Colony B keeps foraging, defending, relocating, and remembering where food was in exactly the same patterns. Then a storm splits each colony into two halves, and both halves keep some of the old routines but slowly drift apart. The question should force students to argue whether Colony A, Colony B, or the later half-colonies have minds or beliefs or a continuing self, and it should not be answerable by just writing emergence is when simple parts make complex wholes. Make them use at least: multiple realizability, the intentional stance, and some criterion for what makes a subject persist through part replacement and splitting.

Please include:
1. the exact exam question, worded so there is a real tension and no obviously correct answer
2. a model answer of about 600 words
3. a marking scheme out of 20, broken into 5 must-hit moves, with marks for each
4. a short note for each move saying how weaker students typically mess it up, e.g. treating same behavior as automatically same mind, or saying robots cant have minds because they are made of metal

Make the A vs B difference super clear: an A should take a position but also say what evidence or argument would change their mind, and it has to deal with the split-colony part, not just the robot part. Dont add a bibliography or citations, we'll fill those in later.
