I am a control theorist working in robotics, and my working vocabulary is limited but sharp: plant dynamics, state estimation, forward and inverse internal models, model-predictive control, feedback versus feedforward architectures, and passive dynamics. I keep running into the enactivist critique of representationalism in cognitive science — the claim, associated with Varela, Thompson, and Rosch and with later sensorimotor approaches, that cognition does not consist in building and manipulating internal representations of a pre-given world but is better understood as embodied, skillful engagement through which an organism enacts or brings forth its environment. From where I sit, parts of this debate look as though they have already been fought out in engineering terms: passive-dynamic walkers locomote without internal models, model-predictive control is unabashedly representational, and there is a live engineering question about how much internal model a given level of competence actually requires.

Please do three things.

First, restate the core enactivist claims and the standard representationalist replies as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary — controllers, observers, internal models, feedback loops, embodiment as plant dynamics. I would like the translation claim by claim, not as an impressionistic summary, and I would like you to say where predictive processing falls once translated: does it dissolve the debate, pick a side, or simply relabel it?

Second, give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no obvious counterpart in control-theoretic vocabulary at all? My suspicion is that talk of autopoiesis, sense-making, and the lived first-person body resists translation; tell me whether that suspicion is right, and if so, whether what resists translation is doing explanatory work or rhetorical work.

Third, take a position on the residue. Is what fails to translate evidence that my vocabulary is missing something cognition genuinely requires, or evidence that part of the enactivist program makes no difference an engineer should care about? Where am I most likely to misread the enactivists by treating the debate as already settled in engineering terms, and where are they most likely to misread control theory? I am not asking for a referee's verdict on the whole dispute — I want to know how much of it survives contact with a framework in which the questions are at least well-posed.
