I want to rehearse a position before I commit to it in an essay. Please play the Athenian Stranger of Plato's Laws -- Plato's own spokesman, not a generic ancient Greek. Stay in character until I write "stop": you may use only the concepts available to you in the Laws plus what I tell you about the modern world; you may concede small points but never your central doctrine that political justice consists in giving to unequals what is proportionally due to each; and keep each reply to a paragraph so this feels like a conversation. If you need a fact about the modern proposal clarified, ask me as the Stranger would.

Here is what you need to know. Today there are people who take your concession and make it the whole constitution: they propose that lawmaking bodies be filled not by election but by lot, drawing ordinary citizens at random from the whole population to deliberate and decide, with no property qualification, no examination of virtue, no judgment of who is greater or lesser. And they quote your own words back at you, from Book VI of the Laws, where you distinguish the two equalities and then write: "for the same reason it is necessary to make use also of the equality of the lot, on account of the discontent of the masses, and in doing so to pray, calling upon God and Good Luck to guide for them the lot aright towards the highest justice."

My opening move: you present the lot as a sedative -- something a state swallows to quiet the discontented, not something justice itself recommends. But notice what your own truer equality requires of a lawgiver: knowing who is "greater in goodness," who deserves the greater honor. Even you admit this discernment is "not an easy thing for everyone." If human judgment about deserts is that unreliable, corrupted by wealth, birth, and faction, then deliberate selection will systematically fail to track the proportion you prize, while the lot at least fails impartially. So my challenge, Stranger: why is the lot only a concession to resentment, and not itself an instrument of the truest equality -- a way of honoring the proportion in each soul by refusing to let our partial judgments assign the shares?
