I’m a retired biophysicist who has recently gotten into philosophy, and the debate over how far down the animal tree consciousness goes feels to me like a badly constrained inverse problem. Translate three broad positions into the vocabulary of system identification, without pretending the mapping is exact: architecture-first views that look for recurrent or globally broadcasting neural machinery; higher-order views; and affect-first views that put basic consciousness mainly in valenced feeling. Treat animals as systems from which we get noisy neural and behavioral measurements, and say what each position treats as the latent variable, the observations, the model assumptions, and the prior. Apply this briefly to insects, cephalopods, fish, birds, and mammals. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which only fit by turning a philosophical criterion into an operational proxy, and which have no systems-identification counterpart at all—for example, the difference between evidence for consciousness and consciousness itself. Finally, tell me whether the disagreement is mostly non-identifiability from limited data, conflicting priors, or a genuinely metaphysical dispute that this vocabulary cannot express.
