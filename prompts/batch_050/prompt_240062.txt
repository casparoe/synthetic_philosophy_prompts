Construct an integrated thought experiment for a seminar on Hume on causation that separates three positions:

1. An austere regularity account, on which a token causal claim is warranted only when events of the relevant types have instantiated a suitable regularity.
2. A simple counterfactual account, on which A causes B only if B would not have occurred had A not occurred.
3. The skeptical causal-realist interpretation associated with the New Hume, on which objects may possess objective causal powers even though observation of successive events never acquaints us with the necessary connection itself.

Use these as working positions for the exercise rather than attempting to settle the scholarly interpretation of Hume.

The scenario must contain two token transitions within one realistic contemporary laboratory, industrial, or engineering setting:

- In the first transition, events of types A and B have regularly occurred in succession, but only because an independent process produces both. There must be no physical transmission from A to B, and B would still occur if A were prevented while that independent process continued to operate.
- In the second transition, a novel event C physically produces D through a traceable mechanism. However, an automatic fail-safe would have activated the same final mechanism and produced the same D, at the same time and in the same manner, had C failed. The fail-safe must remain inactive because C succeeds. The C-D sequence must also be genuinely novel and unrepeated at the relevant level of event description.

Do not secure the second sequence’s uniqueness merely through irrelevant details such as the date, the operator’s name, or an unusually specific description. Its novelty must matter to the regularity analysis even though the underlying components and physical principles can be familiar. Avoid magic, backwards causation, essentially probabilistic causation, and science-fictional devices. Present the scenario in no more than 300 words, supplying enough physical detail that the causal verdicts arise from the case rather than being stipulated.

After the scenario, provide a table giving each position’s verdict on both transitions, distinguishing where necessary between whether A causes B or C causes D and whether an observer is warranted in making the causal judgment. Explain all six verdicts. Then identify which features are doing the philosophical work and state what would change if:

- the independent process behind A and B were removed;
- the fail-safe behind C and D were removed;
- the C-D sequence were repeated under suitable conditions.

Finally, consider the two strongest objections: the regularity theorist may subsume C and D under broader laws or event types, while the counterfactual theorist may appeal to a more fine-grained outcome or to counterfactual dependence among intermediate stages. Explain whether the case survives these objections or must be revised. Conclude by stating precisely what the pair of transitions shows about regularity, counterfactual dependence, necessary connection, and the New Hume’s combination of causal realism with epistemic skepticism.
