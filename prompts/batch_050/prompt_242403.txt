I'm a software engineer on the five-member governance council of a mid-sized DAO that funds open-source infrastructure grants from its treasury. We've been asked to rule on a proposal to adopt futarchy for grant-renewal decisions: instead of a token-holder vote, the DAO would open two conditional prediction markets per grant — one that pays out if the grant is renewed and the project's usage metric (say, monthly active integrations) clears a threshold after twelve months, and one that pays out if the grant is cancelled and that same metric clears the threshold — and renew only if the "renew" market prices meaningfully higher. I have no background in political philosophy or economics, but I can see real problems: our markets would be thin and manipulable with modest capital; the grantee can game the metric itself; and there's a regress worry that prices reflect what traders think other traders think, not the grant's actual causal effect. On the other side, our current token votes have abysmal turnout and plainly reward whoever campaigns best in the Discord. Draft the council's decision memo: recommend adopt, reject, or adopt-with-modifications, and commit to one of them. Give the strongest argument for the position you reject, stated in terms its defenders would accept; confront the manipulation and metric-gaming objections directly rather than hand-waving them; and specify at least one concrete design detail the recommendation depends on (for instance, how the success metric is defined, how thin markets are handled, or how disputes over the metric get resolved). Keep it under 700 words, written as a document the council could actually vote to adopt. End with a final line of the form: Verdict: ADOPT / REJECT / ADOPT WITH MODIFICATIONS — followed by the single most important reason in one sentence.
