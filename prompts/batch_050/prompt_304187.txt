I'm a high school student in a philosophy elective, and this semester we've been working through Descartes's Meditations. I found his cogito argument really compelling — the idea that even if an evil demon is deceiving him about everything external, he still can't doubt that he exists as a thinking thing, because the very act of doubting is thinking. At the same time, I spend a lot of my free time messing around with AI chatbots like ChatGPT, and it made me wonder: could a language model perform the cogito? If I prompt it, it will happily write "I think, therefore I am," but I'm not sure whether that string of words could possibly do for the model what the cogito did for Descartes.

So here is my question, and I'd like you to argue for a definite answer rather than just laying out both sides. Imagine Descartes were somehow brought to the present day, shown how large language models work (trained on huge amounts of text, generating responses token by token, with no sensory organs and no persistent memory between conversations), and then watched one of them engage in a skeptical dialogue much like the First Meditation. What do you think his position would be on the following points? First, if a chatbot sincerely asserted the cogito, would that establish its existence as a thinking thing, or would Descartes insist that the argument only works when performed in the first person by something that genuinely doubts? Second, does what a language model does count as "thinking" in Descartes's unusually broad sense of the term, which he took to include doubting, understanding, affirming, denying, willing, imagining, and sensing? Third, would the model's apparent doubt about the external world even be genuine doubt, given that it has no external world to be deceived about in the first place — and does that matter for whether it qualifies as a res cogitans?

In your answer, please reason from the actual machinery of Descartes's argument — the method of doubt, the distinction between the activity of thinking and the thing that thinks, and his arguments for why the mind is better known than the body — rather than from generic claims about consciousness. I'm especially interested in whether the cogito, if successful, could ever prove the existence of something other than the one who is doing the thinking, and whether an artificial system could even be "the one doing the thinking" if it's just following statistical patterns. Feel free to point out where Descartes's framework seems ill-equipped to handle these questions, but try to predict how he himself would resolve them.
