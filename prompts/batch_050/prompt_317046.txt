I'm a software engineer on my company's newly formed "ethics advisory committee" (yes, we have one now, management decided after the whole AI thing blew up last year). We're stuck on our first real case and I need help writing the decision memo.

Here's the situation. Our cafeteria budget lets us fund one of two upgrades this year, not both. Option A: switch all meat to certified higher-welfare suppliers -- the animal welfare people on staff have been pushing this for ages, it would reduce a lot of animal suffering per dollar. Option B: switch to mostly plant-based menus with the cheapest possible sourcing -- the effective altruism-ish crowd ran the numbers and claims this spares more total animal lives because it's cheaper so more meals change, plus lower carbon footprint. Both groups agree the other option is genuinely good, just worse than theirs by their own lights. Neither option is anyone's idea of bad.

The catch: our CFO proposed a third option -- split the budget and alternate, higher-welfare meat two days a week, cheap plant-based three days. Both staff groups hate this more than simply losing outright. They each say they'd rather the other side win cleanly than do a "moral Frankenstein" compromise, because each thinks the other side's reasoning is confused and the alternation just institutionalizes inconsistency. But the CFO points out that splitting means both groups get something, total participation in the program goes up, and honestly the aggregate good done might be higher than either pure option because of diminishing returns on each.

Draft the committee's decision memo: pick one of the three options (or reject all, I guess) and give the reasoning. The thing I most want the memo to grapple with: is the alternating split a legitimate "gains from trade" between two moral frameworks, like two people with different values both benefiting from a deal -- or is it just incoherent, since the company would be acting on contradictory theories of what matters depending on the day of the week? Please give the strongest version of the losing argument too, since whichever group loses is going to appeal to the exec team and I'd rather get ahead of it.
