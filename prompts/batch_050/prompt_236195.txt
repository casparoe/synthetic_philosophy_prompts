Write an essay on how purely instrumental music can be sad. The question is sharper than it first appears: ordinary sadness is an emotion with an owner and an object -- someone is sad, and sad about something -- yet a sad adagio involves no one who is sad and nothing the sadness is about, and still we call the sounds themselves sad rather than merely saying they make us sad.

To keep the essay from floating above the problem, anchor it in the following case. Two recordings of the same piece are acoustically identical down to the last decibel. One was played by a grieving cellist the week after a death; the other was assembled sample by sample by software with no inner life at all. Is one recording sadder than the other? Does the answer change if the listener can never learn which is which? Use this case as a test that any adequate account must pass.

Weigh the main families of answers against it: resemblance or contour accounts, on which the music is sad because its pitch movement, tempo, and dynamics mimic the sound of sad speech and the movement of a sad body; arousal accounts, on which sad music is whatever reliably induces sadness-like states in listeners; and persona accounts, on which we hear the music as the expressive gesture of an imagined agent and attribute the sadness to that persona. Consider whether these are rivals or whether they answer different questions, and in particular whether the distinction between the music being sad and the music making me sad is doing real work or is a verbal mirage. Briefly say what your favored account implies about the familiar puzzle of why people deliberately seek out sad music and seem to value the experience.

Reach and defend a verdict on the twin recordings, and therefore on what musical sadness is; a shrug between the options is not an acceptable ending. Throughout, keep a clean separation between what is reasonably settled -- for instance, that listeners within a musical culture converge on basic happy-versus-sad attributions for many acoustic features -- and what remains genuinely contested, including how far such convergence extends across cultures and which of the theoretical families best explains it. Do not present any contested theoretical position as the received view.
