So I'm on the disciplinary panel at a mid-sized regional trucking company and we have a genuinely awful case on the docket next week. Two of our drivers, on two different nights about a month apart, each ran the same red light out on Route 9 at like 2am after having a few drinks over the legal limit. Basically identical misconduct — same road, same speed, same everything as far as we can tell. Driver A made it home fine and got reported by a coworker later. Driver B hit a cyclist who swerved into the intersection without lights, and the cyclist died.

Here's the problem: our HR policy says discipline should reflect "the gravity of the incident" and nobody can agree what that means. The union rep is adamant the two drivers have to get identical sanctions because they did literally the same thing, and the difference in outcomes is just luck. Meanwhile half the panel feels that would be monstrous — a man is dead, and giving Driver B the same slap on the wrist as Driver A seems insane, plus the family's lawyer is circling. To make it worse, our past cases are totally inconsistent on this.

Please draft the written opinion I'm going to circulate before the meeting. It needs an actual bottom line — what we do with each driver, concretely (suspension, termination, referral to prosecutors, whatever) — and the reasoning behind it, including a fair-minded treatment of whichever side you don't come down on, because the losing faction will read this closely. Also, since I'm going to have to defend this in the room: at the end, say how confident you are in the recommendation overall and flag the single point in your reasoning where you're least sure, so I know where to expect the most pushback.
