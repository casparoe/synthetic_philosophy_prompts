I'm TAing an undergrad jurisprudence seminar this term and we're doing the Hart–Devlin debate next week, so I've been rereading the main material and skimming the first drafts my students handed in. One draft argues something I initially wanted to mark as confused, but the longer I sit with it the harder I find it to explain exactly where it goes wrong. The student's reasoning is roughly this. Legal positivists like Hart hold that whether something is law is one question and whether it is good law is another: the validity of a rule doesn't depend on its moral content. Devlin, though, wasn't making a claim about validity at all — responding to the Wolfenden committee's recommendation to decriminalise consensual homosexual acts in private, he was arguing that society is entitled to use the criminal law to enforce its shared morality even where no one is harmed, since a common morality is part of what holds a society together. Hart answered that it would be wrong to criminalise private immorality, that societies don't in fact disintegrate when their moral code shifts, and so on. But, my student says, those are moral arguments about what the law ought to be, so Hart is helping himself to precisely the entanglement of law and morality that his positivism denies. On the student's view Hart has to either abandon positivism or concede Devlin's conclusion, and since he clearly does neither, his overall position is unstable.

My instinct is that the student has run two things together, but when I tried to write a margin comment explaining which two, I kept stumbling, and I admit part of me now suspects there is a genuine puzzle here about whether a positivist can object to legal moralism on moral grounds without incoherence. So: is the student right that Hart's stance is unstable? If not, where exactly does the argument misfire — and is there any stronger reading of the objection on which it does land a blow, maybe not against positivism itself but against the way Hart frames his reply to Devlin? I'd like to give the student real feedback rather than just 'wrong, see me', so concrete pointers would help.
