My flatmate and her boyfriend had a proper row about blackmail law last night and both asked me to settle it, which I can't. Her point: blackmail is a bizarre crime because its two ingredients are each perfectly legal -- I'm allowed to tell the world your secret, and I'm allowed to ask you for money -- yet offering them as a package ('pay me or I tell') is suddenly criminal, and no one can explain what changes when you combine two legal acts. His reply: the combination obviously changes things, because the threat is doing coercive work -- the secret-holder is leveraging power over you that they only have because you'd pay to keep it quiet, and it's no more mysterious than two harmless chemicals being dangerous when mixed. She came back that the coercion story can't be right, because if I threaten to reveal your affair *unless you stop seeing her*, that's still blackmail and still coercive, but if I genuinely just keep quiet in exchange for nothing I've done nothing wrong -- so the crime can't be the coercion itself, it must be the money, which is weird because wanting money is legal. He said fine, maybe it's that selling silence is like selling something that isn't yours to sell -- the information partly concerns the other person, so you're profiting from leverage over them that you have no right to monetise. She pointed out that journalists and gossip columnists monetise other people's secrets all the time, legally, and he said that's because they publish rather than suppress, which she called a distinction without a difference. Who's right at each step, and does the 'two legal acts' puzzle actually get solved by anything either of them said?
