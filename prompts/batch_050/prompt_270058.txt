I sit on the grants committee of a small foundation that funds research on which animals deserve the most moral weight per individual (long story, donors choice not mine). Every meeting turns into the same fight about brain size. One member wants to bankroll projects refining neuron counts across species, since better numbers are at least measurable. Another says raw brain or neuron numbers are a terrible proxy and we should fund behavioral work on suffering and flexible behavior instead. A third insists the whole debate is confused because nobody has said what brain size is even supposed to be a proxy for, and until we do the other two are just measuring stuff. One person also floated a weirder idea I can't shake: maybe absolute brain size matters in itself, because a bigger brain could support a richer or more unified moment of experience, so the same pinch hurts an elephant more than a mouse even if both flinch identically. Nobody knew what to do with that.

We can fund about four two-year projects. Which open questions here should we prioritize, and in what order? For each one, tell me why its upstream of the others, what a funder could realistically expect to learn from philosophers or comparative neuro folks rather than from big labs, and what result would make us move it up or down the list. And say which question you think is overrated and gets too much attention. Defend the ranking, don't just give me a survey. Keep the whole answer under 450 words.
