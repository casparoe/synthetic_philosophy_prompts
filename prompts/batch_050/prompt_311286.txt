You are preparing teaching materials for a graduate seminar in formal epistemology. Your students keep dismissing standard modal epistemic logic with the complaint that its agents are logically omniscient, but they treat this as a single objection when it is really a family of distinct failures: knowing every logical truth, having one's knowledge closed under known entailment, having one's beliefs closed under entailment, and maintaining consistency among one's beliefs are separable properties, and different fixes to the framework give up different subsets of them.

Write a decision tree that takes a seminar participant from the starting commitment "I accept that in a normal modal logic of knowledge, the agent knows all validities and everything entailed by what they know, and this is a defect" to a named response. The tree must have between eight and twelve leaves, each labeled with a position: among them must appear the idealization or normative defense (omniscience is a feature, not a bug), the two-tier implicit/explicit belief account, awareness-based logic, impossible-worlds semantics, neighborhood semantics, syntactic or proof-theoretic approaches, a dialetheist or paraconsistent accommodation, and a fragmentation account on which belief holds only relative to a frame or context.

Every internal node must be a question the participant can answer without knowing which formalism it leads to, and wherever possible the question should take the form of a verdict on a concrete case rather than an abstract endorsement. Your tree must route participants through at least the following three cases:

1. The Careful Checker: an agent assents to each axiom of a theory, verifies every line of a long proof one step at a time, and afterwards asserts the theorem; yet before running the check, when asked about a specific non-obvious consequence of the axioms, she sincerely says she does not know whether it holds. Does she know the consequence or not?

2. The Individually Reasonable Set: an agent believes each of ten claims, each of which passed her own scrutiny, but when told the ten jointly entail a contradiction she declines to retract any of them. Is her defect the same kind as the Checker's, or a distinct one?

3. The Premature Universal: an agent assents to every instance of a generalization that is put to her, case by case, indefinitely, but withholds assent from the universally quantified claim itself. Is this a failure of closure, and if so, closure under what?

Construct the tree question-first: map out the combinations of verdicts the questions permit, then locate the named positions in that space. Where two positions land on the same leaf because they deliver identical verdicts on every case in your tree, say so explicitly and state what further question would be needed to separate them. Where a coherent combination of verdicts corresponds to no occupied position, include it as an empty leaf and diagnose whether the vacancy reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.
