I'm trying to work out what I actually think causation comes to, so here are my gut verdicts on some cases, and I'd like to know what principle — if any — sits behind them.

- Two assassins fire at the dictator at the same instant, each bullet independantly lethal, both hit home. I say both bullets caused his death.
- Suzy lobs her rock at the greenhouse a beat before Billy throws his; hers does the shattering, though his would have done if she'd bottled it. Suzy broke the glass, Billy didn't.
- My neighbour forgets to water my plants while I'm away and they die. Her not watering caused it. But the Prime Minister's not watering them caused nothing at all.
- The barometer falls, then the storm hits: had it not fallen, there'd have been no storm (the pressure system saw to both), yet the barometer didn't cause the storm.
- The bodyguard deflects the bullet and the diplomat lives. The deflection caused the survival, even though surviving is just a death not happening.

I've been using this bit of Hume (Treatise, Book I, Part III, Section XV) as a rough test, and it seems to fight my verdicts:

"1. The cause and effect must be contiguous in space and time.
2. The cause must be prior to the effect.
3. There must be a constant union betwixt the cause and effect. 'Tis chiefly this quality, that constitutes the relation.
4. The same cause always produces the same effect, and the same effect never arises but from the same cause."

The neighbour's failure isn't an "object" contiguous with anything, and the assassins look like one effect arising from two causes, flat against rule 4. So: is there one account — counterfactual, interventionist, or otherwise — that delivers all five verdicts, and which of them does it force me to give up? If rescuing the omissions means ditching Hume's framework, does an interventionist one really keep the Prime Minister off the hook rather than lumping him in with the neighbour? And tell me what your answer predicts for a case I haven't listed: the major and the sergeant both bellow "charge!", the troops are drilled to obey officers only, and the major's shout does all the work — did the sergeant cause the charge?
