I teach a seminar on philosophy of law, and next term I want to devote a session to an analogy that is doing real argumentative work in debates over legal personhood for AI: since the law already treats corporations as persons — entities with no body, no experiences, and interests ultimately reducible to those of natural persons — granting personhood to sufficiently autonomous AI systems is presented as a modest extension of existing doctrine rather than a revolution in it. I would like a systematic audit of how far this analogy holds.

Please begin with the correspondence table: what plays the role of incorporation as a sovereign act, of shareholders and directors, of the corporate veil, of the capacity to sue and be sued, of assets against which judgments run, of liability insurance, and of dissolution as the corporate analogue of death? Then identify where the mapping breaks. I am especially interested in whether the disanalogies that matter are the obvious ones (AI systems can initiate actions no human instructed, whereas corporate acts are always traceable to natural persons acting in roles) or subtler ones (personhood is granted to corporations partly to serve the natural persons behind them, so the analogy may presuppose exactly what AI personhood would abandon). Please also bring in, as a contrast class, the personhood granted to entities like rivers, where the rationale is protective rather than organizational, and say which model the AI case more closely resembles.

Then assess what the analogy quietly imports: a theory of corporate personality (concession, fiction, or real entity), an assumption that personhood is a bundle of severable incidents rather than a threshold status, and the background hope that personhood would close the liability gaps left when harms are caused with no satisfactory human defendant. Does the conclusion the analogy is used for — that AI personhood is legally unremarkable and practically useful — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it? If there is a better source-domain for thinking about AI status (agency law, animal law, the status of ships, or something else), say so.

Throughout, separate what is doctrinally or conceptually settled from what remains genuinely contested among legal theorists, and do not present a contested position — including your own preferred resolution — as the consensus.
