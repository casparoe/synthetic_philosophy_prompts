So I maintain a mid-size open source project (a federated messaging protocol, roughly 40 regular contributors) and a few months ago I went down a late-night wikipedia rabbit hole and found Nomic, Peter Suber's game where changing the rules is itself a move in the game. I got kind of obsessed, and being an idiot I've now proposed to our community that we replace our static GOVERNANCE.md with a Nomic-style self-amending ruleset. The sketch I've floated: an initial ruleset with a small "constitution" of core rules, amendments needing a two-thirds vote of active maintainers, a disputes rule loosely modeled on Nomic's judge mechanic where a randomly selected non-involved maintainer rules on rule interpretation, and -- the part everyone latched onto -- the amendment procedure itself is amendable, like in Suber's original, since making it untouchable seemed to defeat the whole point. I'm presenting this at our annual maintainers summit in about three weeks.

The audience is about 30 people: a core of senior maintainers who've been through two ugly governance dramas before and trust process about as far as they can throw it, two people from the foundation that funds us including their lawyer, and a handful of security engineers who think about everything in terms of attack surface. Nobody there will have heard of Nomic, and frankly none of them care about the philosophy of self-amending rules for its own sake -- they care about whether someone can grief the project, capture it, or stall it to death. I want a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections this specific audience will actually raise (not the ones a philosophy seminar would raise), ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way these people would phrase it -- so expect "this doubles the attack surface," "what's the bus factor on your judge rule," "you've built a consensus bug in meatspace," that kind of register. For each one, give me a reply I can deliver in two minutes, plus an honest note on where that reply is soft. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.

One more thing: don't just make me feel prepared. I'd rather know now which objections have real force and which I can wave through, so mark which ones you think are answerable versus which are basically fatal to the proposal as sketched. And if I've misunderstood Nomic itself somewhere, or the whole premise of prepping like this is confused -- e.g. if the self-amendment paradoxes make the idea a non-starter no matter how good my answers are -- say that plainly instead of politely helping me rehearse for a talk I shouldn't give.
