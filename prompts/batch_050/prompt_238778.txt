I'm drafting a short statement of my considered view on Adam Smith's moral psychology for a graduate seminar on sentimentalism, and before I circulate it I want an audit of whether my commitments can be held together. Here is what I currently accept:

(a) The standard of moral judgment is constitutive, not merely heuristic: an action or character is proper or meritorious just in case an impartial spectator — fully informed, and freed of the distortions of self-love and local habit — would sympathize with the sentiments behind it.

(b) The capacity for impartial-spectator judgment is entirely a social product. Conscience, what Smith calls the man within the breast, is built up out of the actual reactions of the people around us; we learn to see ourselves as we imagine others see us, and there is no route to moral standards that bypasses one's social formation.

(c) Moral criticism of entrenched, socially approved practices is sometimes correct even when virtually everyone in the society endorses them — respectable slaveholders, or the ancient custom of exposing unwanted infants — and the history of morals contains genuine progress of exactly this kind, not merely change.

(d) Special attachments are virtues, not compromises: loving one's children and friends more than strangers, and giving them disproportionate care, is precisely what the impartial spectator approves of in a well-formed person.

(e) Moral judgment is motivationally inert on its own. Whether an agent acts on a verdict of propriety depends on a separate desire; approbation by itself is an assessment, not an impulse.

My questions, in order. Which pairs or triples of these commitments are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious; for each, state the least costly revision and what it would cost me. Where a tension dissolves on inspection, show the dissolution rather than just asserting consistency. Finally, tell me whether (a) as I've stated it — constitutive rather than heuristic — is doing more work than it can support, given the rest.

Keep the audit under 700 words, in American English, and don't bother with citations or references to the secondary literature — I'll supply those myself.
