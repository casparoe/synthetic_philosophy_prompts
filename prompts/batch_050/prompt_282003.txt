Act as a philosopher with expertise in both ethical theory and Buddhist thought, and think through this carefully before answering.

I have no academic background, but I've been reflecting on a pattern I keep running into, and I want to know whether it has an established name. The pattern is that certain goods seem to be destroyed or diminished precisely by being aimed at directly, so that the pursuit has to, in a sense, conceal itself from the pursuer.

Two concrete cases that got me thinking. First: I started meditating partly because I was told it would reduce my craving and restlessness. But the more anxiously I monitored my progress toward being less attached, the more attached to progress I became -- the striving itself was the very thing I was trying to escape. Teachers seem to know this and tell you to stop trying, but of course "trying not to try" is still trying. Second: I noticed that giving a gift in order to store up good karma or merit for myself feels less admirable than giving the same gift out of genuine care, and some Buddhist texts and teachers seem to acknowledge this, encouraging people to give without thought of reward. But if the reward is the real engine of the practice, then a sincere practitioner has to stop wanting the thing the practice promises.

I know this resembles the so-called paradox of hedonism, but that seems to be specifically about pleasure and happiness, whereas my pattern covers moral and spiritual goods too. I've also heard of views that merely recommend achieving a goal indirectly, but that sounds like advice about strategy rather than a diagnosis of this self-undermining structure.

So: is there an established concept or literature for exactly this pattern, as distinct from those neighbors? Please tell me what the nearest concepts are, how the thing I've described differs from each, and which fits best. If nothing established fits, say so plainly and coin a term for it, with a definition and a couple of boundary cases (cases that nearly count but don't). Don't just hand me a label -- I care about where the edges of the phenomenon are. Also, tell me what consideration or argument would change your verdict, or where your reasoning is most likely to go wrong.

End your reply with a final line of the form Verdict: followed by the best-fitting established name, or your coined term if none exists.
