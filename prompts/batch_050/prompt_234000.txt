I'm trying to get clear on what an answer to the separateness-of-persons objection to utilitarianism would even have to *be*, before I look at anyone's attempt. You know the objection -- roughly: within a single life we're fine trading off a bad period for a better later period because one and the same person reaps the benefit, but utilitarianism runs the same kind of trade across people, and there's no one who lives both lives and gets compensated. Fine. What I want from you is the spec sheet: what does an adequate response to this objection have to deliver in order to count as an adequate response?

Write the desiderata. For each one, give the case or consideration that motivates it -- e.g., whatever it has to say about why intra-personal compensation is actually legitimate (or whether it even is -- maybe the objection generalizes *into* the person, and time-slices are as separate as people), what it has to say about cases where personal identity gets fuzzy (splitting cases, gradual psychological drift across a life, dementia), what it has to say about aggregation over huge numbers of tiny benefits vs one person's catastrophe, what it has to say about people who don't exist yet, and so on. Also flag which desiderata are genuinely load-bearing and which are just one camp's hobby horse dressed up as a requirement, and tell me if you suspect any pair of them can't be jointly satisfied -- that'd be the interesting result honestly.

One thing I want you to include specifically, because I haven't seen it discussed much: a case where welfare is distributed *within* what we intuitively count as one life but across entities that have basically nothing to do with each other psychologically -- imagine someone's psychology is fully rebooted every ten years, no memory or character continuity between stages, but we keep calling them the same person for legal purposes. Does the intra-personal license to aggregate survive that? I suspect our verdicts here smuggle in a theory of personal identity that does all the work, and I'd want a desideratum that forces a response to come clean about this rather than hide behind the word person.

Then, briefly -- and I mean briefly, the criteria are the point, not the scorecard -- how do the main kinds of response do against your list: biting the bullet (Parfit-style, the separateness matters less than we thought), restricting aggregation somehow, contractualist moves that only compare pairs, and prioritarian weighting. If your desiderata end up ruling out all of them, say so and say which requirement did the excluding.
