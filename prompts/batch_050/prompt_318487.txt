I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm drafting a long essay about the 2006 vote in Prague where the International Astronomical Union demoted Pluto from planet to dwarf planet. The broad facts are well known: the discovery of Eris and other large trans-Neptunian objects forced the issue, the IAU settled on a three-part definition requiring that a body orbit the Sun, be massive enough to be round, and have "cleared the neighborhood" of its orbit, and Pluto failed the third test. The public reaction was famously hostile -- schoolchildren wrote protest letters, some American state legislatures passed resolutions in Pluto's defense, and plenty of planetary scientists refused to accept the ruling. What interests me is not the astronomy but the philosophy of language underneath it, because the episode looks like a real-world experiment in what philosophers call conceptual engineering.

Here is the angle I want developed. In the Carnapian tradition of explication, you replace a fuzzy everyday concept with a precise one that serves your theoretical purposes; in Sally Haslanger's ameliorative analysis, you go further and ask what a concept *should* do for us, given our legitimate goals, rather than what it currently means. On the surface, the IAU vote looks like a pure explication exercise -- but I'd like the analysis to take seriously a distinction I want to propose in my essay: between *venue-internal* engineering, where a credentialed body redesigns a term for its own practitioners (say, how physicists stipulate "work" or "weightlessness"), and *ambient* engineering, where the target word lives its life in public discourse and the engineers must contend with users who never consented to the redesign. Pluto strikes me as a case where venue-internal engineering tried, and largely failed, to operate as ambient engineering.

So: analyze the Pluto affair as a case study in conceptual engineering. Who exactly occupies the role of engineer, what desiderata the cleared-neighborhood criterion was actually optimizing for (taxonomic cleanliness? dynamical realism? pre-empting an embarrassing influx of dozens of new planets?), whether the backlash shows that folk usage can veto an ameliorative project or merely that the IAU picked the wrong venue, where the explication/amelioration framework genuinely illuminates the episode and where it is simply silent, and what the whole affair implies about the prospects for engineering contested public concepts like "health," "rural," or "AI" from inside expert institutions. Please give me your analysis as exactly five numbered points, each no more than two sentences long, with no introductory or concluding material.
