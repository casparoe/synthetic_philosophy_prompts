I'm a PhD student in cognitive science and this semester I'm TAing for an undergrad course on agency and free will. The students keep falling into the exact same script every week: determinism vs indeterminism, could-you-have-done-otherwise, Frankfurt cases, rinse and repeat. I want to shake this up by organizing the whole debate around sphexishness instead -- the digger wasp that will mechanically repeat its burrow-inspection routine forever if you keep nudging the cricket a few inches, and the unsettling question of whether we're just fancier versions of it. My hunch is that a lot of the standard positions are really, at bottom, disagreements about what would count as escaping that kind of mechanical rigidity: is the answer flexibility, rational responsiveness, self-origination, reflectively endorsed rigidity (a committed monk is pretty 'rigid', but presumably not sphexish), or is the whole worry confused because no amount of flexibility makes you free if the flexing is determined?

So: please build me a decision tree that sorts someone into a position on free will using only questions about flexibility, rigidity, and responsiveness to reasons -- no question may mention determinism or ask about positions by name. Each branch node should be phrased as a concrete mini-case the answerer reacts to, and I need these four cases used somewhere on the tree: the wasp reset test itself (is the wasp unfree because of the repetition, or because of what would happen if the environment changed in the *right* way?), a case where a person's deliberation gets an indeterministic jolt injected into it (does genuine randomness de-sphexify, or is that just noise?), a manipulation case where someone's dispositions were installed by an outside programmer but the person is now wonderfully flexible and reasons-responsive, and a case of someone who is extremely rigid in some domain but has stepped back, considered alternatives, and wholeheartedly endorsed the rigidity. The leaves have to include event-causal libertarianism, agent-causal libertarianism, Frankfurt-style hierarchical compatibilism, reasons-responsive compatibilism (guidance control and that family), hard incompatibilism, and illusionism about moral responsibility; add revisionism as a leaf if the space naturally generates a spot for it.

A few more specs. Aim for 7 to 9 nodes total, and phrase every node so the answerer can't tell which position their answer is pushing them toward -- the tree should sort people by what they think about the wasp and the chip, not by which label they like. After the tree, walk the logical space the questions generate and mark any combination of answers that no one holds, with a one-line diagnosis of whether that box is empty because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate -- I suspect hierarchical compatibilism and reasons-responsiveness might collapse into each other on these questions, and if so I want that stated rather than fudged. Each leaf gets a one-sentence gloss explaining whether that position takes sphexishness to be the real threat to freedom or thinks the whole framing is a distraction. This is for second-year undergrads, so keep the node questions vivid and short.
