I have no academic training in philosophy, but I have been thinking on my own for a while about what it actually means to say that one event causes another, as opposed to merely coming before it. From my reading I gather that the field splits roughly into two camps: views on which causation is fundamentally a matter of difference-making (had the cause not occurred, the effect would not have, or would have been less likely, or some intervention on the cause would change the effect), and views on which causation is fundamentally a matter of physical production (some process, energy transfer, or connection running from cause to effect). I suspect the real structure is messier than two camps, and I want help mapping it.

Please build me a decision tree that sorts a person into a position on causation based on their verdicts about cases, not on labels they already like. The tree should start from the commitment that there is a genuine fact of the matter about what caused what, beyond mere temporal sequence and beyond correlation, and each branch node should be a question about a concrete scenario or a plainly stated claim that someone could answer without knowing which theory it points toward. Six to nine branch questions, ending in named positions.

The leaves must include at least: a Lewis-style counterfactual dependence view, an interventionist view in Woodward's sense, a process or conserved-quantity view in the spirit of Salmon and Dowe, a Humean regularity view, and a pluralist view on which more than one of these captures something real. Add further leaves if the logical space calls for them.

Work the following cases into the tree somewhere, since these are the ones I keep getting stuck on: a death caused by an omission (the gardener who never waters the plant); double prevention (a bodyguard neutralizes a poison that would have killed the victim, so the bodyguard's act seems to cause survival by preventing a prevention); symmetric overdetermination (two vandals throw rocks that shatter the same window at the same instant); and a cause no one could ever manipulate, such as conditions in the early universe or the gravitational pull of a distant star. If any of my descriptions of these cases muddies the philosophical point, correct the setup rather than working around it.

Where the answer space contains combinations no named position occupies, note them briefly and say whether the gap looks incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored. And where two leaves cannot honestly be separated by verdicts on cases alone, flag them as inseparable rather than inventing a question that pretends to split them.

One further request: if it turns out that the views I have listed do not actually differ on discrete yes-or-no answers — for instance, if the counterfactual and interventionist views are close enough that a branching tree distorts them, or if the difference-making versus production contrast is not the clean split I take it to be — tell me so plainly and explain what format would represent the terrain better, rather than forcing a tidy tree onto untidy ground.
