I'm preparing a podcast episode that stages an interview with A. J. Ayer shortly after his 1988 near-death experience, in which his heart stopped for several minutes after he choked at a dinner party and he later reported visions involving a bright red light and a river. Ayer wrote afterward that the episode slightly weakened his long-held conviction that death is annihilation, while insisting it gave him no inclination toward theism and no new respect for religion. I want a sequence of six to eight questions that forces Ayer to say, by the standards of his own earlier work — his verificationist scruples, his treatment of personal identity as a matter of memory and psychological continuity, and his insistence that mystical experience yields at most private conviction rather than public evidence — what the experience could actually show. The sequence should press, among other things: whether a memory formed while the brain was deprived of oxygen is better explained as malfunctioning physiology than as a glimpse of survival; what evidence, by his own lights, could ever raise the probability of post-mortem survival; what sense it makes to soften on survival while flatly denying any movement toward God; and whether his later hedging amounts to a change of evidence or merely a change of temperament. Anticipate his characteristic deflections and build the follow-ups into the sequence rather than listing them separately.

Present the result as a table with five columns: Order (the question's position in the sequence); Question (the full question as I would pose it); Anticipated reply (Ayer's likely answer or evasion, in one or two sentences); Follow-up (the question that closes off that reply); and Pressure point (the specific tension in Ayer's overall position that the exchange exploits).
