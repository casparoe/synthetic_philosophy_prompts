I'm preparing a position paper for a political theory workshop and want a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments on collective memory and history are as follows:

(a) When a community's collective memory conflicts with the documentary record, the record should give way only where memory makes straightforwardly factual claims; memory's role in constituting a community's identity is legitimate in its own right and is not answerable to professional historians.

(b) Collective memory is a form of social knowledge: communities can be epistemically better or worse off, and a community that remembers falsely is worse off for it even when the false memory is socially functional.

(c) Obligations of repair can legitimately rest on collective memory even where the documentary record is thin or absent, because archives often record exactly what the powerful chose to preserve and destroy what they chose to erase.

(d) No community is obliged to outsource its self-understanding to academic historiography.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision that restores consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity, state for each what would have to give, and do not treat all four commitments as equally vulnerable.
