So this might turn into a bit of a rant, bear with me. I've been reading rationalist-adjacent stuff for around ten years now -- LessWrong back when the comment section was still alive, the Overcoming Bias archives, the usual anthropic reasoning rabbit holes -- and one debate that keeps resurfacing basicaly unchanged is quantum immortality. The argument, for anyone not steeped in this: if many-worlds is true, then in any death scenario there's always some branch where you survive, and since you can only ever experience branches in which you exist, subjectively you should expect to keep waking up, forever. Eventually you become this ancient mangled person surviving longer and longer odds. The standard counterarguments, which I could now recite in my sleep: (1) the measure of your surviving branches falls off so fast that "you" effectively die anyway, and near-zero measure is death for all practical purposes; (2) death is rarely binary, so what the argument actually predicts is drifting into ever-worse states -- comas, brain damage, locked-in hell -- not clean immortality; (3) the whole thing equivocates between "there exists a branch containing someone who remembers being me" and "I will go on to be that person," which smuggles in a full theory of personal identity that nobody has actually defended.

Here's my frustration: this exact exchange, in nearly these exact words, has been running since I started reading these forums, and probaly since the 90s, and nobody's position ever moves. Everyone already knows all the arguments on both sides. The pro and anti people talk past each other with total reliability, and i cant figure out what would even count as either side winning.

My hypothesis, which I want pressure-tested rather than accepted: this isn't really a disagreement about quantum mechanics or even about probability. It's a disagreement about personal identity and indexicals dressed up as one. The pro side tacitly assumes that "you" just is whatever future copy carries your memories, so survival in any branch at all = your survival. The anti side thinks "you" is tied to measure, or to some thicker kind of continuity, so a measure-epsilon copy doesn't count as you surviving. If that's right, the QM framing is doing zero work and the whole thing should collapse into the ordinary swamp of personal identity -- which would explain exactly why it's stalled. But maybe I'm wrong, and there's a genuinely formal question buried in there about how to reason over branches, or about whether the notion of anthropic measure is even coherent, and the stall has a different explanation.
