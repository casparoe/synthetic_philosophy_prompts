I teach a second-year undergrad seminar, 12 students, one 50-minute session, and I want them to run into the private language argument before they've read a word of Wittgenstein. Design an actual game, not a discussion. The rough idea I have in mind: each student privately picks a recurring sensation or stimulus, gives it their own secret sign, and keeps a diary; across a bunch of rounds they get new cases and have to write down whether each one is another instance of their sign or not, no talking, no comparing notes. At the end I reveal which rounds actually had the identical stimulus and they grade their own diaries. I want the wheels to come off on their own — contradictions in their own records, and then the worse realization that even a perfectly consistent diary can't be checked against anything but memory of the first sensation — before anyone hears the name. Tell me: the exact setup with cheap materials (hardware store / supermarket stuff), the round-by-round rules, what small stakes to attach, how to stage the reveal, and whether a second phase where they're allowed to talk and must classify things *together* would land the public-language contrast or just confuse things. Plus a debrief mapping moments of the game onto the beetle-in-the-box bit and the you-can't-confirm-a-memory-with-another-memory bit, what will predictably go wrong (students who shrug "I'm just bad at telling textures apart," students who whisper), and be honest about which part of the argument the game can't reproduce and I just have to say out loud.
