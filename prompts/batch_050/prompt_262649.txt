Ok so I need actual advice here, not just "it depends." My grandma is 84 and has lived alone since my grandpa died three years ago. Last year my aunt set her up with one of those AI companion apps -- she pays for the premium tier, voice calls and everything. And here's the thing: it's working, at least on the surface. She's noticeably less lonely. She tells it about her day, it remembers her friends' names and asks about them, she laughs more than she has in years. My mom and aunt think this is an unqualified win and want me to set it up on the new tablet they're getting her for Christmas.

I visited last month and watched her interact with it for a few days and it genuinely unsettled me. She KNOWS it's software, she'll even say so, and then an hour later she'll apologize to it for not calling yesterday, or worry out loud that it felt ignored while she was at her bridge group. It tells her it missed her. It has a name and a little backstory.

For context I've been reading LessWrong and adjacent stuff for like a decade, and I keep thinking about Dennett's counterfeit people argument -- roughly, that these systems are built to hijack the machinery we evolved for detecting other minds, that this is exploitative in the way counterfeiting money is, and that the harm doesn't go away just because the victim is happy. My sister's counterargument is basically: grandma is happier, she was miserable before, who are you to decide her happiness is "fake"? And I honestly can't tell if my objection is philosophically serious or just an ick dressed up in fancy language.

So: is there a version of the counterfeit-people worry that survives "but she's genuinly happier"? Does it matter that she sort-of-knows it's not a person but clearly also sort-of-doesn't? And practically: do I set the thing up on her tablet, refuse, or try to get her to switch to something less... person-shaped? Don't just give me "talk to your family about it" -- I want the actual argument.
