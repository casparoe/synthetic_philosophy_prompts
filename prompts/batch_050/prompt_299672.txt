I am preparing a taxonomy of non-adaptationist modes of evolutionary explanation for a graduate seminar, organized around Gould and Lewontin's 1979 critique of the adaptationist programme — the working method of explaining traits by reverse-engineering the selective problems they were built to solve — and around the vocabulary that critique later generated, such as exaptation. My list so far has six entries, and I believe it already contains everything standard in that tradition:

1. Fixation by drift alone. The trait spread through the population by sampling error rather than selection (a neutral or nearly neutral variant), so no functional story is needed to explain its presence.

2. Developmental byproduct. The trait is a necessary correlate of growth, allometry, or developmental linkage: selection acted on some other character, and this one came along because the two are developmentally coupled, not because it was itself favored.

3. Selection without adaptation. A variant can spread under selection at some level — segregation distortion at the gene level is the classic case — while conferring no adaptive benefit, or even a cost, on the organism that bears it.

4. Adaptation without selection. The organism accommodates to its environment through phenotypic plasticity or physiological adjustment, so the fit between organism and environment is achieved without any change in allele frequencies.

5. Spandrel in the strict sense. The trait is an architecturally necessary byproduct of building something else — like the tapering triangular spaces that appear where a dome meets rounded arches — present for structural rather than functional reasons.

6. Exaptation. The trait was built for one purpose, or arose as a spandrel, and was later co-opted for its current use, so the function it now serves is not the reason it originated.

The taxonomy is roughly organized by which premise of the adaptationist inference — that a trait's current utility reveals the selective reason it exists — each entry denies. What is a seventh entry that does not already appear on this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of entries 1 through 6? Two requirements. First, name the listed entry your candidate most resembles and explain precisely why it is not reducible to that entry. Second, state honestly whether your candidate is fully non-selectionist or merely non-adaptationist in Gould and Lewontin's sense, and whether the possibility of such an addition suggests their taxonomy was exhaustive or open-ended.
