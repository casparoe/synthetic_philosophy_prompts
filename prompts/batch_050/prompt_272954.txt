So I'm a PhD student in epistemology, but I've gotten pulled into a reading group on philosophy of religion and now I'm half-convinced my dissertation chapter needs a section on divine hiddenness. The literature has a pile of responses to the problem — free will stuff, soul-making stuff, "God's love isn't coercive" stuff, higher-order goods, stuff about how more evidence wouldn't produce the right kind of belief — and I keep reading them and thinking: okay, but what would a solution to this problem even have to *do*? Everyone seems to be working with a different implicit job description, and then they talk past each other because one person's knockdown requirement is another person's optional extra.

Write me the job description. What I want is the list of desiderata that any adequate theistic response to divine hiddenness has to meet to count as actually addressing the problem rather than changing the subject, with the motivating consideration or case behind each one. Things I'm hoping you'll get into, though don't let me constrain you: whether a theory has to explain why nonbelief seems to afflict people who aren't resisting anything (the honest-seeker cases are the ones I find genuinely hard), whether it has to explain the *distribution* of hiddenness — why some people report overwhelming religious experiences and their equally devout neighbour gets decades of silence, and why alleged dramatic revelations seem concentrated in particular times and places — whether it has to square a God's supposed desire for relationship with every person against whatever epistemic distance the theory posits as necessary, and whether it matters if the desiderata come apart for traditions with an incarnation or prophet versus bare monotheism (like, if your explanation for why God stays hidden *also* rules out the founding revelation your tradition depends on, that seems bad? nobody I've read flags this cleanly).

Some structure, roughly in this order:

1. **The checklist** (~700 words) — the desiderata themselves, each with the case or intuition pumping behind it, stated abstractly enough that it's a criterion rather than a disguised version of one particular theodicy.
2. **Negotiable vs. non-negotiable** (~200 words) — which requirements any response must meet to count as engaging the problem at all, and which are really just the commitments of one camp dressed up as common ground. I definately want you to take a stand here rather than listing everything as contested.
3. **Tension points** (~300 words) — this is the part I care most about: are there subsets of your own desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable, so that meeting one forces you to abandon another? If the list itself proves something, I want to see it.
4. **Quick scorecard** (~150 words max) — the two or three standard families of response ticked briefly against the list, one line each. Keep this brutal and short; the criteria are the deliverable, not the verdicts.

If you think one of my hoped-for requirements above is actually a prejudice rather than a real desideratum, say so in section 2 instead of quietly dropping it.
