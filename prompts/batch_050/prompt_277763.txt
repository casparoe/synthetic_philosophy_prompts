After a long argument last night with a friend who defends luck egalitarianism, I want to see the view taken as seriously as it asks to be taken. Write a mock bureaucratic document: a rejection letter from a Ministry of Distributive Justice to a citizen who has applied for compensation after some ordinary misfortune, denying the claim on the grounds that the misfortune has been reclassified as option luck rather than brute luck. Around 500 to 600 words, in the voice of a courteous, painstakingly reasonable caseworker who sincerely believes the doctrine. The satire must work by applying the doctrine correctly, not by making the caseworker stupid or malicious: the letter should trace the claimant's misfortune backward through a chain of earlier decisions until it locates the voluntary gamble that allegedly caused it, concede that the claimant did not foresee the outcome while explaining why foreseeability is not the test, and answer his objection that literally every misfortune can be traced back to some prior choice. It should also explain why the Ministry cannot reimburse the costs of assembling his appeal, since appealing is itself a gamble he remains free to decline. Keep it deadpan throughout: the reader should be left to locate the harshness in the logic of the distinction rather than in the tone of the letter, and my friend should be able to read it and admit, however grudgingly, that the caseworker has made no obvious mistake.
