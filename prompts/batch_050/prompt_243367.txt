Right, so I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, and I got into an argument at the pub about Moore's "here is a hand" thing. I tried to strengthen it and now my version seems to prove something daft, and I can't see where it breaks. Here it is:

(1) I know that here is a hand (I'm holding it up, good lighting, wide awake).
(2) If I know here is a hand, I know there's an external world, since hands are material things existing outside my mind.
(3) So I know there's an external world.
(4) The only reason anyone gives for doubting (1) is the possibility that I'm dreaming, or a brain in a vat, or in the Matrix or whatever.
(5) But to even raise that possibility, the sceptic has to understand what hands, dreams, brains and vats are, and those are concepts you can only have picked up from the external world.
(6) So scepticism presupposes the very external world it casts doubt on, which makes it self-undermining.
(7) Therefore there's simply nothing left to worry about — radical scepticism isn't even a coherent position anyone could hold.

Step (7) can't be right, can it? People have fretted about this stuff for centuries and I'd rather not conclude they were all just confused about their own concepts. But each step looks okay to me when I stare at it. Which step do I reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost me?
