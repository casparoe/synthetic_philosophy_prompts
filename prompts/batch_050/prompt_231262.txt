Please argue for the following thesis: a defensible form of prioritarianism can accept a restricted version of leveling down. Harming the better-off never makes an outcome better overall, and it is never a sufficient reason to bring that outcome about; nevertheless, in some cases it can make the outcome less unjust in one important respect and therefore weaken one objection against it.

In making the argument, distinguish carefully among:

1. an outcome’s being better overall;
2. its being less objectionable along the dimension of comparative fairness;
3. there being a reason, all things considered, to produce it.

Explain how additive prioritarianism’s separability and Pareto-like commitments generate the standard leveling-down objection. Then defend a nonseparable version of prioritarian concern under which a person’s moral claim depends partly on how that person fares relative to others, while insisting that this relational claim cannot outweigh the direct welfare loss to the person made worse off. Your account should avoid treating equality as independently valuable in a way that makes mere leveling down an improvement.

Develop the argument through these cases:

- Case A: Two isolated people have equally good lives at level 80. One person’s life can be reduced to level 60 without affecting the other.
- Case B: Ten people are at level 40 and ten at level 90. Lowering those at 90 to 70 would benefit no one.
- Case C: The ten people at 90 enjoy that advantage only because the ten at 40 were deliberately excluded from opportunities. Reducing the former group to 70 still benefits no one materially, but removes part of the disadvantage relation created by the exclusion.

State your preferred verdict for each case and explain why Case C differs from Case B, if it does. Address the strongest objections: that harm remains harm even when it eliminates a comparative complaint; that injustice is a property of the distribution rather than of any act that might remove it; and that combining overall worsening with reduced injustice yields an unstable or incoherent ranking. Conclude by identifying the narrowest principle that supports the thesis without licensing leveling down as a policy. The argument should be self-contained and should not depend on citations or attributions.
