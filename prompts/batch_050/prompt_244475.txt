ok so some background first. a while back i got fixated on this thought experiment i read about, pretty sure its from Douglas Hofstadter, one of his older books (Metamagical Themas maybe?). it's called the "careenium". you picture a big tank full of thousands of tiny marbles ("simmballs") bouncing around chaotically at the bottom, and mixed in there are a few much bigger balls ("syms"). the simmballs constantly knock the syms around, and the syms settle into stable configurations that work like symbols, so the whole tank can sort of "think" or do arithmetic. the thing that keeps me up: when the tank "decides" something, did the symbol-level pattern actually push anything around? or is literally all the pushing done by the little marbles at the bottom, and the symbol story is just us narrating from the cheap seats?

i argue about this with friends on a discord server (none of us have any philosophy training, we just like arguing about minds and whether AI could really think) and i want to settle it properly. so: build me a "where do you actually stand on the careenium question" sorting quiz.

requirements:

- 8 to 10 questions. each one has to be a concrete little scenario, not an abstract question. stuff like: did the thermostat's "goal" of 20 degrees cause the furnace to click on, or just the bimetal strip bending? did the glider in a game of life cause the cells it ran into to flip? when my car skidded on ice, did the curve of the road cause anything, or only tire rubber and ice molecules? make up your own, those are just the flavor i want. nobody should need to know any jargon or philosopher names to answer. max 3 answer options per question, written in plain language.
- no question may just ask "so do you think high-level patterns have causal power, yes or no." every question has to be a case where at least two of the positions below genuinely come apart.
- the scoring key has to sort people into these five camps, which is roughly the logical space as i see it (correct me if i've missed one): (1) only the bottom level ever pushes anything, symbol-talk is a handy fiction, full stop; (2) the symbol level really does push, because a pattern can be real and effective even though it's made of marbles, and the marbles alone don't fix what happens; (3) the symbol's pushing and the marbles' pushing are the SAME event described at two zoom levels, so asking which one "really" caused it is a bad question; (4) there is no symbol level at all, not even as a fiction, "symbols" are just a story we tell and we'd do better to drop the word; (5) "causes" means something different at each level, so both levels can legitimately cause things without competing.
- build the key from the answer-combinations outward: first show which combos of answers map to which camp, then tell me which combos NOBODY occupies and guess why (is that spot incoherent, or just unpopular?). if some pair of camps can't be pulled apart by any set of case-questions, flag that honestly instead of fudging the scoring.
- one question should probe my own pet theory, which is: the high level only genuinely pushes when the system was SET UP (by evolution or an engineer) so that the low level reliably tracks it. so a thermostat's setpoint really does something, but a game-of-life glider doesn't, because nobody built life to mean anything. after the quiz, tell me straight whether my view is its own camp or just camp (2) or (3) wearing a hat.
- for each result write a short casual paragraph the quiz can display, telling the person what they apparently believe and the strongest objection to it, since half the fun is my friends getting mad at their results. definately keep the tone like i'm talking to them, not like a textbook.
