Last night I had a long argument with a friend about a decision I'm facing. I have two job offers: one is a funded research position in a field I love, with modest pay and an uncertain future; the other is a corporate role with a high salary, clear advancement, and work I'd find merely fine. Neither strikes me as better than the other — but they don't feel exactly equal either, and the choice remains genuinely hard no matter how long I think about it.

My friend insists this is confused. Her argument: if the two offers were really equally good, then improving either one even slightly — say the corporation adds a few hundred dollars to the annual salary — should break the tie and make that option strictly better. But when she posed the improved offer to me, the choice still felt just as hard as before. She concludes that there must be a fact of the matter about which offer is better, and that my failure to identify it is just ignorance or indecision on my part.

Is she right? Specifically: does the failure of her small-improvement test show that the values at stake here simply cannot be compared at all, or is there some third possibility — a way two options can be comparable without one being better, worse, or exactly equal to the other? And if her argument fails, what does that imply about how I should actually go about choosing?
