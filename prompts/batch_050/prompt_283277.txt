Just got out of a three hour argument with my friend over dinner (it ended with both of us gesturing at a hypothetical cow, don't ask) and now I want to know if my position on animals actually survives or whether I'm contradicting myself somewhere. Here's roughly what I found myself defending:

1. I'm basically with Nozick on the division of labor: utilitarianism is the right framework for animals, but for people you need inviolable rights, i.e. side-constraints that can't be traded off for the greater good.
2. My reason for rejecting utilitarianism among persons is the separateness of persons: there is no super-individual who experiences the sum of everyone's lives, so adding gains and losses across people and letting one person's suffering be outweighed by others' benefits ignores the fact that each life is lived by someone. That's the whole basis of my case against sacrificing individuals.
3. Animals' pleasures and pains count morally and can be added up across animals, which is exactly why factory farming is an atrocity and why I don't eat meat.
4. Species membership by itself is morally irrelevant. What matters is what a being is like (sentience, capacities, etc.), not what species it belongs to. Discriminating on species alone is just speciesism.
5. All human beings get the full Kantian treatment, including the severely cognitively disabled: no tradeoffs, no being used as mere means, full stop. Being human is enough.
6. Painlessly killing a happy animal for food is at worst a minor wrong, and arguably no wrong at all if the animal is replaced by another one that will live an equally happy life. So some idealized pasture-farming-with-replacement setup is basically fine; for animals, what matters is the total amount of good experience, not the continuation of any particular individual.

My friend's two charges were that I'm being speciesist after all and that my vegetarianism is inconsistent with the replaceability thing in (6). I think she's wrong on both counts, but the arguement rattled me enough that now I'm worried there's something else I haven't noticed. So: audit the package. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only look like they do, and for each real conflict how severe is it, what's the cheapest revision, and what does the revision cost me? Rank them by severity rather than just flagging everything, and if one of her charges dissolves on inspection, say so.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the overall verdict on whether these hang together — then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating my commitments back at me.
