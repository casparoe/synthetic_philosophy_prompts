I've been reading about the old socialist calculation debate -- Mises and Hayek arguing that a centrally planned economy can't work because prices carry information no planner could gather, Lange responding that a planning board could just simulate a market by adjusting administered prices trial-and-error style. Everyone in these discussions seems to talk past each other, and I keep having the feeling that my field already has the vocabulary to say who's right, or at least what the disagreement actually is.

So here's what I want: restate the whole debate in the vocabulary I actually think in. Mises's argument, Hayek's knowledge argument, and Lange's trial-and-error proposal, each translated into terms like compute budget, message passing, bandwidth, latency, state aggregation, consistency, convergence, gradient-based vs. closed-form solutions, loss of local information under compression, incentive-compatible reporting, etc. Treat the planning board like a central coordinator node, treat prices like a compressed representation, and so on -- as faithfully as you can, don't just gesture at "it's like MapReduce."

Then the important part: give me a residue report. For each of the three positions, what survives the translation cleanly, what only translates with distortion (say what the distortion is), and what has no expression in this vocabulary at all. And then your verdict: is the residue stuff my framework genuinely can't represent (e.g., maybe the subjective-preference side doesn't fit anywhere in a systems vocabulary?), or is it a sign that part of the original debate was less substantive than it looked?

One thing I specifically want your take on: Lange's scheme, translated, looks a lot like an iterative optimization protocol where the center adjusts parameters based on reported excess demand. In my world that kind of thing has known failure modes -- stale information, strategic reporting by nodes, slow convergence relative to how fast the environment changes. Do those failure modes map onto the actual objections Austrians raised, or did they miss some and overstate others?
