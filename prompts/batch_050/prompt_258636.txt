I am a high school student, and in my philosophy elective we recently studied Augustine's account of time in Book XI of the Confessions. As I understand it, his view has three main components: first, the past and the future are not real in the way the present is, since the past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist; second, when we measure time, we are really measuring something in the mind — a kind of stretching-out of the soul across memory, attention, and expectation — rather than a feature of the world itself; and third, God created time along with the world, so it is meaningless to ask what God was doing "before" creation, since there was no "before" at all.

Here is my assignment. Imagine Augustine were brought into the present day and given a careful, non-mathematical explanation of two developments he never encountered. The first is Einstein's relativity of simultaneity: observers moving relative to one another disagree about which distant events are happening "right now," and physics recognizes no single universal present moment slicing through the cosmos. The second is the modern cosmological picture in which the universe began roughly 13.8 billion years ago, with space and time themselves coming into existence at that point rather than stretching back infinitely.

Predict, in detail, how Augustine would respond to each of these, treating them separately rather than lumping them together. For each one, explain which elements of his position in Book XI it would support, which it would threaten, and how he would most plausibly defend or revise his view using the resources already present in his own reasoning — not by inventing new doctrines on his behalf, but by extending his actual arguments. Specifically address: whether the relativity of simultaneity would force him to abandon his claim that only the present exists, or whether he could reinterpret that claim so it survives; whether a cosmic beginning of time would strike him as a vindication of his answer to the "what was God doing before creation" objection; and whether his view that time is measured in the soul can remain credible in a physics that treats time as part of the fabric of the universe. Conclude with what you take to be the single strongest counter-argument Augustine would raise against a modern physicist who told him that his puzzles about time had simply been dissolved by science, and say whether you find that counter-argument persuasive.
