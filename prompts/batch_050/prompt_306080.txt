Leibniz held that God surveys the whole range of possible worlds and actualises only the best, leaving the rest as unrealised possibilia in the divine understanding. Suppose he had lived to encounter two later developments: Hugh Everett's many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, on which every quantum outcome is realised in some branch of a single wavefunction, and David Lewis's modal realism, on which all possible worlds exist as concretely as ours. Which, if either, could he have accepted? In particular, does many-worlds amount to the actualisation of (a large subset of) the possible worlds and thereby refute his optimism, or could he co-opt it by claiming that God actualises the best possible wavefunction rather than the best possible world? And would his principle of sufficient reason or the identity of indiscernibles give him an independent objection to either view? I want a definite verdict on what Leibniz would have said, with reasons drawn from his metaphysics, not a survey of the options; where you are extrapolating beyond anything in the Theodicy or the Monadology, say so explicitly. Please write in British English.
