Alright, some background: I spent 38 years as a research chemist (polymers, mostly), retired in 2021, and since then I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole the way a lot of retired scientists do. Lately I've been chewing on the question of whether work is actually necessary for a meaningful life, or whether that's just propaganda we tell ourselves because automation hasn't finished the job yet. My old lab group has a WhatsApp thread where this comes up constantly, because half of us watched our own field get partially automated before we left.

Here's what I want you to build for me. Construct a thought experiment involving two societies that are as close to identical as you can make them, except for ONE feature related to work, such that one society's members clearly live meaningful lives and the other's clearly do not (or at least clearly do worse, meaning-wise). The catch: full automation has happened in BOTH societies. Nobody in either one has to work. So you can't make the variable "they have jobs vs. they don't" -- that's too easy and tells us nothing. Instead, I want you to first decide which specific good that work provides is the load-bearing one -- candidates people usually mention are income, social status, daily structure, social contact, mastery of a skill, and contributing to something beyond yourself -- and then give BOTH societies every one of those goods EXCEPT your chosen one, supplied through some other channel. (E.g., if you think contribution is the key thing, then both societies get income via generous stipends, status via sports leagues or whatever, structure via scheduled communal activities, and so on -- but only one gets genuine contribution.)

Constraints: keep it realistic -- near-future Earth, no brain implants, no wireheading, nobody is drugged or brainwashed. Both societies must be politically free and materially comfortable, no dystopia-by-stipulation, because I want the difference in meaningfulness to come from the variable itself and not from you having rigged the scenery. Also the people in both societies should be psychologically normal, not philosophers and not idiots.

After the scenario: (1) state clearly which variable you isolated and defend why you picked that one over the others; (2) walk through what the main rival candidates would predict about your two societies and show that your case survives them; (3) tell me honestly which part of the setup is doing the heavy lifting and whether the case collapses if you tweak it; and (4) this is the retired scientist in me talking -- say whether your thought experiment could in principle be turned into an actual empirical test, even an impractical one, or whether it's the kind of claim that no evidence could settle, and if the latter, whether that should make us suspicious of it.

One more thing: don't just assert that one society is miserable -- show me the ordinary Tuesday of an ordinary person in each one, and let the difference (if there really is one) emerge from that. If you find you can't make the two Tuesdays recieve different verdicts without cheating somewhere, tell me that instead, because that would itself be an interesting result.
