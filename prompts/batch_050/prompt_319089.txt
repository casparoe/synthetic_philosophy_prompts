While revising for the final exam in my philosophy of biology course, I came across a set of revision notes circulated by a previous cohort. They summarize the two main accounts of biological function as follows:

'On the etiological or selected-effects account, a trait's function is the effect for which it was selected in its evolutionary history: the heart's function is to pump blood because pumping is what past hearts did that explains why hearts persisted. Its great strength is that it makes room for malfunction — a diseased heart still has the function of pumping even when it can no longer pump, since function is fixed by history rather than by present performance. The corresponding weakness is novelty: the first organism to benefit from a new trait does not yet have that function, because a function can exist only after many generations of selection. On the rival causal-role account, a function is simply a causal contribution to some capacity of the containing system: pumping is the heart's function relative to the system's capacity to circulate blood. Malfunction is straightforward here — a malfunctioning heart is just one that fails to pump — and the account avoids any appeal to evolutionary history, which is why it is often said to be the notion actually used by working physiologists. Its weakness is that it cannot separate function from accident: relative to a suitably chosen containing system, the heart's function is also to make a rhythmic noise. The dispute between the two accounts is therefore illusory: the etiological account is the evolutionary biologist's notion and the causal-role account the physiologist's, they answer different questions, and once this is seen they can never genuinely conflict.'

I have a nagging feeling that the point about malfunction cuts the other way, but I cannot reconstruct why. Could you audit the summary claim by claim — which parts a defender of each account would accept, which are misrepresentations or overstatements, and which are standard objections presented as if they were settled — and then tell me whether the notes' concluding verdict survives once the errors are corrected?
