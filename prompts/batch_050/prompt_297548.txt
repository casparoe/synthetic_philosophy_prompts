I'm in a grad reading group on knowledge-first epistemology and we're each supposed to post a short statement of our commitments before next week's session so the others can poke holes in it, so here's mine. I've been sympathetic to pragmatic encroachment for a while but I've honestly never sat down and checked whether the whole package I want is coherent as a set. Here's what I want to hold: (1) The bank cases work: two people can have exactly the same evidence that the bank is open on Saturday, and the one for whom nothing rides on it knows while the one who'd be ruined by a missed deposit doesn't. And I want this as interest-relative invariantism, not contextualism -- the word "knows" doesn't shift semantically between contexts, the facts about knowledge themselves change with the subject's practical situation. (2) Knowledge is the norm of practical reasoning: you're in a position to treat p as a premise in your deliberation iff you know p. (3) Williamson-style anti-luminosity: for pretty much any non-trivial condition there are borderline positions where you're in it but in no position to know you're in it; margins for error are just unavoidable. (4) Justification itself is stakes-free: same evidence, same degree of justification, no matter what's riding on it -- the stakes only change what knowledge requires on top of justification. (5) Knowledge is closed under known competent deduction. (6) Sometimes you should just act on p in a high-stakes situation even when you'd freely grant you don't know p -- the paramedic who's administered a drug a hundred times shouldn't stop to re-verify the dosage when there's no time; acting on excellent grounds that fall short of knowledge is sometimes exactly what's called for.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly? I have a suspicion there's a problem somewhere around (2) and (6) but I can't tell how deep it runs, and a vaguer worry that (3) makes trouble somewhere I haven't spotted. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, distinguish them from pairs that only look like they clash, say for each real tension what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, and tell me which single revision you'd make if you could only make one. Please actually commit to an answer here -- don't just lay out a menu of internally consistent sub-packages and leave me to pick one.
