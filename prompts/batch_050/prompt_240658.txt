I am a PhD student working on the metaphysics of time, and for a dissertation chapter on Confessions XI I have written down my working position so that my committee can attack it. Before they do, I would like a consistency audit. Here is what I currently hold:

(1) Presentism: only the present exists. The past has ceased to be, the future is not yet, and apparent reference to past or future entities is at best a façon de parler.

(2) The Augustinian indivisible present: the present has no temporal extent whatsoever, since any extent, however small, would be divisible into a part already past and a part still to come, and so would not be wholly present.

(3) Direct perception of the temporally extended: we perceive motion, change, and temporal wholes such as melodies directly, in experience itself, rather than reconstructing them from memory after the fact.

(4) Mind-independent measurability of duration: that one interval lasted twice as long as another is a fact about time itself, which persons and instruments get right or get wrong.

(5) Augustine's positive account is essentially correct: the past exists only as memory and the future only as expectation, both in a present mind; and when we measure time we measure something that the passage of time has left in the mind, so that time itself is a distentio animi, a distension of the soul.

What I want to know is whether this package hangs together. Please identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which pairs only appear to be; I suspect at least one of the apparent conflicts dissolves once the relevant commitment is stated carefully, and I would rather be told which than have everything flagged as equally problematic. For each real tension, say how severe it is, what exactly would have to give, and what that concession would cost the overall view -- in particular, whether the cost is borne by the Augustinian material or by my independent metaphysical commitments. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether the position that remains after that revision is still recognizably Augustinian or whether I would be keeping the label and abandoning the view. I am not attached to any particular one of these theses, so do not assume you know which I would prefer to surrender.
