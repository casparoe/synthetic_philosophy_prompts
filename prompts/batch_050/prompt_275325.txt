I've been thinking about when it makes sense to say someone owns an idea, and my gut verdicts across cases don't line up, so I want the principle behind them, if there is one. I've read enough rationalist blogs to know my intutions aren't sacred, so be willing to tell me some of them have to go.

The cases: a songwriter lifts a four-bar hook from an obscure B-side she heard once years ago, genuinely doesn't remember hearing it, and passes it off (to herself as much as anyone) as hers. Feels like infringement anyway. Two chemists independantly synthesise the same compound a month apart; the second publishes first. I don't think the first owns anything, even though she got there first in the lab. A fan writes a novel set in someone else's universe, gives it away free, and it's better than the original. Morally fine by me, and I'd be annoyed if lawyers stopped it. A pharma company spends a decade showing a known plant extract treats something new and patents the use. That feels like theft from everyone; the compound was sitting there the whole time. An artist's entire catalogue is scraped to train a model that can now imitate her style on demand and undercut her commissions, even though no single output copies any single work. This one feels clearly wrong to me. And a mathematician proves a theorem, someone else rediscovers the same proof a week later, nobody owns theorems in my book.

The pair I can't reconcile is the songwriter and the style-imitating model: in both, something was absorbed and reproduced without deliberate copying, yet I convict the model and mostly excuse the songwriter. What principle is doing that work? Is it scale, the deliberateness of building the system, that the songwriter added something of her own?

What I want is a principle, or two candidates, stated precisely enough that I can see which verdicts it endorses, which it overturns, and what it says about cases I haven't listed (recipes, perfume formulas, an algorithm kept as a trade secret). Be honest about which of my verdicts no tidy principle can save. Where the law and the ethics come apart, say which is which, and please mark clearly what is actually settled versus genuinely contested among people who work on this, rather than presenting one contested position as the consensus.
