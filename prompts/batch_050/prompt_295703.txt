so I'm in an epistemology seminar this semester and we just did the Meno stuff about why knowledge is supposed to be better than mere true belief (the road to Larissa bit, the statues of Daedalus, etc.). I thought I got it, but then I came up with a case that I think breaks the standard answer and I'd like you to explain where it goes wrong, if it does.

Standard story as I understand it: knowledge beats true belief because knowledge is stable/tied down, or because it's reliably produced, and reliability is valuable because it means you'd still be right in nearby situations where the facts were slightly different.

Now the case. Say two people both truly believe that a certain bridge is safe. Alice figured it out by actually understanding the load calculations, Bob just guessed based on vibes and happened to be right. Fine, Alice knows and Bob doesn't, and the standard story says Alice's state is better because if the bridge had been slightly weaker, or the wind slightly stronger, she'd have adjusted and Bob would've been wrong. Counterfactual robustness, ok.

But here's the twist: suppose it's stipulated that they each cross the bridge exactly once, right now, under the actual conditions, and then the bridge is demolished and no remotely similar situation ever comes up for either of them again. No nearby possible world in which they act on this belief is ever actualized or even relevant to anything they care about. Both cross safely. Has Alice's knowledge lost all its extra value over Bob's true belief? Because intuitively I still want to say her state was somehow better -- but the robustness explanation seems to have nothing to grip onto anymore, since the robustness never cashs out in any actual difference.

So my question: is there a way to explain the extra value of knowledge that DOESN'T bottom out in counterfactual reliability or future usefulness? Like, can the value be non-instrumental in some way that isn't just "it's better because it's knowledge"? Please give me the actual philosophical options here and which one you think survives my bridge case, with intuitions, not just a summary of the litrature.
