I run a small philosophy meetup at my local library (we're not academics, just people who like arguing about this stuff, ~14 people show up on a good night) and next month I want to do a session on that old question of whether a huge number of tiny harms can ever add up to more than one big harm. The classic version is something like: would you rather one person suffer horribly for decades, or an astronomically large number of people each get a single speck of dust in their eye? Everyone in the group has strong gut reactions but they just talk past each other -- half say "obviously the dust specks, each one is basically nothing" and half say "no amount of nothing adds up to something" and then we go in circles for an hour. I want to design an activity or game where they actually *feel* the problem instead of just stating their positions, so they run into the weirdness themselves before we even name it.

Here's what I'm picturing and where I need help. We have about 90 minutes. I want something with real (small) stakes -- like maybe actual tiny unpleasantnesses distributed across the group versus one volunteer taking a clearly-worse-but-still-harmless bad thing, so people have to actually choose, not just opine. I've thought about using things like sitting in an uncomfortable chair, getting a text message ping you can't ignore, eating a gross jellybean, etc. But I'm worried about two design problems I keep hitting. First, if I let people vote on each trade one at a time, they can just be inconsistent without noticing (accept speck-trade A, reject speck-trade B, when B is the same trade with bigger numbers). Second, any real stakes I can ethically impose in a library are all on the "dust speck" end of the scale, so I don't know how to make the "torture" side feel real without it becoming a weird hypothetical again. Maybe there's a way to make one person accumulate enough small harms that it becomes genuinely bad, and then the group has to confront whether that was basically the thing they said couldn't matter?

So: design me the actual runnable activity -- rules, sequence of rounds, how choices get made (individual vs group vs secret ballot?), a rough timeline for 90 minutes, and what materials I'd need to bring. Then the debrief: how do I map what happened onto the philosophical positions (the "harms don't aggregate across people" view vs the "enough tiny harms eventually outweigh" view), what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to recover, and please be honest about which feature of the original thought experiment this can't reproduce -- I suspect it's something about the *scale* being unrepresentable in a room of 14 people, but maybe there's a trick I'm not seeing. Also, one constraint: a couple of our regulars are conflict-averse and I'd like them to be able to participate without having to publicly defend an unpopular vote, if that can be built into the rules somehow.
