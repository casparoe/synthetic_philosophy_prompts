I am putting together a small interdisciplinary research group — one mathematician trained in stochastic processes, one economist, and one philosopher of economics — funded for three years to work on ergodicity economics, understood broadly as the research program that treats the distinction between time averages and ensemble averages as foundational for decision-making rather than as a technical footnote. I need to set the agenda, and I would like your help prioritizing among the following candidate open questions, though you should feel free to substitute others you judge more important:

1. The normative question. Defenders of the program present time-average growth maximization as an alternative foundation to expected utility theory; critics respond that for multiplicative dynamics it simply recovers logarithmic utility and so renames rather than replaces the standard framework. Is there a live, substantive dispute here — for instance about whether the justification of a decision rule should appeal to what happens to the agent over time rather than to a weighted sum over states — or is the disagreement merely presentational?

2. The scope question. The mathematics is cleanest for geometric Brownian motion and close relatives. How much of the framework survives for dynamics that are path-dependent, have absorbing states (bankruptcy, irreversible reputational damage, death), or switch regimes, and do the policy conclusions change in those settings?

3. The pooling question. A recurring claim in this literature is that insurance, diversification, and social safety nets work by letting individuals approximate an ensemble average they cannot achieve along their own trajectory. Is there a general theory to be had of when pooling converts a non-ergodic situation into an effectively ergodic one for its participants, and would such a theory yield non-obvious guidance for institutional design?

4. The empirical question. How much of observed risk aversion and of the classic behavioral anomalies is better explained by time-average considerations than by utility curvature or by cognitive bias, and is this even separable empirically?

5. The identification question. An agent embedded in a single unfolding history must somehow determine which process class she faces before the time-average/ensemble-average distinction can guide her. Is there interesting work to be done on this epistemic problem, or is it just the familiar problem of statistical model selection in new dress?

Please give me a defended ordering, not a survey. For each question you retain: state which of the others depend on its resolution (I want the dependency structure made explicit, since an upstream question may deserve priority over a more exciting downstream one), assess tractability specifically for the team I described rather than in the abstract, and say what result or development within the three years would move it up or down the ranking. If one of the five is prominent in the discourse but you would deprioritize it, say so and explain why. The deliverable I need is a three-year agenda with a rationale, and the rationale matters more to me than the particular list.
