so i just started a job at a small AI startup (fine-tuning chatbots for customer support, nothing glamorous) and its made the symbol grounding problem feel weirdly personal. i spend all day around systems that produce perfectly fluent text, and i honestly can't tell whether anything in there is about anything, or whether it's all just rule-based shuffling of symbols the way the person in searle's chinese room shuffles squiggles.

here's what bugs me. this debate has been going since searle's chinese room in 1980 and harnad giving the problem its name around 1990, and as far as i can tell nobody has actually moved. harnad's own fix -- ground the symbols in sensorimotor experience, a robot that learns the word cat by dealing with actual cats -- seems to just relocate the question to whether the robot's internal states are really about cats either. and now LLMs have thrown fuel on it: one camp says a model trained only on text can never get from words to the world, no matter the scale (there's that octopus thought experiment about an octopus tapping an undersea cable between two humans and learning to fake conversation purely from the statistics of their messages), and the other camp says either that meaning just is use, so if the system uses words appropriately that's all the grounding there is, or that text alone already contains so much statistical structure about the world that the representations end up world-shaped anyway.

my working hypothesis, which i want stress-tested not confirmed: the debate stalled because "grounded" is doing at least three jobs at once -- (a) the symbols being causally hooked up to the world in the right way, (b) the system having some inner what-it's-like of meaning something, and (c) the system being disposed to act correctly on what the words refer to -- and people who basically agree on (c) and even (a) keep fighting because they're silently importing different versions of (b), which nobody can test for anyway. so the "real" disagreement is about consciousness wearing a semantics costume.

is that the right diagnosis? or is there a genuinely substantive question buried here -- say, an empirical one about whether pure text prediction builds representations that track the structure of the world, which some probing/world-model research is arguably already answering? tell me what kind of disagreement the symbol grounding debate actually is, working from what each side really argues (searle/harnad, the embodied cognition people, the LLM optimists, the meaning-is-use people), and given your diagnosis tell me what would count as settling it: a specific kind of experiment on a model, a formal result, a distinction everyone could accept -- or argue that nothing would settle it and why. and if my three-jobs split is wrong or there's a cleaner carving of the positions, say so.
