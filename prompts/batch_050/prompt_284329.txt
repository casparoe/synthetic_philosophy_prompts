Luck egalitarianism, roughly: inequalities between people are unjust if and only if they trace to brute luck rather than to choices for which the people can properly be held responsible. I'd like your actual credence that this is the correct core of a theory of distributive justice -- not a survey of objections, but a number with its derivation.

Structure the answer in five sections, in this order:

1. "The proposition, sharpened" (~3 sentences): state the precise claim you are assigning a probability to, since 'correct core' needs disambiguating -- e.g., whether you take it as a claim about all of justice or only about what makes distributions pro tanto objectionable.

2. "My credence" (one line): a single number between 0 and 1.

3. "The decomposition" (~200 words, the heart of the answer): break the credence into the sub-questions it depends on, each with its own probability and one line of justification. At minimum, address: whether responsibility has intrinsic distributive significance at all (as opposed to being a proxy for welfare or desert); whether the brute-luck/option-luck line is coherent given that choices are themselves products of prior luck; whether a state could in principle track genuine responsibility without either abandoning the unlucky or becoming intolerably intrusive (the harshness and surveillance objections); and whether the view, once fixed up to avoid these, still says anything distinctively egalitarian rather than collapsing into prioritarianism or a sufficiency view.

4. "How the terms combine" (~4 sentences): show roughly how the sub-probabilities produce your headline number, and whether the combination is conjunctive (the view fails if any sub-question resolves against it) or whether some terms compensate for others.

5. "The weakest link" (~3 sentences): identify the sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and state what argument or finding would move it most.
