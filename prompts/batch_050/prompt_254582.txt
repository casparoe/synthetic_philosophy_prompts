Is there an established name for this failure mode in climate assessment? A projected regional rainfall change is called robust because most models in an ensemble agree on its sign. Yet the models are not fully independent, the ensemble samples only part of the relevant parameter space, and agreement about the sign may coexist with wide disagreement about the magnitude and timing of threshold crossings. For a water planner choosing reservoir capacity, the decision depends on the latter quantities, so treating the consensus as sufficient confidence can support the wrong action. Does this mistake—equating agreement on an ensemble summary with robustness in the variable that matters for a decision—have a name of its own? How exactly should it be distinguished from model consensus, ordinary robustness, structural uncertainty, and deep uncertainty? If no established concept fits precisely, propose a term with a definition and some boundary cases.
