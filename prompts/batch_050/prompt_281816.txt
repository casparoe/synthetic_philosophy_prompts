I'm a PhD student in political theory (my dissertation is on republicanism and security policy, roughly) and I have to circulate a short "position statement" before our workshop on military manpower policy next month. Before I commit to it in front of people who actually know the defense-studies literature, I want a stress test. Here are the commitments as I've written them down:

(a) Conscription is a serious rights infringement -- it's forced labor by the state, full stop -- so it's only ever justifiable as a last resort under genuine existential threat, and the burden of proof sits firmly on the state. (b) Switzerland is basically the model society on this stuff: its militia system shows that mass citizen armament is compatible with, maybe even partly constitutive of, a healthy liberal-republican political culture, and more countries should move in that direction rather than relying on small professional forces. (c) Neutrality is only respectable if it's armed; a neutral state without credible self-defense is just free-riding or hoping. (d) Finland did the right thing joining NATO, and Sweden too; given their geography, armed neutrality stopped being a live option for them. (e) If a state does conscript, fairness requires that service fall on the whole cohort, men and women equally -- selective conscription that lets the children of the well-connected buy their way out is worse than universal service even if it's "cheaper." (f) The justification for any conscription scheme is ultimately instrumental: it stands or falls on whether it actually produces the deterrence the state needs, and claims about effectiveness have to be empirically assessed rather than asserted.

My sense is that this is all pretty standard stuff held by lots of people in the republican/civic-republican corner of the literature, but my advisor hinted something in there doesn't sit right and wouldn't say what, which is typical of her. So: go through the set and tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are. For the real ones, rank them by severity and say for each what would have to give and how much it would cost me. Also flag any case where a principle I've stated can't actually deliver a verdict I've attached to it. If you think some pair looks contradictory but dissolves once you distinguish, say, the different threat environments or different things "conscription" can mean, show the dissolution rather than just flagging it. I'd rather fix this now than have it taken apart in the workshop.
