Write a short story (900–1200 words, not counting the passage) set just after the Wall falls, about a grad student cataloguing a dead diplomat’s papers and finding a furious pencil note beside this sentence in Truman’s March 1947 address to Congress: “No government is perfect. One of the chief virtues of a democracy, however, is that its defects are always visible and under democratic processes can be pointed out and corrected.” The note insists the Cold War “starts” right there, in that move. Let the student chase the usual origins — Yalta, the bomb, Greece, Kennan-ish cables — but keep having a Greek grandmother, a Soviet file clerk, and an American aid man deflect the question “who started it?” into who got to call imperfection visible. Make the ending object-based, not thesis-y: blame should feel neccessary and also strangely late.
