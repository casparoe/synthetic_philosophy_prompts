I run the moderation team for a big MMO with tons of NPCs, and we get a steady stream of reports (maybe 30-40 a day) about what players do to those NPCs -- torture roleplay, torturing and killing quest-givers for laughs, keeping named characters locked in pens on player property, etc. Right now mods just go by gut feeling and the rulings are all over the place, which players have noticed. My mods are contractors with no philosophy background and a couple minutes per ticket, and anything above a 7-day ban needs a community lead to sign off.

I want a written triage procedure a mod can actually run through per report, that turns the main serious positions on whether video-game characters can be wronged -- do the characters themselves matter at all, or only what the act expresses about or does to the player, or whether it harms other real people who see it -- into an ordered set of questions with a verdict at each step. Say explicitly which distinctions have to survive being simplified into a checklist and which we can drop. It has to handle the case everyone trips on, the so-called gamer's dilemma, where we permit virtual murder without blinking but flinch at virtual acts that seem intuitively worse, and either resolve it or mark it as unresolvable by the procedure. Give two or three worked examples, including one where the procedure forces a ruling I'm going to find too permissive or too harsh, and be upfront about which kinds of reports it can't settle and have to go to a human.

One more thing: where the underlying philosophy is genuinely contested rather than settled, flag it as such. I dont want the procedure quietly baking in one contested theory as if it were the consensus.
