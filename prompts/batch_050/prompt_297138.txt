I'm putting together a short statement of my views for a workshop on rational choice and want to stress-test the package before I circulate it. My commitments are roughly as follows. (a) A rational agent must have transitive preferences: anyone with a preference cycle A over B over C over A can be run through a money pump, and that settles the matter. (b) The axioms of expected utility theory are best defended pragmatically, via money-pump and Dutch-book style arguments, rather than by appeal to self-evident principles of consistency. (c) Rationality is fundamentally synchronic: what can be criticized in an agent is the state of her preferences and beliefs at a time, and there is no further norm governing how those states evolve across time. (d) Changes of preference that are driven by nothing more than a shift in taste -- no new evidence, no miscalculation -- are entirely permissible; Tuesday-me simply prefers different things than Monday-me, and no one has erred. (e) A sophisticated agent who knows her own future preferences need never be money-pumped, because at each step she can reason about what she will do at later steps and decline trades accordingly; so foreseeably cyclic agents are not exploitable in practice, only myopic ones.

Please audit this set for me. Which pairs or triples of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which merely look like they are and dissolve on inspection, and how severe is each real tension? I'm particularly unsure whether my official methodology in (b) is even available to someone who holds (c) and (d), since the arguments I lean on seem to work by stringing together judgments across time, and whether my verdict in (e) quietly presupposes the kind of cross-temporal constraint that (c) denies. But I don't want to lead the witness: check every pairing, not just those. For each tension you find, say which commitment would most cheaply be revised or reinterpreted to restore coherence and what that revision costs, and end by identifying the single change that would clean up the most. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and write the answer in American English.
