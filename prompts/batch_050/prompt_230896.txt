AI labs have settled on a particular vocabulary and a particular set of practices around LLM falsehoods: wrong outputs are called "hallucinations," chat interfaces carry little disclaimers along the lines of "this model can make mistakes, check important information," and nobody in the industry ever describes a false output as the model lying. Harry Frankfurt famously distinguished the liar, who cares about the truth and tracks it precisely in order to conceal it, from the bullshitter, who is simply indifferent to whether what he says is true. What does this settled industry practice presuppose, philosophically, about the conditions for lying, bullshitting, and merely erring? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does calling it a hallucination (a perceptual metaphor, suggesting an involuntary malfunction of an inner eye rather than a speech act) presuppose that LLMs lack the intentions required for lying, and even the indifference to truth required for bullshitting, so that their falsehoods are mere errors like a miscalibrated thermometer? Which rival view of what LLMs are doing when they generate text does the vocabulary quietly rule out, and is the choice of metaphor philosophically innocent or rather convenient for the labs? Finally, if we concluded that LLMs actually do bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, what would have to change in the practice -- the disclaimers, the interface design, the assignment of responsibility when someone is harmed by a false output?
