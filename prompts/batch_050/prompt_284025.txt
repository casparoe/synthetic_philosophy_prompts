I'm writing up my settled view on modality de re for a grad seminar paper and I'd like an audit before I commit to anything in print. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm a Lewis-style modal realist: worlds are concrete, individuals are world-bound, and "Humphrey could have won" is true iff some counterpart of Humphrey wins at another world. Strictly speaking, no one exists at more than one world.

2. I think Kripke's Humphrey objection lands a genuine hit on the pure qualitative-similarity version of counterpart theory: what befalls some mere duplicate of me in another concrete world seems no more relevant to what *I* could have done than what befalls my identical twin in the next room. So the counterpart relation can't just be overall qualitative resemblance -- it has to track something about which possibilities are really mine.

3. I'm an origin essentialist: I couldn't have come from entirely different gametes, full stop, and an adequate theory of de re modality has to deliver that verdict rather than treating it as a facon de parler.

4. The counterpart relation is context-sensitive and interest-relative: which possibilities count as possibilities *for me* depends on which respects of similarity the conversation makes salient. That's my answer to silliness like "I could have been a poached egg" -- in most contexts, I couldn't.

5. On persistence through time I'm a perdurantist in Lewis's mold: continuants are worms of temporal stages held together by counterpart-like genidentity relations.

Is this package coherent? Tell me which pairs are in real tension, which only look like they are but dissolve on inspection, and rank the genuine ones by severity. For the worst one, be specific about which commitment has to go and what it costs me -- don't just gesture at "some revision needed."
