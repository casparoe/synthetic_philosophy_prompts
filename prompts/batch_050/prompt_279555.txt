I sit on the animal care and use committee of a marine research institute. Our charter requires anesthesia or analgesia for any procedure on animals whose probability of sentience we judge to be above 40 percent, a threshold the previous committee adopted as a way of translating scientific uncertainty into policy. A research group has now applied to study stress responses in shore crabs by exposing unanesthetized animals to aversive stimuli, arguing that sedation would invalidate the measurements. The sentience literature on decapod crustaceans is genuinely contested: there is evidence of motivational trade-offs, wound-directed behavior, and avoidance learning, but respectable researchers still dispute whether these show felt pain or merely sophisticated nociception. A defensible reading of the evidence could land the probability anywhere from 25 to 60 percent. Draft the committee's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions. The memo should address how a body should reason when the evidence straddles its own numerical threshold: should we treat the threshold as a genuine expected-welfare cutoff, apply a precautionary presumption in the animal's favor, or treat the threshold itself as misguided and decide on other grounds? Give an honest account of the strongest argument for the position you reject, and make sure the bottom line is something the committee could actually vote on.
