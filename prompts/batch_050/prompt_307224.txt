(1) I'm a prioritarian: benefiting a person matters more, morally, the worse off that person is, and that's the whole story about why distribution matters. (2) Part of why I left utilitarianism is the separateness-of-persons objection -- utilitarianism treats distinct people as vessels for one big pool of welfare, and that's a real flaw, not just an intuition pump. Inequality isn't bad in itself, so leveling down the better off helps nobody and costs nothing morally; that the leveling-down objection doesn't touch priority views is exactly why I prefer priority over equality. (4) I accept some near-absolute side constraints: you may not kill one healthy person to redistribute their organs to five patients, even in a version of the case where the five patients are all worse off than the donor. (5) Numbers count -- other things equal it's better to save the greater number, and one of prioritarianism's selling points is that it gets this right without flat-out summing. (6) I reject maximin/leximin, because it's absurd to sacrifice enormous gains to millions of people for a tiny gain to the single worst-off person.
