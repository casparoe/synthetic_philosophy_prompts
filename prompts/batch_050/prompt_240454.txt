I've been reading about the Daoist idea of wu-wei — effortless, spontaneous action — on my own, and I keep running into a puzzle I can't dissolve. The usual statement of the paradox is that trying to be effortless is self-defeating, but that can't be right in general: a pianist practices deliberately for years precisely so that playing becomes effortless, and nobody thinks her earlier effort contaminates the later spontaneity. So the problem can't be about trying as such. My best guess is that the self-defeat lies in a specific kind of self-monitoring — attending to your own spontaneity while acting — rather than in the intention to become spontaneous, but I don't know how to state this precisely. Please explain what exactly makes some cases of "trying not to try" self-defeating and others not, and whether the indirect strategies the Daoist texts seem to recommend (ritual practice, fasting the mind, deliberately forgetting what one has learned) genuinely escape the paradox or merely relocate it one level up. I'd also like to understand whether the phenomenon athletes call "choking under pressure" — where renewed conscious control destroys a well-trained performance — is the same problem in reverse, or a genuinely different one.
