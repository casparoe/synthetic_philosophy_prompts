I'm prepping a grad seminar on classification in the sciences, and before I let my students anywhere near the usual roster of views (essentialism, Boyd-style cluster accounts, promiscuous realism, eliminativism about kinds) I want to hand them a spec sheet: what does an account of natural kinds actually have to deliver to count as adequate, for whatever it is we want kinds for (induction, explanation, grounding the authority of the special sciences). Every year someone writes a paper arguing that species are or aren't natural kinds, and it always turns out they're working with a different implicit standard than the person sitting next to them, so I'd like the standard itself on the table first.

So: write the desiderata. What must a theory of natural kinds get right -- the cases it has to handle (water being H2O, gold, jade turning out to be two minerals, biological species after Darwin, whether psychiatric categories or 'planet' count after the Pluto business), the distinctions it can't collapse (real vs conventional grouping vs. degree of naturalness, kind membership vs. kind-hood itself, being projectible vs. being metaphysically privileged), the commitments it has to respect from actual scientific practice -- and for each desideratum give me the case or consideration that motivates it, so it's a spec and not just a wishlist. I'm especially interested in two things: which of the desiderata people usually demand are genuine requirements and which are really just one camp's prejudices dressed up (e.g. must a kind have an essence? must kinds not overlap or cross-cut each other?), and whether some subset of the plausible desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable -- if the list itself turns out to be a result, thats the part I want. Then flag which single requirement does the most work in knocking out contenders, and score the main existing views against the list in a paragraph or two at the end, but keep that short -- the criteria and their motivating cases are the deliverable. Don't give me a reading list, I already have one.
