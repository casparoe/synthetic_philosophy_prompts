Okay so I'm a junior and my school has this little philosophy club that meets at lunch, and last week we somehow got onto karma — like whether the universe actually keeps score of what you do and pays it back. I tried to defend karma as a theory of moral desert, basically that whatever happens to you is in some sense earned by your own past actions (this life or previous ones, whatever), and that's what makes it more than just physics — it's supposed to be justice, cosmic justice, not random luck. My friend Maya then walked me through this argument that seems to prove something completely insane, and I cant figure out which step breaks. Her argument: (1) Whatever a person experiences, good or bad, is the karmic result of their own earlier actions — that's just what the doctrine says. (2) If karma is a theory of justice and not just a blind mechanism like gravity, then karmic outcomes are deserved — that's the whole point of calling it justice. (3) If someone deserves an outcome, then it's unjust for a third party to prevent that outcome or undo it, the same way it'd be unjust to break a prisoner out of jail who's serving a fair sentence. (4) Relieving someone's suffering is preventing or undoing an outcome they're experiencing. (5) So relieving suffering is unjust whenever the suffering is karmic — which, by (1), is literally always. (6) So charity, medicine, pulling a drowning kid out of a pond — all of it is unjust, or at least it frustrates the universe's justice system. Her conclusion was that karma was never a theory of justice at all, it's just victim blaming with extra steps.

I obviously refuse to accept (6), and honestly I'd rather throw out karma completely than accept that helping people is wrong. But I also don't love giving up the idea that karma at least roughly tracks desert, because if it doesn't then it stops being an answer to "why do bad things happen to people" and becomes just vibes. One kid in the club said the rescuer is part of the karmic plan too, like the drowning kid's good karma gets delivered through me pulling him out — Maya called that a cop-out but couldn't say exactly why. So: which premise or step do I reject, why did it look so plausible in the moment, and what does each exit actually cost me? Like if I give up (3), am I saying desert doesn't generate any duties for bystanders, and is that a bullet people just normally bite? I'd rather know what I'm paying than keep going in circles at lunch.
