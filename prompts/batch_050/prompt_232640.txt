I help run a small grant program (think ~$2M a year) that funds work on welfare in aquaculture, and we're redoing our priority list. The field feels like it has a bunch of open questions that all get talked about as if they're equally urgent, and I suspect they really aren't. Here's my rough sense of the landscape, tell me where I'm wrong:

1. Whether decapod crustaceans (shrimp, prawns, crabs) are sentient and what would settle it -- relevant because shrimp farming involves staggering numbers of animals and practices like eyestalk ablation.
2. Developing validated welfare indicators for fish on farms -- right now people use things like cortisol, fin damage, swimming behavior, but there's no agreed "this fish is doing badly" measure the way there sort of is for, say, dairy cows.
3. Humane slaughter -- percussive/electrical stunning vs. ice slurry vs. asphyxiation in air, and whether the nicer methods actually work at commercial scale for small fish where you can't stun individuals.
4. Stocking density, water quality, enrichment -- i.e., quality of life during the grow-out phase rather than the last hour of it.
5. Cleaner fish (the wrasse and lumpfish used to eat sea lice off salmon) -- a whole secondary industry where the "workers" often have awful welfare and basically nobody's looking at it.

What I want from you: if you had to tell us which two of these should get most of the money over the next five years, which would you pick and why? Defend the ordering in terms of how much suffering is plausibly at stake, how tractable progress is, and how neglected each area is relative to its size. Also tell me which one you'd defund even though it has the most vocal advocates, and what result or development would make you reorder the whole list. One thing I'm particularly unsure about: does it even make sense to prioritize the sentience question for shrimp when the answer might not change practice much if cheap welfare improvements are available anyway? Or does that cut the other way? Genuinely torn on that one.
