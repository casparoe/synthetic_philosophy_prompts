I'm taking a seminar this term on existential risk and long-term global governance, and we've just spent two weeks on the singleton hypothesis -- roughly, the idea that Earth might end up with a single decision-making agency at the top, whether that's a world government, an AI system, a corporation, whatever. What struck me is how much of the seminar discussion just assumed the hypothesis matters enormously without ever pinning down which parts of it are actually load-bearing. Like, is the important question whether a singleton is *likely* (some people seem to think AI makes it near-inevitable, others think coordination problems make it basically impossible), or whether it's *stable* once you get one, or whether it's *desirable* -- since presumably a good singleton is great for handling global catastrophic risks but a bad or locked-in one is about the worst outcome imaginable, and those feel like pretty different research projects?

So here's my ask: I'm writing my final paper as a kind of agenda-setting piece, and I want to argue for which open questions about the singleton hypothesis are most worth serious attention right now, from the perspective of someone deciding where to spend effort -- say a grad student or a small research org with maybe five years to spend. Give me your ordering of the top three or four questions and actually defend the ranking: what criteria are you using (how much hangs on the answer, how tractable it is, how neglected, how many other questions depend on it), and where do those criteria pull against each other? I'm also suspicious that some questions people treat as central are actually over-rated -- e.g. endless debate about whether a singleton "should" exist feels less useful than figuring out what early institutional choices would push toward good vs. bad versions if one starts forming. Tell me if you think I'm wrong about that.

And please be honest about the soft spots in your own ranking: which consideration, if it turned out differently than you're assuming, would most change the order -- like if it turned out singletons are basically unstable regardless of design, does the whole agenda collapse, or just reshuffle? I'd rather get a defended agenda with visible weak points than a confident-sounding survey.
