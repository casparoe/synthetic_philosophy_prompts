I help moderate a big hobby community online (about 40k members, mix of Discord and an old-school forum) and lately I've been the one who gets pinged whenever something weird happens -- a spambot wave, the automod going rogue, a user melting down, whatever. I never studied philosophy but a few months ago I fell down a youtube rabbit hole about this guy Daniel Dennett and his idea that there are three different "stances" you can take toward a thing to predict what it'll do: the physical stance (figure out the mechanism), the design stance (assume it works the way it's supposed to and reason from its function), and the intentional stance (treat it like it has beliefs and goals and assume it'll act rationally given them). Plus his thing about "real patterns," where a pattern is real if betting on it actually compresses/predicts better than not. This maps almost embarrassingly well onto my job, because picking the stance basically IS picking the response: patch it, fix the incentives, or reason with it.

So here's what I want: a decision procedure I can actually run in under a minute when something in the community misbehaves, that tells me which stance to take. Questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to move to the next question, and what the procedure says to do when I've picked a stance and my predictions keep coming out wrong (which I take it is Dennett's actual test for whether a stance is "working"). It should cover the annoying middle cases, like: things that act designed but nobody designed them (I guess evolution is the usual example, but for me it's more like emergent trolling norms in a subcommunity), and things where the intentional stance is seductive but a trap -- we have an LLM chatbot in the server and I keep catching myself thinking it's mad at me. I also want the procedure to be honest about where it hands the decision back to me because it's judgment, not steps.

Then run the finished procedure on three real-ish cases from my queue: (1) a spambot network that adapts to our filters within a day or two, which is the one that tempts me toward the intentional stance; (2) our automod, which keeps temp-banning innocent longtime users and whose config nobody fully understands anymore; and (3) a user who keeps flaming out, apologizing sincerely, and doing it again three weeks later. I'd like at least one of these to come out with an answer I won't like -- my suspicion is it's the chatbot or the relapsing user, but surprise me. Also a short bit at the end on what this whole framework can't tell me (like, it won't tell me whether banning is fair, just whether it'll work?). I'm not looking for an essay about Dennett, I want the tool, built out of the idea.
