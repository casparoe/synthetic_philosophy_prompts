I've been reading about the end of the Roman Empire on my own, and I've noticed something odd about the historians' argument over whether Rome "fell" or merely "transformed." One camp describes catastrophe: cities shrinking, trade collapsing, literacy and material comfort vanishing for centuries. The other describes continuity: Roman law, Christian institutions, Latin, and local elites carrying on under new management, with the fifth century as a transition rather than a death. What puzzles me is that both camps seem to know all the same facts. Nobody denies that the aqueducts stopped being repaired, and nobody denies that Roman legal culture survived. And yet they've been arguing past each other for decades.

Here's my hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than accept: the dispute is really about what the word "Rome" refers to, and it has the structure of a personal identity puzzle. If you replace a ship plank by plank, at what point is it a different ship? The transformationists seem to treat Rome as its institutions and culture -- so as long as those persist in some form, Rome persists. The catastrophists seem to treat Rome as a material system -- roads, taxes, armies, cities, living standards -- and by that measure it plainly died. If that's right, then neither side is wrong; they're tracking different bearers of the same name, and the debate can't be settled by more archaeology.

Is that the correct diagnosis, or is there a genuine empirical disagreement underneath -- about, say, how bad the fifth and sixth centuries actually were for ordinary people -- that new evidence could settle? I'd also like to know whether the two camps have an unspoken disagreement about what matters: is "did Rome fall?" partly a question about whose experience counts, the literate elite or the peasant whose pottery got worse? Finally, given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like here? A new kind of evidence, an agreement to split the question into several smaller ones, or should we expect the argument to run forever because it's really a disguised dispute about values? Please give me a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of both sides.
