I'm taking a final-year course in moral psychology and we've just finished the unit on the situationist critique of virtue ethics. For my position paper I've sketched where I've landed, and I'd like to know whether the package hangs together before I commit to it. My commitments are as follows:

(a) Virtue ethics is the most plausible of the major normative frameworks, and moral education should primarily aim at cultivating character rather than teaching rules or decision procedures.

(b) The classic situationist experiments — Milgram's obedience studies, the Good Samaritan study, dime-in-the-phone-booth style mood experiments — are methodologically sound, and they genuinely show that minor situational variables predict behaviour better than trait measures do.

(c) Nevertheless, character traits are real and explanatory; the situationist results only show that most people lack true virtue, since genuine virtue is and has always been rare.

(d) A disposition only counts as a virtue if it manifests reliably across a wide range of situations — cross-situational consistency is partly constitutive of what a virtue is.

(e) Character can be deliberately cultivated through habituation and emulation of role models, and schools and workplaces ought to be designed with this aim in view.

(f) Practical wisdom cannot be codified; no set of rules or guidelines could capture what the virtuous person perceives and does.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each real tension is. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would restore the most consistency overall. Please keep your entire response under 600 words.
