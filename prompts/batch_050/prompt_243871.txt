I've been reading LessWrong and its ancestors since the Overcoming Bias days, and our local meetup is doing a session on the centipede game and backward induction. I want to hand out a decision tree that sorts attendees into positions on the backward induction paradox -- not by which name they recognize, but by what they actually believe. Please build it from the logical space outward: first the diagnostic questions, then the named positions as leaves, with any coherent-looking but unoccupied combinations of answers flagged and briefly diagnosed.

Some constraints. First, the questions should be answerable without knowing any labels, framed as concrete claims about what to infer or do at particular nodes. Second, the tree must be able to distinguish at least the following: the orthodox view that common knowledge of rationality compels taking at the first node; the view that backward induction is self-undermining because any observed deviation destroys the epistemic conditions the reasoning presupposed; the reputation/types view that a small chance of a cooperative opponent makes passing straightforwardly rational; the forward-induction view that a deviation is informative rather than anomalous; the empirical/level-k deflation that the paradox only shows theory mispredicts play; and the FDT-adjacent view, familiar to the meetup, that passing can be rational because your policy is logically correlated with your opponent's model of you. If two of these can't be separated by any question you're willing to include, say so explicitly rather than forcing a split. Third, since this crowd's real interest is Newcomb-like problems, include at least one question whose answers predict where someone lands on one-boxing, and note which leaves pattern-match to which side.

Aim for seven to ten questions and a comparable number of leaves. Each leaf should state the position, its recommended first move in a long centipede, and the cost it knowingly accepts.
