I am a novelist working on a literary short story, and I'd like you to help me develop it. Please take on the role of both a professional philosopher of music and a seasoned fiction editor.

Here is the situation I want to dramatize. In the story, a musicologist discovers, in an isolated mountain community, an instrument and a tuning system that share nothing with any known musical tradition -- no scales, intervals, or rhythms that resemble Western or any documented folk music. The standard explanations for why music sounds sad (that slow, low, descending sounds resemble the voices and bodies of grieving people, or that we've learned cultural associations between certain keys and certain feelings) therefore shouldn't apply. And yet, when the instrument is played, listeners from the outside world consistently weep -- while the mountain community itself hears the very same pieces as festive.

Before you write, think carefully through the philosophical puzzle and jot down a short brainstorm: what would each major position on musical emotion (music as resemblance of expressive behavior, music as learned convention, emotion as somehow *in* the formal structure itself) predict about this scenario, and which position does the scenario put under the most pressure? Then write the short story (roughly 800-1200 words) in a polished literary voice. The story must stage the philosophical tension through scene and character rather than stating it -- for instance, through a confrontation between the musicologist and a village musician, or through a listener whose reaction they cannot explain. End the story in a way that leaves one account of musical emotion looking newly plausible and another looking impoverished, without any character lecturing about which is which.
