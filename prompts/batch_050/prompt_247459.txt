So I keep coming back to this "deliberation crowds out prediction" idea — while you're genuinely deciding between options, you can't just treat your own upcoming action as one more event to assign a probability to, the way you could predict what your roomate will order. Fine, that seems right. But then it looks like deliberation itself gives us a quick disproof of determinism, at least for deliberate choices: if I can't assign any probability to what I'll do while I'm still deciding, then nothing available to me fixes what I'll do, so the choice must be genuinely open and undetermined right up until I make it. That feels way too easy as an argument for libertarian free will, but I can't locate the exact hole. Does it go through? If not, where precisely does it slide from a claim about my perspective while deliberating to a claim about how the world is? And would it change anything to say that I *can* assign probabilities to my own future actions, just not during the deliberation itself — or is that already giving up the whole point?
