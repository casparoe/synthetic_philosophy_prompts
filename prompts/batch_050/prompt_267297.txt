Two people in my ML reading group got into an argument over an interpretability result and I genuinely can't tell who's right, so I want a verdict. Maya trained a linear probe on the residual stream of a mid-size LLM and found a single direction that predicts, with ~90% accuracy on held-out prompts, whether the statement in the prompt is true or false. Her claim: the model internally represents truth. Diego's claim: all she's shown is that truth-correlated info is linearly decodable, and you can decode basically anything that's present that way -- sentence length, part of speech, whatever -- so decodability can't be what makes something a representation.

Maya's comeback: they then added the vector to the activations and it flipped the model's behavior -- it started confidently asserting false things. So the direction isn't just readable, it does causal work, and causally-active decodable structure is exactly what people mean by a representation. Diego: a knob that changes behavior shows the direction matters, not that it's a *truth* direction. Maybe she found the "sounds like a common claim" feature, or an "assert confidently" feature. For it to be truth it would have to track truth itself, and a language model never touches the world, so at best it represents what the training data treats as true, which is a different thing.

Maya: by that standard we couldn't say V1 neurons represent edges either -- they're identified the same way, decodability plus intervention. And the probe generalized to statements about topics that barely show up in training data. Diego: in neuroscience you have an independent grip on what an edge is and can check the neuron against it. Here the only test is more model behavior, so all you ever establish is "what the model treats as true-ish", and the generalization is probably carried by simpler features that correlate with truth.

Can you go through the exchange and say who's right at each step, who's more right overall, and what -- if anything -- would actually settle it? Also curious what you think both of them might be missing.
