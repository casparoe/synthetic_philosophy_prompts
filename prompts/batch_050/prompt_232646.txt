I recently sat in on a graduate seminar on climate economics, and I was struck by how much of the disagreement between the optimists and the alarmists seemed to come down to one parameter: the pure rate of time preference, the rate at which we discount future wellbeing simply because it is future, as opposed to discounting because future people will be richer or because there is some chance humanity will not be around to enjoy the benefits. The claim I want your credence on is this: a strictly positive pure rate of time preference is ethically indefensible for impartial policymaking. That is, there is no sound justification for valuing one person's wellbeing less than an identical person's wellbeing merely on account of the time at which they live, holding wealth, risk, and uncertainty about existence fixed. My own credence in that claim is about 0.85; a colleague who defends positive pure time preference puts it at about 0.15, and I genuinely cannot tell where our disagreement lives.

Give me your number, but more importantly decompose it. Assign rough probabilities to the sub-questions you think the claim rests on: for instance, whether the location of wellbeing in time is morally arbitrary in the way location in space is; whether individual prudential discounting of one's own future self has any bearing on what a social planner should do; whether pure time preference is even conceptually separable from discounting due to growth and uncertainty, or whether it collapses into them on inspection; whether the fact that essentially every human being alive has a time preference counts as evidence that the preference tracks something real or just as evidence of a widespread bias; and whether the practical disaster of a zero rate -- implying we should sacrifice enormous present consumption for marginally better-off distant generations -- is a genuine reductio or an artifact of combining the zero rate with other contestable assumptions about population and growth. Say how these sub-questions combine into your final credence, flag the one to which your number is most sensitive, and tell me which single argument or consideration would move your estimate the most in either direction.
