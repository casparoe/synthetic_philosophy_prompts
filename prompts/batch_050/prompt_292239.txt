Alan Turing's late work on learning machines proposed educating a machine the way one educates a child, through reward and punishment, rather than programming it outright. He also distinguished two dangers in such an education: too little discipline leaves the machine without useful habits, while too much discipline crushes what he called initiative and produces a slavish imitator. Suppose Turing could examine today's large language models, which are first trained by gradient descent to predict human text and then further trained from human feedback into helpful, obedient assistants. Drawing only on views he actually expressed, how do you think he would assess this? In particular: would he regard training on text alone as an adequate substitute for the rich sensory experience he thought a learning machine would need, and does his worry about purely linguistic, unemotional channels of communication still track anything real? Would he see feedback-based fine-tuning as a sound realization of his reward-and-punishment proposal, or as the very over-disciplining he warned against? And what curriculum change or experiment do you think he would propose to test whether these models have any initiative left?
