I'm a software engineer at a mid-sized company, where I lead a small team and have ended up as the de facto owner of our estimation and planning process. I have no formal philosophy background, but over the past year I've been reading around the topic of disagreement and rational belief-updating (it started with a podcast episode about Aumann's agreement theorem), and I've realized I hold a bundle of views that I've never actually checked against each other. I'd like a consistency audit before I keep building team practices on top of them.

Here is roughly where I've landed: (1) I accept the Aumann-style picture: honest, rational agents who fully exchange their views and take each other seriously shouldn't persistently disagree about factual matters; if two such people still disagree after laying everything on the table, at least one of them has failed to update properly on the other's evidence. (2) In practice I try to act on this: when a colleague I respect disagrees with me about something we've both looked at -- a project estimate, a design tradeoff -- I move substantially toward their position, roughly splitting the difference, even when I cannot find the flaw in my own reasoning. (3) The exception I carve out is my home turf: on the subsystem I've owned for years, I defer much less, and my main justification is that I can inspect my own reasoning step by step whereas theirs arrives as a bare conclusion; my judgments come with a legible audit trail, theirs don't. (4) When a disagreement bottoms out in different fundamental starting assumptions, I think agreeing to disagree is legitimate -- there is no fact of the matter about which prior is correct, so neither side is being irrational. (5) I run small internal prediction markets at work and believe the market price is usually a better estimate than any individual's opinion -- yet I regularly bet against the price when the question concerns my own project, and I think I'm right to do so.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and in the genuine cases, which commitment would have to give and at what cost? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and be specific about what follows from what -- for instance, whether a justification I offer for one commitment undermines a different one. I'd rather be told one of my five has to go than be reassured that the package is fine as is.
