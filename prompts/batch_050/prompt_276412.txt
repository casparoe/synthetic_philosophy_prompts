2. The Copy Principle is a serious constraint Hume genuinely endorses: every legitimate idea traces back to an impression, and a term with no impression behind it is literally meaningless, not just unclear.
3. Hume's two definitions of "cause" — the one framed in terms of constant conjunction among resembling objects, and the one framed in terms of the mind's inferential transition from one object to its usual attendant — define one and the same relation.
4. Hume is no skeptic about everyday causal belief: custom-based inference is natural, unavoidable, and reliable enough for life and science.
5. His account of causal inference is descriptive psychology — he explains how we actually come to form causal beliefs and isn't in the business of justifying that we do.
