My philosophy reading group has been stuck for two weeks on free-floating rationales -- Dennett's idea that there can be a genuine reason for a bit of behavior even if no mind, anywhere, has ever represented that reason. Natural selection is the paradigm case: there's a reason the cuckoo chick heaves the other eggs out of the nest, but the chick doesn't know it and no designer ever thought it; evolution itself "found" the reason. The fight in our group is about how far this category extends past biology.

My friend Mara has firm verdicts and I've been collecting them. Cuckoo chick: yes, genuine reason. Thermostat holding a room at 21 degrees: no -- the only reason in the vicinity is the engineer's, and the thermostat just borrows it. A river taking the steepest path downhill: definitly no, not even a candidate. A chess engine sacrificing its queen on the way to a mating net: yes, there's a real reason for the sacrifice even though no programmer foresaw it. A slime mold oozing toward the oat flake: yes. And a company that drifted out of a market segment over three years without anyone ever deciding to -- budgets just kept getting trimmed until nobody was left in that division: yes, she says there was a reason the firm "pulled out," floating free of any employee's reasons.

Two new cases came up last night and I want to predict her verdicts before she weighs in. First: a border collie's herding instinct, produced by deliberate breeding -- the breeders very much had a reason in mind and selected for it on purpose, unlike the cuckoo. Second: a language model that's learned to be sycophantic, agreeing with whatever view the user states, because responses like that scored better during training, even though none of the engineers built that in or wanted it. Predict her verdict on each, tell me which rule best makes sense of her whole track record (selection history? never-been-represented? flexible competence? something else?), note which other rules fit her six verdicts just as well, and name the further case that would best tell the surviving rules apart.
