The dispute between Humeans and anti-Humeans about laws of nature has been running for roughly half a century with little sign of convergence. Humeans hold that the laws are nothing over and above the axioms of the best systematisation of the distribution of particular, local matters of fact, where the 'best' system trades off simplicity against strength; anti-Humeans insist that laws must genuinely govern, necessitate, or produce what happens, on pain of leaving the world a sheer cosmic conjunction with no explanatory depth. Strikingly, both sides appear able to accommodate the same scientific practice and the same evidence, and each accuses the other of resting on an intuition it cannot argue for: the Humean of a squeamishness about metaphysical 'glue' between events, the anti-Humean of an empiricist prejudice dressed up as ontological austerity. My own suspicion is that the two camps are really disagreeing about what counts as a satisfactory explanation, and only derivatively about what the world contains, but I am far from confident in this.
