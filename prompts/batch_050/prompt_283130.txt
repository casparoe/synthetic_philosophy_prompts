I have been asked to circulate a statement of my views in advance of a workshop on the epistemology of disagreement, and before I do I would like to know whether the package is actually coherent. My commitments are as follows:

(1) The Equal Weight View: when I discover that someone I antecedently judged to be my epistemic peer on some question disagrees with me about it, I ought to split the difference in credence, or something very close to it.

(2) Independence: in re-evaluating the disputed question after discovering the disagreement, I must bracket the fact that we disagree and assess the first-order merits on their own; otherwise I would be counting my own reasoning twice.

(3) I remain fully confident in the Equal Weight View itself, even though a number of philosophers I regard as my epistemic peers in this very area reject it and endorse steadfast views instead. I see no obligation to lower my credence in conciliationism in response to their disagreement.

(4) Where I take someone to be my epistemic superior on a matter — climate modelers on climate projections, structural engineers on load-bearing capacity — I hold that near-total deference to their consensus is rationally required of me, even without any independent grip on the details.

(5) I permit myself to retain substantial confidence in my own contested positions in other areas of philosophy (my moral realism, my compatibilism) despite knowing that recognized peers deny them, on the grounds that conciliating across the board in philosophy would leave one unable to hold anything, which cannot be what rationality demands.

(6) I accept that judging whether someone is my peer is itself a fallible exercise of my own cognitive faculties — I have no way of certifying peerhood that floats free of my own assessment.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and where apparent tensions dissolve, explain why they dissolve. Rank any genuine tensions by severity, and for each one specify what would have to give — which commitment could be revised or refined, and at what cost to the rest of the package. I am not asking for a survey of the literature or for the names of positions and their proponents; I want an audit of this particular set, including the single revision that would restore the most coherence for the least collateral damage. Please do not assume I already know where the problem lies.
