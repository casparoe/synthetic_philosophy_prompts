I'm the secretary of a university constitutional-theory society that runs a semester-long game modeled on Peter Suber's Nomic: the society's "constitution" is a ruleset in which the primary moves are proposals to amend the rules themselves, and disputes about whether a duly passed amendment is valid go to a three-member Rules Committee. The amendment clause currently requires a two-thirds vote of members present, and I'm trying to predict how the Committee will rule on a controversial proposal now before it, based on its track record. Its five rulings to date: (1) a proposal raising the amendment threshold from simple majority to two-thirds, passed by simple majority, was ruled valid; (2) a proposal amending the amendment clause itself to lengthen the notice period for proposals was ruled valid, but only prospectively -- it could not govern the vote by which it was adopted; (3) a proposal declaring one substantive rule (annual election of the chair) permanently unamendable was ruled invalid, despite passing with the required supermajority; (4) a proposal simply deleting the amendment clause was ruled invalid; (5) a proposal whose text declared that the proposal itself "shall not be subject to amendment" was ruled invalid, again despite procedurally proper passage.

The new proposal, which passed 8-3 under the existing two-thirds requirement, does something none of the earlier ones quite did: it makes the amendment clause itself unamendable going forward, permanently freezing the two-thirds procedure, while leaving every other rule in the constitution amendable by that procedure. Counsel for the proposal argues it is a permissible exercise of the very power the clause confers; counsel against argues that it smuggles the invalidity of rulings (3) and (5) in through the procedural door, since a frozen amendment clause is immune in just the way those proposals were. I can see two coherent principles in the track record -- one on which the amending power must always survive intact over the entire ruleset including itself, and one on which the power may bind itself as to the manner and form of future amendments so long as no substantive rule is placed beyond its reach -- and I cannot tell which one the Committee has actually been applying.

Please give me your prediction of the Committee's verdict, leading with the predicted verdict itself in the first sentence and then giving the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Identify which prior ruling is the closest analog and why; state explicitly whether the track record genuinely underdetermines this case and, if so, exactly which principle the Committee would be committing itself to under each possible verdict; and describe a single additional hypothetical ruling the Committee could have issued in the past that would have revealed which of the two principles it follows, so that I know what to look for in its opinion.
