So my flatmate and I ended up in this massive argument last night about the Nuremberg and Tokyo trials, like 2am massive, and I'm still annoyed because I think I had the better of it but I couldn't land the kill. I was arguing the trials were basically victors' justice dressed up in legal costume. She was defending them, and annoyingly shes a law student so she kept pulling out actual arguments instead of vibes.

Here's what I want you to do: play her. Defend the trials. But play her properly, not as a cartoon:

- She thinks the trials were on balance legitimate, but she's not a cheerleader. She'll happily concede specific rotten bits (the Soviet judge sitting in judgment while Katyn got pinned on the Germans, nobody Allied answering for the bombing of cities, the conspiracy charge being a mess) without letting me turn any of that into a verdict on the whole thing.
- Her actual view, roughly: the retroactivity objection is weaker than I think because some of the charges had real legal roots before the acts (she'd point to the 1907 Hague Conventions for war crimes and the Kellogg-Briand Pact that Germany itself signed), and where the law genuinely was new, she thinks nullum crimen sine lege is a principle of justice, not an absolute rule, and that it can be outweighed when the defendants obviously knew they were doing wrong. She also thinks the "your side did bad stuff too" move confuses hypocrisy with injustice -- hypocrites can still be right about the other guy.
- Stay in first person as her, talk like a person not an essay, you can ask me questions back, and don't break character to meta-comment. If I make a good point, concede it the way a smart arguer does, don't collapse.

My opening move, which is basically where we left off last night: The London Charter was signed in August 1945, after everything the defendants were charged with. "Crimes against peace" and "crimes against humanity" were categories invented for the occasion, and only the losers ever sat in the dock. If a court can define the crime after the act and only ever try the defeated, what separates that from a show trial except that we like the outcome? Go.
