So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, and I've been going down a rabbit hole on Nāgārjuna and Madhyamaka after a friend in my book club insisted emptiness isn't just nihilism with extra steps. The thing that's tripping me up is the tetralemma thing, where for some claim about, say, the self or the Tathāgata after death, Nāgārjuna considers four options: it exists, it doesn't exist, it both exists and doesn't, it neither exists nor doesn't. And then he rejects ALL four.

As a programmer this looks immediately broken to me. In classical logic those four options are exhaustive -- the first two cover the law of excluded middle, and "both" and "neither" cover the remaining ways things could go. Rejecting all four looks like asserting a contradiction, or like the statement "this statement has no truth value" which is itself a statement with a truth value problem. But I keep having this itch that there's a software analogy that might actually make sense of it: NaN. In floating point, every comparison with NaN comes back false, even NaN == NaN. So "the self exists," "the self doesn't exist," etc. could all come back false not because of some weird fifth truth value, but because the term itself fails to refer to anything with the kind of existence the predicates assume -- like every lemma secretly presupposes svabhāva (intrinsic existence), and rejecting all four is really rejecting the presupposition, the way "have you stopped beating your wife" traps you no matter how you answer.

Please explain whether this presupposition-failure reading is actually the right way to understand what's going on, or whether I'm just pattern-matching Buddhist philosophy onto IEEE 754. In particular: how does rejecting all four lemmas avoid collapsing into just asserting the negation of each (which IS contradictory)? Is there a difference between negating a claim and refusing to commit to it, and does that difference do real work here? And does the NaN analogy break down anywhere important -- e.g. NaN is still a *value*, so am I smuggling intrinsic existence back in through the analogy itself? Also: whatever move makes the tetralemma rejection coherent, does it also protect the claim "all things are empty" from the same treatment, or does emptiness have to apply to itself in a way that gets weird?
