I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for two years I've followed an argument between two camps of animal-welfare-motivated eaters who keep talking past each other. Camp One, the welfare-per-calorie camp, holds that a consumer's duty is to minimize total expected animal suffering per calorie consumed, which they take to imply eliminating chicken and eggs first and regarding pasture-raised beef as among the least harmful animal products. Camp Two, the cultivation-harms camp, holds that crop production itself kills vast numbers of wild animals through harvesting, pesticides, and habitat conversion, so that once these deaths are counted, some extensive grazing systems may cause less total harm than plant-based staples, making the chicken-to-beef swap a distraction or worse. Write the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between them should produce: first, the claims both can sign; second, the surviving disagreements, each phrased in wording both camps would accept as a fair statement of their own view, flagging any dispute that turns out to be merely verbal; third, the cruxes — I suspect one is the moral weight owed to insects relative to vertebrates, another is whether an individual purchase actually causes marginal production, and a third is how to compare the quality of a grazing animal's life against deaths that are quick but numerous — and for each crux, a concrete measurement, study, or observation both camps agree would bear on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance. No citations needed; I want the document, not a bibliography.
