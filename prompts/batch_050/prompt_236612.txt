I've been drafting the "position" section of a workshop paper on AI safety, and before I circulate it to my reading group I want a stress test on whether my commitments actually cohere. For context: I've spent the last few months on the corrigibility and shutdown literature, and what follows is where I've landed — but I haven't yet tried to check the pieces against each other, only against the literature.

Here is the set. (1) The shutdown problem is a real and serious one: an advanced agent that would take steps to prevent its operators from shutting it down is unacceptably dangerous, full stop. (2) I think utility indifference — designing the agent to be indifferent between worlds where the shutdown button is pressed and worlds where it isn't — is the right approach in spirit, and I think its well-known failure mode (the agent acquiring incentives to manipulate operators into pressing the button, or to disable the button so as to preserve the option value) can be patched by adding suitable penalties for tampering with the shutdown mechanism or the operators. (3) I accept expected utility maximization as the correct theory of instrumental rationality for such agents; I don't think bounded or non-maximizing agents are the relevant design target. (4) I hold that corrigibility must be sharply distinguished from obedience: an agent can be fully corrigible while refusing instructions it judges harmful, and conflating the two is a confusion. (5) I think that if an agent had decisive evidence that a shutdown command was issued by an attacker spoofing the operators rather than by the operators themselves, it would be correct for it to disregard that command — requiring compliance with spoofed orders would be a security hole, not a safety feature.

What I'd like is an audit, not a survey of the literature: which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, ordered from most to least severe; for each, what exactly one of them commits me to that the other rules out, and what the cheapest revision would be; and which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection. I'd also like you to flag any case where I seem to be getting a verdict that my own stated principle can't actually deliver. One request about calibration: please indicate how confident you are in each diagnosed tension — in particular, whether it's a strict inconsistency or merely an awkward fit — and at the end, single out the single claim in your analysis you are least sure of and say why.
