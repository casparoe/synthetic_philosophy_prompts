So here's my situation. Eight months ago I got diagnosed with something serious -- treatment is going ok but the odds aren't great, and I've been looking into cryonics. It's expensive, like really expensive, and my wife and I have been going back and forth on whether it's worth it.

Here's where it gets weird. My brother-in-law is a physics PhD student, and at dinner last week he gave me this argument that's been stuck in my head. His view (which he says a lot of physicists take seriously) is that whenever anything with an uncertain outcome happens, reality basically splits, and every outcome happens in some branch of reality. Fine, whatever, sounds like sci-fi but he says it's a mainstream reading of quantum mechanics. His point was: if that's true, then no matter what happens to me, there is always some branch where things go my way -- the treatment works, the trial drug comes through, some fluke saves me. And since I can only ever *experience* branches where I'm still alive (dead branches have no experiences in them), my own stream of experience just... never ends. From the inside, I'll always find myself in the branch where I made it. His conclusion: cryonics is a waste of money, because my survival, at least from my own perspective, is already guaranteed no matter what I do.

I can't find the flaw in this, but it feels like a magic trick. And here's the thing that makes me sure something's wrong: if his argument works, doesn't it prove way too much? Like, it would seem to follow that I should go play russian roulette for money, since I'd never experience the losing branch -- from my perspective I'd always just walk away richer. That's obviously insane advice. But I can't tell whether the insanity is in the roulette conclusion specifically, or whether it traces back to the original argument about survival being guaranteed.

So: is his argument sound, and if not, where exactly does it break? And separately -- if the argument does break, does that mean my cryonics decision just goes back to being an ordinary gamble about probabilities and money, or does the branching stuff still change how I should think about it even if his conclusion doesn't follow?
