So last fall my mom got sick and long story short our whole family ended up in these endless arguments about her care -- whether to tell her the full prognosis, whether to push for the aggressive treatment, that kind of thing. And I started noticing something weird about how these arguments actually go, and I want to know if philosophers have a word for it.

Here's the pattern. Someone states a general rule, like "you never keep medical information from a patient." Then someone else produces a case where that rule gives an answer that feels awful ("so you'd tell grandma on her deathbed that her son isn't coming?"). Then the first person does one of two things: they either carve an exception into the rule ("ok, never keep information unless it would cause serious harm"), or they bite the bullet on the case ("yes, you tell her, just gently"). And then the process repeats with a new case. After enough rounds everyone ends up with a set of rules and case-judgments that all fit together nicely. Fine so far.

But here's what bothered me. My sister and I went through basically this process, starting from mostly the same raw reactions to cases, and we ended up in completely different tidy positions -- she kept revising her rules, I kept biting bullets on cases. And once you're settled, all the judgments you now hold count as support for the rules you now hold, so the whole thing feels like it just launders whatever you were inclined to believe at the start.

So: does this back-and-forth method of adjusting rules and case-judgments against each other have a name? Does the specific worry -- that two people can run it "correctly" and converge on different answers, so it can't be a method for getting at the truth -- have a name of its own, or is it just a generic objection? And where's the line between legitimately revising a gut reaction because it clashes with a principle, versus just refusing to update on a case that should have moved you? Because in the moment those two feel identical.
