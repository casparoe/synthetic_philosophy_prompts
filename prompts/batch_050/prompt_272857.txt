I cover science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, and my desk keeps running into the same problem in different costumes. Someone's press release says "world first"; two labs publish the same result within a month of each other and each claims priority; or a historian writes an essay arguing that if Einstein hadn't come along, someone else would have had relativity within five years, and therefore our hero-worshipping profile format is intellectually bankrupt. I was arguing about this with a colleague over the Darwin/Wallace story -- she says Wallace being basically forgotten proves credit is arbitrary, I think it proves something more interesting but I couldn't say what. The Newton/Leibniz mess is the other obvious case. There's clearly a real debate here about whether discoveries are inevitable products of their moment or genuine individual achievements, and I suspect the answer is "it depends on features of the discovery" rather than one answer for all of them.

So: turn whatever the philosophy and history of science actually has to say about simultaneous discovery and priority into a procedure my writers can run when one of these stories lands. The questions to ask in order -- things like how many people were converging on the result, how much of it was "in the air" versus genuinely missing, whether the counterfactual delay if this person hadn't done it is years or decades, whether the result is more like finding something that was there or constructing something that might not have been -- with what counts as a good answer to each and where the procedure just stops and hands it to a judgement call. Mark the steps that are really value choices dressed up as questions (e.g., how much we should care about credit at all versus the knowledge existing). Then run the procedure on three cases: Darwin/Wallace, a hypothetical where two biotech labs publish the same gene-editing result within a fortnight, and one case where the procedure delivers a verdict a features editor will hate because it kills the story. Say what the procedure can't settle.

British English please, and keep it casual -- this will get passed around the desk, not peer reviewed.
