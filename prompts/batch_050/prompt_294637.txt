I'm interviewing a researcher next week for a podcast series on AI alignment agendas, and she's one of the more optimistic defenders of iterated amplification and distillation -- she thinks you can get superhuman-but-aligned systems by repeatedly training models to imitate the output of a human overseer assisted by a team of earlier models, and she's skeptical of most worries that something gets lost along the way.

Draft around ten questions, in interview order. Here's where I want pressure applied:

- Early on I want a question that pins down what exactly the human overseer contributes at each amplification step, because her usual answer ("judgment") is doing a lot of work -- is it evaluative taste, world knowledge, the ability to spot when sub-answers are subtly wrong? Press on whether judgment decomposes the same way tasks do.
- A question on the distillation step specifically: every cycle compresses the amplified system's behavior back into one model, and compression is lossy. Her standard reply is that you only need to preserve behavior, not the reasoning behind it. Include a follow-up asking what happens when the reasoning is what carries the safety-relevant properties (e.g., an honest answer produced by honest deliberation vs. the same answer produced by a shortcut that happens to agree so far).
- A question about the bootstrapping assumption -- that decomposing hard questions into easier sub-questions terminates in questions a human can actually answer or check. Follow up on what the agenda does with sub-questions that are easy to answer but easy to answer *wrong with confidence*, since amplification amplifies those too.
- Somewhere in the middle, a question on adversarial decomposition: if a late-generation model is misaligned or just has its own quirks, it controls how questions get split up for the next round. Could a decomposer route around the overseer by choosing decompositions whose sub-answers look fine individually but compose badly? Her likely reply is that overseers check composition; include a follow-up asking whether that check itself requires the very superhuman competence the scheme is supposed to be creating.
- A question distinguishing "the final system does what an amplified overseer would approve" from "the final system is aligned," and whether she thinks those can come apart even if every training step goes perfectly.
- End with a question about what result or failure mode would actually make her abandon the agenda -- she tends to answer this vaguely, so write the question so it forces something concrete.

For each question, add the answer she'll most likely give and my follow-up to it. Keep the questions speakable -- this is audio, not a paper -- and no throat-clearing like "so tell us about your background."
