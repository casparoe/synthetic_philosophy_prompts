I'm preparing a statement of my commitments for a workshop on property theory, and I'd like an audit before I circulate it. My position is broadly Lockean and rests on five claims:

1. Original acquisition: a person comes to own an unowned resource by mixing their labour with it, provided the Lockean proviso is satisfied.

2. The proviso must be read strongly: appropriation is legitimate only if enough and as good is genuinely left for others. I reject the weaker Nozickian reading — that appropriation is permissible so long as no one ends up worse off than they would have been in a world without private property — because it makes the proviso satisfiable in nearly every actual case and hence toothless.

3. Justly acquired property rights are absolute and permanent: they include the rights to exclude, use, transfer, and bequeath, and they do not expire or attenuate as circumstances change. Taxation for redistribution beyond what is needed to protect the rights themselves is unjust.

4. Intellectual property is illegitimate. Ideas, texts, and designs are non-rival: my copying your manuscript or your drug formula leaves you everything you had. Labour-based arguments for ownership apply only to rival, scarce goods, so state-granted monopolies over expressions and inventions have no Lockean foundation.

5. Most existing land titles are historically clouded: they trace back to conquest, enclosure, or expropriation rather than to legitimate labour-mixing, and so rectification is owed, in some form, to the descendants of the dispossessed.

My questions are these. Which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, and which only appear to? Please rank the real tensions by severity. For each, state precisely what commits me to what — that is, which features of one claim, given its motivation, are incompatible with which features of another — and identify the cheapest revision: which claim has to give, and what it costs to give it up. Then name the single revision that would restore the most coherence for the least theoretical damage.

Two requests about the form of the answer. First, commit. I do not want a neutral menu of possible fixes with the choice handed back to me; I want your verdict on which tension is deepest, which revision is cheapest, and why, defended with reasons. If one of my five claims is doing work that the others cannot support, say so plainly. Second, argue from the claims themselves rather than from the positions of particular commentators beyond the one I have named; I am interested in whether the set hangs together, not in who has held it.
