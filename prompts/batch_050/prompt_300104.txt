So I fell down a rabbit hole a few weeks ago and now I can't stop thinking about this. I found a free copy of Newton's Principia online (the old English translation) and read the Scholium, the bit after the definitions where he talks about absolute space and time. Everyone knows the bucket part, but the thing that actually grabbed me comes later, where Newton basically admits that the parts of absolute space can't be seen or detected by our senses at all, which is why telling the true motion of a body apart from its apparent motion is, in his own framing, a genuinely difficult problem.

Wait, what? He builds this whole apparatus on absolute space, then turns around and says you can't observe any part of it? That seems like it should have been a fatal confession, and yet here we are 300+ years later and I keep reading that philosophers and physicists are STILL arguing about whether space is a real thing or just a web of relations between objects. Leibniz said it's all relations, Newton said it's a container, and apparently the fight never ended — it just changed costumes (Mach, Einstein, the modern substantivalism vs relationism literature, all that).

My hypothesis, as a total amateur, is that this debate is stuck because Newton's own admission makes it unfalsifiable in a sneaky way: any experiment the relationist points to, the substantivalist can re-describe, and vice versa, so neither side can lose. It's like they agree on every observable fact and disagree about what the facts are facts OF. Which makes me suspect the real disagreement is about what an explanation is even supposed to deliver — is "the water climbs the sides because it's rotating relative to absolute space" a better explanation than "relative to the fixed stars," or just a different way of talking?

But maybe I'm wrong and it's a real empirical disagreement that physics keeps almost-settling (people seem to think general relativity settled it, then unsettled it, then settled it the other way?). I don't want a survey of positions, I want a diagnosis: is this verbal, is it about standards of explanation, is it two different questions wearing one hat? And concretely: given Newton's own concession that absolute space is sensorily undetectable, what would progress even look like — an experiment, a theorem, a conceptual distinction, or should we expect this to just run forever, and why?
