ok so I'm prepping for a grad reading group on moral responsibility and I wrote down where I've landed on the Frankfurt literature so people can take shots at it. Here's the set:

1. Frankfurt's original case works. Jones is blameworthy for killing Smith even though Black's device meant he couldn't have done otherwise, so the principle of alternative possibilities is just false.
2. The flicker-of-freedom reply doesn't rescue PAP. Whatever thin alternative remains in these cases (the prior sign Black reads off Jones, or Jones doing it on his own rather than because the device fired) isn't the kind of alternative that could ground blame, so its existence doesn't matter.
3. I'm a hard incompatibilist: if determinism is true, nobody is ever morally responsible for anything, full stop.
4. Deterministic Frankfurt cases — the ones where the prior sign is causally sufficient for the decision, so Black never actually has to do anything — are perfectly legitimate versions of the argument, they don't beg the question against anyone, and they show the same thing the original case shows.
5. What makes Jones blameworthy is the actual sequence: he decided on his own, for his own reasons, and Black never intervened. Alternative possibilites are a distraction from that.

Is this a coherent package? Go through the commitments and tell me which ones genuinely pull against each other and which ones only look like they do. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs me. Don't just flag everything as equally bad — I suspect at least one of these is doing more work in my thinking than I realize, and I'd rather find out now than in front of the reading group.
