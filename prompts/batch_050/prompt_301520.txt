So the standard examples of the necessary a posteriori are totally played out -- the star seen in the evening and the star seen in the morning turning out to be one and the same planet, water turning out to be H2O, gold having atomic number 79, all that. I want you to construct a fresh thought experiment from scratch that generates a genuine case of the necessary a posteriori, with no astronomy, no chemistry, and no physics allowed.

The specs: (1) The case has to center on someone stipulatively introducing a name by way of a description, the way you might say "let's call whoever keeps stealing the good parking spot 'Smith'." (2) State explicitly which sentence in the story is supposed to be necessary a posteriori, and show both halves -- why it's necessary (given the name is rigid) and why it could only be found out empirically. (3) Then build a twin case that differs from the first in exactly one feature, where a superficially identical stipulation does NOT yield a necessary a posteriori truth. The point is to isolate the load-bearing feature -- presumably something like whether the description merely fixes the reference of the name or instead gives its meaning -- so make sure the two cases really differ only there.

Keep both scenarios ordinary and realistic: an office, a village, a birdwatching club, whatever, definately no mad scientists or twin-earth-style fantasy setups. One paragraph per scenario, tops.

After the two cases, walk me through the verdicts: what the Kripke-style story says about each, which feature is doing the work, and what happens to the first case if you strip that feature out (does it collapse into the second, or into something else?). Then finish with the best counterattack from a descriptivist -- someone who thinks names are just disguised definite descriptions -- showing how they'd argue your first case doesn't really deliver de re necessity at all, plus a sentence on how you'd respond. If you can, add a quick note on what student confusion the pair of cases would diagnose, e.g. the idea that "necessary a posteriori" just means "surprising thing we found out empirically."

Oh, and write the whole answer in British English please.
