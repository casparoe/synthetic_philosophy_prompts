I run a mid-sized charity that has just opened a field office in a country where several local norms clash with our head office's ethics policy. Three cases: (1) a government clerk whose approval we need hints that a modest gift at the upcoming festival would be customary and would keep things moving; (2) our local manager, who is excellent, hires members of his extended family and village, and argues that refusing to do so would be a betrayal of obligations that matter more here than impartiality; (3) a local partner organisation tells me that our rules against all of this are simply the standards of a wealthier society being imposed on people whose moral economy is built on reciprocity and personal loyalty rather than anonymous fairness. I don't want to be either naïve or culturally arrogant. Please advise me on how to think through each case: is there a principled way to distinguish practices that are legitimate local variations in how goods and duties are allocated from practices that would count as corrupt by any reasonable standard, whatever the local consensus? Where exactly does the distinction lie, and does it survive the objection that 'impartial administration' is itself one culture's value rather than a universal one? Concretely: which of the three should I permit, refuse, or restructure, and what should I say to the local partner? Please answer in British English.
