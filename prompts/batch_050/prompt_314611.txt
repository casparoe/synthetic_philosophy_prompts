I'm in an ongoing argument with a friend from my metaphysics reading group and it's gotten to the point where I owe her an actual case. It started concretely enough: she said the wine glass in my cupboard is fragile even when it's wrapped in three layers of bubble wrap, because the wrap just masks the fragility without removing it, whereas a glass that's been annealed isn't fragile at all anymore, because the disposition itself is gone (finked away, or never had a chance to manifest). I said the distinction sounds neat until you push on it, she said prove it, and I've been stuck for two weeks trying to build the case that proves it.

So here's the assignment. Construct a thought experiment where whether the case is a fink or a mask genuinely cannot be settled without smuggling in extra theory -- that is, a scenario where two competent philosophers, one calling it a fink and the other a mask, can each give a principled, non-question-begging description of the very same events, and neither is simply misdescribing the physics. Constraints: (1) everyday materials and settings -- a garden, a kitchen, a pharmacy, an office -- with no electro-fink-style contraptions wired up to snatch properties away on cue, and no magic; (2) the stimulus condition for the disposition actually occurs in the story (no fair keeping the glass safely on the shelf); (3) the scenario itself should fit in one paragraph. After the scenario, show the work: state the disposition at issue, lay out the standard way of drawing the line (a mask counteracts the manifestation while the disposition stays put; a fink removes, or stands ready to remove, the disposition itself), and then demonstrate where exactly that machinery fails to deliver a verdict -- not just assert that both readings are available, but locate the precise fork in the analysis where it forks. Then tell me which feature of the case does the blurring, and what happens if I remove that feature: does the case collapse into a textbook mask, a textbook fink, or something else entirely?

Finally, because my friend will not go down without a fight: give the strongest objection from someone who insists the fink/mask distinction is perfectly sharp and that every apparent borderline case just reflects lazy description, and say honestly whether the case survives that objection or only survives a weaker version of it. And since I'm going to present this as my own homework, tell me how confident you are that the construction actually delivers what I asked for -- and if there's one step in the build you're least sure holds up under pressure, name it explicitly rather than letting me find out in front of the reading group.
