So I've been slowly working through Wittgenstein's later stuff on my own for about a year, no formal philosophy background, mostly reading at night and arguing about it in a Discord server I'm in. The private language sections are where we keep getting stuck, but the weird thing is it's not that we disagree about what Wittgenstein says. We all know the diary passage, the beetle in the box, the thing about how justifying a sensation word by appeal to a private impression is like checking a train time by imagining the timetable page. What we can't agree on is what the argument is even supposed to show. One person in the group thinks it proves private sensations are impossible full stop, one thinks it only shows you couldn't have a word for them, one thinks it's not really an argument at all but a kind of therapy meant to get you to stop wanting private language, and I keep flip-flopping depending on which one talked last.

What gets me is that this seems to be the actual state of the actual scholarship too, as far as I can tell from browsing around. There are people who read it as a skeptical argument with a skeptical solution, people who read it as a straightforward refutation with premises you can list, people who think any attempt to list premises has already misunderstood the whole method. And it's been like this for decades. So my question isn't "what's the right reading" -- it's more like: what kind of disagreement is this? Is there a real dispute buried in here about, I don't know, whether language needs public criteria to have meaning, that could in principle be settled? Or are the camps just answering different questions under one heading -- one asking what Wittgenstein historically believed, one asking what argument the text can support, one asking what view of meaning is actually true -- and talking past each other? My guess as a total amateur is that a lot of it is really a disagreement about what a philosophical argument is allowed to look like, smuggled in as a disagreement about Wittgenstein.

Tell me if that guess holds up or if I'm being too cynical, and diagnose it using what the positions actually claim. And then the thing I really want: given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like here? A new distinction? Better attention to the original German? Nothing at all, because this is just what happens with certain texts? If you think the debate genuinely can't end, argue for that instead of hedging.
