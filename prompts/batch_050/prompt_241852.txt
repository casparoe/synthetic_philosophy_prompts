Analyze the teleconference held on the evening of January 27, 1986 — the night before the Challenger launch — as a case study in the thesis that deliberation crowds out prediction. The thesis (associated with Isaac Levi, Wolfgang Spohn, and others) holds that an agent who is genuinely deliberating about what to do cannot coherently assign credences to her own options and then treat those credences as evidence, since deliberating just is holding the question of what one will do open. Take the following as given: Morton Thiokol's engineers, citing O-ring erosion and the loss of resiliency at low temperatures, initially recommended against launching in the forecast cold; they had test and flight data but no clean statistical model relating temperature to failure; NASA managers challenged the no-launch recommendation and pressed for its reversal; during an offline caucus, a senior Thiokol executive urged the engineering vice president to set aside his engineering judgment and decide as a manager; the company then faxed a recommendation to launch.

Address at least the following. (i) Map the episode: where is someone deliberating, and where is someone instead predicting — noting that NASA's pressure seems intelligible only as an attempt to influence a decision NASA was simultaneously trying to forecast. (ii) The engineers could assign a rough probability to catastrophic O-ring failure; could they also, mid-deliberation, have assigned a probability to their own eventual acquiescence, and what changes if they could? (iii) Organizational decision-making, in which one part of an institution predicts and leans on another part's choice, looks like a place where prediction of decisions is routine: is this a counterexample to the thesis, or simply outside its intended scope of an individual deliberator? (iv) Does the disaster itself say anything about the thesis — does something go wrong when prediction displaces deliberation, or was the epistemic failure here of a different kind? Where the theory is silent about some feature of the episode, say so rather than forcing it to speak.
