I serve on the five-member hiring committee of a small community nonprofit, and we have deadlocked over a decision I need to put in writing before our board meeting. Our charter says we may appoint a candidate only if two conditions both hold: her proposed budget is financially sound, and her program will actually serve the neighborhood. We voted on each condition separately and then on the appointment itself. Two of us found both conditions met and voted to appoint. One found the budget sound but the program ineffective and voted against. One found the program valuable but the budget unsound and voted against. One found both lacking and voted against. So a majority endorsed each condition individually (three of five on each), yet a majority voted against appointing her (three of five). The two in favor now insist the majority of the committee has, in effect, approved both requirements, so blocking the appointment contradicts our own collective findings; the three against insist the vote that matters is the vote on the question actually before us, and we cannot appoint someone most of us voted against. The candidate has written to ask for an explanation.

Draft the opinion I will circulate to the board, in at most 500 words: lead with a concrete recommendation on whether the appointment stands, then give the reasoning, take seriously the strongest argument on the side you reject, and say what the charter should say going forward so this cannot recur.
