I write a column on science and ideas, and a mathematically inclined reader has sent me an argument that purports to show that ordinary talk of "if" cannot coexist with ordinary rational uncertainty. I cannot find the flaw, but the conclusion cannot be right. Write the argument as he sent it, then diagnose it.

His argument: (1) For any rational credence function Cr, and any propositions A and C with Cr(A) > 0, the credence in the indicative conditional "if A, then C" equals the conditional credence Cr(C | A). (2) If a credence function is rational, so is any function obtained from it by conditionalizing on a proposition it assigns positive probability. (3) Since (1) holds for every rational credence function, and every conditionalization of a rational function is rational by (2), it holds of the conditionalized functions too: for any B with Cr(A ∧ B) > 0, Cr(if A then C | B) = Cr(C | A ∧ B). (4) Take B to be the material conditional ¬A ∨ C. Then Cr(if A then C | ¬A ∨ C) = Cr(C | A ∧ (¬A ∨ C)) = 1. (5) Take B to be A ∧ ¬C. Then Cr(if A then C | A ∧ ¬C) = Cr(C | A ∧ ¬C) = 0. (6) Since ¬A ∨ C and A ∧ ¬C partition the possibilities, total probability gives Cr(if A then C) = Cr(¬A ∨ C). (7) But then, by (1), Cr(C | A) = Cr(¬A ∨ C) always. Roll a fair die, let A be "it lands even" and C be "it lands six": the left side is 1/3, the right side is 2/3. So no rational agent may hold these perfectly ordinary credences -- and iterating the same trick forces every rational credence function onto the values 0 and 1.

Please tell me which step should be rejected, and why that step initially looks innocent. Then price the alternatives: what it costs to restrict (1) to the agent's actual credence function and refuse to reapply it after updating, what it costs to deny (2), and what it costs to conclude that indicative conditionals do not express propositions with probabilities at all but merely have assertability conditions. Assume my readers know what conditional probability is but have never heard of the triviality results; keep the technical machinery in the foreground and the history out of it.
