So I'm in a reading group on moral psychology and we've spent the term on the situationist literature -- Darley and Batson's seminarians stepping over the slumped guy because they were in a hurry, Milgram, the dime-in-the-phone-booth study, all of it -- and at the end of term everyone has to write a one-page "here's where I've landed" statement that the others get to attack. Here's mine, roughly:

(1) I'm a virtue ethicist at the level of ethical theory: right action is what the fully virtuous person would characteristically do, and character is the explanatorily and normatively primary notion, prior to rules or outcomes.
(2) I accept that the situationist experiments are real, replicated in the cases that matter, and that they show most people's behavior is driven far more by situational variables than folk psychology assumes.
(3) My reconciliation is the standard moderate one: keep virtue ethics but concede that robust, cross-situationally consistent character traits are much rarer than Aristotle thought; most people have at most local traits (honest-at-home, kind-to-friends), and full virtue is an achievement almost nobody reaches.
(4) I still think moral education should work by habituation into stable dispositions -- that's how we should raise kids and design institutions, aiming at people who will reliably act well across situations.
(5) I think praise and blame presuppose that the person acted from their character rather than being pushed around by the situation, and I want to keep ordinary blaming practices basically intact.
(6) I also think the situationist findings should make us considerably less judgmental and more compassionate toward people who act badly.

What I want from you is an audit before I hand this in. Which of these pairs are actually in tension, and which only look like they are and dissolve on inspection? Rank the genuine tensions by severity -- I don't want a laundry list where everything gets flagged as equally bad. For each real one, say exactly what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is, and at the end tell me which single change, if I could only make one, cleans up the most. Also: I genuinely don't know where the problem is, and I'm not attached to any particular commitment here, so don't pull punches -- but also don't flag a tension just because it sounds sophisticated; if two of my claims are fine together, say so.
