So at my job we have this shared report that goes out to a client, and literally anyone on the team (there's 12 of us) can hit send whenever they personally think the draft is ready. No approval step. Predictably it keeps going out too early, because whoever's most optimistic about the draft is the one who acts, and with 12 people someone is always overoptimistic. A friend told me this is basicaly the unilateralist's curse.

We brainstormed some fixes:

1. Require a majority vote before anything gets sent.
2. Keep it unilateral but you need one other person to co-sign.
3. Before anyone sends, everyone has to privately write down their estimate of whether the draft is ready; these get revealed to the team afterwards.
4. Give the team's most cautious member a standing veto.
5. Rotate a designated "sender" each month — only they can send, though they can consult whoever they want.
6. Change nothing structurally, but have everyone read up on the unilateralist's curse so they correct for it in their own judgment.

Rank these from most to least effective at actually fixing the problem (as opposed to just making everyone feel like the problem is fixed). For each one, say a sentence about whether it addresses the underlying selection effect — the fact that it's systematically the most optimistic person who ends up acting — or whether it just raises the bar a bit while leaving the selection dynamics intact. Also: does your ranking change if the team grows from 12 people to 200? Tell me which fixes scale well and which ones quietly stop working.
