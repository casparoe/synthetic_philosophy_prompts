I am applying for a nine-month early-career fellowship and need a two-page research proposal. The project sits in value theory, on spectrum arguments and the transitivity of 'better than'.

My half-formed starting point is as follows. The standard construction places a long series of items side by side — two headaches differing by a barely noticeable increment of pain, two lives differing by a month, two populations differing marginally in average welfare — where each item is clearly better (or clearly no worse) than its neighbour, yet the endpoints stand in a stark ranking that transitivity then forbids. The two familiar exits are well known: surrender transitivity for the all-things-considered relation, or keep transitivity and insist that the chain of comparisons must break somewhere, with all the awkwardness about where. What strikes me is that both camps accept an assumption that almost nobody examines: that the spectrum is homogeneous, meaning that one and the same dimension of value varies smoothly along the whole series, with no change in which features of the items are carrying the evaluative weight. My suspicion is that the paradoxical force of the celebrated cases depends on hidden heterogeneity — that somewhere along the series the ground of the comparisons quietly shifts, say from the intensity of a pain to its distribution across subjects, or from the sheer length of a life to what a life of that length could contain — and that once such 'category switches' are made visible, some spectra collapse while others retain their bite. The interesting question is then not whether 'better than' is transitive but whether a principled criterion of homogeneity can be stated, and which of the standard cases survive it.

Please turn this into the proposal. It should: state the central question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the existing positions leave it open rather than merely failing to settle it; set out the method, meaning what I would actually do month by month (including how one even tests a series for a category switch without simply inspecting intuitions); describe what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing, including the possibility that no non-question-begging criterion exists; and give an honest assessment of the obvious objection that the homogeneity criterion will be reverse-engineered from whichever verdicts on transitivity I already favour. No bibliography or literature review by citation, please — I will supply references myself; name positions and arguments in prose only.
