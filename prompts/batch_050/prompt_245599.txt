My uncle and I got into this over dinner and its been bugging me for weeks. I read a summary of Hobbes (state of nature, everyone against everyone, so people get out of it by agreeing to obey a common power) and I think the whole thing collapses under its own logic, but that feels way too easy for a book people have argued about for centuries, so I probably goofed somewhere. Here's my argument:

(1) A promise is only worth something if the other person can be trusted to do their part.
(2) Hobbes says that in the state of nature nobody can trust anybody, because whoever keeps their end of a bargain first is then at the mercy of the other guy, who has every reason to just walk off with the advantage.
(3) The deal that creates the government is itself a deal made between people who are still in the state of nature — it has to be, since there's no government yet to enforce anything.
(4) So by (2), nobody can trust that deal either: someone has to lay down their arms first, and everyone else then has a massive incentive not to follow through.
(5) So a government can never actually get started by agreement. The only way you ever get one is brute force, and if someone has the brute force to impose order, the "agreement" was just decoration and contract theory does no work.
(6) So Hobbes's own reasoning shows the social contract is either impossible or just a story told after the fact about whoever won.

This seems to prove too much, since plenty of governments clearly rest on at least some broad acceptance and not pure force. Where does it go wrong? Tell me which step you'd reject and what rejecting it costs — like, what I'd have to give up or swallow if I drop that step. If I had to pick something to keep it'd be (2), the trust point just seems obviously right to me.
