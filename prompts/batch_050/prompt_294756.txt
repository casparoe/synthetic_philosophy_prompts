I've never studied philosophy, but for a couple of years now I've been turning one question over in my head: where exactly is the line between medicine that fixes people and medicine that improves them? It started as an argument about what public healthcare ought to pay for, but it has since spread into how I think about my children's school, competitive sports, even my own daily habits. I've written down everything I currently believe on the subject, and I'd like you to audit the set. Tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear to conflict but are actually consistent, and for each real tension, what I would have to give up to resolve it. Please order the genuine tensions by severity, and if revising exactly one commitment would make the rest hang together, tell me which one and why.

Here is where I stand:

1. The purpose of medicine is to treat disease and disability — to bring people up to normal, species-typical human functioning. That is what separates treatment, which a decent society owes to everyone, from enhancement, which people may pursue but nobody is owed.

2. Vaccination is plainly on the treatment side of that line. Preventing disease is medicine's core business, and I would happily see immunization publicly funded for everyone.

3. Ordinary human aging is not a disease. Nevertheless, if a safe intervention could slow the aging process itself, I would want it developed and publicly covered, since it would prevent more illness than any single cure ever could.

4. A student who takes stimulants prescribed for someone else before an examination is cheating; a student who relies on coffee, tutors, and practice tests is not. The first buys an unearned advantage; the second simply prepares.

5. Parents not only may but should seek real advantages for their children — better schools, good nutrition, music lessons — provided they do not harm other people's children in doing so.

6. Competent adults may do whatever they like to their own bodies and minds, so long as they are informed and harm no one. If a safe pill improved memory or concentration in healthy people, taking it would be nobody's business but theirs.

I believe each of these is something a reasonable person could hold, and I have tried to state them so they could all be true at once. I suspect at least one pair cannot, but I genuinely don't know which, and I don't want hints about where to look — just the audit.
