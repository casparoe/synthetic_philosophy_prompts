At game night we had one of those toy coin-flipping machines, and it came up heads eight times in a row. I said, fine, assuming it really is a fair machine, the next flip is still 50/50; but eight heads is also some evidence the machine isn’t fair, so maybe our credence should drift above .5. My friend shut me down with “the coin has no memory”: the machine doesn’t know what it just did, so no updating, 50/50. Then someone else said that if physics is deterministic, the “real chance” of the next flip is already 0 or 1 and 50/50 is just ignorance, so the whole discussion was confused.

What precise claims could “the coin has no memory” be making? Separate at least: (i) a causal claim about the mechanism, (ii) conditional independence given a fixed chance or bias, (iii) unconditional independence from the observed run, (iv) some kind of exchangeability or symmetry claim, and (v) a Principal Principle claim that if the objective chance is known to be .5, rational credence should also be .5 unless you have special “inadmissible” information about this particular outcome. For each version, say whether it’s a platitude, a substantive statistical claim, or just false, and whether it actually supports my friend’s no-updating conclusion. Also explain how past flips can fail to causally influence the next flip while still rationally influencing my beliefs about the machine’s bias.

Then get into the harder part: if the exact physical state of the machine settles the result, is the objective chance 0 or 1, .5, both at different levels of description, or is “objective chance” just the wrong tool here? Don’t simply declare determinism true or false; show what each answer does to the Principal Principle and to the slogan. Finish with the least question-begging verdict you can give about what I should believe concerning flip nine after seeing HHHHHHHH, under a few clearly stated assumptions. Keep credences, frequencies, and objective chances terminologically straight, because that’s half the arguement.
