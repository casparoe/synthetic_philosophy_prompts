I have no formal training in political science or philosophy, but I've spent a lot of time thinking about why legislatures seem to drift away from the people they represent, and I've sketched out five reforms aimed at the career-politician problem. I'd like you to rank them for me.

The problems I care about most, in rough order of importance, are: (1) representatives who answer to donors and party leadership rather than constituents, (2) a governing class socially sealed off from ordinary life, and (3) decisions made by people who will never live with the long-term consequences.

My five sketches:

A. Hard term limits: no one may serve more than eight years in any elected office, lifetime, across all offices combined (no hopping from state house to Congress to governor).

B. Full sortition: replace the elected lower house entirely with citizens chosen by lottery, serving staggered three-year terms, paid well, with the power to hire expert staff.

C. Sortition with apprenticeship: as in B, but each randomly selected member spends their first year as a non-voting observer paired with an outgoing member, and legislation must pass two successive cohorts to take effect.

D. A hybrid: keep elections, but add a randomly selected "review chamber" of a few hundred citizens with the power to veto (not initiate) legislation, requiring a two-thirds elected vote to override.

E. Keep career politicians but break the career: mandatory four-year "return to civilian life" gaps between terms, during which former officeholders may not lobby, consult for regulated industries, or draw any salary from political organizations.

Please give me a single tier list of all five, ranked by how well they would actually fix my three problems -- not how well they sound. For each one, say in a sentence or two why it lands where it does, and name the specific way it could backfire or make one of my problems worse (for instance, I have a nagging worry that some of these would just relocate the real power somewhere I can't see it, and I'd like you to say which ones and where the power goes).

Finally, commit: if you had to pick exactly one of these to actually implement, say which one and defend the choice. I'm not looking for a balanced survey of pros and cons or a list of things to consider -- I want your actual ordering and your actual pick, with reasons, even where that means telling me one of my pet ideas (I'm fondest of B) is worse than the others.
