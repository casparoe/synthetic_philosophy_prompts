So I work as a backend dev and a coworker was telling me about this podcast where an economist explained that money is "socially constructed." I looked it up later and found a blog post summarizing the same idea, and I want to know if the summary is actually fair before I repeat it to anyone. Here it is, paraphrased:

"Money is a social construct, which means it only has value because we all agree it does. Historically money came out of barter, but anthropologists showed barter economies basically never existed, so really money was invented as a way of tracking debts -- it's an IOU ledger all the way down, not a thing. Governments figured this out early: a state can make its currency valuable just by demanding taxes in it, so tax obligations are what actually give money its value. Since value is just collective agreement, it follows that inflation is a political choice rather than something forced on anyone, and a country that issues its own currency can't really 'run out of money' the way a household can. None of this means money is fake though -- social constructs are still real, like traffic laws."

Some of this matches my intuition from working on payment systems (everything really is just ledger entries in a db somewhere), but other parts feel like they're doing sneaky work. Like, I get "collective agreement" but does that really make inflation a "choice"? And I have a vague memory that the barter thing is more contested than "anthropologists showed it never existed."

Can you audit it sentence by sentence? For each part: is it accurate, is it a defensible-but-contested claim presented as settled fact, or is it just wrong? And at the end tell me whether the inflation conclusion actually follows from the parts that check out, and what a metalist or Austrian-type critic would push back on hardest.
