Next month I'm giving a thirty-minute talk at my company's internal engineering forum, followed by twenty minutes of Q&A. My thesis: in large software systems, default settings are a form of legislation. My argument sketch has three steps. First, at scale, defaults are sticky: deviating from them costs time, review friction, and social capital, so most users -- and most downstream engineers building on top of us -- never change them. Second, defaults distribute real goods: default-on telemetry, default data retention windows, and default ranking thresholds allocate privacy, attention, and visibility just as surely as a statute would. Third, unlike actual legislation, defaults have no moment of decision: no vote, no sign-off, often no record of who chose the value or why, so they present themselves as mere facts of the system rather than as choices. I conclude that engineers who write defaults are doing governance whether they intend to or not, and that the familiar line -- technology is neutral, only its users are political -- fails for defaults even if it holds elsewhere.

The audience is about forty engineers, mostly backend and infrastructure, who strongly identify with the craft-neutrality view and will hear my talk as an accusation. One engineering manager is worried the thesis implies engineers are personally accountable for downstream misuse of anything they ship. One senior engineer has actually read the technology-studies literature and will raise the classic case of Robert Moses's low parkway bridges on Long Island -- along with the complication that the standard story about them is historically contested.

Prepare my Q&A sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this room, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way an engineer here would put it, with what I should concede, what I should hold, a ninety-second reply, and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the objection that my thesis trivializes politics by stretching the word to cover everything, and close with the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
