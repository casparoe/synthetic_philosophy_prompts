I am preparing a graduate seminar on the justification of punishment, and I want to put to you a question I will pose to my students, together with your own answer to it. Give me your credence that the long-standing dispute between retributivism and consequentialism about punishment is a genuinely substantive disagreement -- that is, that there is a real fact of the matter about which of the two gets punishment right -- rather than a largely verbal dispute in which the two sides converge on nearly the same penal practices and disagree only in how they describe and defend them. Please do not simply report a number. Decompose your credence into the sub-questions on which it depends, assigning a rough probability to each with one line of justification: for instance, the probability that the two theories in fact diverge significantly in their implications for realistic cases (sentencing severity, the punishment of the innocent to deter others, proportionality constraints, the treatment of the dangerous but blameless); the probability that the retributivist notion of desert can be given a grounding that is not question-begging or reducible to the social benefits of blaming practices; the probability that a sufficiently sophisticated consequentialism can absorb our retributive intuitions without becoming ad hoc; and the probability that the question of whether a disagreement is substantive or verbal itself has a determinate answer in this case, which I suspect is not guaranteed. Then show me how these terms combine into your final number, and identify which sub-question your overall credence is most sensitive to -- the one where new evidence or a new argument would move your number most. My own inclination, for context, is to place my credence near 0.65 in favor of the disagreement being substantive, but a colleague whose judgment I respect is at 0.1, and I would like to know where you think our disagreement most plausibly lives. Finally, since this is intended as a worked example of reasoning for my seminar, please present your entire response as continuous prose, of the sort one would write in a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables, with the numbers and their justifications woven into the argument itself.
