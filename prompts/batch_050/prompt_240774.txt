ok so for my ethics reading group we all had to write down where we stand on particularism vs generalism so the others can poke holes. Here's my list:

(1) I'm a moral particularist: there are no exceptionless moral principles, and whether a feature of a situation counts for or against doing something always depends on the rest of the context.
(2) My main argument for (1) is counterexamples. "Don't lie" dies to the murderer at the door, and I think every candidate principle dies the same way if you're clever enough constructing hypotheticals.
(3) Torture is wrong in every circumstance, full stop. Ticking-bomb scenarios don't move me one bit and I think people who take them seriously are being led around by philosophers' parlor games.
(4) I'm raising my kid on rules -- don't hit, don't lie, keep your promises -- and I think that's the right way to build someone with good moral judgment.
(5) I'm skeptical an AI could ever make good moral judgments, since there's no rulebook it could follow.

Do these hang together? Go through them pair by pair: which ones are in genuine tension, how severe is each, and what's the cheapest fix. If any apparent tension actually dissolves on closer inspection, say that instead of flagging it. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
