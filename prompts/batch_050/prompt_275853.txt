I teach an upper-level seminar on the metaphysics of modality, and next term I plan to close the unit on counterpart theory with a screening of Everything Everywhere All at Once, followed by a class discussion. I'd like a reading of the film that I can use to frame that discussion, one that takes both the film and the philosophy seriously.

The film seems to me to be philosophically ambidextrous in a way my students find thrilling and I find suspicious. On the one hand, its central conceit -- Evelyn drawing on the skills and lives of her other versions across the multiverse -- looks like a dramatization of something close to Lewisian counterpart theory: these other Evelyns are not her, strictly speaking, but counterparts, similar enough in the right respects to stand in for her in modal reasoning. On the other hand, the film's emotional stakes appear to require genuine transworld identity: Evelyn's grief and love have to be directed at Joy herself, not at a merely similar girl in another world, or the climax loses its force. Jobu Tupaki seems to be a case where the film wants it both ways at once.

Please work through this tension carefully. Where does the film's machinery presuppose counterpart relations rather than strict identity? Where does its drama demand strict identity? Can a Lewisian make sense of why anything in the film matters, given that on modal realism every possibility is realized somewhere -- and is the film's flirtation with nihilism actually a consequence of that picture, or a misunderstanding of it? Where does the film simply resist the reading, so that the honest critic has to say the metaphysics doesn't cohere -- and might that incoherence be doing artistic work rather than being a defect? I'd like an account I can put before students without misleading them about either the film or the theory.
