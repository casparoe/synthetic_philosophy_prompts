I run a small ethics reading group and next week we're doing Wolf's moral saints piece together with some Singer, and as prep we each have to write down where we stand and let the others try to break it. I'd rather find the cracks myself first, so here's my position, roughly:

(1) Singer is basically right about global poverty: if you're comfortably middle-class in a rich country, failing to give a substantial share of your income to effective charities is seriously morally wrong, not just suboptimal.
(2) I'm an impartial maximizing consequentialist -- the right action is the one with the best consequences, full stop, no agent-relative prerogatives built into the theory itself.
(3) Wolf is right about sainthood: a person who devoted themselves wholly and always to the moral good would be a kind of maimed creature -- no humor, no hobbies pursued for their own sake, no deep personal projects -- and that life is genuinely unattractive, not something to aspire to.
(4) It is perfectly morally permissible for me to keep a decent chunk of my income and time for my own projects (I'm learning the cello, I like hosting dinners) rather than redirecting all of it to where it does the most good.
(5) What morality demands and what it's best for a person to be and do can come apart -- the best life isn't necessarily the most morally demanding one.
(6) Ordinary decent people who don't donate much aren't bad people and mostly aren't blameworthy for it; I wouldn't think less of a friend for never giving to charity.

My instinct is that (3) and (5) protect (4), but my friend says (2) is doing all the work in (1) and will wreck everything downstream, and honestly I can't tell who's right. Also possibly (6) clashes with (1)? Audit the whole thing: tell me which commitments actually pull against each other and which pairs only look like they do, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what I'd have to give up and what that would cost the overall view. I'm not attached to any particular piece -- if the cheapest fix is dropping something I thought was central, say so plainly rather than patching around it.
