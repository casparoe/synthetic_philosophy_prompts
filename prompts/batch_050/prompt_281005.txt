I teach a mixed undergrad course (about 24 students, 75-minute session) and want an in-class game that makes students feel why costly signaling works when cheap talk doesn't. Setup I'm imagining: everyone secretly draws a type (say, strong or weak forager), pairs up, and has to decide whether to trust a partner's claim about their type before splitting some points. First round the claim is free, so it should collapse into noise; then introduce a signal that costs points to send -- and here's the part I really want the rules to force: the signal must cost weak types *more* than strong types, so that only strong types find it worth sending. I want them to stumble into the differential-cost condition themselves rather than have me state it, so structure the rounds accordingly. Give me full rules, the payoff numbers (no real money), round-by-round timeline, and a debrief that maps what happend onto the handicap principle -- including why a signal that everyone can afford sends no information at all. Also: one extra round idea for what happens when a signal gets cheaper over time (counterfeiting/inflation), and an honest note on what the game can't capture about real biological signals.
