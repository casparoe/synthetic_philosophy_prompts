So I've been rereading the Careenium bit in Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach -- the thought experiment with the two kinds of marbles, where the tiny ones (the simms) rattle around and bang into each other, and every so often their collisions knock loose one of the big heavy ones (the simmballs), which then roll around and crash into each other on their own slower timescale. Hofstadter sets it up so the simmball-crashings are what symbol-level events look like from above, the way a "thought" is supposed to supervene on neural firings, and his moral seems to be that all the real pushing happens down at the simm level: the small stuff shoves the big stuff around, and any sense in which symbols cause things is at best a handy way of talking about a pattern that's fully driven from below. I've basically talked myself into accepting this. Causation flows bottom-up only; the symbol level is descriptive, not causal. Downward causation is a fiction we tell because tracking 10^11 simms is infeasable.

Now tell me what would count against it. Not "some people find emergent causation intuitive" -- I mean concretely: what would a physical system have to be like for the symbol level to genuinely do causal work rather than ride along? A few things I want you to sort through:

One, on an interventionist picture of causation, it seems like intervening at the simmball level (shove this big ball, watch the downstream big-ball trajectory) gives you clean, stable causal generalizations, and some people argue that's all causation ever was. Does that move actually threaten the bottom-up-only view, or does the Careenium already contain the answer, namely that any such intervention is itself made of simm collisions so nothing downward has happened? If a defender just says that, has the position become true by bookkeeping?

Two, what would I have to observe to be forced to give it up? Would a Careenium whose simmball dynamics couldn't be predicted from any simm-level simulation short of just running the whole thing count -- i.e. does predictive or computational indispensibility do any causal work, or is that just epistemology dressed up? What about a system with the same microphysics where the symbol-level laws were simpler and more exceptionless than any micro-level law? Is there a case the bottom-up view literally cannot describe, or does it absorb everything by construction?

Three, where does this stand or fall with things elsewhere: Kim's exclusion argument, multiple realizability, the way people talk about "the algorithm" doing something in a transformer. If I concede real downward causation anywhere, does the whole view collapse, or is there a principled local patch?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say whether a Hofstadter-style defender would concede it counts or wave it off -- I'm more interested in what the view's own friends would accept as a hit than in what its enemies throw at it. And end with an honest verdict on whether bottom-up-only has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's a vice for a metaphysical position about where the pushing happens or just what being right looks like.
