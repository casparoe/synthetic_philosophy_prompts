So I've gone down a rabbit hole reading about Bayesian stuff and the maximum entropy principle (mostly blog posts and a textbook I got like 3 chapters into, not gonna lie). I came up with an argument that I'm pretty sure is wrong but I can't find the hole in it, and it's bugging me. Here it is:

(1) A rational prior should be the maximum entropy distribution consistent with what you actually know. If you pick anything else, you're basically pretending to have information you don't have.

(2) But entropy depends on how you describe the possibilities. Like if a factory makes cubes with side length between 1 and 3 cm, "maximum entropy" gives a different answer if you're uniform over side length vs uniform over volume. Both seem like equally good descriptions of the situation.

(3) So before you can apply max entropy at all, you need to know which way of describing the space is the "right" one.

(4) The only non-arbitrary way to pick that, if you believe (1), is to be maximally uncertain over the possible descriptions too, i.e. max entropy over parametrizations.

(5) But taking the max entropy distribution over parametrizations requires a way of measuring the space of parametrizations, which is the exact same problem one level up.

(6) So max entropy either regresses forever or bottoms out in a choice that's just as arbitrary as picking any old prior.

(7) So there's no such thing as THE rational prior for a situation, and "objective" Bayesianism is really just subjective Bayesianism wearing a lab coat.

Now the reason I think this proves too much: as far as I can tell, physicists use maximum entropy all the time in statistical mechanics and it works insanely well, like it predicts real measurable stuff about gases. If my argument was right, that should be impossible or at least a complete coincidence, and it's not. So something in there has to give.

Ideally I'd like to keep (1) and (2) because they both seem obviously right to me, but tell me if I can't. Which step do I reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it actually cost me? Like, if the fix is "entropy is always relative to a background measure" (someone in a comment section said this) then doesn't that just move the arbitrariness from the prior to the measure, and isn't that the same problem with a fake moustache on?
