In a discord server I'm in, the argument about whether LLMs solved the frame problem has been going in circles for like two years now. One side says: GPT-style models handle novel situations, update on context, and don't need anyone to hand-code axioms about what stays the same when you act — that's the frame problem, dissolved by gradient descent, time for philosophers to stop citing it. The other side says: LLMs just interpolate over a gigantic corpus, the actual problem (how does a system zero in on whats relevant without searching everything?) hasn't been touched at all, it's just been hidden by scale, and you can see this in how models fall apart off-distribution. My working suspicion is that the two sides aren't even disagreeing about capabilities: the first means the old engineering frame problem (avoiding explicit reasoning about non-effects of actions) and the second means the deeper philosophical one (relevance realization), and "solved" means something different for each. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real empirical bet buried in there somewhere. So: what kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, empirical, or a fight about what would even count as a solution?
