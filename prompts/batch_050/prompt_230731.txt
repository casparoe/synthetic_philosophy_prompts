Bit of background so you know where I'm coming from: I'm a retired marine biologist. Forty years of staring at copepods down microscopes and through camera lenses, and only now, in retirement, have I got round to reading philosophy. Naturally the first thing I stumble into is this arguement about whether we literally *see* things through photographs. Some writers (Walton seems to be the big name) say yes — a photo of my granddaughter is me genuinely seeing her, just indirectly, the way I see through a telescope. Others say no: the photo shows you how she looked, but you don't see *her*.

Before I read any further I want a spec, the way you'd write one before buying an instrument. Give me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of photographic seeing has to deliver, with the case or consideration motivating each requirement. The cases I think it must take a position on, at minimum: an ordinary snapshot; a live CCTV feed of the squid tank in my old lab; a recording of that same feed watched a year later; a telescope; a mirror; a sonogram and an X-ray; a camera-trap photo no human framed; a photorealist painting; my quick field sketches; a doctored photo; and one of those AI-generated images of a whale that never existed. The theory has to say where in that spread seeing stops and mere information-showing begins, and the line has to be principled, not just where intuition happens to wobble.

A few more things I suspect belong on the list: it must respect the difference between seeing an object and learning how it looked; it must deal with the fact that we happily say we see a star that may already be dead, so time-delay alone can't be what disqualifies photos; it must explain whether the photographer's intentions matter and why (a painting has a mind behind every millimetre, an exposure seemingly doesn't); it must explain the special evidential weight we give photos in science and in court, or else argue that weight is superstition; and it mustn't demand a causal purity from photography that ordinary vision itself fails — my visual system was filling in and predicting the whole time I was "directly" watching plankton.

Then the part I most want: tell me which of these are genuine requirements and which are just one camp's prejudice dressed up as common sense, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and name the single requirement that eliminates the most contenders. A short paragraph at the end on how the transparency view and the main skeptical view fare against the list is fine, but keep the weight on the criteria — the scorecard I can do myself over coffee.
