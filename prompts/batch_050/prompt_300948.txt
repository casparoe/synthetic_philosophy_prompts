Ok so in history class we did this unit on Malthus, the guy who said population grows faster than food so eventually there's mass starvation. Teacher said he got it wrong because farming got way better. But then this kid in my class goes "he wasn't wrong, he was just early" and the teacher kinda shrugged and moved on and its been bugging me ever since.

Because once you notice that move you see it everywhere:

- the preachers who say the world ends in 2012, then when it doesn't it's actually 2017, then it's "soon"
- people who've been predicting a giant economic crash for like 30 years and every year it doesn't happen they say the fundamentals are even worse now so it'll be even bigger
- the whole "capitalism will collapse under its own contradictions" thing — it hasn't, but believers say that just proves it's still coming
- my uncle who's been saying AI takes all the jobs "within 20 years" since I think before I was born

So the pattern is: a prediction fails, and instead of the theory taking any damage, the deadline just moves. And sometimes the failure even counts as MORE evidence (the longer it takes, the worse it'll be when it hits). Is there an actual name for this? I don't think it's the same as just being wrong, and I don't think it's the same as making excuses either, because sometimes a theory genuinely has reasons for why something takes forever. Climate change people also basically say "wait longer" and they seem to be right??

And here's the part I actually care about: if "just early" is a legit defense sometimes, how do you ever tell the difference between a theory that's early and a theory that's dead?
