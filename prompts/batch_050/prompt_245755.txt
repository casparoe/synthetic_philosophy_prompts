For a graduate ethics seminar, I am assembling the specification against which theories of complicity will be examined — complicity understood here as blameworthy participation in another's wrongdoing, where the participant's own conduct neither causes the wrong nor is necessary for it. The theory is wanted for a definite purpose: to guide an individual agent deciding whether their own participation in some practice or institution makes them a party to its wrongs, not to serve as a doctrine of criminal liability. Write the list of desiderata such a theory must meet to count as adequate, giving the case or consideration that motivates each one, and mark which requirements you take to be non-negotiable and which are the signature commitments of particular camps (causal-contribution accounts, intention- or shared-plan accounts, taint or association accounts, and so on).

Please have the list answer to the following test cases, which I have stipulated precisely so that no off-the-shelf taxonomy will settle them:

- The spare lookout. One of several willing and equally competent lookouts for a burglary; the crime would have proceeded identically had he stayed home. His presence made no difference to anything.
- The indifferent supplier. A shopkeeper sells an item she knows will be used that evening in a serious crime; the buyer could have purchased an identical item, at the same price, from the shop next door, and would have.
- The bookkeeper. Keeps the accounts of a criminal enterprise with care and skill; never touches the instruments of any particular wrong; indispensable to the paperwork, redundant with respect to every individual offence.
- The designated bystander. Officially charged with intervening, able to do so at trivial cost, and stays at his desk; the wrong proceeds exactly as it would have anyway, since unbeknownst to him a second safeguard was also in place and also failed.
- The thwarted principal. A participant assists in good faith and with full intent, but the principal wrong never occurs: the plan is abandoned for reasons unrelated to the assistance.
- The cheering non-contributor. Would gladly have helped, was never asked and never needed, and celebrates the outcome.
- The marginal consumer. One purchase among millions, with no expected effect on the wrong the industry commits.

Among the questions I want the desiderata to adjudicate: whether complicity must be derivative of an actual, completed principal wrong or can attach to attempts and abandoned plans; whether any form of difference-making is required, and if not, what replaces it; whether purposefully furthering a wrong differs in kind from knowingly facilitating it, or only in degree; whether omissions can ground complicity, and on what basis; whether there is moral residue — something the participant must answer for — when the wrong would have occurred without them; and how a theory should treat collective wrongs in which no individual contribution is necessary.

Two further demands. First, if any subset of the desiderata you list is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, combining a difference-making requirement with the conviction that the spare lookout is complicit, while denying that complicity is subject to moral luck — say so explicitly rather than smoothing it over; an inconsistent specification would itself be the most useful result for my seminar. Second, identify which single desideratum does the most excluding work against the main families of theories, and note briefly how those families fare against it — but keep the weight on the criteria, not the scorecard, which I will construct in class.

Throughout, say how confident you are in each verdict you give, and single out the one desideratum or case about which you are least sure. Please write in British English.
