Write an essay evaluating the following proposal for escaping epistemic bubbles and echo chambers: rather than giving every contrary viewpoint a fixed share of attention, a deliberative community should allocate attention according to the independence of a dissenter’s likely errors and the dissenter’s capacity to expose load-bearing assumptions. Call this the error-portfolio rule. It contrasts with a Millian attention floor, under which every consequential deliberation must reserve some attention for contrary opinions even when those opinions are probably false.

Ground your essay in this passage from Chapter II of John Stuart Mill’s On Liberty:

"The world, to each individual, means the part of it with which he comes in contact; his party, his sect, his church, his class of society: the man may be called, by comparison, almost liberal and large-minded to whom it means anything so comprehensive as his own country or his own age. Nor is his faith in this collective authority at all shaken by his being aware that other ages, countries, sects, churches, classes, and parties have thought, and even now think, the exact reverse."

Begin by explaining what Mill takes the relevant defect to be: a lack of disagreement as such, excessive confidence in a socially local consensus, or something else. Then compare the two rules. Consider finite attention, correlated evidence, strategic manipulation, repetition of known falsehoods, and the danger that a community will underestimate the value of dissent precisely where its own confidence is greatest. Distinguish a legal right to speak from an epistemic claim on scarce attention.

Apply the competing rules to at least two of these cases:

1. An advisory board is ideologically diverse, but every member’s forecast derives from the same proprietary model. A prospective member shares the board’s dominant ideology but uses independently collected historical records.
2. Patients’ anecdotal reports conflict with the statistical consensus of a medical panel; the reports are individually unreliable but arise through an error process independent of the panel’s.
3. A source repeatedly promotes a conspiratorial claim without independent evidence, mainly to secure airtime, although examining the claim might reveal why the misconception persists.

Reach a definite verdict about whether the error-portfolio rule should replace the Millian attention floor, be combined with it, or be rejected. If you recommend a hybrid, state precisely what residual role the attention floor should play.
