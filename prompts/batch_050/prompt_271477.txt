Here's my opening move, which I think is genuinely hard for you: your historical analogies all involve dependence with independent redundancy. When I trust a doctor, there are other doctors trained on overlapping-but-not-identical evidence who can catch a systematic error; when Otto trusts his notebook, he at least retains the capacity to evaluate each entry as he uses it. But picture what I call the last-verifier problem: a research pipeline where every stage is checked by the same class of model that produced it, so an error correlated across the whole model lineage is invisible by construction, and the humans in the loop have let the relevant skills atrophy precisely because the tools were so reliable. In that world there is no longer any mind positioned to notice a certain kind of wrongness, and -- this is the part I want you to address head-on -- the joint system you champion can be reliable in every way we are still able to measure while being wrong in a way we have lost the ability to measure.
