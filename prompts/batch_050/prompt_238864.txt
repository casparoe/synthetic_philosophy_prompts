You are a professional philosopher of history. Think carefully, brainstorm a few rival diagnoses first, then give the best one. In my reading group the debate about moral revolutions has stalled: one side says anti-slavery arguments caused abolition, another says changing economic and state interests did the real work, and a third takes a Williams/Appiah-style view that old practices ended when they became dishonourable. Yet everyone accepts that arguments circulated before abolition, that incentives changed, and that honour mattered. My hunch is that they are confusing tinder, trigger and transmission: what made the norm unstable, what pushed people to act, and how the replacement norm spread. Using abolition and the decline of duelling as anchor cases, is this really a factual disagreement, a conceptual disagreement about causation, or people answering different questions? What would count as progress, if anything?
