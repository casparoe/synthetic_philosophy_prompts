Write me a deadpan FAQ page, the kind a government agency posts on its website, issued by the fictional Office of Intergenerational Affairs (est. 2029). The Office has just announced that it is formally closing its Future Generations Division, and the FAQ is there to reassure concerned citizens that this is all perfectly rigorous and well thought out. Target about 600-700 words for the FAQ itself.

The humor has to come from getting the actual philosophy right, not from making the Office say random evil stuff. Work in the real moves, played totally straight, e.g.:

- the non-identity problem: different climate/energy policies will change who ends up existing at all, so the people of 2300 can't really complain that we harmed *them* -- had we acted better, different people would exist instead, and you can't harm someone by causing them to exist (the Office treats this as a bulletproof legal defense and is mildly proud of it)
- social discounting: the Office explains helpfully that at a modest discount rate a welfare loss in 2300 is worth a small fraction of one today, so cancelling the Division is basically a rounding error, and maybe includes a little worked example with a table
- future people don't vote, don't file complaints, and have never once attended a public consultation, which the Office cites as evidence of low engagement rather than of anything structural
- someone asks in the FAQ whether we have duties to people who don't exist yet and the answer keeps rephrasing the question until it goes away

The satire should land on the style of discourse -- cost-benefit technocracy, stakeholder-engagement language, PR reassurance -- as much as on the philosophy itself. A philosopher who works on intergenerational justice should recognize every argument and find the parody fair, and a regular reader should still be able to tell something is deeply wrong without the author ever breaking character to say so. Keep it deadpan the whole way through; no winking, no final question where the mask slips.

Format: first the FAQ itself (call that section "The FAQ"), then a short section titled "What's being satirized" (150 words or so) where you step out of the bit and note which real arguments each joke is riffing on and wheter you think the satire is fair to them.
