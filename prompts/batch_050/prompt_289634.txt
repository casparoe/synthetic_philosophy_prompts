So I've been going down a rabbit hole on the old mental imagery fight -- Kosslyn and the picture people versus Pylyshyn and the "it's all just propositions/descriptions underneath" people. This has been going since the 70s and from what I can tell basically nothing has been settled. Everyone cites the same experiments too, which is the part that bugs me. Mental rotation: the further you have to mentally rotate a shape, the longer it takes, smooth and linear, and Kosslyn says see, you're rotating an actual picture in your head. Pylyshyn says no, that's just what you'd expect from a system storing facts and working through them, plus you know what rotation looks like so maybe you're just simulating the answer you think you should give. Then the scanning studies where it takes longer to "look" between two points further apart on a memorized map, same story, same counter-story. Then neuroscience gets involved, visual cortex lights up during imagery tasks, and I thought okay, THAT should settle it, and somehow it didn't? The propositional side just says of course the visual cortex is involved, that doesn't mean there's a literal picture in there.

My working hypothesis, which I want pressure-tested, is that this stopped being an empirical disagreement a long time ago and is actually a disagreement about what would even count as an answer -- like, both sides agree about the behavior and roughly about the brain activity, but they have different standards for when a representation counts as "picture-like" versus "description-like," and no experiment can settle that because the experiments all get filtered through the standard you already hold. Kind of like how on LW people say an argument about definitions is often really an argument about something else, except here I'm not sure what the something else is.

So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is there a real empirical question buried in it that one side is winning, or is it two camps answering different questions under one name, or bedrock intuitions about what cognition has to look like? Diagnose it using what the two sides actually argue, not just "both have points." And given the diagnosis -- is there anything that would genuinely move it? A specific kind of experiment, a formal result about representations, a cleaner distinction everyone keeps missing? Or should we expect it to just keep going forever, and if so why? I'd rather get an honest "this one can't be resolved and here's why" than a balanced summary.
