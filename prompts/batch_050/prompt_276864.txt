I have no training in philosophy, but I've been chewing on something for a while and I'd like help organizing it. The thought is this: whether someone counts as "knowing" a thing seems to drift depending on what rides on it. If a friend asks me whether the pharmacy stocks his insulin and nothing much is at stake, I'd say I know it does, because I was there Tuesday. If he's about to skip his dose and drive forty minutes on my say-so, I suddenly want to say I'd better check first — same memory, same evidence, different verdict. I've noticed people around me disagree sharply about what to make of this: some say knowledge itself changes with the stakes, some say only what it's proper to claim changes, some say "knows" works like "tall" and shifts with who's talking, and some say one of the two verdicts is simply correct and the other is just loose talk.

Please build me a sorting instrument — a decision tree or quiz of eight to ten questions — that takes someone from sharing my initial intuition to a named position. Each question must be a concrete case, not a request to pick a label: use my pharmacy case, and construct others along these lines: two people with identical evidence where only one stands to lose something; a case where the stakes are enormous but the person's evidence is also excellent (does the urgency still defeat the knowledge?); and a case where a bystander, not the actor, asserts "she knows" while the actor denies it. Include at least one question that separates "the stakes change whether you know" from "the stakes change only what it's right to say," and one that separates views where truth varies with the speaker's situation from views where two speakers can disagree without either being wrong.

End with a scoring key or set of leaves mapping answer patterns onto the named positions (at minimum: a flat-footed view that stakes are irrelevant, a skeptical view that raises the bar for everyone, a speaker-relative view, a subject-relative view, and a no-fault disagreement view). Flag any pair of positions the instrument cannot tell apart, and note any answer combination that no one actually holds, with a sentence diagnosing whether it's incoherent or merely unpopular.
