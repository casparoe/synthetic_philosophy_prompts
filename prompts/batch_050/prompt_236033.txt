I chair the scientific advisory board of a private research institute, and at next month's meeting we must decide whether to renew the five-year grant for a research programme we have funded for eleven years. The programme is built around a single core theory; its defenders point to a steady stream of publications and a recent headline result. Its critics, invoking Lakatos, argue it is a textbook degenerating research programme: every anomalous finding has been absorbed by post hoc adjustments to the protective belt while the hard core stays untouched, and the headline result turns out to be a retrodiction — the theory was fitted to data the group already possessed, so it was never at risk. A faction of younger researchers wants to keep the funding but split off and modify the hard core itself, which the programme director calls defection and the faction calls a progressive problemshift. Please draft the decision memo I will circulate before the meeting: it must recommend one of renew, terminate, or renew with defined conditions; state concretely what would have to be true in two years' time to justify continuing; and give an honest account of the strongest argument on the losing side — including whether Lakatos's own framework, given that degenerating programmes can stage comebacks, actually licenses defunding one, and whether the faction's proposal counts as continuing the programme or abandoning it. Maximum 700 words.
