So I've been going back and forth about this with a friend for weeks (it started over a film where someone leaves their spouse for a "better" person, don't ask which one) and I've realised I have a bunch of intuitions about love and reasons that I'm not sure actually fit together. We're covering Frankfurt, Velleman and Kolodny in a reading group I'm sitting in on, and I keep nodding along to all of them, which is probably a bad sign. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) I love the particular people I love partly because of their qualities -- her wit, his patience, whatever. If you listed the qualities out and none of them resonated, I think you'd conclude I didn't really love them at all. So the qualities genuinely figure among my reasons; whom I love isn't arbitrary.
(b) Relatedly, if someone I loved permanently lost the qualities the love was built on -- say brain damage left them cruel and dull where they were kind and sharp -- I think continued romantic love would cease to be rational, however much I might keep caring for them. Love should be responsive to the truth about the beloved.
(c) "Trading up" is a betrayal. If I met someone who had every quality I love my partner for, only more of each, leaving for them would be wrong, full stop. The better version of your partner is not your partner.
(d) My love for my (hypothetical) kids wouldn't depend on their qualities at all. Whatever they turned out like, I'd love them. That seems obviously right and also totally different in shape from (b), but I want to say both.
(e) Whatever the reasons for love turn out to be, they have to be genuine reasons -- things I could in principle defend as making love appropriate, not just psychological causes that happen to produce the feeling in me.

My suspicion, which I can't quite pin down, is that (b) and (c) are doing more damage to each other than the rest, but I might be wrong about that. Which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like it? And if something has to give, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me -- i.e. which of my intuitions about the cases (the brain damage case, the trading-up case) do I have to abandon or re-describe? Please be concrete about what commits me to what rather than just gesturing at "appraisal vs bestowal" as if labelling it settles anything.

I don't want a contested position presented back to me as settled wisdom, and equally I don't want to be told "philosophers disagree about everything" if some of this is actually common ground.
