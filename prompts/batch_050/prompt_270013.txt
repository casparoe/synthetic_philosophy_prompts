For several years I have kept notes on a recurring pattern in my own behavior for which I cannot find a proper term. When I open my phone to check the weather and resurface forty minutes later having watched a string of short videos, I do not feel that I was overpowered or tricked. At each moment, continuing felt like my own choice, and even in retrospect — knowing that the app was engineered to produce precisely this outcome — the memory retains the shape of a series of free decisions rather than of a single extraction of my time. The same thing happens when a streaming service starts the next episode automatically: I cannot recall deciding to keep watching, but I also cannot recall deciding against it; the decision simply never occurred, and yet the watching feels like something I did. This seems to me distinct from ordinary weakness of will, since in the moment I had no opposing judgment that I failed to act on; distinct from deception, since nothing was hidden from me; and distinct from compulsion, since I could have stopped at any point and knew it. Is there an established name for this condition, in which one's sense of authorship over one's actions remains fully intact even though the environment did most of the authoring? I am not after a bare label: I would like to know how it relates to the nearest recognized concepts, which of them it actually fits, and where its boundaries lie — whether, for instance, the same thing happens when a supermarket's layout steers my purchases, or when a friend's well-timed flattery steers my agreement. If nothing established fits, what would you call it, and how would you define its edge cases?
