My best friend from college has been in a PhD program for six years and it's clearly destroying him. He hasn't published anything, his advisor barely answers emails, he cries after committee meetings, and every time we talk he says some version of "I should have left three years ago." So last week I sat him down and laid it all out: the stipend is poverty wages, the academic job market is a lottery, he has skills that would get him hired tomorrow in industry, and by his own admission he's miserable. He agreed with literally every single point. Nodded along, said "yeah, all true." And then said he's staying.

This is what's bugging me. When I said "you have every reason to quit," he said something like: I get that those are good reasons *for you*, or for the person I'd become if I left, but wanting to finish this thing is just who I am, and no list of facts is going to make leaving feel like *my* option. And honestly I didn't know what to say back. Because he does know all the facts — he's not confused, not misinformed, he's thought about it more than I have. So is there actually a sense in which he "has a reason" to quit even though nothing in his current psychology is hooked up to it? Or does a reason only count as a reason if it can actually get a grip on the person somehow, and I'm just browbeating him with facts that are true but inert?

Part of me thinks: if reasons only count when they connect to something someone already wants or cares about, then rational persuasion is kind of an illusion — you can't really argue anyone into anything they aren't already half-committed to, you can only remind them of what they already value. Another part of me thinks that can't be right, because people do sometimes get talked into things, and also because it seems to let my friend off the hook for what looks like plain stubbornness dressed up as self-knowledge. So, practically speaking: is there anything left for me to say to him that isn't just repeating the facts louder? And philosophically: when I told him he has every reason to leave, was I saying something true, or something confused?
