Audit that analogy by mapping its principal correspondences, distinguishing the disanalogies that matter from those that do not, and examining its assumptions about intentions, capabilities, credibility and escalation. Include what I call the counterfactual burden: the need to compare not only what followed compromise, but also what would plausibly have followed confrontation, including its costs and risks. Then describe two hypothetical crises with the same surface resemblance to Munich but different hidden variables, such that the analogy supports opposite policies. Please answer in exactly five numbered points of one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion.
