I am a retired experimental physicist — forty years in laser spectroscopy — who has lately joined a philosophy reading group, and I would like help settling a dispute I witnessed last week. Please take on the role of a philosopher of physics specializing in the metaphysics of time. Before passing judgment, think carefully: first reconstruct each move below in its strongest form, then evaluate them one by one.

The topic was whether special relativity, via the relativity of simultaneity, establishes the "block universe" — the view that past, present, and future events are all equally real.

Sofia opened with the familiar scenario: two pedestrians passing each other on Earth have hyperplanes of simultaneity tilted differently enough that, relative to one, the Andromedan council's decision to dispatch an invasion fleet lies in the settled past, while relative to the other it lies in the future. Since what is real can hardly depend on how a distant pedestrian happens to stroll, she concluded, everything everywhere and everywhen must be equally real.

Martin replied that this equivocates: "simultaneous with me now" is frame-relative, so nothing absolute follows from it, and the only becoming-structure anyone has made precise that respects relativistic symmetries is pointwise — an event is determinate only relative to events in its future light cone — which yields no global block.

Sofia then offered a chaining argument. Event A, though spacelike separated from me, is simultaneous with me-now in some frame; an observer at A, suitably moving, finds my future simultaneous with them. If reality transfers along simultaneity within a frame — and it must, she said, unless "the present" shrinks to a single point-event, which is absurd — then my own future is real now.

Martin gave two responses. First, simultaneity across different frames is not transitive, so the chain is invalid; each link silently switches foliation. Second, even granting the chain, the argument secretly requires determinism: the fleet's launch being "already real" presupposes a fixed future, whereas if quantum outcomes are genuinely open, relativity constrains the geometry of becoming without settling whether anything becomes. He added that a privileged foliation is empirically undetectable, so relativity simply underdetermines the metaphysics.

Sofia answered that even under indeterminism relativity forces the future to be at least as real as the distant present — one cannot coherently hold the Andromedan event real for nobody at all — yielding at worst an open block, not presentism; and that an undetectable preferred frame adds far more structure than the block ever did.

They asked me to adjudicate, and I found myself swayed by each in turn. Please assess each move: Who is right about whether frame-relative simultaneity supports an absolute conclusion? Is the chaining argument valid? Does indeterminism undermine the argument? What, if anything, are both parties missing? Who is more right overall? Finally, indicate how confident you are in each verdict, and single out the one point in your assessment about which you are least certain.
