I've been reading about metaphor on my own, and I've run into what looks like a flat contradiction between two famous positions. Donald Davidson argues that a metaphor has no metaphorical meaning at all — it means only what its words literally mean, and nothing more. George Lakoff and Mark Johnson, by contrast, argue that metaphor is not just a matter of words but of thought itself: our ordinary abstract concepts, like time, arguments, and emotions, are structured metaphorically through and through. But if Davidson is right that metaphors mean nothing beyond the literal, and Lakoff and Johnson are right that abstract thinking is built out of metaphors, doesn't it follow that most of our abstract thought is meaningless? Since that conclusion seems absurd, one of the two views presumably has to go. I lean toward keeping Davidson, since his claim at least seems precise. Am I really forced to choose between them, and if so, which side should I drop, and why?
