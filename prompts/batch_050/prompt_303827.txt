Here’s an argument I made that seems to prove too much:

1. A shared factual world depends on institutions that let people check claims, not on every political claim being true.
2. A political lie either remains answerable to those institutions or tries to exempt itself from them.
3. If it remains answerable, being exposed can strengthen the practices of correction and accountability.
4. If it exempts itself, it is no longer an ordinary lie but an attempt to redefine reality, so classifying it merely as “lying” misses what is distinctive.
5. Political action starts something new, and political statements are often partly performative: they help bring about facts rather than simply report prior ones.
6. Therefore political lies are either corrigible and potentially useful, or not really lies. Treating political lying as a special threat to the common world, as Arendt does, is a category mistake.

That conclusion seems absurd, partly because versions of it would excuse ordinary fraud and state propaganda too. Where exactly does the argument go wrong? Identify the step, explain why it seemed plausible, and price the main alternatives. If forced, I’d rather keep premise 5 than premise 3, but tell me what that costs.
