Something I've noticed in myself and in friends, and I want to know if there's an established name for it, because "self-deception" doesn't quite seem to fit. Here's the pattern: someone sincerely judges everyone else's situation correctly but genuinely misjudges their own identical situation -- and the kicker is that they'd judge their own case correctly too if it were presented to them as someone else's. Concrete example: a friend of mine is writing a PhD thesis. He hasn't touched it in months, but he insists he's not avoiding it, he's "waiting until he has a full week free to really get into it." Meanwhile another friend described her situation to him -- almost literally the same situation -- and he immediately said, kindly but clearly, that she was procrastinating and should just work in small chunks. He wasn't being a hypocrite, I really believe that; he just didn't see his own case as the same case. And here's the part that interests me: if you anonymized his own calendar and showed it to him as a stranger's, I'm fairly confident he'd diagnose it instantly as avoidance.

So it's not lying -- he believes what he says about himself. And it's not quite what people usually mean by self-deception, because as I understand the puzzle there, the person is supposed to be somehow keeping the truth from themselves, motivated not to look at it. But my friend isn't avoiding looking. He'll talk about his thesis situation for hours, he keeps perfect track of how little he's written, all the evidence is right there and he handles it fine -- he just files it under the wrong concept when it's his. It's like the misidentification happens at the labeling step, not the evidence step. Is there a name for this specific configuration -- where the failure is in applying a concept to your own case rather than in hiding or distorting the facts? How does it relate to self-deception properly so called, and to things like wishful thinking or hypocrisy, which it doesn't seem to be either? And if the literature doesn't carve exactly here, what would you call it, and where would you draw its edges -- for instance, does it still count if the person couldn't diagnose their own case even anonymized?
