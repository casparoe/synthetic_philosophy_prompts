My friend did a PhD in evolutionary biology and now loves giving snap verdicts on species cases. So far her calls are: horses and donkeys are different species (mules are sterile, after all), a Chihuahua and a Great Dane are the same species, and the ensatina salamanders forming a ring around California's Central Valley are one species even though the forms at the two ends of the ring can't interbreed where they meet. New case: two bird populations on seperate islands that have had no contact for ages, differ a bit in plumage and song, but produce healthy fertile offspring when crossed in an aviary. Will she say one species or two? I'm less interested in the bare answer than in what rule she seems to be running, which other rules fit all three of her verdicts just as well but diverge on the island birds, and what further case would settle which rule is really hers.
