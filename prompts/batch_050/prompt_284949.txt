So I picked up this pop-physics book on quantum foundations (won't name it, it's one of the big bestseller ones) and it has a sidebar on the psi-ontic vs. psi-epistemic debate. It goes more or less like this:

"Quantum mechanics never tells us what a particle is doing, only what the wavefunction is doing -- so what IS the wavefunction? On the 'psi-ontic' view, the wavefunction is a real physical thing out in the world, a field spread through space, as real as the electromagnetic field. On the 'psi-epistemic' view, it's nothing physical at all -- it's just a summary of what we happen to know or believe about the system, pure information in the observer's head, so two people who know different things can assign totally different wavefunctions to the same atom and both be right. Einstein preferred something in this spirit, since he thought quantum mechanics was incomplete. For decades this was left to taste. But in 2011 the PBR theorem settled the question: it proved mathematically that the wavefunction cannot be merely a state of knowledge, so psi-epistemic interpretations are ruled out, full stop -- which is also why hidden-variable completions of quantum mechanics are no longer taken seriously."

Now, I'm inclined to trust this because the rest of the book seemed careful, but two things bug me. First, I follow some foundations people on twitter and I'm pretty sure QBism-types still exist and talk about the wavefunction as information, so "ruled out, full stop" feels too strong. Second, the bit about hidden variables at the end feels stapled on, like they conflated two debates.

So: how fair is this summary? Go through it and sort each claim into: accurate, defensible simplification, or wrong. In particular -- does PBR actually show what they say it shows, and what exactly does it assume? Is the book's description of the psi-epistemic view the one its defenders would recognize? Does anything about hidden-variable theories follow? And if you had to rewrite the last two sentences to be defensible, what would they say?
