Write the FAQ page for the Ministry of Aggregation in a small country whose constitution is, literally, Harsanyi's social aggregation theorem: every public descision must maximize a weighted sum of citizens' cardinal utilities, each citizen's utility function is elicited once a year from their choices between lotteries at the national Calibration Festival, and the weights are set by an Impartial Observer whose entire job is imagining being each citizen with equal probability. Deadpan civil-service voice, around 600 words, the Ministry believes every word and never winks. Cover at least: what happens to citizens whose lottery choices keep violating teh independence axiom (there's a Rectification Retreat, the waiting list is long), why the Ministry awarded this year's entire grain harvest to one citizen by coin flip and why all complaints about ex post inequality "misunderstand the theorem," how the Bureau of Preference Intensity officially establishes that one person's toothache outweighs another person's broken leg, and how the current Impartial Observer responds to accusations that she imagines being cheerful citizens more vividly than sullen ones. The satire should land the real objections -- actual human preferences are a mess that violates the axioms in systematic Allais-ish ways, a rule that only sees expected utilities can't distinguish a fair lottery from a rigged outcome (Diamond's complaint), the weights basically come from nowhere, and nobody has ever actually compared utils across skulls -- but only by enacting those objections, never by naming them. Someone who works on social choice theory should be able to tell you know exactly what's being skewered.
