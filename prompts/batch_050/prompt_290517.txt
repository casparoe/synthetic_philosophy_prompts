So I've been chewing on this for a while, no philosophy training, just reading and thinking on my own, and I want my position properly stress-tested. I basically buy the idea that something being good or valuable just means there are reasons to want it, admire it, choose it, whatever the right attitude is — value is nothing over and above fitting attitudes. The obvious problem everyone raises: the evil demon who'll torture me unless I admire him. I have a real reason to admire him (I want to live!) but he's obviously not admirable. My move, which I arrived at by going back and fourth on this for months: there's a difference between reasons that come from what the object is actually like (he's cruel, vain, boring) and reasons that come from what having the attitude would do for me (admiring him keeps me alive). The demon's threat is the second kind, so it was never the kind of reason that could make someone admirable. Problem dissolved, no extra machinery, the account survives.

Now: what would count against this? Things I've worried about myself: maybe you can't draw the line between the two kinds of reason without already knowing what's admirable, so my account smuggles in the value it was supposed to explain. Maybe there are attitudes where the line goes fuzzy — reasons to love someone seem to be about the person and about the relationship at once. And a rival could say value is basic and fittingness is downstream of it, then ask what my view even adds. Which of these actually bites? What would a smart defender of my position concede as genuine trouble versus shrug off? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying, and be honest about whether my dissolution strategy risks anything at all or whether I've insulated it so well it can't lose — and whether that's a vice.
