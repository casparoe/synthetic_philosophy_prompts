I'm a novelist, about 60k words into a book whose whole engine is a character I might have built on sand. She's this hyper-perceptive woman who claims she can tell what people actually want -- not what they say they want -- just by watching what they do. What they order, who they call back, what they flinch at. The plot hinges on her being right about everyone except one person, and that failure wrecking her. But lately I've started worrying the premise doesn't even make sense, that there is no fact of the matter she could be reading off, and if the premise is incoherent the twist is unearned.

So before I read any of the actual literature on this (inverse reinforcement learning, revealed preference, whatever the psychologists call it), I want to know what I should be asking of it. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of inferring someone's preferences or values from their observed behavior has to deliver, with the case behind each. Stuff I can already see it needs to handle, though you should push past me -- it has to say something about the person who does the polite thing while wanting the opposite, about habits nobody would endorse, about the gap between what I'd choose at 2am and what I'd choose at a job interview, about behavior that's performance for an audience, and about self-deception where the agent themself couldn't report the motive. It also has to face the problem that apparently any behavior is consistent with lots of different preference-sets if you're free enough about the beliefs and constraints you attribute -- my character treats this as a solvable puzzle and I suspect it isn't, or isn't always.

For each requirement, say whether it's a genuine constraint any theory must meet or just the taste of one research tradition, and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- if some subset of my wishlist is mathematically or conceptually impossible, that's the part I most want, because it might actually fix my plot rather than break it. Then briefly, and I mean briefly, how the main contenders do against the list: classical revealed-preference economics, inverse RL with its usual assumption that people are roughly rational-plus-noise, and the more hermeneutic idea that motives only exist once someone narrates them. The criteria are the point, the scorecard is for me.
