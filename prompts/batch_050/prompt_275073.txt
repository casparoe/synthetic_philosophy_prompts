Ok so this came out of an argument I had with my uncle over christmas. He's convinced that countries basically have personalities, and his favorite example is the Greek debt crisis around 2010-2015 -- the whole narrative you saw in the German and international press at the time of the hardworking, disciplined northerners versus the allegedly lazy, profligate Greeks who needed to be taught a lesson via Troika austerity. The thing is, I remember reading that OECD data on actual working hours showed Greeks working among the longest annual hours in Europe, considerably more than Germans, which doesn't fit the story at all. So the stereotype that was doing so much rhetorical work in justifying austerity seems to be factually upside down, at least on the hours dimension.

Analyze this episode through the lens of the concept of national character. I want the analysis to run in both directions. On the one hand: what does the episode reveal about how national character claims actually function in political discourse -- are they causal explanations, moralized just-so stories, coordination devices for assigning blame, something else? Where does the "lazy Greeks" framing come apart (hours worked vs. productivity vs. institutions vs. tax collection), and does the fact that a national-character claim turned out to be empirically backwards tell us something general about how such claims get formed and what they're responsive to? On the other hand: take the episode as a test case for whether "national character" is a coherent notion at all. Could a defender of the concept rescue it here -- e.g., by distinguishing behavior from dispositions, or individuals from institutions, or by saying the stereotype was just a bad application of an otherwise fine concept? And separately, even if some generalizations about national populations are statistically defensible (say, measurable differences in trust levels or savings rates), is there anything morally or epistemically different about using them in a policy context like the bailout negotiations versus using them in everyday life? Tell me where you think the concept survives this case study and where the case study breaks it.
