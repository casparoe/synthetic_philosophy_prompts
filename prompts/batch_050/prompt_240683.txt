I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep getting stuck on arguments that relativity supposedly proves the block universe, especially the Rietdijk-Putnam style argument. From what I understand, special relativity says that for two events that are spacelike separated, different inertial frames can disagree about which one is earlier, or whether they're simultaneous. So some distant Andromeda event can count as already real in one frame, not yet real in another, and because no frame is privileged, the argument says we should treat all events in spacetime as equally real.

But then I read the responses and it feels like everyone is talking past eachother. Some people attack the premise that what is simultaneous with me must be real. Some say no privileged foliation doesn't imply eternalism. Others defend a growing block but seem to need some preferred way of slicing spacetime, which sounds suspiciously like what relativity ruled out. Others say existence is frame-relative, which I can barely parse because I'm not sure whether that's still eternalism, presentism with extra steps, or just changing what exists means.

As an engineer, the whole thing reminds me of distributed systems. Special relativity sounds like there is no global clock, only a partial causal ordering, and different observers are like nodes making different consistent snapshots of the same event graph. But the block universe conclusion seems to add: since no snapshot is privileged, the entire graph must already exist. In a distributed system, that inference would be suspect. Lack of a globally agreed snapshot does not mean every event that will ever occur is already sitting somewhere in the execution. At the same time, I realize physics might be saying something stronger than the engineering analogy because spacetime itself is the thing being modeled, not messages between processes.

So don't give me another generic survey of presentism, growing block theory, and eternalism. Diagnose why this particular debate keeps stalling. Separate what follows from special relativity itself from the extra metaphysical premises in the Rietdijk-Putnam argument. Is the disagreement mainly about:

1. whether reality has to be frame-invariant,
2. what words like exists, real, and present mean,
3. whether simultaneity can transmit ontological status across a chain of events,
4. or two different projects being conflated, one about the structure of spacetime and one about temporal experience?

Also, say where my distributed-systems analogy actually helps and where it breaks. Does the causal partial order in relativity make the analogy deeper than a loose metaphor, or does it hide the crucial difference between an incomplete record of events and the events themselves?

Finally, tell me what would count as progress. Would a formal result showing that no Lorentz-invariant ontology can privilege only a proper subset of spacetime events settle anything? Would a preferred foliation in some future physics settle it, or would that just show the physics had changed? And if neither experiment nor formalism can decide between the remaining views, is that because the debate is ultimately verbal, or because there is a real metaphysical disagreement with no empirical test? I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not a reading list or a victory speech for one camp.
