I'm preparing a position piece for a workshop on low-impact AI -- the project of getting capable agents to avoid side effects without handing them a full specification of human values -- and before I submit it I'd like my commitments audited for coherence. Here is where I've landed:

(1) Impact should be measured against a stepwise inaction baseline: at each point, compare the actual trajectory against the one in which the agent had done nothing, so that the agent can't secure a lenient baseline by first shaping the very world it will later be measured against.

(2) The right yardstick is change in attainable utility: an action counts as high-impact to the extent that, afterward, a broad range of possible goals would become easier or harder to achieve than under the baseline -- in either direction.

(3) Reversibility excuses: an agent that fully undoes its changes has, on net, had no impact and should emerge from the episode with no accumulated penalty.

(4) A correctly penalized agent should still be directable toward useful, irreversible everyday work -- making coffee, driving someone to the airport -- without freezing up or requiring task-by-task exemptions written into the penalty.

(5) None of this requires saying what humans actually care about: the penalty can be defined entirely over a sufficiently broad sample of arbitrary, non-human goals, and that is precisely the appeal of the approach.

Please audit the set rather than review the literature. Identify which pairs of commitments genuinely conflict and which only appear to. For each genuine conflict, state what would have to give and what it would cost, and where two commitments merely look in tension, show briefly why the tension dissolves on inspection. Order your findings by severity, and close by naming the single revision that restores the most consistency at the lowest price. I have not decided which commitment I'd surrender, so don't steer me toward one. Keep the entire audit under 700 words.
