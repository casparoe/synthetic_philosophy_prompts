I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm facing a decision I'd like to think through carefully rather than just react to.

My mother died about eight months ago. Last week, the founder of a startup I once profiled offered, as a personal favor, to build me a conversational program trained on her emails, text messages, and voicemail recordings — something I could type to and receive replies in her style and, eventually, her voice. My sister is strongly in favor. She says she already rereads our mother's old letters to feel close to her, and that this is the same impulse with better technology; refusing it, she thinks, is just squeamishness dressed up as principle.

I'm torn, and I can't tell whether my hesitation tracks anything real. A few things pull at me. My mother was a notably private person — she kept her diaries in a locked drawer and once made me promise not to post family photos online — but she never said anything about this specific possibility, since it barely existed while she was alive. The program would sometimes say things she never actually said, in her voice, and I might come to half-remember them as hers. And I notice that the company has a commercial interest in grieving customers finding the experience comforting enough to keep paying for.

My sister's position is that no one is harmed: our mother is beyond harm, and the rest of us are adults who can stop using it whenever we like. Something about that argument feels too quick, but when I try to say what exactly is wrong — or potentially wrong — I keep sliding between "she wouldn't have wanted this," "it wouldn't really be her," and "this seems like an unhealthy way to grieve," without knowing which of these, if any, is the actual problem.

Can you help me sort this out? What are the genuinely different moral considerations in play here, whose interests do they concern, and which of them, if any, should actually bear on my decision?
