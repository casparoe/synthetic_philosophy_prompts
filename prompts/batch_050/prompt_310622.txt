I've been rereading Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment -- the two-level system in which vast numbers of tiny "simmballs" rattle around and interact purely according to low-level dynamics, while at the coarse-grained level their large-scale configurations constitute "simms," symbol-like entities whose interactions can be described by tidy symbol-level rules of their own. Hofstadter's suggestive answer to the question of what shoves what around inside a system like this (or a brain) is that, in some sense, the symbols do the shoving: the high-level description isn't just a convenient summary but identifies where the causal action really is. After arguing about this with a couple of friends from a cognitive science reading group, I tried to write down exactly what I believe, and I'd like an audit of whether the package is jointly tenable.

Here are my commitments: (1) Causal closure at the bottom: every simmball's trajectory is fully fixed by the microphysical laws together with the prior micro-state; nothing at the micro-level is left over for anything else to do. (2) Symbol realism by identity: simms are nothing over and above patterns of simmballs -- each token symbol just is some configuration of the substrate, not an extra ingredient. (3) Genuine downward causation: symbols don't merely redescribe the action, they cause things -- a symbol-level state pushes the substrate around, and a system in which the symbol level turned out to be causally inert would be a system in which there are no symbols at all. (4) Autonomy of the symbol level: symbols are multiply realizable across different simmball arrangements, and the symbol-level generalizations (the rules describing how simms interact) are robust and explanatory in a way that no micro-level description can capture, which is part of what makes the symbol level the locus of causation rather than a mere bookkeeping device. (5) Semantic causal relevance: what makes a simm the symbol it is partly its content -- what it's about, which depends on the system's history and environment -- and symbols qua bearers of that content figure in causal explanations of the system's behavior.

What I want is a consistency check, not an exposition of the literature. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; I'm fairly sure at least one apparent conflict here dissolves on inspection, and I'd like it identified rather than flagged alongside the rest. For each real tension, spell out precisely what commits me to what -- for instance, which reading of "pushes the substrate around" in (3) is compatible with (1) and (2) and which is not -- and say how severe the problem is. Rank the tensions from most to least serious, and for each one state what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if dropped or weakened, would clean up the most, without presuming that's the one I'd want to sacrifice.
