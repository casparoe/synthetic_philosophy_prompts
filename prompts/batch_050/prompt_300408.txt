So I've been reading up on the sphex wasp stuff and free will (the idea that the wasp keeps doing the same prey-dragging loop even if you move the cricket, so its "intelligence" is really just a rigid program) and I'm trying to pin down my own views for a paper. Here's what I currently think, roughly in order of how attached I am to it:

(a) What makes humans importantly different from the wasp is flexibility and self-monitoring -- we can notice our own routines and revise them. Whatever free will we have basically consists in that.

(b) I'm a compatibilist. Determinism being true wouldn't threaten free will, since freedom is about how your actions flow from you, not about whether things could have gone otherwise.

(c) Skilled automaticity is no threat to freedom and often the opposite. A great pianist on autopilot, or someone whose generosity is just second nature by now, seems to me more free in those moments, not less. Turning reflective capacities into habit is what a well-lived life looks like.

(d) Someone who was conditioned from birth by a manipulative cult so that they always, unhesitatingly hand over their money whenever the leader asks -- never deliberates, never resists, feels totally content about it -- is not free and not responsible for those handovers. Doesn't matter that the behavior flows smoothly from their settled character.

(e) Deliberation is basically always freedom-enhancing. All else equal, the more you step back and reflect on what you're doing, the freer you are.

My question: do these actually hang together? I'm not asking you to referee compatibilism as a whole, just whether this specific set is internally coherent. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and if something has to give, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me? Please rank any tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and don't assume I know where the problem is -- I genuinely don't. Also, where you flag something as a tension, be honest about whether that's a settled point people who work on this agree on or whether it's still contested territory, rather than presenting a contested take as the consensus view.
