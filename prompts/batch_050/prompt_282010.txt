You are a philosopher specializing in early modern epistemology. I am trying to pin down exactly what the cogito proves by reporting my verdicts on a battery of cases, and I want the principle behind them. Think carefully; I suggest sketching two or three candidate principles before committing to one.

My verdicts:

1. Doubting everything while seated by the fire: "I am thinking, so I exist" is rock solid. It proves I exist now.
2. The same thought under the hypothesis of a maximally powerful deceiver: still solid. Being deceived is a way of thinking, so the deceiver would have to make me exist in order to fool me at all.
3. The syllogistic reconstruction — everything that thinks exists; I think; therefore I exist — feels much weaker, since the major premise is exactly the kind of general claim the doubt was meant to suspend.
4. Lichtenberg's deflationary rewrite, on which the cogito licenses only "thinking is occurring" and not "I exist": I want to reject this. The cogito seems to establish a subject, not merely an event — though I cannot say what entitles the "I."
5. "I am thinking, so I existed yesterday" or "so I will exist tomorrow": invalid. The cogito proves nothing about persistence.
6. "I am walking, so I exist": invalid. I could be dreaming the walking in a way I could not be dreaming the thinking, since dreaming that I walk still involves thinking.
7. A parrot or a language model that produces the sentence "I think, therefore I am": proves nothing about it.
8. A dreamer who reasons "I think, therefore I am" inside a dream: the inference works; the dreamer exists even if everything dreamed is false.

The tension I cannot resolve: by case 2 the cogito survives a deceiver who controls my entire inner life, yet by case 7 merely producing those words proves nothing. If the deceiver can puppet my thought the way the model's operators puppet its output, why does my cogito succeed and the machine's fail? Cases 4 and 5 also pull against each other: if the cogito establishes a subject but only for an instant, in what sense is that subject me?

State the most precise principle you can — roughly of the form "the cogito establishes exactly X, for Y, so long as Z" — that captures as many of these verdicts as possible. Say which verdicts it must overturn if it cannot save them all. Finally, report what it predicts for a case I have not listed: a person with total anterograde amnesia finds, in their own handwriting, the sentence "I am thinking, therefore I am." Did that inscription ever prove anything, and does it prove anything to them now?
