Write a poem about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream that can be read in both directions. The setup, briefly: Zhuangzi dreams he is a butterfly, flitting about and content, with no awareness that he is Zhuangzi; he wakes, solidly himself again, and then cannot settle whether he is a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming it is a man. The passage ends not with an answer but with the thought that between Zhuangzi and the butterfly there must be a distinction — and that this is what is called the transformation of things.

The poem should be 15 or 17 unrhymed lines built around a single central line. Read top to bottom, it should recount the episode from the man's side: sleep, wings, waking, doubt. Read bottom to top, the very same lines should recount it from the butterfly's side — the waking becoming the onset of a dream, the doubt running the other way. The central line is the hinge: it must make grammatical and emotional sense as the last thing each side says before the reader crosses into the other, and it should carry the point that is easy to get wrong. This is not Cartesian dream skepticism, not a lament that we can never know what is real. It is the transformation of things: the distinction between man and butterfly is genuine, but neither identity owns it permanently. The hinge line should therefore assert a distinction while loosening its hold, rather than dissolve the distinction into a shrug about illusion.

Constraints to keep it honest: no rhyme and no fixed meter; no proper names anywhere in the poem except, optionally, at the hinge; at most two lines may rely on repunctuation between the two directions of reading, and no line may require rewording. The poem should hold up for a reader who knows the passage — such a reader should be able to point to where the transformation of things enters the form itself — while working as a poem for someone who has never heard of Zhuangzi.

After the poem, add a final line beginning "Hinge:" that states in one sentence what the central line means in each direction of reading.
