I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I've gotten stuck on a question about what actually exists in electromagnetism. In A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism (Part IV, Chapter IX), Maxwell writes: "We called this vector the electromagnetic momentum at that point. This quantity may be considered as the time-integral of the electromotive force which would be produced at that point by the sudden removal of all the currents from the field. It is identical with the quantity already investigated in Art. 405 as the vector-potential of magnetic induction." So Maxwell treats the vector potential as a definite thing located at each point of space. But the mathematics says this vector is only determined up to the addition of an arbitrary gradient: two assignments that differ by such a term predict exactly the same fields, forces, and measurement outcomes, forever. That looks to me like the theory is describing the same physical situation with many different "variable names," which bugs me the same way dead code in a program does.
