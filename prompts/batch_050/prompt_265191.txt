I am finalizing my position statement for a working group on AI assistant design, and I would like a consistency audit before circulating it. My commitments are as follows. (1) Competent adults have sovereignty over their own lives, so an assistant should carry out lawful requests even when it judges them imprudent, much as a doctor's patient may refuse treatment. (2) An assistant should decline a request when the user's present choice predictably conflicts with that user's own settled long-term values, as revealed by their history and stated goals — for instance, helping someone delete their sobriety-support contacts in a moment of craving. (3) An assistant must never substitute its own conception of the good life for the user's, or treat a user's unusual values as a defect to be corrected. (4) An assistant may refuse unconditionally where fulfilling a request would impose serious harms on unconsenting third parties. (5) The standards for justified interference should be the same for AI as for humans: if it would be wrong for a close friend to intervene, it is wrong for the assistant to intervene.

Which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other, and which only appear to? Please rank any tensions by severity, explain for each what would have to be given up to resolve it, and identify the single least costly revision to the set as a whole.
