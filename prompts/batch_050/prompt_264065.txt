I write a long-form magazine covering science and ideas, and I'm working up a piece on dueling -- the formal, seconds-and-pistols kind practiced by European and American gentlemen and military officers from roughly the seventeenth through the nineteenth centuries, of which the Burr--Hamilton affair of 1804 is the most famous American instance. For the salient facts, assume the standard picture: duels were fought over insults to honor; refusal of a challenge, or of an insult, could end a military or political career by branding a man a coward; smoothbore dueling pistols were wildly inaccurate, so death rates were real but far from certain; elaborate norms (deloping, exchanging apologies on the field, letting seconds negotiate) allowed both parties to walk away with honor satisfied; and the institution faded out across the nineteenth century even as -- some would say because -- governments kept raising the legal penalties for participating.

Analyze this episode through the lens of costly signaling theory and Zahavi's handicap principle. Be disciplined about the mapping: identify the signaler, the audience(s), the quality being advertised (resolve? willingness to bear costs for reputation? social rank?), and explain on the theory why a *cheap* declaration of courage could never have done dueling's work. Then complicate it. If the whole point of the handicap principle is that cost keeps signals honest, why did the elaborate escape hatches -- the deloping norms, the reconciliations negotiated by seconds, the famously inaccurate weapons -- not collapse the signal into cheap talk? Does the low hit rate make a duel a genuine handicap or merely a theatrical one, and does the theory have the resources to distinguish those? Finally, run the analysis in reverse: costly signaling seems to predict that raising the penalty for dueling should have *strengthened* the signal by raising its cost, yet the institution died. Lay out the candidate explanations for that decline within the theory -- collapsing audiences, changing payoffs, the arrival of cheaper substitutes for establishing standing -- and say which you find most defensible. Where the theory is simply silent about some feature of the episode, say so rather than forcing it to speak, and close with a verdict on whether dueling is a showcase for the handicap principle, an anomaly it struggles with, or evidence that the theory was never really about institutionalized human rituals in the first place.
