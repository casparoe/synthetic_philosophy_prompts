hey so I'm a phd student in philosophy (decision theory / normative ethics broadly) and I need to put together a short research proposal -- like 1 to 1.5 pages -- for a small internal summer grant at my department. Three months of funding, so the scope has to be realistic.

The topic I want to work on is evidential cooperation in large worlds, but with a twist I haven't seen developed. Quick recap of the basic idea so we're on the same page: in a very large universe (or multiverse, or just a big cosmos) there are plausibly a huge number of agents whose decision-making is correlated with mine -- similar minds facing similar choice situations. On an evidentialist way of thinking, when I decide to, say, act altruistically rather than selfishly, that's evidence about what all those correlated agents are doing too, so my decision "controls" a lot more good or bad than what's in front of me. Hence a case for cooperation at cosmic scale even without any causal reach.

Here's the thing that bugs me. Almost all the informal discussion of this I've seen quietly assumes the correlated agents share my values, or at least that I'm reasoning about copies of myself. But realistically the agents correlated with me will have heterogeneous values: some are altruists like me, sure, but some are pure egoists, some have values I actively think are mistaken (say they care only about their own in-group), and the correlation between my decision and theirs presumably varies with how similar their reasoning and values are to mine. So the question I want the proposal to center on: how should value heterogeneity among your evidentially correlated "coparticipants" change what evidential cooperation recommends? I can see at least three candidate answers -- (a) only count correlation with agents whose values you endorse, (b) treat it as a bargaining problem and look for across-values trades where you partly advance values you reject in exchange for them advancing yours, (c) weight purely by correlation strength and ignore value content entirely, which seems to lead somewhere pretty weird. My hunch is (b) is the interesting one but it might collapse into (a) or (c) on inspection, I'm not sure.
