The exam question was:

"In the Sleeping Beauty problem, Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday. A fair coin is tossed: if heads she is woken on Monday only, if tails she is woken on Monday and Tuesday, with her memory of any Monday waking erased. Each time she wakes, what should her credence be that the coin landed heads? Defend either the 'halfer' or the 'thirder' answer, and respond to the strongest objection to your chosen view."

Here's the student's answer:

"The correct answer is the thirder position: Beauty's credence in heads on waking should be 1/3. This was David Lewis's view, against the halfers like Adam Elga. The argument is simple. When Beauty wakes she could be in one of three situations: heads-Monday, tails-Monday, or tails-Tuesday. Since the experiment is symmetric and she has no way to distinguish these situations, she should divide her credence equally among them, giving 1/3 to each, and only one involves heads.

The strongest objection is that the coin is fair and Beauty learns nothing new when she wakes — she knew in advance she'd be woken — so her credence in heads should stay at 1/2. But this fails, because Beauty plainly does learn something: she learns that she is awake now, which she couldn't have known on Sunday. Since she has new evidence, updating away from 1/2 is unproblematic.

A nice check on the thirder view is what happens if Beauty is told it's Monday. Given it's Monday, heads and tails are still equally likely, so the thirder should then have credence 1/3 in heads, which confirms the coherence of the view."
