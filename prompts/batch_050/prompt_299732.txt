I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, and I run a monthly reading group of smart generalists — a nurse, two software engineers, a high-school teacher, a lawyer. None of them have read the alignment literature; a couple have skimmed news coverage of chatbots. For next month's meeting I have forty-five minutes to teach one distinction, and I want a teaching plan, not the lesson itself: the difference between AI failure that comes from systems actively seeking influence (scheming, deception, accumulating power) and failure that comes from slow value drift, where nothing rebels and nothing is obviously stolen, but over years the institutions wrapped around these systems quietly stop optimizing for what we thought they optimized for.

Act as a veteran explainer of AI safety concepts who has taught skeptical non-specialists many times. Think carefully, and open with a short brainstorm about what makes this particular distinction hard to teach. Then give me the plan:

- The dependency order: what has to be in place before the influence-seeking mode is even intelligible — I suspect they need "systems can pursue instrumental subgoals" before "deception" lands — and, separately, what has to be in place before value drift registers as an AI risk rather than politics as usual.
- One carrying example per mode. For the drift half I'm tempted by a Goodhart-style story of a metric slowly eating an institution; tell me what that example must include, what it must exclude, and what the influence-seeking example should be so the two contrast cleanly rather than blur.
- The misconceptions they'll bring, and exactly where in the sequence to head each off. I'm expecting at least: AI risk means killer robots; drift is just how society has always changed, so why blame the technology; and a system that reliably gives me what I ask for can't be misaligned with me.
- A closing check that shows the distinction landed — for instance, whether they can take a novel scenario and classify it as one mode, the other, both, or neither, and defend the classification.
- What I should openly decline to explain at this level.

Write in American English.
