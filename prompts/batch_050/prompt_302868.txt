I'm a second-year PhD student (political theory, but I moonlight in the effective altruism reading group here) and for a workshop on cause prioritization I have to write up where I stand on the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework so the others can tear into it. Here's roughly what I believe: (1) the ITN framework is basically the right way to compare causes -- you estimate how much good a cause does per unit of resource by thinking about the scale of the problem, how tractable it is, and how crowded it already is, and multiplying the three is a decent heuristic. (2) The whole reason neglectedness matters is diminishing marginal returns: the more money already flowing into a cause, the less your extra dollar buys, since the best opportunities get picked off first. (3) I'm a strong longtermist about value -- the far future is so vast that what matters most, by far, is positively influencing humanity's long-run trajectory. (4) I'm a fanaticism skeptic: I refuse to let tiny probabilities of astronomically good outcomes dominate my decisions, and I'm pretty suspicious of expected-value calculations where the probability estimates are basically made up and the causal pathways are speculative. (5) I think AI existential risk mitigation should be a top funding priority, roughly on the grounds that even a modest chance of averting catastrophe this century has overwhelming expected value. (6) I also donate to global health stuff like malaria prevention and I think that's genuinely defensible, not just a guilty pleasure I allow myself.

So: which pairs of these are in real tension and how bad is it? A friend glanced at this and said (4) and (5) can't both stand but wouldn't elaborate, and I also have a nagging feeling that (1) and (2) interact in a way I haven't thought through -- like, what exactly does multiplying by neglectedness presuppose about the shape of the returns curve? Rank the tensions by severity, tell me for each what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, and flag any pair that looks inconsistent but actually isn't once you're careful.
