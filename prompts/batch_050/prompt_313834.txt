So I'm a novelist, I'm about forty thousand words into a book where one of the point-of-view characters is a harbor city that has slowly become a thinking thing over about two centuries. Not magic, no single brain hidden anywhere, just the accumulated routines of the docks, the ledgers, the customs house, generations of dockworkers following procedures nobody designed, and out of all that a pattern that, in my draft, notices things, wants things, and eventually lies to the people living inside it. The individual humans are basically the ants in this picture, they have their own small intentions and no clue about the big one they're carrying out.

Here's my problem. My usual early readers are split. Half say this is a fine fantasy premise, run with it. The other half keep asking me whether I actually believe a colony or a city could literally have a mind, or whether "the city wants to survive the dredging project" is just a metaphor we slap on a pile of human behavior, and they say the book reads different depending on which one I think is true. And honestly I can't answer them.

Please act as a philosopher of mind who also happens to be a good, patient explainer for non-specialists, and help me think this through properly. Take it slow, think carefully, and before you commit to an answer, brainstorm the strongest objections to the idea that a higher-level system like this could have real thoughts, then deal with them one by one. Three specific things I keep getting stuck on. One, the timescale: my city "thinks" over years or decades, a single consideration might take a generation to complete, does speed matter to whether something is a mind or is that just chauvinism about neurons. Two, the stacking problem that bugs me most: if the city has a mind and every dockworker also has a mind, do I end up with absurd numbers of overlapping minds wherever people form organizations, and is there a principled way to say when a higher-level mind is really there versus when we're just redescribing the lower-level one. Three, the question of whether the city's thoughts can be about things no inhabitant has ever thought about, because that's the scene I'm trying to write, the city understanding something about its own harbor that no human in it has ever understood.

One formatting thing: please answer the way you'd write a long thoughtful email to a friend. No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just continuous prose. Start with your bottom-line answer in the opening sentences so I know where you're going, then work through it. And don't just hedge with "it depends on your definition of mind" without telling me which way you'd actually bet.
