I'm a retired molecular biologist and since hanging up my lab coat I've been reading a lot of philosophy of mind, which is a humbling experience. Last month I read an article in a popular science magazine (I won't name it, but the sort of thing that lands in every dentist's waiting room) about the view that consciousness is a "user illusion", along the lines Dennett and the illusionists defend. The article summarised the position like this:

"According to these philosophers, consciousness doesn't exist. What you call your inner life -- the redness of red, the ache of a headache -- is a fiction your brain tells itself, the way a desktop icon is a fiction the computer shows you. There is nothing it is like to be you, any more than there is something it is like to be a thermostat. Pain isn't really painful; you only believe it is. The upshot is that the 'hard problem' evaporates: there was never anything to explain, and the whole mystery of consciousness is just an artefact of bad introspection."

Now, my gut reaction as a scientist is that this has to be a caricature -- nobody serious denies that headaches hurt, surely -- but I've been burned before by assuming a strawman is a strawman, and I know Dennett at least is nobody's fool. So I'd like an accuracy audit, please. Which parts of that paragraph are a fair rendering of the actual view, which parts are sloppy but forgivable pop-science compression, and which parts would make an actual illusionist wince? In particular: does the view really deny that there's something it's like to be me, or is it making a narrower claim about what my introspective reports misdescribe? And does the article's conclusion -- that there's "never anything to explain" -- actually follow from the view, or does the view itself admit there's still a sizeable explanatory job left (just a different one)?

If it helps: I'm not asking you to decide whether illusionism is true, just whether the summary is honest. And please write your answer in British English -- American spellings still grate on me after all these years.
