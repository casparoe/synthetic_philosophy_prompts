I'm one of three trustees of a small UK charitable trust (about £300k a year to give out) and we've deadlocked badly enough that I want to put a proper memo in front of the board before our March meeting. The situation: trustee A is basically a global-health effective altruist, wants everything on malaria prevention and will happily quantify it all in lives saved per pound. Trustee B is an animal welfare person, but not the fungible kind -- she thinks industrial animal agriculture is an ongoing moral atrocity, so she doesn't just *prefer* animal grants, she thinks funding anything connected to that industry (e.g. a farming-community health programme that partners with a big meat producer, which A quite likes) is actively wrong, not merely suboptimal. Trustee C wants local community stuff in our town, which both others privately think is a rounding error.

We've been doing strict rotation -- each year one trustee directs the whole pot -- and everyone's miserable, and C has started arguing we should instead split into thirds permanently, each directing our own share, as a "compromise where everyone gains". A loves this and keeps saying it's just gains from trade, like in economics: we each get more of what we value than under war or rotation. B says that's confused: you can trade when you merely want different things, but if you think the other person's project is *immoral*, letting it happen with money you're a custodian of, in exchange for them tolerating yours, isn't trade, it's being bought off. She calls it laundering complicity.

Draft me the memo. It needs: a concrete recommendation on thirds-vs-rotation (or some third option I haven't seen), a general criterion for when compromise between value systems is genuine mutual gain versus a rotten compromise -- I'm pretty sure the key difference is between disagreeing about how to *weigh* goods, disagreeing about *which* goods, and one side thinking the other's good is a *bad*, but pressure-test that -- and an honest treatment of whichever position loses. Also address whether B's offer to formally record her dissent from grants she objects to actually cleans anything, because I suspect it doesn't. British English please.
