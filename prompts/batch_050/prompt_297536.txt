I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a long piece about whether we could ever understand a genuinely alien intelligence — partly spurred by the AI interpretability debates, where everyone keeps name-dropping Davidson and his radical interpretation stuff. I've been reading up, and I want to include a section on the strongest objections, so the piece isn't just a puff piece for the principle of charity.

Here are the objections I already have in my notes, so don't bother repeating these:

1. The irrationality objection: real humans are systematically biased (the whole heuristics-and-biases literature), so a method that interprets people as mostly rational and mostly right will just misattribute beliefs to them.
2. Quine's indeterminacy worry, which Davidson inherits: charity doesn't pin down one interpretation, since gavagai-style alternatives can be equally charitable, so the method underdetermines meaning rather than fixing it.
3. The massive error objection: surely a whole community could be wrong about almost everything (think of a systematically deceived population), and Davidson's omniscient interpreter argument seems to rule this out by fiat rather than showing it's impossible.
4. The anthropologist's objection: maximizing agreement is assimilating — it forces the interpreted into the interpreter's mold and erases genuine conceptual difference.
5. The thought-without-language objection: babies and animals plausibly have beliefs but can't be radically interpreted, so interpretation can't be constitutive of thought the way Davidson wants.

What I need is one more objection that is genuinely different from these — not a rewording, not a special case, not two of them combined. Ideally something that attacks the setup of radical interpretation itself rather than just its consequences, since I think that's a gap in my list. And then, honestly, tell me how much damage you think it actually does and where Davidson's defenders would push back, because I don't want to hang a section of the piece on a dud.
