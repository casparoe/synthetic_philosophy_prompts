At a leaving do last week, someone drew a wobbly analogy between modern Britain and the late Roman Empire, and a colleague shut it down with: Rome didn't fall, it just transformed. Everyone nodded sagely and the conversation moved on, but its been nagging at me since. What precise claims could that line actually be making? I can see a few candidate readings — that the Eastern half carried on for another thousand years so nothing "fell", that Roman institutions survived inside the Church and the barbarian successor kingdoms, that the rupture only really came when the Mediterranean stopped being a Roman lake a couple of centuries later, that the material record (coins, pottery, stone building, long-distance trade) tells a much grimmer story than the word "transformation" suggests, or that "fall" was always just a loaded metaphor the tradition since Gibbon lumbered us with. But I can't tell which of these are trivial or merely verbal, which are substantive and actually defensible, and which are false. Lay out the readings as a menu with a one-line verdict on each, then say which reading my colleague would need to be right about for his shrug at the Britain analogy to be justified. No more than 400 words.
