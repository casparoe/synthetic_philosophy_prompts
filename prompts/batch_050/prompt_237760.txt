I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind with a side appointment in a computational linguistics lab, and I want a diagnosis rather than another contribution. Since the stochastic parrots paper appeared a few years ago, the debate over whether large language models understand anything seems to me to have completely stalled: the grounding side keeps re-running octopus-style and Chinese Room arguments that a system trained on text alone only ever manipulates form, the other side keeps pointing to behavioral competence and to interpretability results suggesting that models build internal representations tracking properties of the situations their training text describes, and nobody on either side has visibly updated in response to the other's evidence. New benchmarks and new probing results arrive, both camps claim them, and the disagreement survives unchanged. My working hypothesis, which I offer as a hypothesis, is that this is not really a dispute about language models at all but a dispute about the evidential status of linguistic behavior: one side holds that sufficiently rich behavior could never settle the matter without the right causal history or embodiment, the other holds that nothing beyond behavior and internal functional organization could even in principle be required, and the other-minds problem is doing the real work under a new name. I want you to tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is -- verbal haggling over the word understanding, a hidden empirical question about the models' internal representations, a difference about which cases count as data, or bedrock starting intuitions neither side can argue for -- whether the old functionalism-versus-biological-naturalism split maps cleanly onto it or whether text-only training introduces a genuinely new element, and what, given your diagnosis, would count as progress: a specific interpretability result, a conceptual distinction, an agreed test, or an argument that no progress should be expected. Please give your answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.
