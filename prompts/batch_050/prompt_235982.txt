Our department's evolutionary biology group has two camps that have been sparring in seminar for years, and a funder has made a grant contingent on their running an adversarial collaboration. Draft the joint statement they would have to produce before any experiments begin.

The first camp is adaptationist: its members hold that natural selection is the default and most productive explanation for organismal design, that optimality modeling is the field's best engine of testable hypotheses, and that apparent failures of adaptationist explanation usually reflect an insufficiently clever hypothesis rather than a non-adaptive origin. The second camp is pluralist, in the tradition of Gould and Lewontin's 1979 critique of the adaptationist programme: its members hold that developmental constraint, drift, phylogenetic inertia, and byproduct construction are systematically underweighted, that the habit of partitioning organisms into isolated traits and proposing a selective story for each protects the programme from falsification, and that a failed adaptationist story is too often met by redescribing it rather than abandoning it.

The statement should contain:

1. An agreed-claims section: roughly ten numbered claims both camps can sign. These should be substantive where possible, not just platitudes both sides trivially accept; where a claim looks vacuous, sharpen it or drop it.
2. A disagreements section: the residual points of dispute, each stated in neutral wording that both camps would accept as a fair rendering of their own position, and each flagged as empirical, methodological, or a dispute about where the burden of proof lies.
3. Three to five cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one camp. For each, give a concrete case, experiment, or body of evidence both sides agree bears on it -- something assessable within about a decade -- and register each side's prediction in advance, including which outcome would change their mind and which they would explain away without updating.
4. At least one longstanding dispute between the camps that dissolves as verbal, with the disambiguation that dissolves it. (I suspect one candidate is the conflation of a trait's current utility with its mode of origin, but take your own view.)

Where a shared case is needed, use these three contested traits: the human chin, zebra striping, and the near-neutral variation at synonymous sites within genes.

Do not write a diplomatic fiction in which the disagreement evaporates. Conversely, if you think my setup has misconstrued either position -- for instance, if I have treated adaptationism as a single doctrine only by conflating its methodological, empirical, and explanatory versions, or if one of my three cases doesn't actually bear on the dispute as I've framed it -- say so plainly and repair the framing before drafting, rather than complying politely with a confused brief.
