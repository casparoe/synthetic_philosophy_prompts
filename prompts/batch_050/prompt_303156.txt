I'm running an undergrad poli sci seminar and want to make a "what's actually wrong with this market?" sorting tool, where students find out which theory of repugnant markets they hold by reacting to cases rather than picking a label. Build it as a decision tree: the root is "this trade feels wrong" and each branch is a diagnostic question someone could answer without knowing any philosopher's name -- things like: would the wrongness go away if the good were distributed by voucher instead of sold? if the seller were rich? if we knew paying people actually increased the supply instead of crowding out the volunteers? if the trade were legal but heavily taxed? Cases to sort: kidney sales, paid plasma donation, hurricane price gouging, paying a stranger to stand in line for you, ticket scalping, vote buying, and paying parents for the right to adopt their baby (use that last one as the stress test, since it tends to split views that agree everywhere else). Leaves should get named positions where they exist (Sandel-style corruption-of-the-good, Satz-style noxious markets, the permissive "if you can do it for free you can sell it" view, plus whatever else genuinely occupies the space), and any coherent-sounding combination nobody defends should get a one-sentence diagnosis of why the seat's empty. Flag pairs of leaves the tree can't really separate. Roughly 8-12 leaves, and please give the branch questions in plain english, not jargon.
