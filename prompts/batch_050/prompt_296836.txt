I am a novelist at work on a book whose narrator volunteers for an experiment: she is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and she is woken once if it lands heads and twice — with her memory of the first waking erased — if it lands tails. Each time she wakes, she must decide what to believe about the coin, and much of the novel's tension depends on my getting her reasoning philosophically credible rather than merely atmospheric. So I have been reading up on the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I keep encountering the same small canon of positions. Here is what I already have and do not need repeated: (1) the thirder argument associated with Elga — on any waking, Beauty should treat the three possible awakenings (heads-Monday, tails-Monday, tails-Tuesday) as epistemically on a par, and since tails occupies two of them her credence in heads should be 1/3, a verdict also reached by noting that learning it is Monday should leave her even between heads and tails while the two tails-awakenings are symmetric; (2) the halfer reply associated with Lewis — Beauty knew in advance that she would wake at least once, the waking itself is guaranteed and so constitutes no new evidence, and a rational agent does not change her credence in a fair coin on learning something she was certain of all along; (3) the double-halfer position, which splits the question — Beauty stays at 1/2 on heads but concedes to the thirder everything about self-location among days, treating the day-question and the coin-question as separable; and (4) the betting-based arguments, where fair odds at each individual awakening appear to favor the thirder because tails generates twice as many occasions to bet, together with the standard halfer rejoinder that per-awakening and per-experiment wagers come apart here and that fair betting odds need not coincide with credence.

What I want from you is a position or argument on what Beauty should believe upon waking that is genuinely not on this list. By genuinely new I mean: not a rewording of any of the four, not a hybrid or compromise stitched together from two of them, and not merely an application of one of them to a dressed-up variant (a thousand wakings, a biased coin, a dozen copies — I have read those too, and they change the numbers without changing the structure). It may defend 1/3, 1/2, or some other verdict entirely, and it may proceed by questioning something all four of my listed positions take for granted — about what a credence is for, about what counts as evidence when memory is tampered with, about whether "Beauty upon waking" picks out a single epistemic subject at all — so long as it yields a definite answer rather than a shrug.

Once you have given me the position, do two further things. First, test it honestly against the two pressure points that do the most damage in this debate: the symmetry between the two tails-awakenings, which pulls toward 1/3, and the no-new-evidence intuition, which pulls toward 1/2 — a position that simply ignores one of these is of no use to me. Second, say a word about its dramatic viability: my narrator has to think her way through this in real time, in a voice a reader can follow, so I would like to know whether your proposal is the kind of reasoning a reflective person could actually undergo, or only the kind a philosopher could ascribe to her from the outside.
