I've been reading about the meta-problem of consciousness — Chalmers's project of explaining why we report and judge ourselves to be conscious, and why the hard problem strikes us as hard in the first place. What I don't understand is why this doesn't simply settle the matter. Once cognitive science fully explains the mechanisms behind our consciousness-talk, our intuitions of ineffability, and our sense of an explanatory gap, there seems to be nothing left for a theory of phenomenal consciousness to do: the "problem" was always a set of dispositions to report certain things, and those dispositions are precisely what the meta-problem explains. So why does Chalmers himself, having framed the meta-problem, continue to take the hard problem seriously instead of declaring it dissolved? Is phenomenal realism at this point just a refusal to follow through on the implications of the meta-problem, as illusionists suggest? In your answer, please separate what is genuinely settled among people who work on this from what remains contested, and don't present either the realist or the illusionist reading of the meta-problem as the consensus view.
