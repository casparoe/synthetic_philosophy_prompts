I just finished writing up my dissertation and in the acknowledgments I added the standard line that despite everyone's help, errors no doubt remain and they're all mine. Then it hit me: I believe every individual claim in the thing, I'd defend each one, but I also believe at least one of them is wrong. Apparently this is the preface paradox, and going down the rabbit hole I found the lottery paradox and a whole literature on whether they're the same problem or two different ones.

That debate looks weirdly stalled to me. Everyone seems to agree on the data: in the lottery case you rationally believe of each ticket that it'll lose, but you don't rationally believe they all lose; in the preface case you believe each claim and believe the conjunction is false somewhere. And everyone agrees both have something to do with rational belief not being closed under conjunction. But after that it fragments -- some people treat them as one phenomenon with one solution, others insist the lottery is about mere high probability while the preface involves actual evidence for each claim, others think the real divide is between people who think rational full belief is even a coherent notion versus people who want to go all-in on degrees of belief.

My hypothesis is that the disagreement isn't really about the paradoxes anymore, it's a proxy war about what rational belief is (or whether there is such a thing beyond credences), and the two paradoxes are just the battlefield. Is that right? What kind of disagreement is this actually -- a verbal one where the sides mean different things by 'rational belief', a substantive one about the structure of justification, or a hidden formal question that could in principle be settled? And given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like -- a distinction, a formal result, or is this the kind of thing that only dissolves if one side's whole framework wins?

One more thing: please be careful to separate what's genuinely settled in this literature (like the basic structure of the two paradoxes) from what's still up for grabs, and don't present one camp's preferred solution as if it were the consensus view.
