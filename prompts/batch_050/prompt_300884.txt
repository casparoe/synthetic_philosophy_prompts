Here's something I've been chewing on re: effective field theory and the autonomy of scales. Everyone gestures at "decoupling" with the same two examples (integrating out a heavy particle, or fluid dynamics not caring about atoms), and I want a case that isn't just one of those with the serial numbers filed off.

So: construct a thought experiment — a toy world you make up, with its own little mechanism (coupled springs, an ant colony, a bond market, whatever, but *not* a relabeled particle-physics example) — engineered so that all of the following hold at once:

1. There are observers *inside* the world whose probing resolution is limited as a matter of principle, not laziness — i.e., the limit comes from how their measuring apparatus is itself built out of the world's stuff, not from them just not having built a better microscope yet.
2. There are two *different* micro-level mechanisms that could underlie what the observers see, and both generate exactly the same low-resolution behavior, up to a small correction.
3. You can point to a single parameter of the world that controls the size of that correction, and say what happens as it goes to zero and to infinity.
4. There is at least one experiment the observers could in principle run where the two micro-mechanisms disagree observably — so the difference is real physics, not something you've made unobservable by construction. (If the only way to satisfy #2 is to stipulate that the mechanisms are definitionally indistinguishable, that's cheating; the verdicts have to fall out of the setup, not be written into it.)

After the scenario, do the work: write down the kind of effective description the observers would end up with, explain *why* both micro-mechanisms collapse into the same effective theory at low resolution, what the leading correction term looks like and whether there's some structural feature of the world (a symmetry, a conservation law, a locality constraint) that forbids other correction terms from appearing. Then say precisely which feature of your setup is doing the heavy lifting — I'm guessing scale separation, but show it — and what happens if you remove it: does the "the low-resolution science is autonomous from the micro-details" claim collapse entirely, or does some weaker version survive?

One more thing: if any part of this request is confused — e.g., if constraints 2 and 4 are actually in tension in a way I haven't noticed, or if "autonomy of scales" as I'm using it is doing more work than the physics supports — say so plainly and tell me what's wrong with the ask, rather than politely constructing something that papers over it.
