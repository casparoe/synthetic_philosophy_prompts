A friend of mine has this puzzle she tests on everyone: a dial with 1001 settings. Each setting is imperceptibly more painful than the one before, but pays you $10,000 more per year. Setting 0 is no pain at all, setting 1000 is constant agony. Her verdicts so far: starting at 0, she'd move to 1 in a heartbeat. At 500, she'd move to 501. But at 999 she flatly refuses the last step to 1000, and asked straight up whether she'd rather live at 0 or at 900 (keeping all the money 900 payed out along the way), she says 0 without hesitating. New case: she's sitting at 400, still feels nothing, has banked $4M, and gets offered a one-time deal -- jump straight to 900 and pocket the extra $5M, or stay put forever. What does she do? Tell me which rule best rationalizes all four of her verdicts, what that rule and the strongest runner-up each predict about the jump, and what further case would finally pry those two rules apart.
