I got into this long argument with my brother-in-law about whether anyone actually owns their land in any deep sense, and I ended up trying to build a case out of that old idea (Locke, I think?) that you come to own unowned stuff by mixing your labor with it, provided you leave enough and as good for everybody else. But the argument I built seems to prove way more than I wanted and now I'm stuck. Here it is:

(1) Mixing your labor with something unowned is what makes it yours, and only if you leave enough and as good for others.
(2) Every piece of land I could possibly mix my labor with is already owned by somebody, and has been for ages, so the condition in (1) can never actually kick in for people like me.
(3) Worse, once land got scarce, there was never enough and as good left for the latecomers, so even the original acquisitions stop satisfying the proviso at some point.
(4) So either current property is illegitimate, or labor-mixing was never really what legitimized it in the first place.
(5) If it's the second option, then nothing justifies property at all, since labor-mixing is the only justification anyone's offered that isn't just circular (it's ours because we say it's ours).
(6) So basically nobody legitimately owns anything, including my brother-in-law's stupid boat.

I refuse to accept (6), mostly because it would make his whole smug point for him, but I genuinely can't find the weak step. If I have to give something up I'd rather drop the proviso part than the labor-mixing part, but tell me what that costs. Where does this go wrong?
