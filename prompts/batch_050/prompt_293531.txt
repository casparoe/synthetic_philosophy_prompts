I teach an upper-level undergraduate metaphysics seminar (14 students, 80 minutes) and I want a structured in-class activity on the principle of the identity of indiscernibles and Max Black's two-sphere counterexample -- a universe containing nothing but two exactly similar spheres, qualitatively identical in every intrinsic and relational respect, yet apparently two rather than one. Most of my students read the case, nod, and file it under "intuition pump, possibly question-begging." I want them to feel the actual bind: that if objects can be picked out only by their qualitative features, then Black's universe is genuinely impossible to describe as containing two things -- and that the escape routes (bare particularity, primitive thisness, irreflexive spatial relations, haecceities) each cost something they won't want to pay until they've been forced to try paying.

Design the activity so the problem emerges from what participants do, not from anything I say up front. Specifically: I want some kind of staged communication or identification task in which students must single out one of two qualitatively identical objects for a partner using only shareable, qualitative descriptions -- no pointing, no naming, no "the one I'm holding" -- and discover in real time that every description either fits both or cheats by smuggling in an unshareable fact. Ideally the activity should then let them try the standard repairs themselves (allowing indexicals, allowing spatial relations, allowing a primitive designation) and feel how each repair either works by theft or reopens the original problem, before anyone hears the terms haecceity, thisness, or descriptivism.

A debrief plan that maps specific moments of the activity onto the philosophical machinery -- which failure corresponds to the descriptivist constraint, which repair corresponds to haecceitism, which corresponds to treating being two feet from the other sphere as an irreflexive relation Black's spheres already have -- and how to run it so students articulate the mappings before I do.
