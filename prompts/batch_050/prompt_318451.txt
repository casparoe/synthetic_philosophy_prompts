I help run a small ethics reading group at my university (mostly undergrads, some grad students lurking) and we're spending a month on whether pleasure and suffering can actually be weighed against each other on a single scale, or whether something weird happens at the extremes. I want to make a little "where do you actually stand?" flowchart people can work through at the start of the month, because last time I just asked everyone their view directly and of course five people said "utilitarian, I guess" and then disagreed with each other about every single case we discussed. So the tree should sort people by their verdicts on cases, not by what label they like.

Please build it as a decision tree with maybe 7-9 branching questions, each one a concrete scenario or claim someone can react to without knowing any terminology -- things like whether some enormous number of barely-noticeable annoyances (a mild itch, a stubbed toe, a song stuck in your head) could ever add up to be worse than one person undergoing a month of torture, whether a life containing one truly horrific hour plus decades of bliss is on net worth living, whether there's any amount of mild discomfort you'd accept to cure a single case of unbearable agony, that kind of thing. Each branch should be labeled with the claim that splits it, and the leaves should land people on distinct positions -- I want at least these in the mix: straight-up symmetric weighing where any pain can in principle be offset by enough pleasure, the view that preventing suffering takes absolute priority over creating happiness no matter what, a threshold version where suffering below some intensity can be traded off but suffering past it is lexically untouchable, a "weak asymmetry" view where pain just counts for more per unit, and the hardline incommensurability view that there's no exchange rate between them at all and talk of trading them off is a category error.

Two more requests. First, if the questions I've sketched (or the ones you add) can't distinguish two of these positions from each other, say so explicitly and tell me what case *would* pry them apart, instead of pretending the tree works. Second, for any combination of answers nobody seems to occupy, don't just leave it off the tree -- include it as an empty leaf with one or two sentences on whether it's empty because it's incoherent, just ugly, or genuinely unexplored. And please include at least one question designed to catch people who give the "sophisticated" answer but flinch when the numbers get big, since I suspect that's most of the room.
