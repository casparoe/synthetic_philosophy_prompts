So I do amateur woodworking and sometimes sell pieces at a local craft market, and a running joke/argument at our stall circle is whether the guy who makes those hyper-minimal walnut benches is an artist or just a fancy carpenter, while the woman selling hand-thrown mugs definitely calls herself one. That got me down a rabbit hole about what a definition of art would even have to accomplish. I don't want a definition itself -- I want the checklist first. What are the requirements any proposed definition of art has to meet before I'd take it seriously? Like, what cases does it have to get right (the urinal someone signed and put in a museum, my neighbor's benches, a Bach fugue, a great gymnastics routine, a Michelin-star dish, a five-year-old's drawing of a cat, something a model generated from a text prompt), and which of those are actually load-bearing cases versus just ones people bring up to score points for their preferred theory?

A few specific things I want on the list, and you can tell me if I'm wrong to want them: it should say whether art status can be conferred by the right people just accepting something, or whether something in the object itself has to do the work; it should handle the fact that lots of things we now call art were made as religious objects or tools by people who had no concept remotely like ours; it should explain why a perfect forgery of a famous painting is somehow worth less even if it looks identical, without the definition just smuggling in "made by the right person"; and it shouldn't be so generous that literally everything counts, but also shouldn't collapse into "art is whatever gives a special feeling" because my kid's cat drawing gives me a special feeling and I'm not sure that settles anything. For each requirement, give me the case or consideration motivating it, and mark which ones you think are genuine non-negotiables and which are just the taste of one camp (institutionalists, aesthetic-experience people, historical-definition people, whatever).

And the part I most want: are any of these requirements jointly unsatisfiable? I have a suspicion that "must explain why the urinal counts" and "must rule out mere crafts like benches" pull against each other in a way that no definition can fix, and if the checklist itself is incoherent that would be a result worth knowing. Brief scorecard at the end for the main contenders is fine but keep it brief -- the spec is what I'm after. Also flag any requirement that sounds reasonable but actually does all the excluding by itself.
