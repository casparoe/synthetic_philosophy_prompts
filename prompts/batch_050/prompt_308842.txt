In my course on logical empiricism, we've been discussing Carnap's advocacy of a scientific world-conception, his sympathies with socialism and rational social planning, and his lifelong project of formal inductive logic. Suppose Carnap were alive today and confronted with two developments he never saw: first, modern machine learning systems that successfully perform inductive inference without anything like explicit logical rules or confirmation functions; second, contemporary proposals for computational economic planning, in which optimization algorithms and vast data collection are claimed to make centralized allocation feasible, reviving the socialist calculation debate. What would his position on these developments most likely be? In particular: would he regard machine learning as a vindication, a refutation, or simply a pragmatic successor of his inductive logic program, and would he concede or resist the claim that modern computing answers the classic objections to rational economic planning? Please ground your answer in his documented commitments, such as the principle of tolerance, the distinction between internal and external questions, and his views on the relation between theoretical frameworks and practical decisions, and indicate where you think the extrapolation from his actual writings becomes genuinely speculative.
