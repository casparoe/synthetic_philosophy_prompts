I am a Year 12 representative on my school’s conduct policy panel. After several cheating incidents, we must recommend one of two responses: a private restorative process that encourages guilt by requiring offenders to understand the harm, apologise, and make amends; or a public accountability process that uses moderated shame by acknowledging the offence before the school community. I am concerned that guilt can become mere private discomfort while shame can turn into permanent social stigma, but also that removing shame altogether may weaken shared standards.

Please draft a concise decision memo, in British English, recommending one approach. It should set out an implementable policy and review period, explain which moral emotion the school should try to cultivate, and address whether an institution can really choose its emotional culture. Please engage seriously with the strongest objection to your recommendation and suggest safeguards against both evasion of responsibility and lasting humiliation.
