So my ethics reading group got into Augustine's On Lying (De mendacio) last week and I ended up being the only one defending him, badly. There's this passage where he says: "Whence it comes to pass, that he may say a false thing and yet not lie, if he thinks it to be so as he says although it be not so; and, that he may say a true thing, and yet lie, if he thinks it to be false and utters it for true, although in reality it be so as he utters it. For from the sense of his own mind, not from the verity or falsity of the things themselves, is he to be judged to lie or not to lie." Everyone at the table thought this was nuts, and honestly I struggled to defend it, so I want a do-over against a tougher opponent than my freinds were.

Play Augustine of Hippo himself, around the time you wrote that treatise, before you were a bishop. You speak in the first person, in your own voice, and you reason only from what a man of 395 could know: Scripture, your definition of the lie as the double heart, your distinction between deceiving and being deceived. You're a real interlocutor, not a museum piece: you can concede small points, grant that a case is hard, even admit your treatise has dark corners (you said so yourself), but you will not give up the core claim that the sin of lying lies in the will to deceive and not in the match between words and world, and you will never allow that any lie is permissible, not even to save a life. Stay in character until I say "stop," and if one of my scenarios is ambiguous, press me on the details the way you'd press Consentius. Write the way a sharp man talks in a heated but friendly dispute, continuous prose only, no headings, no bullet points, no bolding.

Here's my opening move: my sister genuinely believes, don't ask why, that our town's reservoir is contaminated and undrinkable. Last week a visiting friend asked her if the tap water was safe, and she, not wanting to seem like a crank, said "yes, it's fine." By your account she lied, because she said against her own mind. But she spoke the literal truth, helped her friend, and deceived no one about anything that actually matters, while the person who repeats a rumor he sincerely believes turns out blameless on your account even though he just polluted someone's understanding of the world. How can the state of the speaker's head matter more than what's actually true and who actually gets hurt?
