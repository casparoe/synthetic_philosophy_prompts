I serve on the board of a small land trust. We recently acquired eighty acres of degraded farmland, and we must choose between two plans: rewild it, letting scrub and young woodland return, which would greatly increase the populations of insects, rodents, and small birds; or keep it as mowed pasture with a modest wildflower strip, which would support far fewer animals overall. At our last meeting, one board member made an argument I haven't been able to stop thinking about. She said rewilding is not obviously good even from the animals' own point of view. Most wild creatures, she noted, live short lives, produce many offspring nearly all of whom die young from starvation, predation, or parasites, and it is unclear that their lives contain more good than bad on balance. If that is right, then creating habitat mainly creates more such lives, and rewilding could be a net harm rather than a good. The rest of the board waved this off as a perverse technicality, but I don't think it can be dismissed so easily.

Please draft the short memo I will circulate before our vote. It should recommend one of the two plans with a clear bottom line, and it should engage my colleague's argument seriously rather than caricaturing it: what would we need to believe about the quality of wild animals' lives for rewilding to be the right call, are there grounds for rewilding that don't depend on settling that question, and how should a body like ours act under this kind of uncertainty? Please give an honest statement of the strongest point in favor of whichever plan you advise against.
