So my friend Mara and I have been arguing about complicity for like a year now — when are you on the hook for wrongdoing you didn't cause, just because you bought something or showed up somewhere. It started with the standard EA-adjacent consumer ethics debates and has since metastasized into basically every purchase either of us makes. Her verdicts are pretty stable but I cannot figure out what rule she's running. Her calls so far:

- Buying factory-farmed chicken at the store: complicit, won't do it. She fully buys the expected-marginal-harm argument (each purchase has some small chance of triggering the next bulk order, so in expectation you're paying for roughly one chicken's worth of suffering).
- Fast fashion from brands with documented sweatshops: complicit, same logic.
- Paying taxes to a government running a drone program she considers murder: not complicit. Coerced, no choice, and nothing changes based on what she does.
- Eating factory-farmed chicken at a friend's dinner party when it's already cooked and would get thrown out otherwise: not complicit, or at most a social-signaling issue, not real complicity.
- Buying a *used* paperback from an author who donates all his royalties to a political cause she thinks is actively harmful: totally fine, since no money reaches him.

New case we're fighting about: a musician she likes is touring and has publicly pledged 10% of tour profits to an organization Mara considers genuinely bad — not just a politician she dislikes, think funding for a harmful political movement. The show in our city will definately sell out no matter what; small venue, insane resale market. So if Mara buys a ticket, total attendance, total revenue, and the artist's donation are all exactly the same as if she stays home — someone else just gets her seat. I say she's clearly fine to go by her own lights; she says it still *feels* like complicity and can't articulate why, which annoys her as much as it annoys me.

Two questions. First, what rule is she actually running? I can see at least two candidates that fit all five earlier verdicts — an expected-difference rule, and a "does your money end up with the wrongdoers" rule — but maybe there's a better-fitting one I'm missing. Second, predict her verdict on the concert if she wants to stay consistent with herself, tell me which rule you'd bet she's implicitly committed to, and give me a further case that would cleanly tell the candidate rules apart, because the concert apparently isn't doing it.
