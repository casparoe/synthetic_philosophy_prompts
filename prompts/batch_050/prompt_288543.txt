(3) This priority, like special obligations generally, is grounded intrinsically in the relationship and its shared history; it is not merely a useful rule for maximizing welfare, so a modest gain to the stranger cannot by itself override it. (6) If I were an emergency worker responsible for both my mother and a stranger, I should apply the same triage rule to them, even when it requires treating the stranger first. (7) Yet in a strictly private rescue involving no professional role, if the stranger had a moderately better prognosis than my mother, I would be morally required—not merely permitted—to save the stranger; choosing my mother might be understandable, but it would be wrong.

Please determine whether these seven commitments form a coherent package. Identify each genuine tension in order of severity, explain exactly which commitments conflict and why, and distinguish them from tensions that only appear to conflict. For every genuine tension, state the cheapest plausible revision and what it would cost the overall view. Finally, say which single change would restore the most coherence and whether the revised position is closer to impartial care, graded love, or a principled hybrid of the two.
