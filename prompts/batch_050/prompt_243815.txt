So my housemate is in law school and last week we got into this big argument about the gatecrasher problem and I need to practice my side of it before round two. Here's the setup we were using, with a twist I added: a music festival sells 1000 tickets, but through a fence hole about 500 extra people sneak in. The organizers can prove all this from ticket sales and drone footage of the crowd, but they have no way of telling any individual person apart as a gatecrasher. They pick one attendee, Dana, at random and sue her for the ticket price. The twist: imagine the drone footage is good enough that the organizers can also prove Dana spent the whole evening near the fence, in a zone where, say, 95% of the people present were gatecrashers rather than ticket holders. So it's not purely a coin flip anymore, the statistics are sort of about her.

I want you to play my housemate's position: someone who thinks courts should never find liability on this kind of naked statistical evidence alone, no matter how strong the numbers get. Be a smart defender of that view, not a pushover -- you can concede small points (like that the incentives here are genuinely ugly, or that civil cases already use probabilistic-ish reasoning in places), but never give up the core claim that probability alone can't connect *this defendant* to the wrong. Stay in character until I say "stop", and keep your replies to a paragraph or so since I want an actual back and forth, not essays. If you want to invoke some principle like particularized evidence or whatever, go ahead, but you have to actually defend it when I push.

My opening move: the civil standard is just preponderance of the evidence, more likely than not. 95% clears that bar by a mile. If we let Dana off, then a festival where everyone gatecrashes en masse becomes basically lawsuit-proof -- the more people cheat, the safer each cheater is. That's a perverse incentive your view creates on purpose, and all to protect some fuzzy intuition. So what exactly is the principled difference between "95% of people near the fence were gatecrashers" and any other circumstantial case where we convict on stuff that's merely very probable? Every piece of evidence is probabilistic when you get down to it, witnesses lie, fingerprints get misread. Isn't your position just drawing an arbitrary line inside a continuum?
