I'm working on a climate-adaptation methods chapter and could use a consistency audit of the position I've ended up with. The case is designing flood defenses that are supposed to last until at least 2100, under a changing and possibly nonstationary climate.

My commitments are:

1. A raw multi-model ensemble mean and spread should not be treated as the best estimate and probability distribution of future flooding. The models are not independent draws from some space of possible Earths: they share code, parameterizations, tuning targets, and sometimes whole model families.

2. Nevertheless, for an actual planning decision we should produce a weighted probabilistic projection, with weights based on historical performance and model interdependence.

3. Models that duplicate important code or make essentially the same structural assumption should not count as independent confirming evidence.

4. A model should get little or no credit merely for reproducing the observed historical trend if it does so through the wrong mechanism. Getting the right answer for the wrong reason is not evidence that it will extrapolate well under stronger forcing.

5. Because the remaining structural uncertainty is deep and any prior over model structures would be partly arbitrary, the final design should use minimax regret rather than expected utility.

6. The regret analysis should cover every future that remains compatible with current observations and basic physical constraints, including plausible tail futures that no existing model happens to simulate.

7. New observations should change the model weights and the set of viable futures, but not our degree of risk aversion or the decision rule. I want to keep empirical updating separate from the value judgment about how cautious to be.

Does this package actually hang together? Please be committed rather than just laying out options: identify the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, and say which apparent tensions dissolve once the distinctions are made. For each real problem, say exactly which commitment has to be weakened or reformulated. If more than one repair is possible, choose the one you think is cheapest and defend that choice. In particular, don't let me slide between rejecting ensemble-derived probabilities and then helping myself to an equally arbitrary notion of plausibility in the robustness analysis.
