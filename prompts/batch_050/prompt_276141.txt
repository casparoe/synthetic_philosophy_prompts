I've been a LessWrong reader for over a decade and I'm finally writing up my settled views on confirmation theory for a reading group, and I want a consistency audit before I inflict them on anyone. Here's where I've landed:

(a) Conditionalization is the correct norm of credence revision, and degree of confirmation is adequately measured by the Bayes factor, i.e., the log-likelihood ratio.

(b) Old evidence genuinely confirms: the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion, known for decades, substantially confirmed general relativity once Einstein showed his theory entailed it. A theory earns no less credit for accommodating data gathered before its invention than for predicting novel data.

(c) The right solution to the old-evidence problem is the counterfactual-prior maneuver: when the evidence E is already in one's credence function with probability 1, evaluate the relevant likelihoods under the credences one *would have had* had one not learned E.

(d) Confirmation is an objective relation between a hypothesis and a total body of evidence: two ideally rational agents with the same total evidence must agree on what confirms what, and by how much, regardless of the order in which they acquired it. Epistemic rationality should not be hostage to biographical accident.

(e) Logical omniscience has to go: bounded reasoners can rationally assign intermediate credences to unproven conjectures, and discovering that a theory entails a known datum is a genuine epistemic event, not merely the noticing of something one's credences already implied.

Which of these are actually in tension, and which only look like they are? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, give the cheapest fix for each, and say which single revision would clean up the most.
