A friend and I were arguing about a thought experiment I came across in the AI-safety literature: two advanced systems, built independently but from strongly overlapping designs and training data, will each make a single irreversible decision that affects the other's operators. The systems can never communicate, cannot directly verify that the other exists, and have no means of rewarding or punishing one another afterwards; each can, however, reason about how a system so similar to itself is likely to reason. I claimed that each could rationally choose a costly action that benefits the other, expecting the symmetrical choice in return — cooperation without any possible commerce, of the sort sometimes called acausal trade. My friend shut the discussion down with: 'It takes two to trade. If no message can pass between them, there is no deal to be had — just two systems unilaterally burning resources on a fantasy of reciprocity.'
