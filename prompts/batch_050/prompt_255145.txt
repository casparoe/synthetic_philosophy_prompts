ok so I'm a novelist, and my current book is set at the end of the Late Bronze Age — palaces burning, scribes writing their last letters before the city falls, the whole dramatic apparatus. A friend of mine (an archaeologist, annyoingly) read a draft and told me the entire 'collapse' genre is basically a myth: societies don't really collapse, she said, they transform, and what looks like civilizational death from the outside is often ordinary people quietly abandoning a system that had stopped working for them. I argued with her for a week and then, worse, found myself convinced. So my working position is now: there is no such thing as societal collapse, only transformation that later storytellers (people like me, apparently) dress up as catastrophe. The Maya, the Bronze Age, Easter Island — change in a black cloak.

Here's what I want from you: what would count against this position? Not reasons it's unpopular with disaster novelists, but the actual defeaters. Which real cases, if the evidence shook out a certain way, could it simply not redescribe as transformation? Which distinctions does it lean on that might not survive scrutiny — I'm thinking of things like the difference between an elite order falling and a society falling, or between people leaving a system and a system leaving people without options. What would its defenders themselves concede counts against it, versus what would they wave away? Rank the threats from 'this actually kills the position' down to 'merely awkward, I'd have to write around it.' And if it turns out the position has been padded with so many qualifications that nothing could ever count against it, say that outright — that's a finding, not a relief.

Since this is load-bearing for my book, flag the one defeater that would make the premise outright dishonest rather than just debatable. And tell me where your own answer is most likely to fail — which single consideration, if I actually checked it or pushed on it, would most plausibly flip your verdict. I'd rather know that than feel falsely reassured.

Keep it under 600 words. The novel is long enough already.
