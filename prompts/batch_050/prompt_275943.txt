Construct a thought experiment in which the classic Ellsberg preference pattern — strictly preferring a bet on a known fifty-fifty prospect over an otherwise identical bet on a prospect of unknown composition — is instrumentally rational, in the sense that an agent who instead bets on the ambiguous prospect does strictly worse in expectation. Constraints: the composition of the ambiguous prospect must remain genuinely unknown to the agent at the moment of decision (no peeking, and no reliable expert available to consult); no perfect predictors, mind-reading, or exotic physics; and the setting should be an ordinary commercial one — a market, an auction, or an insurance contract — rather than a laboratory with urns. Then do the analysis: identify which axiom of subjective expected utility theory the rational agent's behaviour violates or renders inapplicable; explain why a committed Bayesian cannot restore orthodoxy merely by placing a prior over the possible compositions; state which single feature of the case is doing the work and what happens to the verdicts if that feature is removed; and close with the strongest objection a defender of Savage's framework would press against the case, together with your verdict on whether that objection succeeds. Please write your answer in British English.
