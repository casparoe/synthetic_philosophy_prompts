My sister has strong views about when it is rational to walk away from something one has invested in, and I have been collecting her verdicts:

- She walked out of an acclaimed film at intermission despite the expensive tickets, on the grounds that the money was spent either way and two more hours of boredom would buy her nothing.
- She pushed through the final hundred pages of a novel she was not enjoying, because she wanted to find out whether its threads came together.
- She remained in a book club she found tedious for the rest of the season, because she had chosen that season's reading list, and then resigned.
- She abandoned a sourdough hobby after a month of daily feedings, discarding a starter she had carefully maintained, because the bread was not worth the effort to her.

Now she faces a new case. She has spent two years training for a marathon and no longer enjoys running. She is uninjured, the race is three weeks away, nobody knows she has been training, and finishing would prove nothing to anyone. Yet she says she would hate for the two years to have been wasted. Predict her verdict, and explain your reasoning: her track record is consistent with more than one underlying rule, so identify at least two candidate rules that fit all four verdicts, state what each predicts about the marathon, say which prediction you consider more likely and why, and describe the further case whose verdict would best discriminate between the two rules.
