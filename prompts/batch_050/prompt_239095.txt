In biology class, DNA is presented as a code: a sequence of letters that the cell reads to build proteins, much as a computer reads a program. I have begun to suspect this might be only a metaphor, and I want to know what would actually settle the question. Write the desiderata for a theory of genetic information: what any adequate account of whether DNA is literally a code must deliver -- which facts it must accommodate (for instance, that the same stretch of DNA can yield different proteins depending on cellular context, as in alternative splicing), which distinctions it must draw (such as that between information as mere correlation and information as content, or between the DNA and the cellular machinery that does the "reading"), and which ordinary intuitions it must either vindicate or explain away -- giving the case behind each requirement, and flagging any desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, how a literal code view, a view based on statistical information theory, and a view on which DNA gets its content from its evolutionary history fare against the list.
