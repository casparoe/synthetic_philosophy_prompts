A friend and I recently had a long disagreement about coherent extrapolated volition and, more generally, about when idealized preferences should have authority over actual ones. She argued that my position combines an anti-paternalist constraint with a willingness to let the extrapolation process remake people beyond recognition. I am not convinced, but I would like an internal consistency check rather than a discussion of whether such extrapolation is technically feasible.

My commitments are as follows. First, an actual preference is not automatically authoritative: ideal deliberation may revise or discard desires that depend on factual ignorance, coercive upbringing, addiction, framing effects, or adaptive preferences formed because better options seemed unavailable. Second, idealization must nevertheless count as self-revision rather than replacement. The endpoint must preserve enough of the person’s identity-constituting concerns for the result to be hers; it is not sufficient that the imagined successor is happier, more coherent, or more rational by some external standard. Third, extrapolation should initially be performed separately for each person rather than by constructing a single average human volition, because collective averaging could erase morally significant minorities. Fourth, for decisions affecting many people, the individually extrapolated volitions should then participate in a fair bargaining procedure in which actual wealth, bargaining skill, and social power confer no advantage. Fifth, an extrapolated recommendation should be path-independent: if two deliberative sequences supply the same evidence and capacities in different orders, they should not yield different ultimate recommendations merely because of that order. Sixth, a legitimate extrapolation may sometimes endorse irreversible self-transformation—for example, acquiring a radically new sensory modality, emotional repertoire, or class of relationships—even when the transformation’s value cannot be adequately understood except by undergoing a particular sequence of experiences. Finally, where several equally coherent endpoints remain, the procedure should preserve option value rather than manufacture uniqueness by aggregating their intensities.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which merely appear to be, and what exactly creates each conflict? Please rank the tensions by severity and identify the least costly revision needed to resolve each one, including what my position would lose under that revision. In particular, distinguish conflicts over the authority of the actual self, conflicts generated by path-dependence, and conflicts arising when individual extrapolations must be combined into a social choice.
