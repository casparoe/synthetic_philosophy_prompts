I moderate a mid-sized local discussion forum for my city (about 35,000 members, a mix of neighborhood news, lost pets, politics, and constant drama about landlords and development projects). There are five of us on the mod team, all volunteers, and we probably make thirty or forty removal decisions a day, usually in under a minute each, with no real expertise in anything and only the platform's own vague rules ("no harassment, no hate") to lean on. Lately the hard cases keep having the same shape: someone posts something inflammatory about a specific identifiable person or business, and we have to decide whether it's an opinion or something worse. In a philosophy reading group I sit in on, we just did Mill's On Liberty, and I got kind of stuck on this passage from chapter 3:

"No one pretends that actions should be as free as opinions. On the contrary, even opinions lose their immunity, when the circumstances in which they are expressed are such as to constitute their expression a positive instigation to some mischievous act. An opinion that corn-dealers are starvers of the poor, or that private property is robbery, ought to be unmolested when simply circulated through the press, but may justly incur punishment when delivered orally to an excited mob assembled before the house of a corn-dealer, or when handed about among the same mob in the form of a placard."

What I want is for you to turn Mill's distinction here into an actual moderation procedure my team could follow: the questions to ask about a flagged post, in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each one, and where the procedure should stop with a remove or a leave-up. The tricky part I want you to spend real effort on is the translation. Mill's variables are 1859 variables -- the press versus the placard, the mob physically assembled outside the house. My variables are things like: the post is fine in the main feed but someone has screenshotted it into a thread where people are organizing to show up somewhere; the post names a person and includes their workplace; the post is an opinion but it's pinned at the top of a thread that's already 400 comments deep and furious, twelve hours before a city council meeting on the topic. Tell me which features of an online context are the genuine analogues of "delivered orally to an excited mob" and which are superficial resemblances that would let the procedure swallow Mill's whole point about opinions being unmolested. Also be honest about a wrinkle I noticed: Mill is talking about punishment by society or the state, and I'm just a guy who can delete posts and ban accounts, so say something about whether that makes the translation easier or actually breaks it.

Then run the finished procedure on three cases, in prose: one, a post arguing at length that a specific local landlord is a parasite who's destroying the neighborhood, including the building he lives in, the week before a tenant-organizing rally; two, a long crankish post arguing that the city's homeless shelter should be shut down and its residents made to leave town, with no specific individuals named; three, a post I personally sympathize with calling out a business owner who fired a friend of mine, where the thread has started collecting his home address. At least one of these should come out with an answer I won't enjoy, and tell me which one you think that is. Finish by saying what the procedure flat-out cannot settle -- the judgment calls that stay judgment calls, like what counts as "mischievous" or how much hostility in the comments is enough -- so I know where the checklist ends and I just have to decide. One more thing on format: please write the whole thing as continuous prose, like a long email, no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no numbered lists -- if the procedure has ordered steps, write them out in sentences, because I'll be pasting this into our mod wiki and our wiki software mangles formatting anyway.
