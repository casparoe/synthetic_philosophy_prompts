okay so i cover biotech and neuroscience for a general-audience science magazine, and there's a move i keep watching scientists make when their explanations get challenged, and i want to know if philosophers of science already have a name for it. act as my philosophy-of-science consultant for this one — think carefully and brainstorm a few candidate framings before you commit to anything.

here's the pattern, two examples (details fudged to protect people, but the shape is real):

1. a team says compound X prevents blood clots because it blocks a particular enzyme in the clotting cascade. a rival lab shows that at the doses anyone would actually take, that enzyme is barely inhibited. instead of conceding, the first team starts saying X "modulates the whole clotting network" — suddenly the claim is about a bigger, fuzzier thing where the rival's experiment doesnt bite anymore.

2. a neuro group claims a small brain region makes mice freeze when afraid — they can switch the freezing on and off by manipulating that spot. critics point out the region's cells fire for basically everything, not just fear. the group replies that the real causal work happens in specific receptor subtypes on specific cell types within the region, and the criticism was aimed at the wrong grain of detail.

so in both cases the story is one of those part-acts-on-part explanations — one component does something to another component, and out comes the overall behavior of the system — and when evidence turns against the story at one scale, the explanation quietly slides to a different scale, up or down, wherever the critics' experiment can't reach. it never gets refuted, it just relocates. and here's the thing that actually bugs me: each individual relocation can be perfectly legitimate science. sometimes the bigger system genuinely is the right unit, sometimes the molecular detail genuinely is the answer. so this isn't just dishonesty or fraud. but as a general pattern it seems to make this whole style of explanation able to absorb any criticism forever, which feels like it should worry someone.

does this have an established name? it seems adjacent to a bunch of things — unfalsifiability, moving the goalposts, the worry that you can always push an explanation one level down forever — but i dont think it's exactly any of those, since the slide here is specifically between scales *within one part-whole style explanation*, not between unrelated claims, and the parts at every scale are still supposed to connect up into the same machine. if nothing established fits, coin a term — but honestly the label matters less to me than the carving. i need to know exactly when a slide between scales counts as this dodge and when it's just normal science correctly relocating where the causal action is. give me cases on both sides of that line, including some genuinely hard ones.
