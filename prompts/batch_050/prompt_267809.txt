everyone in my corner of the internet who talks about consciousness eventually reaches for the GEB move: Gödel showed that any sufficiently powerful formal system can encode statements about itself via Gödel numbering, and Hofstadter's idea is that the self is the same trick at a higher level -- a strange loop where a symbol system bends back on itself and that bending somehow is self-awareness. people then use this to argue that a big enough AI system would just wake up the same way, no special sauce needed. how far does the analogy actually hold? i want the explicit mapping: what plays the role of the Gödel number in a brain, what's the provability predicate, what's the fixed axiom system. then the places it breaks -- e.g. the Gödel sentence is constructed from outside by a mathematician using a coding scheme the system itself never perceives or chooses, the formal system can't in any sense tell that the sentence is about it, nothing in the proof is supposed to feel like anything, and brains arent static axiom sets anyway. which of these disanalogies actually matter for the waking-up conclusion, and does that conclusion survive once they're on the table, or was the Gödel analogy carrying it the whole time? if it breaks, is there a repaired version of the self-reference idea that still does work?
