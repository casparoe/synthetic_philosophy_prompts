I'm designing the final exam for an upper-level seminar in population ethics. Draft one essay question on the nonidentity problem that students can answer in 75 minutes, built around a case of your own construction (not Parfit's risky policy or the fourteen-year-old girl) in which a present-day choice both lowers the quality of future lives and determines which future people exist. The question should force students to do three things: state exactly why a comparative, person-affecting conception of harm yields the conclusion that no one is wronged in the case; assess whether retreating to an impersonal betterness principle avoids that conclusion and what it costs elsewhere (e.g., exposure to repugnant-conclusion-style results or leveling-down intuitions); and say whether an account of wronging-without-harming can steer between the two horns without merely asserting that it can. The question must be unanswerable by reciting the standard taxonomy of same-number versus different-number choices. Along with the question, supply a model answer of roughly 600 words and a marking scheme organized around five must-hit points, the marks each is worth out of 20, and a one-line note on how students typically mishandle each point.
