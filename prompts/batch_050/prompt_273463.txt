I'm a philosophy PhD student and my advisor walked me into this at office hours last week, and I still can't find where it breaks. It's about Frankfurt-style bullshit and LLMs:

(1) Bullshit, in Frankfurt's sense, is speech produced without any concern for whether it's true -- the bullshitter isn't lying, because lying requires caring enough about the truth to hide it, and the bullshitter just doesn't care either way, only about the effect on the audience.

(2) An LLM generates text by producing statistically plausible continuations. It has no attitude whatsoever toward the truth of what it outputs -- it doesn't track truth, doesn't aim at it, isn't capable of being concerned with it.

(3) So every LLM output is bullshit, including the true ones.

(4) But then the same must hold for humans producing speech in the same functional role: the undergrad who skimmed one wiki article and writes confidently, the anchor reading a teleprompter she's never thought about, the guy repeating a statistic because it wins the argument. None of them are tracking truth either.

(5) So a huge chunk of ordinary, unremarkable human speech is bullshit even when true, and my own seminar contributions most weeks presumably qualify.

(6) And by the same logic a calculator, a dictionary, and the teleprompter itself are bullshitters too, which is obviously absurd.

My advisor says this shows that "LLM outputs are bullshit" is the wrong diagnosis of hallucination, but I think the argument might just prove too much and die somewhere around (4). Two questions: which step do you actually reject, and why did it look fine? And if the flaw is in how (2) characterizes the model -- i.e. lacking a truth-attitude isn't the same as the bullshitter's indifference -- does that mean LLMs can't lie either, and hallucination is just plain error? I'd like to know what each exit costs.
