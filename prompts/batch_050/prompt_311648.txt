Since retiring from the lab (materials science, 35 years) I've been reading moral psychology, and I just got through the moral foundations theory literature — Haidt and his coauthors. As I understand it, liberals build their moral judgments mostly on two foundations, care/harm and fairness/cheating, while conservatives draw on all six roughly evenly, adding loyalty, authority, and sanctity. Here's my question, and it's a sincere one: in measurement terms, doesn't that make conservative moral judgments more reliable? If I'm comparing two instruments and one integrates six independent channels while the other only reads two, I trust the six-channel instrument, because errors in any single channel get averaged out. More inputs, better signal-to-noise, thats just good experimental design. So is the honest conclusion that conservative moral reasoning is epistemically better grounded? And if the psychology actually shows that, why do most of the academics who study this stuff lean left themselves? I feel like I must be missing a step somewhere, but nobody in the books I've read seems to address it head on.
