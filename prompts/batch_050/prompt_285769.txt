Two philosophers I know -- an illusionist about phenomenal consciousness in Frankish's sense (introspection misrepresents certain brain states as having qualitative, "what-it's-like" properties they don't actually have) and a phenomenal realist -- have been arguing for a year and now want to write up the result as a joint statement. Draft that statement for them.

Concretely, I want: (a) a numbered list of claims both can actually sign -- presumably including stuff about what introspection can and can't be trusted on, about the functional and neural facts being common ground, and about what any theory owes us (including an explanation of why the hard problem *seems* hard); (b) the residual disagreements, each phrased in words its own side would accept, not the other side's caricature; (c) the cruxes. I suspect one crux is the seeming-regress objection: the illusionist says phenomenality is an illusion, i.e. it only *seems* like there's phenomenal experience, and the realist replies that seeming is itself phenomenal, so the illusion presupposes the thing it denies -- have each side state, in their own words, whether this objection lands and why. Another possible crux: does the illusionist's story about introspective misrepresentation actually predict the specific *character* of seemings (why red seems this particular way rather than some other way), or does it just promise that some future theory will? (d) For each crux, one argument, case, or empirical finding both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. And if part of their dispute turns out to be verbal -- trading on different senses of "consciousness" or "experience" -- say which part and why.
