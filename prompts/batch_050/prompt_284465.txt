I keep hearing people say an LLM is basically an improv actor: the training gives it a huge repertoire of possible characters, the prompt is like the setup from the audience, and the helpful assistant is just the role it happens to be playing at the moment. In an argument with my brother, he used this to claim that ChatGPT is not really an agent at all, only a simulator producing agent-shaped behavior, the same way an actor pretending to be furious is not actually furious. But I pushed back because an actual improv actor is still a person with a body, memories between performances, and reasons for staying in character, whereas the model seems to be doing both the acting and somehow the whole theater company too.

Audit the improv-actor analogy for me. Give a clear mapping of what corresponds to what: the training data, the model weights, the prompt, the context window, sampling, the assistant persona, and maybe an AI system connected to tools or given a persistent goal. Then say where the analogy breaks in ways that matter for deciding whether an LLM is an agent, a simulator, or some third thing. For example, does having no stable preferences outside a conversation mean there is no agent, or could an agent exist temporarily within the role being played? Does using tools or remembering past chats make the system itself more agent-like, or just build an agent around a simulator? Please don’t treat agent as an all-or-nothing category if that doesn’t make sense. In the end, tell me whether my brother’s conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and suggest a better analogy if there is one.
