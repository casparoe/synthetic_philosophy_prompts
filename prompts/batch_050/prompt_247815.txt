I'm designing a workshop handout for a graduate seminar on the demandingness objection to consequentialism, and I want a taxonomy rather than another essay. Please construct a decision tree that sorts the main responses to the objection. Start the tree from the complaint itself -- that impartial maximizing morality seems to require agents to sacrifice their projects, relationships, and resources up to the point of marginal utility whenever others' needs are greater -- and let each branch node be a single yes/no or either/or question phrased so that someone could answer it without already knowing which response it lands them on. Good splitting questions might include: whether morality may grant agents a prerogative to weight their own concerns more heavily than a purely impartial calculus would; whether rightness comes in degrees rather than being binary; whether there is a threshold of goodness an act must reach, past which more good is optional; whether demandingness is a genuine moral cost to be minimized or simply a fact about what morality happens to require; and whether the unit of assessment is the individual act or a shared scheme whose demands arise only under general compliance.

The leaves must include: the bullet-biting maximizing view associated with Singer; Scheffler-style agent-centered prerogatives; satisficing consequentialism; scalar consequentialism of the Slote/Norcross sort; rule consequentialism; a compliance-conditional or collective view on which individual obligations shrink when others aren't doing their share; and the Kantian or contractualist reply that the objection simply mistakes consequentialism's structure for morality's. At least one combination of answers on your tree should end in an unoccupied leaf: give that combination a one-sentence diagnosis of whether it is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Flag any pair of named positions your tree sorts identically and say what further question would be needed to separate them.

Present the splitting questions first, as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences and phrased as a question; then the leaves as a numbered list of exactly seven items, each one or two sentences stating the position and its most awkward commitment. No introduction or conclusion.
