I'm in a labor studies reading group and we've all been asked to write down our positions on unions and strikes so the others can try to poke holes in them. Here's what I came up with:

1. The right to strike is grounded in basic individual freedoms — the freedom to quit your job and the freedom of association. Nobody should be forced to work against their will, alone or together with others.

2. Sympathy strikes are fine for the same reason — it's just more workers, at a different company, excercising the same freedoms in solidarity.

3. Secondary boycotts aimed at neutral third-party businesses should stay illegal though, since those drag in parties who have no dispute with anyone.

4. Employers shouldn't be allowed to permanently replace striking workers (temporary replacements during the strike itself are fine).

5. It's legitimate for a union to sign a no-strike clause for the duration of a collective agreement, and those clauses should be enforceable against the members.

6. Police and firefighters can be barred from striking because of the harm to the public, but transit workers and hospital nurses shouldn't be.

Go through these and tell me which pairs pull against each other and how badly. For each genuine tension, explain what's generating it, give the cheapest fix, and say what that fix costs me. Also flag any tensions that look bad at first but dissolve once you think them through, so I can tell the difference. Finally, if you had to change exactly one commitment to make the set hang together as well as possible, which would it be?
