Two members of my experimental philosophy reading group have argued for a semester about the Knobe effect: the robust asymmetry whereby an agent who knowingly brings about a harmful side effect (a chairman who harms the environment as a foreseen consequence of a profit program) is judged to have acted intentionally, while an agent in a structurally identical case who brings about a helpful side effect is not. One is a moralized-concept theorist: she holds that the folk concept of intentional action is itself partly evaluative, so that moral assessment enters into the concept's application conditions rather than contaminating its downstream use. The other is a pragmatist: he holds that the folk concept is amoral and purely causal-psychological, and that the asymmetry arises because saying "intentionally" in ordinary conversation does the work of assigning blame and flagging norm violations, so the effect tracks perceived norm violation in general rather than moral badness in particular. Write the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a numbered list of claims both accept, including what the basic experiments do and do not show, which deflationary explanations (demand characteristics, comprehension failure, mere valence matching) both agree fail, and what each camp's theory is genuinely committed to; then the precise statements of where they still disagree, each worded so that its own side would accept the formulation, explicitly separating genuine disagreements from any that turn out to be verbal; then the cruxes — candidates include whether non-moral norm violations (conventional, statistical, or aesthetic) generate the same asymmetry, whether the effect survives designs that strip out blame-implicating features, whether young children display the asymmetry before they reliably make the relevant moral judgments, and whether probes other than "intentionally" (such as "in order to," or desire and belief attributions) pattern the same way; and, for each crux, one concrete experiment or vignette design both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Present the disagreements as a table.
