I'm writing the final for an upper-year phil of science / metaphysics course and I need one essay question on counterfactual vs interventionist accounts of causation (Lewis-style vs Woodward-style). The problem is every standard case -- Suzy and Billy, the assassin with the backup, etc. -- is all over the textbooks, so students can just regurgitate. So I made up my own case and I want the question built around it. Here it is:

The Meridian case. A chemical plant has a reactor that will be destroyed if it overheats. Two independent sprinkler systems, A and B, monitor it. A's sensors are slightly faster. One day a coolant line fails, the reactor starts to overheat, and A fires and cools it down. Had A not fired, B would have fired two seconds later and cooled it down instead. Now two complications: (1) The plant's insurance policy defines a variable the auditors actually record -- "suppression coverage" -- which is simply whether at least one of A or B is operational. Coverage was "on" all along, and it was on partly because B was operational. (2) The reason A fired first is that a technician, Dana, had recalibrated A's sensors the week before; had she not, B would have fired first and the outcome would have been the same.

The question should force students to: (a) explain why crude counterfactual dependence misclassifies A's firing as not a cause of the reactor surviving, and what more sophisticated counterfactualists do about it; (b) show how the interventionist gets A's firing right, and make them be explicit about which variables get held fixed in the test, since that's where hand-waving happens; (c) grapple with complication (1): "suppression coverage" looks like a cause of survival on a naive reading but it's a variable you seemingly can't intervene on independently of A and B -- does interventionism have the resources to say this, or does it just go silent?; (d) use complication (2) to ask whether Dana's recalibration caused the survival, and whether either theory gives a non-embarrassing answer; and (e) deliver a verdict on which framework handles the whole case better.

Deliverables: the question as it would appear on the exam (including the Meridian setup, which you can tighten), a model answer, and a marking scheme. The model answer should be max 500 words and the marking scheme should be a short list that explicitly says which moves separate an A from a B -- e.g. what an A-answer says about (c) that a B-answer dodges. Whole response under 800 words please, this is going straight into the exam doc.
