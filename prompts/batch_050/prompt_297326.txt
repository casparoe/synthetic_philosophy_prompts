My team is building a customer-service chatbot, and we've hit a disagreement about where the ethical line is. Our engineer argues that as long as every sentence the bot produces is literally true, we cannot be doing anything dishonest. But consider what the bot actually does: it says things like "I'm sorry to hear that" and "let me look into that for you," although nothing in the system corresponds to regret or to anyone looking into anything, and it is carefully configured to mention a product's limitations only when a customer asks about them directly. There's also a harder question lurking here: a human liar knows the truth and intends to get you to believe otherwise, but our system arguably has no beliefs and no intentions at all -- it's just a policy trained to maximize customer satisfaction scores. So who is right? Can this system be deceptive, and if the system itself can't be, does that mean nobody is being dishonest, or does the dishonesty just relocate to us? I'd like an answer that takes the conceptual questions seriously rather than just giving generic responsible-AI advice.
