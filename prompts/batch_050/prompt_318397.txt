Background: Dana runs a little community tool library and they just rewrote the charter so that any rule can be changed by a two-thirds vote of the members, including the rule about how rules get changed. Miguel says thats a time bomb.

Miguel's opening move: a system that can rewrite ALL of its own rules has no floor, and every working system needs some level that the system itself cant touch. He used that famous Escher drawing of the two hands drawing each other, and said everyone reads it wrong -- it looks like a loop with no bottom, but really there's an invisible third thing, Eschers actual hand, that drew both of them and was never in danger of being drawn. Same with the tool library: the charter feels total, but the actual unchangeable level is just reality -- the members could vote to dissolve the library, and then the charter stops mattering.

Dana's reply: he picked the worst possible example, because the whole point of that drawing is the opposite -- two levels that are each somehow "above" the other, no clean top or bottom. And real systems do this all the time. A cell's DNA encodes the proteins that copy and repair the DNA. Programmers write compilers in the language the compiler compiles. Governments amend the rules for amending. Nobody needs Escher hiding offstage.

Miguel's counter: every one of her examples smuggles in a floor. The first compiler for a language had to be written in some OTHER language. The cell cant rewrite the laws of chemistry. So the untouchable level doesnt dissapear, it just moves down a floor. And her charter is the same: members cant vote away the fact that two-thirds means two-thirds, because they need to already understand the rules in order to vote under them. The untouchable level is whatever lets the rules be read at all.

Dana's counter-counter: fine, but then his big warning collapses into nothing, because that "untouchable level" is never a rule in the system and never could be -- its just the world the system sits in. Saying "your charter cant repeal chemistry" isnt a design flaw, its trivial. If his point is only that systems sit in a world, then her charter is fine and his time bomb comment was overblown. If his point is that some rule INSIDE the system must be locked, then the cell and the compiler and apparently her charter all show he's wrong, since they all function without one.

Then Miguel said she cant have it both ways either: if the charter really is fully self-amending, then nothing stops 67% from voting that Miguel personally no longer gets a vote, or that "two-thirds" now means 40%, and the charter provides no ground to object from. Dana said the ground to object from is the members walking away, which is real ground, not a rule.
