I've been reading philosophy of physics for fun lately (started with a pop-science book, ended up knee deep in the Newton's bucket literature, as one does). While poking around the Principia in the old Motte translation I got stuck on a sentence in the discussion of Definition III: "motion and rest, as commonly conceived, are only relatively distinguished; nor are those bodies always truly at rest, which commonly are taken to be so." So Newton concedes right there that the rest we actually ever deal with is rest-relative-to-stuff, but he still wants a *true* rest behind it, which by its nature we can't point at. Fine, that's the classic debate. But reading the back-and-forth over the centuries I started noticing a move that repeats, and I can't find a word for it. When the unobservable background thing gets attacked as metaphysical excess, its defenders never just defend it as-is. They swap it for a thinner, more abstract version that does the same job. Absolute space becomes rotation relative to the fixed stars, that becomes rotation relative to distant matter, and by the time you get to the moderns it's some structure of spacetime itself, which everyone agrees is nothing like a giant invisible container, but which -- suspiciously -- still tells you whether you're really spinning. Everytime the posit gets caught being unobservable, it sheds the offending property and continues it's career one level of abstraction up.

Second instance, seperate field, same shape: the light medium. Light is a wave, waves need a something, so there's a something; it has to be rigid enough for fast transverse waves but dragless, everywhere but weightless, and by the end the only property left is "that which is truly at rest," at which point people said fine, we've deleted it. What strikes me is that this is NOT the usual bad move where a theory gets patched with epicycles and gets clunkier and harder to test -- I know there's a literature on that. This is the opposite direction: the theory gets cleaner and more elegant at each step, the posit survives by subtraction rather than addition, and it only dies when it has thinned itself into literally nothing, or arguably never dies at all and just becomes "the math." Does this pattern have a name? And please don't just hand me a label -- I want the carving. Where exactly does this differ from an ad hoc rescue, from a degenerating research program, from an honest case of one theory replacing another (caloric to kinetic theory: is that the same pattern or the contrasting case? why?). And is Newton's own move in that Definition III sentence already step one of the pattern -- i.e. is the posit born pre-thinned, or does the thinning only start under attack? If there's genuinely no established term for this, tell me so and coin one, with a definition sharp enough that I could sort real historical cases into "this pattern" vs "not this pattern."
