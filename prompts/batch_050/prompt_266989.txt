The exam question was: "Explain the problem of temporary intrinsics and why it is thought to pose a difficulty specifically for endurantists. Set out how an endurantist and a perdurantist each respond to it, making clear what 'wholly present' and 'temporal part' mean. Which response do you find more satisfactory, and why?"

The student's answer:

"Endurantism is the view that ordinary objects persist by being wholly present at each time at which they exist: all of the object is there at every moment of its career, so the object sitting here now is strictly and numerically identical to the object that was here yesterday. Perdurantism is the view that objects persist by having temporal parts. A persisting object is a four-dimensional whole extended across time, sometimes called a space-time worm, and at any given moment only one temporal part, or stage, of it is present.

The problem of temporary intrinsics, due to David Lewis, is this. Consider a person who is bent when seated and straight when standing. Shape is an intrinsic property: a matter of how something is in itself, not in relation to other things. If the seated person and the standing person are numerically identical, then one and the same object has two incompatible intrinsic properties, bent and straight, and that seems impossible. The problem bears specifically on endurantists, because only the endurantist says that the very same object, wholly present at both times, is the bearer of both properties.

The endurantist's standard response is to deny that anything is bent simpliciter: being bent is instead a relation the object bears to a time, so the object is bent-at-t1 and straight-at-t2, and there is no contradiction, just as something can be to the left of one thing and to the right of another. Lewis's objection to this response is that it is ad hoc. He grants that relativizing properties to times avoids any formal contradiction, but complains that positing a special time-indexed relation for every intrinsic property is ontologically extravagant and otherwise unmotivated.

The perdurantist's response is to say that the incompatible shapes are borne by different objects: the bent stage is bent simpliciter and the straight stage is straight simpliciter, and since these are distinct temporal parts of the worm, no single thing has incompatible properties. On the perdurantist picture, ordinary names like 'Socrates' refer to the stages, and claims about what Socrates did or will do are made true by counterpart relations holding between stages. Because ordinary objects are four-dimensional on this view, perdurantists also have a ready treatment of the statue and the lump of clay: the statue and the lump share their temporal parts during the period when they coincide but differ in their parts at other times, so they are distinct four-dimensional wholes.

I find the perdurantist response more satisfactory. The endurantist escapes the problem only by denying that shape is genuinely intrinsic, whereas the perdurantist preserves intrinsic properties at the cost of accepting temporal parts, which the analogy with spatial parts makes unmysterious: we already accept that objects have spatial parts, so accepting temporal parts is no great further step."
