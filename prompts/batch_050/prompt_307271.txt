A friend and I spent last evening arguing about theories of truth and ended up talking past each other: he treated every deflationary view as the same position (truth is not a real property, end of story), while I insisted the deflationists disagree deeply among themselves and that some substantive views are closer to deflationism than to each other. To settle it, I want a decision tree that sorts the main theories of truth, built from the logical space rather than from the textbook list. Each internal node should be a single yes/no question phrased so that someone can answer it without knowing any labels or jargon -- ideally a concrete commitment, e.g., whether a claim like "everything the witness said is true" requires any property beyond the individual claims it covers, whether truth does explanatory work (say, in explaining why people with true beliefs tend to succeed), and whether the Liar paradox forces a revision to logic or to the concept. The leaves should be: correspondence, coherence, pragmatist, epistemic/verificationist, redundancy theory, disquotationalism, minimalism, and prosententialism. Each leaf gets one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Mark any combination of answers no one actually defends, and say in a phrase whether it is empty because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. If two views cannot be separated by any question in the tree, flag that explicitly rather than papering over it. Maximum 600 words.
