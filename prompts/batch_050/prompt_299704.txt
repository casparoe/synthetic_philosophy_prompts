Roughly, a commitment race is a situation where a party can gain an advantage over a rival by being the first to bind itself, credibly and as irreversibly as possible, to a course of action -- because once the commitment is locked in, the rival has to take it as given and adjust -- but where both sides racing to commit first can leave everyone worse off than if neither had committed, partly because rushing commitments cuts corners on safety and control. Write an essay analyzing the US--Soviet nuclear arms race through this lens. Map the theory carefully first: who the players were, what actually counted as a commitment (doctrines like massive retaliation, hardware like MIRVed missiles, postures like launch-on-warning), and why committing before the other side seemed to pay. Then be honest about where the model strains: the race eventually slowed through arms control like the ABM Treaty and SALT, which looks like the two sides agreeing to de-commit, and the theory needs to explain how that's possible if commitments are supposed to be irreversible. Finally, tell me what the episode shows about the theory itself -- does the arms race confirm it, does the theory need an account of how commitment races can be called off, or is the historical fit weaker than it first appears?
