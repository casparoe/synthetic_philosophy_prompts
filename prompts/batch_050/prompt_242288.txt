i'm drafting a paper on the demandingness objection to consequentialism and before i survey what anyone has actually said i want the spec first: what would an adequate response to the objection have to do to count as one? write the desiderata for me.

to be clear what i mean by a response: anything from biting the bullet (yes, morality really does demand you give until the marginal utility of your last dollar is lower for you than for its recipient) to limiting principles (agent-centered prerogatives, satisficing, hybrid views) to dissolving the objection (the theory was never in the advice-giving business to begin with). i don't want you to pick a winner yet. i want the requirements list any of these would have to satisfy.

some things i'd want the list to wrestle with, though you should add your own:

- the cases it has to get right. at minimum: Singer-style pond cases, ordinary decent non-saintly lives (the person who donates 2% and goes to the movies), and what i call the ratchet problem -- each time you comply and sacrifice, your own marginal utility of resources rises relative to before, but global need is basically unchanged, so the point of "enough" seems to recede the more you give. does a response owe us an answer to where compliance actually stops?
- the distinctions it can't collapse. e.g. between a theory being demanding to *comply with* vs demanding to *believe or internalize* (these seem to generate different objections and the first might survive even if the second is fatal). also between blame and requirement: a theory could demand the max while withholding blame from non-compliers -- is that an escape hatch or a cheat?
- here's one i haven't seen discussed much: imagine a pill that makes helping others costless to you (no opportunity cost, no alienation, you'd enjoy it). if a theory's demandingness problem evaporates in that world, does that show the objection was really about welfare economics all along, or is there a residue (the "one thought too many" style complaint -- that even costless, being *required* to orient your whole deliberation outward is the problem)? a good desiderata list should say whether handling the pill case is required.
- the meta-question i most want treated: is livability even a legitimate desideratum of a *true* moral theory, or only of a public/action-guiding one? a response needs to take a stand on which game it's playing.

for each desideratum give the case or consideration motivating it, and mark which you think are non-negotiable vs which are just one camp's prejudice. flag any subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable -- my guess is something like "ordinary decent lives are permissible" + "no deep moral asymmetry between killing and failing to save" + "the pond intuition" can't all hold, but tell me if i'm wrong. then say which single requirement knocks out the most existing responses, and only after that, briefly, score the main contenders against the list (bullet-biting, Scheffler-style prerogatives, satisficing, rule-consequentialism, the blame/requirement split). keep the scoring short, the criteria are the point.

and at the end tell me how confident you are in the list as a whole and single out the one desideratum or claim in it you're least sure of and why.
