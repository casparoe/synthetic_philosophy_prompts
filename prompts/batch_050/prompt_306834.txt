Write a satirical op-ed, around 500-600 words, in the voice of a man who has discovered pure time preference (the idea that future goods count for less just because they're later, not because of uncertainty or expected growth) and has decided to apply it with total consistency to his own life. He's thrilled: eating the whole cake tonight is rational because tomorrow's slice is worth less, saving for retirement is a scam perpetrated on behalf of a heavily discounted stranger, and his procrastination is not a vice but a discount rate. The humor should come from him getting the economics right -- he should sound like someone who's read the Stern Review debate, knows the difference between exponential and hyperbolic discounting, and can sneer at people whose time-inconsistent behavior he considers mere arithmetic illiteracy -- while the reader slowly notices that what he's defending as rigor is just being a slob with a spreadsheet. The kicker: he should gesture smugly at the fact that governments use basically his logic when they discount future generations in cost-benefit analysis, and not seem to realize this is the one place a reader might think he's accidentally making a point. Keep it deadpan, no winking, and don't have him break character at the end.
