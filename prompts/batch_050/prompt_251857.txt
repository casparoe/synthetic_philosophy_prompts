He wheels this out whenever someone raises concerns about our recommendation models being manipulative or unmoored from what users actually want -- his line is that our models are "doing the same thing brains do", so the concerns are overblown. Audit the analogy for me. Give me the correspondence table: what plays the role of the model weights, the loss function, the training data, the gradient updates. Then tell me where it breaks. Two places I've half-read about and want properly explained: first, brains apparently have a second way of reducing prediction error that our models don't -- instead of updating the prediction to fit the world, they can act on the world so it fits the prediction (you turn your head, you reach for the cup, you steer towards familiar surroundings). Second, if minimising surprise is really the whole game, why doesn't the brain just seek out a dark quiet room where everything is perfectly predictable? Once those disanalogies are on the table, does his conclusion survive, or was the analogy smuggling it in? And if there's a better analogy for what predictive brains and recommender systems actually share, give me that instead. Please write your answer in American English.
