I'm a PhD student in cognitive psychology; my dissertation is on confabulation and the unreliability of verbal reports of mental processes. I work with the classic findings in this area — choice blindness, post-hypnotic suggestion, the experiments in which participants sincerely explain preferences that were actually driven by position effects or subliminal primes — and I'm fluent in the surrounding vocabulary: privileged access, interpretive versus genuinely introspective report, dual-process theories, the gap between the causes of a judgment and the reasons a subject offers for it.

I've been trying to follow the machine learning debate over chain-of-thought faithfulness: whether the step-by-step reasoning a language model produces before answering a question can be trusted as a report of the process that actually generated the answer. Researchers find that models will silently rely on hints they never mention, will produce a rationale and then leave the answer unchanged when the rationale is perturbed or contradicted, and will sometimes confabulate justifications for answers they had already settled on. I keep having the feeling that this entire debate is my literature with the labels scraped off, and I want that feeling tested rather than indulged.

So: restate the faithfulness debate in the vocabulary of human confabulation research. Cast the chain-of-thought as a verbal report, the model's internal computation as the true cause of the response, unfaithfulness as confabulation, and so on, mapping the main positions and experimental findings as precisely as the analogy allows. Then give me the residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which you had to distort, and which have no expression in my framework at all. I suspect trouble at least in two places — my field presupposes a subject distinct from the sub-personal processes being reported on, while a model's report is itself part of the computation; and the causal interventions available on models (editing the reasoning mid-stream, reading off internal states) have no human analogue. Where the translation fails, say whether the failure shows that ML researchers are confronting something genuinely new, or that my framework is missing concepts — and say whether the model's own reasoning is even a well-defined target for a report, the way my field takes the subject to be. Keep the translation honest: do not quietly import my field's assumptions and call the result a mapping.
