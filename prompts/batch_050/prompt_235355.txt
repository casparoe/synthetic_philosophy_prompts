Okay so here's a fun one. I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm drafting a piece about emoji, data viz, and whether the internet has accidentally built the universal picture language the Vienna Circle dreamed about a century ago. For a sidebar, I want a serious counterfactual (well, semi-serious): what would Otto Neurath make of all this?

For context, and to anchor your answer, here's what I take his core commitments to be. Neurath spent decades building ISOTYPE, an international picture language for public education. Key principles: quantities should be shown by repeating the same symbol, never by stretching one symbol bigger; a trained "transformer" should mediate between the statistician's numbers and the artist's pictures; pictures cut across language barriers and can educate ordinary people in a way text can't — his slogan was, roughly, that pictures unite where words divide; and the whole thing was meant to be standardized and international, not a private artistic style.

Now: act as a historian of analytic philosophy who specializes in the Vienna Circle but also genuinely knows modern internet culture and data visualization. Think carefully and reason from his actual commitments — I don't want generic "he'd be fascinated" mush. Start with a short brainstorm of his principles, then derive predictions from them.

Specifically I want his predicted take on:

1. Emoji as a de facto global picture language, including the fact that a private consortium votes each year on which new pictures get admitted to the standard. Is this his dream realized or his dream corrupted — no transformer discipline, inconsistent rendering across platforms, piles of decorative redundancy?

2. Modern public data-viz: COVID dashboards, the blue-to-red climate stripes graphic, flatten-the-curve charts. There's alot of "chart crime" discourse out there — would he see the transformer role as alive and well, or as having been eaten by software?

3. Whether he'd revise any ISOTYPE rules for an interactive, zoomable era. Does the rule that bigger quantities mean more symbols rather than bigger symbols still hold?

4. Where he'd be caught in a genuine tension, e.g. his faith that pictures are neutral and uniting vs. modern worries about visualization-driven misinformation.

My editors occassionally let me run these speculative sidebars, but they hate hand-waving, so flag anywhere the historical record is too thin for a confident prediction instead of making stuff up.
