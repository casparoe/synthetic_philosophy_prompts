A friend and I have just emerged from a long and faintly accusatory argument about Pascal’s mugging. She defended a rule she called Unbounded Stake Escalation: for any finite payoff, however grotesque, there is some nonzero probability at which refusing to act becomes irrational, so a sufficiently creative threat or promise can always defeat ordinary caution. I maintained that this turns practical reason into a permit office for extortionists. Please resolve our dispute indirectly by writing a satirical in-universe document from a world that has taken her side.

Produce Compliance Bulletin No. 7 of the Municipal Bureau of Astronomical Contingencies, a deadpan interdepartmental memo with embedded FAQ and one short incident addendum, approximately 700-850 words, in the voice of a capable, exhausted civil service that sincerely administers expected-utility maximization under arbitrarily small probabilities. The target is fanatical longtermist decision procedure, probabilistic blackmail, utility inflation by description, and the bureaucratization of impossible stakes; do not attack any named living person, and invent officials, forms, acronyms, and precedents as needed.

The humor must arise from real pressure points of the view rather than from caricature: the absence of a principled credence floor short of zero; the ease of manufacturing vast finite utilities by appending simulated persons, cosmic audits, counterfactual torturers, or moral mirror-universes; the instability between paying once and becoming permanently payable; the difference between model error and genuine evidence; the asymmetry of threats and promises; the tendency of due process to become a tribute-collection mechanism; and the quiet disappearance of mundane goods such as lunch, pensions, sleep, and the fire code. Include procedures for intake of apocalyptic emails, ranking rival claimants who each offer larger exponentiated outcomes, vetting whether a claimed catastrophe is merely very surprising or also action-guiding, reimbursement rules when staff privately mortgage their homes after a persuasive voicemail, and etiquette for telling a mugger that the Bureau cannot currently verify his authority over 10^80 descendants.

Keep the irony legible without winking: no asides to the reader, no overt statement of the objections, no citations, bibliography, or reading list. The tone should remain courteous, procedural, and mildly soothing throughout, with numbered clauses, annex references, rubber-stamp solemnity, and euphemisms such as stakeholder alignment and downside realization. End with an official next step that seems perfectly sensible within the Bureau and, by that very fact, shows that the whole edifice has become an elegantly stamped surrender note.
