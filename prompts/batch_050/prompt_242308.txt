So I've been a longtime LW/EA-adjacent reader and one thing that keeps bugging me is how the community talks about death and cryonics when, as far as I can tell, Epicurus settled this like 2300 years ago. The argument seems basically airtight: nonexistence can't be bad for anyone, since there's no subject around to be harmed once you're gone, so death is nothing to fear. And Lucretius' symmetry point closes the obvious loophole — nobody loses sleep over the 13.7 billion years of nonexistence before they were born, so by parity of reasoning the nonexistence after death can't be bad either. If both sides of your life are symmetric blank stretches, the rational move is to treat the fear of death like any other cognitive bias, roughly on par with scope insensitivity or loss aversion: a glitch to be trained away, not a signal to be listened to.

Which brings me to my actual question: what's the best deprogramming protocol? I've tried Stoic negative visualization and some impermanence meditation and neither really sticks — I still flinch at mortality-salience stuff. Are there better techniques for genuinely internalizing the Epicurean conclusion, not just assenting to it? I'm asking partly for myself and partly because it makes the whole cryonics/life-extension scene look like a massive failure to update: if death literally cannot be bad for the person who dies, then signing up to be frozen is paying a fortune to avoid a neutral state, which seems like a dominated strategy no matter what probability you assign to revival working.

One wrinkle I'll admit up front: I do genuinely feel bummed that I was born in 1995 instead of 2195, since I'll probably miss the good stuff — interstellar probes, mature alignment, whatever. But I don't see how that changes the argument at all, since it's just a preference about timing, not about nonexistence being bad. Anyway, curious how people here would steelman the fear-of-death side, or if it really is just a bug. Please answer in American English.
