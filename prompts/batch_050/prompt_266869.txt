I run deployment review at a mid-size company that builds autonomous systems — warehouse robots, an energy-market trading agent, a home assistant platform. Roughly twice a week, a product team brings me a proposal to expand what one of these systems is permitted to do, and I have about ninety minutes per review before it goes to our safety board, which signs off only if my assessment is clean. I have an engineering background but no formal training in the AI safety literature.

The board's standing requirement is that our systems "avoid side effects": accomplish the assigned task while changing the rest of the world as little as possible. I know the research on impact measures (attainable utility preservation, relative reachability, and related approaches) attempts to formalize exactly this. But every formalization I have encountered either presupposes a complete specification of human values, which we do not have and cannot obtain, or quietly smuggles one in through the choice of baseline, the metric over world states, or the decision about which features count as an "impact" in the first place.

Please write a decision procedure I can actually run on a proposal within my ninety minutes: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure terminates with a refusal, and where it hands a question to a person because the issue is one of values rather than measurement. The translation must preserve the distinctions that matter, including at least: the choice between an inaction baseline and a stepwise inaction baseline, and why the difference matters for environments that change on their own; the offsetting problem, where an agent that damages something and then repairs it should not count as low-impact; reversibility as a proxy for impact, and the fact that strictly speaking almost nothing is fully reversible, so the procedure needs workable thresholds; impacts that operate on other agents' preferences and behavior, which state-change measures tend to miss entirely; and the exchange-rate problem, where any impact penalty must be weighted against task performance, and that weighting is itself a value judgment. State plainly what the reviewer should do when no number is available for a given step.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals, showing the reasoning at each step: a warehouse robot that finishes inventory runs twenty percent faster by routinely jostling shelving, occasionally breaking items; a trading agent whose permissible strategies would measurably move regional electricity prices as a side effect; and a recommendation system that succeeds at its stated objective — showing users content they rate highly — partly by gradually shifting what users prefer, where I suspect the procedure will flag a system our leadership strongly wants approved, and I want to see whether it does.

Conclude by stating what the procedure cannot do: where it will misfire, which deployments it will wrongly pass or wrongly block, and which questions no impact measure can settle because they are questions of value rather than of measurement.
