So my sci-fi book club got into a huge fight last week over mind uploading and I want to settle it with a poll of sorts. The argument wasn't the usual "would a copy of you be you" thing, everyone there already had rehearsed takes on that. It was about *gradual* replacement, and specifically about which details of the process actually matter. So here's what I'd like: below are six replacement procedures a hypothetical neurotech company offers. Rank them from "I'd sign up tomorrow, confident I'd wake up still being me" to "that's just an elaborate way of dying while something else takes notes," and for each one give a sentence or two on why it lands where it does.

1. Neuron-by-neuron nanobot replacement over ten years, one neuron swapped for a functionally identical synthetic unit every few hours, while you're awake and living your life. No downtime.
2. Same nanobot process but compressed into 48 hours, done under anesthesia. You go to sleep biological, wake up synthetic.
3. Hemisphere-at-a-time: the left hemisphere is replaced over a month while the right keeps running the show, then once the synthetic left has been online and integrated for a year, the right is done too.
4. Replacement at the level of whole brain regions rather than single neurons, but very slowly -- one small region every six months, over roughly 25 years, so at any moment 95%+ of the original tissue is still there.
5. No replacement at all: synthetic neurons are *added* alongside the biological ones and gradually take over the computational work as the biological neurons naturally die off over 30 years, until nothing biological is left. Nothing is ever removed.
6. The whole brain is scanned destructively in one afternoon and emulated perfectly, but -- here's the twist -- the emulation is then run for a year in a simulated body while your biological body, reconstructed from the scan data with vat-grown neurons, lives your life in parallel. At the end of the year one of the two is switched off, and you get to pick which, after talking to both.

Once you've ranked them, tell me which variable the ranking is most sensitive to: is it speed, continuity of consciousness, whether anything is destroyed, the proportion of original matter at each moment, or something else? And be honest about the case where your ranking does the weirdest work -- like, if you rank 1 above 2, what exactly are you trusting that a 48-hour version breaks? The book club member I'm most trying to beat insists 1 through 5 are all obviously fine and only 6 is death, and I suspect his view collapses into either "speed is everything" or "it's all fine including instantaneous destructive upload," neither of which he'll admit to. If you think he's actually right, say so, but show me the principle that separates 5 from 6 without also separating 1 from 2.
