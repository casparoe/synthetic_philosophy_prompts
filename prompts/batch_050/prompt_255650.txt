Two friends of mine had a row I couldn't referee and I'd like you to adjudicate it properly. Background: one of them, Mina, has a brother on trial for fraud. The evidence against him is pretty strong, but she told us she believes he's innocent, and when pressed she said believing in him is the only way she can be a decent sister to him through the trial, and that this is a genuine reason to believe it.

Dev said no: that's a reason to *want* to believe him, or to act supportive, but not a reason to believe he's innocent — like when a gunman says he'll shoot you unless you admire him, the threat is a reason to somehow make yourself admire him, not a reason that counts in favour of admiration itself, since it's got nothing to do with whether he's admirable.

Mina said the distinction smells fishy: a reason is just a consideration that counts in favour of an attitude, full stop, and if loyalty counts in favour then it's a reason; philosophers who call it "the wrong kind" are just stipulating a rule that beliefs can only be supported by evidence, which is exactly what's in question.

Dev replied that her view has a mad consequence: if someone offers you £500 to believe the earth is flat, that's then a real reason to believe the earth is flat, sitting on the scales against the evidence, and a theory of reasons with that result has gone wrong somewhere.

Mina's last move was to concede the gunman and the £500 cases but deny they generalise: those reasons are external to the relationship, whereas the loyalty reason flows from what being a sister constitutively involves, and relationships partly set what it's reasonable to believe — parents who instantly accept guilty verdicts against their kids are worse parents, not more rational ones. Dev said this just redescribes wishful thinking as virtue.

Can you go through it move by move — who's right about each step, not just who wins overall — and say what both of them might be missing? And please be honest about the state of play: mark clearly what's settled ground in the literature on reasons for attitudes versus what's a genuinely live dispute where you'd be taking a side, rather than presenting one camp's view as the consensus. In British English, please.
