A few months ago I took on a mentoring role at my firm, and watching one of my mentees in meetings has pushed a pattern into view that I can't find a word for. She is believed. When she reports that a client's numbers don't reconcile, or that a certain bug has appeared three times this month, everyone accepts it without question — people take notes, the facts enter the record. But when she goes one step further and offers her account of what is going on — why the numbers don't reconcile, what the bugs have in common — the room simply moves on, as though she had stopped talking after the facts. Then, sometimes weeks later, a senior colleague presents substantially the same account as his own analysis, and it lands as an insight, and he gets credit for it. What unsettles me most is that I don't think the senior colleague is lying or even consciously stealing; I genuinely believe he doesn't register that she said it first. It is as if her factual reports are heard as information but her interpretive contributions are not heard as contributions at all — they only become audible once they come out of someone else's mouth.

I've since started noticing the same shape elsewhere. At a community board meeting I attend, an elderly resident described patterns in how the neighborhood has changed; her observations were received warmly as anecdotes and local color, but her explanation of what was driving them was ignored until the hired consultant said something nearly identical in slide-deck language, at which point it became "the analysis." I've read a little of the literature on epistemic injustice — I know Fricker's notion of testimonial injustice, where a speaker's word is discounted because of prejudice — but this doesn't seem to be that, because no one is discounting her word. Her credibility about facts is, if anything, above average. What's being refused is something further downstream: the standing to be a source of interpretation, explanation, or theory rather than merely of data.

Does this phenomenon have a name, and if so, how does it relate to and differ from testimonial injustice and the other notions in that neighborhood — is it a species of one of them, or a distinct thing? I'd also like help locating its edges: does it count if the senior colleague would eventually have reached the same analysis on his own? Does it require that the reframer actually picked the idea up from her, or is the damage done whenever her contribution is inaudible as a contribution, whatever its causal fate? And if there is no established term for it, what would you call it? Give me a definition and a couple of cases that fall just outside the boundary, so I can tell the concept is doing real work rather than just relabeling my anecdote.
