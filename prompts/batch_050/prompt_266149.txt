A colleague and I each flipped a fair coin twenty times. My sequence came out perfectly alternating (HTHTHTHT...); his came out jumbled, with no visible pattern. He insists his sequence is random and mine is not, even though both had exactly the same probability of 2^-20, and he defends this by appealing to Kolmogorov complexity: his string is incompressible, mine is not. I suspect he is gesturing at something real but mislocating it. Give me your credence that randomness can be a property of a finite sequence itself, rather than only of the process that produced it, decomposed: the probability that some mathematical account (Kolmogorov complexity, Martin-Löf randomness, or similar) captures the intuitive concept of randomness rather than merely replacing it with a well-behaved substitute; the probability that the pre-theoretic intuition being captured is itself coherent, as opposed to a confusion between frequencies, compressibility, and unpredictability; the probability that the process/product distinction dissolves the dispute entirely; and any other terms you think belong in the derivation, each with a number and one line of justification, then how they combine. Finally, tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, and whether your answer changes when the sequence is too short for any statistical test or compression scheme to get a grip on it, say four flips.
