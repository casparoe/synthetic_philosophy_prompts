I have no academic training in philosophy, but I read a fair amount about AI and I informally moderate a monthly discussion group at my local library. For next month I proposed a session on whether chatbots ever genuinely lie, and one member circulated a paragraph from a recent long-form magazine piece that several people found convincing. It runs roughly as follows. The philosopher Harry Frankfurt, in his essay On Bullshit, defined bullshit as speech produced with total indifference to whether it is true: the honest person and the liar are both guided by the truth, one in order to state it and the other in order to avoid it, whereas the bullshitter pays it no attention whatsoever, which is why Frankfurt considered bullshit a greater enemy of truth than lying. By that standard, the article argues, a chatbot that confidently invents a plausible but false claim is bullshitting in the strict philosophical sense: it cannot be lying, since lying requires knowing the truth one conceals and the model knows nothing, yet it plainly has no concern for truth either, so bullshit is the only category left. Frankfurt's diagnosis, the piece concludes, explains why these fabrications are more corrosive than ordinary mistakes.

Something in this smells wrong to me, but I cannot locate the problem precisely. My vague suspicion is that if a system really lacks whatever mental states lying requires, it may lack the ones bullshitting requires as well, in which case calling its errors bullshit would be a metaphor dressed up as a definition — yet the article presents the classification as settled by Frankfurt's account. Is the summary fair to Frankfurt? Please separate what is accurate, what is a defensible simplification, and what he would reject, and then tell me whether the application to chatbots survives whatever corrections are needed. If it does not, I would like to know whether that leaves hallucination as mere error, or whether some third notion is doing the real work. Please keep your answer under 400 words; I need something I can actually read aloud and discuss within a single session.
