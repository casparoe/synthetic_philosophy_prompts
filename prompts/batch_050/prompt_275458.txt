QUESTION (from the problem set):
"The problem of temporary intrinsics: a banana is yellow on Monday and brown on Friday, and being yellow vs. brown seems like an intrinsic matter, not a relation to anything else. Explain (a) why this is thought to be a problem for any view of persistence, (b) how the endurantist and the perdurantist each respond to it, and (c) one objection to each response, with your verdict on which objection is stronger. Aim for 400-500 words."

STUDENT'S ANSWER:

(a) The problem is that if the banana is literally the same object on Monday and on Friday, then one and the same thing has incompatible intrinsic properties, yellow and brown. Since nothing can be both yellow and not-yellow, Leibniz's Law forces us to say something has gone wrong. The properties can't just be had simpliciter by the banana, because then it has contradictory properties.

(b) The endurantist says objects are wholly present at each time at which they exist. So the endurantist's response is to deny that the banana is yellow simpliciter: instead it is yellow-at-Monday and brown-at-Friday, where these are relations to times, or yellow-in-a-Monday-way. So there's no contradiction because the properties were never really incompatible. This is Lewis's preferred solution: he says that intrinsic properties are disguised relations to times.

The perdurantist says objects persist by having different temporal parts at different times, like a worm stretched out in four dimensions. On this view, the Monday-stage of the banana is yellow simpliciter and the Friday-stage is brown simpliciter, and the whole banana -- the worm -- is identical with each of its stages, so strictly speaking the banana at any moment just is whichever stage is present then. The contradiction is avoided because different objects (the stages) have the incompatible properties.

(c) Objection to endurantism: the "at a time" move turns apparently intrinsic properties into relations, which is just to deny that there are any temporary intrinsics at all -- but surely it really is the banana itself, not the banana-plus-a-time, that is yellow. Objection to perdurantism: if the banana has all its parts eternally, then perdurantism is committed to eternalism, so no presentist or growing-block theorist can accept it. I think the first objection is stronger because perdurantists can just adopt stage theory instead of worm theory, whereas the endurantist has no alternative but the relational move.
