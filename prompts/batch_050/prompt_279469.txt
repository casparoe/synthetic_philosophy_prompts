I'm a novelist working on a satirical scene where a burnt-out novelist attends a luxury wellness retreat run by a fictional startup called The Inward Instrument. The startup sells an expensive inner-listening program that supposedly lets customers bypass interpretation and directly observe their real motives. I was telling a friend about it and realized the funniest version would be completely deadpan, so please write the retreat's official FAQ, around 900–1,200 words, in the serene, reassuring voice of premium wellness marketing. It should definately feel like something tucked into a welcome basket beside herbal tea and a linen eye mask.

The humor should come from genuine problems about the reliability of introspection rather than random absurdity. Build in confabulation, the way people invent confident explanations for choices they made for inaccessible reasons; the transparency of belief, where deciding what you believe often means looking outward at the world rather than scanning an inner fact; and the difference between having some first-person authority and being infallible. The FAQ can mention mood-as-information, self-interpretation, suppressed desires, and post-hoc rationalization, but make the staff answer every hard question with soothing corporate confidence. For example, if a guest asks whether a powerful feeling of certainty guarantees truth, the answer should be evasive in a way that is funny precisely because the underlying issue is real.

Please don't turn the piece into a lazy dismissal of introspection. The retreat should accidentally make one fair distinction: immediate experience can be authoritative evidence about how things seem to someone without settling why they feel that way or what they truly want. Include several mock customer questions, a few increasingly strained testimonials, and a final legal disclaimer that sounds more philosophically honest than the marketing team intended. No citations, no scholarly names, and no jokes that depend on winking at the reader.
