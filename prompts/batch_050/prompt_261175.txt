A popular book on the philosophy of time devotes a chapter to Augustine's discussion in the Confessions and summarizes it as follows: 'Augustine's view is that time is nothing over and above a feature of the human mind. The past, he argues, no longer exists, and the future does not yet exist; since only what exists can be measured, and the present shrinks under analysis to a durationless instant that can no more be measured than a point can, Augustine concludes that the only place time can live is in the soul. His solution is that time is a distension of the mind: what we call the past is present memory, and what we call the future is present expectation. Time is therefore subjective in roughly the way dreams are subjective — it would not exist in a world without minds, and it places no constraints on the physical world. This is why Augustine would have rejected the modern picture of time as a dimension laid out alongside the three dimensions of space.' Something in this feels off to me — I have a vague memory that Augustine treats time as bound up with the creation of the world rather than with minds, and the comparison to dreams strikes me as stronger than anything he says — but I cannot put my finger on what exactly is wrong, and the author seems otherwise careful. Please audit the summary: separate what a careful reader of Book XI would accept, what is a defensible simplification, and what distorts Augustine's actual position (including anything important the summary omits from his account of how the present is experienced). Then tell me whether the closing inference — that Augustine's view rules out treating time as a dimension of the physical world — follows from the parts of the summary that survive correction, or whether it depends on precisely the parts that do not. Finally, a friend pressed me with this objection: if God knows the future, then the future must exist in some sense, so the summary's claim that the future does not yet exist cannot be Augustine's whole story. Does Augustine's own picture of divine eternity give him a way to hold both that the future does not exist and that God knows it, and does the summary's framing make that move easier or harder to see?
