Two scholars have spent years arguing about the age of nations, and both claim Ernest Renan as an ally. The modernist, following Hobsbawm and Anderson, holds that nations are recent constructions: invented traditions and imagined communities produced by states, mass education and print culture, and that before the late eighteenth century there were peoples, kingdoms and faiths but no nations in our sense. The perennialist holds that nations are old: that they grow out of much older ethnic communities with shared myths, names and memories, and that what modernity changed was the form and self-consciousness of nations, not their existence. Their exhibit is this passage from Ernest Renan's 1882 Sorbonne lecture, Qu'est-ce qu'une nation ?:

Les nations, entendues de cette manière, sont quelque chose d'assez nouveau dans l'histoire. L'antiquité ne les connut pas, l'Égypte, la Chine, l'antique Chaldée ne furent à aucun degré des nations. C'étaient des troupeaux menés par un fils du Soleil ou un fils du Ciel. Il n'y eut pas de citoyens égyptiens, pas plus qu'il n'y a de citoyens chinois. L'antiquité classique eut des républiques et des royautés municipales, des confédérations de républiques locales, des empires ; elle n'eut guère la nation au sens où nous la comprenons.

Draft the joint statement their adversarial collaboration should have produced. It should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims about nations and nationalism that both can sign; (2) a disagreements table in which each side's position is phrased in wording that side would itself accept, beginning with their rival readings of Renan's phrase entendues de cette manière and what work the qualification does; (3) the cruxes — I expect one to be whether continuity of ethnic name, myth and memory suffices for continuity of nation, and another to be how much weight to give mass participation against elite identity — and for each crux, a historical case or kind of evidence both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded; and (4) an agreed analysis of the passage stating what, if anything, it settles between them, and which part of their quarrel over it is verbal rather than substantive.
