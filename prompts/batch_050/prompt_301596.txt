I am drafting a short philosophical essay on the metaphysics of time and would like detailed comments before I develop it further. First, some working definitions as I am using them: presentism is the view that only presently existing things are real, so that Socrates and the first manned mission to Mars are alike unreal, differing only in that one used to be real and the other (let us suppose) will be. Eternalism is the view that past, present, and future objects and events are all equally real, with "present" functioning like "here" — an indexical marking location within a four-dimensional block rather than a boundary of being.

Here is the essay draft:

---

Eternalists standardly rest their case on the relativity of simultaneity: since which distant events count as simultaneous with my now depends on my frame of reference, and no frame is privileged, the presentist's inventory of what exists would have to be frame-dependent, which looks like an incoherent thing for an inventory of everything to be. I want to offer a different argument, one that concedes the presentist a privileged frame for the sake of argument and attacks elsewhere.

Suppose the presentist is granted a cosmic now. Her view still faces what I will call the problem of deferred identity. Consider a particle pair created in an entangled state and separated over years. The presentist says: at each moment, only the present states of the two particles exist. But the correlations we later measure are not correlations between two present states; they are correlations fixed by a past interaction and revealed across spatial separation at later times. To explain the pattern of measurement outcomes, the presentist must either (a) say the past interaction, though unreal, continues to constrain the present — in which case the unreal does real explanatory work, and we should ask why nonexistence is compatible with that kind of work — or (b) load everything into present states, so that each particle carries, in its present condition, a complete record sufficient to determine the correlations. Option (b) is not crazy; physicists call such records local hidden variables, and Bell-type results are usually taken to show that no purely local present state can do the job. The presentist might retreat to nonlocal present states — the whole universe's present configuration as one fact. But then her ontology of "the present" has become so holistic that it is no longer clear what work the restriction to the present is doing, since the whole point of presentism was a sparse, local, common-sense ontology.

My tentative conclusion is not that eternalism is true, but that presentism faces a squeeze independent of simultaneity: either the unreal past explains, which drains "unreal" of meaning, or the present must be inflated until it carries everything the past would have carried, which drains "present" of meaning.

I should acknowledge two weak spots. First, I am not sure the entanglement case adds anything beyond what any temporally extended causal process raises — a tree's rings also require the past to explain the present, and presentists have standard replies (present traces, tensed facts about how things were). Second, my dismissal of the holist retreat may be too quick; perhaps a holistic present is a respectable view and I have only shown that presentism has a surprising consequence, not a fatal one.

---

My questions for you:

1. Is the argument in the second paragraph valid as stated, and is the dilemma (a)/(b) genuinely exhaustive, or have I missed a third option a presentist would actually prefer?
2. Does the entanglement framing earn its keep, or would the essay be stronger if I ran the same dilemma on the humble tree-rings case instead?
3. Are my two acknowledged weak spots the real weak spots, or is there a more serious problem I have not noticed — either a mischaracterization of presentism, of the physics, or of what an ontology of the present commits one to?
4. Stylistically and structurally, what would you cut, and where does the essay become unclear?

In answering, please indicate how confident you are in each of your main claims, and single out the one point in your response you are least sure of.
