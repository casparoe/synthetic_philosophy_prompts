A research foundation is planning a four-year program on psychopathy and moral knowledge. It can fund five projects combining philosophy with closely related empirical work, but it cannot fund everything. Its guiding question is not simply whether psychopathic offenders “know right from wrong,” but which notion of moral knowledge is relevant to moral responsibility and criminal blame.

To make the problem tractable, distinguish at least four candidate capacities: (1) propositional classification, or the ability to state that an act is conventionally or morally wrong; (2) moral recognition, or the ability to identify wrong-making features in unfamiliar cases; (3) affective appreciation, or the capacity for wrongness to register with an appropriate emotional significance; and (4) practical authority, or the capacity for recognized wrongness to guide deliberation when doing wrong would be advantageous. Add a further complication: call a capacity portable when an agent can exercise it across changes in context, incentives, emotional state, and social pressure, and merely local when the agent can exercise it only under favorable test conditions. The foundation’s central hypothesis is that moral responsibility may require less than all four capacities but more than isolated, non-portable classification.

Which open questions should the foundation prioritize, and in what order? For each question, explain why it matters, what conceptual or empirical progress is realistically possible within four years, which other questions depend on it, and what kinds of results would move it up or down the agenda. In particular, address whether affective appreciation is itself a component of moral knowledge or only one normal route to it; how one could distinguish absent knowledge from knowledge that lacks motivational force; whether responsibility can vary by situation if an agent’s relevant capacities are portable only to some degree; and what follows for excuse, mitigation, punishment, and treatment if psychopathic agents retain some capacities while lacking others.

I am interested in a defended research agenda rather than a survey of existing views. Identify any distinctions that you think are misleading or unnecessary, note where an apparently empirical dispute is really a disagreement about the meaning of moral knowledge, and conclude by explaining which single project you would fund if only one were possible.
