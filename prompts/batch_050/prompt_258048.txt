I spend a fair amount of my free time reading online debates about advanced artificial intelligence, mostly the arguments about whether it poses a serious long-term risk and how soon any of this is supposed to happen. I have no formal training in computer science or philosophy; I just find the topic gripping. Over the past year I have noticed something odd about how these debates actually proceed, and I would like to know whether the thing I have noticed is a recognized pitfall with a name, or just an unremarked muddle that everyone lives with.

Two examples should make it concrete. Last month, in a forum I follow, someone argued that once an AI system becomes capable of substantially improving its own design, progress could go from merely impressive to far beyond human level within weeks or months. A critic replied that predictions of rapid technological upheaval have always been wrong, that researchers in the 1960s expected machine intelligence within a generation, and that things always take longer than enthusiasts think. Everyone in the thread seemed to accept this as a rebuttal, but it struck me that the critic had answered a different claim: the original argument was about how quickly things might unfold after some threshold is crossed, while the rebuttal was entirely about how far away that threshold is. Similarly, a friend of mine who thinks genuinely dangerous AI is fifty years off told me he does not worry about the topic because, in his words, "the transition, if it ever comes, will be gradual enough for society to adapt." When I pressed him, it became clear he had never treated the question of when as separate from the question of how fast. Once I started watching for this slide, I saw it everywhere: people move between "it is far away" and "it will unfold slowly" as though evidence bearing on one were evidence bearing on the other, sometimes within a single paragraph.

So here is what I am after. Are these in fact two distinct quantities in the serious literature on this topic, and if so, what are they called and how exactly are they defined and measured? Does the conflation of the two have an established name of its own? If it does not, I would like you to coin one, defined carefully enough that I can tell it apart from ordinary skepticism about predictions, from the general unreliability of forecasting, and from simple equivocation between two meanings of a word like "soon." I am less interested in the label than in the carving: what exactly the two questions are, where the boundary cases lie (for instance, an AI that arrives late but then transforms everything in a year), and why evidence about one so often fails to bear on the other. I am not looking for a bibliography or reading list, just the concepts spelled out. Please write your answer in American English, in continuous prose of the sort one would write in a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
