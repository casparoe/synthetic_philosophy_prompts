The street was empty in both directions — no headlights, no engine noise, nothing — yet every person at the curb waited out the red light, some for several minutes. When I crossed anyway, a man reprimanded me sharply and pointed to a child on the opposite curb: the point, he said, wasn't the traffic, it was what the child learns from watching adults. My American editors find the whole scene absurd — if there is no car, there is no reason to wait, and obeying a rule that serves no purpose just because it is a rule looks to them like a failure of individual judgment.

Which side has the better of it, and why? Does breaking pointless rules corrode something real, or does treating them as binding erode something more important?
