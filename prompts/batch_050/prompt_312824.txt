A classmate in my epistemology seminar offered an argument against the view that testimony is a basic source of justification, and it seems to prove far more than he intended. (1) If a hearer could be justified in accepting testimony without possessing independent grounds for the reliability of testimony, then a hearer would be justified in accepting testimony from any source whatsoever. (2) But we are plainly not justified in accepting testimony from any source whatsoever — not from the known fabulist, not from the stranger with an obvious motive to deceive. (3) So testimonial justification requires independent, non-testimonial grounds for reliability. (4) The same style of argument applies to perception and memory: if seeming to perceive that p could justify believing p without independent grounds for the reliability of perception, then any seeming-perception would justify any belief. (5) So perceptual and memorial justification likewise require independent grounds. (6) Such grounds are unavailable, since any check on a faculty must employ that very faculty. (7) Therefore no one is justified in believing anything. I refuse to accept (7), and I want to keep (2). Which step should I reject, why did it look plausible, and what does rejecting it cost?
