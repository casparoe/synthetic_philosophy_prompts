I should like a numerical estimate, with the reasoning laid bare, on a question in normative epistemology that my colleagues and I have been unable to settle over dinner.

The proposition is this, which I shall call the Modesty Thesis: on a contested philosophical question where one knows that competent, well-informed, and apparently sincere reasoners are roughly evenly divided, one's credence in one's own answer should be driven principally by the distribution of opinion — the outside view — rather than by one's own first-order assessment of the arguments, the inside view, wherever the two come apart. For definiteness, take 'principally' to mean that the outside view should dominate in any case of direct conflict, and take the thesis to concern idealised reasoners in positions recognisably like our own, not hypothetical perfect observers with unlimited processing time.

Please give your credence that the Modesty Thesis is correct as a norm of belief revision, and derive the number rather than simply asserting it. Structure your response in the following sections, in this order:

1. Precisification (roughly 100 words). Restate the thesis in the strongest defensible form you can give it. Flag any ambiguity you are resolving — for instance, what counts as a 'contested' question or as a 'competent' reasoner — and say how you are resolving it.

2. Decomposition (roughly 300 words). Identify the sub-questions on which your credence depends, and give a probability with one or two lines of justification for each. I would expect, at minimum: the probability that the inside view / outside view distinction marks a genuine difference in kind rather than a continuum; the probability that anything deserving the name of a track record exists for first-order philosophical reasoning, given the discipline's conspicuous failure to converge; the probability that conciliation towards peer opinion is truth-conducive in domains where no such track record is available; and the probability that the thesis survives self-application — that is, that being modest about modesty itself does not collapse the position. Add any further term you believe the calculation requires, and say why it is needed.

3. Aggregation (roughly 80 words). Show how the terms combine — multiplicatively, or by whatever structure you think appropriate — and state the resulting credence as a single number.

4. Sensitivity (roughly 150 words). Identify the term to which the final figure is most sensitive, and say what argument or evidence would shift it most.

5. Locating the disagreement (roughly 150 words). My own credence stands at about 0.5; a colleague whose judgement I generally respect puts hers at 0.85. Using your decomposition, say where our disagreement most plausibly lives, and name one consideration that might move each of us.

I am not interested in a survey of the literature, and I do not want citations; I want the calculation itself, with its weak points marked.
