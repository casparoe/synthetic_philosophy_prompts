For the final meeting of my undergraduate seminar on Chinese philosophy of language, I plan to close our session on Gongsun Long and the School of Names with a poem, and I would like you to write it.

Compose a villanelle on the white horse dialogue (the Bai ma lun). The two refrains should be the disputants' two utterances: Gongsun Long's thesis that a white horse is not a horse, and his interlocutor's rejoinder that of course it is. The tercets should unfold the case as the dialogue itself does — that one who asks for a horse will accept a yellow or black horse in answer, but one who asks for a white horse will not, so the two requests were never requests for the same thing — and the refrain's meaning should shift with each of its returns: patent absurdity on first hearing, then a respectable point about the non-identity of extensions once the stable test is in view, and something stranger still by the end, once it emerges that the interlocutor's retort and Gongsun Long's thesis may both be true, depending on whether the copula marks class inclusion or identity of the things named. The closing quatrain, where the two refrains finally stand side by side, should make that ambiguity visible rather than adjudicate it in Gongsun Long's favor. I also want the poem to register the dialogue's further observation that "horse" picks out a shape and "white" a color, and that naming a shape and naming a color are different acts — but without turning the poem into a prose paraphrase in line breaks.

The poem should be accurate enough that a scholar of the School of Names would recognize the argument in it rather than merely the slogan, and good enough as verse that a student with no Chinese philosophy would still find it worth reading. Please append a short prose note indicating where the poem commits to a contested reading — whether you take the thesis to concern the reference of compound terms, the relation between a kind and its instances, or the separability of the attributes themselves — and where you have taken liberties with the dialogue as we have it.

One further request: if you think the task as posed cannot be done well — if the villanelle's machinery resists this material, or if my sketch of the dialogue misrepresents it — say so plainly rather than complying politely. I would rather be corrected before the seminar than after it.
