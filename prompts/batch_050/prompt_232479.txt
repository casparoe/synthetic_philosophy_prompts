I'm supposed to write up where I stand on the Libet/free will stuff for a philosophy of mind reading group, and before I circulate it I want to know whether the position actually coheres or whether I've stitched together views that dont fit. Roughly what I believe:

1. Physicalism is true and the causal closure of the physical holds: everything my body does, including every action, is fully caused by physical goings-on in my brain and body.

2. For an action of mine to be genuinely free, though, my conscious intention has to be among the things that actually cause it. If the conscious intention is a mere afterglow of a decision the brain already made, the action isn't free in any sense worth wanting. Epiphenomenalism about conscious will would be a disaster for free will, not a footnote.

3. Libet's experiments dont show what everyone says they show. On the Schurger-style reading, the readiness potential is just random accumulation drifting towards a threshold, not the signature of an unconscious decision, so the experiments tell us essentially nothing about when decisions get made. The timing methodology is too shaky to support the dramatic conclusion.

4. That said, Libet was basically right about "free won't": even granting the setup, the subject retains a conscious veto in the final couple of hundred milliseconds, and that last-minute power to abort is, on its own, enough to secure the kind of freedom that matters.

5. The Soon et al. decoding studies are the real deal: activity in frontopolar cortex several seconds before the subject's reported awareness lets you call the button choice, which shows the choice is effectively settled well before consciousness comes on the scene.

6. None of this touches moral responsibility anyway. Responsibility attaches to the person as a whole system, and it makes no difference to that whether the decisive cause of the action was conscious or not -- which is why ordinary folk practice survives whatever the labs throw at it.

So: which of these are actually in tension, and which only look like they are? I suspect 1 and 2 get flagged a lot but I'm fairly sure they're fine together, it's the others I'm less certain about. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each one say concretely what would have to give. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, assuming I want to keep as much of the package as possible. Please do this as an audit of my position, not a survey of the literature -- I don't need a reading list, I need to know what commits me to what.
