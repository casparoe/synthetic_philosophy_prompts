I'm designing a graduate seminar on formal epistemology, and before I let my students spend three weeks reading rival proposals for how rational agents should update on evidence that arrives with less than certainty, I want to hand them the specification first: what any adequate theory of updating on uncertain evidence must deliver to count as a serious candidate. Write the desiderata -- the phenomena it must accommodate, the results it must recover, the distinctions it must respect, the pathologies it must either avoid or defend -- with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark which requirements you take to be non-negotiable for any theory worthy of the name and which look like the preferences of one camp dressed up as adequacy conditions. If some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly, since for my purposes that would be the most interesting output of the exercise, and identify which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates.

To make sure the list gets into the weeds rather than restating the textbook setup, please make sure it engages at least the following pressure points, though you should add any I've missed. First, the relation to ordinary conditionalization: must a theory of uncertain evidence be a conservative extension that reduces to conditionalization when evidence is certain, or is that demand itself contestable? Second, the rigidity condition -- the standard rule preserves an agent's conditional probabilities within the cells of the evidential partition -- should this be a theorem, a postulate, or a dispensable artifact, and what cases bear on that choice? Third, iteration: successive uncertain updates of the standard kind need not commute, so a theory must take a stand on whether order-dependence is a defect or a rational sensitivity to the sequence in which evidence arrives. Fourth, the input problem: the standard rule takes new probabilities over the partition as given and says nothing about where they come from or whether they can be criticized -- state whether silence here is an acceptable scoping or an abdication. Fifth, coherence with later certainty: if an uncertain shift over a partition is followed by learning one cell for sure, should the result match having conditionalized on the conjunction all along, and what goes wrong either way? Finally, close with a brief scoring of three contenders against your list -- strict conditionalization with the evidence-as-certainty gloss, Jeffrey's rule, and the approach that embeds uncertain evidence in a richer probability space so that updating becomes ordinary conditionalization there -- but keep it short, since the criteria and their motivating cases are the deliverable and I'll have the students do the fine-grained scoring themselves.
