I'm a junior taking a history of education seminar, and for our unit on early modern theories we read big chunks of Locke's Some Thoughts Concerning Education (the 1693 one, based on letters he wrote advising a friend about raising his son). Over the weekend I was complaining about the reading to my roommate, who's a psych major, and he confidently explained Locke to me like this: "Locke is the blank slate guy. That's why his whole book is about cramming knowledge in as early as possible and using strict discipline, including corporal punishment, to break bad habits before they form. Kids are just miniature adults to be programmed, and since the slate is blank, any child can be turned into anything if you're rigorous enough about it. Honestly it's kind of a dystopian view and it's the root of everything wrong with drill-and-grill schooling today."
