You know how mid-century anthropologists wrote up remote tribes in that slightly awed, slightly condescending register, where everyday local customs get described as bizarre rituals? I want a parody ethnography in exactly that style, but the "tribe" under study is WEIRD people (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) and their folk morality. Think a field report from a visiting ethnographer who finds their moral habits genuinely perplexing.

Some beats the piece should hit, deadpan throughout, never winking:

- The tribe's strange obsession with intentions: two villagers cause the same harm, but the one who *meant* it is treated as a different species of sinner.
- Their bizarre practice of extending moral concern to total strangers, foreigners, and even animals, while their neighbouring tribes sensibly restrict it to kin and clan.
- The ritual of the "trolley problem": elders pose hypothetical dilemmas involving runaway carts and fat men on bridges, and treat the tribe's answers as windows into universal human morality, despite the sample being almost entirely undergraduates from one continent.
- Their worship of abstract rules that apply to everyone equally, even when following the rule produces obviously worse outcomes for their own family.
- A final section where the ethnographer wonders, with total sincerity, whether this tribe's morality is the one true morality or just what happens when you grow up rich, safe, and literate -- and notes that the tribe itself has not considered the second option.

The target is the assumption (common in moral psychology and analytic ethics) that WEIRD intuitions are a stand-in for universal human morality, so the satire should come from exaggerating real features of that literature, not inventing ones. Around 800 words, formatted like a journal field report with section headings. Please write it in British English (colour, whilst, etc.), even though I'm not.
