So some background: I spent forty years as an experimental biologist (cell signaling, nothing to do with people) and since retiring I've fallen in with a philosophy reading group at the local library. This month we're on virtue ethics and I keep getting into arguments, so they told me to write down where I actually stand so everyone can pick at it next week. Here's roughly my position. (1) Character is real: some people genuinely are braver, kinder, or more honest than others, and those differences explain a decent chunk of what they do. (2) The classic social psychology experiments have convinced me that situations swamp character for most people — Milgram's obedience setup, the seminary students who stepped over a groaning man because they'd been told they were running late, that study where finding a dime in a phone booth made people dramatically more likely to help a stranger. Tiny details of the situation, not stable traits, do the causal work. (3) Despite that, I think virtue ethics is basically the right moral framework: the point of morality is to become a compassionate, honest, courageous person, and the way to get there is habituation and practice, more or less like learning to play the cello, just as Aristotle describes. (4) On blame: the seminarians who walked past the collapsed man deserve moral criticism. Saying "the situation made them do it" feels like a cop-out — if we excuse everyone whose situation nudged them, the whole idea of responsibility dissolves. (5) I also think character education in schools (habits, role models, practice at being decent) can work and is worth funding, and I said so at the last school board meeting.

The group already smells blood but nobody's told me exactly where the problem is, and honestly I can't see it myself — each of these seems defensible on it's own. So: which of these commitments actually pull against each other, and which pairs only look like they conflict but dissolve once you're careful? For each genuine tension, tell me how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be — what I'd have to give up or soften, and what that costs the rest of my position. And if you had to pick the single revision that makes the whole package most coherent with the least damage, which one is it? Please rank things by severity rather than just listing every possible friction as equally fatal, because I suspect at least one of the "tensions" the others will raise is bogus.
