Okay so for context: I run a little discussion group at my local library, mostly nonfiction and ethics stuff, and none of us have any formal training in this. Last month we got into a genuinely heated argument about consent — like, what makes a "yes" actually count as a yes. I've been making a list of all the ways consent can technically be given but still be kind of broken, and here's what I have so far:

1. Deception — you lie about a key fact and the person agrees based on the lie. Lying about being single, a fake doctor doing "exams," that sort of thing.
2. Coercion — explicit threats or blackmail. Sleep with me or you're fired, hand over the money or else.
3. Incompetence — the person can't meaningfully consent at all: too young, blackout drunk, unconscious, severe cognitive impairment.
4. Power imbalance — no actual threat is made, but the asker controls something the person needs (boss, landlord, parole officer), so "no" costs too much to say.
5. Agreeing to the wrong thing — you consent to X and they do Y instead, like signing off on one surgery and waking up having had a different one.

Here's what I want: give me ONE more way consent can fail that isn't on this list, and isn't just a rewording or special case of something already on it (so no "lying by omission," I count that as deception, and no "threat implied by the power gap," that's #4). Ideally it's a case where people could genuinely disagree about whether the consent is invalid or just a little sketchy. Explain how it's structurally different from the five above, and give me your verdict on whether it fully invalidates consent or only taints it.

One more thing, since we always end up arguing about how sure anyone should be: after your verdict, tell me what would change your mind — what case or consideration would make you decide your new item is actually just one of the five in disguise, or not a consent problem at all — and point at the step in your own reasoning where you think you're most likely to be wrong.
