Next month I'm presenting at a value theory seminar -- mostly normative ethicists who work on hard choices and incommensurability, a couple of formal epistemologists, and (importantly) nobody who knows any complexity theory past the words P and NP. My thesis: what philosophers call incommensurability or "parity" between options is mostly a computational artifact. Roughly my argument: our value representations are multi-dimensional with no built-in common scale, so comparing two options is an aggregation problem that in the general case is wildly intractable; the felt inability to say whether one career is better than another is what it feels like from the inside to run a comparison procedure that doesnt terminate, not the perception of a metaphysical gap between values. So positing irreducible incomparability (or parity as a sui generis fourth relation alongside better/worse/equal) is explanatorily redundant -- the complexity story predicts exactly which choices feel hard (many dimensions, no dominant option, high stakes forcing an actual answer) and metaphysical incommensurability predicts nothing extra.
