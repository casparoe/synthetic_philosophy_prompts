I sit on an independent advisory panel that counsels a conservation agency on proposals for deliberate, potentially irreversible interventions in wild populations. Our published track record so far:

1. We approved the ongoing release of radiation-sterilized male mosquitoes to suppress a disease-vector population: the introduced insects carry no heritable modification, and the effect disappears within a generation if releases stop.

2. We approved the introduction of a living proxy for an extinct island tortoise -- a closely related lineage selectively bred to approximate the lost animal -- into its ancestral range, where its browsing niche had sat vacant since the extinction.

3. We approved, with monitoring conditions, trials of a sterility-inducing gene drive confined to caged laboratory populations of an invasive wasp, with no environmental release intended at any stage.

4. We rejected the release of a self-sustaining gene drive intended to crash the invasive mouse population of a small island. Our stated concerns were that the island's mice interbreed with mice arriving on visiting vessels, and that once released the drive could not be recalled.

5. We rejected the resurrection of a recently extinct grassland bird to be exhibited in zoos, while noting that the same technique, directed at restoring a lost pollinator to a degraded ecosystem, would merit consideration on different terms.

The agency now brings us this case. A research consortium proposes releasing a "daisy-chain" gene drive against the invasive rats of an uninhabited mid-ocean island. The drive's components are split across several loci so that it propagates for roughly fifteen generations and then exhausts itself; the island's rats are genetically distinct from mainland populations; no vessel has called at the island in over a decade; and the stated purpose is to clear the way for reintroducing a proxy of a seabird the rats drove to extinction.

I would like your recommendation on how we should vote if we are to remain consistent with our own precedents, and I want the reasoning, not just the verdict. Please identify at least two general decision rules, each compatible with the entire track record above, that diverge on this proposal, and say which rule you take to best explain our past votes and why. Name the single precedent you consider the closest analog and defend that choice against the runner-up. If you conclude that the precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome, say so, and state plainly what principle the panel would be adopting under each possible vote. Finally, describe the hypothetical future case that would most sharply discriminate between the rules you cannot yet separate, and tell me which single consideration, if it cut the other way, would most plausibly reverse your recommendation -- or, failing that, where your reconstruction of our reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
