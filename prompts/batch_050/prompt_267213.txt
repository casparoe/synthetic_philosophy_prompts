So I keep running into this thing and I can't tell if it's a known concept or not. Here's the pattern. When the stakes are on *me*, I seem to apply a different evidential standard to *other people's* beliefs than I apply to my own. Two examples: (1) My flatmate says she locked the door on the way out. If we're just going to the corner shop I'm happy to take her word for it, I believe her, done. But if I suddenly remember my passport and laptop are on the kitchen table while we're already at the airport, the exact same statement from her stops being good enough -- I want her to actually picture locking it, walk me through it, maybe call the neighbour to check. I don't think she's lying, and I don't even think she's unreliable. I just... won't let her belief count as settled anymore. (2) A colleague tells me the meeting got moved to 3pm. Normally fine. But if I'm about to tell my boss he can leave early based on that, I suddenly need my colleague to have *checked* the email rather than just glanced at it.

The part that feels like it might not have a name: the stakes that matter aren't the believer's, they're the person *relying on* the believer. My flatmate's situation hasn't changed at all between the shop version and the airport version. She's the same person with the same memory. What's changed is what I stand to lose by leaning on her. And I know there's a literature about whether high stakes for the believer themselves can make a difference to whether they really know something, but this feels like a different move -- it's one person externally raising the bar on another person's knowledge while still granting, sort of, that the belief is fine. It's also not just distrust, because I do trust her, I trust her enough for the cheap version.

Is there an established term for this specifically -- stakes-dependent standards that get applied by a *consumer* of someone else's knowledge, rather than by the knower? If it's just a special case of something bigger, which bigger thing, and does the consumer/knower asymmetry actually matter there or am I seeing a distinction that dissolves on inspection? And if nobody's carved this off, what would you call it and where would you draw its boundaries -- like, does the same thing happen when I raise the bar on my *own* belief only because someone else is about to act on it?
