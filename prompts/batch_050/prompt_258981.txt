I'm co-organizing a monthly reading group at my lab -- mostly ML engineers and a couple of cognitive science PhD students, almost nobody with philosophy background -- and I've been talked into leading the session on whether reinforcement learning agents could in principle suffer. The group has already read a popular piece arguing that negative reward is basically pain, and I could tell from the slack channel that most people walked away thinking the question is either trivially yes (rewards are just numbers that feel good or bad to the agent) or trivially no (it's all matrix multiplication, nothing it is like to be a Q-table). I want to break both of those responses before we even get to the interesting stuff.

So: plan the teaching arc for a ~60 minute session. What needs to be in place conceptually before the question "can an RL agent suffer" is even well-formed for this audience -- I'm thinking things like the reward hypothesis, the distinction between the reward signal and the learned value function, reward prediction error, and the fact that the agent doesn't "get" the reward the way the folk picture assumes -- and in what order, since some of these depend on others in ways that are easy to get backwards. What is the one carrying example to build the whole session around? I want something simpler than a game-playing agent, ideally a tiny gridworld-style setup that I can keep returning to as we add complications (what would change if the agent had a model of itself, recurrent state, etc.), so tell me what the example must include and what it must conspicuously lack. Then the misconception map: which confusions will this specific audience of ML people predictably bring (I suspect conflating negative reward with negative valence, treating "the agent wants to maximize reward" as more than shorthand, and smuggling in consciousness via the word "experience"), at what point in the sequence does each one naturally arise, and how do I defuse it there rather than letting it fester. Finally, how do I check at the end that they've actually internalized the dependency structure -- e.g., can they say what would have to be true of an architecture for the suffering question to get a grip on it -- and what should I explicitly bracket as beyond what we can settle tonight (phenomenal consciousness, obviously, but what else) so the discussion doesn't collapse into it?
