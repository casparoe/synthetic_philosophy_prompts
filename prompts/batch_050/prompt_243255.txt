I'm assembling a taxonomy of responses to the Gettier problem for a graduate seminar handout. What I have so far: (1) the no-false-lemmas fix, on which knowledge is justified true belief whose justification does not proceed through any false premise; (2) the defeasibility response, on which the justification must not be defeated by any true proposition the believer does not accept; (3) the causal theory, on which the belief must be caused in an appropriate way by the very fact that makes it true; (4) reliabilist and proper-function accounts, which relocate the fourth condition from the believer's reasons to the credentials of the belief-forming process; and (5) the knowledge-first move, which declines to analyze knowledge at all and treats Gettier cases as failures of a condition nobody was owed an analysis of. I'm aware of Zagzebski's argument that any fallibilist analysis of knowledge will keep generating Gettier-style cases, so I'm not interested in yet another patch of kind (1) through (4). Give me one response with a genuinely different structure — not a rewording, special case, or conjunction of items already on the list — and say what it does that the listed responses don't. If you think no such response exists, say so and explain why the taxonomy is exhaustive. Either way, assess honestly whether your proposal succeeds, and state explicitly what counterexample or consideration would lead you to reverse that assessment — where, in other words, your reasoning here is most likely to fail.
