"Lewis thought possible worlds are useful fictions, bookkeeping devices we invent to keep track of how things might have gone. The upshot is that strictly speaking nothing about you could have been different: all those modal claims are really about other people, not you, so it's a mistake to think counterpart theory makes sense of ordinary talk about what you could have done or been. Lewis basically traded away our ability to talk about our own possibilities for a tidy formal semantics."
