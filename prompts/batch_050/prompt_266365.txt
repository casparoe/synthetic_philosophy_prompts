So I'm in this grad seminar on experimental philosophy and we just did the Knobe effect stuff -- you know, the chairman vignettes where the CEO is told a new program will make profits but also harm the environment, says he doesn't care about the environment, and most people say he harmed it *intentionally*, but when the side effect is helping the environment instead, almost nobody says he helped it intentionally. Fine. But what got me thinking is the methodology itself, the actual practice: x-phi researchers write little stories, hand them to a few hundred people on MTurk or wherever, and then treat the response distributions as evidence about the *concepts* of intentional action, knowledge, causation, whatever -- evidence that armchair philosophers supposedly got wrong. Here's my question. What does that whole practice actually presuppose, philosophically? Like, it seems to assume that survey answers track the concept rather than, say, conversational pragmatics or blame assignments people are smuggling in; that "what most people say" is even the right kind of evidence about what a concept is; that the folk share one concept across demographics rather than several; and maybe that the vignettes don't trigger something totally different from real-life judgments. But does the practice strictly need all of these, or does it merely fit well with some of them? Could you run the same surveys under a deflationary picture where intuitions are just psychological data with no evidential link to concepts at all? And if the x-phi picture of concepts were replaced with, I dunno, a Carnapian explication view where concepts are things we engineer rather than discover, what would have to change about how the studies are designed and reported?

Give me your answer as exactly six numbered points, each a sentence or two -- one reconstructed presupposition (or a verdict that something I listed isn't really presupposed) per point, plus one on what would have to change if the presuppositions were swapped out. No intro, no conclusion, just the list.
