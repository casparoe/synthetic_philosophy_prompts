Is there an established name for the following phenomenon? Two examples from my own experience. In a strategy game, I mismanaged my economy and my civilization collapsed, and although I lost, I felt a kind of satisfaction in the defeat -- as if the game had meant it -- that I never feel when I watch a film character fail because of their own mistakes. In a narrative game, I chose to abandon one companion to save several others, and the guilt I felt afterward struck me as something the work had achieved, on par with a beautiful passage in a novel. In both cases, the artistic effect seems to run through something I did and am answerable for, rather than through anything I merely witnessed. It is not just interactivity, because clicking through menus is interactive and produces nothing of the sort; and it is not quite immersion either, because I have felt it in abstract games with no story at all. Does aesthetics have a concept for this -- a work whose meaning depends on the audience's own actions and their weight? If so, how does the nearest established concept differ from what I am describing, and if not, what would you call it, and where would its boundaries lie?
