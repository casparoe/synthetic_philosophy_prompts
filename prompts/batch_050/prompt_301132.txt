I'm giving a colloquium talk next month defending a paper on LLM hallucination and Harry Frankfurt's notion of bullshit. My thesis is roughly this: hallucinated outputs can't be lies because lying requires knowingly asserting a falsehood and these systems have no beliefs to conceal; they're not bullshit in Frankfurt's strict sense either, since bullshit requires a speaker who is indifferent to whether what they say is true, and I want to argue that genuine indifference to truth is an intentional state the model can't instantiate. So my move is to relocate the bullshit: the indifference that makes Frankfurt's category apply lives in the developers and deployers who ship systems optimized for plausibility rather than accuracy, and the model itself is more like an instrument of bullshit, analogous to how Frankfurt might treat a press secretary reading a script someone else wrote. The upshot I defend is that "hallucination" is itself a misleading term (it implies a perceptual glitch, i.e. honest error) and calling it bullshit-by-proxy gets the moral landscape right, since the blameworthiness tracks the indifference, not the output.

The audience: a philosophy department colloquium, mostly philosophers of language and epistemologists who work on assertion and testimony, plus a couple of people from cog sci who do machine learning and will be allergic to any anthropomorphizing of LLMs. Frankfurt's essay will be familiar to everyone. Nobody in the room works on AI ethics specifically.
