A friend who recently read about the zombie argument summarized it for me like this: "David Chalmers argues that if we can vividly imagine a world physically identical to ours but lacking consciousness, without contradicting ourselves, then such a world is metaphysically possible, and physicalism is false. Of course, not just any flight of fancy counts: what does the work is ideal conceivability, meaning no contradiction could be uncovered even by ideal rational reflection, and Chalmers claims that this entails genuine possibility. Critics who raise cases like water and H2O — where we seem able to conceive of water's failing to be H2O even though that's impossible — are, according to Chalmers, describing scenarios that turn out on closer reflection to harbor a hidden contradiction after all."

Something in this doesn't sit right with me — the last claim in particular seems too easy, since the water case doesn't feel contradictory in the way a round square does. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what misrepresents Chalmers' actual position, and then say whether the corrected version of the conceivability premise still supports the anti-physicalist conclusion.
