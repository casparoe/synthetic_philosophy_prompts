(1) In a race to the bottom, any player who unilaterally restrains themselves just hands an advantage to the players who don't -- restraint without enforceable coordination is pure cost, and the defectors capture whatever the restrainer gives up.
(2) Situations like AI labs cutting safety corners to ship first, countries competing on lax environmental rules to attract industry, athletes doping, and banks taking on tail risk are races to the bottom in exactly this sense.
(3) So in those situations, unilateral restraint is irrational for any individual player; the only rational moves are defecting or pushing for binding coordination on everyone.
(4) But the same structure -- my restraint costs me, benefits others or no one, and only pays off if everyone matches it -- shows up in ordinary life constantly: not littering, driving the speed limit, voting, cutting your carbon footprint, paying full taxes when others cheat, an employee refusing to work unpaid overtime when coworkers will.
(5) So by the same reasoning, unilateral restraint is irrational in all of those too. Nobody has reason to hold back in any collective action problem unless there's enforcement.
(6) But that's obviously nuts. It would mean there's nothing even slightly wrong with me tossing my trash on the trail, since my not doing it changes nothing, and that a whistleblower or a lab that slows down for safety is just a sucker, full stop.
