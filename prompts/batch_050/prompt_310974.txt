I'm in a philosophy reading group (we meet in the back of a pub on Thursdays, which may explain some of our debates) and all term we've been circling the Epicurean line that death is nothing to us, becuase when we exist death is not present and when death is present we don't exist. Two of our members — a card-carrying Epicurean and a committed deprivationist who thinks death is bad precisely because of the good life it cuts off — have been bickering for six weeks with zero progress, so the rest of us have ordered them to settle it the way adversarial collaborations do: produce one joint statement both can actually sign.

Write that document for them. It should have: a numbered list of claims both accept (about what the Epicurean argument does and doesn't establish, about what deprivationism commits you to, about how dreamless sleep and the nonexistence before your birth fit in), then the exact remaining disagreements worded so each would accept the description of their own position, then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would force somebody to budge. I'd expect the timing problem (when exactly is death supposed to be bad for you, given you're not around to be badly off?) and the symmetry thing with prenatal nonexistence to show up, but include whatever is genuinely doing the work. For each crux, give a concrete case both agree bears on it and record what each side predicts about it in advance. Two cases they must take a stand on: a man in an irreversible coma who will die painlessly in his sleep tonight — does anything bad happen to him tonight that wasn't already bad before? And a healthy 90-year-old who refuses a treatment that would give her exactly one more ordinary, happy week — is her refusal irrational, neutral, or rationally required? Also make them address whether the Epicurean can still coherently prefer a later death to an earlier one, since that seems like where the view gets wobbly in practice. Don't let either side hide behind the standard slogans, and if part of their fight turns out to be verbal (I suspect the bit about whether harm requires an existing subject is), flag which part and why.

Format: keep the whole statement under 900 words, and start it with a short paragraph giving the bottom line — whether the two found any real convergence or whether the debate is exactly as stuck as when they started. This is meant to be signed, not published, so no footnotes or name-dropping beyond what's needed.
