Alright, here's my situation: I spent 35 years as a field entomologist, mostly social wasps, and since retiring I've fallen down the philosophy-of-biology rabbit hole about what actually counts as an organism. Ant colonies, coral, those weird siphonophores, humans-and-their-microbiomes as "holobionts," aspen clones like Pando that are genetically one individual spread over a hundred acres. The literature seems to run in circles because every proposed criterion (genetic uniformity, germ-soma separation, reproducing through a single-cell bottleneck, internal policing of cheaters, aligned fitness interests) knocks out something we obviously want to call an organism, or lets in something we obviously don't.

So here's what I want you to write: the minutes of a fictional hearing before a government "Organism Certification Board," maybe 600-700 words, where four petitioners argue their case for official organism status. I'd suggest: a leafcutter ant colony, a Portuguese man o' war, a human being who gets tripped up by the fact that his gut microbes are doing half the metabolizing and can reproduce without him, and a quaking aspen clone. The board members keep applying their official criteria and each petitioner exposes why that criterion is broken -- e.g. the aspen fails the single-cell-bottleneck test, the man o' war's zooids can't survive alone, the human fails genetic uniformity of parts, the ant colony gets told the queen is the real germline so the workers are just disposable soma, that sort of thing.

The humor has to be deadpan bureaucratic -- stamps, form numbers, appeals procedures, a clerk reading definitions aloud -- but the jokes must come from the ACTUAL conceptual problems in the biological individuality literature, not from making the petitioners act silly. A philosopher of biology should read it and go "yes, that's exactly the problem with that criterion," while a normal reader still finds it funny. No one should win cleanly; the board's criteria should visibly collapse by the end, and the final minute entry should be something quietly despairing, like tabling the definition until next quarter.

Oh, and dont have the board just be stupid -- they should be smart people trapped by a genuinely hard problem. That's the whole joke, really. I've been trapped by it for two years now.
