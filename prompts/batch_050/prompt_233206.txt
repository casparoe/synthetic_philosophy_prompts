I’m in Year 11 and our philosophy club is doing conceptual engineering, which I definately thought was just arguing about dictionary definitions until recently. As I understand it, a descriptive analysis tries to capture how we currently use a concept, while an ameliorative analysis asks what version of the concept would best serve the purposes we care about. My teacher said the tricky part is that one word can be doing several jobs at once, so fixing it for one purpose might break it for another. I’ve been trying to make a thought experiment about bullying that shows this, but my examples always end with a teacher enforcing the policy stupidly, which feels like cheating because then the problem is just bad enforcement.

Construct a realistic thought experiment set in an ordinary secondary school where the existing policy defines bullying as repeated, intentional aggression by someone with greater social power, while a proposed ameliorative definition says bullying is conduct that foreseeably creates a hostile environment, regardless of intention or repetition. The case has to meet these conditions: the descriptive analysis and the ameliorative analysis give opposite verdicts about the central incident; the ameliorative definition is motivated by a genuine flaw in the old concept, not by a desire to punish more students; applying the new definition predictably creates a serious moral or practical cost because the single label bullying is being used both to identify who needs protection and to decide who deserves blame or punishment; and the old definition, despite its flaw, preserves one distinction that matters. Nobody should knowingly exploit a loophole, misunderstand the policy, or act like a cartoon villain, and the verdicts should arise from the facts of the case rather than being simply stipulated. Keep the scenario under 350 words. After it, explain what verdict each analysis gives, identify the exact feature that makes them diverge, and design a role-splitting solution with at least two replacement concepts or procedures. Finally, say how an ameliorative theorist could object that your role-splitting approach abandons the original political purpose of the reform, and whether that objection works.
