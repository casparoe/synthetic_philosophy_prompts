So my political theory reading group just finished the lordship and bondage section of the Phenomenology of Spirit, and afterwards we got into an argument I want you to help settle-ish. One of my friends claims that TikTok/Instagram creator culture is basically the master-slave dialectic playing out at scale. Her version: the influencer craves recognition from millions of followers, but recognition from anonymous strangers who double-tap out of habit is worthless for the same reason the master's recognition from the slave is worthless, since it doesn't come from someone who's judgment the influencer can respect as free and independent. Meanwhile the real bondsman figure is the mid-tier creator grinding out content every day: through the discipline of work, and through the constant fear of irrelevance (which plays the role of the fear of death), she supposedly forms herself and gets something closer to genuine self-certainty than the mega-influencer ever will. Another friend thinks the whole analogy is dead on arrival because Hegelian recognition has to be mutual, and a parasocial relationship with an audience can't supply that.

Let's say Hegel got resurrected, spent a month on TikTok, and read my first friend's take. Where would he say it gets the dialectic right, and where does it go wrong? A few specific things I'd like addressed: (1) does recognition need to be freely given to count for Hegel, and are likes given freely in any sense he'd care about, given that the algorithm is shaping who sees what and nudging what gets rewarded; (2) does the bondsman's route through formative labor really apply to making content, or does working for an algorithm that can't recognize you back break the analogy with the slave's relation to the thing he shapes; (3) Hegel treats the master's position as a dead end and the slave's as containing the seeds of progress, so would he predict the influencer economy is unstable in the same way, and what would the slave's victory even look like here (creators owning the platforms? audiences becoming co-creators? something else?); and (4) would he agree with my second friend that the parasocial point is a knock-down objection, or would he say she's overestimating how mutual recognition needs to be at this stage of the dialectic? Please don't just summarize the master-slave dialectic back at me, we've read it -- engage with the analogy on its merits and tell me which of my two friends Hegel would side with.
