I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing an application for a twelve-month residential fellowship that supports long-form book projects. The deliverable is a proposal of roughly two pages, and I would like you to draft it.

The project concerns the old puzzle of why mathematics, much of it developed with no thought of the physical world, turns out to describe nature so well. My working conviction is that the puzzle has been miscast as a single phenomenon when it is really at least three, with different causes: mathematics built expressly for physics (the calculus); mathematics developed for purely internal reasons and only later conscripted (complex numbers, non-Euclidean geometry, group theory); and, on the other side of the ledger, the conspicuous cases where mathematics has conspicuously not delivered — turbulence, protein folding, macroeconomic forecasting — which the standard retelling quietly excludes. If the three categories have genuinely different explanations, then the famous mystery may be partly an artifact of selection: we narrate the history using only the marriages that worked, and the miracle dissolves into survivorship bias plus a great deal of unglamorous trial and error. The book would test this by reporting out a handful of case studies — how a given piece of mathematics actually entered a given piece of physics, who carried it across, and whether anyone at the time found its applicability surprising — rather than by offering yet another armchair verdict on Platonism.

Please turn this into a fellowship proposal. It should contain: a precise statement of the central question and of the tripartite hypothesis; an argument for why existing treatments — metaphysical, evolutionary, and conventionalist — leave that question open; a defense of the case-study method against the obvious alternatives (a historical survey, a philosopher's monograph, a journalist's tour of the greatest hits); a month-by-month plan indicating which cases I would pursue, in what order, and what archival and interview work each would require; a description of what the finished book establishes if the hypothesis holds; and an honest account of what would count as failure, including what I would write if the cases turned out to resist my three-way split. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will supply those myself.
