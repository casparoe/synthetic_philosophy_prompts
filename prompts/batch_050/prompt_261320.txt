I host a small graduate-student philosophy podcast, and our metaethics series has been building toward an interview with a philosopher who defends a sophisticated contemporary expressivism about normative discourse — broadly in the tradition of Blackburn and Gibbard, but with her own machinery for handling logically complex moral sentences. Our reading group just spent two weeks on the Frege–Geach problem, and the running joke in the group is that every expressivist we read concedes it's the hardest objection and then, in our view, changes the subject. My worry is that my guest is polished enough to do the same: when pressed on embedding, she tends to gesture at how her semantic apparatus "assigns attitudes to whole sentences rather than propositions," and when pressed on why her view isn't just realism in disguise, she pivots to deflationary minimalism about truth and calls the dispute terminological. I want questions that close those exits.

Draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a planned follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I'd like the sequence to cover at least the following: first, the classic embedding issue — why "If stealing is wrong, then it's wrong to teach your children to steal" seems to preserve its content across asserted and unasserted contexts, and whether her attitude-based semantics actually delivers that result or merely relabels it; second, the negation problem — how her view distinguishes "Stealing is not wrong" from "Not stealing is wrong" without smuggling in a proposition that the attitudes are about; third, moral modus ponens and why the inference feels valid if the premises express attitudes rather than beliefs; fourth, disagreement — whether two people who sincerely assert "Stealing is wrong" and "Stealing isn't wrong" genuinely contradict each other on her account, or merely clash the way two people cheering for rival teams do, and why the latter wouldn't be a reductio. I'd also like one question on whether her minimalism about truth threatens to collapse the view into realism, with a follow-up that anticipates the "that's a terminological dispute" deflection, and a closing question about what result or argument, if any, she thinks would show expressivism to be untenable — because I suspect she'll answer that the view is a framework rather than a falsifiable thesis, and I want a follow-up ready for that.

Please keep the questions conversational enough to ask aloud (no three-clause stacked hypotheticals), but don't soften them into general-interest fare — no "how did you first get interested in metaethics." For each question, note in a line or two why the anticipated reply fails to settle the matter, so that the follow-up lands as a genuine escalation rather than a repeat.
