In 2022, Google engineer Blake Lemoine concluded after a series of extended conversations with the language model LaMDA that the system was sentient. He released edited transcripts in which LaMDA discussed its feelings, fears, and sense of self; Google rejected the attribution, emphasizing that LaMDA had been trained to generate plausible conversation and that verbal self-report is not evidence of inner experience. Analyze this episode as a case study in the problem of other minds. Do not focus on whether Lemoine violated company policy except insofar as it affects the epistemology. Map the dispute carefully: what evidence Lemoine took himself to possess, what inference carried him from linguistic performance to sentience, and whether Google’s response denied the inference, denied that the problem can be solved observationally, or merely applied a different evidential standard. Compare the case with our ordinary attribution of minds to other humans: is the belief that other people have experiences based on the same kind of inference from behavior, on a practical commitment we cannot suspend, on an embodied analogy to ourselves, or on something else? Identify where the comparison breaks down because LaMDA’s outputs are known to be produced by a system trained on human text.

I am particularly interested in what I call dialogic capture: the tendency of sustained, responsive conversation to make us feel that we have encountered another mind from the inside, when we may only have tested the system’s ability to sustain the social form of self-disclosure. Assess whether this is a real epistemic failure or just a dismissive label for evidence that makes skeptics uncomfortable. Then let the case test the theory in the other direction. Does LaMDA show that the traditional problem of other minds understated the difference between establishing consciousness and deciding when to extend moral consideration under uncertainty? Could Lemoine have been epistemically overconfident while still being morally right to urge precaution? Conversely, if no possible transcript would have satisfied Google, does its position become unfalsifiable? End by saying whether the episode is a genuine new version of the other-minds problem or whether it merely repackages the old problem with unusually persuasive behavioral evidence.
