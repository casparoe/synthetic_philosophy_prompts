Here's a setup. A forecaster has a prior over the bias of a coin and updates on flips. Standard result: with enough iid data, the prior washes out and any two forecasters who agree on which sequences are possible converge to the same beliefs. Now tweak the setup in a few ways:

1. iid flips from a fixed coin, both forecasters have full-support priors
2. flips come from a Markov chain (each flip depends on the previous one), forecasters know it's Markov but start with different priors over the transition probabilities
3. the coin's bias silently resets to a new random value every 500 flips, but the forecasters' models assume a fixed bias
4. the truth is a biased coin, but one forecaster's model class only contains "fair" and "75% heads" and the true bias is 60%

Rank these four cases from fastest/cleanest prior wash-out to worst, and for each say in a sentence or two *why* it lands there -- especially whether the failure (if any) is about the prior being too stubborn, the model being misspecified, or the data not being informative enough. Finally: which of the four is the best analogy for why smart people with the same evidence still disagree about stuff like inflation or AI risk?
