A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about Pascal's wager, and I would like an outside verdict on the move she considers decisive. When I pressed the standard objections, she fell back on an analogy: wagering on God, she said, is simply insurance. Nobody considers it irrational to pay fire-insurance premiums on a house that almost certainly will not burn down; a small, certain cost is rationally paid to hedge against an enormous, improbable loss. Religious observance is the premium, salvation is the payout, and declining the wager is like refusing coverage to save money — defensible only if one has private knowledge that the risk is zero.

Please audit the analogy itself rather than the wager in the abstract. Concretely, I want:

1. The explicit correspondence table — what plays the role of the insurer, the premium, the actuarial probability, the policy terms, and the payout — including the points where she would say the fit is tightest.

2. The disanalogies, sorted by whether they actually bear on her conclusion rather than being merely pedantic. Candidate issues I want adjudicated: insurance is rational because of the diminishing marginal utility of money, not expected value alone, and it is unclear what plays that role in the wager; actuarial probabilities are derived from observed frequencies, while the probability of God's existence has no such source; buying a policy is an action one can simply perform, whereas the wager appears to demand a belief that cannot be adopted at will; an infinite payout has no actuarial analogue and threatens to make any action with a nonzero chance of securing it infinitely valuable; and competing religions look less like rival insurers than like mutually exclusive policies, each one void if you hold another.

3. The verdict: whether the insurance framing smuggles in its conclusion by presupposing a single known insurer with stated terms; whether, once the analogy is stripped away, a bare expected-value argument survives on its own; and, if the analogy fails, what better comparison, if any, she should reach for instead.

I want the mapping and the verdict stated plainly enough that I can bring them back to her.
