I sit on the funding board of our grad student association, and we have a mess on our hands. One of the clubs we fund ran a charity fundraiser where they auctioned off a $100 gift card using a format they saw online: bidding starts at a dollar, each bid has to top the last by at least a dollar, but here's the twist -- every bid you place is non-refundable, win or lose. So when the bidding stalled at $37, the second-highest bidder kept going because dropping out meant eating the $36 he'd already sunk in, and the top bidder kept going for the same reason. The card eventually sold for $214, the runner-up paid $211 and got nothing, and now both of them are furious and demanding the association reimburse them, since we technically sanctioned the event.

Here's the part that makes it awkward: the club wants to run the same format again next semester because, financially, it worked great, and our treasurer is sympathetic -- she says the bidders were adults who read the posted rules and nobody forced anyone to bid. Meanwhile I've noticed the board itself is doing a version of the same thing: we've already spent two meetings and a bunch of political capital defending this club, and part of me wonders whether we're just doubling down because backing out now feels like admitting we approved something dumb.

Draft the short written opinion I'll bring to the board: it should rule on (a) whether we reimburse either bidder, (b) whether this auction format gets banned going forward, and it should take seriously the treasurer's consent-based argument rather than just calling the bidders irrational -- because honestly I'm not sure they're being irrational move-by-move, which is kind of the whole problem. Keep it to something we could actually vote on.
