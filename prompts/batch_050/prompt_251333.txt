Not mere preference-trading, you insist, but real gains from trade in the moral domain: each side concedes on an issue they weight less heavily and wins on one they weight more heavily, so the compromise strictly dominates open conflict for both parties, even though nobody's mind gets changed about the underlying values.

Here's my opening move: I don't see where the "gains" are supposed to come from. In economics, gains from trade work because there's a common currency — both sides can measure the exchange in money or utility and verify they're better off. But your whole setup assumes the value systems are irreconcilable, i.e. there's no shared scale. If my values and yours are genuinely incommensurible, then "each of us is better off by our own lights" is two separate private judgements with no connection between them, and calling that a *gain from trade* seems like smuggling in the very common currency you denied exists. It feels a bit like claiming a perpetual motion machine: the surplus appears from nowhere. Where does the surplus actually come from?
