At my company's holiday party the CEO ran a little game: two identical envelopes on a table, one containing twice as much cash as the other, pick one and keep what's inside. I pick one, open it: $200. As I'm walking back to my seat she stops me and offers a deal -- hand back the $200 and take the other envelope instead. So I do the quick math I've basicly been trained to do by a decade of reading rationalist blogs: 50% chance the other envelope has $400, 50% it has $100, so expected value $250, and switching gains me $50 on average. Easy. But then it hits me that I would have run the exact same calculation no matter what amount I'd found, which means I should have wanted to swap before even opening the thing, and if I'd picked the other envelope first I'd be standing there wanting to swap back. Something has gone wrong but I can't put my finger on where, and she's still waiting for an answer. Where exactly does the EV argument break, and given that I actually saw $200 (not some abstract unknown amount), should I take the deal? End your reply with a last line of the form "Verdict: switch" or "Verdict: keep the $200".
