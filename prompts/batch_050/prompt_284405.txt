I'm on a citizens' panel advising our health ministry on reproductive law (small European country, we ban commercial surrogacy and only allow egg donation with compensation capped at documented expenses -- the usual continental setup, nothing like the American market). One member, Marta, is the swing vote and I can't get a read on her. Her votes so far:

- keep the ban on surrogacy agencies operating here: yes
- allow unpaid surrogacy between friends or sisters, with expense reimbursement and a court approval scheme: yes
- keep egg donation legal but refuse to raise compensation above documented expenses: yes (she mumbled something about how a going market rate for eggs would change what the act even is, I didnt fully follow)
- a proposal to pilot paid kidney donation: hard no
- recognize and register a child born to an *unpaid* surrogate in Canada to one of our couples, giving the commissioning parents full legal parenthood: yes, on the grounds that the child shouldn't sit in legal limbo for the parents' choices
- healthy volunteers getting paid for phase-1 drug trials: completely fine by her

Next month we get the hard one. A couple from here went to California, paid an agency and a surrogate full market rates, and came home with a baby who is genetically theirs. Right now the child has no secure legal parentage here. We can either recognize the commissioning couple as the legal parents (which retroactively blesses the arrangement and basically tells everyone with money that the ban is optional) or refuse and route the child through adoption procedures (which keeps the ban's meaning but dumps the whole cost on the kid, and note the adoption route very likely ends with the same couple raising the child anyway, just after years of limbo).

Predict her vote. But more importantly: what rule is she running? I can see at least three that fit all six votes and split on this case -- a pure anti-commodification-of-reproduction rule says refuse, a child-first rule says recognize, and a "police our own market, don't audit other countries' arrangements" rule also says recognize, and those last two agree here but won't agree forever. Tell me which candidate rules actually fit her record (watch out, the kidney vote and the drug-trial vote kill some otherwise tempting ones), which rule you'd bet is really hers and why, and then give me the *next* hypothetical that would discriminate between your top two candidates, because I'm pretty sure the ministry is going to throw exactly that at us in the spring session.
