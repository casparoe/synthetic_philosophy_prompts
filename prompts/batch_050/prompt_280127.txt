I'm teaching a short unit on Wittgenstein's On Certainty next term and I want to pair it with a screening of The Truman Show, since students always get the skeptical-possibility bit but miss the hinge bit. Help me build the discussion.

Read the film through hinge epistemology: which of Truman's beliefs actually function as hinges -- stuff like "Seahaven is a real town," "Marlon is my best friend," "the people around me aren't performing" -- beliefs he's never even formulated, let alone checked, but which organize everything else he does and believes? And here's the part I find genuinely tricky: Wittgenstein's line of thought is that doubt only makes sense against a background that stands fast, that you can't doubt everything at once because the doubting itself needs somewhere to stand. Truman's awakening seems to respect that at first -- the stage light falling out of the sky, the radio narrating his drive, Meryl's weird product pitches are treated as anomalies against an otherwise trusted background, and his investigation procedes by holding most things fixed while testing one thing at a time. But by the end he's effectively concluded that his entire world, all of it, was staged. Is that a move On Certainty even allows as coherent, or does the film cheat -- does Truman do something Wittgenstein would say you can't actually do, like doubt all the hinges simultaneously and somehow stay sane and articulate while doing it?

Also: what do I do with Christof? Is the control room a dramatization of what a community's inherited hinges look like from outside, with Christof literally manufacturing what stands fast for Truman? And the final scene, Truman walking through the door into the dark -- is that a hinge being replaced, or is the film pretending you can step outside hinges altogether, which is exactly the thing OC denies? Where does the film genuinely resist the Wittgensteinian reading, and where does it maybe dramatize something the text underplays (I'd say: the emotional cost of hinge loss, OC is weirdly calm about that). I want to use the film without teaching the students that On Certainty is just "maybe you're in the Truman Show," because it pretty much isn't.
