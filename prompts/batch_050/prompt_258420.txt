So our philosophy department's grad student association hosts this thing every spring where two people with genuinely opposed views have to spend a term meeting weekly and then produce a joint document, and this year it's a Frankish-style illusionist about phenomenal consciousness and a realist who thinks phenomenal properties are irreducibly real (not necessarily a dualist -- she's happy to be a physicalist about them, she just thinks denying their existence is a non-starter). They've been meeting for months and apparently it's going well, but now they have to actually write the thing, and one of them jokingly asked me to help because I keep sitting in. So: draft the joint statement of their adversarial collaboration.

What I want from the document: first, a numbered list of claims both sides can actually sign -- not mush, but real commitments. I suspect there's agreement available about things like what introspective reports consist in, that something has to explain why we sincerely judge ourselves to have phenomenal properties, and that naive "it just seems obvious" appeals don't settle anything, but I'd like you to find the strongest set of genuinely shared commitments and phrase them so neither side is smuggling in their own vocabulary. Second, a disagreements section (a table would be great) stating each residual disagreement in wording the side being described would accept as a fair statement of its own position -- especially watch out for the illusionist being described as "denying experience exists" when she means something subtler, and the realist being described as committed to magic. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. I'd guess one crux is whether the seeming-obviousness of phenomenality is itself a datum to be explained or the thing doing the explaining, and another is whether "explaining why we think we're phenomenally conscious" is a full explanation of consciousness or a change of subject, but push past my guesses if those aren't the real joints.

For each crux, I want the test, argument, or case both parties would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction or expected verdict written down in advance -- like, what would the realist have to concede if the illusionist's account of introspective misrepresentation turned out to handle every proposed phenomenal property, and what would the illusionist concede if some specific residue kept refusing to be analyzed away. Finally, if part of their dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, the document should say which part and why, because honestly I suspect a chunk of the "illusion" vs. "seeming" fight is about what the word "experience" is allowed to mean, and it would be embarassing if the whole term boiled down to that without anyone noticing.
