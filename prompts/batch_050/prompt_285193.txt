So at my company a few of us do this informal philosophy-at-lunch thing every couple weeks. We're a software shop, ~12 people show up, zero formal philosophy training between us, just engineers who like arguing. Next slot is mine and I want to do something on an idea I half-remember from an old Hofstadter piece, the "careenium": picture a zillion tiny billiard-ball things (he calls them simmballs) knocking around on a huge table, and from far enough above their clumps form shapes that act like symbols in a mind -- the shapes interact, react to each other, apparently "decide" stuff. The claim I want to chew on is that the symbol-level story isn't just a convenient summary for lazy observers, the symbols genuinely shove the substrate around. Downward causation, I think it's called? As in the pattern is what's really doing the work, not just the balls.

Design me an activity or game for this group, about 75 minutes, that makes us feel both horns of this without me lecturing. Seed idea I've got, feel free to rework: everyone gets an index card with a stupid local rule (a logic gate, a Life cell, whatever) and the room physically enacts one big automaton, with some high-level pattern buried in it -- a counter, a glider, something -- that they can track. What I'm after is a moment where they're spontaneously talking and intervening at the pattern level ("the counter's about to overflow, quick, block it") and then it hits them that every actual push in the room was person-on-person, so either the pattern-level talk is causally real or it's a free-rider on the physics that only *seems* to explain anything. I don't know how to stage it so that collision actually lands instead of being a neat demo where everyone shrugs "huh, levels of description, cool" and goes back to work.

Requirements: they have to run into the problem through the rules and roles themselves, not have it explained; no vocab like "epiphenomenalism" or "causal closure" until the debrief; give me actual rules, turn structure, roles, and ideally some small stakes since these people are competitive (a bet, a leaderboard, a prize, whatever works); a debrief that maps specific moments from the game onto the positions you could take (symbols do real causal work / symbols are just a redescription and all causation is at the bottom / some middle option) and what it costs to hold each one; warn me what will predictably go wrong when I run this with engineers -- someone will try to optimize their card instead of playing it, someone will announce "it's just abstraction" five minutes in, etc. -- and how to handle each; and be honest about which part of the idea a game like this structurally can't reproduce. E.g. we designed the automaton on purpose, so of course the pattern level is informative, which maybe proves too much -- if there's a gap like that, tell me what I'd have to just say out loud at the end.
