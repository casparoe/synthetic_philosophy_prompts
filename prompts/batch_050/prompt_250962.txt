Construct a thought experiment that stress-tests Freud's analysis of religious belief in The Future of an Illusion. On that analysis, a belief counts as an illusion when wish-fulfilment is a prominent factor in its motivation and the believer sets no store by verification; crucially, an illusion is not thereby false, since a wish-motivated belief might turn out true, and Freud reserves the stronger charge of delusion for wish-motivated beliefs that positively conflict with reality. Build a single, ordinary contemporary scenario — no clinical setting, no conversion narrative, no crisis of faith — containing two otherwise similar adult believers in the same comforting doctrine, engineered so that in one believer wish-fulfilment is present but indifference to verification is absent, and in the other the reverse holds. The scenario itself should be one paragraph. Then, in no more than 300 words (the scenario excluded): state what Freud's classification delivers for each believer — illusion, delusion, or neither — and why; identify which feature of the case is doing the work, and whether removing it collapses Freud's illusion/delusion distinction or merely relocates it; and give the strongest objection a defender of religious belief could press against the way your case forces the classification, noting whether that objection targets Freud's premises or only your construction of them.
