I am a PhD student working in the philosophy of probability, and I am drafting a chapter on whether randomness can be a property of particular things — tokens rather than processes or types. The central example in the chapter is a single finite binary string: suppose a physicist writes down a specific sequence of 1,000 bits produced by a quantum device, and suppose, counterfactually, that the same string had instead been produced by a simple computer program. My chapter asks whether the string itself, considered apart from its origin, can be objectively random.

I have reached the point where I must state and defend a position, and before I commit myself in print I would like your overall credence, with the reasoning made explicit. The proposition I want your credence in is this: it is objectively true of at least some finite sequences (such as a particular 1,000-bit string) that they are random, where this is a property the sequence bears in virtue of its own structure and not merely a statement about the process that produced it or about our ignorance.

Please decompose your credence along roughly the following lines, assigning a number and one or two lines of justification to each term:

1. The probability that incompressibility-based accounts (of the Kolmogorov-complexity family) successfully capture the relevant notion of randomness, i.e., that they are extensionally adequate to what we mean, or should mean, by calling a sequence random.
2. The probability, conditional on (1), that these accounts can be legitimately applied to finite strings rather than only to infinite ones, given their dependence on a choice of universal machine and the resulting additive constant.
3. The probability that randomness is genuinely a structural property at all, as opposed to an irreducibly process-relative property, on which the same string is random if generated by a quantum device and non-random if printed by a short programme.

State how these terms combine into your final credence, noting where the independence assumptions in the combination are doing work. Then tell me which of the three terms you are least confident in, and what argument, result, or consideration would most shift your estimate of it. Finally, if you think I have mis-partitioned the question — that is, if the real locus of uncertainty about the proposition sits somewhere my three terms do not reach — please say so and propose the correction.
