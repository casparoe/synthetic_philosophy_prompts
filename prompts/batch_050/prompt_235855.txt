so I've been chewing on the causal exclusion argument again -- the Kim-flavored one: every physical event with a cause has a sufficient physical cause, mental properties aren't identical to physical ones unless you go full reductionist, so either the mental is causally idle or everything is systematically overdetermined. the thing that keeps bugging me is that the argument seems to generalize way past minds. take a chess engine: its move has a sufficient cause at the level of transistor voltages and clock cycles, so by exclusion logic the algorithm -- the actual chess-playing part, the thing you'd cite to explain why it sacked its queen -- apparently causes nothing. same goes for a gene, a gear, a market price. it starts to look like only the bottom level of physics causes anything at all, which nobody believes, including the people advancing the argument.

what I want is the spec, not another round of takes: the desiderata a theory of mental (or more generally higher-level) causation has to meet to count as adequate. give me the requirements list -- the cases it has to get right (ordinary "her remark caused him to leave" cases, the chess engine, cases where the higher-level description predicts better than the lower-level one, whatever else you think belongs on the list), the distinctions it can't collapse (causal sufficiency vs causal relevance? overdetermination vs dependence? explain why each distinction matters with a case), the constraints from physics it can't violate (closure, no magical downward forces), with the consideration motivating each requirement and a note on whether it's genuinely load-bearing or just one camp's pet intuition that the other camps are entitled to reject. then the payoff questions: is some subset of the reasonable-sounding desiderata jointly unsatisfiable, such that the list itself is the interesting result? and which single requirement knocks out the most popular existing accounts? a table for the requirements is fine, prose for the analysis.

one wrinkle: I care about this for engineering reasons, not just metaphysics bookkeeping -- if you want a theory of causation that's useful for building and debugging minds (uploads, AIs, whatever), does the spec change? flag any requirement that's only a desideratum relative to one of the two purposes.
