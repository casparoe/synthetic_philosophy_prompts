So I'm a retired fisheries biologist who has fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole over the last year, and at my reading group a friend tried to explain an idea he'd picked up called the unilateralist's curse. His version went something like this: "when a bunch of people each have the individual power to do something big and hard to reverse -- publish risky research, release a new organism or technology, whatever -- the outcome effectively gets decided by the most reckless person in the room, because anyone who wants to act just acts and nobody else can undo it. And since confidence and bad judgement tend to go together, the curse means that even groups of sensible, well-meaning people will keep ending up doing the most extreme available thing, which is why the only real fix is to require unanimous consent before anyone is allowed to move."

Some of this rings true to me and some of it smells off, but I can't pin down which is which. It rings true because I sat on a reintroduction committee for years and it often felt like whoever was keenest sort of got their way. But "the most reckless person decides" and "confidence and bad judgement go together" seem like they're doing more work than they should, and I'm suspicious of any argument that lands neatly on a fix as tidy as unanimity. So: which parts of his summary would someone who actually takes this idea seriously accept, which parts are distortions or conflations with something else, and does the unanimous-consent conclusion actually follow from the parts that are right? And since I've noticed this is exactly the sort of topic where a confident summary can itself be a bit cursed, please say how confident you are in each of your corrections and flag the single point you're least sure of.
