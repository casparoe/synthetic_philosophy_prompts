I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm writing a satirical column for the ideas section of a general-interest magazine. Write a parody of a corporate customer-service training manual issued by a company whose CEO, after a late-career conversion to the philosophy of Emmanuel Levinas, has rebuilt the entire support department around the face of the Other. The manual should be deadpan and fully in genre — onboarding modules, escalation procedures, scripts agents are forbidden to use, performance metrics, an FAQ for skeptical team leads — while taking Levinas completely seriously: the encounter with the face as a command that precedes all policy, the asymmetry of a responsibility I never chose, why no customer can be substituted for another or folded into a queue of interchangeable cases, and how every canned response betrays the living saying by freezing it into the said. The comedy must arise from genuine friction between infinite obligation and hold music, ticket backlogs, and quarterly KPIs — exaggerating real features of Levinas's view rather than inventing ones — so that a reader who knows the work finds the satire fair, while a reader who doesn't still gets the joke. Around 700 words.
