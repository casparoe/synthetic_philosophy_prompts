I’m a backend engineer, thirty-two, no dependants, and I earn a decent salary by UK standards. For the last few years I’ve given 8% of my take-home pay to charities I trust, partly because it seems obvious that the money can do much more good there than it would as another gadget, nicer holiday, or marginally better flat. The problem is that I can’t see where this argument stops.

A friend recently pushed me on it. If I accept that a modest reduction in my comfort is worth a major benefit to someone else, why stop at 8%? Why not 15%, 30%, or everything above what I need for rent, food, savings and a basically decent life? I can’t give a principled answer. “Because that’s the number I chose” doesn’t count, and “because I’d become miserable” seems to let almost anything through once I start treating hobbies, convenience and career breaks as essential to avoiding misery.

My best defence is that I need to protect my future capacity to help: emergency savings, not burning out, paying for training, maybe supporting my parents later, and having enough slack to take a lower-paid but more socially useful job. My friend thinks this is just an infinitely expandable loophole. I suspect some version of it matters, though.

Please advise me on the actual decision, not just the general theory. I have a £12,000 bonus arriving next month. Should I give all of it away, some fixed share, or only whatever remains after a carefully defined personal budget? More importantly, how do I define that budget without either smuggling in every preference I happen to have or treating myself as having no claims at all? I keep going round in cirlces.
