So in my philosophy elective last week the teacher told us this story about Zhuangzi, the old Chinese Daoist guy, where his wife dies and his friend comes over expecting to find him wrecked, and instead Zhuangzi is sitting there banging on a pot and singing. And afterwards two kids in my class, Maya and Daniel, got into this huge argument about it that nobody could settle, so I said I'd ask around and get back to them.

Maya's position was basically that Zhuangzi is doing denial and calling it wisdom. Like, if you can actually talk yourself out of being sad when your wife dies, then either you didn't really love her or you're just numbing yourself, and grief is kind of the price you pay for loving someone. Daniel pushed back and said she's missing the whole point, because in the story Zhuangzi admits he WAS sad at first, but then he thought about how before his wife existed there was no body and no life at all, and then change made her alive, and now change made her dead again, sort of like how seasons come and go. So he's not suppressing anything, he just sees it differently. Maya said the seasons thing is exactly what's wrong with it: people aren't seasons, a specific person is irreplaceable, and treating your wife as just another phase of some cosmic flow is actually kind of insulting to her. She also said if death is no big deal because everything's just transformation, then Zhuangzi's view can't even explain why murder is wrong. Daniel answered that murder is obviously about what the killer does and not about death itself, so Zhuangzi can be against causing death while still being at peace with death happening. Then Maya said that's a cop-out because the victim still dies either way, so if dying is fine then the victim isn't really harmed, and the argument kind of went in circles from there.

Can you go through it step by step and tell me who's actually right at each point, and also who's more right overall? And if there's something both of them are missing about Zhuangzi's view, I'd love to know that too, since I have to write a short reflection on this for class and right now I honestly can't tell if Zhuangzi is deep or just weird.
