I am a novelist, and I am trying to think clearly about a decision before I make it rather than rationalize it afterward. My current manuscript is narrated in the first person by a woman belonging to an indigenous culture that is not mine — a culture I have researched for years and visited, but in which I have no ancestry and no standing. I would like you to help me by comparing two ways of understanding what, if anything, is wrong with cultural appropriation, and then applying them to my situation.

The first understanding treats appropriation on the model of theft or trespass: a community stands in something like an ownership relation to its stories, ceremonies, and styles, and an outsider who takes them without permission wrongs the community even if no one is harmed in any measurable way — the wrong is in the taking itself. The second understanding treats appropriation on the model of harm: the wrong lies entirely in downstream effects — flat or distorted portrayals that become authoritative because the outsider's platform is larger, insider writers crowded out of the market for their own stories, sacred material cheapened into décor. On this view, absent such effects, there is nothing to object to.

Please compare these two accounts with care. In particular, test each against two cases I keep turning over. First: imagine the identical manuscript, word for word, written once by me and once by an insider. Do the two accounts deliver different verdicts about the two manuscripts, and is the divergence a mark for or against each account? Second: imagine my book is so faithful and illuminating that members of the culture in question say it taught them something about their own tradition — no distortion, and insider writers report being helped rather than crowded out. On the harm account this seems to dissolve the objection entirely; on the ownership account it may not. Which result is the right one?

Then apply your analysis to my actual case, including one further wrinkle: my novel's second storyline is set in a culture I invented wholesale, though I stitched it together from ceremonies, cadences, and social structures drawn recognizably from three real ones, none of them mine. Does the invented-culture case change anything, or does it merely launder the same problem?

I am not asking for a survey of positions or for sources. I want you to weigh the two accounts against each other, reach a verdict on which better captures what is actually at stake — or defend a way of dividing labor between them — and then tell me, on that basis, what an honest writer in my position may and may not do. Commit to a conclusion and justify it; a shrug would be of no use to me.
