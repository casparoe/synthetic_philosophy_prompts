In the late 1970s through the early 2000s, a serious research program tried to explain cultural change as a Darwinian process: ideas, tunes, rituals, and technologies were cast as replicators ("memes") competing for occupancy of human minds, with analogues of genes, mutation, and selection. Its champions promised a genuine science of culture; its critics replied that culture has no discrete copying units, no faithful high-fidelity replication, nothing like a genotype/phenotype split, and that transmission is reconstructive rather than duplicative — so the program produced evocative metaphors but few predictions that could fail. What strikes me is how the episode ended: not with a refutation, a synthesis, or even a clear verdict, but with the field quietly deflating (its dedicated journal ceased publication), while the word "meme" escaped into popular use meaning something entirely different, an internet joke template.
