Write a parody corporate compliance training module, roughly 600 words, for employees of a large company whose every policy must be certified as compatible with the doctrine of double effect before rollout. The module should walk staff through a three-part certification form they must file before taking any action expected to cause harm: attesting that the harm is not intended as an end or a means but merely foreseen as a side effect, certifying that the good outcome would have occurred without the harm (with a space for the standard objection that this is hard to check), and justifying proportionality. The humor should come from the doctrine's actual pressure points: the intend/foresee distinction dissolving under honest self-report; the closeness problem, handled via a form field requiring employees to describe their own action neither so coarsely that the harm disappears into it nor so finely that it detaches from it; and a genuinely hard edge case buried in the FAQ that reproduces the structure of the loop problem (e.g., a delivery rerouted onto a side track where the blockage that would stop it is itself the obstacle) that the form cannot score. Include one deadpan note from Legal explaining why the company abandoned the form's predecessor, which simply asked employees what they intended, and one worked example (a layoff, a demolition, or similar) whose certification passes on paper while being transparently the kind of case the doctrine was meant to forbid. Keep the tone of a real HR document throughout, and let the reader, not the module, draw the conclusion about how much the intend/foresee distinction can bear.
