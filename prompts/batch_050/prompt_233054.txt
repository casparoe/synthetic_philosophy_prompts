A friend of mine ended a pub arguement about international law by quoting Pascal at me: "Justice without might is helpless; might without justice is tyrannical." His point was that war crimes tribunals with no army behind them are pure theatre, so things like the ICC are a waste of money. I left annoyed but unable to say why, because the sentence sounds obviously true and yet I'm not convinced it says what he needs it to say. Could you take the slogan apart properly? Lay out the distinct precise theses it might be expressing — e.g. a causal claim that norms only get complied with when enforced, a conceptual claim about what tyranny is, a normative claim that the just are obliged to go and get power, or a thoroughly cynical claim that talk of justice is always just power dressing itself up — and for each one say whether it's a platitude, substantive and defensible, or actually false. Then tell me which reading, if any, would genuinely support his conclusion about the ICC, and whether the second half of the sentence in fact undercuts him rather than helping. (Someone also told me the fragment continues in a much gloomier direction — roughly that since we can't make the just strong, we've made the strong just — so if that's right, does it change the verdict?)
