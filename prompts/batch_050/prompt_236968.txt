I'm a grad student and I'm drafting a chapter on Marx's theory of exploitation, and I just had a long argument about it with a friend from the econ department who kept insisting that every objection I'd collected is either old hat or a technicality. Here's my list of the standard objections, which I'm fairly confident is complete:

1. The theory depends on the labor theory of value, which is false or at least unnecessary -- you can explain prices and profits without it, so "surplus value" does no explanatory work.
2. The transformation problem: Marx never gave a successful derivation of prices of production from labor values, and the attempts since show you can't have both equalized profit rates and conservation of value.
3. The heterogeneous labor problem: there's no non-circular way to reduce skilled labor to simple labor, so "abstract labor" isn't a well-defined magnitude.
4. The exchange-of-equivalents objection: Marx himself concedes the capitalist buys labor power at its full value, so nobody is cheated in exchange -- calling the result "exploitation" is a moral label slapped on a fair transaction.
5. Roemer's objection: you can generate exploitation without any surplus-labor extraction at all (e.g., through credit markets), so the real moral problem is the initial distribution of assets, not the labor process.

Here's my question: is there a sixth objection that is genuinely different from all of these -- not a rewording, not a special case, not a combination of two of them? If you think the literature really has run dry here, then construct one yourself, but make it something that attacks the exploitation claim itself rather than the machinery around it (i.e., don't give me yet another objection to the labor theory of value -- I've got those covered). Either way, tell me briefly how your objection relates to the ones on my list, and then I want a committed verdict, not a both-sides write-up: does your objection actually succeed against Marx's position, or does it fail, and why? If you think it only succeeds against a strawman version of Marx and the real theory survives it, say that plainly instead of hedging.
