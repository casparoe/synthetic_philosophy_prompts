So in my psych class last week we watched a video about Thomas Szasz, the guy who argued mental illness is basically a myth because real illness means something wrong with the body, and the mind isn't a body part. After class two of my friends got into a huge argument about it, specifically about ADHD since one of them takes meds for it. I stayed out of it but I keep thinking about who was actually right.

It went roughly like this. (1) Marcus said Szasz had a point: there's no blood test or brain scan for ADHD, the diagnosis is just a checklist of behaviors, and you can't be sick just for acting in ways other people find annoying -- being gay was listed in the DSM once and that didn't make it an illness. (2) Dana said that's a bad argument because plenty of real illnesses are diagnosed purely by symptoms with no test, like migraines, and nobody calls those fake; plus ADHD responds to medication in a predictable way, which a mere behavior-people-dislike wouldn't. (3) Marcus said medication response proves nothing since coffee also predictably changes how people act and we don't call sleepiness a disease, and the bigger problem is that the boundaries of ADHD are set by a committee deciding how many symptoms you need, and no real disease works like that. (4) Dana said committee-drawn lines don't make something unreal -- the line between planet and not-planet was literally voted on, and Pluto still exists; what matters is whether the category latches onto something in the world, and ADHD predicts real outcomes better than chance, so it latches onto something.

Then they both turned to me and I panicked and said lunch was basically over lol. Can you adjudicate? Go move by move through (1) to (4) and say who's right at each step, and whether either of them is missing something big.
