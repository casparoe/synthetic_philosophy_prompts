I teach a second-year undergrad ethics course, about 18 students, and I have a 75-minute slot for a session on whether a corporation itself can be morally blameworthy (as opposed to just the people in it). Design a role-play activity I can run where the students themselves run a fictional company through a decision procedure that produces a genuinely bad outcome that no single participant chose or even wanted. My idea is that afterwards we hold a mock trial, and first the prosecution and then the defense should each run out of people to blame even though everyone agrees something blameworthy happened. So I need: the setup of the fictional company and its decision rules, the roles students get, how to structure the procedure so the bad outcome emerges from the rules rather than from any villain, and the trial format with time allocations. Then a debrief plan that maps what happened onto the actual philosophical positions (the company as an agent in its own right vs. blame always reducing to individuals), including what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this (e.g. someone will play their role as a cartoon villain on purpose) and how to handle it. Also tell me honestly which feature of real corporate agency the activity can't capture and that I'll have to just state in the debrief.
