For the closing page of a special issue I am editing on how societies choose under radical uncertainty, I want to commission a poem with a precise brief. Write a villanelle spoken by a deliberator behind the veil of ignorance, whose two refrains are the rival rules for choosing there: the Rawlsian counsel to choose as though you will turn out to be the worst-off person in whatever society you select, and the Harsanyian counsel to choose as though you are equally likely to be anyone at all. The form must do the argumentative work. In the tercets, the refrains should pull apart over some concrete stake -- how much growth to sacrifice for a guaranteed floor, say -- each return of a refrain sharpening why its rule rejects the other. In the closing quatrain, where the villanelle forces the two refrains side by side for the first time, the poem should mark the one condition under which the rules coincide: as the deliberator's tolerance for risk collapses toward nothing, maximizing expected average utility becomes indistinguishable from protecting the minimum. A reader who knows this debate should be able to say which refrain belongs to whom and why they can meet only at that limit; a reader who does not should still find a good poem.
