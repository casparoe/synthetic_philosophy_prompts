So I'm a junior and I help run my school's philosophy club with two friends, and this semester we've been doing a unit on classical Chinese philosophy. Next week we're holding a mock exam to prep people for a regional ethics-and-philosophy bowl thing, and I got stuck writing the question on Gongsun Long's white horse dialogue (the School of Names guy). The problem is our club basically split into two camps after reading it: half of us think the claim that a white horse is not a horse is obviously just a sneaky word trick, and the other half think there's a real insight about language buried in it, and I don't want to write a question that secretly assumes one side is right.

Could you draft me one essay question on the dialogue that a student could answer in about 40 minutes? It shouldn't be answerable by just saying "it's a paradox" or "it's a pun on the Chinese word for not." I want it to force students to actually reconstruct the arguments Gongsun Long gives -- like the bit where asking for a horse is satisfied by a yellow or black horse but asking for a white horse isn't, and the bit about horse picking out the shape while white picks out the color -- and then take a position on whether the dialogue is equivocating between two readings of "is not" (not identical to vs. not a member of the kind) or whether exposing that exact ambiguity is the whole point. Bonus points if the question makes them engage with the fact that the "white horses are just a subclass of horses, so the claim is trivially true" reading is technically available but kind of deflating, since if that's all he meant, why write a whole dialogue about it.

Along with the question, give me a model answer of roughly 500-600 words and a marking scheme: five must-hit points, what each is worth out of 25 total marks, and the typical way a student fumbles each one. One more thing -- since our club clearly can't agree on the right interpretation, please have the model answer and scheme flag which readings are pretty standard among scholars and which are still genuinely contested, so I don't end up docking marks from someone just for disagreeing with whatever interpretation I happen to like. Definately don't want the answer key presenting one reading as the settled consensus if it isn't.
