Two people in my grad ethics reading group have been going at each other all semester. One's a committed prioritarian: benefitting people matters more the worse off they are, and that's all there is to fairness. The other keeps hammering the leveling-down objection and thinks prioritarianism is just egalitarianism with the part that matters surgically removed. They've finally agreed to write one joint statement that both can sign, and they've asked me to draft it. I'm out of my depth, so draft it for them.

What I need from the document:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims, things both of them genuinely accept about what the priority view actually says, about what leveling down would involve, and about what the objection does and doesn't establish. No strawmen, but also no filler claims so vague they commit nobody to anything.
2. The precise disagreements that remain, each stated in wording each side would accept as a fair description of their own view. Two things that have to show up in there somehow, phrased neutrally: the egalitarian's claim that a levelled-down world is worse in at least one respect even if better in none, and the prioritarian's suspicion that the whole fight really turns on whether value attaches to how people fare or to patterns across people.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions where getting an answer would actually move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is whether equality has any intrinsic value or only matters via what it does for people, and another is whether the impersonal verdicts in cases like the divided world or giving a scarce treatment to someone blind versus someone sighted can be trusted at all. For each crux, give a concrete case both agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction about that case in advance.
4. If some chunk of their dispute is really verbal, flag which chunk and why -- they've both independently said they suspect this is true of part of it, which is probably a sign.

Format it as the numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table, then the cruxes. Keep it in their register as collaborators, not a textbook summary of egalitarinism vs prioritarianism.
