For a philosophy of mathematics reading group next term, each of us has been asked to circulate a short statement of where we stand on infinity, so that the others can try to pull it apart. I've been arguing about this with a friend who is a convinced finitist and another who treats Cantor as settled scripture, and I've ended up somewhere in the middle that I can't quite vouch for. Here is roughly my position:

(1) Cantor's diagonal argument is sound, and it is a discovery rather than a convention: the real numbers genuinely outnumber the natural numbers, and any mathematics that cannot recover this fact is to that extent deficient. (2) The only intelligible infinity is potential. The natural numbers are never all there at once; "every n has a successor" expresses an open-ended process, not membership of a completed totality, and talk of "the set of all naturals" as a finished object is a façon de parler at best. (3) Hilbert's hotel cases — shifting every guest along one room to free up a vacancy, accommodating infinitely many new arrivals at once, and so on — are genuine reductios: they show that an actually realised infinity permitting such operations is impossible, not merely counterintuitive. (4) All the same, a spatially infinite universe strikes me as a live physical possibility, and I don't think anything in the hotel arguments counts against it. (5) Mathematical truths about infinite structures hold, or fail to hold, regardless of whether anything physical ever instantiates them.

Could you audit this package for me? Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look as though they are. For each real tension, say how severe it is, what precisely generates it, and what the cheapest fix would be — including the option of clarifying a commitment rather than abandoning it, where that would do the work. Where a tension dissolves on inspection, explain why the appearance arises anyway, since I'd like to be able to answer that move when the others make it. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most and what it would cost me. I'd rather you were specific about what has to go than reassuring about how much can stay.
