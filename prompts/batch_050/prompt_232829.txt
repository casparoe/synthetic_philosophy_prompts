I am drafting a position statement on complicity — that is, participation in wrongdoing one did not oneself cause — and before circulating it I would like to know whether the commitments I have assembled are mutually consistent. They are as follows:

1. Complicity requires that one's participation make some difference, at least in expectation if not in fact, to the wrongdoing or its severity. A person whose involvement is wholly redundant — such that the wrong would have occurred identically without them — is not complicit, whatever their attitude toward it may have been.

2. An ordinary consumer of factory-farmed meat is complicit in the wrongs of factory farming. The fact that no individual purchase perceptibly alters production decisions does not exempt the consumer, because consumption at scale sustains the industry.

3. A citizen who abstains from voting in a safe-seat election, and who deplores the winning party's signature policy, bears no complicity in that policy. Their vote could not have changed the outcome, and principled condemnation without participation is a coherent stance.

4. A getaway driver is complicit in an armed robbery even in the version of the case where the robbery would have succeeded in exactly the same manner without any driver. Facilitation undertaken with the intention of enabling the wrong suffices for complicity.

5. Assessments of complicity should be insensitive to motive. Whether one participates out of greed, loyalty, or sheer indifference has no bearing on whether one is complicit, since complicity concerns one's relation to the wrong rather than one's reasons for acting.

Please audit this set. Distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones, and for each apparent tension explain briefly why it dissolves on inspection. For each genuine tension, state which commitments are involved, how severe the conflict is, and what the cheapest revision would be — that is, which commitment to drop or weaken, and what that revision would cost the rest of the position. I have not decided in advance which commitment I would surrender, so do not treat any of them as sacrosanct. Conclude by identifying the single revision that would restore consistency with the least collateral damage to the set as a whole.
