In a historiography seminar I help run, the old intentionalist–structuralist fight keeps resurfacing in its crudest form: would there have been something like Nazi Germany, the Second World War, and the Holocaust without Hitler? The striking thing is that both sides know the same scholarship — Weimar's structural crisis, the radicalization of institutions, Hitler's personal fixations and improvisations — and neither moves. My hypothesis is that the debate has stalled because it is really three counterfactuals wearing one costume (no Nazi movement, no regime of that kind, no genocide), and because the two camps are running different counterfactual rules without saying so: intentionalists hold the political landscape fixed and remove the man, structuralists ask what the landscape would have selected for in his absence — so each side's 'obvious' answer is an artifact of which variables it freezes. Am I diagnosing this correctly, or is there a genuine first-order disagreement underneath — about how much explanatory weight individual psychology can bear, or about what the documentary record actually licenses?
