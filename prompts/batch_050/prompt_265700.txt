So I've been going down a rabbit hole on the philosophy of perception and photography (partly because of a stupid argument in a group chat about whether security cam footage "shows" you what happened), and I keep coming back to the transparency debate -- whether we literally see things through photographs, or just see representations of them. Rather than ask who is right, let me just dump my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, because I can't tell what my own theory is, and I'd like you to find the principle that systematizes them, or tell me which gut verdict I have to abandon.

Verdicts: (1) When I look at a faded photo of my grandmother as a young woman, I really do feel like I'm seeing her, even though she died before I was born. (2) A hyperrealistic oil painting of her from the same sitting, indistinguishable from the photo at a glance -- nope, that's just a picture of a picture-ish thing, I don't feel like I'm seeing her at all. (3) Looking at my friend through a periscope or in a mirror: obviously seeing her, no question. (4) Watching a live CCTV feed of the parking lot: seeing the lot, I think. (5) Watching the recording of that same feed the next morning: still seeing it, just in the past tense somehow. (6) An AI-generated image that's photorealistically perfect but of a person who never existed: clearly not seeing anyone, even though -- and this is the part that bugs me -- the visual experience could be pixel-for-pixel identical to (1). (7) A night-vision or thermal camera pointed at my dog: leaning yes, I see the dog, though less confidently than the mirror case. (8) A photocopy of a photocopy of a photo, degraded into grey mush: at some point the seeing runs out, but I couldn't tell you on which copy. My tentative principle was something like "seeing is preserved by mechanical/counterfactual dependence on the scene, and broken by human interpretation," which handles (1) vs (2) nicely, but I can't figure out what it says about (5) vs (6) -- a recording and a generated image are both just data on a screen, and the temporal gap in (5) seems like it shouldn't matter if the mirror in (3) counts. So: is there a single principle (causal dependence? belief-independence? information-theoretic channel?) that gets all eight, or at least seven of them? State it precisely, tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up and why that verdict was seductive, and then apply it to two cases I didn't list: an ultrasound image of a fetus, and a photorealistic drawing made by a police sketch artist working from an eyewitness description.
