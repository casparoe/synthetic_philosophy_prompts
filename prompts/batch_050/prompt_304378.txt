In a seminar I'm TAing, a student defended epistocracy by reaching for the old ship analogy: nobody would let passengers vote on how to steer a ship -- you'd want the person who actually knows navigation -- so why do we let just anyone vote on running the state? He says the case for rule by the knowledgeable is basically done once you grant that governing is a skill like piloting. Audit the analogy for him properly: give the correspondence table (who's the captain, who's the owner, what's the destination, what's the storm), then tell me where the mapping breaks -- I'm especially suspicious that ships have a destination everyone already agrees on and states don't, and that on a ship the experts can be checked against results in a way that's murkier in politics. Do the breaks matter for his conclusion, or only for the details? And if the ship picture can't carry the argument, is there a better analogy for epistocracy that does -- or does every decent candidate smuggle in the same fix?
