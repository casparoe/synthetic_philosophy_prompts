For a philosophy of science reading group I have to write down where I stand on the "is elegance evidence" question so the others can pick at it, and before I hand it in I want a consistency check. Here's the profile, roughly as I'd defend it:

(1) Elegance is genuine evidence for truth, not just a tiebreaker or a sign that a theory is pleasant to work with. Of two theories that fit the data equally well, the more elegant one is more probably true.

(2) The justification for (1) is the track record. Maxwell cleaning up electromagnetism, Einstein betting on general relativity's formal beauty, Dirac taking the mathematically prettier branch of his equation and landing on antimatter — physicists who followed beauty won often enough that this inductively licenses trusting it now.

(3) What "elegant" means, when you pin it down, is basically high unification and compressive power relative to what the theory says — a Bayesian simplicity prior. It's not some irreducible aesthetic hunch.

(4) Elegance judgments are pretty clearly fashion-sensitive and shift over time. The naturalness arguments that made supersymmetry look almost forced in the 80s and 90s now read as wishful thinking, and nobody has a principled account of why today's taste is better than that taste.

(5) A concrete verdict I want to keep: the steady state theorists were right to find the perfect cosmological principle beautiful, but wrong to hang onto it once the CMB data came in — which I take to show that beauty-based priors are real but weak, and should yield to even moderate empirical friction.

(6) I also think the Ptolemaic system was genuinely elegant for its day (uniform circular motion, the perfection of the sphere and all that), and that doesn't count against (1).

So: does this hang together? Go through the pairs, tell me which are in real tension and which only look like it, and rank the real ones by severity — for each, what has to give and what the cheapest repair costs. My sense is (1) vs (5) and (1) vs (6) will look like the flashpoints, but don't take my word for it. I'm not attached to dropping any particular one, so don't assume which revision I'd prefer.

And two requests on top: first, be honest about how confident you are in your severest-flagged tension — if it hinges on a contestable reading of one of the historical episodes, say so rather than presenting it as settled. Second, tell me what would change your mind: what argument, or what historical case I haven't considered, would most plausibly flip your verdict on the worst tension, and where is your reasoning most likely to be going wrong?
