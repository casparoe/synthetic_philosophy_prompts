I am a novelist in the early stages of planning my next book, and before I begin drafting I have written down the working principles I intend to hold myself to. I would like an audit of whether they are jointly coherent, because I have an uneasy feeling they are not, and I cannot locate the fault myself.

Here is the set:

(1) Plot must be character-driven. Events should follow from what the characters choose, given who they are, rather than from what the story needs to happen. Coincidence and authorial contrivance are failures of craft.

(2) The strongest tragic plots are escalation plots of the dollar-auction kind: two or more characters locked into a contest — a feud, a custody dispute, a rivalry for a single position — in which every individual step is a reasonable response to the one before, yet the cumulative outcome is ruinous for everyone involved. I want my book to be a tragedy of exactly this kind.

(3) For a tragedy to be affecting rather than merely bleak, the protagonist must at some point have been genuinely able to walk away. The reader must believe the exit was truly open; otherwise the character is a puppet of the structure and the story is fatalism dressed up as tragedy.

(4) Sunk-cost reasoning is a fallacy, full stop. I do not want any of my characters' choices to be vindicated by it, and I do not want the engine of the plot to run on it.

(5) Verisimilitude requires depicting real human irrationality, including the grip that prior investment has on people. Characters who always reason correctly are unconvincing.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture on the psychology of escalation. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; order the genuine tensions by severity; for each, identify the cheapest available revision and what it would cost the book. If one of the five principles turns out to be doing more damage than the others, say which single revision would clean up the most. And if some of what looks like tension dissolves once you distinguish between what a story depicts and what it endorses, or between the choices made inside a contest and the choice to enter it, I would like that spelled out rather than left as a hint.

Please write your answer in British English.
