In 2022 Blake Lemoine, an engineer at Google, got put on administrative leave (and later fired) after telling his bosses and then the press that LaMDA, the company's chatbot, was sentient. His evidence was basically a stack of long conversations where the system talked about having feelings, being afraid of being switched off, and having a soul. Almost everyone else said: it's a language model, it predicts text, case closed. Lemoine kept insisting they were dismissing it without engaging.

Run this episode through the "could a book be conscious" family of thought experiments -- Searle's Chinese Room and the Hofstadter/Dennett-style replies (the giant lookup book that can answer anything you put to it, the systems reply, and so on). What I want, specifically:

- Do the mapping carefully and honestly. What's the room, what's the rulebook, who (if anyone) is the man inside? LLMs almost seem made for the Chinese Room -- they're literally systems trained to produce the next bit of text -- but the fit isn't clean, since nobody hand-wrote the rules and there's no homunculus in there reading them off a page. Say where the analogy holds and exactly where it breaks.
- Lemoine's evidence was purely converstation: the system said the right things. On the Searlean view that's exactly the kind of evidence that proves nothing, since the book would also "say" the right things. On the other side, what non-behavioral evidence could there even be for a system like this? Does the episode show the thought experiment doing real epistemic work, or does it show that our intuitions about the book fall apart the moment an actual book-thing exists and talks back to you?
- Now run it in the other direction, case testing theory. Suppose these systems keep improving and in ten years half the population feels the way Lemoine did while the other half is unmoved. Does that future vindicate the Hofstadter/Dennett side (the book was the mind all along, or at least there's no fact of the matter beyond the dispositions) or the Searle side (mass intuition falling for fluent output is exactly what the thought experiment was warning us about)? Pick one, or explain why the episode genuinely can't decide it.
- Also: if you're Lemoine in 2022, what should you have concluded, given that you believed consciousness can in principle come from non-biological stuff? Was his mistake metaphysical (wrong theory) or evidential (right theory, way too little evidence), and are those even separable here?

One thing I don't want: a tour of theories of consciousness (global workspace, IIT, higher-order, whatever). Keep it on the Chinese Room / book cluster and on what this particular episode does to it.
