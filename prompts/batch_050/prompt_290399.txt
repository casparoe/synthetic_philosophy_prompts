My department has two people who keep fighting about moral sainthood: a Wolf-style skeptic who thinks the ideally moral person would be a bland, one-dimensional creature nobody should want to be, and a hard-nosed maximizer who thinks the best person just is whoever does the most good, full stop, and that complaining about their personality is moral frivolity. Draft the joint statement they'd produce if someone forced them into an adversarial collaboration.

Structure it exactly like this: first "What we both accept" — numbered claims, maybe 5-8 of them, ~200 words total — which should cover what the moral saint concept even is and that the dispute is about ideals, not about whether helping others is good. Then "Where we still disagree" — a table with columns for the claim as the skeptic words it, as the maximizer words it, and what hangs on it. Then "Cruxes" — pick three, one paragraph each, and for each a case or argument both sides accept as relevant, with each side's prediction recorded. One crux must be whether Wolf's objection survives if sainthood is evaluated as a social ideal rather than an individual one, i.e. whether a world of saints is unattractive even if one saint among non-saints is. Also make them settle whether their fight about "the saint lacks non-moral interests" is substantive or just two people using "interest" differently — if verbal, say so and which side's wording survives. End with "What would change my mind" — two sentences from each party, written in first person. Keep the whole thing under 800 words and don't let either side sound like a strawman.
