I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm writing a long piece on money-pump arguments and whether being exploitable in a sequence of trades actually shows anything about rationality. I've interviewed two camps who mostly talk past each other, and for the article I want to reconstruct the document they'd produce if they ever sat down together properly.

Act as a professional philosopher specialising in decision theory. Before drafting anything, do a short brainstorm (bullet points is fine) about what each side could genuinely sign without crossing its fingers -- then think carefully and write the joint statement.

The two camps:

1. The money-pump camp. They hold that if your preferences let a trader cycle you through options (A for B plus a fee, B for C plus a fee, C for A plus a fee...) until you're back where you started but poorer, then something is rationally defective in you, and that this kind of argument genuinely supports requirements like transitivity. Diachronic coherence is a real demand of reason, not just a nice-to-have.

2. The synchronic camp. They hold that all requirements of rationality apply to your attitudes at a single time. At each step the cycled agent prefers the trade on offer, so nothing irrational happens at any point; the pump merely shows that certain preference profiles can be made poorer, and rationality was never a guarantee against losses. They think money-pump arguments smuggle in extra premises -- that the agent is naive or forgetful about what's coming, or that "A later, after some trades" counts as the same option as "A now".

The statement should have: (a) a numbered list of agreed claims -- I expect both can accept the bare arithmetic of the pump, that exploitation needs extra premises beyond the preferences themselves, and that an agent told the full schedule in advance can just decline steps; work out what else belongs; (b) a disagreements table where each row states the issue neutrally and then gives each camp's position in wording that camp would actually accept (this is the hard bit -- nothing slanted, nothing vacuous); (c) a list of cruxes, and for each crux a case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

As a shared test case, include what I'll call the announced pump: the trader publishes every trade and its timing upfront, the agent understands the whole sequence, and each trade is still offered one at a time. If the agent walks through all of it knowingly, the money-pump camp should say what exactly went wrong and the synchronic camp should say why nothing did -- and both should commit to that in writing before seeing each other's answers.

Keep the whole thing under about 800 words, and make sure you seperate real disagreements from ones that are just about words.
