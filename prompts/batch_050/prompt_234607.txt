i'm auditing a seminar on fear and trembling this semester and the students keep flattening the knight of faith into either a hero or a fanatic, so for the last meeting i want to hand out a poem that does better than their essays did. write me a villanelle about abraham on mount moriah where the two refrains track the double movement -- one line for infinite resignation (giving isaac up, genuinely, with no secret reservation) and one for faith (the absurd belief that he gets him back anyway) -- so that the way the refrains keep returning and finally land side by side in the closing quatrain enacts the point that faith isn't resignation plus time, it's a second movement on top of the first. someone who knows the text should be able to point at each refrain and say which movement it is.

a few things i care about. the poem shouldn't cheat by letting abraham comfort himself with the sacrificial lamb in advance -- kierkegaard's whole point is that the knight of faith makes the movement of resignation fully, and the lamb arriving isn't faith, it's just a happy ending. it also shouldn't resolve into either "he trusted god so it was fine" or "he was willing to kill his son so he was a monster" -- the teleological suspension of the ethical should sit there unresolved and uncomfortable, the way it does in the book. and please no narrator moralizing at the end; if the poem has a verdict it should be in the form, not in a closing statement.

form-wise: actual villanelle rules, 19 lines, the refrains repeating properly and rhyming with each other, but i'm fine with small variations in the refrain lines as long as they stay recognizable (kierkegaard would probably approve of repetition-with-variation anyway). keep it readable aloud, not overly archaic, and under no circumstances rhyme "moriah" with anything corny. thanks!
