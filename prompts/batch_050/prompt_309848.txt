I am planning a realist novel about an agricultural county transformed by the arrival of remote professional workers, and I am trying to articulate the moral outlook governing the book before I begin drafting. I should be grateful for an audit of whether the following commitments form a coherent position rather than a bundle of attractive slogans:

1. Neither the rural working-class outlook nor the urban professional-class outlook is, as a complete way of life, more defensible than the other; each is an adaptation to different material circumstances and forms of dependence.

2. Cosmopolitan impartiality is morally superior to strong local partiality, since the former more consistently recognises the equal standing of strangers, migrants and future outsiders.

3. A community’s distrust of public institutions may be practically rational even when the factual beliefs supporting that distrust are inaccurate: where information is poor and past betrayals are salient, suspicion can be a reasonable defensive policy.

4. Nevertheless, beliefs about society should be judged by their truth, and an outlook that systematically misidentifies the causes of local decline is, to that extent, defective.

5. In the novel, both communities should be portrayed as arguing in good faith. Neither side may function merely as the author’s ventriloquist dummy or as a cautionary caricature.

6. The narrative should expose professional-class universalism as an ideology that often conceals class advantage, particularly when fluency, credentials and geographical mobility are mistaken for superior moral insight.

7. It should also show rural localism becoming cruel when loyalty to “people like us” ceases to be a form of mutual protection and instead excuses the exclusion of vulnerable newcomers.

8. Understanding why a value arose from a group’s circumstances does not by itself confer authority upon that value, but refusing to understand its origins is a failure of both artistic and moral imagination.

Please assess which commitments genuinely pull against one another, which only appear to do so, and how severe each tension is. For every genuine tension, identify the smallest revision that would remove it and state what that revision would cost. Finally, say which single amendment would make the overall position most coherent without turning the novel into a brief for either side.
