So last night I sat through a two-hour argument between my friends Dan and Mara about when a bunch of things actually become ONE thing (they'd both just read about the special composition question in some anthology) and I couldn't settle it, so please adjudicate.

Here's the back-and-forth as best I remember it:

(1) Dan: composition basically never happens except with living things. A table is just simples arranged tablewise -- nothing genuinely new comes into existence. But an organism is a real composite object, because its parts get caught up in a life, and that's a non-arbitrary, mind-independent boundary.

(2) Mara: that just relocates the puzzle. "Life" is vague -- viruses, dormant seeds, a body two seconds after death vs. two seconds before. So his view says composition flickers on and off along a blurry border, and if we're tolerating vagueness anyway, why is HIS vague line better than anyone elses?

(3) Dan: every interesting boundary is vague (heap, bald, rich) and nobody thinks that's fatal. At least his boundary is drawn by nature, not by our interests, which is more than you can say for "composition happens whenever it's useful to say so."

(4) Mara: fine, here's her own rule -- composition happens exactly when a group of things falls under a single control loop whose outputs can't be broken down into independent contributions of the parts. A beehive regulating its temperature: one object. A pile of rocks: no. She says this explains why organisms feel special WITHOUT invoking life, and it's more precise than "life" anyway.

(5) Dan: that rule has insane consequences. A platoon of self-driving trucks coordinating their braking shares one control loop, so on her view the platoon literally pops into existence as a new object on the highway and pops out at the exit. A view that generates objects that fast has to be wrong.

(6) Mara: she bites the bullet -- sure, the platoon is one thing while it drives as one. That's a feature: it explains why we naturally say "the platoon merged into the left lane" instead of listing trucks. Dan's view can't explain why that talk feels so natural, since for him the platoon is just simples arranged platoonwise.

They both looked at me and I had nothing. Please rate each move (1)-(6) from clearly mistaken to clearly right with a sentence or two each, say what if anything BOTH of them are missing, and give an overall verdict on who's more right.
