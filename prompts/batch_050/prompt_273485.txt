Darwin is often read as showing that adaptive design can arise without a designer, and Turing as showing that rule-governed symbol use can be mechanized without first settling what understanding is. Compare these as two species of competence without comprehension by using this distinction: an upstream inversion occurs when a system lacking a higher-level capacity reliably generates that capacity at another level, while a downstream masking effect occurs when the generated capacity later tempts observers to infer insight, intention, or reasons that were never part of the mechanism. Which kind of move is primary in Darwin, which in Turing, and where does each risk equivocating between producing a competence and explaining its normativity? In your answer, address whether natural selection is better described as an algorithm, a filter, or a reasoner; whether Turing’s machine makes comprehension explanatorily dispensable or only methodologically optional; and whether fluent language-model behavior is best treated as a possible case of downstream masking rather than as evidence either for or against understanding.
