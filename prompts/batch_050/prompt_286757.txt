I'm a PhD student working on 19th century philosophy of religion, and I'm drafting the framing chapter of my dissertation, which is basically my attempt to bring Fear and Trembling into conversation with contemporary religious ethics. Before I send it to my supervisor I want a consistency check on my core commitments, because I have a nagging feeling something in there doesn't fit but I can't locate it.

Here's what I'm committed to:

(a) The teleological suspension of the ethical is a genuine possibility, not just a thought experiment. Abraham really does step outside the universal ethical order, and he's justified in doing so by his absolute relation to the absolute.

(b) The horror of the story has to be preserved at all costs. The worst misreading is the domesticated one where Abraham is just obviously right and we all nod along. If the story stops being scandalous, we've lost it.

(c) I'm a divine command theorist. What makes an action obligatory is that God commands it, and what makes it wrong is that God forbids it. I've defended this in a paper and I'm not really looking to relitigate it.

(d) Abraham cannot speak. There's no justification he could offer Isaac or Sarah or the ethical community that would make his action intelligible to them; faith at this level is essentially incommunicable.

(e) Agamemnon is a tragic hero, not a knight of faith: his sacrifice of Iphigenia stays within the ethical, since the good of the city is itself an ethical demand, which is exactly why we can weep for him and understand him.

(f) If someone today killed their child and claimed a private command from God, the state would be right to prosecute them, and no court should accept a teleological suspension as a defense.

(g) Abraham's faith is the paradigm of religious faith, the thing every serious believer should aspire to.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I'd like the tensions ranked by how serious they are, with what would have to give in each case and roughly what that revision would cost the chapter. Also flag any pair where the apparent problem dissolves once you're careful, because I suspect at least one of my examiners will raise it and I'd like to have the dissolution ready. Don't just tell me everything is in tension with everything, that wouldn't help.
