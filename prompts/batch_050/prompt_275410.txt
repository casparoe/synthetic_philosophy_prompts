I run a faculty colloquium series, and next term's guest is a philosopher defending a view she calls hedonic panqualityism: the position that whatever phenomenal properties panpsychists attribute to fundamental physical entities are not neutral proto-experiences but carry positive or negative valence, so that suffering and enjoyment are woven into the fundamental furniture of the world, with familiar sentient suffering being a concentrated, integrated case of something ubiquitous. Draft seven to eight colloquium questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. I want the set to press on at least the following: why the fundamental phenomenal properties should be valenced at all rather than affectively neutral, given that valence arguably presupposes an appetitive or motivational structure that electrons and fields plausibly lack; how micro-valences are supposed to combine or aggregate, and whether the view thereby inherits the combination problem in a sharpened form, since summing tiny sufferings threatens grotesque totals wherever matter is dense or interactions frequent; whether the causal closure of physics renders micro-valence epiphenomenal, and if so why moral reasons should track a feature of the world that does no causal work; and whether the view is ever action-guiding for us -- does it speak to, say, high-energy physics, computing, or entropy production, or does it conveniently converge with ordinary morality wherever it might otherwise make revisionary demands. End with a question asking her to state, on her own terms, what would count against the view, with a follow-up ready for the reply that metaphysical positions are not subject to empirical falsification.
