My roommate is in a phil bio grad program and he has this argument he thinks kills functional explanation in biology, and it's been bugging me all week because every premise looks fine on its own but the conclusion is obviously nuts. Here it is:

(1) Saying the heart is for pumping blood, or that the cuckoo's egg mimics the host's eggs, is giving the reason why those traits are there.
(2) A reason is the sort of thing that could in principle be someone's reason: something an agent could weigh, act on, or offer in defense of what they did.
(3) Nobody designed hearts or cuckoo eggs. Natural selection is a mindless sieve, not a planner, so there is no agent whose reason any of this could be.
(4) So the "reason" these traits exist isn't a reason at all -- function talk is at best a metaphor we project onto the machinery.
(5) Metaphors can be handy teaching devices but they can't do explanatory work, because an explanation has to state facts.
(6) So functional explanation in biology explains nothing, and rigorous biology should restrict itself to mechanisms and actual causal history.

The conclusion has to be wrong -- hearts really are for pumping blood in a way that kidney stones aren't for anything, and biology would be crippled if it couldn't say so. I also want to hang onto both (3) and that claim. So: wich premise or step do I give up, why did it look so reasonable, and what does rejecting it cost? If there's more than one live candidate for the weak point, price the main alternatives (biting the bullet on eliminativism about function, treating selection itself as some kind of proto-agent, grounding function in selected effects). I know this connects to Dennett's idea that reasons can float free of any reasoner, competence without comprehension and all that, but I want a diagnosis of the argument, not a book report on him. One more thing: be straight about what's basically settled here (no designer needed, selection isn't an agent) versus what's genuinely contested among philosophers of biology (whether function ascriptions are merely heuristic, whether selection explanations are even causal in the first place), and don't present one camp's position as the consensus.
