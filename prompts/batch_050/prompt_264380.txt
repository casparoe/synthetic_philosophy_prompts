Please act as an expert on philosophy of mind who's read this debate closely. Brainstorm a bit first about where the theory is genuinely vulnerable, then draft 8-10 interview questions for me. I want questions that go beyond "so what is the global workspace", specifically:

- At least one question on Ned Block's access vs phenomenal consciousness distinction -- whether GWT just *defines* consciousness as access and thereby explains away phenomenal consciousness rather than explaining it
- At least one on rich-but-unattended experience (the classic Sperling-style full-array results, the feel of the fridge hum you only notice when it stops) and whether GWT is committed to saying those aren't conscious
- At least one on the "no-report paradigm" move in consciousness science -- whether stripping out report requirements actually isolates consciousness or just isolates whatever correlates with it
- At least one on why broadcasting should generate *experience at all* rather than just fancier information processing (the residual hard-problem worry)
- And one on what would falsify the view, since critics sometimes say it absorbs every result

For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give -- e.g., if she says the fridge hum becomes conscious the moment you attend to it, press on whether that's unfalsifiable without report-independent markers. No softballs about her career path please.
