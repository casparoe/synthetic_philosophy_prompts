A friend and I keep circling the same snag whenever we talk about the ethics of how we treat very simple creatures, and I want to know whether the snag is something philosophers have already identified and named. The conversation usually goes like this. Someone says we needn't worry much about, say, the shrimp in industrial aquaculture or the insects in a mealworm farm, because their nervous systems are tiny. When pressed, this claim splits into two quite different things, and my friend and I noticed that people slide between them without noticing. Sometimes the claim seems to be: the creature probably isn't conscious at all, so there's nothing there to worry about. Other times it seems to be: fine, maybe it is conscious, but whatever it feels is so faint or simple that it barely counts. These strike us as completely different moves. The first is a bet that there's nobody home; the second concedes somebody might be home but discounts whatever happens to them. And they behave differently under uncertainty -- if you're only 40% sure the shrimp feels anything, that seems like a different kind of reason than being fully sure it feels something but rating that something as nearly weightless.

We noticed the same slide at the other end of the scale. Someone brings up a thermostat, which has a sensor and a feedback loop and in some thin sense "detects" temperature, as a reductio of taking simple systems seriously. But again the reply equivocates: is the point that the thermostat almost certainly has no experience whatsoever (a claim about the probability of consciousness), or that its experience, if any, is infinitesimal (a claim about degree)? People swap these mid-sentence. My friend thinks the two scales even get traded against each other, as if being fairly sure a creature is barely sentient were somehow equivalent to being unsure whether it's highly sentient, when morally these could diverge enormously depending on how you aggregate risk and magnitude.

Is there an established term for this conflation -- treating "how likely is it that the being is sentient at all" and "how much sentience does it have, and how much does that sentience matter" as if they were one dial rather than two? I'd guess it sits somewhere near discussions of moral status under uncertainty and of graded versus threshold views of consciousness, but I don't know whether anyone has isolated the slide itself as a distinct error with its own name. If there is such a term, please tell me what it is and how the literature draws the boundary I'm gesturing at; if there isn't, I'd appreciate you saying so plainly, proposing a name and a precise definition, and working through some boundary cases -- for instance, whether the same conflation shows up in debates about fetal pain, about AI systems, or about patients with severe brain injuries, or whether those turn out to be different confusions that merely look similar.
