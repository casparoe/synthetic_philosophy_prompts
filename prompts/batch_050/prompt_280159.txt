I maintain the backend for a small multiplayer VR world, and two incidents last year split our team. In one, a player exploited a duplication bug to copy a rare in-game item that other players had bought with real money; in the other, a user was followed and blocked across instances for weeks and described the experience as genuinely distressing. Our lead engineer's position is that nothing real was stolen and nothing real happened to anyone: the item is rows in a database that we can restore with a query, and whatever harm occurred happened to a real person offline, caused by -- not in -- the software. Our community manager's position is that the theft was a real theft, the harassment was real harm, and both took place in a real (if virtual) environment whose objects are as real as anything made of information.

Draft the joint statement these two would write if they committed to an adversarial collaboration. It should contain: a numbered list of claims both can sign, about what software is, what users experience, and what actually happened in each incident; the residual disagreements stated precisely, in wording each would accept as a fair description of their own view; the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one of them (I suspect one concerns whether 'real' is doing more than one job here, and another whether duplicability undermines value); and for each crux, a concrete case or possible finding both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about it recorded. If part of their dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
