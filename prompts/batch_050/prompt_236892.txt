I'm a software engineer who has been working slowly through an introductory set theory text, and there is a pattern in how mathematicians handle definitions that I can't find a word for, and it's starting to bother me. Three instances. First, the ordered pair: the text defines ⟨x, y⟩ as {{x}, {x, y}}, proves that two pairs are equal exactly when their coordinates are equal, and then never appeals to the definition again — everything afterward talks only about coordinates. A remark mentions that an older, differently encoded definition works just as well, and treats the choice between them as arbitrary. Second, the natural numbers: built from the empty set in such a way that each number is an element of every later number, so "is 2 an element of 4?" comes out yes — while a footnote admits an alternative construction on which it comes out no, and insists that nothing in arithmetic is affected. Third, the real numbers: constructed laboriously out of sets of rationals via something called Dedekind cuts, after which the construction is dropped entirely and the reals are treated as points on a line obeying certain algebraic laws.

The pattern is this: a definition is supplied, a handful of key facts are extracted from it, and the definition is then treated as scaffolding to be kicked away. Two people who built the object differently will agree on every theorem and take themselves to be studying the same thing, even though, on the face of it, their objects have different properties — one person's 2 is an element of their 4, the other's isn't. Note that the awkward question isn't meaningless: on the standard construction it has a perfectly definite answer. The puzzle is that the answer seems to be no part of arithmetic. In my day job this pattern is utterly familiar and has standard names — interfaces with multiple implementations, information hiding, the rule that code should depend on behavior rather than representation. What I want to know is whether the mathematical version has an established name, and whether it is the same phenomenon as the programming one or a genuinely different one wearing similar clothes.

If it's a recognized issue, I'd like the best articulation of the problem it poses — my text's silence makes me suspect mathematicians consider it solved, or ill-posed, or somehow both, and I can't tell which — plus an account of how it differs from the software case. Please don't hand me a bare label and stop: I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges are. And I'd rather you commit than catalogue. Tell me which existing concept you judge to be the one I'm groping toward, and defend the choice against the nearest rivals; or, if you think none of them fits, coin a term, define it precisely, and give me two or three borderline cases so I can test whether it captures what I noticed.
