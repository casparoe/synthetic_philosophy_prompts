So I played club cricket for years and last season I developed what bowlers call the yips -- suddenly couldn't land the ball anywhere near the pitch, the harder I concentrated the worse it got, and my teammates advice ("just dont think about it!") was useless. Someone told me Merleau-Ponty's stuff about motor intentionality and the lived body is supposedly the best philosophical account of exactly this. So: take the yips in sport (baseball second basemen who can't make the throw to first, golfers who can't putt, darts players, whatever example you know best) and analyze it through his theory. What does the theory say is actually happening when a skill that lived in the body gets dragged into explicit deliberation -- why does thinking about the throw break it rather than improve it? Why does "stop thinking about it" fail as advice on this picture, and what remedies would the theory actually predict to work (or not)? And run it the other way too: the yips seems to sometimes have a straightforwardly neurological side (focal dystonia etc.) -- does a case where the body just misfires show a limit in Merleau-Ponty's account, or does he have resources for it?
