Whenever our magazine runs a piece on ranked-choice voting or electoral reform, the same line surfaces in the comments and letters: Arrow's impossibility theorem proves that no fair voting system exists, so arguments about which system to adopt are rearranging deck chairs. Some of my editors repeat it too, as though it closed the question. What are the distinct claims this slogan could actually be making, from the trivial to the radical? For each reading, say whether Arrow's theorem establishes it, overstates it, or is simply irrelevant to it — and, crucially, which readings, if any, would license the dismissive use the slogan is being put to, given that reformers are choosing among systems that are all in some sense "unfair." Lay the readings out as a menu with verdicts I can take into our next editorial meeting.
