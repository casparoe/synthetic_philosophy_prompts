I sit on the methods committee of a regional network of diagnostic labs, and I need a decision memo to circulate before our next meeting. Our analysts pool findings from sources of very mixed reliability — a radiologist might file "about 80% confident this scan shows a tumour", an ageing sensor logs readings we only half-trust, and so on. Current policy says treat every report as certain and update by ordinary Bayesian conditioning, which everyone quietly knows is daft. A proposal on the table would switch us wholesale to Jeffrey conditionalisation: the analyst shifts probability across the relevant partition (tumour/no tumour, say) to match the reported confidence and renormalises, keeping everything else's proportions fixed.

Two worries have come up in discussion that the memo has to engage with properly, not wave away. First, Jeffrey updating only seems principled when the report's evidential impact is exhausted by that partition — i.e. the conditional odds between hypotheses within each cell are unchanged — and nobody thinks our analysts can reliably verify that in practice. Second, unlike ordinary conditioning, successive Jeffrey updates don't in general commute: applying the radiologist's report and then the sensor's reading can land you somewhere different from the reverse order, and our workflow imposes no canonical order on incoming reports. The main rival on the table is requiring reporters to supply likelihood ratios (or a reliability model of themselves) so analysts can just condition on the report-as-event, but our smaller labs say they can't produce those.

Recommend adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions, giving the strongest case for the losing side. End by saying explicitly what consideration or argument would change the recommendation. Maximum 600 words, British English.
