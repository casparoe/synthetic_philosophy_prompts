I'm trying to figure out what a formal theory of Occam's razor would actually have to accomplish before I'd trust it, and I want the spec before the arguments. Solomonoff induction gets sold as the success story here, but it drags in well-known baggage: the universal prior depends on the choice of reference machine so 'simplicity' is only pinned down up to an additive constant, the measure itself is uncomputable, and the convergence theorems are asymptotic and say nothing about performance on the finite data anyone actually has. Meanwhile the naive razors -- count entities, count parameters, count bits in your favorite notation -- all get wrecked by grue-style predicate tricks and reparameterization. So: give me the desiderata list for an adequate theory of parsimony. The cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse (e.g. simplicity of a hypothesis vs. simplicity of its description vs. simplicity of the world itself, or epistemic vs. pragmatic justifications of the razor), the mathematical results it has to stay consistent with, and for each requirement the case or consideration that motivates it. Also tell me which of the usual demands are really just prejudices of one camp, whether the full list looks jointly satisfiable, and which single desideratum knocks out the most contenders.
