Here is the exam question as it appeared:

Consider two rival cosmological theories, T1 and T2, which agree on all currently available empirical evidence and are regarded by the scientific community as equally well supported on ordinary grounds. According to T1, the universe contains exactly one intelligent civilization (ours). According to T2, the universe contains roughly one trillion intelligent civilizations, each too far from the others ever to interact. (a) State the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) precisely, including what each treats as the relevant evidence. (b) Show what each assumption implies about the relative credences a reasoner should assign to T1 and T2 before conducting further astronomical observation, and explain why one of these results has been called presumptuous. (c) State whether you think the so-called presumptuousness of that result is a decisive objection to the assumption that generates it, and give one argument either way. (d) Briefly indicate which of the two assumptions figures in the standard Doomsday argument, and what each assumption implies about the answer to the Sleeping Beauty problem.

Here is the student's answer:

(a) SSA says I should reason as if I were a randomly selected observer from my reference class — the class of observers relevantly like me. The evidence I condition on is essentially that an observer like me exists at my position. SIA says I should reason as if my own existence is evidence, and condition on it. Since a world with more observers gives my existence a higher prior likelihood, SIA amounts to conditioning on the proposition that observers exist at all, which favors theories that predict more observers, in proportion to how many they predict.

(b) Under SSA, the existence of one trillion civilizations instead of one makes no difference to whether I exist — my civilization exists under either theory — so SSA leaves T1 and T2 at their prior odds, roughly 1:1 given the equal empirical support. Under SIA, I should update by the ratio of observers: T2 predicts about a trillion times as many observers as T1, so it becomes about a trillion times more likely than T1, before anyone looks through a telescope. This is the result critics call presumptuous: a philosopher in an armchair can supposedly settle a question of empirical cosmology at overwhelming odds, without gathering any evidence about the actual universe.

(c) I think it is a decisive objection. Any principle of inductive reasoning that lets us assign credence 0.999999999999 to an empirical hypothesis on purely a priori grounds has clearly gone wrong somewhere. The whole point of cosmology is that the size of the universe is something we have to go out and measure.

(d) The Doomsday argument relies on SIA: it treats me as a random sample from all humans who will ever exist, and my low birth rank then tells against a long human future. In the Sleeping Beauty problem, SIA yields the halfer answer of 1/2 that the coin came up heads, because Beauty's existence is guaranteed on either outcome and so provides no new evidence.
