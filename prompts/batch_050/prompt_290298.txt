I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm preparing a piece on what biologists take themselves to be doing when they explain something. A researcher I interviewed sent me the following summary of the "new mechanist" account of explanation in biology, associated with Peter Machamer, Lindley Darden, and Carl Craver, and I'd like to know how much of it I can trust before I build a section of the article around it.

The summary runs as follows: According to the new mechanists, to explain a phenomenon in biology is to describe a mechanism that produces it, where a mechanism is a set of parts, organized the way a machine's parts are organized, whose interactions deterministically generate the phenomenon from start to finish. This account, they argue, vindicates the old reductionist ambition: explaining something like memory consolidation or protein synthesis means decomposing the system into its lowest-level physical components and deriving its behavior from the bottom up, which is why mechanism talk is gradually replacing talk of functions and systems in mature areas of biology. On their view, an explanation that leaves any component unspecified is not yet an explanation at all, only a promissory note, so the many incomplete models in contemporary biology are aspirations rather than accomplishments. The mechanist framework thereby also shows that the special sciences are unified in method even where their vocabularies differ, since every field from neuroscience to cell biology is ultimately in the business of machine description.

My correspondent is a working philosopher of science, not a crank, so I assume most of this is accurate. But a few claims struck me as stronger than what I remembered from my own reading, and I can't tell whether I'm misremembering or he is overstating. Could you go through the summary claim by claim and tell me: which parts Machamer, Darden, and Craver would themselves endorse; which are common simplifications that a careful presentation would hedge; and which, if any, misrepresent the account, including anywhere the summary slides between claims the mechanists keep apart or uses a term in its everyday rather than its intended sense. Then, once the summary is corrected, tell me whether the closing thought about methodological unification follows from the accurate parts, and whether anything in the piece licenses the further claim I was hoping to make in my article -- that mechanistic explanation has displaced law-based and functional explanation in biology rather than merely supplementing or reframing them. If some of the disputed points turn on interpretive questions about what the mechanists committed themselves to versus what friendly critics later added, please flag that rather than forcing a verdict.
