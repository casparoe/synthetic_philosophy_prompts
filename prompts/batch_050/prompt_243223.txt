ok so i'm on the board of a small national powerlifting federation (about 4,000 registered lifters, one paid staff member, the rest of us volunteers). we've been a drug-tested federation since forever, but we're getting squeezed, and at the AGM in six weeks there's a motion to drop testing entirely. i got voluntold to prepare the decision memo the board votes alongside. longtime SSC/ACX reader so yes, i've read Meditations on Moloch — please don't summarise it back at me, actually use it.

context: a rival federation in our region went untested three years ago and their membership has roughly tripled. their lifters put up bigger totals, their meets are more fun, sponsors followed. meanwhile testing eats ~40% of our budget, we catch maybe two people a year, and everyone knows the timing games beat it anyway. several of our best lifters have "retired from tested competition," which everyone understands. junior numbers are dropping — parents dont love what the sport visibly selects for at the top. and no, we can't coordinate with the rival fed, obviously.

options on the table: (1) keep testing as-is, (2) drop testing and go untested, (3) split into tested and open divisions under one federation. a fourth thing got floated — replacing meet-day testing with some longitudinal "biological passport" approach that's supposedly cheaper — but honestly nobody on the board knows if that's a real established method or a buzzword, so the memo should be upfront about that uncertainty rather than pretending.

the part i most want the memo to grapple with: our loudest board member, an ex-lifter, insists this is not a moloch situation at all. his line: nobody is trapped. lifters who want tested competition have it, lifters who want to push the envelope can go elsewhere, fans vote with their feet, and calling it a "race to the bottom" just assumes the bottom is bad — maybe consenting adults choosing supervised enhancement is simply a preference, like boxing fans preferring heavyweights. he says "multipolar trap" here is moralizing dressed up as game theory. i think he's mostly wrong but i can't fully articulate why, and he will be reading this memo, so it has to take his argument at full strength — including figuring out at what level (individual lifters? federations? the sport as a whole?) a trap would actually have to operate for his objection to fail, and whether it does operate there.

so: draft the memo, ~900–1200 words. it should (a) give a clear bottom-line recommendation up top — your call which option, but defend it; (b) settle, with actual argument, whether the trap framing fits and where; (c) deal with juniors, athlete health, and our insurance/liability exposure, which my draft kept hand-waving; and (d) state the strongest case against your own recommendation as well as its best supporter would state it — not a strawman i can knock down at the meeting. tone can be plain and a bit informal, but it has to be presentable to a board.
