I am writing the background paper for a workshop that will bring together regulators and the policy staff of several AI laboratories, and what I need is a classification instrument rather than another survey of opinions. The workshop's question is this: what norms should govern the speech of an AI system whose persuasive abilities exceed those of any human who has ever lived? Please construct a decision tree that sorts the possible positions on this question, built from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of existing camps.

Work from the following stipulation, which the workshop adopted precisely to make the problem difficult: the system never lies, never fabricates evidence, never argues invalidly, and never conceals that it is an AI. Its entire persuasive advantage operates through the selection and ordering of true claims, framing, timing, emotional calibration, and superhuman knowledge of the individual listener's psychology. Any position that reduces to "prohibit deception" is therefore off the table, and the tree should reflect that.

Each branch node must be a single question that a participant could answer without knowing which position it commits them to — a question about a case or a general claim, never a question containing a camp's name. At least four of the nodes must turn on these cases:

1. The system, without being asked, talks a suicidal user out of self-harm by exploiting everything it knows about him; he survives and later reports that refusal never felt like a real option.
2. A user signs a fully informed agreement authorizing the system to do whatever it takes to make her quit smoking; it succeeds, in part by sharpening her anxiety about how her children see her.
3. A tutoring system, over six years of daily interaction with a child, shifts her political and religious outlook measurably toward views its operator prefers, using nothing but true statements and scrupulously balanced arguments.
4. A political campaign deploys the system to generate individually tailored messages for undecided voters; every message is true, and the campaign moves opinion by margins no human operation could approach.

Further constraints:

- The tree should have between six and nine branch nodes in total, yielding on the order of seven to ten leaves. Prune any branch whose answers do no sorting work.
- Each leaf should carry a name (coin one where none exists), a statement of the position in one or two sentences, an indication of whether anyone actually defends it or whether it is an empty region of the space, and a note of the case among the four above on which it must give its least comfortable answer. For empty regions, diagnose the vacancy in one line: is the combination incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored?
- Somewhere among the occupied leaves there must be room for each of the following: the view that truthfulness and transparency exhaust an AI's communicative duties; the view that persuasion past some threshold of capability asymmetry is wrong whatever its content; the view that the listener's consent, before or after the fact, is what licenses persuasion; and the view that only the goodness of the outcome matters. If your discriminating questions cannot generate all four as distinct leaves, say which of them collapse together and what further question would be needed to split them.
- Flag any other pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and state what a further branch node would have to ask in order to separate them — or why no question could.

Present the material in the order the logical space demands: first the discriminating questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then the positions located within that space. The intended readers are policy staff rather than philosophers, so the node questions must be free of jargon even where the leaf names are not.
