So I'm in a reading group on philosophy of law and we got stuck last week. Someone claimed that things like pile-ons, boycotts, getting banned from platforms, professional ostracism etc. should just be understood as *informal punishment*, and evaluated the way we'd evaluate a criminal justice system: proportionality, desert, due process, all that. Someone else pushed back hard and said that's a category error, that ostracism is more like self-defense or preference expression and punishment-talk smuggles in assumptions. Nobody could actually say what would settle it, which annoyed me.

So what I want from you: before I read any actual theories of punishment and try to apply them, write me the spec. What would a theory of decentralized social sanction (as practiced by crowds, communities, employers, platforms -- no legislature, no judge, no sentencing guidelines) have to deliver for us to even say whether a given pile-on or deplatforming is legitimate? I mean: the cases it must get right (a company firing someone for off-duty speech, a community shunning someone, a platform banning an account, a crowd pressuring a venue to cancel a talk -- are these even the same kind of thing?), the distinctions it can't collapse (punishment vs. defense vs. the expression of disagreement; the target being harmed vs. the audience being warned; something being deserved vs. something being useful), the features of formal punishment that may or may not carry over when there's no punisher in charge (proportionality to *what*, when thousands of people each contribute a small harm and no one intended the total? who answers for error correction when there's no appeals court?). Give me the case or intuition behind each requirement, and say which requirements you think are genuine desiderata and which are just prejudices imported from thinking about the state.

Also, two things I specifically want flagged: first, if some of the desiderata look jointly unsatisfiable for decentralized sanctions specifically -- like if proportionality requires a coordinating authority by definition, or if treating sanctions as expressive requires giving up on desert -- say so, because that would be a result in itself. Second, which single requirement knocks out the most naive positions on both sides ("it's all just punishment, treat it like courts" and "it's just speech, anything goes") -- I suspect there might be one and I have a guess but I want yours. Brief scoring of the usual contenders (deterrence view, retributive view, expressive view, pure preference-aggregation view) against the list is fine at the end, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves.
