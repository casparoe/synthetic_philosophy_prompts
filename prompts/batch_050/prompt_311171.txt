Someone brought up Dennett's intentional stance -- roughly, the idea that you're licensed to treat a system as having beliefs and goals whenever doing so actually helps you predict its behavior, as opposed to the "physical stance" (predicting from its parts and physics) or the "design stance" (predicting from what it was built to do).

Design me a session activity, not a lecture. I want a game or structured exercise where the group *discovers for themselves* when treating something as a believer buys you real predictive power and when it seduces you into hallucinating a mind. Concrete constraints:

- At least one round with a system that talks or behaves like an agent but is actually dumb or random underneath, so at least one team gets burned by over-attributing beliefs *before anyone names the failure*
