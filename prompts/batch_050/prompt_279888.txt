Write me a parody of a corporate compliance manual, maybe 600-700 words, for a company that has adopted Nyāya epistemology as its official epistemic policy: every assertion made in a meeting, memo, or budget proposal must be certified via one of the four pramāṇas before anyone can act on it. Format it as numbered policy sections with the occasional footnote or incident report, totally deadpan.

The jokes have to come from actually getting pramāṇa theory right, not generic "ancient wisdom" gags. Things to work in: perception claims need documentation of proper sense-organ/object contact; inferential claims must be submitted as the full five-membered syllogism (proposition, reason, example, application, conclusion) and the compliance office rejects any submission without an established vyāpti, so the smoke/fire case sails through but shakier concomitances get sent back; the upamāna desk handles comparison claims and there's an ongoing problem with employees certifying gavaya-related assertions who have never seen a cow; testimony claims require sign-off from a certified āpta, with an appeals process everytime someone's reliability is contested. Also include at least one incident report about the Cārvāka in accounting who keeps rejecting everyone's inferences because he only accepts perception and therefore won't approve next quarter's budget, and a stern footnote warning employees not to smuggle in arthāpatti or anupalabdhi, since those are NOT on the approved list -- that's Mīmāṃsā territory.

Someone who knows the Nyāya material should feel the satire is fair (the five-membered syllogism really is delightfully redundant, testimony really does have a suspiciously powerful status), and someone who doesn't should still read it as a funny story about bureaucratic overreach. Write in American English. End with a final line beginning "Summary:" giving one sentence on what the piece is satirizing.
