For an undergrad phil language/metaphysics seminar, write a decision tree that sorts the classic positions on future contingents, starting from Aristotle's sea battle: it's true now that either there will be a sea battle tomorrow or there won't, and we're asking what that does and doesn't settle. The root should be the bivalence question (must "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" have a truth value *today*?), and the leaves should land on: fatalist/necessitarian, Peircean-style open futurist (denies bivalence, later tensed truths), Ockhamist (bivalence holds, truth depends on how the future actually goes), supervaluationist (true iff true on all admissible futures), and relativist/"unsettled truth at the moment of utterance" views.

Phrase every branch as a question a student could answer without knowing which view it leads to (e.g., a case where the prediction comes out wrong vs. a case where the world could still go either way), and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really pry apart -- I suspect supervaluationism and some relativist views will give you trouble, so say so if they do. Also include at least one empty combination of answers and briefly diagnose whether it's empty because it's incoherent or just unpopular.

Format it as: **Tree** (the questions and branches, ~half the response), **Leaves** (one short paragraph per position saying its most awkward commitment, like the Ockhamist owing us a story about what makes future truths true *now*), **Where the tree fails** (~a short paragraph on which leaves it can't separate and why). Keep the whole thing under 900 words or so.
