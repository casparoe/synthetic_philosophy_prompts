I keep hearing transformative AI compared to nuclear weapons, and I want to actually pressure-test the analogy instead of just nodding along. So: take the theory that states will race for any technology promising a decisive military advantage, but cooperate to limit it once the destructive potential becomes common knowledge and verification is feasible -- call it the race-then-regulate model. Run it against the real history: Manhattan Project secrecy, the failure of the Baruch Plan, the arms race, then the partial test ban and the nonproliferation regime. Where does the model map cleanly onto what happened, and where does it strain or go silent? Then turn it around on the AI analogy. Nukes had a chokepoint you could count (fissile material), they were useless unless actually deployed, and their destructiveness was horrifyingly demonstrable in a way everyone could see. Which of those load-bearing features carry over to AI and which dont, and does the nuclear episode, properly analyzed, end up supporting the analogy or quietly gutting it?
