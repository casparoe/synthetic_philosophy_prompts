That struck me as far too quick, but I couldn't articulate which of the many things the slogan might mean he was actually relying on. For each reading, say whether it's platitudinous, substantive and defensible, or actually false, and test it against cases the original debate would have cared about (a stone horse, a white mare, riding a white horse as a case of riding a horse) as well as modern ones (toy, fake, former, robotic). Finally, tell me which reading my TA's use of the slogan required, and whether that reading is one a defender of the slogan -- or its Mohist critics -- would have accepted.
