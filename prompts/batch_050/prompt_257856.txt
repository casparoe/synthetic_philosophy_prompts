I'm preparing a short position paper for a political theory workshop and would like a consistency audit of my commitments before I circulate it. The topic is the category of "Western values." Here is what I currently hold:

(1) The phrase "Western values" is not merely a rhetorical weapon. It picks out a real, historically continuous tradition of thought and institutional practice — natural and individual rights, the rule of law, freedom of conscience and inquiry, constitutional constraint on power — that can be traced through European and settler-colonial history and distinguished from other traditions. Someone who denies the category has any referent at all is overstating.

(2) The values themselves are universal in scope. If free inquiry or legal equality is good for anyone, it is good for everyone, and nothing about these values essentially ties them to European geography, ancestry, or culture. Their claim on us does not depend on whose heritage they are.

(3) The standard historical narrative attached to the phrase is substantially false. The tradition absorbed a great deal from non-European sources, several of its signature values were articulated earlier or more consistently elsewhere, and its supposed bearers practiced slavery, colonialism, and religious persecution alongside it for centuries. Consequently, when the phrase is invoked in actual political argument, it mostly functions to mark an in-group and an out-group rather than to describe anything.

(4) Despite (3), I use the phrase myself in political argument — for instance, urging that free expression and secular law are Western values worth defending against illiberal movements — and I take this usage to be legitimate rather than hypocritical.

(5) I accept that a political ideal stands or falls by whether it can be justified to everyone subject to it, not by its pedigree. Origin is irrelevant to validity.

My question: does this set hang together? I would like you to go through the commitments pairwise where relevant and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, ordered by severity. For each genuine tension, state exactly what I would have to give up or qualify, and what that concession would cost the overall position. Then tell me which single revision would restore the most coherence at the lowest cost. I am genuinely unsure where the weak point is, so please don't assume any particular commitment is the one that has to go. Finally, indicate how confident you are in each of your main claims, and single out the point in your analysis where you are least sure of yourself.
