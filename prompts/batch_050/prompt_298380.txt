I write the object labels and wall text for a mid-size history museum, and I keep getting into the same fight with my editors: when are we allowed to say a legendary story is "based on real events," when do we have to hedge with "according to tradition," and when do we just have to bite the bullet and call it a legend? Right now the answer is vibes plus whoever argues loudest in the meeting, and the board has asked for a house rule.

So build me an actual decision procedure — a checklist or a flowchart in words — for sorting a tradition into something like: historical core / distorted memory of something real / pure legend. Base it on the criteria working historians actually lean on: independent attestation, whether archeology converges on the story or just on the setting, the size of the gap between the alleged events and the first written account, whether the specific details are the kind oral tradition preserves well or the kind it invents, and so on.

Hard requirements:

- Runnable in about an hour of library research per story, by someone with a history BA and no specialst training. If a step needs an expert in Near Eastern languages or whatever, flag it and tell me what a non-expert does instead.
- It has to handle the awkward cases explicitly, not dodge them: what to do when absence of evidence is exactly what you'd expect even if the events happened (nomads in a desert don't leave much); what to do when there's exactly one source and it's written centuries after the fact; and what to do when archeology confirms the backdrop but not the plot — a ruined city where Troy was supposed to be doesn't automatically make the Iliad a war diary.
- Mark the steps where the procedure bottoms out in raw judgment and can't be mechanized further. I'd rather have three honestly-labeled judgment calls than a fake algorithm.
- Worked applications: run the whole procedure on the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus. I suspect at least one of these comes out with a label I won't enjoy printing — tell me which one and why the procedure forced it.
- End with a short list of questions the procedure structurally can't settle, so I know what to keep off the labels entirely.
