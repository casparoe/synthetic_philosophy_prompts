So I've been chewing on this for months, no philosophy background or anything, just can't get my head straight about wether protecting nature is actually good for the animals living in it. Here's my gut on some real-ish cases:

- Guy I know drained the swampy corner of his property to put in a garage. Frogs, tadpoles, mosquitos, all gone. Honestly? Fine by me, maybe even a net plus.
- A wildlife shelter near me raised money to rescue rabbits and squirrels after a brushfire and patch them up and release them. I donated and I'd do it again.
- Our county does a deer cull every fall, sharpshooters, quick headshots, because otherwise they starve over winter and get hit by cars. Seems right, maybe necessary.
- But then someone proposed reintroducing wolves to fix the deer problem instead, and that feels way worse even though it's the same number of deer dying? They get chased down and torn apart. Except everyone says wolves "restore the ecosystem" and that feels like it should count for something, though I couldn't tell you what exactly.
- A rich guy bought a massive chunk of rainforest to keep it wild forever, explicitly "for the animals." And this is the one I keep going back and forth on: if most wild animals live short brutal lives full of parasites, hunger, and getting eaten, is creating MORE wilderness actually doing anyone a favor?

Is there one principle that captures all of these, or a couple candidate principles, and what does each one say about cases I didn't list, like feeding birds in winter or dropping vaccine baits for wild animals? And be straight with me: if my verdicts can't all be saved, which one has to go, and why that one and not one of the others?
