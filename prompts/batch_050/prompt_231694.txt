I am developing a theory of when an artefact has politics, adequate for deciding when engineering design should face political scrutiny. Write the desiderata it must satisfy, using three test cases: a speed bump that slows ordinary drivers but also emergency vehicles; a recommendation system whose engagement objective unintentionally amplifies extremist material; and an encryption tool that protects dissidents and criminals alike. The theory must cover affordance asymmetry, where a design makes some conduct materially easier or harder, and deployment drift, where political effects emerge only within a population or institution and change with scale. Distinguish material constraint, designers’ intentions, user appropriation, background injustice, causal influence, and normative blame. State which requirements are indispensable, which are commitments of particular camps, and whether any attractive pair is jointly unsatisfiable; then give a definite verdict on the best overall account rather than merely listing options. Answer in exactly seven numbered items, each no more than two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
