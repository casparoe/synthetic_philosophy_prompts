I teach a 90-minute undergraduate philosophy of mind seminar for 16 students who have informal logic but no metamathematics. Design a runnable classroom activity that makes them feel the force of the Gödelian analogy popularized in cognitive-science discussions of Hofstadter: a sufficiently expressive formal system can encode statements about its own derivability, and the strange loop that results is proposed as a model for how a brain might contain a self-model without magic. The activity should not prove Gödel’s theorem or name it until the end; students should first build, with paper tokens, a tiny formal game in which one sentence comes to mean something like this very sentence is not producible in the game, and then discover that whether it is producible depends on a level-confusion they can actually trip over. The point is to let them experience three things before vocabulary arrives: coding, diagonal-style self-reference, and the uneasy inference from a self-describing mechanism to a self-aware subject.

Give me something I can run next week: concrete materials; setup; roles; exact rules; a low-stakes scoring or elimination scheme using points, tokens, or chocolate rather than money; timing for each phase; facilitator beats that do not lecture up front; and a debrief that maps specific moments in play onto coding, provability, self-reference, meta-level versus object-level, and the further contested step to minded self-awareness. Include one variant for students who finish fast and one simplification if the room gets lost. Be explicit about what the game cannot reproduce and what I must supply by framing: for example, a paper formalism has syntax but no experience, no embodiment, no motivations, and no one home to be surprised.

End with a table whose columns are Moment in activity, Formal or cognitive feature it is meant to instantiate, What students should feel there, Likely misreading, and Facilitator correction. After the table, include a JSON object with fields named activity_title, setting_fit, learning_targets, materials, setup, rules, stakes, timeline, debrief_map, fast_variant, rescue_variant, predictable_failures, missing_feature, and confidence_notes; in confidence_notes, mark each central claim as secure, plausible, or contested. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. If you think the request rests on a confused premise, especially if it slides from formal self-reference to phenomenal consciousness too quickly, say so plainly in pushback within the JSON and tell me how you would redesign the activity rather than politely complying with a bad version.
