So I'm a novelist, and my next book is about an art forger -- a guy who can produce a "lost" Vermeer-style painting so good that no test, human or chemical, can tell it apart from the real thing. I've been reading around the philosophy of fakes and originals to give the book some intellectual spine, and I've hit the point where I need to triage. I have maybe three big set-piece scenes to spend on the philosophical material (through plot and dialogue, not lectures), so I can't do everything.

Here are the live questions I've pulled together, plus one I sort of invented myself:

1. Whether a perfect copy really loses any value at all, or whether our preference for originals is just snobbery and status-signaling dressed up as taste.
2. Whether the history of an object -- who made it, when, why -- is somehow *part* of the artwork, so that the copy is literally a different (lesser) work even though it looks identical.
3. Whether what's wrong with forgery is mainly the *deception* (it would be fine if sold honestly as a copy) or something about the object itself.
4. My own invention, which I haven't seen discussed anywhere: what happens when the *maker's inner history* is the thing copied. My forger isn't just mimicking style -- he's reconstructed the master's whole training, reading, and even daily routine, so he paints the way the master would have painted a new canvas. Call it a process-forgery rather than a product-forgery. Does the painting's pedigree change if the *causal process* is genuinely continuous with the original tradition, even though the master's hand never touched it?

Which two or three of these would you actually build the book around, and in what order? I want a defended ranking, not a survey -- tell me what criteria you're using (dramatic potential, philosophical depth, how much one question drags the others along with it, how fresh it is vs. how much it's been done to death in fiction already), where those criteria pull against each other, and what about a novel specifically -- as opposed to, say, a philosophy paper -- should change the weighting. And if my #4 turns out to collapse into one of the others on reflection, say so and tell me which one eats it.
