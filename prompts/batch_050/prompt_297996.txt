Longtime lurker, occasional commenter here — posting because an argument at my local ACX meetup last week broke my brain a little and I want to get unstuck before the next one. Someone brought up tranquilism, the axiology where the value of an experience is wholly a matter of the absence or presence of craving (tanhā in the Buddhist sense): the best states are ones of completely undisturbed, wanting-nothing contentment, and pleasure only counts for anything insofar as it isn't tangled up with felt wanting. At first this seemed like an elegant way to dodge the repugnant conclusion and a bunch of other population ethics weirdness, and I nodded along.

But then I realized what looks like a knock-down objection, and honestly it's ruining the view for me: if the good just IS the absence of craving, then dreamless sleep, general anesthesia, a coma — heck, a rock — all contain zero craving, so they should be maximaly good by tranquilist lights. Which seems to mean the tranquilist ideal is a world where everyone is permanently unconscious, and that any tranquilist should rationally prefer being knocked out cold to even the most serene waking moment. My friend (who runs a meditation circle, so take this with a grain of salt) insisted tranquilists obviously don't believe this and that meditative jhana-type states are the paradigm of the good life on this view, not unconsciousness. But I genuinely don't see how that helps — doesn't "value consists in the absence of craving" commit you to ranking non-experience above any experience, since non-experience has the least craving of all? What exactly am I missing about how the view is supposed to be structured? Or is my meetup opponent just wrong and tranquilism really does collapse into anesthesia-maximization unless you smuggle in some independent value for positive experience?

Secondary question, feel free to be brief on this one: is tranquilism just negative utilitarianism wearing Buddhist robes, or does switching the target from suffering to craving actually change what follows? I'm not looking for citations or a reading list, I can dig those up myself — just the conceptual straightening-out. And please answer in American English, the British spellings in the last thing I read on this kept throwing me off.
