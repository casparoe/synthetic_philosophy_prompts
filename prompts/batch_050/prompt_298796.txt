While revising a mystery novel, I asked a philosophically trained reader to assess two pairs of conditionals. Her verdicts were:

- A reliable but fallible witness has narrowed the murderer to the butler or the gardener. She accepts If the butler is innocent, the gardener is guilty, but rejects If the butler had been innocent, the gardener would have been guilty.
- A motor can run only while its safety switch is closed. She accepts both If the motor is running, the switch is closed and If the motor had been running, the switch would have been closed.

For a new scene, suppose rain is by far the likeliest cause of a wet pavement and always makes the sky grey, although a burst pipe can wet the pavement beneath a clear sky. Predict her verdicts on If the pavement was wet, the sky was grey and If the pavement had been wet, the sky would have been grey. Identify the best candidate rule behind her record, at least two rival rules that fit it equally well, what each predicts here, and one further pair of conditionals that would distinguish them. Please answer in British English.
