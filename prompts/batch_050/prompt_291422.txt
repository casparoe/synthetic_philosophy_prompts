I work in value theory, and for some years I have held — largely on the strength of spectrum arguments of the sort developed by Larry Temkin — that 'all things considered better than' is not transitive. The rough idea: take two outcomes A and Z, where A is clearly vastly better than Z (say, an ecstatic life versus a life of drudgery barely worth living). One can interpolate a long sequence of intermediate outcomes A, B, C, ..., Z, adjacent members of which differ only marginally in the relevant respects (a slightly lower welfare level spread over slightly more people, a slightly shorter duration of slightly higher quality). At each step, the later outcome strikes me as at least as good as the earlier, or at worst not worse. If I endorse every pairwise judgment while denying that Z is at least as good as A, consistency requires that one of the comparisons in the chain be a case of 'better than' whose transitivity fails, or that some adjacent pair harbors a sharp evaluative discontinuity despite the marginal descriptive difference. I find intransitivity the least costly option.
