So last year I finally took one of those giving pledges -- 10% of my income to whatever charity does the most good, after months of my friend Dan nagging me about it. It's been fine, actually kind of satisfying. But Dan has now cornered me with an argument that he says follows from the exact reasoning that got me to sign the pledge, and the conclusion is nuts, and I can't find the hole. Here's his argument, roughly as he gave it to me over beers.

(1) Other things equal, your money should go where it produces the most wellbeing per dollar. That's the whole reason I give to malaria charities instead of the local opera house, and I still think it's right.
(2) What matters is the amount of wellbeing produced, not whose wellbeing it is or what kind of being is having it. A happy creature is a happy creature, whether it's a person, an animal, or something weirder.
(3) It's at least possible, and maybe not that far off, that we could create beings that convert resources into wellbeing at rates no human can touch -- genetically tweaked animals that live in constant bliss, or digital minds running on cheap hardware that experience the equivalent of the best day of your life every few seconds. Per dollar, they'd out-convert any human by a factor of thousands.
(4) So the moment beings like that become available, every dollar of my pledge should go to them rather than to any human in need. Same logic as the opera house, just iterated.
(5) And since it doesn't matter whether a potential recipient already exists -- a dollar is a dollar -- once creating these beings is cheap enough, I should spend the money bringing more of them into existence rather than relieving the suffering of people who are already here.
(6) Conclusion: the morally best use of my pledge is, eventually, bankrolling warehouse-scale farms of ecstatic rats or blissed-out software, and humanity matters exactly until something happier can be manufactured.

Dan calls step (6) the utility monster endgame and says I have to swallow it or admit the pledge was sentimentality all along. I'm not willing to do either, but honestly the premises each look like things I already believe, and I said so at the time, which he is definately not letting me forget. If I have to pick, I'd most like to keep (1) and (2), since they're the whole basis of my giving, and if something has to die I'd rather it be one of the later moves -- but I genuinely want to know which step actually breaks and what giving it up costs me. Does (3) being merely possible let (4) through, or is the problem in the hop from (4) to (5), or somewhere earlier that I'm too close to see?
