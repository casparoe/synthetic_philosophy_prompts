So I'm putting together the position statement for a paper I've been shopping around on suffering-focused ethics, and before I send it off I want a genuinely hostile consistency audit, because my last referee report just said "the author cannot hold all of these at once" without saying which ones. Here's where I've landed. (1) I'm a lexical threshold negative utilitarian: there's a level of suffering intensity such that no amount of pleasure, however large or long-lasting, can compensate for even a single episode of it. (2) I think pleasure and pain are fully commensurable — they're magnitudes on one continuous hedonic scale, and for ordinary goods and bads straightforward tradeoffs are fine; a headache can be worth enduring for a good enough concert. (3) I think hedonic intensity varies continuously across its whole range — there's no natural joint between "very unpleasant" and "unbearable," just a smooth spectrum from an itch to agony. (4) I think rational action under uncertainty is expected-value reasoning all the way down, and I reject fanaticism: I won't restructure my whole life around a one-in-a-trillion chance of someone being tortured. (5) I think duration aggregates linearly — an hour of a given pain is roughly sixty times as bad as a minute of it.

A colleague in my department keeps insisting that (1) and (5) are obviously at war, on the grounds that if duration aggregates, a mild enough pain stretched long enough must eventually reach threshold badness, which contradicts the lexicality. I'm fairly sure that one's a pseudo-problem, since the threshold is about intensity rather than accumulated duration, but I include it because I'd like you to confirm or deny that it dissolves rather than just assume I'm right. What I actually want is the full audit: which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and for each real tension, how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be — i.e., which commitment I'd have to drop or weaken and what that costs the overall view. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than flagging everything at equal volume, and if you think one single revision would clean up the most damage, say which one and why. I have no attachment to any particular fix, so don't pull punches about which commitment is the weakest link.

One constraint: I'm not asking whether lexical threshold NU is true, just whether my version of the package is internally coherent, so please don't litigate the truth of the premises. And please write the answer in American English, since the journal I'm targeting is US-based and I'd like the phrasing to be usable.
