I am a retired physicist, trained in statistical mechanics and variational methods, who has lately turned to philosophy of mind, and I keep running into the free energy principle and predictive processing. The presentations I find, Friston's especially, are dense with idiosyncratic vocabulary. Here is what I would like. Restate the core claims of the free energy principle as precisely as possible in the vocabulary I already command: variational inference, evidence lower bounds, recognition densities, partition functions, and the distinction between a bound and the quantity bounded. Then give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly into standard variational Bayes, which require distortion (I suspect the treatment of action, i.e., active inference, goes here, as does the move from a bound on surprise to a claim that systems minimize surprise itself), and which have no counterpart in the standard framework at all -- the Markov blanket as an ontological rather than merely statistical boundary seems a candidate, but tell me if I am wrong. Crucially, I do not want a neutral survey of opinions. Commit to a verdict: is the free energy principle a genuinely new principle with empirical content beyond variational inference, a useful repackaging, or a relabeling that mistakes a mathematical identity for a law of nature? Defend whichever position you take, and where your restatement depends on a contested reading of the literature, flag that explicitly rather than papering over it.
