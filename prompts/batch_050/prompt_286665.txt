I serve on the five-person hiring committee of my academic department, and after a fractious search last year I've been asked to draft standing rules for how we reach collective decisions. Before circulating them, I want to know whether my guiding principles are jointly tenable:

(1) Our official positions must be logically consistent — the committee may not, for instance, hold that a candidate meets all our stated criteria, hold that we hire whoever meets the criteria, and also decline to hire that candidate.
(2) Every proposition before us — factual premises about the candidate as well as the final recommendation — is settled by simple majority vote, taken one proposition at a time.
(3) Our final recommendation must be one that a majority of members actually endorse; the committee should never officially recommend an outcome most of us oppose.
(4) The procedure must treat members symmetrically: no one's vote counts extra and no one holds a privileged role in determining outcomes.
(5) Discussion beforehand is welcome but optional; the voting rule by itself should be enough to produce acceptable results.

Please audit this set: tell me which commitments genuinely conflict and which only appear to, ordered by severity. For each real conflict, state the cheapest fix and what it costs, and then identify the single revision that resolves the most. If any commitment is itself confused, or I've misdescribed what's actually at stake, say so plainly rather than working around it. Keep the whole reply under 600 words.
