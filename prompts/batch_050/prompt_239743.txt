Epicurus, in the Letter to Menoeceus (trans. Robert Drew Hicks), writes: "The wise man does not deprecate life nor does he fear the cessation of life. The thought of life is no offence to him, nor is the cessation of life regarded as an evil. And even as men choose of food not merely and simply the larger portion, but the more pleasant, so the wise seek to enjoy the time which is most pleasant and not merely that which is longest."

Suppose Epicurus could survey the modern longevity movement: cryonic preservation, serious research into slowing or reversing aging, and a subculture that treats death as an engineering problem to be solved rather than a fate to be accepted. What would he make of it? Reconstruct his verdict from the passage above and the surrounding argument of the letter, and do the reconstruction carefully rather than gesturing at the familiar slogan that death is nothing to us. In particular, work through the tensions. The food analogy subordinates quantity to quality, but it does not say the larger portion is worthless; does it leave room for an Epicurean to prefer, all else equal, more pleasant years over fewer — and if so, is death at forty worse than death at eighty after all? Elsewhere in the letter he says right understanding makes mortality enjoyable not by granting unlimited time but by removing the yearning for immortality; is a wish for thirty or sixty more healthy years that yearning, or is it a natural desire on par with wanting another pleasant meal? And since he holds that fear of death is a groundless pain in expectation, would he see cryonics as prudent insurance or as the symptom of exactly the disease his philosophy exists to cure?

If you think this counterfactual is ill-posed — that his arguments simply cannot bear on technologies he never conceived, or that the exercise distorts them — say so plainly and explain why, rather than manufacturing a confident answer. Give me your bottom line in the first sentence (he would endorse it, condemn it, or regard it with indifference), then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
