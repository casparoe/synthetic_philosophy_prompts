I'm TAing an intro phil of mind course this semester and I'm drowning in grading. Below is the background blurb the class got in lecture, the exam question I set, and one student's answer. Grade the answer for me.

Background as given to the class: In Metamagical Themas (1985), Douglas Hofstadter describes the careenium, a giant frictionless pool table covered with zillions of tiny fast marbles called simmballs and a smaller number of big slow balls called symbols. The simmballs are pure physics, individually meaningless, bouncing around deterministically, and their collective impacts shove the big symbols this way and that. But the setup can be tuned so that the slow symbol-level dance, read at the right level of description, implements something like cognition, say a conversation. Hofstadter's question: who shoves whom around inside the cranium?

Exam question (10 points): In the careenium, does anything genuinely push the simmballs around besides other simmballs? Explain Hofstadter's answer, and say what the thought experiment is meant to show about whether our thoughts cause anything.

Student's answer:

The careenium shows how a mind can be built out of mindless parts. At the bottom are the simmballs, and each one carries a tiny fragment of meaning, the way a single neuron carries a tiny bit of a thought; when millions of them converge on a symbol, their combined meanings add up to an idea, and the symbol moves accordingly. So at the lowest level of description, everything that happens is just simmballs knocking into things per deterministic physics: the simmballs do all the literal pushing, and no symbol ever deflects a simmball from the path its physics dictates. Hofstadter's conclusion from this is that symbols are epiphenomenal. Like shadows cast by the real causal actors, they are inert byproducts, and this is his model of the human situation: the sense that my thoughts cause my actions is a persistent illusion, since the real pushing happens at the neuronal level. This is also his answer to Searle's Chinese Room: understanding lives in the overall symbol-level pattern, not in any inner homunculus, so the systems reply survives. Admittedly the symbol-level story is still the one we have to use in practice, since tracking every simmball is hopeless and would miss the conversation entirely, just as a computer is best explained at the level of its program rather than its electrons. So the final picture is two descriptions of one process, with all the real causation at the bottom.
