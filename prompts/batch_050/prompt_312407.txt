Exam question: In Grice's framework, what distinguishes what is said from what is conversationally implicated? Explain how the Cooperative Principle and its maxims are supposed to generate conversational implicatures, illustrating with one particularized and one generalized conversational implicature. Finally, explain cancellability as a diagnostic and note any limitations of it that Grice himself acknowledged.

Student's answer: For Grice, what is said is the truth-conditional content of the utterance, fixed by the conventional meanings of the words together with reference assignment and disambiguation. A conversational implicature, by contrast, is a proposition the hearer works out on the assumption that the speaker is observing the Cooperative Principle and the maxims of Quantity, Quality, Relation, and Manner, or is flouting one of them in a way the hearer is meant to notice.

For a particularized implicature: A asks where C lives, and B replies "Somewhere in the south of France." B's reply is less informative than required, and A concludes that B does not know exactly where C lives. This depends on the specific context, so it is particularized. For a generalized implicature: "Some of the students passed" implicates that not all did, because a cooperative speaker who knew all had passed would have said so. Since this inference attaches to the word "some" itself rather than to any special features of the context, it is a particularized implicature of the kind Grice thought requires no contextual supplementation.

Irony is Grice's standard case of exploiting the maxim of Relation: when someone says "What a fine friend" of a person who betrayed them, the remark is not relevant to the conversational purpose, so the hearer recovers the opposite of the literal meaning. Cancellability distinguishes implicatures from entailments: "Some of the students passed; indeed, all of them did" is consistent, showing the not-all inference is cancellable and therefore not part of what is said. Grice acknowledged that this test is not decisive in every case, since some apparent implicatures resist cancellation for independent reasons.
