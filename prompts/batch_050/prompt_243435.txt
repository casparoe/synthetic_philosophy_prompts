I’m a PhD student in linguistics helping design the final for an upper-level philosophy of language course, and I need an exam question on norms of assertion that goes beyond “state the knowledge norm and one objection.”

Please write:

1. One essay question that students could answer in about 75 minutes.
2. A model answer of roughly 650–800 words.
3. A detailed marking sceme out of 30 points.

The question should be built around a short original scenario involving several speakers whose assertions differ along these dimensions: one knows what they assert but says something conversationally misleading; one has a justified true belief that is Gettierized; one has an extremely well-justified false belief; and one asserts something true while violating a practical or institutional rule about confidentiality. The scenario should force students to distinguish at least four things that are often collapsed together: whether the assertion is true, whether the speaker is justified, whether the speaker knows, and whether the speaker is blameworthy.

A strong answer should compare knowledge, truth, and justified-belief norms, but it should also address whether the relevant norm is supposed to be a constitutive rule of assertion, a standard for permissible assertion, or merely a basis for criticism. I especially want students to explain why an assertion can violate a norm without the speaker being blameworthy, and why knowledge alone may not settle whether an utterance is conversationally appropriate. Please have the exam question ask whether that last point shows that the knowledge norm is incomplete or whether it just shows that assertion is governed by more than one norm.

For the marking scheme, identify the specific moves an excellent answer must make, assign points to each, and briefly note common mistakes that should lose marks—for example, treating Gettierized belief as knowledge because it is justified and true, inferring blameworthiness directly from norm violation, or using Gricean complaints as a straightforward refutation of the knowledge norm. Also include a short note distinguishing an A-range answer from a B-range answer. No bibliography or citations needed.
