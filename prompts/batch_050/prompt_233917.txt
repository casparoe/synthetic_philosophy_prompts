I'm a high school junior writing a position paper for my civics class on drug policy, and before I can defend a view I want to figure out what I actually believe. So here are my gut verdicts on a set of cases, and I'd like you to tell me what principle, if any, unifies them -- or tell me honestly if no principle can.

Some background as I understand it: Portugal decriminalized possession of small amounts of all drugs around 2001, treating it as an administrative matter rather than a crime and steering users toward treatment instead of jail. Meanwhile several U.S. states have fully legalized recreational cannabis for adults. Those two models -- decriminalization and legalization -- are the poles I'm thinking between.

My verdicts:

1. An adult using cannabis at home, harming no one: this should not be a crime, full stop.
2. An adult caught with a small amount of heroin clearly meant for personal use: also should not be a crime or lead to jail; a treatment referral, like Portugal's approach, seems right.
3. Someone selling heroin to strangers for profit: this is a serious crime and I'm fine with punishing it harshly.
4. A college student giving a friend one of her own prescription stimulants during exams: this feels wrong, but nowhere near as wrong as case 3, even though both are technically distribution of a controlled substance.
5. Alcohol and tobacco being legal for adults while cannabis isn't: this strikes me as straightforwardly inconsistent, since alcohol seems at least as harmful as cannabis by most measures I'm aware of.
6. Making overdose-reversal medication freely available to anyone: obviously good, no dilemma at all.
7. Supervised consumption sites, where people use their own illegal drugs under medical supervision: I'm comfortable with these, even though they in a sense facilitate illegal use.
8. Prosecuting a pregnant woman for drug use on grounds of harm to the fetus: not fine, even though here there clearly is a third party being harmed -- which I realize cuts against how I'd usually argue.
9. Licensed shops selling cannabis to adults: fine. Licensed shops selling heroin to adults: not fine, and I can't fully articulate why the difference.
10. If someone sells drugs that cause a fatal overdose, charging them with manslaughter: I genuinely can't settle this one. Some days it seems like holding a seller responsible for a foreseeable death; other days it seems like punishing one person for another adult's free choice.

The tensions I can already see: cases 2 and 3 pull apart on the question of whether the transaction itself is wrong or only one side of it; case 5's consistency argument, pushed hard enough, might force me to say alcohol should be banned, which I don't believe; and case 8 seems to violate whatever harm-to-others principle I'd use elsewhere.

What I'd like from you: state the most plausible principle (or two competing candidate principles, if no single one fits) that captures as many of these verdicts as possible, precisely enough that I could apply it myself. For each candidate, show which of my verdicts motivate which part of it, identify which verdicts it cannot accommodate and must ask me to abandon, and apply it to two cases I haven't listed -- say, a brand-new synthetic drug that no legislature has classified yet, and an adult buying cannabis from an unlicensed street seller rather than a legal shop. Then tell me what each candidate says about case 10, the one I'm stuck on.

One request about how to answer: please don't force a tidy theory onto my intuitions just to be helpful. If two of my verdicts are genuinely irreconcilable, or if one of them rests on a confused premise or a factual error, say so plainly and tell me which one I'd have to give up -- I'd rather have an honest account of an inconsistent set of views than an elegant principle that papers over the cracks.
