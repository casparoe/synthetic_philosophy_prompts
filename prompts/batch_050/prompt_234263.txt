Someone brought up Davidson's view that a metaphor means only what its words literally mean and everything else is just effects it causes in you, and someone else countered with Lakoff-style conceptual metaphor theory, where mappings like argument-is-war supposedly structure how we think whether we notice or not. I have zero philosophy training so half of this went over my head, but the argument is still going and I want ammunition.

Write me a dialogue between two equally sharp characters -- one a convinced Davidsonian who denies metaphorical meaning exists, one a defender of conceptual metaphor theory -- hashing it out using software metaphors as their shared test cases: technical debt, code smells, the cloud, bugs (including the famous moth), spaghetti code, and my manager's favorite, "we're building a cathedral here, not a shed." I want real back-and-forth where each one responds to what the other actually just said instead of re-reading their opening statement. And end with each of them naming the example or evidence that would actually make them switch sides, because that's the part I want to quote back into the slack thread.
