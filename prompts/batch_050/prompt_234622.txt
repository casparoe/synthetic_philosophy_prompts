I spent fourty years as a computational chemist, so this is really bugging me. We ran simulations of molecules all the time, and nobody ever thought a simulated glass of water was wet, or that a simulated fire could warm the room. Fine. But now I keep reading that if you simulated a human brain in enough detail, the simulation would actually be conscious and feel things, maybe even deserve moral consideration. A former colleague of mine is pouring her retirement savings into a "mind uploading" startup based on exactly this bet. Is there a principled difference between simulating water and simulating a brain, or is she buying a very expensive screensaver? And suppose the hardware does matter — if we swapped out her neurons for little silicon chips one at a time, each doing the same job as the neuron it replaced, would she still be herself at the end of it, or would we have quietly replaced her with something else along the way? I'd like to know what to tell her.
