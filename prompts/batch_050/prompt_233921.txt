So I've been hanging around LessWrong and the SSC/ACX comment sections for like a decade now, and every six months someone rediscovers Popper's paradox of tolerance and we have the exact same fight again. Half the thread says Popper showed a tolerant society can justly suppress intolerant movements, the other half says he's being misread and tolerance is really more like a peace treaty that only binds the people who keep it, and then someone always chimes in that there was never a paradox at all and its a category error. It goes nowhere every time because everyone is arguing from labels.

I want to settle this in the most rat way possible: a sorting instrument. Write me a "where do you actually stand on the paradox of tolerance" thing for my blog — either a decision tree or a 7-9 question quiz with a scoring key, whichever you think sorts cleaner.

Constraints:

- Sort by concrete cases, not labels. Every question should be a scenario a reader can react to without knowing any position names. Stuff like: a movement that peacefully campaigns to abolish elections is polling at 30% — do you ban it? A speaker argues some groups should have fewer legal rights but carefully never calls for violence. The intolerant party wins a fair election — now what? A private platform deciding whether to host anti-democratic advocacy. Invent your own, these are just the vibes.
- Leaves I want to see: (1) militant Popperianism — a tolerant society should be willing to suppress intolerant movements by force before they can organize, even while they're only talking; (2) the peace-treaty / contract view — tolerance is a reciprocal agreement and people who renounce it forfeit its protection, so suppression becomes legitimate only once they've actually broken the contract, whatever breaking it turns out to mean; (3) Mill-style near-absolutism — refute bad speech with better speech, only direct incitement to imminent violence is out; (4) the speech/action split — intolerant advocacy is protected but intolerant conduct isn't, and the paradox dissolves once you stop treating speech as a kind of action; (5) the deflationary view — there was never a paradox, tolerance is just a norm with built-in limits and only a confused framing makes it look self-undermining.
- Flag any pair of leaves your instrument can't reliably seperate. I suspect (1) and (2) collapse into each other on most ordinary cases and only come apart somewhere weird, like whether someone born into an intolerant group counts as having broken a contract they never signed.
- If your logical space has a combination of answers that produces a coherent-sounding position none of my five leaves holds, note it and tell me whether it looks incoherent, merely unattractive, or actually unexplored.
- If you go the quiz route, the scoring key should map answer patterns onto leaves, and at least one question should exist purely to split two positions that otherwise give identical answers everywhere.

Keep it readable for smart non-philosophers, no citations or reading list — I've got my Popper and Mill bookmarked already, I just want the instrument.
