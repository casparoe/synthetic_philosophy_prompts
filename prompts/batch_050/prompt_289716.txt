before i read any more proposed solutions to the st. petersburg paradox, i want the spec first. write me the desiderata list: what does a resolution actually have to deliver to count as solving the paradox rather than patching it?

the stuff i already suspect it has to handle, so either build these in or push back on them:
- the original game, obviously, where naive expected value is infinite but almost nobody would pay more than the price of a nice dinner to play
- the super st. petersburg move: for any unbounded utility function you can rescale the payoffs so that expected utility diverges again, so "marginal utility diminishes" doesnt obviously end the matter
- the opposite embarrassment: if you cap utility to dodge that, the agent will now turn down some arbitrarily favorable bets (astronomical upside, small chance of a fixed loss), which also looks crazy
- it should explain the actual behavior -- why sensible people price the game around $20 -- without declaring them irrational, and without making ordinary insurance purchases or ordinary gambling come out irrational either
- it should take a stand on whether "no real bank could fund an unbounded payout, so the game can't exist" is a legitimate resolution or just a change of subject

for each desideratum give the case or consideration motivating it, flag any pair of desiderata you think cant be jointly satisfied (i suspect the boundedness ones collide but maybe im missing a way out), and say which single requirement knocks out the most of the usual candidates (diminishing marginal utility of wealth, expected utility with a bounded utility function, risk aversion as a primitive, prospect theory, the game-is-physically-impossible reply). keep the weight on the criteria themselves, the scorecard can be brief.
