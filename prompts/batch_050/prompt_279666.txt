so I'm reading this pop neuroscience book on pain, and its big move is the smoke alarm analogy. pain, it says, is like a smoke detector: the ache/burn/throb is just the sensor picking up a signal, and the badness -- the "make it stop" part -- is the alarm being set off. evolution cranked the alarm loud because loud alarms get you to act. the book then leans hard on pain asymbolia: here are people who clearly get the signal (they'll tell you yes, that's a burn, it's intense) but the alarm doesn't go off, it doesn't bother them, and on that basis it argues that pain's badness is never intrinsic -- it's a separable motivational add-on, so suffering is in principle optional and trainable. it even extends this to argue that anything lacking our motivational machinery (octopuses, maybe future AIs with damage sensors) "detects" but doesn't suffer, so a lot of animal welfare concern is misplaced.

my psychologist friend pushed back and said the book is cheating: asymbolics aren't clean evidence for the two-boxes picture because their whole affective system seems blunted, so you can't conclude that the pure signal minus the alarm is a pain that just happens not to be bad -- maybe it's not really pain anymore at all.
