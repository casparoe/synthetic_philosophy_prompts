I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, but I've been reading a pop economics book that has a chapter on Marx and automation. Its summary goes roughly like this:

"Marx's view of machines follows from his labor theory of value: since all value comes from labor, a machine is just crystallized past labor, and when a factory automates, the value of what it produces comes from the workers who built the machines rather than the ones running them. Marx thought capitalists automate to deskill workers and drive wages down, and he predicted this process would continue until almost no human labor was needed, at which point capitalism would collapse more or less automatically under its own contradictions and give way to communism. So despite his admiration for industrial technology, he basically agreed with the Luddites that machinery itself was the workers' enemy."

This is from an actual academic's book so I assumed it was solid, but the bit about machines producing value seems to contradict other stuff I've seen about Marx saying only labor creates value, and the "collapse happens automatically" part smells like a caricature. Which parts of this summary are accurate, which are common oversimplifications a specialist would grudgingly accept, and which parts would a Marx scholar flatly object to? Also does the overall conclusion (Marx as ambivalent technophobe predicting inevitable collapse) survive once you fix the errors?
