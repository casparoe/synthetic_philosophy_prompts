I'm preparing the ethics module of a professional development seminar for engineers at a defense contractor, and I need a taxonomy of the philosophical positions on complicity — participating in wrongdoing one does not oneself cause — built as a branching tree rather than as a list. Each branch should be labeled by the claim that divides it: for instance, whether participation must make a causal difference to the wrong (consider an engineer whose component design would simply have been supplied by a colleague had she refused), whether the agent's intention or attitude toward the wrongdoing matters independently of its effects, whether knowingly benefiting from a wrong one publicly opposes still counts as participating in it, and whether complicity is even a property of individuals or only of collectives whose members then share in it. The leaves should include, at minimum, a causal-contribution account, a no-difference account that grounds complicity in the structure of the practice rather than in counterfactual impact, an intention- or endorsement-based account, and a moral taint account; add unnamed leaves for any combinations of answers you find defensible but apparently undiscussed, and say in a sentence for each empty region whether it looks incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Phrase every branching claim as something a participant could answer by reflecting on a concrete case — the redundant weapons engineer, the consumer of unethically produced goods, the junior employee in a corrupt firm — without already knowing which position it sorts them into, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate. Present the whole taxonomy in continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
