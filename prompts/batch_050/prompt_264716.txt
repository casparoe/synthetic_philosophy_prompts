My roommate and I keep getting into it about what actually counts as corruption, so I started writing down her verdicts to figure out her system. So far: (1) a city building inspector accepts a fancy bottle of whiskey every Christmas from a developer whose projects he inspects, swears it never changes any decision — she says corruption. (2) A minister's nephew wins a government job through the normal open competition and is genuinely the best qualified — not corruption. (3) In a country where giving licensing clerks a cash envelope at New Year is an old, totally open custom and nobody asks for anything specific — not corruption, that's just how things work there. (4) A lobbyist wines and dines a senator monthly for years, never requests anything concrete, in a place where this is legal but most people grumble that it's buying influence — corruption, and she got annoyed I even asked. (5) A civil servant joins a company his old department used to regulate two years after retiring, with zero evidence any of his decisions favored them, in a place where this is common and tolerated — not corruption, people have careers. New case: in a town where everyone openly treats it as normal politics, a newly elected mayor gives residents of the neighborhoods that voted for him first crack at municipal jobs and leaves the neighborhoods that voted against him unpaved for years, and locals just shrug, this is how it always goes. What does she say? More importantly: what rule is she running, what other rules fit all five verdicts just as well but diverge here (especially on whether clientelism like this is corruption at all or just politics), and what new case would settle which rule is hers?
