I've been reading about iterated distillation and amplification as a prosaic alignment scheme, and there's something I don't get. Every amplification round keeps a human overseer evaluating the model's outputs, and every distillation round just trains the model to imitate that overseer. So alignment seems gauranteed by induction: each step stays anchored to human judgment, so the final system should be exactly as aligned as the human we started with. Given that, why do alignment researchers still treat IDA as potentially unsafe? What is this inductive argument missing?
