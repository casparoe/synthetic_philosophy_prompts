I'm giving the best man speech at my old college roommate's wedding next month. He did his PhD on Bernard Williams, and his soon-to-be wife (also a philosopher, unfortunately for the seating chart) once told me, only half joking, that her one lingering fear was that he loves her the way the guy in Williams's rescue case saves his wife: correctly, defensibly, with a permissibility principle filed away in case anyone asks. One thought too many, in other words. The crowd will be half philosophy department and half aunts and uncles who think a categorical imperative is a car part. Write me the toast, roughly four minutes spoken. The hard constraint: it can't be a list of reasons she's great or evidence he chose well, because building the case for her is exactly the failure mode -- the thing has to celebrate them without ever making the marriage sound like the conclusion of a sound argument. Format: first the toast itself, then a short section called "Why it's built this way" (~150 words) explaining how you handled the Williams problem, then "Emergency cuts" -- two bits I can drop if the caterer starts waving at me.
