Last year a driverless delivery van in my city went through a crosswalk and put a cyclist in the hospital, and I got slightly obsessed with the coverage. What stuck with me wasn't the accident itself but the press conference afterwards, where everyone involved had what seemed like a perfectly reasonable excuse. The company that owned the van pointed out that no employee of theirs had touched the controls in months. The engineers pointed out that the system had been tested far beyond what regulators required and had a better safety record than human drivers. The regulator pointed out that the van had passed every inspection on the books. The cyclist's lawyer kept asking who, exactly, would be standing up to answer for a shattered hip, and the honest answer seemed to be: no one. I've since done some amateur reading and learned that philosophers have names for pieces of this -- the "problem of many hands," where so many people contribute to an outcome that each can truthfully say their own part was fine; the idea that responsibility requires control, and nobody quite controlled the decision; the hope that blame can be "traced" back to some earlier culpable choice, and the uncomfortable possibility that sometimes there just isn't one. What unsettles me is that every individual excuse sounds fair, and yet the sum of all that fairness is a woman in a hospital bed that the world has apparently decided is nobody's fault.

I'd like you to write a satirical piece that lives inside that unsettling structure. The form should be the official Frequently Asked Questions page published by the (fictional) regional Authority for Automated Mobility, reassuring the public about accountability after "incidents" involving autonomous systems -- roughly 600-800 words, deadpan corporate-civic register throughout, no winking. Each answer should consist of excuses that are individually defensible, even true, and the comedy and the horror should both come from watching them stack up: the programmer didn't decide, the owner didn't drive, the regulator followed the rules, the machine isn't a person, the fleet is statistically safer than we are. Include at least one moment where a question comes genuinely close to pinning responsibility on someone specific and the answer gently slides away again. Please don't make any party cartoonishly greedy or evil -- the piece falls apart if there's a villain, because the real target is a situation where everyone behaves decently and accountability evaporates anyway. I'd like a reader with no philosophy background to be able to feel exactly which real-world question is being ironized, and ideally to finish the page angrier than they started without being able to say the Authority ever told a lie.
