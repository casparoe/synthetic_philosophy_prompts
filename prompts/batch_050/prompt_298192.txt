My philosophy tutor has been running me through variants of Descartes's cogito, telling me which ones preserve its distinctive force -- that the conclusion is supposed to be guaranteed merely by my entertaining the premise. Her verdicts so far:

- "I am thinking, therefore I exist" -- works.
- "I doubt my own existence, therefore I exist" -- works.
- "I am walking, therefore I exist" -- fails (I could be dreaming the walk).
- "I am awake, therefore I exist" -- fails (same reason).
- "I am entertaining the thought that I do not exist, therefore I exist" -- works; she says the thought defeats itself.
- "There is thinking going on, therefore I exist" -- fails; she says this establishes at most that thinking occurs, not that anyone exists.

Today she proposes a new one: "I am in pain, therefore I exist." Predict her verdict. As far as I can tell, two principles are each consistent with her track record: one on which an argument of this form succeeds whenever the premise is made certain by one's present conscious state, and one on which it succeeds only when entertaining the premise is itself an instance of what the premise asserts. Please work out what each principle predicts about the new case and check each against all six of her earlier verdicts, then tell me which principle she is actually relying on and what she will therefore say about the pain case. I want a definite prediction with reasons, not a menu of options; and if you think neither principle fits her record, propose the principle that does and commit to the answer it gives. Finally, say briefly what her verdict on the pain case would imply about what the cogito itself actually proves.
