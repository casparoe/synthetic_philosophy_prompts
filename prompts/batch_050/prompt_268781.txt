In my history class last week, we were discussing how textbooks cover colonialism, and when I asked whether we can trust accounts written centuries ago, my teacher said, almost as a way of closing the discussion, that history is written by the victors. I have heard this line many times, but the more I think about it, the less sure I am what it actually claims. Victors do not always get the last word -- plenty of history is written by the losing side, sometimes very influentially -- and winning a war does not obviously give anyone control over what scholars write generations later. Could you lay out the different precise theses this slogan might be expressing? For example, is it a claim about who produces the surviving documents, about who decides which events get remembered at all, about the perspective historians unavoidably write from, or something stronger, like the idea that there is no getting past power to what really happened? For each reading, say whether it is a truism, a substantive claim with real evidence behind it, or something that is probably false, and which reading, if any, would actually justify my teacher's skepticism about trusting historical accounts. I would like a menu of options with verdicts, not just one interpretation.
