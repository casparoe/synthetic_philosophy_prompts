So I just got back from a four-hour dinner argument with my friend Marcus and I need ammunition for round two. He's quitting his job to open a restaurant, and I made what I thought was the obvious point that most new restaurants fail within a few years, so he should at least weight that heavily in his planning. His response, delivered with total confidence, was that "statistics don't apply to individuals" — the failure rate is a fact about a population, his restaurant is a single specific case, and you can't infer anything about the one from the other. He said it like it was a theorem.

The thing is, he doesn't believe this in general. He won't start smoking because of lung cancer statistics, he wears a seatbelt because of crash statistics, and last month he told his sister not to invest in some scheme because of base rates about that kind of investment. So the slogan gets wheeled out exactly when the statistics point somewhere he doesn't like, and retired when they don't. But when I pressed him he dug in and said the other cases are different because there the stats "are actually about him" whereas he has specific information about his restaurant (his location, his menu, his work ethic) that the average restaurant owner didn't have.

Here's what I want. Take his slogan — "you can't apply statistics to individuals," or "base rates don't apply to single cases," however it's best phrased — and lay out the different precise claims it could be making, as a list I can actually work through. For each reading I want to know: is it trivial, is it substantive and defensible, or is it just false? And crucially, for each one, would it actually license what he did, which is dismissing the restaurant base rate while keeping the smoking and seatbelt ones? I have a suspicion that every reading strong enough to protect his restaurant decision is also strong enough to torch his seatbelt argument, and that the only readings consistent with the seatbelt argument leave the base rate standing unless he can show he's genuinely atypical — but I can't quite nail down why. Also: one of the readings he gestured at was something like "which group you count me as a member of is arbitrary, so the numbers are arbitrary" — he pointed out he's also a member of the class of restaurants started by people with savings, or by chefs, or whatever, and those have different failure rates. Is that a good point, a deep problem, or a trick? Tell me what the honest answer to it is, because if it's a genuine problem I want to know how bad it is for my side too, not just his.

Menu format, verdicts per item, and don't just explain the reference class problem at me — I want the disambiguation with each reading judged on whether it does the work he needs.
