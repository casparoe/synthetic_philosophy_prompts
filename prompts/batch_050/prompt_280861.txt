Okay so I finally got around to reading that Hofstadter essay where he tears into Google Translate — his whole thing is that it's pure statistical crunching with zero understanding of what the words actually mean. Fair enough. But then he spends half the piece going through specific examples and showing how the translations are wrong, and that's the part I don't get. If no meaning is involved anywhere in the process, then wrong relative to what? A translation can only be a mistranslation measured against the intended meaning, which by his own account the system never had in view. So it seems like he has to pick: either the outputs are just statistically typical or atypical strings and all this talk of errors is out of place, or understanding was quietly doing the evaluative work all along, in wich case maybe the machine understands more than he wants to admit. Which horn of the dilemma should he take?
