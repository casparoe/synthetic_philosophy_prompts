I'm a philosophy PhD student in tech ethics, and a labmate and I are stuck on griefbots. Draft the joint statement we should produce after a serious adversarial collaboration. My side is posthumous authorization: because the bot trades on the dead person’s identity, a competent person’s prior consent or refusal should normally settle whether it may be built, subject to serious harm to the living. My labmate’s side is relational stewardship: griefbots mainly reshape survivors’ continuing relationship with the dead, so mourners’ welfare, vulnerabilities, and ability to move on can sometimes outweigh or supplement the deceased person’s prior wishes.

Use this case: a service trains on someone’s messages and voice after death. It can either recieve archived material only, or generate new statements in that person’s style; the dead person may have opted in, opted out, or left no instructions; and different close relatives want different levels of access.

Give us (1) numbered claims we can both sign about autonomy, identity, authenticity, grief, and surviving relationships; (2) a table of the remaining disagreements, each worded neutrally enough that both sides would endorse their own formulation; (3) the main cruxes and, for each, a concrete variation of the case that would bear on it, with each side’s prediction recorded. Separate genuine moral disagreement from places where we’re probably just using words like respect, authenticity, and relationship differently.
