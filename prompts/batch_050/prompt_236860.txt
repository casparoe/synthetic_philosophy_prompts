I'm writing a magazine piece on the philosophy of equality and I need a thought experiment I can walk readers through in a sidebar, so it has to be airtight. Here's the spec: build a case where prioritarianism -- the view that a benefit matters more the worse off its recipient is -- gets dragged into endorsing, or at least being unable to rank against, a pure leveling-down move: nobody gains anything, the better-off just lose. Constraints: realistic policy setting (taxes, school funding, hospital budgets -- no utility monsters, no omniscient social planner), exactly two outcomes to compare, and you cannot smuggle in desert, fairness, or envy to make the better-off's loss feel earned. The verdict has to fall out of the priority weighting itself, so show the arithmetic with actual welfare numbers for each group. I suspect you'll need a pretty extreme weighting function to get there, which is fine, but then tell me: which feature of the case is doing the work, is "indifferent between the two worlds" genuinely any less embarrassing than "prefers leveling down," and can the prioritarian escape by softening the concavity without losing whatever made the view atttractive over plain utilitarianism in the first place?
