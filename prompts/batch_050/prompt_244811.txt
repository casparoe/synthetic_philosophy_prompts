So I'm working on a novel set in a small made-up republic that has just held a constitutional convention and voted to abolish political parties entirely, partly inspired by Simone Weil's old proposal. My problem: two of my characters get stuck on a committee that has to draft the convention's official joint statement, and I need the actual document they'd come out with, not just a scene of them bickering. I've been staring at a blank page for weeks.

Quick sketch of the two, so you know who's signing:

Character one is a Weilian abolitionist. Her line, roughly following Weil: parties are machines for generating collective passion. Once a party exists it has to take a position on every question, even ones nobody in it has thought about, because it can't afford to be silent; it pressures members to back the line over their own judgment; and it evaluates every proposal by whether it helps the party rather than whether it's true or just. So parties shouldn't be reformed, they should be dissolved, and representatives should vote their conscience on each issue separately.

Character two is a party man, twenty years a whip, and he thinks that's naive. His line: modern mass democracy is impossible without parties. Voters can't research every issue, so parties do the epistemic work of bundling positions into legible packages; they aggregate scattered interests into something that can actually govern; they give voters someone to punish at the next election; and loose collections of independent representatives just means power flows to lobbyists, civil servants, and whoever can coordinate off the books. Abolish parties and you don't get conscience, you get chaos plus unaccountable coordination.

Draft the statement these two could both actually sign after a long honest weekend of hashing it out. I want:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims -- things about what parties really do (good and bad), about what voters can and can't reasonably be expected to know, about what the abolition in the novel will and won't accomplish, that both would sign without crossing their fingers.
2. A disagreements table with each remaining dispute phrased precisely enough that each character would accept the wording of their own side. Please flag any disagreement that turns out to be about words rather than the world -- I suspect at least one of their fights is like that, maybe the one about whether independence of representatives is "real" or just shifts coordination elsewhere, but tell me if I'm wrong.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move one of them. And for each crux, some observation or comparison -- how coalition governments behave, how independent legislators vote in systems without strong parties, historical cases of party bans, whatever both would accept as relevant evidence -- with what each of them predicts it would show, written down in advance.

The tone should be dry and official, since it's a committee document, but the underlying disagreement should still be visible underneath. Also: don't make either of them stupid, my editor will kill me if the party guy is a cartoon hack or the Weilian is a dreamy saint.
