I'm a junior on my high school's Ethics Bowl team, and our coach runs a prediction exercise before each tournament: we study a teammate's verdicts on past practice cases and try to anticipate how she'll rule on new ones. My teammate Mara describes her moral outlook as suffering-focused, but I've never been able to pin down exactly what she means by that, and I'm supposed to present my analysis of her position at Friday's practice. Here are her verdicts so far.

First, given a fixed budget, she would rather fund a cure for a disease that causes chronic moderate pain in a thousand people than a program that would substantially raise the happiness of a hundred thousand healthy people. Second, she thinks a couple who know their child would have a genuinely good life, with the ordinary mix of disappointments and aches, do nothing wrong by having the child -- but they also have no obligation to have one. Third, she thinks it would be wrong for a billionaire to establish a colony whose citizens would live ecstatic lives except for one unavoidable week of extreme agony in middle age, even if the citizens were informed in advance and every one of them would retrospectively judge the trade worth making. Fourth, she thinks it's perfectly fine to accept a bad headache for a day in exchange for a genuinely wonderful month, whether for yourself or for someone else who consents.

The new case for Friday: a foundation can fund exactly one of two projects. Project A spares ten people a final year of excruciating, untreatable pain. Project B gives a hundred thousand people, who would otherwise live merely fine lives, a lifetime of deep fulfillment, and it involves no additional suffering whatsoever. Which project will Mara choose?

Please do the following: predict her verdict on the foundation case and explain your reasoning; state as precisely as you can the rule she appears to be running, given all four verdicts; identify at least two distinct rules that are both consistent with her entire record but that diverge on the new case, and say which attribution you find more plausible and why; check whether her verdicts are mutually consistent, paying particular attention to the second and third, since I suspect there's tension there but can't articulate it; and describe a further case -- not the foundation case -- that would settle which of the candidate rules she actually holds. If you think her position, correctly reconstructed, would refuse to give a determinate answer to the new case at all, say that instead and explain what that would show about the view. I'm less interested in which verdict is morally correct than in getting her implicit principle right.
