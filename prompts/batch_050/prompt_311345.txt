Earlier this year, after the sudden death of an old friend, my therapist asked me a disarmingly simple question: where is the grief located — in your body, in your thoughts, or in how the world now looks to you? I could not answer, and the attempt to answer led me into the philosophical literature on what emotions are. My reading group has since split into camps — some insist emotions are judgments or evaluations, some that they are perceptions of value, some that they are bodily feelings, and a few that they are culturally assembled constructions — and we have discovered that everyone likes the label 'perceptual theory' while meaning wildly different things by it.

I would therefore like you to design a 'which theory of emotion do you actually hold?' sorting quiz for the group, one that sorts us by our verdicts on cases rather than by the labels we find attractive. The quiz must contain eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete scenario with two or three answer options, and no question may mention a theoretical label or ask directly what an emotion is. The questions must include, at minimum, the following diagnostic cases, since these are where I believe our camps genuinely diverge: a recalcitrant emotion (someone afraid of flying who sincerely judges flying to be safe); an emotion directed at something known to be fictional; a grief that persists in someone who insists they no longer believe the loss matters; an objectless mood such as free-floating anxiety, and whether it counts as an emotion; and whether an infant or a non-human animal, lacking the relevant concepts, could feel, say, indignation.

The scoring key must map answer patterns onto at least these five positions as its leaves: a James-style feeling theory (the emotion is the perception of the bodily disturbance), a judgment or evaluative-belief theory, a perceptual theory (emotions represent evaluative properties of the world, as sight represents colour), a motivational or action-readiness theory, and a psychological-constructionist view on which emotions are assembled from more basic ingredients and conceptualised through culture-specific categories. Where two of these positions deliver the same verdicts on all ten cases, do not paper over the difference: flag the pair and state which further case, if any, would pry them apart.

Please structure the response under the following headings, in this order: The Quiz (the questions and their options, roughly 500 words); The Scoring Key (the mapping from answer patterns to positions, including how to handle mixed patterns, roughly 250 words); The Logical Space (a reconstruction of the quiz as a small decision tree, showing which questions do the separating work at each fork and noting any combination of answers that corresponds to no named position, with a brief diagnosis of whether that combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored, roughly 350 words); and finally Pairs the Quiz Cannot Separate (roughly 150 words). Write in British English throughout.
