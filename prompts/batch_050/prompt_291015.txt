So last night I got into this stupidly long argument with my friend Marcus (he's an econ PhD student, I'm doing philosophy) about whether groups can be irrational in the same way individuals can. His line was basically that when a committee cycles between options -- picks A over B, B over C, then C over A -- that's just "politics happening," not genuine irrationality, because there's no single agent there who could be doing better. I kept insisting that if you can systematically exploit a group the way a money pump exploits an intransitive individual -- e.g., a savvy agenda-setter keeps offering pairwise votes, extracting a small "meeting cost" or concession each round while the group marches in a circle back to where it started -- then the group is diachronically irrational in a perfectly literal sense, not just by loose analogy. Neither of us convinced the other, and I realized this might actually make a decent paper.
