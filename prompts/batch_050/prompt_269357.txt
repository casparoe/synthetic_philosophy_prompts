For my phil of language seminar I watched two classmates argue after section and couldnt settle it. Case: in a factory, a siren and a safety valve are both triggered by the same pressure sensor; cutting the siren wire does nothing to the valve. Maya: the indicative “If the siren is silent, the valve is shut” and the counterfactual “If the siren had been silent, the valve would have been shut” stand or fall together, since they have the same antecedent/consequent except tense. Leo: no, the indicative can be good evidence (silent siren tells you about the sensor/valve) while the counterfactual is false: making the siren silent wouldn’t shut the valve. Maya said he’s smuggling in an interventionist reading; ordinary “had been” lets you reason backward to what must’ve been different, so if the siren had been silent maybe the sensor would have failed and the valve wouldn’t have opened. Leo said that’s backtracking and only saves the conditional by changing which past facts you hold fixed. Who’s right at each step, and what’s the closest way to make both sentences come out true without equivocating? Max 230 words, bottom line first.
