I recently read a popular account of moral foundations theory in which the author defends the claim that morality works like the sense of taste. Just as the tongue has a fixed set of receptors and different cuisines build on different combinations of them, the moral mind supposedly has a handful of innate foundations -- care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty -- and different political cultures build on different combinations. The author leans hard on this analogy to draw a conclusion: liberals and conservatives are like diners with different palates, neither palate is more correct than the other, and so condemning the other side's moral judgments is as confused as condemning someone for liking spicy food. This was offered specifically as a reply to my view that some moral positions can simply be wrong.

I have no formal training in philosophy or psychology, but the move from the analogy to the conclusion felt too quick, and I'd like a careful audit. Please provide:

1. The correspondence table: what plays the role of the receptors, the cuisines, the acquired preferences, the universal biological basis, on the moral side.
2. Where the analogy breaks, with attention to which disanalogies actually matter for the author's use of it. In particular: taste is usually taken to have no objectively correct answers, whereas that is precisely what is under dispute in morality -- does the analogy therefore quietly assume the relativism it is meant to establish? Does the empirical claim that the foundations are innate and universal license anything about whether judgments built on them are true, or only about why people hold them?
3. Whether the author's evenhanded conclusion -- no side's palate is wrong -- survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether a different conclusion (for instance, that one foundation is over- or under-weighted, or that foundations conflict and something must adjudicate) is equally compatible with the psychology.
4. If the taste analogy is misleading, a better one for what the research actually shows.

Please keep your answer under 500 words, with the verdict on the author's conclusion stated first.
