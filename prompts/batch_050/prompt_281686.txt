So my formal epistemology reading group spent the last two sessions on the Judy Benjamin problem and I got into a long argument at the pub afterwards about what I actually think, so I wrote down my commitments and I'd like an audit before I bring this back to them. Quick recap of the case as we're using it, so we're on the same page: Judy is dropped by parachute and is equally likely to have landed in Red territory or Blue territory. Each territory is split between a Headquarters area and a Second Company area; in Red territory those are equal halves, while in Blue territory HQ covers three quarters and Second Company one quarter. Then she recieves a radio message: if you are in Red territory, you are in the Headquarters area. The question everyone fights about is what her credence in Red should be after that.

Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) Conditionalization on the proposition learned is the uniquely rational update when you come to be certain of something — the imaging-style alternatives people wheel out for this case strike me as ad hoc maneuvers to secure a preordained answer. (2) The content of the message is fully captured by the material conditional: either not-Red or Headquarters. (3) Even so, Judy's credence in Red should stay at 1/2, because the message is neutral between the two territories — it's automatically true in every Blue world, and the only worlds it rules out are within Red. (4) The message should leave her more confident than before that she's in HQ. (5) Only the propositional content of the message should matter for the update, not the radio operator's policy about which true message to send — the right credences shouldn't change depending on what he would have said in other situations. (6) By symmetry, if the message had instead been "if you are in Blue territory, you are in the Headquarters area," her credence in Blue should likewise stay at 1/2.

I have a nagging feeling (3) and (4) are in tension — the extra confidence in HQ has to come from somewhere, and the only place it can come from is Red-and-Second-Company, so maybe it drags Red down after all — but I could be wrong about that, and I genuinely can't tell where the real problem is. Which of these commitments actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and for each genuine tension what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and finish by naming the single change that would clean up the most.
