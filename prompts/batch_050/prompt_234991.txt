I am a PhD candidate in philosophy, working on the definition of art, and I help edit the annual satire issue of our department's graduate journal. For this year's issue I would like a parody document: the official assessment rubric and accompanying guidance notes of a fictional public funding body — call it the National Council for the Certification of Art — whose enabling statute has adopted, wholesale and without reservation, Tolstoy's theory of art from What Is Art? (in the standard Maude translation).

The document should turn on one specific stretch of that book: Tolstoy's chapter on counterfeit art, in which he claims that artists who lack genuine feeling can still produce passable counterfeits by four methods — borrowing the subjects and devices of earlier admired works, imitating the surface appearance of things in exhaustive detail, striking the audience through novelty and shock, and making the work interesting through plot, cleverness, or philosophizing. Please paraphrase this material rather than reproduce it; do not put any wording in quotation marks as though it were Tolstoy's, since neither of us can verify exact wording here, and invented quotations would defeat the point of the exercise.

Specifications:

- Form and voice: roughly 600–700 words of deadpan bureaucratic prose — numbered assessment criteria, scoring bands, guidance notes for caseworkers, an appeals procedure. No framing narrator, no introduction explaining the joke. It should be funny to a reader who has worked through Tolstoy's essay and the secondary literature on expression theories of art, and still legible to a reader who has not.
- The Council must operationalize Tolstoy, not a straw man. Every scored criterion should be traceable either to the four counterfeit methods above or to his account of infectiousness as the measure of art's excellence, with its three conditions — the individuality of the feeling transmitted, the clearness of its expression, and the artist's sincerity, which he treats as the most important of the three. Where Tolstoy supplies a test but no procedure — how, precisely, does a caseworker audit sincerity? how is the infection of a preview audience to be measured? — invent the bureaucracy, not the theory. The Council is permitted to be sinister; it is not permitted to be un-Tolstoyan.
- The humor should come from exaggerating real features of the view: that Tolstoy's four methods are learnable techniques rather than failures of character; that infectiousness, operationalized, collapses into measurable popularity; and that a faithful application of the test excludes most of the established canon. The guidance notes should register, without any sign of alarm, the ineligibility of figures such as Shakespeare and Beethoven and of the Council founder's own earlier novels — Tolstoy's condemnation of his own fiction included — and let the reader draw the conclusion.
- The target is the position and the discourse of arts-bureaucratic evaluation, not Tolstoy as a person and not any living artist or institution. Keep the irony deadpan but legible: no winking, no aside in which the document admits it is absurd.
- After the document, append a short editor's note of at most 150 words (outside the word count) that separates what is settled from what is genuinely contested in the reading of Tolstoy: it is settled that he lists these four methods and condemns the counterfeits they produce; it is contested whether the list is meant to be exhaustive, and whether infectiousness functions for him as a criterion of art-hood or only of artistic merit. Flag every point at which the parody leans on a contested reading, and do not present any contested interpretation as the scholarly consensus.
