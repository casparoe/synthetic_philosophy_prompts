I direct a small philanthropic fund — roughly $8 million a year for five years — devoted to what our charter calls coordination failures: situations where individually rational behavior produces collectively worse outcomes, and where the actors mostly see the trap but cannot climb out alone. Our trustees have asked me to bring them a prioritized agenda at the next meeting, and I want your help constructing it before I do.

Here is the candidate list I am working from, deliberately kept at the level of mechanisms rather than causes: (1) capacity races, where actors invest in speed or scale at the expense of safety or quality because being second is nearly as bad as not competing — the development of advanced AI systems is the live example, but military procurement and pharmaceutical development share the structure; (2) signaling spirals, where participants keep adding costly credentials or effort that mostly cancels out — higher education, academic publishing metrics, and professional hours-culture are the usual cases; (3) attention extraction, where platforms and media compete for engagement in ways that degrade the shared epistemic environment; and (4) regulatory underbidding, where jurisdictions compete to attract mobile capital by lowering standards, so that no single regulator can raise them without losing the tax base.

Please give me a defended ordering of these four for our funding, along with the reasoning the trustees will actually press me on. In particular: state the criteria you are using — I suggest you at least consider how much harm each trap currently causes, whether philanthropic money can plausibly move it (as opposed to only states or the dominant firms), how crowded the space already is, and whether the trap is best understood as a coordination problem at all rather than a genuine conflict of values, since that diagnosis changes what interventions even make sense. Explain the trade-offs among your criteria where they pull apart. For your top pick, sketch what a plausible intervention looks like at our scale, since an ordering without a theory of change is useless to me. And for each of the four, tell me what observation or development over the next two years would most change its ranking, so the trustees can see the agenda is falsifiable rather than a statement of my priors.
