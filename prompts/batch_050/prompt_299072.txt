Before I pick a side in the literature on Moore's paradox, I want the spec first. Write the desiderata that any adequate explanation of the absurdity of "it's raining, but I don't believe that it is" has to meet -- with the consideration behind each. In particular, I want you to cover things like: why the sentence can be true while being unassertable, why the absurdity survives when you only *think* the thought rather than say it out loud (which simple "assertion expresses belief" accounts seem to fumble), why the past-tense and third-person versions ("it was raining but I didn't believe it", "it's raining but *she* doesn't believe it") are totally fine, and the difference between the omissive form ("p, but I don't believe that p") and the commissive form ("I believe that p, but not-p"), since a decent theory presumably shouldn't treat them the same way. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- e.g. I have a feeling that fully explaining the belief case and fully explaining why it isn't a straightforward contradiction might pull against each other. Then say which single requirement rules out the most standard solutions. 500 words max, and say at the end which desideratum you'd drop first if forced.
