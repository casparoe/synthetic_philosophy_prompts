I'm a data scientist by training, with a working fluency in the formal machinery of causal inference: directed acyclic graphs, do-operators, counterfactual queries in the Pearl sense, sensitivity analysis, and the familiar anxieties about identifiability and about drawing causal conclusions from observational data. I recently got into a long argument with a friend who is a historian about whether there would have been something like Nazi Germany without Hitler. She thinks the structural conditions in Weimar Germany — the Versailles settlement, the economic collapse, anti-Semitism already entrenched across the political spectrum, the weakness of democratic institutions — made some form of fascist, expansionist, genocidal regime likely, and that Hitler was close to replaceable as the particular individual who rode those forces to power. I found myself wanting to say: that's just a claim that the treatment effect of "Hitler" on the outcome is small relative to the background covariates, and you can't estimate it because you have a sample of one. She replied that I was flattening the debate. I want to find out if she's right.

So: restate the Great Man versus structural explanation debate, as it applies to the Hitler counterfactual, in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. What does "Hitler was replaceable" become — a claim about the variance of outcomes across counterfactual assignments of the individual-level variable, given fixed structural covariates? What does the historian's talk of historical forces, contingency, and inevitability map onto? Where does the distinction between "there would have been a fascist Germany anyway" and "there would have been the Holocaust anyway" show up in the formalism — as different outcome variables, different levels of granularity in the outcome space, or something else?

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the historians' disagreement translate cleanly into claims about causal structure, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in the vocabulary of interventions and counterfactuals? My suspicion is that the residue is mostly my framework's problem — that causal inference was built for repeatable units and simply has no honest way to represent singular historical claims — but I'm open to the opposite conclusion, that some of what historians argue about dissolves once you force it into a formalism. Tell me which it is, and be specific about where the translation breaks rather than gesturing at it.
