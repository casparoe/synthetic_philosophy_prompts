Exam question (worth 20 marks): "Herbert Simon argued that real agents satisfice rather than optimise. Explain what satisficing is, how it differs from optimising, and why Simon thought it wasn't just a defect in human reasoning. Student's answer:

"Satisficing is Simon's term for decision-making under bounded rationality. Instead of computing the best possible option, the agent just takes the first option it comes across and stops. Optimising, by contrast, means evaluating every available option and picking the one with the highest expected utility, which is what rational choice theory says we should do. Simon's point was that because of cognitive limitations humans can't actually do this, so satisficing is an irrational shortcut we're forced into -- this work kicked off the whole heuristics-and-biases research programme that Kahneman and Tversky later made famous. Simon coined the term 'fast and frugal heuristics' for these shortcuts. An example: renting a flat in London. Trying to optimise would mean viewing every flat on the market, which is impossible, so you view a few and take the first decent one. Interestingly Simon also thought satisficing was the normatively correct procedure for machines, which is why his ideas were so influential in early AI search algorithms."
