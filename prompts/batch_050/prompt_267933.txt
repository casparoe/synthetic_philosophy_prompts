Last night a friend and I argued for hours about the laws of nature and got nowhere. She defends the Humean view: the laws are nothing over and above the totality of particular facts, fixed by whichever systematization of those facts best balances simplicity against informativeness. I hold the anti-Humean view that the laws govern the mosaic of events and could in principle have been different even holding that mosaic fixed. We both suspected we were talking past each other at times, so I want you to draft the joint statement our argument should have produced, as though we had run it as an adversarial collaboration. Write it entirely in continuous prose, like a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables.

The statement must accomplish four things. First, the claims both of us can sign, concerning what the actual regularities are, what any account of lawhood owes us, and what each view does not say. Second, the residual disagreements, each formulated so that the person whose position it describes would accept the wording. Third, the cruxes, and here I want commitment rather than a menu: identify the one or two sub-questions whose resolution would genuinely force one of us to move, and state outright whether you judge any part of the dispute to be merely verbal, rather than diplomatically treating all of it as substantive. Fourth, for each crux, a case or argument we would both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

To keep this from floating in abstraction, work through a case we invented and could not settle: a universe containing exactly two particles orbiting their common center in a perfect, unchanging circle for all eternity, with nothing else ever occurring. Many distinct force laws are compatible with that single eternal orbit, and the complete history of events is thereby fixed. State what each view is committed to saying about which law holds there: whether the Humean must say the simplest fitting law is the true one, whether I may say the governing law could be any of the fitting ones despite the identical events, and whether either of us can describe any observation, in that world or in ours, that would settle which law really governs. If you conclude that at this point the difference between our views outruns any possible evidence, say so plainly and draw out what that implies for the larger debate. Take definite positions throughout instead of laying out options and leaving the choice to me.
