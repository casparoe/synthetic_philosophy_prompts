"In the 'Ant Fugue' chapter of Gödel, Escher, Bach, Hofstadter introduces Aunt Hillary, an ant colony that converses with the Anteater. Explain the picture of mental organization this thought experiment is meant to illustrate, and assess whether the analogy supports the claim that an ant colony could literally have a mind. What, if anything, does the case establish?"

Here is the student's answer:

"Hofstadter asks us to imagine an ant colony, Aunt Hillary, that can carry on an intelligent conversation even though no individual ant understands English. The point of the thought experiment is that mental descriptions apply at the level of symbols rather than at the level of the substrate. In the analogy, each ant plays the role of one neuron: just as no single neuron in a human brain understands English but the firing of many neurons adds up to a mind, no single ant understands anything but the movements of the individual ants add up to Aunt Hillary's utterances. Because the same functional organization can be realized in neurons or in ants, the case supports functionalism and multiple realizability — what matters for having a mind is the causal structure of the system, not the material it is made of. If the colony's organization mirrors that of a brain closely enough, then the colony literally has beliefs and desires: Aunt Hillary really does understand English, even though her parts do not. The case also shows that consciousness is substrate-independent. Since the colony replicates the functional profile of a brain, it must have the same inner experience a person has, and our reluctance to ascribe consciousness to it is mere biological chauvinism. One might object, with Searle, that the colony is merely manipulating symbols without understanding them. But on the functionalist picture this objection begs the question, since understanding just is the right kind of symbol manipulation. So the thought experiment establishes that a mind can exist at the colony level, and that mindedness has nothing to do with what a system is made of."
