Is there a name for this? I keep running into a style of argument where someone states a "law" of history and then uses it like a prediction machine. Examples: a columnist says every dominant power eventually gets challenged by a rising one, so a big US-China conflict is basically baked in; an uncle of mine says revolutions always end up eating their own, so any mass protest movement will eventually produce a dictator; a guy at work insists all great powers decline after roughly 250 years so America is due. What bugs me is twofold. First, the "law" is usually just a trend someone eyeballed off a handful of cherry-picked cases. Second -- and this is the part I really want a name for -- when the prediction fails, nobody ever ditches the law. They just say the timing was off, the clock is still ticking, wait another decade. So the law can never lose: every outcome either confirms it or postpones it.

I don't think this is just "being vague," because the predictions are often quite specific about *what* will happen, only never about when. And I don't think it's the fortune-cookie thing where a statement comes out true under any interpretation -- these have a definite interpretation, they're just immune to ever being proven wrong.

So: does this style of reasoning have an established name, and has anyone made a serious principled argument that history simply *can't* have laws the way physics does (rather than just "historians are sloppy")? If there are a few nearby concepts, tell me how my thing differs from each of them and which fits best. And if nothing quite fits, coin a term and define it so it covers the can't-lose feature specifically, not just the extrapolating-from-trends feature.
