So I'm a PhD student in political theory and my dissertation is on secularized providence -- basically how 19th and 20th century political rhetoric keeps smuggling in the idea that history has a direction or a destination even after nobody believes in divine providence anymore (Hegel's cunning of reason, Fukuyama's end of history thesis, the line everyone repeats about the arc of the moral universe bending toward justice, that kind of thing).

For one of my chapters I want to use the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989 as my central case, and I need someone to pressure-test the analysis before I commit to it. So: analyze the fall of the Wall through the lens of teleological history. What would a strong teleological reading say the episode shows? I mean the Fukuyama-style reading where liberal democracy is where history was always heading and 1989 is the destination announcing itself, but also the softer moral-arc reading where the crowd at Bornholmer Strasse is justice finally catching up with a regime. Then do the reverse: the actual events of that night were absurdly contingent -- Schabowski botching the press conference and announcing the new travel rules as effective immediately when they weren't supposed to be, the border guards getting no orders, one officer basically deciding on his own to open the gate. If the wall falls because of a miscommunication in a press briefing, what happens to the claim that history was bending anywhere?

The part I most need help with is the middle position I'm toying with: maybe teleology is false as metaphysics but true as a political force -- i.e., the East German protesters, and later the people writing the history, needed the arc narrative, and narratives like that partly make themselves come true (or at least get treated as inevitable afterward, which then shapes what people think is possible). Does the episode support that reading or undermine it? And be honest about where each of the three readings (strong teleology, pure contingency, narrative self-fulfillment) just goes silent about the facts rather than forcing it to speak. If the case ends up being an anomoly for all three, tell me that too, I'd rather know now than in my defense.
