Software engineer here, no philosophy background, so sorry if this is a well-known thing. I've noticed a pattern in political arguments (online, and at family dinners lol) that I don't have a word for. When I'm arguing with someone from the other side, I'll sometimes privately think ok, that's actually a decent point -- but conceding it out loud feels like losing ground, so I don't. And afterwards I kind of forget I even granted it, and the next time the topic comes up I present my position as more clear-cut than I actually believe. I assume the other person is doing the same thing. Which would mean the public version of every debate is systematically dumber and more extreme than what the participants actually think in private, and both sides then judge each other's good faith based on those degraded public versions. It's not exactly lying, and not exactly bad faith, because inside my own head I'm being honest -- the distortion happens somewhere between my head and my mouth. Is there an established name for this? I've heard of motivated reasoning and strawmanning, but those seem to be about how you process the other side's arguements, not about this private/public gap. And if nothing quite fits, what would you call it, and where are its edges -- e.g. is silently staying quiet in a work meeting the same phenomenon, and does it count if you eventually do the editing to your own beliefs too, not just to what you say?
