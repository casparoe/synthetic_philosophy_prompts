I'm a retired atmospheric chemist (35 years of it) who fell into moral philosophy about a year ago and now reads it the way I used to read journal articles, i.e. trying to break the arguments. I came across population ethics and the so-called repugnant conclusion, and I tried to reconstruct the argument for it as cleanly as I could, the way I'd set up a model. The conclusion strikes me as obviously absurd, but I can't find the broken part. Here it is. We're ranking whole worlds by how good they are, full stop.

(1) Take world A: ten billion people, all with wonderful lives.
(2) Consider world A+, which is A plus another ten billion people whose lives are clearly worth living but only half as good. A+ is at least as good as A. Reason: nobody in A is harmed or even affected, and adding people whose lives are worth living can't make a world worse — there's just more good in it than before.
(3) Now world B: same twenty billion people as A+, but smoothed out — the original ten billion drop a little, the newcomers rise a bit more, so the total good stays the same and the inequality shrinks. B is at least as good as A+. Reason: same total, less inequality, and nobody gets pushed below a life worth living.
(4) From B, do it again: tack on another batch of lives worth living at a somewhat lower level (by (2)'s reasoning), then smooth again (by (3)'s reasoning). Each round is justified by reasons already accepted.
(5) Repeat enough times and you get world Z: a colossal population whose lives are barely worth living — drab, mildly pleasant, nothing in them you'd call wonderful. Since "at least as good as" chains the way any ranking does, Z is at least as good as A.

That last line seems mad to me. If forced, I'd rather keep the intuition behind (2) — that adding worthwhile lives without hurting anyone can't make things worse — than accept Z ≥ A. So: which step do you reject, why did that step look fine in the first place, and what does rejecting it actually cost — i.e. what else do you have to swallow somewhere else, since I gather each exit lands you in some other odd conclusion? And please say how confident you are in your diagnosis overall, and flag the single point in it you're least sure about.
