I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a long piece about how physicists, chemists, and engineers talk about the properties of materials -- fragility, solubility, conductivity, elasticity, flammability. Reporting it has pushed me into the philosophy of dispositions, and I've emerged with a settled view I'd now like stress-tested. My position is that dispositions are never fundamental: when a wine glass is fragile, that fact is fully grounded in some categorical, non-dispositional basis (the glass's molecular structure) together with the laws of nature, and a disposition ascription is really just a compressed claim about what that underlying structure would do under specified conditions. On this picture, talk of "powers" is a useful bookkeeping device, not a layer of reality, and I find the rival view -- that dispositions could exist "bare," with no categorical basis underneath -- occult and unmotivated.

What would count against this view? I already know the standard troublemakers, so please go beyond simply naming them: finks, where the basis is removed or the wire is cut the instant the stimulus arrives; masks and antidotes, where the basis remains but the manifestation is blocked by packaging or poison-counteracting pills; mimics, where the manifestation appears without the disposition. What I want to know is whether any of these, properly developed, actually refute the grounding picture rather than merely complicating the conditional we use to express it -- my sense is that finks and masks embarrass the simple conditional analysis but leave my grounding claim untouched, and I'd like that sense either confirmed or dismantled. I'm also interested in defeaters from other directions: the argument that fundamental physics only ever attributes dispositional properties to particles, so there may be no categorical base layer for my view to rest on; the regress or circularity worry that every candidate categorical property turns out to have its identity fixed by causal powers of its own; and the case of probabilistic or multi-track dispositions that seem to resist any single stimulus-manifestation pattern.

Please distinguish which of these would be fatal to my position, which would force revision but not abandonment, and which are merely awkward. For each, say whether a committed reductionist about dispositions would themselves concede it as a genuine threat or dismiss it as a definitional shuffle, and indicate what observation, argument, or result -- in metaphysics or in physics -- would settle the matter if anything could. If it turns out the view has quietly been qualified into something unfalsifiable, I want to be told so plainly. I'd like the answer written as continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables -- just flowing paragraphs, as if you were replying to me in a letter.
