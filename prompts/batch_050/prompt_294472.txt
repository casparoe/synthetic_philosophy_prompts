In my undergrad lit seminar last week we were arguing about a novel whose author has said in interviews that the ending is supposed to be hopeful, and someone cut in with "the author is dead, so that settles nothing." It ended the discussion but I'm not convinced it should have. Lay out the different precise claims people could be making when they invoke the death of the author -- everything from "we can't know for sure what an author meant" to "authorial intention exists but has no authority over interpretation" to whatever the most radical version is, where maybe even the reader doesn't control meaning. For each reading say whether it's a truism, a real defensible position, or something that quietly overreaches, and which of them actually justify telling us to stop asking what the author meant in the first place. I want a menu I can bring back to class, not one theory explained in depth.
