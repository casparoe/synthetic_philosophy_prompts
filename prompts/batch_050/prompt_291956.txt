The exam question was: "HCH (Humans Consulting HCH) has been described as an idealization of human deliberation. Explain what HCH is, in what sense it 'factors' cognition, why it is called an *idealization* rather than a proposal for an actual deliberation procedure, and what role it plays in the iterated amplification training scheme. Finally, give one reason why the scheme might preserve alignment and one reason it might not."

Here's the student's answer, verbatim:

"HCH is a model where a human answers questions, but instead of answering directly, they can delegate sub-questions to copies of themselves, and those copies can do the same, building a tree of consultations that bottoms out in questions a human can answer quickly. This factors cognition because a hard problem gets decomposed into smaller and smaller pieces until each piece is within the reach of a bounded reasoner, so the overall system can solve problems far beyond what any individual human could solve alone. It's called an idealization rather than a procedure because the tree is potentially infinite in depth, which means the construction is only well-defined as an infinite process and can't actually be run; it's a limit object describing what a deliberator would conclude given unbounded consultation, not something you could implement. In iterated amplification, HCH is the thing being approximated: you train a model to imitate the amplified human (the human plus model assistants), then amplify again with the better model, and the fixed point of this process is supposed to be HCH. It might preserve alignment because if the human at the root is aligned, and each decomposition step is just an aligned human breaking a question down, then alignment carries through the whole tree by induction. It might fail because the model distilled at each step only imitates the amplified human's outputs, and if there's any gap between imitation and the real process, errors can compound across iterations until the fixed point drifts away from HCH."
