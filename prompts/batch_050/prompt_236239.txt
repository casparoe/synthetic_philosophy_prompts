Write a set of software release notes for Phenomenality 11.0, the user interface that a human brain renders to its operator, written in the deadpan house style of a major tech company's changelog and committed, without a flicker of irony, to the illusionist picture of consciousness associated with Dennett and Frankish: introspective reports are user-facing summaries of processes the user has no access to, qualia are icons that represent data without resembling it, and the self is a login screen with nobody on the other side of it. Structure it as a real changelog -- Resolved Issues, Known Issues, Deprecated Features, and a Support Ticket Digest -- so that the jokes fall out of the formatting. Resolved issues should include users reporting that their sensations seem to have intrinsic, non-relational properties (fixed: the seeming is the property). Known issues should include the hard problem, marked "won't fix: not a bug, a rendering," and the persistent impression of being an inner observer, reclassified as a display artifact. Deprecated features should include infallible introspective access, direct acquaintance, and the Cartesian theater, each with a migration note. The Support Ticket Digest should contain at least one exchange in which a user's complaint about the redness of red is escalated and then closed on the grounds that the complainant is itself a UI element, and one in which a user insists the ticket system cannot capture what it is like to file the ticket. The humor should come from taking the view's actual commitments literally rather than from strawmanning them -- funny enough that a philosopher of mind laughs in recognition, legible enough that anyone who has skimmed patch notes follows along. About 600 words.
