I am drafting the statement of epistemological commitments that my department requires as an appendix to a dissertation prospectus, and my supervisor has warned me that the set I currently endorse does not hang together, though she declined to say where. I would like you to act as a professional epistemologist — someone equally at home in traditional epistemology and in formal work on credence and full belief — and audit the set before I submit it. Work through the commitments against each other carefully, pairwise and in combination, and reason from what each commitment actually says; do not simply recite the textbook versions of the lottery and preface paradoxes, since my question is about this particular set.

The commitments:

1. In a fair lottery with one thousand tickets and exactly one winner, it is rational to flat-out believe, of any particular ticket, that it will lose, on the basis of the odds alone.
2. Purely statistical evidence never suffices by itself for rational flat-out belief about a particular matter: if four hundred ninety-nine of the five hundred people at a rodeo entered without paying, that fact alone does not make it rational to believe of a randomly selected attendee that she did not pay.
3. Rational flat-out belief is closed under conjunction: if it is rational to believe p and rational to believe q, then it is rational to believe their conjunction.
4. The author who believes every individual claim in her meticulously researched book, while also believing that the book contains at least one error, is being fully rational; her humility is an epistemic virtue rather than a confusion.
5. It is never rational to believe a contradiction.
6. Rational flat-out belief is nothing over and above sufficiently high credence — credence above some threshold such as 0.95.

The audit I want, in this order: first, every pair or small set of commitments that is in genuine tension, ordered from most to least severe, stating for each precisely what generates the conflict, the cheapest available fix, and what that fix costs; second, at least one pair that appears to be in tension but is not, together with the distinction that dissolves the appearance; and third, the single revision that would remove the most tension at once, identified in terms of the standard position it amounts to (for instance, abandoning closure, denying that statistical evidence can support full belief, or rejecting the threshold picture of belief), without recommending that I adopt it, since I have not decided which commitment I would surrender. Please keep the entire audit to at most 650 words.
