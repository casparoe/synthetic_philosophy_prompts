I'm putting together a handout for a value theory seminar and I want a decision tree that sorts people on the wrong kind of reasons problem. The setup everyone can start from: a demon credibly threatens to punish you unless you admire him (or desire a saucer of mud, or whatever your favorite worthless object is). The threat seems to give you a reason to have the attitude, but intuitively not a reason that speaks to the demon's admirableness or the mud's desirability -- which is a headache if you think value just is what we have reason to value (fitting-attitudes/buck-passing style views).

So: start the tree at 'you accept that reasons to admire track admirableness' and branch via concrete questions (e.g., what exactly is the demon a reason FOR -- the attitude, the act of bringing it about, or nothing at all? is 'object-given vs state-given' a real distinction in reasons or just a label for the puzzle? does it matter whether you can get yourself to feel the admiration deliberately?), with each leaf a named response: exclude the demon cases as not genuinely reason-giving, distinguish kinds of reasons (epistemic vs pragmatic readings of the FA schema), give up on buck-passing value altogether, or embrace the counterintuitive verdict. Phrase every node so someone could answer it without knowing which camp it lands them in, keep it to maybe 6-8 leaves, note any combination of answers that's unoccupied and say why you think nobody defends it (incoherent vs just unloved), and flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really pull apart.
