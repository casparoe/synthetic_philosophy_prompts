Dana said the sacrifice is exactly what makes Leo's commitment believable -- anyone can say they care, but nobody fakes caring at the cost of a $200k salary, so the costliness is the proof. Marcus said that's not how costly signaling works: the cost only signals if it's higher for fakers than for the sincere, and Leo openly hated law, so quitting was cheap for him, maybe even a relief. He added that Leo now gets a ton of status and admiration from the sacrifice, and if the applause pays the cost back, it isn't really a handicap at all. Dana shot back that by that logic no visible sacrifice could ever prove anything, since only secret action would count, and secret action can't persuade anybody. Marcus conceded that but said at best Leo's sacrifice shows he believes what he's saying, not that he's right -- people burn their lives down for cults too. Neither would budge. Who's right about which points here, what's the strongest version of each side, and is there anything they're both missing? Please answer in American English.
