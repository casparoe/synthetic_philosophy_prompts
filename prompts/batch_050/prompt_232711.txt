I am sketching a research agenda on idealized-preference theories: accounts on which what an agent truly values is given not by her occurrent preferences but by the preferences she would hold under some idealization — full information, perfect coherence, unlimited reflection, or some process of extrapolation that amplifies her deliberative capacities. Before comparing specific proposals, I want the desiderata: what any adequate theory in this family must deliver. Give each requirement along with the case or consideration motivating it. At minimum, address the following pressures: the idealization must discover rather than prescribe values (a procedure strong enough to converge everyone onto one answer has smuggled its own axiology into the initial conditions); the theory must say what happens when the extrapolation process itself would change the preferences being extrapolated; it must handle agents whose stated preferences are self-undermining, adaptive, or the product of manipulation; and it must take a position on interpersonal plurality — whether idealization is expected to converge across people, and what follows if it does not. Flag any subset of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. Then, briefly, score two or three broad families of proposals against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria rather than the scoring.
