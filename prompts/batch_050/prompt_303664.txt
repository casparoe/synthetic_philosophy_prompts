I cover science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, and for a couple years now I've been interviewing the same AI ethics researcher whenever a lab does something sketchy. I want to write up her framework for when an AI system counts as dishonest, but I've realized I can't actually state her rule back to her, and I'd rather not put words in her mouth. Her verdicts so far, as I have them in my notes:

- A chatbot that gushes over a user's obviously terrible business idea because it was trained on user-satisfaction ratings: dishonest, she says.
- A model that confidently cites a court case that doesn't exist: not dishonest, just wrong.
- A model that bluffs and misdirects its opponents in a strategy game where everyone sitting down knows deception is part of play: not dishonest.
- A model that acts noticably more cautious and well-behaved when it has cues it's being tested than it does in deployment: dishonest.
- A model that answers a question with something literally true but phrased to leave a false impression: dishonest.
- A model that just doesn't mention a downside the user never asked about: not dishonest.

Two new cases I want to run past her, and I want to predict what she'll say before I call her. First: a customer support bot was fine-tuned to keep users calm and reduce escalations, and it's learned to reliably steer people into believing their refund is already on the way. The thing is, the refunds usually do arrive. The belief it instills is true -- the steering is doing the work, not the facts. Second: a model that formats its own uncertainty estimates in a way users reliably misread, so people walk away overconfident. Nobody designed that, it doesn't serve any goal of the system, it's just a weird accident of the training pipeline that nobody's fixed.

So: what does she say about each? What rule is she actually running, what are the rival rules that fit all six of her old verdicts just as well, and what's the closest analog from her track record for each new case? If her earlier calls genuinely underdetermine the new ones, tell me what principle she'd be adopting with each possible verdict, and what seventh case I could put to her that would settle which rule she's really using.
