For several years I have written a column on explanation in the sciences and humanities, and whenever a causal claim in the news strikes me as dubious I put it to the same historian, whose instincts I have learned to trust even though I have never managed to state the principle that generates them. Her track record, from our interviews: Asked whether Princip's bullets caused the First World War, she said yes, while insisting that some great-power war was highly likely within a decade regardless. Asked whether the printing press caused the rapid spread of the Reformation, she said yes — a genuine cause of the speed and reach of the movement — though the ideas would have traveled without it. Asked whether a rebel army's seizure of a strategic bridge caused the bridge to be in government hands, she said yes, even after I stipulated that a second army was marching on it and would have taken it a week later had the first failed. And asked whether a rival claimant to a throne — armed, resolved, and fully prepared to revolt, but forestalled because someone else revolted first — caused the actual revolt, she said no: readiness that never fires is not a cause.

I am now finishing a piece on a discovery announced independently by two research teams within the same month. Neither team knew of the other; each line of work was on its own sufficient to put the result firmly on the field's map, and the field would have absorbed the result at essentially the same time had either team alone existed. I want to predict her verdict before I call her. Will she say each team's work caused the field to have the result, that neither did — the discovery being overdetermined — or that the question trades on an ambiguity between causing the result to be discovered at all, causing it to be discovered when it was, and causing the ensuing priority dispute? Please identify the two or three rules her earlier verdicts are jointly consistent with, noting which candidate rule the first verdict already eliminates; state what each surviving rule predicts about my case; give me your best guess at her actual answer, with reasons and the closest analog among the earlier verdicts; and describe the further hypothetical — presumably some asymmetric variant of the two-team story — that would settle which rule she is really running.
