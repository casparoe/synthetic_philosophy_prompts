My reading group is doing Quine's Two Dogmas of Empiricism (1951) and last night two members, Elif and Marco, got into a fight over §1 that we couldn't settle. The passage at issue is this one, right after Quine brings up Kant's definition of analyticity:

"Kant conceived of an analytic statement as one that attributes to its subject no more than is already conceptually contained in the subject. This formulation has two shortcomings: it limits itself to statements of subject-predicate form, and it appeals to a notion of containment which is left at a metaphorical level."

Elif's position: this is a cheap shot. Containment isn't really a metaphor here -- we all understand what it is for one concept to include another, 'bachelor' contains 'unmarried' roughly the way a set contains a subset -- and the subject-predicate limitation is just a complaint that Kant didn't write in 20th century notation. Worse, she said, Quine's standard in this passage -- that a notion only counts as clear if it's spelled out in non-metaphorical, scientifically respectable terms -- already assumes the austere extensionalist scruples he's supposed to be arguing *for*. So the objection smuggles in the conclusion before the arguement even gets going.

Marco pushed back on two fronts. (1) The subset gloss doesn't survive contact with actual cases. Quine's own example a few paragraphs later -- 'creature with a heart' and 'creature with a kidney' are true of the same animals but differ in meaning -- shows that extensional inclusion is exactly NOT what concept-containment is supposed to be, so the intuitive "we all understand it" move fails. (2) Quine isn't demanding extensional clarification specifically; he's saying that however you try to cash out containment -- synonymy, definition, necessity -- you get bounced to another member of the same family of notions, which is what the rest of the paper shows. The metaphor jab is just the opening move, not the whole case.

Elif said that concedes her point: §1 by itself does no work, and the "metaphor" line only looks like an objection if you already know where the paper is going. Plus, plenty of notions are unanalyzed in exactly this way without anyone concluding there's no distinction -- we can't define 'game' either, but chess is one and diplomacy isn't. Unclarity of analysis doesn't mean no real difference.

Marco's last move was that the game analogy backfires: nothing hangs on where the game/non-game line falls, whereas analytic/synthetic is supposed to be a sharp cleavage in kind, and Quine's complaint isn't that the line is fuzzy but that every proposed criterion presupposes the very distinction it's meant to explain.

Then it devolved into them talking over each other and we moved on to drinks. Can you go through the exchange move by move and tell us who's right about what? Specifically: does the containment passage do any real argumentative work on its own, is Elif's circularity charge fair, and whose analogy (sets vs. games) actually fits? And if there's something they're BOTH missing about what Quine is up to in §1, tell us that too. Not looking for a reading list, just a verdict with reasons.
