Two people in my cohort (I'm doing a PhD in philosophy of physics, mostly thermodynamics stuff) got into a long argument after our stat mech reading group and it never got resolved, so I want an outside referee. It was about Maxwell's demon and where the thermodynamic cost of the Szilard engine actually sits. Reconstructing as best I can:

(1) Ana: the Szilard engine shows that *acquiring* information is what costs. The demon has to find out which side of the partition the particle is on, and that measurement has to dissipate at least kT ln 2 per bit, otherwise you could run the cycle and violate the second law. So the demon is exorcised at the measurement step, and Szilard basically had it right.

(2) Ben: no, measurement can be made thermodynamically free -- you can couple the detector to the particle arbitrarily gently and reversibly, so the measurement cost can be driven toward zero. The unavoidable cost comes later: the demon's memory now holds a bit, and to run the engine cyclically that bit has to be erased, and erasure is what's irreversible and costs kT ln 2 (Landauer/Bennett). The demon is exorcised at the memory-reset step, not the measurement step.

(3) Ana: but the erasure cost is only forced on you if you insist on a strictly cyclic process. Give the demon a huge blank memory tape -- a long string of zeros. It measures, writes results onto fresh tape, and never erases anything. The tape just gradually fills up. No erasure, no dissipation, and the engine keeps extracting work. So Landauer's principle doesn't actually save the second law here, it just kicks the can down the road.

(4) Ben: filling the tape with measurement outcomes isn't free even if you never erase. A tape full of random bits is in a higher-entropy state than a blank tape, and if you count that entropy increase in the memory the books balance: you can either pay the kT ln 2 at erasure, or let the memory absorb the entropy, but you can't do neither. The second law survives either way, you just choose where to do the accounting.

(5) Ana: that's circular. Declaring the written tape "higher entropy" than the blank tape already assumes the information-theoretic bookkeeping whose justification is exactly what's at issue. Physically, the blank tape and the full tape are both just one definite microstate each; calling one of them high-entropy presupposes a coarse-graining, and choosing *that* coarse-graining just is assuming the second law you're supposedly deriving from the exorcism. So the Bennett move doesn't ground the second law, it borrows it.

(6) Ben: the circularity charge proves too much. Every use of entropy anywhere in thermodynamics is relative to a coarse-graining, and nobody thinks thermodynamics is circular for that reason. If "you picked a coarse-graining" invalidates the memory argument it invalidates the whole field, including the original claim that the demon is a problem worth exorcising.

Can you adjudicate this move by move? For each of (1)-(6), say whether it's right, wrong, or right-in-a-way-but-misleading, and why. Separately: who is more correct overall about where the cost sits -- measurement, erasure, or memory-filling? And is there anything both of them are missing, e.g. about what kind of necessity (logical vs. statistical) the kT ln 2 bound is even supposed to have? Feel free to be opinionated, I'm tired of them both claiming victory.
