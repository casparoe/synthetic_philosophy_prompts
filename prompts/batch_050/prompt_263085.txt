Each spring, in the epistemology of AI course I teach, I close our unit on testimony and deception with Frankfurt's essay On Bullshit and the question of where LLM "hallucination" falls in his taxonomy: lying, bullshit, or mere error. My students reliably notice something I want pushed harder this year -- that the industry's preferred vocabulary does quiet philosophical work. Calling a confident falsehood a "hallucination" borrows the dignity of a perceptual mishap, and the debate about whether these systems can lie or bullshit is often conducted in a way that conveniently exculpates everyone involved. I would like a piece of satire that makes this visible, to be read aloud at a small workshop I am organizing on the philosophy of machine testimony.

Please write a parody of a corporate white paper issued by a fictional AI laboratory (invent the lab and, if useful, the boutique philosophy consultancy it has retained), announcing a new "epistemic taxonomy of model outputs" developed through rigorous application of Frankfurt's framework. The satirical engine should be this: every section applies one of Frankfurt's criteria correctly and draws the conclusion that most flattered the company. The system cannot lie, because lying presupposes both a grasp of the truth and an intent to deceive, which the paper solemnly establishes the model lacks; nor can it bullshit, because bullshit requires indifference to the truth-value of one's assertions, and indifference is an attitude, and attitudes, the consultancy has certified, belong to persons. What remains is rebranded as a benign, blameless residue, with the euphemisms compounding as the paper proceeds. The humor must come from the accurate application of real distinctions, so that a reader who knows the essay recognizes that the author understands exactly what is being gamed, while a reader who has never encountered Frankfurt can still follow the reasoning and sense where it rots. Keep the voice deadpan corporate throughout, with no winking; formatted with the numbered sections, executive summary, and self-congratulatory apparatus the genre demands; roughly 800 words. Do not satirize any real company or named living person.

Because the piece is satire, it has no verdict to defend, but I want its intellectual honesty policed anyway. After the white paper, append a brief author's note in a straight voice -- perhaps 150 words -- separating what is settled in Frankfurt's account from what is genuinely contested when the account is extended to language models: for instance, whether bullshit in Frankfurt's sense requires a self-conscious stance toward truth that these systems arguably cannot have, whether "indifference" can be attributed to a process rather than an agent, and where your parody leans on a contested reading of the essay rather than the text itself. Do not let the note present any disputed application of Frankfurt as the consensus view. American English, please.
