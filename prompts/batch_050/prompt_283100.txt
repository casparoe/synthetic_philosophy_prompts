Before I read any more thinkpieces about statues coming down or staying up, I want to know what I should actually be asking of a theory here. So: write the desiderata for an ethics of public commemoration -- what any adequate account of when it's fine to erect, keep, alter, move, or remove statues and street names has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

The cases I think it has to be able to handle, as a starting set (push back on this list if some item shouldn't be a fixed point): a founder-general who did the thing the country celebrates him for AND owned slaves, with the fame and the badness both real; a statue that started out unremarkable but got adopted as a rallying point by a movement most people now condemn, so the object acquired a second meaning it never asked for; a street name honoring a genuinely admirable person whose one famous achievement turns out to have been fabricated; a memorial to victims of an atrocity (does whatever principle you give for honoring heroes also cover mourning victims, or are those different theories wearing the same clothes?); and the situation where the statue itself has become historically interesting -- a hundred years old, a document of the era that erected it -- so removing it destroys evidence of the thing people object to.

The distinctions I suspect a theory must respect without collapsing: honoring someone vs recording that they mattered; the wrongness of the person vs the wrongness of the act of putting up / taking down (removal is itself a public act with a message, not just the absence of the old message); and who has standing to decide -- the locals who walk past it daily, the nation whose history it is, descendants of the people the figure wronged, future people who'll inherit whatever we leave up.

Also: any account that implies a city must audit every name every decade is out, and so is any account that makes removal effectively impossible. And I have a pet proposal I want tested against the desiderata -- all commemorative honors should come with a sunset clause, expiring after, say, 75 years unless a body affirmatively renews them, so retention is something you choose rather than the default. Tell me which desiderata this satisfies elegantly and which it violates.

Flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied -- I suspect the "respect what the object has come to mean" requirement and the "judge the figure by what they did" requirement pull apart hard -- and tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular positions on both sides (the contextualize-with-a-plaque people and the remove-what-offends people both). Then, briefly, score those two camps plus a pure retentionist view against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases, not the scorecard.
