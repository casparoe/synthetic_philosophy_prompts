I've been turning over a question on my own and I'd like help finding the principle behind my reactions. When two people who seem equally well-placed disagree, when should you move toward the other's view, and when may you hold your ground? Here are my verdicts on some cases:

When my sister and I, both careful with numbers, get different totals splitting a restaurant bill, I think I should drop my answer entirely and we should just recompute — I shouldn't trust mine over hers at all. When my equally thoughtful friend and I disagree about whether our mutual friend's comment at dinner was cruel or just awkward, I feel I should move somewhat toward his reading but not halfway — I was paying closer attention that evening, and that seems to matter. When two doctors with the same scan give me different diagnoses, I think I have no business siding with either; I should just get a third opinion, and it would be irrational to believe the first one. When a coworker I respect disagrees with me about whether it was wrong of me to hide a mistake from our boss, I find I don't want to budge at all, even though I'd call her my equal in judgment — the case feels too entangled with who I am for her view to count against mine. And when I learn that my own past self firmly believed something I now deny, I treat that as barely any evidence at all against my current view, though I can't say why my past self counts for less than my sister does.

Is there a single principle about disagreement that captures all of these verdicts, or even most of them? If so, state it precisely, tell me which of my verdicts it vindicates and which it forces me to give up, and say what it predicts about a case I haven't listed — for instance, disagreeing with a stranger whose track record of being right is better documented than my own. If no principle can save all five without becoming a patchwork, tell me which verdict is the weakest and should be abandoned first. And if you think my way of setting up the question is confused — say, if the cases differ in some respect I've overlooked that makes them not really one problem — please point that out plainly rather than forcing a unified answer onto them.
