Here's the question the students were given:

"The Liar sentence ('this sentence is not true') yields a contradiction from the T-schema (Tr(⌜A⌝) ↔ A). Two standard diagnoses are: (i) something is wrong with classical logic's treatment of negation (motivating truth-value gaps or dialetheism), and (ii) something is wrong with self-reference. Explain Curry's paradox and Yablo's paradox, and say what problem each poses for diagnoses (i) and (ii). Does either paradox show that the unrestricted T-schema must be rejected? Justify your answer."

And here's the student's answer:

"The Liar works because L says of itself that it isn't true, so the T-schema gives Tr(L) ↔ ¬Tr(L), contradiction.

Curry's paradox: take a sentence C that says 'if this sentence is true, then p', where p can be anything at all, e.g. 'the moon is made of cheese'. The T-schema gives Tr(C) ↔ (Tr(C) → p). Assume Tr(C). Then by the T-schema, Tr(C) → p, so by modus ponens, p. Discharging the assumption, Tr(C) → p, so by the other direction of the T-schema, Tr(C), and by modus ponens again, p. Since p was arbitrary, everything follows. Notice that the assumption Tr(C) gets used twice in this proof (once to extract Tr(C) → p, once for the modus ponens), which is why logicians who restrict the structural rule of contraction take themselves to have blocked the paradox. As for diagnosis (i): gap and glut theorists actually do fine here, because the Curry derivation never produces an explicit contradiction of the form A ∧ ¬A — it just proves p outright. So paraconsistent logic, by blocking explosion (the inference from a contradiction to everything), prevents the triviality.

Yablo's paradox: consider an infinite sequence of sentences Y1, Y2, Y3, … where each Yn says 'for every k > n, Yk is not true'. Suppose some Yn is true. Then every sentence after it is untrue, including Y(n+1), but Y(n+1) being untrue means some sentence after it is true — contradiction. So every Yn is untrue. But then in particular everything after Y1 is untrue, which is exactly what Y1 says, so Y1 is true. Contradiction. Since no sentence in the sequence ever mentions itself, this directly refutes diagnosis (ii): self-reference turns out to have nothing to do with semantic paradox. More generally, any infinite chain where each sentence makes a claim about the ones after it will be paradoxical in this way.

So to the final question: Curry doesn't strictly force us to reject the T-schema, since restricting contraction is an alternative that keeps the T-schema fully intact. But between the two diagnoses, (ii) looks dead, and (i) only survives if the non-classical logic restricts contraction rather than negation."
