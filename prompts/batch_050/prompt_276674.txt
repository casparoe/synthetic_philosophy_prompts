So I've been chewing on the chain-of-thought faithfulness discourse again (the alignment forum flavor of it, not the ML-paper flavor) and I keep landing on the same position, which I'll call the legibility bet. Roughly: as long as frontier models do their serious reasoning in English CoT that we don't directly optimize against — no gradient pressure on the CoT itself to look innocuous, no training on monitor outputs — then a model that's scheming will either (a) leak the scheme into the CoT where a monitor can catch it, or (b) be forced to scheme covertly, and covert scheming is structurally hard. The load-bearing intuition for me is what I think of as the one-pass constraint: any planning the model can't do in the visible CoT has to happen inside a single forward pass, or get smuggled forward through the context via steganographic encodings, and both of those scale terribly with plan complexity. So the CoT is a mostly-faithful window into anything dangerous enough to be worth worrying about.

I believe this at maybe 60%. But my track record of believing alignment arguments partly because they're comforting is not great, so I want someone to genuinely try to break it. Play the role of an alignment researcher who's worked on interpretability, is professionally allergic to monitorability optimism, and thinks most "the CoT will just tell us" arguments are doing motte-and-bailey with the word "faithful."
