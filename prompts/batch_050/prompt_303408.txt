DRAFT: "Maxent as a referee, not a player"

1. Setup. Objective Bayesians want constraints on rational credence beyond the probability axioms. The maximum entropy principle (maxent) says: given constraints on your evidence (say, you know the mean roll of a die is 4.5), adopt the distribution with maximum Shannon entropy among those satisfying the constraints. The pitch is that maxent is the unique rule that adds no information beyond the evidence. If that pitch works, we get something like rational priors -- not one universal prior, but a function from evidential situations to uniquely rationally mandated distributions.

2. The standard objection: partition dependence. Entropy is defined relative to a space of possibilities and a background measure. Ask for the maxent distribution over a cube's side length given only that it's between 1 and 3 cm, and you get uniformity over length; ask over volume (1 to 27 cm^3) and you get uniformity over volume. Same physical situation, different answers. Jaynes thought transformation-group methods fix this for "well-posed" problems, but most real problems don't come with an obvious symmetry group. Keynes's principle of indifference died on exactly this rock and maxent seems to inherit it.

3. My proposed fix: the reference experiment principle. Here's my move. Entropy isn't a property of the world or of your language -- it's a property of a *measurement process*. So: the constraints you feed maxent must be statistics of an actual repeatable procedure you could run, and the background measure must be the one invariant under the physical symmetries of *that procedure*, not of the object in the abstract. For the cube: you don't get to ask about "the length" simpliciter. You ask about length-as-measured-by-calipers or volume-as-measured-by-water-displacement, and each comes with its own physical randomization (drop the cube into the tank, etc.). Partition dependence dissolves because the partition isn't chosen -- it's read off the experiment.

4. Why I think this isn't just Jaynes again. Jaynes relativizes to *problems*; I relativize to *procedures*. Two agents with the same evidence but different available measuring instruments should, on my view, rationally end up with different priors. I call this instrumental perspectivalism, and I defend it via a game-theoretic gloss: maxent is minimax against Nature, but Nature's strategy set is fixed by what your apparatus can actually detect, not by what's metaphysically there.

5. The problem I can't solve: order-dependence. Here's where I'm stuck, and it's the part I most want comments on. Suppose two labs study the same biased die. Lab A first learns the mean (constraint 1), incorporates it via maxent, then learns the variance (constraint 2) and re-runs maxent with both. Lab B learns them in the opposite order. Sequential maxent is not, in general, commutative -- and worse, neither lab's intermediate state agrees with the joint-constraint maxent distribution you'd get from pooling both constraints at once. This seems related to the well-known awkwardness around conditioning on uncertain evidence (the Judy Benjamin-type cases), but I think it's worse for me: my reference experiment principle *requires* sequential updating, because experiments happen in time. So my fix for partition dependence seems to generate a new, sharper irrationality: rational credence depends on the order in which you happened to build your lab equipment.

6. My attempted escape, stated honestly. I want to say: order-dependence is a feature, because evidential situations genuinely are temporally extended, and demanding order-invariance is smuggling in an idealized God's-eye evidence set. But I suspect this collapses into "rational priors are path-dependent historical accidents," which is just giving up on objective Bayesianism while keeping its vocabulary. Section 6 currently ends with a paragraph I wrote at 1am that I can't tell is profound or embarassing.

7. Tentative conclusion. Maxent survives as a local rule -- rational *given* a procedure -- but the hoped-for objectivity was always going to have to come from the constraints, and constraints come from history, not reason. Rational priors exist but they're perspectival in a way that should make objective Bayesians uncomfortable.
