I teach a masters course on ethics of AI and I'm putting together the take-home final. I want one essay question on the responsibility gap for autonomous systems that students can't answer just by rehearsing Matthias's two conditions (control and epistemic) and then listing candidate gap-fillers like strict liability or design fixes. Here's the angle I want the question to force: most of the literature just assumes a responsibility gap is obviously a bad thing that needs closing, but we arguably live with plenty of responsibility gaps already -- nobody is blameworthy for a freak accident, corporate harms diffuse blame across dozens of people and we mostly shrug, etc. So the question should push students to either (a) explain what makes the autonomous-systems gap different in kind from these familiar gaps we tolerate, or (b) bite the bullet and argue the whole gap framework needs rethinking, i.e. say when an unattributable harm is actually a moral problem and when it isn't.

Deliverables: the exam question itself (should be answerable in ~2000 words over a week), a model answer of about 700 words that takes a definite position rather than surveying both sides, and a marking scheme out of 30 with five must-hit points -- the marks each is worth, and one line per point on how weaker students typically fumble it (e.g. treating "nobody is responsible" as automatically unjust without argument, or assuming vicarious liability solves the moral problem when it only solves the compensation one).
