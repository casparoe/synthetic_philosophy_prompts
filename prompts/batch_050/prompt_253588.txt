For a graduate seminar on social epistemology I've been asked to circulate a one-page statement of my working position on deepfakes so the group can attack it in advance. Rather than defend it, I'd like you to stress-test it first. Here are my commitments, roughly in the order I formed them:

(1) The primary epistemic damage of deepfakes is not deception but dismissal: once synthetic video is common knowledge, genuine footage loses its power to settle disputes, because "this could be fabricated" becomes a standing defeater that costs the liar nothing to invoke. The lie succeeds not by being believed but by making belief expensive for everyone else.

(2) Automated detection is a losing arms race, since every detector trains the next generator, so the only structurally sound response is provenance infrastructure: cryptographic signing of media at the point of capture, with unsigned content treated as presumptively untrustworthy.

(3) Authenticity is an intrinsic property of a recording. A pixel-perfect fabrication of an event lacks something no amount of indistinguishability can supply, the way a perfect forgery of a painting lacks what the original has, and that deficit matters epistemically and not merely aesthetically.

(4) The warrant of photographs and video was never really grounded in the technology anyway; it was always institutional, resting on trust in broadcasters, archives, and chains of custody. The deepfake crisis is therefore at bottom a crisis of institutions, not of images.

(5) Empirically, deepfakes have so far fooled remarkably few people at scale — the track record of successful deception is thin — and yet the stakes I describe in (1) are real and urgent.

Audit this set. Tell me which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, ordered by severity, and for each, what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Identify any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, and explain why. Finally, name the single revision that would restore the most coherence. Keep the whole response under 550 words.
