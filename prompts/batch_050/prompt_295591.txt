I've been arguing with a friend about the metaphysics of time and at some point I relized I should probably check whether my own position is even coherent before I keep defending it. Here's roughly what I believe:

(1) Presentism: only present things exist. Dinosaurs don't exist anymore, my future grandchildren don't exist yet, full stop.
(2) Endurantism: I persist through time by being wholly present at each moment, not by having temporal parts scattered across a four-dimensional block.
(3) Physics-first constraint: special relativity is roughly the final word on the structure of spacetime, so there is no physically privileged simultaneity relation, and I don't want my metaphysics to posit spacetime structure that physics says isn't there.
(4) Truthmaking: there are determinate truths about the past (it is true that the Library of Alexandria burned, even if we don't know exactly when), and truths like that need something in reality that makes them true.
(5) Open future: by contrast with the past, there are no determinate truths about future contingents -- it's not true and not false right now that a particular coin toss next Tuesday lands heads.
(6) The passage of time is real and not just a psychological phenomenon. The now moves, or things come into and go out of existence, and this isn't merely about how things feel to us.

So: does this hang together? I'm pretty sure there's at least one real problem in here but I genuinely can't tell where the pressure is worst. What I'd like: go through the commitments and tell me which pairs (or triples) are actually in tension and which only look like they are -- I have a suspicion one or two of the apparent conflicts here dissolve if you think about them for five minutes, and I'd rather be told which than have everything flagged as equally fatal. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give. Then tell me what the single cheapest revision to the whole package is, counting "cheap" as: gives up the least of what motivates the package, not just the fewest number of theses.

One thing I don't want: don't just tell me to become an eternalist. I know eternalism exists and I know it dissolves some of this; what I want to know is whether the package above, or some minimally edited version of it, survives on its own terms.
