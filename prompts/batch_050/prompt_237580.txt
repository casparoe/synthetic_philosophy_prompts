Ok so something that's been bugging me all semester in my epistemology reading group. The bootstrapping / easy knowledge debate has been going for twenty-plus years now and as far as I can tell literally nobody has changed their mind. The setup is well known: on the one side you've got cases like using your gas gauge to check the fuel level, noting that the gauge says F and the tank is full, doing this a bunch of times, and concluding the gauge is reliable — which seems like an obviously terrible way to find out your gauge works. On the other side you've got the easy knowledge cases, like looking at a red table, concluding it's red, concluding it's not a white table under tricky red lighting. And the reliabilists mostly just... bite the bullet and say these inferences are fine, actually, and the critics keep insisting they're absurd, and that's been the state of play forever.

Everyone agrees on the logic. Everyone agrees on what the cases are. Nobody's disputing an empirical premise. So what's actually going on here? My advisor thinks it's a basically verbal dispute — the two camps are just using "justification" for different things, one for a property of the belief-forming process and one for something like dialectical defensibility, and once you see that the disagreement evaporates. I'm not convinced. My own half-baked hypothesis is that the debate keeps stalling because both sides run together two separate questions: is the inference valid, and could running it ever put anyone in a better epistemic position than they were already in? Once you split those, maybe each side is right about one of them.

So: diagnose this for me. What kind of disagreement is it really — verbal, a clash over which intuitions count as data, or bedrock starting points about what epistemic appraisal is for that neither side can argue for from neutral ground? Is my two-questions hypothesis right, or my advisor's verbal-dispute line, or neither? And given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should we just expect this one never to resolve, and why?
