Take the 2006 demotion of Pluto and run it through Hofstadter's Copycat framework — fluid concepts, the slipnet, codelets, temperature, conceptual slippage under pressure. You know the story: Clyde Tombaugh finds Pluto in 1930, it gets called the ninth planet for decades, then in the 90s and 2000s we find a bunch of Kuiper belt objects, Mike Brown's team finds Eris which turns out to be pluto-sized or bigger, the Hayden Planetarium had already quietly stopped grouping Pluto with the other planets in 2000 and people got mad, and finally the IAU votes in Prague to define "planet" in a way that boots Pluto into the new "dwarf planet" category. Huge public backlash, bumper stickers, the whole thing.

What I want is the mapping done carefully, not just name-dropped. What plays the role of codelets here — the individual scouting processes that notice features and build structures? (Astronomers' individual judgments, the Eris discovery, the "it's smaller than our moon" observation, the museum curator's decision?) What are the pressures that raise temperature and force slippage, and what actually slips — does "planet" slip to "planet-with-orbital-dominance," or does Pluto itself slip from the planet region of the slipnet toward the Kuiper-belt-object region, and is that a difference that matters in the framework? Copycat is good at explaining why abc→abd and iijjkk→iijjll feel like the same move; is "we cleared the neighborhood criterion" a genuine deep analogy or just a rule being invented to launder a slippage that had already happened emotionally?

Then tell me where the model strains. Copycat has no institutions, no votes, no one who loses face, no bumper stickers. The temperature cools when a good structure is found — but the Pluto episode arguably never cooled, the structure that "won" in Prague didn't win in the culture. Does fluid-concepts theory have anything to say about slippage that happens at the level of a community rather than a single mind, or is that just an analogy-of-an-analogy that Copycat was never built for? And in the other direction: does the Pluto case show anything about the theory — e.g., that real conceptual slippage is driven by prestige and identity in ways a pure perception model can't capture, or does it actually vindicate the claim that concepts slip only when pressure from rival groupings gets strong enough?
