I spent my career as an experimental physicist and have devoted my retirement to philosophy of biology. Having read around the propensity interpretation of fitness, I have written down my current commitments and would like them audited for internal consistency before I circulate them to my reading group.

Here is where I stand:

1. Fitness is a propensity — a probabilistic disposition toward reproductive success — rather than a tally of actual offspring; and on precisely this ground the old charge that "survival of the fittest" is an empty tautology fails.
2. Fitness attributions genuinely explain evolutionary outcomes: when I say a trait spread because its bearers were fitter, I am giving a causal explanation of the spread, not redescribing it.
3. Fitness is environment-relative: the same organism can differ in fitness across environments, since a disposition's manifestations depend on background conditions.
4. Fitness can in principle be estimated independently of the actual reproductive outcomes in a given population — from viability, fecundity schedules, and the like.
5. Drift is real and pervasive: in finite populations, actual reproductive outcomes routinely diverge from what fitness values would lead one to expect.
6. Natural selection is a cause of evolutionary change, not merely a statistical pattern retrospectively discernible in genealogies.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly, distinguishing real conflicts from apparent ones that dissolve on inspection. For each genuine tension, state what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me; and conclude by identifying the single revision that would purchase the most consistency at the lowest price.
