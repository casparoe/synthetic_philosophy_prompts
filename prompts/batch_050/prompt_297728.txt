You are a professional philosopher of science acting as a research consultant to a literary novelist. I am writing a near-future novel about a institute divided over a quantum-gravity theory that implies causally disconnected bubble universes. Think carefully, brainstorm three designs briefly, then present only the strongest.

Construct a thought experiment I can dramatize in one committee meeting, in the spirit of Huygens’s admission in Treatise on Light that physical principles may be verified by the conclusions drawn from them rather than proved like geometrical axioms. Constraints: no direct observation of other universes, no aliens or oracle-like simulators, no mathematics beyond what the scene can gesture at, and no verdicts stipulated by the narrator. The theory must gain support only from three non-empirical sources: a prolonged no-alternatives search, an unexpected unification of previously separate constants, and convergence between two formalisms developed by rival groups. Include a defined risk of confirmation laundering, where sociological agreement is counted twice as if it were independent evidence.

After the scenario, give the verdicts of a Bayesian defender of non-empirical confirmation, a Popperian falsificationist, and a social epistemologist; identify the single feature that makes the Bayesian case strongest; show what happens if it is removed; and state how the falsificationist would attack the case without caricature.
