I retired from a physics department three years ago and have been falling down the philosophy-of-rationality rabbit hole ever since, so bear with me if this is naive. I've been drafting a little statement of my views on the sunk cost business for a discussion group, and before I read it out loud I'd like someone to check whether the pieces actually fit together. Here's what I currently hold:

(a) The sunk cost fallacy is a genuine fallicy. Money and time already spent are gone, and letting them influence a decision is a mistake, full stop.

(b) When I'm deciding whether to continue a long project, the sheer fact that I already invested heavily in it is legitimate evidence. Past me wasn't an idiot; he presumably had reasons for starting and continuing this thing, and I may have forgotten half of them. So the size of the investment carries information about the project's expected value, even if I can't reconstruct the original case for it.

(c) It can be rational to finish things partly *in order to* remain the kind of person who finishes things. If I abandon every project the moment the short-term calculus turns against it, I lose the ability to take on decade-long commitments at all — and in my old field, nothing worth doing took less than five years. The finishing is an investment in my own future capacity to commit.

(d) Rational choice just is maximizing the expected value of outcomes going forward. The past is fixed; there are no backward-looking reasons, only forward-looking ones wearing costumes.

(e) Two verdicts from my own life I'm confident about. First: a colleague kept her experiment running for two extra years after another group scooped the result, mostly because she'd sunk so much into it, and I still think that was flatly irrational. Second: I finished a monograph in my last year even though I'd lost interest and the field had moved on, partly because I'd promised the publisher, and I think that was right — and I'd think it was right even setting aside any reputational benefit, just because I'd said I would.

So: do these hang together? Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it at first glance? Rank whatever you find by severity rather than treating all conflicts as equal, and for each one say what would have to give. And please actually commit — if one of (a) through (e) has to go, tell me which one you'd cut and why, don't just lay out the menu and leave me to order. I'm especially suspicious that some of my "confident" life verdicts can't be delivered by my stated principles, but I can't see which.
