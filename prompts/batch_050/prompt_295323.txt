i want a poem about Buridan's ass, somewhere around 18-24 lines, with a formal constraint that does the philosophical work. the two haystacks should get one stanza each, and the stanzas have to be exact metrical mirrors of each other -- same syllable counts line for line, rhyme scheme flipped, imagery as close to identical as you can manage -- so the poem itself performs the symmetry that makes the choice impossible. then a short third movement where the ass finally eats, but the poem is not allowed to supply any reason why that stack and not the other: no gust of wind, no fly landing on one pile, no memory of sweeter clover. the arbitrariness has to be felt in the form rather than stated, e.g. the last stanza breaks the established meter in a way that reads as a lurch rather than a resolution. someone who knows the picking-vs-choosing distinction should be able to tell the poem is about that, not just about being indecisive, and it shouldn't name-drop the paradox or explain itself. bonus if the hunger (the deadline, the real cost of deliberating forever) shows up as mounting pressure on the form itself instead of being described.
