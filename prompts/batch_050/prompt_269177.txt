I'm on my city's planning commission, and next month we vote on whether to rezone a single-family block to allow a 60-unit affordable apartment building. The neighbors who showed up to our hearings are against it — traffic, parking, "neighborhood character," a few say property values. The people who'd actually live in the building obviously can't show up, because they don't live here yet, and a lot of them probably can't even afford to stay in the city at all. One commissioner says our job is to represent the current residents who elect and fund us, full stop. Another says that's exactly the problem and that we owe something to people who'd benefit but have no seat at the table. Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the vote recommending yes or no on the rezoning, and have it actually take a position on whose interests we're supposed to count and why — not just hide behind traffic studies. Where it draws on the literature about who has a claim to a city or how standing works in local decision-making, please separate what's broadly agreed on from what's genuinely contested, and don't pass off the contested stuff as consensus.
