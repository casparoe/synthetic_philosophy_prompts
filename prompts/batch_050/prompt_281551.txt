I'm in a small reading group on suffering-focused ethics and population axiology, and we've each been asked to write up our "axiological profile" so the others can try to break it before we discuss. I'd like a consistency check on mine before I circulate it, because I have a nagging feeling some pieces don't fit but can't locate the problem myself. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I accept tranquilism: what makes a state of consciousness valuable is never anything positive in it but only the absence of cravings, disturbances, or suffering. Pleasure matters morally only insofar as it relieves a craving or accompanies a craving-free state; an experience of bliss that neither removes nor prevents any craving adds no value to the world. (2) I am nonetheless a hedonist about value in the broad sense: states of consciousness are the only bearers of intrinsic value, and nothing outside anyone's experience — achievements, knowledge, authenticity, natural beauty — matters for its own sake. (3) I hold the standard procreative asymmetry: there is a strong moral reason not to bring into existence a life predictably full of suffering, but there is no moral reason to bring into existence even a very happy life for that person's own sake, since nonexistence is not bad for anyone. (4) At the same time, I'm convinced it would be a genuine loss to the universe — not merely a regrettable event — if a distant civilization of perfectly contented, entirely craving-free people were instantaneously and painlessly annihilated tomorrow, and I would say the same even if no one anywhere would ever learn of it. (5) Offered Nozick's experience machine, I would refuse to plug in even on the assumption that it guaranteed a lifetime of undisturbed, craving-free bliss, because I care about actually accomplishing things and actually knowing people rather than merely seeming to.

When you go through this, please do three things. First, sort the pairs of commitments into genuinely inconsistent pairs, pairs that are merely in tension depending on how a term is sharpened, and pairs that look incompatible but actually aren't — I'd rather you explain why an apparent conflict dissolves than flag everything indiscriminately. Second, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one state exactly which commitment would have to go and what it would cost the overall view. Third, tell me which single revision would restore consistency at the lowest total cost, given that I'm not willing to announce in advance which commitment I'd sacrifice. I don't need any literature references — assess the position set on its own terms.
