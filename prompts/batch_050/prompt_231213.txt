I'm running a reading group that's been chewing on strange loops — the idea that the 'I' is a symbol the brain builds that loops back and refers to the very system that built it, so the self is real-ish but not in the way people assume. I want to end the series with a "what theory of selfhood do you actually hold?" sorting quiz. Write me 8-10 questions, each a concrete scenario rather than an abstract ask — things like: someone whose autobiographical memory is wiped but who keeps their skills, habits, and taste in music; a person who discovers most of their treasured life story is confabulated; a chatbot that can accurately answer questions about its own internal workings; a split-brain patient whose two halves want different things; an octopus; someone under anesthesia. Each question gets 2-3 answer options, phrased so people answer from gut reaction to the case, not by recognizing a label. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto leaves: the strange-loop / emergent-symbol view, the narrative self, the embodied-biological self, a bundle or no-self view, and a simple-substance/soul option. Flag any pair of leaves the quiz genuinely can't separate, and note any answer combination that's logically open but apparently unoccupied, with a one-line guess as to whether its incoherent or just unloved.
