You coordinate training for a psychiatric assessment service. New clinicians, each with about twenty minutes per patient interview, must document whether a patient's fixed conviction (a spouse replaced by an impostor, a belief that one's thoughts are being broadcast, a conviction of being persecuted by colleagues) counts as a delusion for the record, since this label affects diagnosis and treatment review. Write a decision procedure they can follow, grounded in the two-factor theory of delusion and in the live debate over whether delusions are beliefs at all (as opposed to imaginings, acceptances, or responses to anomalous experience). The procedure should consist of ordered tests: how to probe for an anomalous experience that could rationally motivate the conviction, how to probe for the reasoning or evidence-weighting deficit that separates delusion from mere rational inference from strange experience, and how to distinguish delusion from overvalued ideas and culturally shared beliefs. Include two worked applications to the cases above, including one where the procedure yields a label the treating clinician will resist, and mark explicitly where judgment cannot be eliminated. At most 650 words.
