I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for over a decade, and the tool-AI-versus-agent-AI framing has been background noise for most of that time. But recent practice has scrambled it: frontier systems are trained as comparatively passive, helpful assistants, and then deployed inside scaffolding — persistent memory, tool use, looping planners, goals drafted or refined by other AI systems — such that the deployment stack becomes agent-shaped even when the model at its core arguably isn't. I've come to suspect the classic debate targeted the wrong unit of analysis: agency may live in the wrapper rather than the weights, and "tool at the model level, agent at the system level" may be the actual trajectory rather than a stable middle ground.

Assume I'm advising a small funder who can support roughly six researchers for three years on purely conceptual work in this area — philosophy, decision theory, formal modeling; no empirical ML and no policy research. Which open questions about the tool/agent distinction should they prioritize, and in what order? I don't want a survey of the debate; I want a defended agenda. In particular:

- For each question you prioritize, explain what makes it upstream of other questions: which further questions become tractable, and which become moot, once it's settled.
- Say which well-known questions you'd deprioritize despite their prominence — whether because they're effectively settled, because no decision hinges on their answer, or because conceptual work can't move them.
- Engage my suspicion directly: if agency really resides in the scaffolding rather than the model, which classic tool-AI questions dissolve, and which reappear in a sharper form at the system level?
- Weigh importance against tractability explicitly: where a question seems both crucial and resistant to purely conceptual methods, say whether you'd fund it anyway or defer to the empiricists, and why.

Format: state your bottom line — the single question you'd fund first — in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. For each element of the ranking, indicate roughly how confident you are, and close by singling out the one point in your agenda you are least sure of.
