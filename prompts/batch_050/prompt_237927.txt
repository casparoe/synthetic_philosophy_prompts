I serve on the conduct board of a small residential college, and a case has split us. Our housing code makes the named resident of a room strictly responsible for violations committed in it, whoever commits them: a first-year has been written up because her visiting brother vaped in her room while she was at class. The college's lawyers defend the clause because it is enforceable without messy inquiries into who knew what, and they note that residents can control whom they admit; several board members counter that punishing the faultless forfeits the code's moral legitimacy and teaches students the rules are arbitrary. Draft a short memo recommending whether we should keep, narrow, or abolish strict-responsibility clauses of this kind. The memo should state a clear bottom line, give the reasoning behind it, propose criteria the board could actually apply going forward (which categories of violation, if any, should require culpability, and of what kind), and treat honestly the strongest argument on the side I reject — including the enforcement and deterrence rationale. If you think the request rests on a muddled premise — for instance about what mens rea strictly requires, or whether deterrence could ever license punishing someone concededly blameless — say so plainly rather than accommodating it politely.
