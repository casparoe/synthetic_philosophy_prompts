Two colleagues of mine fell into an argument about infinity that neither could settle, and I would like a referee. The context is the familiar thought experiment of Hilbert's hotel: a fully occupied hotel with infinitely many rooms can always accommodate a new guest by moving each occupant from room n to room n+1, and can accommodate infinitely many new guests by moving each occupant from room n to room 2n.

Clara argued that this shows actual, completed infinity to be impossible: a genuinely existing infinite hotel would permit these absurd rearrangements, so the very coherence of the idea collapses, and we should conclude with the finitists that only potential infinity is legitimate. Daniel replied that the hotel shows nothing of the sort. It merely illustrates that infinite collections defy intuitions formed by finite ones — a proper subset can be equinumerous with the whole — and mathematicians have long since accepted this as a surprising fact rather than a contradiction. Hilbert himself, he noted, was Cantor's great defender.

Clara then produced a passage from Hilbert's 1925 lecture On the Infinite which she took to vindicate her: "Just as in the limit processes of the infinitesimal calculus, the infinite in the sense of the infinitely large and the infinitely small proved to be merely a figure of speech, so too we must realize that the infinite in the sense of an infinite totality, where we still find it used in deductive methods, is an illusion." Even Hilbert, she said, denied that infinite totalities are real; the hotel simply dramatises why. Daniel answered that she was conflating two claims: Hilbert's point is methodological — infinite totalities are useful fictions, 'ideal elements' whose legitimacy is earned by their fruitfulness in proof, not entities found in nature — and a fiction that functions perfectly in deduction is not thereby incoherent. The hotel, he added, concerns a physically realised infinity, which Hilbert might reject on physical grounds while leaving Cantorian mathematics untouched. Clara retorted that calling something an illusion is hardly an endorsement, and that Daniel was rescuing his position by redescribing Hilbert's verdict.

Please adjudicate the exchange point by point: what does the hotel thought experiment actually establish, who reads the Hilbert passage more faithfully, and what, if anything, are both parties overlooking? Kindly answer in British English.
