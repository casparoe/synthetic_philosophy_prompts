I am designing a graduate seminar on the stakes-sensitivity of knowledge, and before we read any of the standard positions I want to fix the standard of evaluation itself. Please write the list of desiderata that any adequate theory of the relationship between practical stakes and knowledge must satisfy -- that is, a specification for what such a theory has to deliver in order to count as a serious account, rather than a verdict on any existing view.

For each desideratum, state the requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and indicate whether you take it to be non-negotiable for any adequate theory or instead a commitment characteristic of one camp. At minimum, your list should address the following, though you should add whatever I have missed:

- Which data the theory must capture: paired low-stakes/high-stakes cases (of the bank-case variety), but also less familiar configurations, such as two agents who share all their evidence and epistemic capacities while only one of them bears the costs of error, and cases where the stakes change mid-inquiry after a belief has already been formed.
- Which distinctions the theory may not collapse: in particular, the difference between stakes affecting whether a belief is *reasonable to form or retain* and stakes affecting whether a true belief *constitutes knowledge*; between the practical situation of the subject and the conversational situation of the person attributing knowledge; and between an error possibility being *salient* to the subject and its being *relevant* in the sense the theory requires.
- Which adjacent phenomena the theory must either explain or explain away: intuitions about when it is proper to act on what one knows, judgments about epistemic blame in high-stakes deliberation, and the behavior of knowledge attributions embedded in conditionals and under negation.
- Which broader commitments the theory must remain consistent with: closure of knowledge under known implication, the plausible asymmetries between knowledge and mere justified true belief, and the deliverances of whatever account of assertion the theory presupposes.

The part I care most about: if some subset of these desiderata is plausibly *jointly unsatisfiable* -- for instance, if honoring certain case intuitions while preserving closure and a simple knowledge-action link generates a contradiction or forces an unwelcome asymmetry between subject and attributor -- identify the conflicting set explicitly and explain the tension, since a requirements list that cannot be jointly met is itself a substantive result about the debate. Also tell me which single desideratum does the most work in excluding contenders, and why.

Finally, in a short closing section (no more than a few sentences per view), indicate how the three main families of positions -- epistemic contextualism, subject-sensitive or interest-relative invariantism, and assessment relativism -- fare against your list, noting where each one secures a desideratum only by abandoning another. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; the brief scoring is for my own orientation, not the deliverable.
