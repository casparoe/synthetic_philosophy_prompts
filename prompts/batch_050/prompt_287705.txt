I just had a long argument with a friend about Dennett's treatment of the color phi phenomenon, and to settle it he sent me this summary, which he claims is standard: 'In the color phi effect, two differently colored spots are flashed briefly at different locations, yet subjects report seeing a single spot that moves and changes color partway along its path — apparently before the second spot has even appeared. Dennett distinguishes two mechanisms the brain could be using: Stalinesque revision, where a fabricated experience is assembled before anything reaches consciousness, and Orwellian revision, where the experience occurs veridically but memory is rewritten immediately afterward. His conclusion is that sufficiently careful experiments could in principle determine which of the two mechanisms the brain actually employs, and that whichever it turns out to be, the phenomenon demonstrates that conscious experience is an illusion.' I suspect this gets Dennett subtly wrong, especially in the final sentence, but I can't articulate where. Please go through the summary and tell me which parts a philosopher of mind would accept, which misrepresent Dennett's actual position on whether the Orwellian/Stalinesque question has a determinate answer, and whether the closing claim about illusion follows from anything accurate in the summary.
