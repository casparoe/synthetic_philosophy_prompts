I'm a machine learning researcher; my working vocabulary is gradient descent and loss landscapes, amortized inference, meta-learned optimizers, inductive biases, curriculum design, self-play, evolutionary search over architectures, and large pretrained models treated as compiled artifacts of someone else's long search. Lately I've been trying to absorb Daniel Dennett's machinery from Darwin's Dangerous Idea: the distinction between cranes — design-building processes that do real explanatory work at their own level without miracles — and skyhooks, alleged sources of design that descend from nowhere and explain everything at a stroke, together with his charge of greedy reductionism against people who insist that all legitimate explanation must bottom out immediately at the lowest level.

Please restate this cluster of ideas in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report. Concretely:

1. The core translation. Render the cranes/skyhooks distinction and Dennett's argument that complex design accumulates stepwise, each stage building on artifacts deposited by earlier, dumber stages. Candidate mappings I can see: natural selection as population-based search; the Baldwin effect and genetic assimilation as amortization of within-lifetime learning into inherited priors; cultural transmission as distillation across generations of agents; explicit science as an increasingly self-aware, engineered search procedure sitting on top of all the others. Tell me which of these mappings are honest and which quietly smuggle in assumptions that the original didn't have.

2. The skyhook question. Does the skyhook concept even have a referent in my frame? Hand-engineered features, hand-coded reward functions, and pretrained foundation models handed down from above all look skyhook-like from inside a training run — but the engineer is herself, on Dennett's view, a crane-product, so the translation may collapse the distinction into exogenous versus endogenous inputs and lose the point entirely. Say whether the distinction survives translation, and what exactly it becomes if it does.

3. Greedy reductionism. Translate the charge into a claim about levels of explanation in machine learning — for instance, insisting that an emergent capability must be explained directly in terms of SGD dynamics on the loss, while skipping the level of the learned algorithm or the internal representation that actually carries it. Is that translation faithful? And can the line between good reductionism and the greedy kind be drawn precisely in optimization terms, or does it stay a matter of taste?

4. The residue. What in Dennett's argument has no counterpart in the optimization vocabulary at all? My suspicion is that the mindlessness condition doesn't translate: Dennett's whole apparatus is built to establish that competence can precede and produce comprehension, that cranes build design without understanding what they're building. But competence without comprehension is the water my field swims in, not a thesis anyone argues about. If that's right, tell me whether the residue is a defect of my frame — something real it cannot express — or a sign that part of Dennett's argument was aimed at an audience my framework has already converted, and therefore amounts to less than it looks from inside.

Format: the translation first, then a numbered residue list, and end with a verdict on whether the cranes/skyhooks distinction is doing explanatory work my vocabulary cannot do, or work my vocabulary already does without noticing it.
