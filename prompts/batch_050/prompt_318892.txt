I’m writing a novel where a city hands its traffic grid over to an autonomous routing system. After a routine update, two separately tested subsystems interact in a way nobody anticipated: the system interprets a stalled funeral procession as a temporary blockage and sends an ambulance onto a slower route. The patient dies. The programmers followed accepted standards, the city did reasonable safety checks, the logs reveal no negligent choice, and the harmful interaction probably couldn’t have been predicted beforehand. Still, the family feels that somebody has to answer for it, while everyone involved says it wasn’t really their fault.

What’s your credence that this is a genuine responsibility gap: a case where someone ought morally to be answerable for the death, yet no individual or collective can fairly be blamed without effectively scapegoating them? Give me an actual number, not just “responsibility is complicated.”

Decompose your reasoning into the subquestions doing the real work, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each. For example, consider whether moral blame requires foreseeable control, whether responsibility can be distributed across the designers, operators, and city even when no node did anything clearly wrong, whether the institution counts as a collective agent, whether answerability has become confused with legal liability or punishment, and whether the family’s sense that someone must answer is evidence of a gap or just evidence of tragedy. Show how those probabilities combine, but don’t mechanically multiply them if they aren’t independent.

Finally, tell me which part of the decomposition your final number is most senstive to, and what change in the fictional facts would move it the most. I’m trying to make the moral logic of the scene feel honest, not turn the chapter into a seminar paper.
