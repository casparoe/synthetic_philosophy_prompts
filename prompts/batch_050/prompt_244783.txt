I'm a PhD student in political theory (mostly democratic theory, but I've wandered into religion-and-the-state territory for a dissertation chapter) and I've been stuck refereeing the same fight between two of my committee members for months. One is a dyed-in-the-wool separationist who thinks the US First Amendment model, or even French laïcité, is roughly right and that German-style church taxes and the Church of England's establishment are just injustice that's had a long time to normalize itself. The other defends what she calls "cooperationist" arrangements -- she's fine with the Kirchensteuer, fine with establishment in principle, thinks strict separation is its own kind of sectarianism that pretends the state is neutral when it isn't.

I want to force them into an adversarial collaboration, so I need the document they'd produce. Draft it for me. The two positions: strict separationism vs. cooperationist accommodationism about church-state relations, taking the actual range of cases seriously -- US-style separation, French laïcité, German church tax + state-collected contributions, English establishment with bishops in the Lords.

The document should have:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims. I don't want vacuous stuff like "religious freedom matters." Push for substantive things both could actually sign, e.g. about what the historical track record of establishments and of separationist regimes actually shows, about whether church taxes are coercive given that you have to formally exit the church to stop paying, about whether "neutrality" is even a coherent ideal for a state. If the honest answer is that they agree on less than people assume, say that and list the thin agreement.

2. A disagreements table where each position is stated in wording its own side would accept -- no strawmanning, I'll be checking this against what my committee members actually say.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions where settling them would actually move one or both sides. Some candidates I'd want you to evaluate rather than just accept: whether the disagreement is really about the empirical effects of establishment on religious vitality (the "establishment kills churches" claim vs. "separation is what hollowed out religion" claim), whether it's about a deontological claim that the state endorsing a church wrongs dissenters regardless of effects, and whether it's about establishment running in both directions -- i.e., that establishment also gives the state leverage over the church, which separationists might actually want.

4. For each crux, a concrete case, comparison, or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. E.g. if the vitality question is a crux, what would each side predict about a country that disestablished or abolished its church tax, and over what timescale?

One thing I keep suspecting: a chunk of their fight is verbal -- they use "establishment," "neutrality," and "privilege" differently. If some of the disagreement dissolves under disambiguation, identify which part and show the work. End with a short section on what the collaboration failed to resolve even in principle, i.e. where the disagreement is down to starting intuitions neither side can argue the other out of.
