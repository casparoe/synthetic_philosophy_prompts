Next month I'm giving a forty-five minute talk at our lab's internal alignment seminar defending the claim that, for the next generation of capable systems, approval-directed training is a safer default than goal-directed training. The rough idea I'm working with: rather than building agents that pursue some objective out in the world (however carefully specified), we should train agents to take the actions a well-informed human overseer would approve of, where the overseer gets to inspect reasoning, ask questions, and is itself assisted by earlier models. My argument has three planks: (1) goal-directed agents have instrumental incentives to resist correction and accumulate resources whatever the goal is, whereas an approval-directed agent's target lives in the overseer's judgement rather than in the world, so it can in principle stay uncertain and deferential about what it's meant to be doing; (2) approval signals are dense and cheap compared to trying to specify good behaviour in advance; and (3) current fine-tuning practice is already mostly approval-directed anyway, so this is the path of least surprise rather than a radical proposal. I was stress-testing this with a friend over pints and she immediately said "isn't maximising approval just a goal with extra steps?", which annoyed me because I think it's wrong but I couldn't articulate why on the spot.

The audience is about twenty people: mostly prosaic alignment researchers who natively think in terms of reward models and RLHF, plus two or three philosophers who've read their Bostrom and keep using the word "deception" in a way that makes the engineers glaze over, plus my manager, who mainly cares whether any of this is actionable or just philosophy. There's one particular engineer who will definately bring up Goodhart's law within the first five minutes, and a philosopher who I suspect thinks the whole approval/goal distinction is cosmetic because "seek approval" is itself an objective function. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this specific room, ordered by how likely they are to come up, each phrased the way that person would phrase it (engineer-speak for the engineers, the philosophers in their own idiom), with what I should concede up front, what I should hold, a two-minute reply for the three most damaging ones, and a note on where each reply is soft. I especially want help with the "approval is just a goal" objection, since I suspect the whole talk stands or falls on having a crisp answer to it, and on the objection that an approval-directed agent has even stronger incentives to manipulate or decieve its overseer than a goal-directed one does. And tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
