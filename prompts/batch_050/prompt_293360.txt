I'm sympathetic to epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness — the view that experiences like the felt redness of red are real but causally inert, with all the causal work done by the brain's physical goings-on. But I keep running into the self-stultification worry: if my experiences cause nothing, then they can't be what causes me to say, think, or judge that I'm having them, so my confident judgment that I'm conscious seems to float free of the very thing it's about. What would actually count against epiphenomenalism here? I want the strongest defeaters sorted by how much damage they'd do — arguments from the judgment-paradox angle, but also anything from evolution-by-natural-selection considerations or from the view's apparent consequences. Also: is there any observation or result that could even in principle disconfirm it, or is the view constructed so that no evidence can touch it — and if so, is that a fatal flaw or just what you'd expect from a thesis about causal inertness? Please be upfront about which of these objections are treated as decisive by most people working on this and which are still genuinely live disputes, rather than presenting a contested objection as a settled refutation.
