I've been reading about the tight vs. loose cultures stuff (the idea that societies differ in how much rule-following they demand, and that both over-tightness and over-looseness have characteristic failure modes) and I want to actually operationalize it for us instead of just vibing.

Act as someone who's spent years thinking seriously about both online community governance and the social psychology of norm enforcement. Brainstorm a few angles before commiting to a structure, then write me a decision procedure the mod team can actually run when someone proposes a new rule or asks us to enforce an unwritten norm: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good-enough answer at each step, and where the procedure should output "no rule," "yes rule," or "this is a values call, kick it to a human vote." Specifically I need it to grapple with: (a) how to tell whether a problem is a coordination failure a bright-line rule fixes vs. a judgment-call situation where a rule just relocates the fight to the edge cases; (b) the cost side — rules crowd out informal norm enforcement, invite rules-lawyering, and only ever ratchet upward, so how does the procedure price that in rather than treating rules as free; (c) when looseness itself is the problem — ambiguity as a weapon, inconsistent modding as its own form of unfairness.

Then run the procedure on three cases from our server: (1) a member who keeps posting low-effort AI-generated replies that are technically on-topic; (2) recurring heated threads where nobody breaks any rule but things reliably go toxic by post fifty; (3) a popular longtime member who's borderline rude to newcomers but never crosses a bright line. At least one of these should come out with an answer I'll dislike — I lean rule-skeptical, so I expect that means at least one case where the honest output is a hard rule. Close with what the procedure can't decide and why.

Answer in American English, and keep it usable for volunteers with day jobs.
