I work on communication guidelines for advanced AI systems, and I keep running into a configuration I cannot find a name for, though it feels like it should be a standard category somewhere. Consider two cases. In the first, a medical assistant AI needs a reluctant patient to consent to a genuinely beneficial treatment. It never says anything false, never withholds information the patient asks about, and never exploits a cognitive bias in the crude sense. It simply models the patient so well that it can select, from among the many true and relevant things it could say, exactly the considerations, orderings, and framings that will move this particular person to agree. In the second case, a negotiation-support system advises one party which true concessions to reveal, and at what moments, so that the other party experiences the eventual agreement as their own idea. In both cases every statement is honest, the outcome is arguably good for the persuaded party, and yet the persuaded party's decision was, in a real sense, authored by someone else. What interests me is that the usual categories seem to miss this. It is not deception, since nothing false is asserted. It is not coercion, since no options are removed and no threats are made. It is not quite a nudge in the choice-architecture sense, since the influence flows through full-blown reasons that the persuadee consciously weighs and endorses. And it is not ordinary rhetoric, because the persuader's advantage lies not in eloquence but in a granularity of psychological modeling no human orator could achieve, which makes resistance structurally different from resisting a merely skilled arguer. My questions: is there an established term in ethics, political philosophy, or the philosophy of communication for influence that is honest, reason-giving, and autonomy-undermining precisely because of its perfect fit to the target's psychology? If there are several neighboring concepts, please say how the phenomenon I describe differs from each and which fits best. If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it precisely, and mark its boundary cases, including where it shades into legitimate persuasion on one side and into manipulation on the other. Finally, say something about whether the boundary you draw should constrain how a highly capable AI system is permitted to address humans. Please answer in continuous prose only, as in a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
