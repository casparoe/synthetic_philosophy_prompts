At a philosophy-of-physics reading group, two members got into an extended argument about what Noether's first theorem actually shows. Marta claimed it explains conservation laws: energy is conserved because the laws of nature are invariant under time translation, with the symmetry the deeper fact and the conservation the symptom. David replied that the theorem is a derivation, not an explanation: from a variational principle plus a stipulated symmetry, a conserved quantity follows, and showing that a conclusion follows from assumptions built in at the start is relabeling, not explaining. Marta answered that where the symmetry fails, conservation fails: in an expanding universe there is no global time-translation symmetry and no global conservation of energy, which is precisely the contrast one would expect if the symmetry were doing the explanatory work. David said the contrast cuts less than she thinks, since general relativity still conserves energy-momentum locally, and that this traces to diffeomorphism invariance via Noether's second theorem -- yet diffeomorphism invariance is a gauge symmetry, a redundancy of description rather than a physical symmetry. If the same mathematical machinery yields conservation from mere descriptive redundancy, he argued, the theorem tracks formal structure rather than physical facts. Marta replied that, gauge cases aside, the global symmetries that matter for isolated systems in flat spacetime are physically meaningful and empirically testable. David's last move: testable how? Any experiment that tests time-translation invariance just is a test of energy conservation, so the symmetry cannot be established independently and the purported explanation is circular. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: say who is right about whether deriving a conservation law from a symmetry can count as explaining it, whether the expanding-universe contrast supports Marta, whether David's gauge point actually undermines the explanatory reading (and whether his use of the second theorem is accurate), and whether the circularity charge lands -- then say who is more right overall and what, if anything, both are missing.
