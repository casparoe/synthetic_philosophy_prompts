Okay, here's an exercise I've been meaning to try with my grad seminar. Frankfurt cases have been gridlocked for decades, so let's force some progress: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps. Camp A holds that Frankfurt-style scenarios (Black and the counterfactual intervener, plus refinements like prior-sign cases) show that moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities. Camp B defends the principle of alternative possibilities, running some combination of the flicker-of-freedom strategy (there's always some alternative, however thin, and it's doing the work) and the dilemma defense (the case either assumes determinism and begs the question against the incompatibilist, or it doesn't, and then a genuine alternative remains).

Give me: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can actually sign — about what the cases do and don't establish, about what a successful Frankfurt case would have to accomplish, about what counts as an alternative possibility worth the name; (2) a disagreements section where each side's position is stated in words that side itself would accept; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling them would actually move at least one camp, and for each crux an argument or case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded. If part of this dispute turns out to be verbal — I suspect some of the fight over "robust" alternatives is — say which part and why.

One more thing: tell me how confident you are that your agreed-claims section would genuinely get signatures from both camps rather than smuggling in slanted wording, and flag the single formulation you're least sure about.
