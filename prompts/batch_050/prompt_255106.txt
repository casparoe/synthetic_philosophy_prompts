So the deflationism about truth debate has been running for what, 40+ years now — Horwich's minimalism, Field's disquotationalism, Ramsey's redundancy idea before that — against correspondence and truthmaker people. And from what I can tell the sides basically talk past each other: deflationists keep saying truth is just a logical device for generalizing and endorsing, like the truth predicate lets you say "everything in that file is true" instead of listing every claim, and there is no interesting property underneath. The correspondence side keeps insisting that obviously truth is a relation to reality and that deflationism just leaves out the thing everyone cares about. Both keep publishing, nobody converts.

My working theory is that the debate is actually two debates: one about what the truth PREDICATE is for in a language (logical/expressive role), which the deflationists might genuinely win, and one about what makes beliefs and sentences hook onto the world (representation, reference, explanation of success in action), which deflationism never tried to answer but gets accused of failing. So the impasse is partly about who's allowed to set the question.
