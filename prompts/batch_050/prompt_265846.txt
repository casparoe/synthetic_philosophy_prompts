My intro metaphysics professor explains presentism and eternalism with a film-reel analogy. Presentism is supposedly like a reel where only the frame currently passing through the projector exists: yesterday’s frame and tomorrow’s frame are not real objects. Eternalism is supposedly like treating the entire reel as already sitting in the can: the year 1900, today, and the year 2400 all exist at their respective places in the sequence, and the present is just wherever the projector happens to be. She then argues that eternalism must be false because a film canister can contain the whole reel, but the future cannot literally be sitting somewhere in a container waiting for us.

This analogy feels useful, but also as though it might smuggle in exactly what the debate is about. Please audit it carefully. Explain what each part is supposed to correspond to, where the correspondence works, and where it breaks. In particular, address:

- whether the film sequence has an analogue of duration and change, or only static frames arranged in order;
- whether the projector corresponds to consciousness, an objective present, an observer’s temporal location, or nothing at all;
- whether presentism really says that the past and future are unreal in the same way absent frames are unreal;
- whether eternalism really says that dinosaurs and future Martians exist in the same unrestricted sense as currently existing things;
- what the analogy wrongly suggests about simultaneity, becoming, time travel, causal dependence, and our evidence for the past;
- whether my professor’s container objection targets eternalism itself or only the physical film model;
- whether the analogy favors a preferred frame of time, and how that matters if the analogy is extended to relativistic physics;
- whether either view is committed to time literally passing in a second, higher-order time, as the film seems to be;
- whether a moving spotlight, a musical score, a growing film reel, or a spatial road would be a better analogy, and for what limited purpose.

Separate harmless disanalogies from ones that matter to the metaphysical conclusion. Also distinguish the claim that only present things exist from the claim that only present truths exist, since the film example seems to blur them. I’d like the answer to end by saying whether the reel analogy can support any real argument against eternalism, or whether it is just an intuition pump.
