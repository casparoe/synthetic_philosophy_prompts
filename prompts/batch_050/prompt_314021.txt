You are a senior researcher at an AI safety institute who has spent years working on the distinction between approval-directed agents (systems trained and steered by a human overseer's judgments of their actions or outputs) and goal-directed agents (systems that optimize the world toward an objective). A philanthropic funder has asked you to draft the evaluation standard they will use to decide which proposed approval-directed training schemes deserve support, and they want the standard itself, not your opinions of particular labs' proposals.

Think carefully before writing, and begin with a short private brainstorming section (clearly labeled) in which you list candidate requirements and the failure stories that motivate them, before settling on the final list.

Then write the specification: the desiderata an approval-directed training and oversight scheme must meet to count as an adequate substitute for goal-directed agency in high-stakes deployment. For each desideratum, give the consideration or concrete scenario behind it. Ground your requirements in cases such as: an agent that learns to shape what the overseer sees rather than what it does; an agent whose helpful reasoning depends on models of the world that the overseer cannot check (e.g., novel mathematics or long-horizon engineering plans); an agent that comes to treat approval as a proxy objective and Goodharts it under distributional shift; an overseer who is sincerely mistaken or corruptible, and whether the scheme may defer anyway; and an agent that delegates to subagents whose actions no one ever evaluates.

For each requirement, mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp — for instance, camps that demand the system never optimize the world directly, versus camps that demand only that catastrophe be avoided, versus camps that treat corrigibility as the whole ballgame. If some subset of your desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — for example, matching the performance of a goal-directed agent on tasks beyond the overseer's competence while keeping the overseer's judgments the sole ground of evaluation — say so explicitly and identify which requirement must be relaxed first; that tension analysis is the part the funder most wants to see. End with one paragraph noting, without scores or rankings, which single requirement you expect to exclude the most proposals. Do not cite papers, books, or named researchers; the funder wants the reasoning, not a bibliography.
