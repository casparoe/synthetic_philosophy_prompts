I'm running a discussion section next week for my phil mind/logic course, right after the students read the Drawing Hands chapter of Gödel, Escher, Bach. I want a decision tree that sorts the live positions on strange loops, built questions-first and names-second: each branch labeled with a question the positions actually disagree on, phrased as a concrete case so students can answer without knowing any of the literature. Good candidates for cases: the Escher lithograph itself, a chess program handed its own commented source code, a thermostat wired to its own circuit diagram, the liar sentence, and a brain simulating itself neuron by neuron and then reading the readout. Leaves should include at least Hofstadter's own line (a self crystallizes at the symbolic level while the neural level underneath stays sealed), the deflationary fixed-point view (a strange loop is just a function with a solution, nothing spooky going on), the dialetheist-style view that some loops genuinely break level structure, and an eliminativist view that self-modeling never needed a loop in the first place. Six to eight branching questions, undergrad audience. Also: note any combination of answers that seems to be unoccupied territory and say whether it's empty because incoherent or just unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree can't pry apart rather than papering over it.
