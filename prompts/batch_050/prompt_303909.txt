The accounts of how this is even possible that I already have in the chapter, so DO NOT just give me one of these:

1. Partitioning (Davidson-style): the mind splits into semi-autonomous compartments, so one part can deceive another without the person as a whole both believing p and not-p in any problematic way.
2. Deflationary accounts (Mele-style): there's no intention to deceive and no contradictory belief. Motivation just biases the process -- you gather evidence selectively, scrutinize welcome evidence less, stop searching early -- and you end up with a false belief through garden-variety irrationality.
3. Pretense accounts (Gendler-style): the self-deceiver isn't really believing the false thing, they're engaged in a kind of make-believe or imaginative rehearsal that they get lost in.
4. Straight intentionalist accounts: the person really does intend to deceive themselves and somehow really does hold both beliefs, and the theorist just bites the bullet on one or both paradoxes.
