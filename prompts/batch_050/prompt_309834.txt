I’m a novelist working on a book about a successful painter who builds her career on a fictional dance-painting tradition called threadwork, developed by a poorer coastal community she didn’t grow up in. She credits the community, doesn’t copy any one artist’s design, pays two local collaborators, and sincerely loves the tradition. Meanwhile, threadwork artists are mostly treated as folk decorators while museums call her paintings daring and universal.

One of my charaters, an art critic, gives this arguement and I worry it proves way more than I want:

1. Cultural appropriation can be wrongful even when nothing legally owned is stolen, nobody is deceived about authorship, and the borrower does not mock the source culture.

2. One thing that can make borrowing wrongful is an asymmetry of uptake: an outsider receives prestige or profit for using a form while insiders are ignored, penalized, or treated as less sophisticated for practicing the same form.

3. That kind of asymmetry makes the outsider’s benefit partly depend on an unjust background condition rather than only on the outsider’s talent or labor.

4. If someone knowingly benefits from an unjust background condition, their benefit is morally tainted even if they did not create the injustice.

5. Almost every artistic form—perspective, blank verse, blues harmony, manga paneling, tragic structure, whatever—was developed within some community rather than invented privately by the artist now using it.

6. Almost every art market also contains some asymmetry of uptake between communities, classes, regions, or generations.

7. So almost every artist knowingly benefits from an unjust background condition when working in an inherited form.

8. Therefore almost every artist is engaged in morally tainted appropriation, and the difference between my protagonist and everyone else is at most one of degree.

The last step makes the critic’s position feel unusable for the novel. If threadwork painters are themselves appropriating older fishermen’s knot patterns, children’s clapping games, court textiles, etc., then “appropriation” stops naming anything distinctive and just means participating in culture.

Which premise or transition should I attack? I’d especially like to keep the idea that my protagonist can do something wrong without stealing a particular image, lying, or intending disrespect. But if I reject 4, 7, or the move from 2 to 8, what exactly am I giving up? Please don’t just offer a better definition of appropriation—identify the weakest step, explain why it initially looked plausible, and price the main alternatives.
