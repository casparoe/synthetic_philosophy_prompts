So I've been going back and forth on open borders for years and I want to pin down what I actually believe, so here are my gut verdicts on cases and I want you to find the principle underneath, if there is one.

1. A refugee fleeing real persecution who will probably be a net cost to the welfare state for a decade: let her in, not even close.
2. A healthy 25 year old from a poor country, no job lined up, no special skills, just wants to come work and will very likely do fine and pay more in taxes than he uses: let him in.
3. Same guy but let's be honest he'd probably be a net fiscal drain for a long while, low wages, kids in school, healthcare. He'd still be vastly better off than at home. Still let him in. The fiscal ledger doesn't move me much.
4. A guy who comes in an interview and openly says he despises the country's values -- thinks blasphemy should be illegal, women shouldn't be out at night alone, all of it -- but he's never committed a crime and would follow the law. Keep him out. I know this one is in tension with the others, that's why I'm writing this.
5. A million people a year arriving in a small country of eight million. Each one of them individually is basically case 2 or 3. But housing collapses, the schools can't absorb the kids, low wage natives see their pay stall. Slow it down or cap it, even though I can't point to a single individual in the wave I'd want to turn away.
6. Someone brought over at age two, now twenty five, commits a serious violent crime: deporting him to a country he doesn't remember feels barbaric. A guy who arrived two years ago commits the same crime: deport him, don't care. Can't justify the difference but I feel it strongly.
7. A deal where anyone can come but new arrivals get no welfare benefits for the first five years, so the whole thing is politically survivable: take the deal, it's better than closed borders, though the two-tier residency bothers me.

What principle, if any, gets me all or most of these? I'd especially like to know whether 3 and 5 can really live together without smuggling in some threshold where numbers suddenly matter, and whether 4 is just me being a hypocrite since we'd never dream of values-testing citizens. Don't give me a menu of five possible frameworks and tell me to pick one -- commit to the principle you think fits best, say exactly which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, and tell me what it predicts for a case I didn't list: a millionaire retiree who wants to move in, never work, never pay much tax, just spend his fortune locally.
