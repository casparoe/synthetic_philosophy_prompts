I help run a fortnightly reading group at a mid-sized software consultancy. Most attendees are working engineers with no philosophy background, and last month's session on whether intelligence implies anything about motivation went well enough that they have asked for a follow-up. So the next topic is the distinction between tool AI and agent AI: the idea that one and the same underlying capability — planning routes, drafting text, scheduling — can be built either as a system that produces outputs for a human to act on, or as a system that pursues outcomes in the world over time, and that this difference matters more for safety and for governance than raw capability does. I do not want to improvise the session; I want a plan for the explanation before I write anything.

Plan it for me. Give me the order in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced and why — that is, what depends on what. My suspicion is that the session collapses if the word 'goal' gets used before the audience has been given a minimal, non-psychological sense of it, but tell me whether I have the dependency structure right. Give me the one example to carry the whole session: it must show the same underlying capability on both sides of the distinction, so that nobody can conclude that agency is just more intelligence, and it must lack robots, consciousness, and anything they could not picture running on our own infrastructure. Map the misconceptions this audience will bring, roughly in the order they will surface — that agency means sentience, that tool means safe, that agent is a code architecture (a model in a loop calling tools, and therefore a taxonomy of software patterns rather than anything deeper), that a system only ever does what it was told, and that the distinction is binary — and tell me at which point in the sequence each should be headed off rather than left to fester. Tell me what check at the end would show the distinction has actually landed: ideally they should be able to take a deployed system they have never seen, locate it on the tool–agent spectrum, and say what would have to change to move it in either direction. Finally, tell me what I should openly refuse to cover in ninety minutes; consciousness and superintelligence are my own candidates for exclusion, but I would like to know what else is a trap for this particular audience.
