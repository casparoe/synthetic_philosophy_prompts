I am a PhD student in machine learning, working on what my field calls performative prediction: the study of predictive models whose deployment causally shifts the distribution they are predicting, and of equilibria in which retraining converges to a model that is optimal for the distribution it itself induces. I am fluent in the surrounding vocabulary -- distribution shift, model-induced concept drift, performative stability, strategic classification, feedback loops between predictor and population -- and I have been reading Ian Hacking's work on the classification of people, which strikes me as an earlier and richer treatment of something my literature rediscovered in narrowly formal terms.

The passage I keep returning to is from Hacking's essay "Making Up People" (London Review of Books, 2006):

These reflections on the classification of people are a species of nominalism. But traditional nominalism is static. Mine is dynamic; I am interested in how names interact with the named.

My request is a disciplined exercise in translation. Restate Hacking's account of the looping effect -- as developed in that essay, including his five-part framework of classification, people, institutions, knowledge, and experts -- in the vocabulary of performative prediction, as precisely as my framework allows. Treat the quoted sentence as the load-bearing claim: my framework's natural rendering is that a deployed classifier is an intervention on the joint distribution of features and outcomes, so that the fitted model ceases to describe the population it was fit on. Tell me whether that rendering captures what Hacking means by names interacting with the named, or whether it already concedes too much and too little.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about. Which of Hacking's claims map cleanly onto formal results or standard phenomena in my literature (moving targets as performative distribution shift; patients acquiring expected symptoms as something like gaming or self-fulfilling prediction)? Which translate only under distortion -- and state the distortion explicitly, for instance what is lost when a person who takes up a diagnosis as a way to be a person is modeled as a strategic agent perturbing features at a cost? And which parts have no expression in this vocabulary at all: the five-part framework's institutions and experts, the distinction between expert and popular knowledge, the nominalism itself? For each piece of residue, I want your judgment on whether it marks a genuine blind spot of the formal framework -- something a theory of deployed classifiers would need new machinery to say -- or whether it is material Hacking's informal vocabulary permits him to gesture at without ever being forced to state precisely. Be honest in both directions; do not smuggle my framework's assumptions into the translation and call the remainder trivial, and do not treat untranslatability as automatic evidence of depth.

Aim for roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words.
