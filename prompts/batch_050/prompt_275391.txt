I cover physics and information stuff for a general-audience science magazine, and every December we do a "verse issue" where the features desk commissions poems about big ideas. This year I called dibs on Maxwell's demon before anyone else could grab it, and now I actually have to deliver something.

Here's what I want: a villanelle. And not a poem that merely mentions the demon -- I want the form itself to do the work. The two refrains should be the demon's two claims-to-fame, something like "I only watch and open the door" (the sorting, the measurement, the part that seems to cost nothing) and something like "the heat runs uphill when I say so" (the apparent victory over the second law). They should trade places through the tercets while the demon, in its own voice, gets progressively smugger, and then meet in the closing quatrain -- which is where the actual resolution has to land. The demon never pays for looking or sorting; it pays when it has to wipe its memory clean to run the cycle again, and that erasure is where the entropy bill comes due, one bit at a time. Conveniently a villanelle's two refrains literally end up side by side as the final two lines, so use that: their collision should feel like the trap closing on the demon mid-sentence.

Accuracy matters because our letters page is ruthless. The poem should be right enough that a physicist who knows Szilard's single-molecule engine and Landauer's principle nods instead of winces: information is physical, forgetting costs, and thats why the demon loses. But don't name-drop Szilard or Landauer or Bennett in the poem itself, and don't let it read like a lecture with line breaks. It should work as a poem first for a reader who's never heard of any of this, with the thermodynamics humming underneath for the ones who have. If the demon gets a moment of dawning horror, the third or fourth tercet is probably where the smugness should start to curdle.
