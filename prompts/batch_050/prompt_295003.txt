Ok so I've been chewing on this for a while and I want to know if the position I keep landing on is an actual thing that has a name, or if I've just invented something incoherent.

Here's the intuition. Take the worst kinds of suffering -- say someone being tortured, or a parent watching their kid die, that level of bad. My gut says there is NO amount of ordinary good stuff that balances it out. Not a million people having a really nice weekend, not a billion, not everyone on earth getting an extra year of pleasant life. It's not that the number would have to be very large, it's that there isn't a number. Different kind of thing, not just different amount.

But -- and this is the part that makes it weird -- I don't feel this way about ALL suffering. If you told me a million people could avoid a mild headache in exchange for one person getting a slightly worse headache, fine, trade away, no problem. Stubbed toes, missed buses, burnt dinners, that stuff seems totally addable and tradeable against pleasures. Even something like a broken arm feels like it could in principle be outweighed by enough genuine good, though you'd need a lot. So somewhere between "stubbed toe" and "tortured for a month" there's a line, and above the line nothing compensates, below the line normal trading works.

So the view is roughly: pain matters more than pleasure (I'm definitely not symmetric about them), AND above some threshold of badness, suffering can't be offset by pleasure at all, no matter the quantity.

Questions: does this have a name? I have a vague sense it lives in the neighborhood of utilitarianism but a pessimistic version, but I don't know the vocabulary. Also, is it one position or two stapled together? Like, could someone coherently hold the threshold part without the "pain counts more" part? And where do people who hold this put the line -- is it supposed to be a sharp cliff (one unit worse and suddenly no compensation is possible, which seems crazy) or is there a fuzzy zone, and does a fuzzy zone even make sense or does it collapse back into plain trading? If the answer is just "this is a known view called X," don't stop there -- I want to know what the view actually commits me to, what its standard problems are, and whether my broken-arm intuition accidentally breaks it.
