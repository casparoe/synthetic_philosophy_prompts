Take the GPT-2 to GPT-4 scaling run (2019-2023) and analyze it as evidence in the takeoff speeds debate. First map it carefully onto both positions: on the slow/continuous takeoff view (capabilities grow smoothly with inputs, and economic impact trails even further behind), what does the episode look like, and on the fast/foom view (some threshold where the system starts improving itself and everything cascades), what does it look like? Get into the details that seem to cut against each side: loss curves were predictable in advance but downstream capabilities mostly weren't, the whole fight over whether "emergent abilities" are real jumps or just artifacts of discontinuous metrics, and the fact that none of this was actually recursive self-improvement -- humans were doing the improving, just with 100x more compute each round. Then run it the other direction: does the episode confirm slow takeoff, or does it only show that LLM scaling in particular is smooth while saying nothing about what happens once models start doing AI research themselves? Where is each theory just silent rather than vindicated or refuted? I'd rather you say "the theory doesn't speak to this" than force a verdict on every point.
