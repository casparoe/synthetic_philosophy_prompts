Exam question: "State the two axioms of 'cosmic sociology' that ground the dark forest hypothesis, and explain the two mechanisms -- chains of suspicion and technological explosion -- that are supposed to carry us from those axioms to the strike-first equilibrium. Then assess: could two civilizations that have detected each other plausibly escape this equilibrium through communication?"

Student's answer:

The dark forest hypothesis, from Liu Cixin's novel, is a proposed resolution of the Fermi paradox: the universe may be full of civilizations, but they stay silent because revealing yourself invites destruction. Its two axioms are (1) survival is the primary goal of every civilization, and (2) civilizations grow and compete for resources while the total resources of the universe remain fixed.

From these, two mechanisms generate the equilibrium. First, chains of suspicion: because intentions cannot be verified across interstellar distances, each side must ask whether the other is hostile, and whether the other takes it to be hostile, and so on -- an unresolvable regress of distrust. Second, technological explosion: civilizations develop at wildly uneven rates, so a civilization that looks harmless today may leap ahead tomorrow, and you cannot treat present weakness as a guarantee of future safety. Together these imply that the only safe policy upon discovering another civilization is to strike first, since waiting risks being struck by a superior foe.

On whether communication can dissolve the equilibrium, I think it can, for the standard game-theoretic reason: iterated interaction builds trust. The prisoner's dilemma dissolves under repetition and reciprocity, so two civilizations in sustained contact would learn to cooperate, just as rival states on Earth have. The light-speed lag slows this down but does not change the logic.

The hypothesis also yields a testable prediction. Since hiding is the only safe strategy, it follows that we should broadcast our location as loudly as possible, to signal that we have nothing to hide and thereby remove any incentive for others to strike us preemptively.
