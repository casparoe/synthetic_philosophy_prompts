Ok so I write literary fiction for a living, and I've been stuck on something for months that I keep circling back to. In Anarchy, State and Utopia Nozick has that passage about animals where he floats the idea that maybe something like utilitarian weighing is appropriate when it comes to animals — add up the pleasures and pains, see what comes out ahead — while for people you get hard side-constraints, things you just may not do to someone no matter how good the overall consequences. And he's visibly squirming the whole time: he does the bit about super-intelligent aliens farming us for the superior taste of human meat, and he basically admits he can't see why eating animals clearly passes his own test.

Here's my ask. Restate that whole position — the two-tier structure, the alien farmers, his unease about where the line falls — in the vocabulary I actually work in every day: characters, point of view, interiority, treating a character as a vehicle for the plot versus letting them have their own gravity, "fridging" a character to motivate the hero, gratuitous suffering scenes versus earned ones, that thing readers do where they can *feel* when an author has been cruel to a character for cheap effect. Because honestly it seems to me like every novelist already operates with a two-tier system: some characters are furniture, they exist to be used, and some characters you feel you'd be commiting a small wrong against if you bent them out of shape just to make the plot work. I want to know if that instinct of mine is the same shape as Nozick's distinction or only looks like it.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What doesn't survive translation? Characters don't suffer, they can't consent, they don't exist — so is there anything in Nozick's view that simply has no counterpart in the ethics of how you treat people on the page? And when you hit that residue, tell me which way it cuts: does it show that my novelist framework is missing something real (maybe there IS a genuine ethics of character treatment and I've been gesturing at it clumsily), or does it show that Nozick's unease was really about something my vocabulary handles better than his does? Don't smooth over the gaps to make the mapping look tidy — the gaps are the interesting part, I'd rather have three honest failures than one fake clean translation.
