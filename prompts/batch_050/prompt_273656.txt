Write a dialogue between two game theorists arguing about a (fictional) game called The Long Correspondence: it's a narrative game about a chess match played by post over thirty years, but here's the catch -- every single "move" the player makes is illusory. The game silently re-interprets whatever the player does into the one scripted sequence of moves; only the *letters* between the players, which the player writes freely, actually vary. One character argues this is a fraud: artful agency requires that the player's decisions actually change something, so the game has no more artistic value *as a game* than a film. The other argues the opposite: the player's felt deliberation over each move IS the artwork, and the authored letters reacting to your play are where the real aesthetic of agency lives -- consequences are just one way of honouring deliberation, not the only way. Both should be sharp and genuinely engage with each other's points (no strawmen, they should concede things where cornered). Have them end up disagreeing about a specific concrete test case, like a player who discovers the trick on their second playthrough. Write it in British English.
