Okay so last night I got into a genuinely ridiculous three-hour argument with my friend Dani that started over something stupid -- whether I was justified in believing a bit of office gossip she'd passed on -- and ended with us yelling about Hume and Reid at closing time. Her position is hardline reductionism about testimony: you should only trust what people tell you to the extent that you've inductively confirmed, from your own experience, that testimony of that kind from those kinds of people tends to check out. No track record, no warrant. I was defending the anti-reductionist line that testimony is a basic source of justification, more like perception, and doesn't need that kind of independent inductive backing. We basically fought to a stalemate and I've been turning it over since, and I want to stress-test my side of it.

Here are the objections to her reductionism I already used, so please don't just hand me one of these back: (1) the kid objection -- children acquire practically their entire worldview from testimony long before they could have run any inductive checks on speaker reliability, and it seems absurd to say all those beliefs are unjustified, yet on her view they are; (2) the circularity objection -- any serious attempt to inductively validate testimony at a global scale itself leans on testimony (other people's observations, things you were taught, books), so the project can't get off the ground without presupposing the very thing it's meant to justify; (3) the thin-base objection -- each of us has personally verified only a laughably small sample of the testimony we've accepted over our lives, so even where induction is possible the evidential base is way too skinny to underwrite the confidence we actually have; (4) the interpretation argument -- you can't even understand someone's utterances as assertions with meanings unless you already take them to be broadly truthful, so reliability is a precondition of interpretation rather than an empirical hypothesis you could test.

So: is there a fifth objection to testimonial reductionism that I'm missing? It has to be genuinely different from those four -- not a rewording, not a special case, and not two of them stapled together. If you give me one, explain exactly where it parts company with the ones above, and then be honest about whether it actually survives scrutiny or whether Dani would swat it away in thirty seconds. Also, while you're at it: if you think one of my four is weaker than I'm assuming, tell me which and why, because I'd rather find out from you than from her at the pub next week.
