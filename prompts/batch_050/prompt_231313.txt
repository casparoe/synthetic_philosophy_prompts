You are a professional philosopher and an experienced university examiner in metaphysics, known for writing exam questions that cannot be answered by reciting textbook material. I am setting the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the metaphysics of dispositions, and I need you to design one essay question, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. Before writing, think carefully about where students' understanding of this material actually breaks down: begin with a few lines of brainstorming identifying the two or three confusions the question should be built to expose, and then design the question around them. Write everything in American English.

Background the exam may presuppose: the seminar has covered the simple conditional analysis of dispositions (roughly: x is disposed to manifest M in response to stimulus S if and only if x would M if S occurred), and the two standard families of counterexamples to it — finks (cases in which the stimulus, or the process leading up to it, removes the disposition or its causal basis, so that the manifestation fails even though the object was genuinely disposed) and masks (cases in which the disposition and its basis remain in place but some interfering factor blocks the manifestation, as when a fragile glass survives a fall because it is wrapped in protective packing). Students have also encountered antidote-style cases, in which the causal basis is left intact but its characteristic operation is counteracted as it unfolds, and a reformed conditional analysis according to which having a disposition is a matter of possessing an intrinsic causal basis that, if retained, would produce the manifestation under the stimulus.

The essay question should be built around the following scenario, which you should incorporate into the question text:

The Stabilizer. A ceramic vase is fitted with a micro-device that continuously monitors its molecular bonds. Whenever an impact begins to stress those bonds toward the breaking point, the device instantaneously reinforces the bonds exactly where, and to exactly the degree, needed, so the vase never breaks. Unlike a classic fink, the device never removes or replaces the vase's molecular structure; unlike ordinary packing, it does not absorb the blow or prevent the stress from reaching the bonds — the full impact registers in the vase's structure, and the device cancels its effects from within, bond by bond, as they arise.

Requirements for the question itself:

- It must ask students to do three things: (a) determine whether the Stabilizer case is a fink, a mask, an antidote, or none of these, and say whether the vase, as fitted, is fragile; (b) argue for what the case shows about whether the fink/mask distinction marks a genuine metaphysical difference (disposition lost versus disposition retained but blocked) or is merely a difference in how we happen to describe interference; and (c) state the consequence of their verdict for conditional analyses — whether the Stabilizer is a counterexample to the simple analysis, to the reformed analysis, to both, or to neither.
- The question must not be answerable by reciting the definitions of "fink" and "mask" and slotting the case under a label. Part of the point is that the Stabilizer has features of more than one category, and a good answer must diagnose why classifying it is difficult rather than simply classify it.

Then provide the following:

1. A model answer of about 600–700 words, written at the level of a strong student working under exam conditions (roughly fifty minutes of writing time). The model answer should take a definite position rather than survey possibilities, while acknowledging the strongest consideration on the other side.

2. A marking scheme out of 40 marks. The scheme must: allocate marks across the three tasks above; name the specific moves an A-grade answer must contain that a B-grade answer will typically lack (for example, noticing that the Stabilizer interferes with the operation of the causal basis without removing the basis itself, and drawing the right moral from that fact); and name at least four specific errors, each with a stated mark penalty, that the scheme is designed to catch (for example: treating every blocker of a manifestation as a mask; assuming the Stabilizer must be a fink merely because it is internal to the object; asserting that the vase straightforwardly loses its fragility without addressing the sense in which its molecular structure is unchanged; and treating the reformed conditional analysis as untouched without asking whether a basis that is retained still "would produce" the manifestation under the stimulus).

Finally, check the model answer against your own marking scheme and confirm, in a sentence or two, that it would earn full marks, revising it if it would not.
