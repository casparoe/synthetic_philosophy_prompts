Write the joint statement that could come out of a good-faith adversarial collaboration between an appraisal theorist of love's reasons (love is called for by the beloved's genuinely valuable qualities) and a bestowalist in Frankfurt's camp (love confers value on its object rather than finding it there). I want: the claims both sides can actually sign, including ones people usually assume are contested but aren't; the residual disagreements, each worded so its own side would accept the formulation; the cruxes; and for each crux, a case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Run both camps through at least these stress cases: love that survives discovering your beliefs about the beloved's qualities were false, "trading up" when a funnier and more accomplished partner comes along, whether a parent can love a newborn *for its qualities*, and whether the beloved could be swapped for a perfect duplicate without loss. Also: some of this debate is probably just the two sides using "reasons" differently (normative vs. motivating vs. justifying) -- flag disagreements that are terminological separately instead of laundering them into the real disputes. Format: numbered agreed claims first, then a disagreements table, then the cruxes with the recorded predictions.
