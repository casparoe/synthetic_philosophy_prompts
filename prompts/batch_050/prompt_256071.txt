I got into an arguement with my friend after our school gave out its annual awards. He said grades don’t really show who deserves recognition because some students have tutors, quiet houses, parents who help, and probably even genes that make concentrating easier. So he thinks a fair merit system would have to estimate everyone’s “achievement above advantage,” meaning a C student who overcame a lot might deserve the award more than an A student who had everything easy. That already sounded impossible to measure, but then I said the bigger problem is that effort is luck too: nobody chooses their personality, self-control, motivation, or whether they were raised to care about school. If even trying hard comes from causes outside us, doesn’t real meritocracy end in the conclusion that nobody deserves anything more than anyone else? At that point, shouldn’t scholarships and jobs just be assigned by lottery?

My friend said I was mixing together several ideas of desert, but I don’t really see it. Please explain where my argument goes wrong. In particular, does “nobody ultimately deserves their abilities or effort” actually imply that nobody can deserve a scholarship, prize, or job more than anyone else? I’d like you to distinguish being a morally better person from being the right candidate according to an institution’s legitimate purpose, and to explain whether a lottery could ever count as meritocratic or whether it is just a completely different principle of fairness.
