I'm prepping my upper-level seminar on animal ethics and moral status (14 undergrads, 80-minute slot) and I need an activity for the week on whether sentience comes in degrees. Here's the thing I want them to feel in their bones: everyone is perfectly happy saying moral status is a gradient -- fruit flies matter a little, mice more, chimps more still -- right up until they have to act on it, because protection is lumpy. You either inspect the lab or you dont; you either ban the procedure or you don't. So I want a game where a sliding scale of moral status collides with binary decisions under a fixed budget.

Design it roughly along these lines, but improve on my sketch wherever it's weak: students play a campus welfare committee with a limited pool of protection units to distribute across a set of "clients" -- I'm thinking a fruit fly colony, an octopus, a mouse colony, a human brain organoid, and a reinforcement-learning climate control system that's very good at predicting and preempting building occupants' discomfort. Round one they rank the clients by moral status with no resource constraint, round two the budget only covers ~60% of what protecting everything would cost, and round three new "evidence" cards revise two of the clients' sentience estimates (one up, one down) while the budget gets cut again. Give me the actual rules, any scoring or payoff mechanics, a timeline with real timings, and the debrief plan.

The debrief should map specific moments in the game onto the positions they'll read the following week -- threshold views, scalar/gradualist views, and expected-value-of-moral-status reasoning for the uncertain cases -- and say where each position would have played the game differently. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong (e.g. students endlessly negotiating with each other instead of actually deciding, or treating the octopus as obviously maximal so they don't have to think) and how to handle each, plus an honest note on which feature of the real problem the activity can't reproduce.

One thing I really care about: the game should force them to notice that "X has 0.4 of the moral status of Y" and "X deserves 0.4 of the protection" are not the same claim, and that nothing in the gradient itself tells you how to convert one into the other. If the activity works, at least one group should find themselves unable to spend the budget at all without smuggling in a principle they never stated.
