I am a novelist working on a book whose narrator, after dreaming with complete sensory conviction that she is someone else entirely, can no longer take her waking identity on trust. For a set of dinner-table dialogues, I want to give each guest a distinct and defensible stance, so please write me a decision tree that sorts the positions one might hold on dream skepticism. Start from the concession 'while dreaming, I have been fully convinced of things that were false' and lead to named positions; the leaves must include at least the Zhuangzian parity of dreaming and waking, Cartesian radical doubt, a common-sense refusal of the skeptical inference, a view on which dreams are imaginings rather than beliefs and therefore cannot deceive, and contextualism about when dream-doubts are legitimate. Each branch node should be a question answerable by someone who does not yet know which position it leads to — for instance, whether one can genuinely hold beliefs while asleep, or whether the inference from dream to waking is symmetric with the inference from waking to dream. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate, and note any coherent combination of answers that no one seems to occupy, with a one-line diagnosis of why that seat is empty. The whole instrument, tree and annotations together, must come in under 800 words.
