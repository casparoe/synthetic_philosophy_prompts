I've been running an informal philosophy of mind reading group with some grad students, and for three sessions now we've been stuck on the same argument and I can't tell if anyone is actually disagreeing about anything. It started with a thought experiment I love: imagine a two-level billiard table (Hofstadter's version has cute names for the pieces but I'll skip those). On the bottom level you have thousands of tiny marbles bouncing around, and their collisions are completely governed by micro-physics, full stop. On the top level, when you squint, clusters of marbles act like big billiard balls, and the big balls' trajectories line up with something meaningful -- say, the pattern of big-ball collisions spells out a computation, or encodes a conversation, the way neural firing patterns might encode thoughts. The two descriptions always agree. Nothing the big balls "do" ever violates or adds to the marble-level physics. The fight in my group is over whether the symbolic level -- the big balls qua symbols -- *causes* anything. One faction says of course it does: the best causal story of why the system ended up in state X at time t is told at the symbol level, and that's just what causation is. The other faction says this is a homuncular fallacy in a trench coat: every push happens at the marble level, and symbols are at best a useful bookkeeping device for observers, like saying the average taxpayer has 2.3 kids.

What bugs me is that both sides cheerfully accept all the same facts -- same physics, same patterns, same predictions -- and then draw opposite conclusions and call each other confused. My working hypothesis is that this is really the old causal exclusion problem wearing new clothes, and that the whole thing turns on what you're willing to count as a cause: if you buy an interventionist account where causation is just about which level of description supports the most stable counterfactuals, symbols cause things basically by definition, and if you buy a more production-y, oomph-requiring notion, they can't. So the "debate" might just be two rival analyses of the word "cause" talking past each other while gesturing at a table of marbles. But I'm not fully sure, because the anti-symbol side keeps insisting they have a *metaphysical* point and not a terminological one, and I can't get them to say what observable or even conceivable difference it would make if they were right.

So: diagnose this disagreement for me. Is it substantive, verbal, or a case of two camps answering different questions (one about explanation, one about ontology) under one heading? Use what each side actually says in this setup to support the diagnosis rather than just surveying positions, and stress-test my interventionism-does-all-the-work hypothesis against the strongest version of the other side. Also tell me what would count as progress here: a distinction, an argument, an empirical result from actual neuroscience or AI, or should I just accept that this one never resolves and tell my reading group to move on? Please write your answer in American English, and don't bother with citations or a bibliography -- I want the reasoning, not a reading list.
