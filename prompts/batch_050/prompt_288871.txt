So a couple months ago I had a health scare (turned out fine, but the waiting-for-results part rearranged my head a bit) and in the aftermath I ended up reading Nietzsche, and the eternal recurrence thing is the one idea that actually stuck. The setup where a demon shows up and asks whether you'd want to live this exact life again, every moment of it, forever. Everyone treats it as a vibe or an attitude, but I want to know if it can be made into an actual decision procedure I can run on real choices, and if not I want to know exactly where and why it breaks.

Context on me: I'm 38, I make maybe ten or fifteen medium-sized decisions a year (whether to take a job, whether to move cities, whether to keep investing in a friendship that's gone weird) plus the usual daily small stuff. I can give a decision maybe 20 focused minutes, not a journaling retreat. What I keep finding when I try to use the test raw is that "could I will this to repeat eternally?" quietly turns into "do I like the sound of this?" which is a different and much worse question. Also it seems to operate differently on (a) choices I control, (b) outcomes I didn't choose and have to live with (which is more the amor fati territory), and (c) moods and passing states -- and I suspect a good procedure handles those three differently. There's also the obvious problem that if everything repeats infinitely then trivial stuff gets infinite weight, which can't be what the test is for, but I can't articulate the fix.

So: build me the procedure. Steps in order, each with the actual question I'm supposed to ask myself (worded so it doesn't collapse into "does this feel good"), how long to spend, and the known way that step fails. Then a section on where the whole thing degenerates into something more familiar -- regret-minimization, "live each day like it's your last," plain preference ranking -- and what specifically to do to keep it distinct from each of those. Then apply it to two of my actual live cases: (1) whether to take a clearly better-paying job in a city I actively dislike, and (2) whether to have a long-overdue ugly conversation with my brother. Make at least one of the applications come out with a verdict I'd rather not hear, if that's where it honestly goes. And mark the points where no procedure can substitute for judgment instead of pretending.

After the prose, give me a JSON summary with this schema:
- "steps": array of objects, each with "name", "question_to_ask", "time_budget_minutes", "failure_mode"
- "collapse_risks": array of objects with "familiar_idea", "how_the_test_degrades_into_it", "safeguard"
- "worked_cases": array of objects with "case", "verdict", "uncomfortable" (true/false)
- "judgment_calls": array of strings, the points where the procedure can't decide for me

Don't quote Nietzsche at me, I've read the passage, I want the engineering.
