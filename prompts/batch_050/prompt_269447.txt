And here is the student's answer, verbatim:

"Dennett's term 'Cartesian theater' names the assumption that there is a single place in the brain where all sensory information arrives and is presented to consciousness, like a screen watched by an inner observer. The term refers to Descartes' substance dualism: the pineal gland was Descartes' candidate for where mind and body interact, and the Cartesian theater is essentially the dualist picture dressed in modern vocabulary. Dennett's objection is that a theater requires a homunculus to watch it, and that homunculus would itself need a theater, generating an infinite regress. His multiple drafts model replaces the theater with distributed, parallel processing: many 'drafts' of narrative content are continuously produced across the brain, revised, and competed over, with no single draft becoming official by being displayed in a privileged location. A content becomes conscious by achieving what Dennett calls 'fame in the brain'—influencing behavior, memory, and report—rather than by entering a theater. The color phi phenomenon illustrates this. When two differently colored spots are flashed in succession at different locations, subjects report seeing a single spot move and change color midway. How can the subject experience the color change mid-trajectory before the second spot has appeared? Dennett uses this to show that the Stalinesque interpretation is correct: the brain fabricates a coherent narrative after the fact, filling in the intermediate frames before the experience occurs, like a show trial where the verdict precedes the evidence. The Orwellian alternative—where the subject experiences the first spot veridically and memory is then rewritten—would only make sense if there were a determinate moment when experience 'happened' in a theater. Because the multiple drafts model denies that there is any such canonical moment, it follows on Dennett's view that there is no fact of the matter about exactly when the experience occurred, only various contents achieving temporary influence."
