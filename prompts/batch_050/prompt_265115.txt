Write a short story of roughly 800–1,200 words set in a near-future city that operates an institution called the Quiet Office. Its function is simple and legal: any resident may request "the Quiet," an instant, painless, irreversible end to their existence, administered after a thirty-day reflection period. The Office was founded on an explicitly suffering-focused principle: that what ultimately matters morally is not how much joy exists in the world but how much suffering, and that the surest way to honor that principle is to let anyone exit who judges their own balance of misery to be irremediable.

The story should center on two employees of the Office. The first is a senior administrator who has processed thousands of requests and believes, with genuine conviction, that the Office is the most compassionate institution ever built. The second is a younger intake officer who, over the course of the story, confronts three cases that each crack the administrator's certainty in a different way: a terminally ill patient for whom the request seems obviously humane; a healthy, financially secure, apparently content person whose stated reason is a low-grade but unrelenting sense of pointlessness that no treatment has touched; and a couple who request the Quiet together, explicitly describing it as a joyful act rather than an escape from suffering.

Use these cases to dramatize, without lecturing, some of the hardest questions facing a suffering-focused ethic: whether preventing suffering can justify removing a life that still contains goods; whether mild, chronic, "ordinary" suffering deserves the same moral urgency as agony; whether the value of ending one person's pain can be separated from the grief their exit inflicts on others; and whether a policy designed to reduce suffering could, if taken to its logical limit, point toward a world with no one left in it at all — and what, if anything, is wrong with that conclusion.

The story must be driven by concrete scenes and dialogue rather than exposition, should include at least one vivid detail about the bureaucratic texture of the Quiet Office (its forms, rituals, or architecture), and should end without resolving the underlying question — the final scene should leave the reader genuinely unsure which character, if either, is right.
