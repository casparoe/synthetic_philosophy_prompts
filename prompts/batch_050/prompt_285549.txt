I'm a PhD student in machine learning working on side-effect penalties for reinforcement learning agents, and I sit on a small reading group that vets proposed impact-measure designs. I'd like you to predict our verdict on a new proposal from our track record, and to make the reasoning behind that prediction explicit.

Our verdicts so far:

1. Penalize any deviation, under a fixed state-distance metric, from the state that would have occurred had the agent never acted. Rejected.
2. Penalize only decreases in the agent's attainable utility, measured against the counterfactual in which the agent takes no-op actions from the current point onward (recalculated at each step). Approved.
3. Penalize both increases and decreases in attainable utility against that same recalculated no-op counterfactual. Rejected.
4. Enumerate in advance a list of permitted outcome types and heavily penalize everything else. Rejected.

The new proposal: penalize only decreases in attainable utility, but measure them against the state of the environment at deployment, held fixed, rather than against the recalculated no-op counterfactual.

Please structure your answer in four sections, in this order: "Predicted verdict" (two or three sentences, committing to approve or reject); "Candidate rules" (at most a page — state at least two general rules, each consistent with all four prior verdicts, that diverge on the new proposal, and say which you find more plausible given the record); "Closest precedent" (a short paragraph identifying which earlier verdict does the most work and why); and "The case that would settle it" (one short paragraph describing a further proposal whose verdict would discriminate between your candidate rules).

If you think the verdicts are underdetermined in a way that makes any prediction arbitrary, or that the framing of the question is confused, say so directly rather than manufacturing confidence.
