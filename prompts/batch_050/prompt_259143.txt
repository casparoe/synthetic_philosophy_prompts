I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on realism and anti-realism and I'm writing the final exam. I want one essay question on Fitch's knowability paradox that students cannot answer by reproducing the derivation from the distribution and factivity axioms. The question should force them to explain why the argument is a threat specifically to the moderate anti-realist claim that all truths are knowable, rather than to epistemic conceptions of truth generally, and it should make them adjudicate between the two standard escape routes: restricting the knowability principle to truths whose being known is consistent (Tennant's proposal), and weakening the background logic (for example, by refusing distribution of knowledge over conjunction, or by moving to intuitionistic logic). The exam gives 45 minutes for this question, worth 30 marks. Write the question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain. These should include: noticing that the derivation concludes that every truth is actually known, not merely that some truth is unknowable; explaining why dismissing the paradox as a curiosity about iterated modalities fails, since the premises make no use of any exotic modal principle; and identifying at least one concrete cost of whichever escape route the answer endorses — for instance, that Tennant's restriction is arguably ad hoc and severs the link between anti-realism and verification, while logical revision has to be motivated independently of the desire to block this one result.
