So I run a phil-of-bio reading group and last week we did the cranes and skyhooks chapter of Dennett's Darwin's Dangerous Idea. As a warmup I tried to sharpen his distinction into a formal argument, and now I think I've accidentally proven that no explanation of anything is possible. Which is absurd, so something's off, but I can't find the step. Here's the argument:

(1) On Dennett's picture, a legitimate explanation of design has to be a crane, i.e. built from mindless, motiveless, algorithmic processes, and a skyhook is any appeal to a mind-first force or miracle that lifts complexity into existence without having been built up itself.
(2) So a genuine explanation of complexity can't leave its own lifting power unexplained -- if the thing doing the explaining is itself unaccounted for, it's a skyhook.
(3) Natural selection only explains adaptation given background conditions it doesn't itself explain: heredity, variation, differential fitness, and underneath all that the laws of chemistry and physics.
(4) Either those background conditions and laws have a further mindless explanation, or they don't.
(5) If they don't, the crane bottoms out in an unexplained lifter, which by (2) is just a skyhook with the mind taken out of it.
(6) If they do, that further explanation has its own unexplained background conditions, so we get either an infinite regress or, eventually, a brute stop -- which lands us back at (5).
(7) An infinite regress explains nothing, and a brute stop is disallowed by (2).
(8) So Darwinism, Dennett's paradigm crane, is either explanatorily empty or secretly skyhook-dependent. And since the argument doesn't depend on anything specific to biology, the same goes for physics and everything else. Nothing explains anything.

Now, Dennett opens Part I of the book with this passage from Quine, which I suspect is doing more work than my argument allows:

"Analyze theory-building how we will, we all must start in the middle. Our conceptual firsts are middle-sized, middle-distance objects, and our introduction to them and to everything comes midway in the cultural evolution of the race."

Tell me which step I should reject, why that step looked fine, and what rejecting it costs me -- including what exactly Quine's point licenses and what it doesn't. If I have to choose, I'd rather keep the ban on skyhooks than accept that nothing explains anything, but I want to know what I'm paying either way.

One more thing: tell me where your diagnosis is most likely to be wrong, i.e. what consideration or objection would flip your verdict and make the regress charge stick after all.
