Spinoza insists in Part I of the Ethics that nothing in nature is contingent: everything follows from the necessity of the divine nature (E1p29), and things are called contingent only insofar as we are ignorant of the causes that determine them (E1p33s1). Suppose he were brought up to speed on the Everett or many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, on which the universal wave function evolves deterministically and every outcome of a measurement occurs on some branch. Would he take Everett to vindicate his necessitarianism, or to abolish contingency in the wrong way — given that his claim is not that everything possible happens but that only one order of things was ever possible? Second, many Everettians interpret pre-measurement uncertainty as self-locating ignorance about which branch one will occupy; would Spinoza's doctrine that probability merely measures our ignorance entitle him to that move, and would it solve the Born-rule problem — why should uncertainty track the squared amplitudes when every outcome occurs? — or only redescribe it? Finally, would adequate understanding of the universal wave function constitute, for Spinoza, the knowledge of things sub specie aeternitatis in which the intellectual love of God consists (E5p36), or is a mathematical object of physics structurally the wrong kind of thing to play the role of God or Nature? In answering, argue from the premises of his own system rather than from a generic rationalist temperament, and flag where the two frameworks genuinely resist assimilation.
