Consider this fictional but concrete practice. A licensed continuity clinic offers gradual neural replacement: over three to seven years, tiny regions of a patient’s cortex and hippocampus are substituted with synthetic tissue that is individually trained against the patient’s ongoing activity, so no single operation removes more than a small fraction of the person’s neurons. Patients receive a Continuity Certificate that lets them keep legal identity, property, marriage, insurance status, and priority for future life-extension. To keep the certificate valid, they must pass weekly continuity checks: autobiographical recall, preference stability, long-term plans, emotional response profiles, and counterfactual probes asking what they would do in situations drawn from their baseline personality. The clinic also keeps a dormant whole-brain scan as insurance, but promises never to run it unless biological continuity is catastrophically interrupted. Its brochures say gradualness guarantees survival, yet its consent forms add that if a patient’s divergence budget is exceeded before replacement is complete, the clinic may pause treatment and treat the later person as a successor beneficiary rather than the original patient.

What view of personal identity does this institution presuppose? Reconstruct its implicit theory precisely: does it treat survival as biological continuity, psychological connectedness, a closest-continuer relation, narrative authorship, a pattern- or information-based account, an animalist insistence on the living organism, or some layered hybrid? Which rival views does the practice quietly rule out, and which parts of it would still be licensed by several competing theories? Pay special attention to three tensions: identity is treated as binary for law and insurance but scalar for clinical monitoring; first-person reports are used as evidence of continuity until they conflict with the baseline, after which they may be discounted as confabulation; and the dormant scan is forbidden as a replacement yet retained as a metaphysical backstop. Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, identify where it is philosophically underdetermined, and say what would have to change in its rules if the board replaced the presupposed theory with animalism, with a psychological-continuity view, or with a view on which duplication destroys the original no matter how gradual the process is.
