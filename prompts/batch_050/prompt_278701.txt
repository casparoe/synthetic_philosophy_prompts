So I've been chewing on Benatar's asymmetry argument for antinatalism, and I don't actually buy the standard version, but I've ended up defending a modified position and I want to stress-test it. Here's the position: I think the asymmetry (pain of the never-existent is not bad because there's no one for whom it's bad; absence of pleasure in the never-existent is not bad either, but crucially the presence of pain for someone who does exist counts against existence while absence of pleasure for them doesn't count for it in the same way) only gets its grip when applied to whole lives evaluated at the moment of the decision to procreate, not when applied moment-by-moment or to individual experiences within a life. So I'm basically saying the asymmetry is a decision-procedure-level fact about creating people, not a general axiological principle. I'd also add that it can't be extended to arguments about whether already-existing people should continue to exist -- I think that extension is where the view goes wrong.
