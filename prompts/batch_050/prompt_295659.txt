I've been chewing on the Browning–Goldhagen dispute again, and what strikes me is that both sides build their case from essentially the same historical record — same battalions, same opt-outs, same compliance patterns — which makes me suspect the evidence radically underdetermines the theories. I want to know whether the disagreement is resolvable in principle or just a verbal dispute about where "situation" ends and "culture" begins. So: construct the discriminating case the actual record never gave us. Treat this as designing an ideal experiment for historiography.

Design a single fictional but realistic episode — a mid-twentieth-century-style occupation or counterinsurgency staffed by ordinary conscripts, no speculative devices — containing two units that are indistinguishable in their present environment (same orders, same briefings and propaganda exposure, same peer visibility, same stated and actual cost of refusal) and differ only in whether they were raised in a culture saturated with the eliminationist ideology. Then add whatever second contrast is needed so the episode also tests the other direction (same upbringing, varied situational pressure), or argue that one contrast already does the work. Keep the scenario itself to a paragraph or so.

Then do the bookkeeping: give the participation pattern a Browning-style situational account predicts, the pattern a Goldhagen-style ideological-dispositional account predicts, and the single observation that would discriminate between them. Name the one design feature carrying the discriminating load and say which confound in the real record it removes — the apparently low historical cost of stepping out is worth thinking through carefully here. Finally, take whichever side your case cuts against and give its strongest counter-move — the obvious one being that ideology is itself transmitted situationally, which would make your contrast ill-posed — and say whether the case survives it.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the core design of the scenario and what it would show — then give the scenario and the analysis, with no preamble and no restating of the brief.
