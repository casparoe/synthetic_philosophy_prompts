Yesterday evening I had a long and unresolved argument with a friend — a physicist with ontic structural realist sympathies — about where I stand in the scientific realism debate, and before I commit the position to paper I would like it audited for internal consistency. My commitments, as I defended them over the course of the evening, are these:

(a) Epistemic structural realism, roughly in Worrall's sense: mature scientific theories capture the relational structure of the unobservable world, but the intrinsic natures of whatever bears that structure are in principle closed to us.

(b) The Newman objection — the worry that the claim that the world instantiates a given relational structure is satisfiable by any collection of objects of the right cardinality, and therefore cannot be the substantive content of a scientific theory — does not defeat (a). Purely formal structure is indeed cheap, but structural realist claims concern physically instantiated structure, and instantiation is what gives the notion its content.

(c) The pessimistic meta-induction is a genuine problem for realism about theoretical entities, but not for my view, because what is retained across revolutionary theory change is precisely structure — the canonical case being Fresnel's equations surviving, suitably reinterpreted, within Maxwell's electrodynamics.

(d) Here I parted ways with my friend: I deny that there can be relations without relata. There must be entities standing in the structural relations, even if their natures are unknowable by us. A world consisting of pure structure with nothing being structured strikes me as unintelligible.

(e) I take the no-miracles argument to be the principal motivation for the whole position: the predictive and instrumental success of mature science would be miraculous unless its structural claims were at least approximately true.

One moment from the argument has stayed with me. When I gave the reply to Newman described in (b), my friend claimed I had thereby conceded his side of (d) — that once instantiation is what does the work, my unknowable relata are idle, and I should either embrace his relations-without-relata picture or admit some access to natures after all. I am fairly sure he is wrong, but I could not articulate exactly why.

So: do (a) through (e) hang together? Please tell me which pairs of commitments are in real tension, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest revision would be in each case; identify any tension that is merely apparent and explain why it dissolves on inspection; and rank the genuine problems by severity rather than treating them as equally threatening. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — your overall verdict on the package — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
