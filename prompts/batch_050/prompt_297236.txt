I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether movies can actually do philosophy of mind rather than just gesture at it. I want to read Being John Malkovich through Thomas Metzinger's self-model theory -- the idea that the self you experience is a model your brain builds, and that it's "transparent", meaning you don't experience it as a model, you just look through it and take yourself to simply be the person. The film feels like a gift for this: Craig crawls through the portal, ends up behind Malkovich's eyes, and the story keeps shifting between him being a passenger, a puppeteer, and gradually dissolving into Malkovich. So my question: does the film actually dramatize a transparent self-model, or does the whole portal conceit depend on transparency failing, since Craig stays aware the entire time that this is someone else's experience -- which is exactly the state Metzinger thinks we basically can't be in? Where does the film line up with the theory and where does it cheat? And please flag where your reading is most likley to go wrong -- what would change your verdict on whether the film genuinely engages the theory or just borrows its spooky vibes?
