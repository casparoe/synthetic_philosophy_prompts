I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, and my reading group has been stuck on the preface paradox for like three sessions now. My version of it: I wrote a long design doc, I stand behind every single sentence when you ask me about them one at a time, and I'm also completly certain at least one sentence in it is wrong. One camp says fine, rational belief just isn't closed under conjunction -- believing each of p1...pn is rational, believing the big conjunction isn't, end of story. The other camp says the whole paradox is an artifact of the word "belief": once you only allow degrees of confidence (0.99 for each claim, tiny for the conjunction) there's nothing left to explain, and talking about outright belief was the mistake all along. A third guy thinks rationality can straight up require inconsistency here and we should just accept that. Nobody has budged an inch. My suspicion is that the first two camps aren't actually disagreeing about what rationality requires, but about whether "outright belief" is even a real state distinct from high confidence -- i.e. they're arguing about what the topic is, not about a thesis. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a genuine question here that one side is simply wrong about? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a recap of positions, plus an honest answer about what would actually move the debate -- a distinction, an argument, something formal -- or why nothing will.
