In Gödel, Escher, Bach, Hofstadter presents Gödel's construction as a model for the emergence of mind: a formal system of meaningless symbols, once rich enough to encode statements about itself, produces a sentence that is simultaneously a string of marks manipulated by rules and a statement about its own provability. Selfhood, on this picture, is the same trick played at larger scale — a strange loop in which moving up through levels of description (neurons, symbols, thoughts) returns you to where you began, and the "I" is the loop rather than anything at its base.

Write a villanelle about this emergence of self from self-reference, in which the form does the philosophical work rather than merely decorating it. Specifically: the two refrains should each admit a double reading, the way Gödel's sentence is at once an object in the system and a claim about the system — one refrain readable both as a description of mechanical symbol-shuffling with nobody home and as a first-person assertion, the other readable both as a claim about level-crossing and as the self noticing itself. Their compulsory recurrence through the tercets should enact the strange loop: each time the poem seems to have climbed to a higher vantage point, the refrain pulls it back to where it started. In the closing quatrain, where the refrains finally stand side by side as a couplet, their adjacency should stage the meeting of the two levels without resolving the question of whether the "I" is something over and above the symbols or nothing but their pattern. End with the loop intact, not with a verdict.

The poem should be nineteen lines in traditional villanelle structure, and accurate enough that a reader who knows the Gödelian argument will recognize the construction — meaning arising from meaningless manipulation, self-reference achieved through encoding rather than magic — while a reader who doesn't should still find a good poem.
