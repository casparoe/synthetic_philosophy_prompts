I'm writing a problem set for a phil of science seminar and need some help. Write the list of desiderata an account of verisimilitude (closeness-to-the-truth) has to meet if its job is to let a scientific realist say that science makes progress toward the truth even though our best theories are very likely false. For each desideratum give the consideration or toy case that motivates it, and mark whether it's genuinely non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp in the debate. At a minimum it should cover: whether an account can order theories that are all strictly false (the earliest definitions in this area notoriously collapsed on exactly this point), whether the ordering survives redescribing the same world in a different but logically equivalent vocabulary (the classic language-dependence objection), and whether it can handle idealized models that are known to be false on purpose, like frictionless planes, without either calling them worthless or pretending they're true. Also make the list answer to a case I've been using with students: two broken clocks, one exactly 12 hours slow so it's right twice a day, one exactly one minute slow so it's never right -- an adequate account should either say something principled about which is closer to the truth and why, or explain why that question is ill-posed. If some subset of the desiderata turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, say so, that's honestly the part I care about most. Then end with a note on which single desideratum does the most work in ruling out candidate accounts. Don't bother scoring the big existing theories of truthlikeness against the list, we'll do that in class.
