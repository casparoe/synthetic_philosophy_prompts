So I want to dump my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and find out whether there's actually a coherent principle under them or whether I'm just inconsistent. Here they are:

- A concert pianist who can't articulate anything about finger placement or music theory still knows how to play. Obvious, I think.
- A physicist who has spent her career on the biomechanics and fluid dynamics of swimming, but who sinks immediately in a pool, does not know how to swim, even though she knows more true propositions about swimming than almost anyone.
- A beginner cook who follows a recipe and flukes a perfect soufflé on his first try does not know how to make a soufflé. Doing it once by luck isn't the same as knowing how.
- But then: a retired gymnast with a spinal injury who physically cannot do a backflip anymore — I strongly want to say she still knows how to do a backflip. My roommate says this clashes with the soufflé verdict if knowing how is basically having the reliable ability, and I can't see my way out of that.
- An amnesiac who's forgotten every fact he ever learned but can still ride a bike: still knows how to ride a bike.
- A chess engine that crushes everyone but (arguably) has no beliefs or understanding at all: I genuinely flip-flop. Half the time it clearly knows how to play chess, half the time calling it that feels like a metaphor.

Is there one account of knowing how that delivers all six verdicts, or something close?
