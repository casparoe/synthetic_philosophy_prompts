Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of verisimilitude must meet, with the case, theorem, or intuitive judgment motivating each one.

Please include at least the following, and add any I have missed: the requirement that the theory deliver non-trivial comparisons between false theories (e.g., that Newtonian mechanics comes out closer to the truth than Aristotelian physics, even though both are strictly false); the requirement that logically equivalent formulations receive the same verisimilitude; the requirement that the rankings not flip under a trivial change of primitive vocabulary, which is the pressure point of Miller's translation argument; the requirement that the theory say something sensible about idealized models whose parameters are strictly false but structurally apt, such as frictionless planes or infinite-population models in population genetics, rather than treating all idealizations as on a par; and the requirement that adding a true conjunct to a false theory can increase verisimilitude, which is exactly where Popper's own consequence-based definition provably fails.

Identify which single requirement does the most work in excluding currently defended accounts. Close with a brief scorecard of the main contender families against the list, but keep it short; the criteria and their motivating cases are the deliverable, not the verdicts.
