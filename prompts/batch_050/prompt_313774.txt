As one of five members of my company's engineering review board, I've been assigned to draft our written opinion on a contested disciplinary case, and I'd like you to write it for me. The facts: during a documented code freeze, an engineer — call her R. — manually pushed a change to production and triggered a four-hour outage. That much is undisputed. The complication surfaced in the postmortem: a misconfigured scheduled job was set to auto-deploy that exact commit six hours later, and everyone who has examined it agrees it would have caused the same outage. The job never actually ran — the deploy system was halted during the incident and the job was disabled before its window — but it was live, armed, and would have fired. So the outage looks to have been unavoidable: had R. scrupulously observed the freeze, customers would have been hit anyway.

The board is split. Two members argue that disciplining R. for the outage is flatly unfair: no one can be responsible for an outcome they were powerless to prevent. Two argue that R. made exactly the same culpable choice as someone whose deploy was genuinely avoidable, and the backup failure waiting in the wings is mere luck that shouldn't excuse her — she broke the freeze knowingly, and that is what discipline attaches to. For comparison, another engineer received a formal reprimand last quarter for an identical freeze violation in a case where no such job existed. Meanwhile R.'s advocate on the board points to our blameless postmortem culture and says any discipline here betrays it.

Draft the opinion, about a page. It needs a clear bottom line — discipline, no discipline, or some principled carve-up such as sanctioning the violation but not the outage — with the reasoning behind it. Address what R. is properly answerable for (her decision, the outage itself, the risk she created), whether she and last quarter's engineer should be treated alike, and how whatever you recommend coheres with blameless postmortems. Present the losing side's strongest argument seriously enough that they'd recognize their own position in the document. It will be attached to the case file and read by R.'s manager and HR.
