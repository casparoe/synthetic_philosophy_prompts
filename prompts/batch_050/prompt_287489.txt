The interpretations of probability I know, roughly: classical (equally likely cases, à la Laplace), logical/evidential (probability as a degree of entailment between propositions, Keynes/Carnap style), finite frequentism (actual ratio of outcomes in actual trials), hypothetical frequentism (limiting relative frequency in an infinite sequence that never actually happens), subjective/Bayesian (degrees of belief, cashed out via betting behavior and coherence), and single-case propensity (a dispositional tendency of the experimental setup itself).

Is there another genuinely different interpretation I'm missing? Not a hybrid of these (like "frequentism but with Bayes on top") and not a technical fix within one camp (like switching from Kolmogorov to Popper functions), but a different basic answer to what probability *is*. If you think there is one, state it precisely enough that I could tell it apart from the six above, give the strongest argument for it, and say which of the standard objections to the ones on my list it dodges and which new problems it creates for itself. If you think the list is actually exhaustive and every apparent seventh option collapses into one of these, argue for that instead. Answer in American English.
