I sit on the fundraising committee of a small charity, and we keep arguing about where legitimate persuasion ends and manipulation begins. Our rulings so far:

- Approved: a letter setting out accurate figures for what each £10 donation buys.
- Approved: an appeal telling the true, distressing story of one named beneficiary, chosen precisely because it is more moving than statistics.
- Rejected: an appeal claiming that a matching donor would double every gift when no such donor existed.
- Rejected: a door-to-door fundraiser trained not to accept a first refusal, although nothing he said was false.
- Rejected: an appeal deliberately sent to the recently bereaved, with addresses gathered from death notices.

We must now rule on a new proposal: an online donation form on which the box for a recurring monthly gift is pre-ticked. Every figure on the page is accurate, the pre-tick is clearly visible, and unticking the box takes a single click.

Please tell me how we should rule if we are to be consistent with ourselves. Identify the two most plausible principles that would each explain all five earlier rulings, say what each principle would decide about the pre-ticked box, give your best guess as to which principle the committee is really applying and why, and describe one further tactic on which the two principles would deliver opposite verdicts. At most 400 words.
