A colleague in the poli sci department cornered me at a faculty party last week and summarized the so-called paradox of deontology like this: 'Deontology tells you not to violate people's rights even when violating one right would prevent more violations of the very same kind — you may not kill one innocent person to stop someone else from killing five. But that makes the view self-defeating: it forbids you from minimizing violations of the rights it claims to take so seriously. So deontologists turn out to care more about keeping their own hands clean than about the rights themselves. The only consistent way out is to become a consequentialist about rights and just minimize violations.' I nodded along at the time, but something nags at me now. How fair is this, actually? Which parts would Nozick or Scheffler accept, where is he conflating claims that should be kept apart (is 'paradoxical' the same as 'self-defeating'? is the constraint really about the agent's hands being clean?), and does his punchline — that the puzzle forces you into rights-consequentialism — survive once the summary is corrected?
