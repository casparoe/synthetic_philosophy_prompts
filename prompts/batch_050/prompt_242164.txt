Okay so the strategy-stealing assumption in AI risk debates says, roughly, that for any strategy an unaligned AI could use to gain power, aligned humans/AI could in principle run the same strategy first and get the same benifits, so alignment doesn't put you at a strategic disadvantage. I'm skeptical this holds in general.

Construct a thought experiment where strategy-stealing fails: an unaligned AI has a power-seeking strategy that genuinely works, but an aligned agent (human institution or aligned AI) cannot copy it, even knowing the full strategy in advance. Constraints: no exotic physics or magic intelligence levels — the AI can be smart but the scenario should work via recognizable human-style mechanisms (deception, commitment, reputation, trust, that kind of thing). And don't make it trivial, e.g. don't just say "the strategy requires lying and the aligned agent is hardcoded to never lie" — the obstacle should be structural, not a stipulation.

After the scenario, explain: (1) which feature of the situation makes the strategy non-stealable, (2) whether removing that feature collapses the case back to where stealing works, and (3) how a defender of the strategy-stealing assumption would attack your case, and whether their counterattack succeeds.
