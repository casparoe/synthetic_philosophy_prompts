"Picture a lamp with a toggle switch. One minute before noon it's switched on, thirty seconds before noon it's switched off, fifteen seconds before, on again, and so on, halving the interval every time, so infinitely many toggles happen before noon. Thomson's point was that this setup is logically impossible, because at noon the lamp would have to be both on and off, which is a contradiction. So supertasks — completing infinitely many actions in a finite stretch of time — can't happen, not even in principle. And this is also really the answer to Zeno: Achilles never completes infinitely many steps, he just performs one continuous motion, so there was never a paradox to begin with. Physicists back this up, since nothing in nature can do infinitely many things, which is why the infinite divisibility of time is just a mathematical idealization."
