So I keep going around in circles on the nonidentity thing and I want to pin down what I actually think by going off my gut verdicts on cases. Here they are:

A couple is told that if they conceive this month, their kid will have a lifelong condition that means chronic pain a few days a month, manageable but real. If they wait two months for a medication to clear the mother's system, they'd have a different kid without it. They conceive now anyway. My gut: not ideal maybe, but they haven't wronged anyone.

Same couple, doing IVF, two viable embryos, one carries that same condition, one doesn't. They pick the one with the condition for no particular reason. My gut: clearly wrong, they should've picked the other one.

A government decides to burn through a resource bonanza now instead of conserving, and it's pointed out that either policy shifts travel, jobs, who meets whom, so completely different people get born under each. My gut: conserving is still the right call and depletion is still wrong, the identity stuff doesn't get them off the hook.

A woman takes a drug knowing that any baby conceived while on it will have a slightly shortened lifespan, and she'd rather not wait the six weeks it takes to leave her system. My gut: this one feels wrong, worse than the first case, even though I can see they're structured the same.

Nobody is obligated to have kids at all, but I also think that if you're going to have one, you haven't done anything wrong by having a kid with a good-but-not-amazing life when you could have arranged things to have a different kid with an amazing one.

So cases 1 and 2 and 4 are killing me, because 1 and 2 look like the same choice in different clothes and 1 and 4 look identical and I land differently on all three. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give something up? And tell me which of my verdicts you'd overturn first if you had to overturn one.
