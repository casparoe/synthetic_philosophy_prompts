So Adorno and Horkheimer get revived in 2025, given a smartphone and a couple months to poke around. Obviously they'd hate TikTok, that's the boring answer, so don't give it to me straight. What I actually want is a careful prediction of how they'd update (or refuse to update) the culture industry thesis from Dialectic of Enlightenment in light of a few specific things:

1. Short-form video feeds, where the "content" is largely made by consumers themselves, for free, often remixing corporate IP -- i.e. the audience now does the producing too, and pays in attention for the priviledge. Does this fit their framework (maybe better than 1940s radio did?) or does it break it? Would they see fan labor as pseudo-activity, as something like the hobbyist who reproduces the logic of work in his spare time, or as a genuinely new wrinkle they didn't anticipate?

2. The explosion of hyper-niche microgenres and subcultures -- people forming communities around incredibly specific aesthetic niches that no studio executive would ever have greenlit. Is this a counterexample to standardization, or would they argue the niches are just finer-grained pseudo-individuality, pre-formatted by the platform's affordances and recommendation system? Be honest here: I think this one genuinely cuts against a flatfooted reading of them, so steelman both directions.

3. Generative AI flooding streaming platforms with functional background music nobody really listens to. On the one hand this looks like the culture industry finally admitting the product never mattered. On the other hand, some of their sharpest claims are about how real art has to be difficult and resistant -- does AI slop actually confirm that, or does it show their whole high/low art distinction was doing the work?

Ground each prediction in their actual concepts (standardization, pseudo-individuality, style, amusement as the prolongation of work under late capitalism) rather than generic Frankfurty gloom. Flag where you're extrapolating vs. where the texts pretty much settle it. And for each of the three, name one observation that would have forced them to revise the theory rather than just apply it.
