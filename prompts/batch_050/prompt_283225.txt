So I'm a journalist, I cover science and ideas for a general audience, and I keep running into a pattern while reporting that I don't have a clean word for, and it's starting to bug me because I want to use the right term in print rather than mangle a technical one. Concrete cases, all from stories I've worked on or pitched in the last year:

1. Navigation apps. The app tells a bunch of drivers that route A is fastest. Enough of them take it that route A becomes slow. The app now flips and says route B, drivers slosh over there, B clogs, A clears, and the whole thing oscillates. The prediction keeps making itself false, then true again, on a lag.

2. A restaurant gets written up as an underrated hidden gem. It gets mobbed, the experience degrades, the next round of reviews say it's overrated, crowds thin out, and eventually it plausibly becomes a hidden gem again. Same sloshing.

3. Polls before an election say one candidate is a lock. Supporters stay home because it's a lock. Candidate loses. Next cycle the polls show a tight race, turnout spikes, and the result overshoots the other way.

What interests me is that in all three the description only works because the thing being described *reads* the description, and believing it is exactly what breaks it, but then disbelieving it fixes it again. I reread Gödel, Escher, Bach on a flight recently (long story) and I'm tempted to call these strange loops in Hofstadter's sense, but I'm not sure they qualify, since there isn't obviously a level-crossing where the system perceives itself, it's more like feedback through an information channel. I also know self-fulfilling prophecy is a thing, but my cases seem like the opposite -- self-defeating, and then back again, in a cycle, which feels like maybe a third thing.

So: does this specific oscillating setup have an established name anywhere -- economics, philosophy of social science, control theory, wherever? How does it relate to and differ from self-fulfilling prophecy, reflexivity, and performativity, and does it count as a strange loop or is that a stretch? Please don't just hand me a bare label -- I need to know exactly what the phenomenon is and isn't, with some boundary cases (e.g. is a plain boom-bust market cycle the same thing or not?). And if there's genuinely no established term for the oscillating version, coin one for me and define it tightly enough that I could put it in an article without embarrassing myself. definately don't want to coin something that already exists though, so be honest if I'm reinventing a wheel.
