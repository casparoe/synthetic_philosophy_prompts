So next month I'm giving a talk to my department's political philosophy reading group and I'm already dreading the Q&A, which is why I'm writing this. My thesis is a bit of a weird one: I'm arguing that the slogan "taxation is theft" is true (or at least defensible on libertarians' own premises) but dialectically useless, and that libertarians should retire it. My thought is basically that whether some coercive taking counts as "theft" is entirely downstream of your theory of just property acquisition -- so if you haven't already established self-ownership plus a Lockean-ish account of original appropriation, calling taxation theft is just table-pounding, and if you have established those, the slogan adds nothing. The real action is in the property theory, and the slogan lets libertarians skip it while annoying everyone else.

Here's the catch: the audience is mostly *libertarians*. Like, eight or nine grad students and a postdoc, ranging from soft Nozickians to two people I'm pretty sure are full anarcho-capitalists, plus one Kantian who comes for the arguement and one visiting economist. So I'm not going to get the standard Rawlsian pushback ("property is a legal convention, the state defines the bundle of rights, etc.") -- I'll get friendly-fire objections from people who love the slogan and will feel like I'm selling out the movement, plus the Kantian poking at my premise from the other side and the economist asking why any of this matters.

Write me a proper prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from *this* crowd, phrased the way a libertarian grad student would actually phrase them (so expect Wilt Chamberlain, the Lockean proviso, maybe some Hoppe-style argumentation ethics from one of the an-caps), ordered roughly by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do to my talk. For each one: what I should concede, what I should hold, a reply I can give in two minutes, and a note on where my reply is weakest. Also flag the places where my "the slogan does no work" claim is itself doing sneaky work, because I'm sure the Kantian will find at least one. And tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
