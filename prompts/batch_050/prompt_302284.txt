For several years I have kept a rough personal ranking of how much moral weight to give various creatures, and lately I have noticed something embarrassing about how I actually construct it. I tell myself the ranking tracks my best estimate of each creature's capacity for experience, but in practice it tracks how loudly the creature advertises that capacity. Three examples. I feel worse about boiling shrimp than about eating oysters, and the honest reason is that the shrimp writhe; my actual guess about oyster sentience hasn't changed in years, but my conduct toward oysters changed the day someone told me they have no face and no flinch. My nephew's toy robot dog whimpers and crouches when left alone, and every adult in the house feels a small irrational pang about switching it off, even though we all agree there is nothing it is like to be that toy. Meanwhile the thermostat on my wall runs a feedback loop that a biologist friend assures me is structurally similar to homeostasis in simple organisms, and it moves me not at all, which I think is the correct reaction, though I cannot say why the shrimp's movement should count for more than its regulation.

So the pattern is this: where direct access to another mind is impossible, I let the strength and vividness of the outward signals set the degree of my moral concern, rather than treating those signals as fallible evidence feeding an independent estimate of the underlying capacity. The signals and the capacity can come apart in both directions -- distress theater with nothing behind it, and silent systems that might matter anyway.

Does this conflation, or the graded moral practice built on it, have an established name? I suspect it is adjacent to anthropomorphism and to speciesism without being identical to either, but I would like that assessed precisely rather than asserted. And if no existing term carves at exactly this joint, I would ask you to propose one, define it, and map its boundary cases on both the over-signaling and under-signaling sides, including the awkward case where the signal and the capacity are both plausibly zero and yet disagreement persists about whether that settles the moral question.
