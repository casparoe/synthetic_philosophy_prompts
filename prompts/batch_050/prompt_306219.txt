I run a philosophy of science reading group for graduate students in the natural sciences, and I need a one-page flowchart handout for our session on natural kinds — the question of whether the sciences carve nature at its joints or whether our classifications are partly our own contribution. Design the flowchart as a decision tree that starts from the commitment "our best current science is at least roughly right about what kinds of things exist" and ends at named positions.

Requirements:

- Every interior node must be a single diagnostic question a scientist could answer just by reflecting on cases, without knowing which philosophical position it sorts them into. The questions should probe things like: whether a real kind needs a necessary-and-sufficient microstructural definition, whether several cross-cutting classification schemes can all be legitimate within one domain, whether boundaries may be vague or interest-relative without the kind thereby becoming unreal, and whether a kind owes its unity to underlying causal mechanisms rather than to an intrinsic essence.
- Build the tree from the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they permit — and then locate actual positions in that space. The leaves must include microstructural essentialism, homeostatic property cluster realism, promiscuous or pluralist realism, and an eliminativist or conventionalist position; add further leaves if the space generates them.
- The tree must route respondents using exactly these case studies, introduced at the nodes where they bite: chemical elements, biological species, jade (which turned out to be two different minerals grouped under one name), the category "planet" after Pluto's demotion, psychiatric diagnoses such as schizophrenia, and race. At each node where a case does the work, state in a sentence what answering one way versus the other commits the respondent to.
- At least two combinations of answers in the space should end up unoccupied; mark those points explicitly and diagnose in one or two sentences each whether the gap exists because the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate — I suspect cluster realism and promiscuous realism may be one such pair — and say what further question would be needed to split them.
- Keep the tree to at most eight interior nodes and ten leaves, so it fits on one page.

Format the tree as nested indented text or a numbered branching list that can be transferred directly onto a poster. Begin the response with the tree itself, with no preface and no framing paragraph, and write in American English.
