I am preparing a short position paper for a graduate seminar on well-being and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My working commitments are as follows:

(a) Patients with pain asymbolia — who, after certain brain lesions, continue to report painful sensations while insisting that the sensations no longer bother them, and who stop protecting injured limbs — are genuinely in pain. What the lesion removes is the painfulness of the pain, not the pain itself.

(b) The badness of pain is exhausted by its unpleasantness: strip away the unpleasantness, as asymbolia and morphine appear to do, and the badness goes with it. Asymbolic pain is therefore at most marginally bad for the subject, however intense the sensation.

(c) Unpleasantness is an irreducible phenomenal feature of experience, not analyzable as a desire, aversion, or avoidance disposition directed at the sensation. I regard the failure of attitudinal accounts of unpleasantness as one of the settled lessons of this literature.

(d) On the question of how good or bad an experience is for a subject, the subject's own settled, reflective judgment is authoritative: if someone lucidly and stably reports that what they are feeling is not bad for them, and their behavior bears this out, that verdict settles the matter.

(e) The pain of infants and non-human animals is bad in the same way, and for the same reason, as the pain of reflective adults.

Please tell me whether this package hangs together. Identify which pairs or triples of commitments are genuinely in tension and which are only apparently so, ordering the genuine tensions from most to least severe. For each genuine tension, state precisely which commitment must give, what the least costly revision would be, and what theoretical work I would lose by making it; pay particular attention to whether the evidence I rely on for some commitments is licensed by principles that my other commitments rule out. Conclude by identifying the single revision that would restore the most coherence to the overall position.

Lead with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence — consistent, or not, and where the deepest problem lies — then give the analysis, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Please write in American English.
