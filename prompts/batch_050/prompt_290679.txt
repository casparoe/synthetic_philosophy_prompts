I’m a novelist working on a near-future city, and I’m stuck on a feature of ordinary policing that most residents barely notice: the same armed generalists are dispatched to a noisy-neighbor dispute, a mental-health crisis, a traffic violation, a welfare check, a fight at school, and someone sleeping in a doorway. The officers’ common qualification is not expertise in all these problems but the authority to command, search, detain, and use force if the encounter goes badly.

What theory of public safety and state legitimacy does that arrangement presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view as charitably as possible: does it treat authorized coercion as a neutral tool that becomes dangerous only when misused, or does it assume that social disorder is best managed by keeping overwhelming force in the background? Is the practice internally consistent if police are simultaneously described as guardians, social-service providers, and enforcers of state order? Also say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined rather than committed to one view, and what each of police reform, defunding, and abolition would reject or preserve in that implicit theory. I need this for fictional worldbuilding, so give me the conceptual architecture, not a policy whitepaper. Max 700 words, with the central diagnosis in the opening sentence.
