So I recently quit my job to help start a worker co-op (we roast coffee, nothing glamorous) and my brother-in-law keeps throwing On Liberty at me like Mill would obviously hate anything that smells of socialism. But from what I've read, late Mill actually drifted toward a qualified sort of socialism -- the cooperative chapters in the Principles, the posthumously published Chapters on Socialism. So here's my question: if Mill could see today's economy -- gig platforms, UBI pilots, the Nordic welfare states, startups where three founders own everything -- where do you think he'd actually land? Would he see co-ops like ours as the gradual transformation he was hoping for, or as a sideshow? And would a big modern welfare state strike him as the overweaning beaurocracy he warned against, or as a tolerable stopgap? Please reason from his actual late views, not the cartoon free-market Mill.
