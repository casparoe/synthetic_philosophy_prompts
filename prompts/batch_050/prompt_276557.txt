In my ethics seminar, the professor has ruled on five cases so far: diverting a runaway trolley from a track with five workers onto a track with one — permissible; pushing a large stranger off a footbridge to stop the trolley — impermissible; killing one healthy patient to transplant his organs into five dying patients — impermissible; lying to a would-be murderer about where his intended victim is hiding — permissible; and killing one innocent bystander when doing so is the only way to prevent a third party from murdering ten other innocents — impermissible. For the final class, she has assigned a sixth case: the only way to stop a plot that will kill a million innocent people is to kill one innocent person, someone who is not involved in the plot and would survive it unharmed. I want to anticipate her ruling and defend my anticipation. Please structure your answer in four sections, in this order: (1) Prediction — her likely verdict on the sixth case, in one short paragraph; (2) Candidate principles — two or three rules (for example, an absolute side-constraint against killing innocents as a means, threshold deontology, a principle of minimizing violations of the constraint itself, or a doing-versus-allowing distinction), each in a sentence or two, noting which of the five earlier verdicts each rule accommodates; (3) Best fit — one paragraph arguing which principle best explains her track record and what it predicts for the sixth case; (4) The discriminating case — one further case, roughly a paragraph, whose outcome would tell your favored principle apart from its strongest rival.
