Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have debated divine hiddenness for over a decade and finally agreed to produce one document they can both sign. One defends the argument from nonresistant nonbelief: roughly, that a perfectly loving God would ensure that anyone capable of a personal relationship with God, and not culpably resisting one, believes that God exists — so the existence of sincere, seeking nonbelievers is evidence that no such God exists. The other is a theist who concedes the phenomenon but holds that God has morally sufficient reasons to permit it.

To keep them from trading stereotypes, stipulate two pieces of shared data at the outset and have both parties accept them in the agreed-claims section: (1) Ada, a composite of real cases — a person raised without religion who spends twenty years sincerely seeking God, prays and studies and wants to believe, yet finds herself unable to; and (2) Evidentia, a counterfactual world in which God's existence is as obvious to everyone as the weather, which both must use to test claims about what divine obviousness would actually accomplish.

The statement should contain: a numbered list of claims both parties accept, including what the argument does and does not require (e.g., whether it needs belief in God's existence to be constant, or merely available; whether it targets all nonbelief or only the nonresistant kind); a disagreements section in which each remaining dispute is stated in wording its own side would sign, without caricature; the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — which I expect to include whether explicit propositional belief is strictly necessary for the relevant personal relationship, whether nonresistant nonbelief is actual or merely apparent, and whether goods obtainable only under hiddenness (uncoerced trust, certain virtues, genuine moral freedom) could outweigh the cost of Ada's situation; and for each crux, the case or consideration both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction registered in advance — for instance, what each view predicts about what would follow in Evidentia: whether undeniable evidence of God would produce loving relationship or mere resentful acknowledgment.

If any part of their long dispute dissolves as verbal — say, over what "resistance" or "belief" means — the statement should identify it and explain why. Begin the document with one sentence stating how many of the original disagreements survived as genuine rather than terminological. The whole statement must not exceed 1,000 words.
