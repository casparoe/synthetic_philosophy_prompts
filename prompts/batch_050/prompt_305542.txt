I am a PhD student working on the foundations of probability and induction, and I have run into a dispute with a colleague that I would like to locate precisely rather than continue arguing past. The question is whether randomness can be a property of individual finite sequences, as opposed to a property of the processes that generate them or of infinite sequences in the limit.

Here is the precise proposition I would like your credence on. Take two specific 1000-bit strings, both in fact produced by the same physical coin-tossing device. The first string looks statistically typical: roughly half ones, no discernible compressible pattern. The second string happens to be the alternating sequence 010101... for all thousand bits. The proposition is this: there is an objective fact of the matter that the first string is more random than the second, and this fact is (i) not merely a report of our epistemic situation or our inability to spot a pattern, and (ii) not merely shorthand for a claim about the generating process, since by stipulation both strings came from the same fair device. In other words: is randomness of a token finite string a real, mind-independent property, or does the concept of randomness only get a grip on processes, ensembles, or limiting frequencies?

For context, my credence in the proposition is about 0.65; my colleague's is about 0.15. The obvious formal candidate for making sense of it is algorithmic: a string is random to the degree that it is incompressible, i.e., the shortest program that outputs it is not much shorter than the string itself, with invariance across universal machines up to an additive constant. But that approach is exact only asymptotically, and for any fixed finite string the complexity depends on the choice of universal machine in a way that no constant can wash out.

Please give me your own credence, but more importantly, decompose the question. I would like you to identify the sub-questions your credence depends on -- candidates include whether the machine-dependence of finite-string complexity is a fatal objection or a benign kind of imprecision; whether the intuitive verdict about the alternating string can be vindicated without smuggling in facts about typical generating processes; whether 'random' as applied to sequences is really doing metaphysical or only epistemic work in our ordinary and scientific usage; and whether the asymptotic objectivity of algorithmic randomness licenses extrapolation to the finite case -- assign a rough probability to each, and show how they combine into your final number. Then tell me on which of these sub-questions my colleague at 0.15 and I at 0.65 most likely disagree, and what argument, result, or consideration would move your own credence the most in either direction.
