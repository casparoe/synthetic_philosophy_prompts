So I sit on the grant committee of our local community foundation — it's seven of us, we disburse about $200k a year to local projects, mostly by consensus, and it's usually pretty harmonious. This year it's gotten tense though. One member, Diane, really wants to fund a program that brings therapy animals into schools, and she's passionate about it. My problem is that I've read a bit about it and I'm fairly convinced the program is a waste of money at best — the evidence for benefits is super weak — and honestly I have some low-level worries it's mildly bad for the animals involved. Meanwhile the project I care most about, a mosquito net distribution thing we fund abroad through a partner org, needs renewal and Diane has hinted she's lukewarm on it because she thinks we should "keep money local."

Here's where it gets tricky. Last week Diane proposed, pretty explicitly, a deal: she'll stop raising objections to the mosquito nets if I stop raising objections to the therapy animals. My gut says this is icky — it feels like I'd be signing off on something I believe is bad in exchange for something I believe is good, like bartering with my conscience. But then again, we each have equal votes and roughly equal standing, she genuinely believes her project is worthwhile and probably thinks mine is misguided in the opposite direction, and from her side this presumably looks exactly as icky. So maybe refusing the deal on principle is just me insisting my judgment trump hers, which seems arrogant given that I've been wrong about stuff before.

Please act as a professional philosopher who's thought a lot about compromise between people with conflicting values, and think this through carefully rather than just telling me to "follow my conscience" or "be pragmatic." Before giving advice, brainstorm what general structure this situation has — when, if ever, is it legitimate to help bring about something you think is bad in exchange for someone else helping bring about something you think is good? Does it matter that both of us are somewhat uncertain about our own judgments? Does it matter that neither of us would be *doing* the bad thing directly, just voting for it? And is there a difference between actively endorsing her project versus just staying silent about my objections? End with concrete advice on what I should actually do at the next meeting.
