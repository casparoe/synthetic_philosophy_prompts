I am a retired condensed matter physicist who has spent the past two years working through philosophy of mathematics with a reading group of fellow emeriti, and we have reached Skolem's paradox. I understand the technical core tolerably well: by the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem, if first-order set theory has a model at all it has a countably infinite one, and yet the theory itself proves that uncountable sets exist. I also understand the standard dissolution of the air of outright contradiction — countability is assessed from outside the model, while inside it the relevant bijection simply fails to exist as a set. What I do not yet have, and what I want before reading further, is a specification: what must any adequate philosophical resolution of Skolem's paradox actually deliver? Please write the desiderata — the things a resolution has to get right to count as one — with the case or consideration behind each. Among the items I would expect, though I want you to correct and extend my list: it must state the tension precisely enough to separate the theorem from the morals drawn from it; it must say whether uncountability is an absolute property of collections or only relative to a background model, and what either answer costs; it must address the regress that the metatheory in which we call the model countable is itself subject to the same theorem; it must account for the contrast with second-order formulations of set theory, which determine their models up to a much stronger result, and must say what that contrast can and cannot show; and it must either vindicate or defuse the stronger conclusion, pressed by Putnam and others, that our mathematical language cannot fix an intended interpretation of set talk at all.

Two further requests about the structure of the list. First, mark which desiderata strike you as genuine requirements on any resolution and which are better understood as the commitments of a particular camp — the set-theoretic relativist, the platonist who takes the intended model as given, the formalist who regards the whole puzzle as an artifact of first-order formalization. Second, if any subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly and explain why, since for me that would be the most interesting output; likewise, if one requirement does most of the work in excluding the positions currently on offer, identify it. I am less interested in a final verdict on the paradox than in knowing what a verdict would have to accomplish.

Finally, a request about intellectual manners. Throughout, please distinguish what is settled — the mathematics itself, and I take it the absence of any formal inconsistency — from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested philosophical reading (for instance, that the relativity of set-theoretic notions is simply a fact we must live with, or that second-order logic straightforwardly restores the intended model) as though it were the consensus view. Where the literature is divided, say that it is divided, and say on what.
