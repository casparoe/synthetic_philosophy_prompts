Mine is about 0.3: I suspect that declaring the two models identical merely defines away the underdetermination instead of dissolving it, and that the theory's equations genuinely fail to fix what happens inside the hole. Please decompose it: the probability that general covariance truly forces the equivalence claim rather than permitting a subtler individuation of points (say, through material events or through the metric's invariant structure); the probability that a real determinism problem survives even if point identity turns out to be conventional; and the probability that the resolution carries over to quantum gravity, where the metric itself is supposed to fluctuate. Assign a rough number and one line of justification to each, show how you combine them, and then tell me which sub-question would move your final answer most, what argument or result would move it, and which sub-question most plausibly separates someone at 0.85 from someone at 0.3.
