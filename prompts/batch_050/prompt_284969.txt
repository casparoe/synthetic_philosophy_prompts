so my friend and I just spent like three hours at the bar arguing about epiphenomenalism and I want to send her something instead of the last word I didn't get. she's convinced experience is basically steam off the engine -- caused by brain stuff but causing nothing in return, not even our belief that we're conscious. I kept pushing what I later found out is an actual thing, the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if the feeling itself does nothing, then my saying "this hurts, I'm experiencing it right now" is fully explained by neurons firing, and the hurt never actually gets a say in my saying. the experience and the judgment of the experience never touch. we left it unresolved and it's driving me nuts.

write me a villanelle about this. 19 lines, two refrains, the whole deal. here's the important part: make the form DO the argument. the two refrains should be the experience itself and the report of the experience, and the structure of the villanelle should dramatize the problem -- the refrains keep coming back close together, almost touching, but the poem should make you feel that they never actually make contact, that one is always just trailing the other. by the closing quatrain I want the reader to feel the weird vertigo of the thing: that the poem itself, being a bunch of words, is on the judgment side of the gap, and whatever it's about is on the other side, causally mute.

it should be accurate enough that my friend cant just reply "thats not the view." keep it a real poem though, not a philosophy paper with line breaks. no title needed, I'll think of one.
