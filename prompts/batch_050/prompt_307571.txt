ok so in my philosphy class we got into self-deception and there's this paradox thing where it seems like it should be impossible. like, to lie to someone you have to know the truth and then get them to believe something false, but if you lie to YOURSELF then you have to both know the truth and not know it at the same time, which seems contradictory. and also if you *intended* to fool yourself you'd notice yourself doing it, so the trick would never work. our teacher said some philosophers think this proves self-deception isn't real and it's always just bias or motivated reasoning, while others think its obviously real and the paradox just shows our model of belief is wrong.

anyway i want you to run this on a real case: Lance Armstrong. quick facts in case you need them — he won the Tour de France seven times in a row (1999-2005), people accused him of doping for over a decade and he denied it super aggressively the whole time, like suing people who said he doped, calling former teammates liars, always pointing out he'd never failed a drug test. then in 2012 the US anti-doping agency put out a huge report with testimony from a bunch of teammates, he got stripped of all seven titles, and in january 2013 he admitted it all in an interview with Oprah. the interesting part is he basically said that at the time he didn't really see it as cheating, because in his head everyone was doping so it wasn't giving him an unfair advantage — it was just leveling the field. (i might be misremembering small details of the interview but that was the gist.)

so here's what i want:

Does the case even make sense if self-deception is impossible, or does the "he knew the whole time and was just a really committed liar" reading work fine?
