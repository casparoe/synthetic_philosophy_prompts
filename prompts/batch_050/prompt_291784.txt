I'm trying to work out what I actually believe about the ethics of killing insects and other pests, so I've been going case by case and trusting my gut. Here are my verdicts, and I'd like to know whether a single principle lies behind them.

Spraying a malaria-stricken region to kill the mosquitoes that transmit it, knowing that the drift will also kill many harmless insects: clearly permissible, arguably obligatory. Crushing a cockroach I find in my kitchen: fine, I do it without a second thought, even though in principle I could trap it in a cup and put it outside. Killing a house spider that has wandered into the bathroom: faintly wrong, since I could just as easily carry it out in a glass, and I feel I ought to. A child pulling the wings off flies out of curiosity: wrong, even though the flies would have died soon anyway and even though I squash flies myself without scruple. A restaurant running a UV bug zapper on its terrace that mostly kills harmless moths: wrong, gratuitous. Fumigating my house to stop termites from destroying its structure: fine. A farmer spraying a broad-spectrum insecticide that kills the aphids threatening his crop along with the bees and ladybirds nearby: permissible if the harvest genuinely depends on it, but he ought to prefer a narrower agent if one exists at comparable cost. Using glue traps for mice, which leave them to die slowly over hours, when quick-acting traps are available: not fine, even though killing the mice as such is fine. Releasing sterile males to crash the population of one particular mosquito species so that it goes locally or globally extinct: I genuinely cannot decide whether this is better than ordinary spraying, since no mass killing occurs and much future suffering is prevented, or worse, since it eliminates a whole species rather than merely individuals.

Is there one reasonably simple principle that delivers all or most of these verdicts? Please state it precisely, tie each of its clauses to the case that motivates it, and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and would force me to drop; I suspect the cockroach and the spider are hard to hold together. Then tell me what the principle implies for two cases I haven't listed: a vineyard using pheromone traps that attract and drown moths of a species whose caterpillars damage the grapes, and a homeowner who poisons an entire ground nest of bees by his front door after a single sting, although a beekeeper would relocate the nest for a modest fee.

Please reply in continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
