So I've been going back and forth with a mate about ergodicity economics -- the Ole Peters, time-average growth rate stuff -- and we keep talking past each other in a really specific way, which made me wonder what an actual adversarial collaboration between the two camps would produce. So: draft the joint statement that a committed ergodicity-economics advocate and a committed defender of standard expected utility theory could both sign after a proper good-faith collaboration.

Some context for framing the two sides. The ergodicity camp says expected value is the wrong operator for decisions under repeated uncertainty because what an individual actually experiences is growth over time, and the time average can diverge wildly from the ensemble average whenever payoffs are multiplicative rather than additive. The standard camp says this is all old news dressed up: expected utility theory never claimed you should maximise expected wealth, Bernoulli and the St Petersburg paradox sorted this centuries ago, and once you pick a logarithmic utility function you recover the time-average result anyway, so there's no new theory here, just a re-derivation with extra marketing.

The deliverable should have three parts:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. Both sides should be able to sign things about the maths (what the time average actually converges to and under what conditions, when ensemble and time averages coincide, what multiplicative dynamics do to the distribution of outcomes), about the history (what Bernoulli and later utility theorists actually did and didn't say, kept vague enough to not hinge on exact quotes), and about what a rational agent's objective function could legitimately be. Find formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted towards one side's framing.

2. A disagreements section, ideally as a table, where each residual disagreement is stated precisely and phrased so that each camp would accept the wording of its own position. Some disagreements I suspect are in there: whether maximising the time-average growth rate is a *derivation* of rational behaviour or just a *rebranding* of log utility; whether utility functions are explanatory primitives or ad hoc fits; whether the ensemble average is ever the right tool for a single agent making sequential decisions; and whether ergodicity economics has produced any actionable recommendation that expected utility with the right function couldn't.

3. The cruxes: for each disagreement, state what empirical result, formal theorem, or worked example both sides agree would actually bear on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. For instance, both might agree that cases where the two frameworks give genuinely different advice would be decisive -- so describe such a case (or explain why the disagreement camp thinks none exist), and note where each side predicts the other will have to bite a bullet.

If you conclude that part of the dispute is purely verbal -- e.g. that "maximise time-average growth" and "maximise expected log wealth" are the same instruction wearing different hats -- say exactly which part, and what (if anything) survives once that part is stripped out. And if one camp's position turns out to depend on a claim the other camp can grant without losing anything, flag that too.

Keep the maths informal but correct; don't invent specific citations or papers, since I mainly care about whether the conceptual carving is right.
