I am a research-integrity officer at a midsize university, and about twice a month I must triage complaints that an author, grantee, or press office deceived readers without uttering a clear falsehood: relative-risk framing, denominator neglect, cropped images, “available on request” claims, selective reporting where every reported result is accurate, and abstracts whose implicatures outrun the data. Please operationalize the lying-versus-misleading distinction into a reusable procedure I can run in about ninety minutes by a generalist panel, with counsel sign-off required for sanctions, using only distinctions you define in the answer: fixing the question under discussion and intended audience; separating assertion, conventional or pragmatic implicature, presupposition laundering, omission under a duty of candor, and truthful paltering; testing what a reasonable hearer would take away, what the speaker counterfactually commits to, whether a correction would change behavior, materiality and reliance, intent against innocent explanations, and whether severity should track falsity, deceptiveness, harm, or breach of role. Answer with exactly five numbered items, no heading, preface, or closing, each item no more than two sentences: (1) the ordered tests with pass/fail thresholds, including where the procedure stops with “no case,” where it requires correction rather than blame, and where judgment is irreducibly evaluative; (2) a scoring or classification rule that compares an outright lie, a misleading truth, and a negligent ambiguity, with aggravators and mitigators and at least one misfire you accept as the cost of usability; (3) application to a press release saying a treatment “halved risk” when absolute risk fell from 0.4% to 0.2%, stating outcome and rationale; (4) application to a principal investigator who accurately reports three supportive experiments, omits two failed replications known to the lab, and writes that the effect is robust, stating outcome and rationale; (5) application to a grant report saying data are “available on request” while the archive is corrupted and requests are slow-walked, choosing one application to produce an answer I will dislike, and naming the limit of the procedure that forced it.
