For a graduate seminar on semantic paradox I've drafted my working position, and before I present it I'd like a rigorous consistency audit. For definiteness: by the Curry sentence I mean a sentence C equivalent to "if C is true, then absurdity follows"; by Yablo's paradox I mean the infinite list in which each sentence says of every later sentence in the list that it is not true. Here are my commitments:

(a) The correct response to Curry's paradox is structural: restrict the contraction rule while retaining modus ponens, conditional proof, and classical negation, including the law of excluded middle.

(b) Truth should be fully transparent: a sentence and the attribution of truth to it are intersubstitutable in every context, so the unrestricted T-schema must be preserved.

(c) Yablo's paradox is a genuine paradox despite involving no self-reference or circularity, and any adequate account of the semantic paradoxes owes us an explanation of it, not a dismissal.

(d) The liar, Curry, and Yablo should receive a unified diagnosis: ideally a single structural fix handles all three, rather than a patchwork of case-by-case maneuvers.

(e) Solutions that declare paradoxical sentences meaningless, ungrounded, or not truth-apt are ad hoc evasions and should be avoided.
