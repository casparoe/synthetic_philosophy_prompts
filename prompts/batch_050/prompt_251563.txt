Ok so for like two years now my sociology teacher and my psych teacher have had this running argument that sometimes spills over into lunch, and it's about whether "generations" are even real. The sociology teacher says stuff like Gen Z and Millennials and Boomers are actual groups with their own outlooks because people who grow up through the same big events (covid, 9/11, everyone getting smartphones at the same age) end up shaped by that in a lasting way. The psych teacher says the whole thing is basically astrology with charts -- the cutoff years are arbitrary, most of the supposed differences are just "young people have always been like that," and the labels mostly exist because of marketing and the media. The weird part is they both know all the same surveys and statistics. Nobody's hiding data. They just keep talking past each other and neither has moved AT ALL.

So what kind of disagreement is this actually? My working theory is that the word "generation" is doing like three jobs at once -- a neutral demographic label, a scientific claim that cohorts really are psychologically different in a lasting way, and a media stereotype -- and the fight only feels stuck because they keep sliding between those. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real factual question buried in there, like whether you could ever statistically separate "people change as they age" from "people are marked forever by when they were born." I'd like a diagnosis with actual reasons, based on what the two sides would say, not just a summary of the debate. And tell me what would count as progress -- a study design, a sharper definition, a distinction everyone would have to accept -- or if you think nothing can settle it because it's not really a factual fight, say that and explain why. (Please don't just tell me who's right, I can get that from listening to them argue definately too often already.)
