ok so I'm a novelist, mostly lit fic but I'm working on something set on a generation ship where the colonists have to decide how many kids to have per couple, and I realized while writing it that my own gut takes on population stuff are a mess. The book keeps coming out muddled because I am muddled. Here's what I find myself believing:

(a) The repugnant conclusion really is repugnant. A trillion lives that are barely worth living, muzak-and-potatoes lives, is genuinely worse than a few hundred million lives that are wonderful. I don't bite that bullet and I don't trust people who bite it easily.

(b) When people I know have a kid they wanted and that kid is happy, the world is better for it. Not just better for the parents, not just good for the kid once it exists — better, period. I'd say the same about a stranger's kid on the other side of the world.

(c) Nobody is harmed or wronged by not being conceived. There's no waiting room of unborn people getting passed over. If a couple decides to stay childless, no one has been deprived of anything, because there's no one there to be deprived.

(d) If humanity deliberately went extinct — everyone just stops having kids, last generation dies out peacefully at 90 with good palliative care — that would be a tragedy, one of the worst things that could happen, not a neutral shrug.

(e) I buy the non-identity point. If a couple conceives this month instead of waiting a year, they get a different kid, and that kid can't complain about their timing decision as long as its life is decent. Choices that change who exists don't wrong the people who end up existing.

Now, my ship-board council in the novel basically has to legislate on the basis of something like these intuitions and I keep writing scenes where the characters expose cracks in them. So before I embarrass myself in the ms: do these five actually hang together? Tell me which pairs are in real tension, which pairs only look like they're in tension but are fine once you think about it, and rank the real ones by how bad they are. For each real tension tell me the cheapest revision — which commitment to weaken and how — and I want to know which single change un-knots the most, because I'd rather lose one limb than three. Don't tell me what population ethics is, I've read Parfit, I just need the audit.
