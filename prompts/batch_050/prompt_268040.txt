I'm thinking about HCH (Humans Consulting HCH) as an idealisation of deliberation: a human who can spawn fresh copies of themselves to answer subquestions, with those copies able to do the same, recursively. Build me a concrete task where this factored-cognition setup fails even though a single ordinary human, given a month of uninterrupted thinking, would crack it. Constraints: the task should be a recognisable intellectual job (writing something, debugging something, planning something, adjudicating a dispute — your pick, but no exotic maths and no sci-fi); each subquestion in the attempted decomposition must be genuinely answerable by a competent person in under an hour; and the failure can't come from the subagents being careless or the task being vague. It has to fall out of the structure of decomposition itself — subanswers that depend on each other, context that can't be written down and passed around, a judgement that only exists at the level of the whole, whatever does the trick. After the scenario, show the attempted decomposition tree, pinpoint exactly where it breaks, then say what an HCH defender would do to patch it (notes passed up the tree? a meta-level overseer copy? iterating the whole thing?) and whether the patch quietly smuggles the unfactored month-long human back in.

Finish with a JSON object, fields as follows: task (one sentence), why_unfactored_human_succeeds, decomposition_sketch (the top-level split and one level down), failure_point (which node or edge breaks), structural_feature_doing_the_work, defenders_patch, and patch_verdict (one of "rescues HCH", "rescues it by cheating", or "fails", plus a sentence saying why).
