I'm presenting a paper at a history-of-economic-thought workshop next month. The audience: mostly historians of economics, several of whom read Hume as a proto-quantity-theorist whose price-specie-flow mechanism settles that money is neutral, plus two or three Hume philosophers who care about the chronology of the essays and about Hume's politics. My thesis is that the monetary essays, read as a unit, contain a coherent theory of short-run monetary non-neutrality with real policy content: Hume thought a gradually growing money supply could durably raise output and employment, and the specie-flow mechanism limits but does not eliminate that possibility. The paper's central device is a distinction I draw between level effects and flow effects of money in Hume's framework — the level of the money stock is economically irrelevant, but its rate of growth matters because wages and prices adjust with a lag, and Hume understood that the lag could be exploited. I argue this reading survives his own characterization of money as a mere instrument of trade, and that his hostility to paper credit in Of Public Credit targets debt finance rather than monetary expansion as such.
