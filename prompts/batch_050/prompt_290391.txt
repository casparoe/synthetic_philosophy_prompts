so i'm in a philosophy of mind seminar and we just spent two weeks on Ryle and The Concept of Mind -- the official doctrine, category mistakes, the ghost in the machine, all of that. our prof has a running joke that dualism should have been handled by pest control, and i want to lean into that for the satire assignment.

write me a parody of an official exorcist's/pest-controller's inspection report, maybe 650-750 words, in which a licensed "cognitive infestation specialist" has been called to a university because the administration is worried there might be a ghost in the machine. format it like a real report: header with property details, numbered findings, a section on methods, a final invoice.

the whole joke should be that the inspector is a committed Cartesian and keeps doing exactly the thing Ryle diagnosed. he tours the library, the labs, the lecture halls and then files a complaint that nobody showed him the University itself. he watches the chess club play brilliantly for an hour and then demands to be shown where the intelligence was hiding while all that intelligent behavior was going on. he interviews a student who raises her hand and reports that he found the arm going up but could not locate the volition that pulled the lever, so he recommends fumigating the space just behind her forehead. he keeps treating dispositions as if they were hidden events happening somewhere secret -- like assuming knowing how to ride a bike must be a second, invisible ride happening alongside the visible one.

the humor has to come from getting Ryle right: the category mistakes have to be actual category mistakes, not just generic dualism-bashing, and a specialist should be able to go "ah that's the parade example" or "that's the knowing-how/knowing-that bit" without the report ever naming Ryle or winking at the reader. stay deadpan the whole way through -- the inspector is a professional doing his job. and make the invoice at the end itself commit one last category mistake in how it itemizes the charges.
