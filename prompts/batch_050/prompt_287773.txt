I am a distributed-control engineer with a strong working command of graph theory, feedback systems, state synchronization, modularity, communication bottlenecks, and fault tolerance, but little training in philosophy of biology. Please restate the debate over whether holobionts, multicellular colonies, and insect superorganisms count as individual organisms using that engineering vocabulary.

Use honeybee colonies, corals with their algal symbionts, lichens, and the human body together with its gut microbiome as recurring test cases. Distinguish, as precisely as the engineering vocabulary permits, at least four candidate conceptions of biological individuality: an integrated physiological system, a bounded agent acting in an environment, a unit whose components share reproductive fate, and a lineage that persists through component turnover. Translate these into claims about nodes and modules, boundaries and interfaces, material and information flows, feedback loops, distributed versus centralized control, consensus, shared objectives, observability, failure containment, and robustness to component replacement.

Do not simply stipulate that biological entities are machines. Where an engineering term has no exact biological counterpart, say so rather than stretching it. Where several translations are possible, present them separately and explain what each one would count as an organism. In particular, examine whether reproductive alignment can be represented as convergence on a common objective or whether that translation loses something essential, and whether the distinction between an organism and its symbionts can be captured by control authority or communication bandwidth alone.

Conclude with a residue report organized under three headings: claims that translate cleanly, claims that survive only with distortion, and claims that have no plausible expression in distributed-control vocabulary. For each item in the last two categories, judge whether the residue exposes a limitation of the engineering framework or merely reveals vagueness in the biological dispute. Finally, apply your translation and residue report to the four test cases and identify which one most sharply breaks the engineering analogy.
