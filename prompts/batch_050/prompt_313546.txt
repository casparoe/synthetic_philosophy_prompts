I'm in a reading group that's been stuck for three sessions on the same fight, and I want a diagnosis rather than another referee report. The fight: did Hegel glorify war? One side (basically Popper's line, which alot of us absorbed in undergrad) says the war paragraphs near the end of the Philosophy of Right, where he talks about war preserving the ethical health of peoples and compares perpetual peace to a sea going foul for want of wind, give the whole game away: the individual is fuel for the state. The other side says he's making a structural observation -- with no world state, disputes between sovereigns have no arbiter but force -- that describing that isn't endorsing it, and that he only ever defends defensive war anyway.

The passage the first side keeps wheeling out as the load-bearing premise is from the remark to §258 (Knox translation): "Since the state is mind objectified, it is only as one of its members that the individual himself has objectivity, genuine individuality, and an ethical life." Their argument: if individuals only get to be real ethical agents through the state, of course Hegel will smile on the state asserting itself in war. The defenders say the sentence is about what membership is, not about war at all, and importing it into this debate is exactly the move that keeps it spinning.

So what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it verbal (nobody has fixed what "glorify" even means -- derive it in your system? ascribe it positive value? discuss it without visible horror?), or is it about what counts as evidence, or about the standards for attributing an endorsement to a philosopher versus a consequence of his premises, or is there a genuine substantive dispute about whether the §258 premise actually commits him? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress -- a distinction, a textual test, agreeing to drop the question -- or is no progress to be expected? My hunch, offered only as a hunch: the real fight is about what a philosophy of right is for, ideals versus diagnoses, and the war passages are a proxy battle. Tell me whether that holds up.
