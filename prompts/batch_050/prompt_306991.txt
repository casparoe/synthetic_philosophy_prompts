I'm writing a short essay for a film-and-philosophy night at my dept and I want to read Inception through Zhuangzi's butterfly dream instead of through Descartes, which is what literally everyone reaches for. The contrast I have in mind: Descartes uses dreams to doubt the external world (maybe none of this is real and there's a problem to be solved), while Zhuangzi, after dreaming he was a butterfly, isn't so much worried that the world is fake as genuinely unsure which of his two selves is the real one -- and the takeaway is something about the transformation of things rather than a skeptical threat to be defeated.

Some specific things I want the reading to graple with:

- The totem system. Cobb's spinning top is basically an anti-skeptical machine, a test that settles which side of the dream/wake line you're on. Zhuangzi conspicuously never looks for a test. So is the film fundamentally Cartesian after all? Or does the way the film undermines totems (Arthur's loaded die only works because nobody else knows its exact weight, the top was Mal's to begin with, a totem tells you about other people's dreams but arguably not your own) push it toward Zhuangzi?
- Mal. She "woke up," concluded the waking world was the dream, and jumped to escape it. Is she the film's butterfly -- someone who took the wrong horn of Zhuangzi's either/or -- and does the film present her error as epistemic (bad evidence, a bad inference) or as something more like a spiritual failure?
- The ending. Cobb walks away from the still-spinning top to go to his kids and the camera cuts before we see it fall. One reading: he's stopped caring which side he's on, which is exactly the Zhuangzian move. Another: the film is cheating, withholding the answer to a question it spent two hours teaching us to ask. Which reading does the rest of the film actually support?
- Where does the film flat-out resist the Zhuangzi reading? Its dreams are engineered, shared, rule-governed, stacked in a hierarchy with a time-dilation formula -- about as far from a spontaneous butterfly nap as you can get. Does that difference matter philosophically or just cinematically?

I want it fair in both directions: not Inception as an illustration of a parable, and not Zhuangzi stretched to fit a blockbuster. And be blunt at the end about where the film is philosophically shallow versus where it genuinely adds something to dream skepticism that the argument alone doesn't give you.
