I'm on the disciplinary committee of my amateur football club (about 40 members, everyone knows everyone, small town). Here's the situation. Our treasurer, Marta, has formally complained that another member, Jens, skimmed roughly 200 euros from the bar takings at our summer tournament, and honestly the evidence is pretty solid. The wrinkle: three years ago Marta did almost the same thing, took about 150 euros from the kit fund. It was hushed up at the time -- the old committee let her quietly pay it back and never told the membership. Marta says that's ancient history and totally irrelevant, what matters is what Jens did, and the club can't just shrug at theft because of old stuff. Jens's friends say Marta of all people has no business pointing fingers and the complaint is tainted from the start. To make it messier, two of our current five committee members knew about Marta's thing back then and kept quiet.

I've been chewing on this on my own for days and I keep flip-flopping. Does having done the same wrong yourself actually undercut your right to call it out in someone else, or is that just a convenient way for Jens to dodge what he did? I genuinly don't have a settled view, which is why I need help.

Draft the committee's decision memo. It needs a clear bottom line -- hear the complaint as normal, refuse to entertain it, or something in between (like hearing it but with restrictions on who sits on the panel or what sanctions are available) -- with reasons the rest of the committee can actually vote on. Whatever you recommend, give the strongest argument for the option you reject a fair shake, because the membership will absolutely raise it at the general meeting. The memo also has to say something about Marta's old case, since it can't just pretend that never happened -- should it be reopened, left alone, or handled some other way? Keep it practical, we're volunteers not lawyers.
