I've been a longtime reader of LessWrong and adjacent blogs, and a running frustration in my circle is how debates about career choice, donation targets, and "should I go vegan or just offset?" keep stalling at the point where someone says the options are simply incomparable and the conversation dies. So for a rationalist community meetup I'm hosting, I want to build a decision tree that sorts attendees by what they actually believe about incommensurable values and hard choices -- not by which label they find respectable. Act as a professional philosopher with deep familiarity with this literature, and think carefully before drafting: first sketch the logical space of questions on which positions on hard choices come apart (for example: is hardness merely epistemic, or is it metaphysical; is a fourth value relation beyond better-than, worse-than, and equal-to coherent; can small improvements break parity or vagueness; can rational agents create reasons by committing), then place the actual defended positions in that space, and only then write the tree itself.

The tree should start from a shared commitment anyone at the meetup would grant ("when I compare becoming a malaria researcher against becoming a sculptor, neither option seems clearly better, worse, or exactly equal") and branch via diagnostic questions, each phrased as a concrete case rather than a theoretical slogan -- no node may ask anything like "do you believe in incommensurability?" Good diagnostics might include: whether learning one option is slightly better on a single dimension while remaining a hard choice overall licenses concluding the options were never truly on a par; whether it is rational to regret a choice made between putatively incomparable options; whether flipping a coin between the options would be rationally defective or perfectly fine; and whether sincerely resolving to be a sculptor could itself make sculpting the choiceworthy path. Each leaf should carry a named position (epistemicism, vagueness or imprecise comparability, parity as a fourth relation, Raz-style incommensurability, trumping, voluntarism about reasons, and whatever else belongs), one sentence stating its most awkward commitment, and an explicit note wherever the tree cannot genuinely separate two leaves rather than papering the collision over. Note any combination of answers in the logical space that no one occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Fix the tree at seven to ten branch nodes and seven to nine leaves.

After the tree, include a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "positions", an array of objects each with "name" (the position), "path" (the sequence of node answers that reaches it, as short strings), "awkward_commitment" (one sentence), and "diagnostic_cases" (the concrete cases that most sharply identify it); "unseparated_pairs", an array of objects with "pair" (two position names) and "why_tree_cannot_split_them" (one or two sentences); and "unoccupied_regions", an array of objects with "answer_combination" (short description) and "diagnosis" (incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored, with one sentence of justification). Keep the total output focused; the tree and the JSON are the deliverables, not an essay.
