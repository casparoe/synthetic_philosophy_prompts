I'm giving a talk next month to my department's mind and cognition reading group defending the claim that an ant colony is a genuine subject of experience -- not in some deflated metaphorical way, but literally: there's something it's like to be the colony, over and above whatever it's like to be the individual ants (if anything). My argument leans on the standard functionalist moves -- the colony integrates information, learns foraging patterns, remembers nest sites across years even though every individual ant has turned over, makes tradeoffs, and its "decisions" aren't reducible to any single ant's internal state, kind of like how none of my neurons knows what I'm thinking. Hofstadter played with this in the Aunt Hillary dialogue and I basically think he was onto something the field filed away as a cute thought experiment instead of a live hypothesis.

Here's the audience problem: the group is about half philosophers of mind who are mostly sympathetic to functionalism but very protective of the "mark of the cognitive" debates, and half are from the biology side -- two people who actually study social insects and one neuroscientist who keeps saying "no neurons, no problem to solve" as a conversation-ender. The biologists know way more about actual ant behavior than I do (my entire empirical case rests on colony-level learning and turnover stuff I've read secondhand), and the philosophers will smell hand-waving on the consciousness part even if they grant the cognition part.

Write me a prep sheet for the Q&A. The objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way these specific people would phrase them -- the biologists won't talk about "multiple realizability," they'll talk about pheromone stigmergy and say my examples are just emergent chemistry. For each objection: whether it has real force against the consciousness claim specifically or just against the cognitive framing, what I should concede immediately to keep credibility, and a two-minute reply. Also flag which part of my own case is weakest (I suspect it's the move from "colony-level information processing" to "colony-level phenomenal experience," which honestly I have not nailed down) and what the question I'm hoping nobody asks is -- the one where I'd have to either bite a really weird bullet or retreat to panpsychism-adjacent territory in front of a room full of people who'd remember it.
