I spent forty years as a research chemist, retired about three years ago, and lately I've been reading moral philosophy, so sorry if this is a well-worn question with a proper name I should know by now. Here's my situation. Our county has two things on the ballot this November. One is a school measure that would protect funding for the science curriculum, wich I care about a great deal and my neighbor Diane thinks is a waste of taxpayer money. The other is a zoning change that would let her expand her cafe, which she cares about a great deal and I'd mildly rather see fail. Neither of us was really going to bother with the other's issue — I can't follow zoning fights, she has no kids in the schools. Last week over the fence she proposed a deal: she votes yes on my school measure if I vote yes on her zoning one. As far as I can tell, by both our lights we each come out ahead, nobody's being coerced, nobody's being paid. But the whole thing makes me a bit queasy and I honestly can't tell if that's a real moral intuition or just something left over from civics class. So: is this deal fine, maybe even good? And if it's not, what exactly is wrong with it, given that everyone directly involved prefers it to the alternative? One more wrinkle that confuses me. Diane and I already have an arrangement where I donate to her animal shelter and she donates to the malaria fund I support, instead of each of us just funding our own cause, and that has never bothered me in the slightest — if anything I'm proud of it. If someone tells me the voting deal is wrong but the donation deal is fine, I'd like to understand what the actual difference is, because right now I can't see one and it feels like I should be able to.
