I help run a small animal-welfare charity and our board has the same arguement every quarter. This time the trigger is real: a developer wants to drain a seasonal marsh near us and put up warehouses, and one faction says we should quietly cheer, on the grounds that marsh life is mostly r-selected invertebrates and amphibians with horrific juvenile mortality, so less marsh means less suffering. The other faction says that's hubris dressed up as maths. Before we pick a side I want to know what we'd even be asking of a method for answering this kind of question.

So: write the desiderata. What does any adequate procedure for estimating whether a habitat intervention raises or lowers net wild-animal welfare have to be able to do? Cover the phenomena it must account for — my list so far is that the intervention changes which animals exist, not just how existing ones fare; that it changes species composition, not just abundance (generalists move in as specialists crash, so welfare per hectare could rise while diversity falls); and that the counterfactual isn't 'marsh forever', it's marsh until the next drought or the next planning application, so the baseline is itself a guess. The distinctions it can't collapse: total vs average welfare, deaths caused vs lives prevented, uncertainty about sentience vs uncertainty about numbers. The results from ecology it can't contradict. For each desideratum give the case that motivates it, and say whether you think it's a genuine requirement or one faction's prejudice in disguise.

Two things I especially want. First, whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect 'sensitive to invertebrate sentience', 'usable with the data a small charity can actually get', and 'not decided in advance by whichever taxon has the most individuals' can't all hold at once, but I can't prove it. Second, which single desideratum knocks out the most methods currently on the table.

Then, briefly, score the three approaches we keep hearing against the list: hedonic accounting (estimate net welfare per species per hectare, multiply out), the 'count deaths averted' shortcut, and the conservationist line that habitat has value beyond welfare. Keep the scoring short — the desiderata are the deliverable. Assume the purpose is advising a real decision about a real marsh this year, not writing a paper.
