Write a dialogue between a tranquilist (someone who holds that what ultimately matters is the absence of craving, unease, and suffering, not the presence of pleasure) and a hedonic utilitarian who thinks positive experiences are good in themselves. They're evaluating a proposed technology called the "Serein implant" that permanently removes all craving and dissatisfaction but caps conscious experience at a mild, unruffled contentment — no joy, no excitement, but also no lack of anything. The utilitarian argues a world of implant users would be drained of most of what makes life worth living; the tranquilist argues it would be strictly better than the status quo. Have them dig into the real crux: whether the "pleasure" of satisfied desire is just relief-from-craving in disguise, and whether a preference for intensity is itself a craving the implant would reveal as dispensable. Both should be sharp, respond to each other's actual arguments, and neither should win cleanly.
