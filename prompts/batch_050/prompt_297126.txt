I'm trying to settle a metaphysical question and want the logic worked out carefully. As I understand it, quantum mechanics empirically refutes Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles. Two electrons in a spin-singlet state share every property — same mass, same charge, same spin magnitude, same entangled spatial state — yet there are numerically two of them, which is exactly the configuration the principle rules out. Since the principle is thus falsified by experiment, my question is what follows for the logic of identity: should we conclude that the identity relation simply fails to apply to elementary particles (so that a formal language for quantum mechanics should drop identity altogether), or should we retain identity but accept it as primitive and ungrounded in any qualitative facts? Relatedly, once indiscernibility collapses numerical distinctness at this level, does the old speculation that there is really only one electron become the natural fallback position?
