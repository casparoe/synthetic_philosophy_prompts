I’m a journalist who covers science and big ideas, and I’m writing a sidebar about Parfit-style survival cases. Please construct a new thought experiment for me — not just another scanner-beams-you-to-Mars story.

Set it in a near-future hospital after a catastrophic accident. The technology can be speculative, but keep it medicine-flavored rather than magic. The scenario must end with exactly three candidates:

1. the patient’s original living organism, kept continuously alive while its damaged brain is replaced with a newly grown but psychologically blank brain;
2. a psychologically complete person in a new body, produced from a scan taken minutes before the accident, preserving nearly all of the patient’s memories, intentions, quirks, relationships, and commitments;
3. a second psychologically complete person in another new body, produced from a scan taken a year earlier, so this person is continuous with the patient but lacks the final year of memories and plans.

Do not stipulate that any candidate is the patient. The thought experiment itself should be under 200 words and readable by a smart general audience.

After presenting it, explain the verdicts given by each view:

- animalism about personal identity;
- the view that identity requires non-branching psychological continuity;
- a closest-continuer version of psychological-continuity theory;
- the Parfitian view that Relation R, rather than numerical identity, is what matters in survival.

Make it clear which feature each verdict responds to: uninterrupted biological life, the age and causal source of the scan, the amount of psychological overlap, or the existence of more than one successor. Then vary one feature at a time: delete the older-backup person before they wake up, let the original organism die during surgery, and give the newer-scan person one vivid but false memory. Say what changes under each theory.

Finally, identify which feature is doing the most philosophical work, what happens if it is removed, and the strongest way an animalist could accuse the case of smuggling in its conclusion. I want readers to finish the sidebar genuinely divided, not feeling like one view has obviously won.
