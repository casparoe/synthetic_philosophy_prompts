I am finalizing a short statement of my considered views on the aggregation of harms, to be circulated at the start of a graduate seminar in normative ethics, and I would like a consistency audit before it goes out. My commitments are the following:

1. The dust-speck verdict. One person's being subjected to severe torture over an extended period is worse than any number of people's each suffering a single dust speck in the eye. There is no finite multiplier at which the specks come out worse.

2. Monotonicity. Adding a genuine harm to an outcome, with no compensating benefit, always makes the outcome at least slightly worse. In particular, n+1 people's receiving dust specks is worse than n people's receiving them, for every n.

3. Transitivity. The relation "all things considered, morally worse than" is transitive.

4. Local trade-offs. For any pain of a given intensity suffered by some population, there is a pain of very slightly lower intensity such that its being suffered by a sufficiently larger population is at least as bad. At small scales of intensity difference, badness is smoothly tradeable against numbers.

5. Continuity of harm. Serious harms and trivial harms differ only in degree, not in kind. There is no morally significant line of category between the worst and the mildest sufferings; there is only more and less of one thing.

Please audit this set. I want to know which commitments genuinely stand in tension with which others — whether pairwise or in larger combinations — and for each genuine tension: a precise statement of why the conflict arises, a severity rating relative to the other tensions you identify (do not present them as equally serious), and the least costly repair, with an explicit statement of what that repair sacrifices. For any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, show that it dissolves and explain why it might have looked real; I suspect at least one of the pairs above is innocent, and I would rather be shown which than have the whole list flagged indiscriminately. If two of my commitments turn out to be doing the same work, or if one is a disguised restatement or a consequence of another, please note that as well. Finally, if a single revision would restore the consistency of the whole package at the lowest total cost, identify it and say what it costs me.

To be clear about the scope: I am not asking you to adjudicate the first-order merits of the dust-speck verdict itself, nor to recommend which horn of any dilemma I should prefer. I want to know whether the package coheres, where it strains, and at what price the strain can be relieved.
