so i'm writing a novel where one of the main characters is a cognitive scientist who's convinced she's losing her mind, and she scribbles poems in the margins of her old textbooks. i need a villanelle she'd write about the multiple drafts model of consciousness — the idea that there's no little theater inside the head where everything gets played on one screen for an audience of one, just lots of competing versions of experience being drafted and redrafted in parallel, with no master copy and no fact of the matter about which telling is the "real" one.

the form has to do the philosophical work, not just the words. a villanelle's two refrains keep coming back, and i want hers to come back slightly *wrong* each time — a word swapped out, a tense shifted, one of them eventually not showing up at all — so the reader slowly realizes there's no original line the others are misquoting. no first draft. that's the whole point of the theory, landed structurally instead of stated. the old picture (the self as a tiny viewer sitting in the dark watching the show) should haunt the poem as the thing being dismantled — maybe as a refrain that gets promised and never quite arrives.

it's going as the epigraph to part two, so it has to work as a poem first: someone who's never heard of dennett should still find it genuinely unsettling, and someone who has should feel like she got it right. standard villanelle skeleton otherwise, 19 lines, two rhymes — the drifting refrains are the one sanctioned rule-break. don't make it cute. she's scareed.
