So a while back I came across this game idea where changing the rules is itself a move in the game, and it got me obsesed with the question of whether a set of rules can protect itself from being changed -- like can a rule say "I can't be repealed" and what happens when someone proposes to repeal it anyway. I run a weekly game/puzzle night at our local library, usually 10-14 adults and older teens, about 75 minutes. Design me a playable game that makes people feel this problem in their gut instead of just hearing about it: the group should start with a few simple written rules (including rules about how rules get changed), play for points or some small stake, and somewhere in the middle they should actually run into a genuine tangle -- e.g. someone proposes a rule that's self-defeating, or tries to entrench a rule permanently, or two rules contradict each other and nobody can agree on which one governs the vote about which one governs. Give me the exact starting rule set (keep it short, like 6-8 rules), how scoring and winning works so there's real incentive to manipulate the rules, and how to handle disputes when the rules genuinely don't settle something (do we need a judge? can the judge's powers be amended?). Then a short debrief script that connects what happened to the idea of self-amending legal systems -- but only after they've hit the wall themselves, no explaining upfront. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this (my group loves rules-lawyering) and one thing about the real version of this problem that a card game at a library just can't capture.
