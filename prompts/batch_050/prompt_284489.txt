In my philosophy of mind reading group, the Searle–Dennett dispute over original and derived intentionality has been stuck for as long as anyone there has followed it, and it was stuck for decades before that. Searle's Chinese Room is meant to show that no amount of rule-governed symbol manipulation constitutes understanding: the person inside the room follows instructions without grasping any Chinese, so the room as a whole understands nothing, so syntax by itself never yields semantics. Dennett's reply, as I understand it, runs through artifacts like the two-bitser: a coin-sorting machine built to accept one country's quarters, then shipped somewhere whose lookalike coins it proceeds to sort instead, is about quarters only courtesy of its designers' intentions, and Dennett's point is that human intentionality is derived in just the same way — conferred on us by natural selection rather than by engineers — so there is no special "original" intentionality for the room to lack in the first place. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are not disagreeing about anything checkable: Searle is making a claim about what understanding essentially is, and Dennett is making a claim about where aboutness comes from, with each treating the other's question as already settled by his own framework. Is that the right diagnosis — two different questions prosecuted under one heading — or is there a genuine substantive disagreement underneath, say about whether causal-historical grounding of the right kind could itself constitute meaning? Please support your diagnosis by identifying what each argument actually commits its proponent to, and then say what, if anything, would count as progress: a sharper criterion for understanding, a finding from AI or cognitive science that one side would have to concede, an agreed-upon test case, or an argument that the impasse is permanent, with reasons.
