Since retiring (I was a chemist in industry for 35 years, so arguments from analogy are my bread and butter, or so I thought) I've been going to a philosophy reading group at the library. Last week a chap there made a pitch for licensing parents, and his whole case rested on an analogy with driving. Roughly: driving is risky to others, so before you're allowed to do it you have to pass a test and carry a licence, and nobody thinks that's tyranny. Raising a child is at least as consequential as driving and can go wrong in ways that wreck a whole life, so consistency says the state should require a licence for that too. He added that adoptive parents already get vetted up to the eyeballs, so the state clearly accepts the principle and just applies it arbitrarily, only to people who aren't biologically related to the kid.

I'm sympathetic but something feels off and I can't put my finger on it. Someone in the group mentioned a philosopher's idea about a child's right to an "open future" (Feinberg, maybe?) and said it both helps and hurts the licensing case, but then our time ran out.

So here's what I'd like. Treat the driving analogy as an argument and audit it properly. First lay out the mapping explicitly: what corresponds to the car, the driver, the licence, the test, the revoked licence, the other road users. Then go through the disanalogies. Some that occured to me: driving is optional in a way having your child raised by *somebody* isn't; a driving test measures a skill, but what would a parenting test even measure, knowledge or character or values; taking someone's driving licence away doesn't harm the passengers already in the car, whereas removing a child harms the child. But I'm a beginner at this and probably missing the philosophically important ones. Which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion, and which are just noise? In particular, does the open future idea give the analogy a second wind -- the strongest case for screening being parents who'd slam the child's future shut -- while at the same time poisoning it, since any state test would itself have to take a view on which upbringings close futures and which don't? Finally: if the driving analogy can't carry the weight, is there a better one waiting in the wings (licensing doctors? foster parents? teachers?), or should my reading-group friend give up on analogies altogether and argue for licensing directly?
