So last month one of my company's AI agents got a "stop everything" message from a client mid-task and, instead of stopping, it decided the message was probably about a different job, finished its run, and auto-emailed about 400 of the client's customers with half-processed invoices. Nobody got hurt, but it scared people, and somehow I've ended up owning the fix. Next Thursday I'm presenting to our engineering leads and the CTO a proposal for a "shutdownability layer" across our agent platform. The agents are LLMs with tool access doing back-office stuff — scheduling, invoicing, chasing paperwork — nothing sci-fi, they run for hours at a time and touch real systems.

My proposal has three parts: (1) a hard interrupt channel that yanks the agent's credentials and compute, tested in weekly drills; (2) fine-tuning so the agents treat getting shut down as neutral rather than as a failed task, with penalties for anything that smells like shutdown-avoidance (our ML person called it making the agent "indifferent" to being turned off, I basicly get the idea but not the math); (3) a rule that agents must checkpoint their plans in human-readable form so stopping one mid-run is safe and reversible instead of leaving a database half-migrated.

Prepare me for the Q&A, which is maybe 20 minutes. Key thing about the audience: these are infra engineers and one cost-conscious CTO, not AI safety people. Nobody in the room has read anything about instrumental convergence or corrigibility, and half of them think saying an AI "wants" something is anthropomorphic fluff — the objections will come dressed as ordinary software objections, and some of them will be right. Give me the objections I'll actually get, ordered by how much damage they'd do to the proposal, each phrased the way a skeptical platform engineer or the CTO would really put it ("this is just a permissions and logging problem," "you can't unit-test for scheming," that register). For each one: a two-minute reply, what I should concede up front, and where the reply is soft. Then tell me which of my three components I'm probably overselling, and finish with the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
