"Jackson asks us to imagine Mary, a brilliant scientist who knows every physical fact about color vision — wavelengths, retinal processing, the whole neurophysiology — but who has lived her entire life in a black and white room. When she leaves and sees a red tomato for the first time, she learns something: what seeing red is like. Since she already had all the physical information, what she learns must be a non-physical fact, so physicalism is false.
