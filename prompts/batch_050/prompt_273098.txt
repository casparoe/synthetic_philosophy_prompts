Okay so I'm a novelist, fifteen years in, and lately all my writer friends are panicking about LLMs producing fiction. The line I keep hearing is that AI can't REALLY do metaphor — it just shuffles dead metaphors around, there's nothing behind it, it doesn't feel anything, etc. etc. I went down a rabbit hole on this and ended up reading about Davidson's argument that metaphors don't have any special metaphorical meaning at all — that a metaphor means only what its words literally mean, and everything interesting happens on the reader's side, in what the metaphor makes you notice or see. It's all effect, no hidden message.

And here's what's been bugging me: if Davidson's right, doesn't the whole "AI can't do metaphor" argument collapse? If the work of a metaphor happens entirely in the reader, then who cares what was going on in the writer's head? Or does Davidson's account secretly need a speaker who is intentionally trying to get you to notice something — and is *that* where the LLM falls short? I genuinely can't tell.

Then on the other side there's the Lakoff-style conceptual metaphor crowd, where metaphor is supposedly grounded in embodied experience — like, we say prices "rise" because as a kid you watched piles of stuff grow as more got added, that kind of thing. Would those folks say LLMs are permanently locked out of real metaphor because they have no bodies and no childhoods? Or, awkwardly, would they have to concede that LLMs are actually amazing at metaphor, since they've soaked up basically the entire conceptual-mapping structure of human language from text? Both answers seem available and both seem kind of embarassing for somebody.

What I want: reconstruct what each side would say about an LLM writing, say, the metaphors in my next novel. Davidson died before any of this existed, so you'll have to extrapolate from his actual position rather than just gesturing at it — same deal for the conceptual metaphor people (they're around but haven't settled it for me). Push each view to its real conclusion, including the uncomfortable bits. And at the end, tell me which verdict you actually buy and why — no "both sides have a point" shrug, I get enough of that at dinner parties.
