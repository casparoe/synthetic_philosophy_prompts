I got into an argument with a friend about whether infinity is “real,” and he sent me this summary of what he takes Cantor, Hilbert’s hotel, and finitism to show:

Cantor proved that there are genuinely different sizes of infinity. An infinite list of natural numbers can never contain every real number, because whatever list you give him, Cantor’s diagonal method constructs another real number that differs from every entry on the list. Hilbert’s hotel illustrates the same basic weirdness: even if every room is occupied, the manager can fit in one more guest by moving everyone from room n to room n+1, or an entire infinite busload by moving everyone from n to 2n. Since the hotel was already full, this shows that an actually infinite collection can gain members without becoming any larger. In fact, the hotel can absorb any infinite crowd whatsoever, because infinity plus infinity is still infinity. Finitists reject this whole picture. They think endless processes make sense, but not completed infinite totalities, so their objection is not that Cantor made an ordinary logical mistake; it is that his proofs begin by pretending that all the natural numbers or real numbers are already there as a finished object. Modern physics seems to support the finitists, since space and time may be discrete at extremely small scales, so Cantorian infinity is probably just a useful fiction rather than something that could actually exist.

My friend is generally pretty careful, but I half-agree with him and can’t put my finder on exactly where the summary slides from “infinite sets behave counterintuitively” into something stronger.

Please audit it sentence by sentence. What would a set theorist accept as basically right? Which parts use loose language that can be made precise in a harmless way? Are there places where he conflates the number of rooms in the hotel with whether every room is occupied, or countably many new guests with any infinite collection of new guests? Also, is “Cantor proved there are different sizes of infinity” better put in terms of no bijection/no surjection, rather than “more members”?

Then explain the hotel cases without relying on a slogan like “infinity plus infinity equals infinity”: one extra guest, countably many buses, and guests labeled by the real numbers. Would the last crowd fit if each person still needed their own numbered room?

Finally, what exactly is the finitist denying, and what would the diagonal argument look like if it were stated as cautiously as possible without assuming more than necessary? Even if physics turned out to be fundamentally discrete, would that settle the mathematical issue, or only the question of what infinity physically instantiates? I’m less interested in who wins rhetorically than in which parts of my friend’s summary survive a careful correction.
