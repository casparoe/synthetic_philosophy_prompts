Okay this one's a bit out there but bear with me. I've been chewing on the insect sentience literature for a while now (the Rethink Priorities-adjacent stuff, the expected-value arguments where even a small credence in bug suffering times something like 10^18 insects alive at any moment threatens to swamp basically everything else on the moral priority list), and I want a poem about the *scale argument itself*. Not a poem about how bugs are neat or factory farming is bad — a poem about the structure of the move where a tiny probablity and an absurdly huge number multiply into a conclusion you'd really rather not have to deal with.

Form: a villanelle. The constraint I care about: the two refrains should be the two factors in the product. One line carries the low credence (something in the register of "it's almost certainly nothing"), the other carries the scale (the sheer count of insects). The way villanelle refrains alternate all poem and then finally stand next to each other in the closing couplet should *be* the multiplication — the reader should feel the two lines converge in those last two lines the way the argument converges on "this might dominate everything else." Someone who knows the argument should be able to tell instantly which refrain is which and why they had to meet at the end.

One more thing: keep the asymmetry honest. The scale refrain should be the confident one — we genuinely do have a decent grip on the population numbers, at least to within orders of magnitude. The sentience refrain is where all the doubt lives. That asymmetry is the engine of the whole argument and also its weakest joint, and the poem should let that show rather than quietly resolving it. If the poem ends up smug about the conclusion it's failed.

After the poem, one section titled "Where the poem leans on contested stuff" (~150 words): tell me where the poem took a side or fudged something for the meter. Specifically — did any line treat a particular credence in insect sentience as if it were the agreed number? It shouldn't, because the range among people who actually work on this is enormous. Did it bake in the additive expected-value framing, which plenty of serious people push back on? Separate what's basically settled (the population figures, the logical form of the scale argument) from what's genuinely contested (the sentience estimates, whether expected-value reasoning applies cleanly at these extremes), and don't let the contested parts masquerade as consensus.

Length: one villanelle, 19 lines, plus the commentary. Don't explain the form back to me.
