I'm a high school student and this year I joined our school's history society, where we've spent the whole autumn term on one question: why did the Roman Empire fall, and did it fall at all? The debate has completely stalled, and I'd like your help understanding why, rather than another retelling of the arguments.

Here's where the discussion sits. One group defends the traditional picture in the spirit of Gibbon: a real civilization genuinely collapsed, and you can roughly date it, with 476 and the deposition of the last western emperor as a reasonable marker. They point to barbarian invasions, civil wars, and the loss of tax revenue as the machinery of collapse. A second group, influenced by the historians who study "late antiquity," insists that nothing fell; the Roman world was gradually transformed into the medieval one, Roman law, language, and Christianity carried on, and calling 476 a "fall" is just an administrative technicality dressed up as a catastrophe. A third group wants to skip the political story entirely and says the real causes were material: climate change, the plagues, soil exhaustion, maybe even lead poisoning. Every side keeps talking past every other side. The traditionalists produce dates and battles, the transformation people produce continuity of pottery styles and saints' lives, the materialists produce ice cores and plague mortality, and nobody has changed their position in three months.

My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is mostly about what would have to be true for it to count as a "fall" -- the sides are using one word for different events, one about political institutions, one about everyday culture, one about population and living standards -- and that once you split those apart, each side might be right about its own question. But I'm not confident in this. Maybe there is a real factual disagreement hiding underneath, for instance about how bad ordinary life actually got in the fifth century west, and if so I'd like to know which facts would settle it.

So please give me a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal, with the camps answering different questions under one name? Is it substantive but about standards of evidence, with the sides disagreeing about which traces of the past should count as data? Is there a hidden empirical question doing the real work? Or are these just incompatible intuitions about what makes a civilization the same civilization over time, which no amount of archaeology could move? Support the diagnosis with what each side actually says, not with caricatures, and tell me honestly whether my hypothesis about the word "fall" is right, partly right, or a dodge. Then tell me what progress would look like given your diagnosis: a distinction we should adopt, a question we should stop asking, a piece of evidence that would genuinely decide something, or an argument that the impasse is permanent and why.

Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way you'd write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables. I'd rather have a single sustained argument than a structured breakdown.
