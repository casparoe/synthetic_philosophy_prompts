So I keep getting into the same argument in comment sections (LessWrong-adjacent places, substack comments, you know the drill) about whether it's fair to judge historical figures by modern standards. Someone posts about Jefferson owning slaves, someone else jumps in with "he was a man of his time," and then the thread goes in circles for 200 comments because everyone is talking past each other about what question we're even answering.

What I want is a decision tree / flowchart I can link people to that sorts them into their actual position on the "man of his time" defense based on their verdicts about concrete cases, not based on which abstract label sounds good to them. Kind of like those "which ethical theory do you actually endorse" quizzes, but for this specific debate.

Requirements:

- The tree should start from a shared setup (something like: person X in era Y did thing Z which we now consider clearly wrong, and it was within normal practice for their society) and each node should be a question about a concrete case or a concrete variation on it, answerable by someone who's never heard the words "moral relativism" or "presentism." So NOT questions like "do you think morality is relative?" -- instead questions like "suppose plenty of people in his own time were publicly arguing slavery was wrong and he'd read some of those arguments. does he get less slack?" Yes/no or three-way answers are fine.
- Leaves should include at least: (a) judge them fully by today's values, context be damned; (b) only judge by the standards available at the time, so if it was normal, no blame; (c) the "contemporary dissent" test, where it hinges on whether the person could plausibly have known better given debates happening around them; (d) the view that we can condemn the act but not the person; (e) the view that the whole question conflates assessing someone with honoring them (statues, buildings, holidays) and these need totally separate treatment; (f) the view that moral ignorance excuses the same way factual ignorance does. If you think one of these collapses into another, show that in the tree and flag it instead of pretending they're distinct.
- Also: somewhere the tree should handle the distinction between "this person was average-bad for their time" vs "this person was bad even relative to their peers" (Columbus was reportedly cruel even by the standards of his contemporaries, Churchill had critics in his own day, etc), and the question of whether actively benefitting from the practice changes anything vs. just passively going along with it.
- Max depth about 5 questions so it's actually usable. If you can't get all the leaves separated within that, say which positions end up lumped together and whether that's because they're secretly the same view or just because the tree ran out of room.
- If there are combinations of answers that lead nowhere -- i.e., a coherent-sounding set of verdicts that no actual position in this debate holds -- note them and take a guess at why (incoherent? just unpopular?).

Format: give me the tree as a JSON object with a "nodes" array, where each node has "id", "question" (the case-based question, written out), "answers" (mapping each possible answer to either the id of the next node or to a "leaf" object with "name" and "gloss" fields), and a "notes" field if anything needs flagging at that node. After the JSON, a short plain-prose section (like 150 words) noting which leaves you think the loudest voices in this debate actually occupy and where the tree can't tell people apart.

American English please, and keep the questions punchy enough that I could actually paste them into a flowchart tool without editing.
