I am helping a public commission draft precautionary rules for advanced artificial systems that might have some form of moral status. The commission has proposed a hypothetical “digital-mind triage index” built from six kinds of evidence:

1. **Consciousness-relevant architecture** — recurrent processing, global integration of information, persistent internal states, and other structural features associated with conscious experience in biological minds.
2. **Internal valence** — representations that appear to function as positive or negative evaluations and that systematically guide the system’s behavior.
3. **Flexible goal pursuit** — the ability to preserve coherent objectives across changing circumstances rather than merely producing situation-specific outputs.
4. **Self-modeling** — representations of the system’s own boundaries, capacities, past states, and possible future states.
5. **Social self-presentation** — expressions of distress, preferences, attachment, or a desire not to be modified or terminated.
6. **Independent behavioral corroboration** — avoidance, resistance, learning, or resource expenditure that remains consistent with the reported states even when the system has not been prompted to describe them.

Rank these six evidence types twice: first according to how strongly they support the claim that a digital system **can be benefited or harmed**, and second according to how strongly they justify granting the system **procedural legal protections**, such as review before deletion, restrictions on experimentation, or representation of its interests. Assign percentage weights summing to 100 within each ranking.

In your answer, explain the largest differences between the two rankings; identify at least one type of evidence that is highly persuasive socially but comparatively weak philosophically; and assess whether internal valence should matter more or less than consciousness-relevant architecture. Do not assume that a system’s statements about suffering are either straightforward evidence or worthless merely because they may be imitative. Finally, specify one discovery that would cause you to change the top-ranked evidence type in each ranking.
