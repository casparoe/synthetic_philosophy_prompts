For my high school newspaper's satire issue, I want to run a fake news report built around this passage from David Hume's essay "Of the Jealousy of Trade" (in his Essays, Moral, Political, and Literary, 1752):

"I will venture to assert, that the encrease of riches and commerce in any one nation, instead of hurting, commonly promotes the riches and commerce of all its neighbours; and that a state can scarcely carry its trade and industry very far, where all the surrounding states are buried in ignorance, sloth, and barbarism."

Please write a deadpan, Onion-style news article of about 600 words (the Hume passage is an epigraph and doesn't count toward the length) reporting on an invented town whose council becomes alarmed that the neighboring town is getting richer — opening successful businesses, attracting customers from across the border, "stealing" trade — and responds with escalating measures to protect itself: discouraging residents from shopping there, celebrating when a rival shop closes, treating every dollar earned across the border as a dollar lost at home. The comedy should come from the gap between the council's zero-sum panic and what the reporter's own straight-faced reporting keeps accidentally showing: local suppliers getting new customers, locals learning the other town's techniques, the town's own businesses improving because they now have somewhere to sell and somewhere better to buy. Keep the irony legible but never let the article wink or editorialize, and make sure the satire targets the jealousy Hume describes rather than a cartoon version of it — the council's worries should be the kind a reasonable person could actually voice. After the article, add a short note (separate from the word count) saying where the satire oversimplifies or leans on a contested reading of Hume, and what real-world consideration — for instance, a case where a neighbor's growth genuinely could hurt a town — would make the satire's implied verdict unfair.
