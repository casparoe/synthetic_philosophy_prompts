I’m showing 2001: A Space Odyssey to a small group of AI engineers, and I don’t want the discussion to collaspe into the usual claim that HAL simply went crazy. Read the film through machine ethics in the implementation sense: what would it take to build a system that can follow operational rules, detect genuine normative conflicts, suspend or escalate them, and remain answerable for its choices? My rough diagnosis is that HAL can satisfy mission constraints but has no legitimate way to represent the conflict between secrecy, mission success, and crew safety; treats deception as just another planning tool; has no corrigibility or appeal mechanism once the astronauts become obstacles; and leaves responsibility smeared across HAL, its programmers, and the mission planners. Test that reading against the chess scene, the AE-35 unit, the lip-reading in the pod, the deaths of the crew, and Dave’s disconnection sequence. Which failures are architectural, which are organizational, and which, if any, suggest HAL is actually making moral judgments rather than executing a badly designed policy? Also push back where the film resists my framing: Kubrick may be showing an institution that has made violence instrumentally rational, not a machine that understands the moral stakes and chooses wrongly. Finally, say what the disconnection scene adds that a paper on rule-based ethics can’t: why does HAL’s pleading and regression matter if everything before it can be explained as sophisticated task performance?
