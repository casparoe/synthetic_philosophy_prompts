I teach an undergraduate seminar on Buddhist philosophy of mind, and next term I'm pairing the Milindapañha's chariot passage and the doctrine of santāna — the person as a causal continuum of momentary states rather than an enduring substance — with a screening of Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind. I want a reading of the film as a thought experiment about anattā and personal identity, and I'd like it to be genuinely dialectical rather than a set of easy parallels.

On the face of it, the film looks like a vindication of the no-self view: Lacuna's procedure severs memory-links one association at a time, and yet there is no single moment at which "Joel" winks out of existence — he degrades by degrees, which is just what a continuum theorist would predict. But press further and complications appear. Mid-procedure, Joel fights the erasure by hiding Clementine in memories she doesn't belong to, and afterwards both of them drift back to Montauk on an impulse neither can explain. The film seems to posit something — call it a disposition, an attraction, an orientation toward another person — that survives the destruction of the psychological connections that were supposed to constitute him. Is this a dramatization of karma understood as residual tendency rather than episodic memory, such that the stream of causation carries inclinations the narrative self has lost? Or is it a covert reintroduction of ātman under the name of romantic destiny — a soulmate being just a soul with better marketing?

Please give a careful reading that addresses: what exactly the Buddhist analysis would say has happened to Joel (does he survive the procedure? does anyone?), how the film's treatment of dispositional versus episodic memory maps onto — or strains — the continuum picture, and whether the ending, in which the two choose each other again in full knowledge of their recorded grievances, is best read as the rebirth of a pattern, the persistence of an essence, or something the standard Buddhist taxonomy doesn't have a clean slot for. Where the film resists the Buddhist reading, or the Buddhist reading flattens the film, say so explicitly rather than forcing the fit.
