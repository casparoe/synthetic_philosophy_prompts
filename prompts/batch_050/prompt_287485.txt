My best friend from grad school is getting married and I'm giving the toast. She works on quantum gravity — specifically the idea that spacetime isn't fundamental but emerges from quantum entanglement, that what we call distance and geometry is really a shadow of how strongly systems are correlated with each other. Half the guests are her physics collegues who will catch every reference and cringe at every oversimplification; the other half are family who think she studies black holes, somehow.

Write a four-minute toast built on this move: if space itself is woven out of entanglement, then closeness was never really about location — two things can be so correlated that a complete description of one stops making sense without the other, and if she's right that's not a metaphor for marriage, it's the deepest thing physics knows about togetherness. The physicists should nod because the analogy is structurally sound, not just pretty; the aunts should tear up anyway. One optional beat: the monogamy of entanglement (a system can only be *maximally* entangled with one partner) — but only use it if you can keep it tasteful and not weird. Constraints: no more than a paragraph of actual physics explanation, don't say the word "quantum" more than twice, and end with an actual raised glass, not a summary.
