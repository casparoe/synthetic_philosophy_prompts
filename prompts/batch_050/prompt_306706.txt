So I'm TAing for an undergrad seminar on philosophical methodology this semester, and next week's session is on intuition pumps -- how thought experiments work on us rhetorically, when they illuminate and when they just bullly us into an intuition by controlling the framing. The canonical cases we're covering are all the greatest hits: Thomson's violinist, Nozick's experience machine, the trolley problem(s), Searle's Chinese room, Jackson's Mary's room, twin earth, Gettier cases. Dennett's point that these are basically little machines for manufacturing intuitions, and that you should always try turning the knobs to see if the intuition survives.

Here's my problem. I want to end the session with an exercise where I present the students with a NEW intuition pump, one that isn't a re-skin of any of the classics, and have them diagnose how it's trying to manipulate them. And ideally it should do something none of the ones on my list do. The classics mostly work by stripping away detail (trolley), by swapping a familiar substrate for an unfamilar one (Chinese room), or by isolating one feature of a rich concept (violinist, experience machine). I want a pump that misleads in some *other* way -- different mechanism of persuasion entirely.

So: construct me a new intuition pump, on any philosophical topic you like, that (a) actually generates a strong intuition, and (b) does so via a manipulative mechanism that isn't just detail-stripping, substrate-swapping, or feature-isolation. Then explain to me, as if prepping me for the seminar, exactly where the trap is -- what the pump makes you not notice, and which knob-turns break the spell. If you can also tell me whether you think the pump could be *fixed* to be fair rather than misleading, or whether the trick is load-bearing, that would be great.
