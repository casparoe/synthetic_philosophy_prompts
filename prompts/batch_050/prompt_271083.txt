So I help run a small policy shop (three of us, tiny budget) that advises a regional government on tech regulation, mostly AI procurement stuff right now. The deeper I get into this work the more I keep bouncing off the old "do artifacts have politics" debate -- Winner's side saying technologies can embody political arrangements, the social-construction-of-technology people saying design is contested and reversible all the way down, plus various positions in between. I don't want to re-litigate who's right. What I actually need is a defended agenda: given about two years and three people, which OPEN questions in this area would most change how we do our job if they got resolved, and in what order should we try to get traction on them?

A couple candidate questions to react to, but add your own:

1. The "politics by design vs politics by drift" question. Winner's famous cases are about deliberate or at least forseeable design choices. But a lot of the tech we regulate seems to acquire its politics later, through scale, network effects, who ends up owning it. Is there a principled way to tell when an artifact's political effects were baked in versus emergent, and does the answer change what regulators should even try to do at the design stage?

2. Whether "the artifact has politics" claims survive once the artifact is software that gets updated weekly. Classic examples (bridges, tomato harvesters) are static. A recommendation algorithm is a moving target. Does that make the whole framing obsolete, or just harder?

3. The boring-sounding one: is "neutrality" talk actually doing any harm in practice? Vendors and agencies both love saying tech is neutral. Does debunking that actually change procurement outcomes, or is it the kind of thing everyone nods at and nothing changes?

For each question tell me: what other questions depend on its answer, how tractable it is for a shop like ours (we can commission small empirical studies, we can't fund a research program), and what finding would actually change our advice versus what we'd do anyway. Also tell me which of my three you'd cut entirely and why. I'm suspicious #3 is the one that's overrated but I've been wrong before. Defend the ordering -- "its all important" isn't useful to me.
