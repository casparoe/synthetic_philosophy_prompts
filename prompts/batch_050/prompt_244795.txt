my gender and philosophy seminar just spent three weeks on the so-called gender-equality paradox — the finding that in the countries ranked most gender-equal, women are if anything less likely to go into STEM, not more. our discussion kept splitting into two readings: one says that once economic pressure is off, people are free to follow whatever dispositions they actually have, so the gap is preferences expressing themselves; the other says in poorer countries women pick lucrative secure fields out of necessity, so the gap there is smaller because of constraint, not nature. i want a villanelle for the last session, full 19 lines, where the two refrains each carry one of these readings so they keep circling back the way the argument itself did — every time we thought we'd settled it the same data seemed to support both sides at once. dont let the final quatrain come down on one side, just let the tension sit. it should be accurate enough that someone who knows the literature would nod along, but still a good poem for everyone else
