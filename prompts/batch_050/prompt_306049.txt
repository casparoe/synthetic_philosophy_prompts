Longtime LW/SSC reader here. For about a year now two of my friends have been having the same argument on our Discord and getting nowhere: one is a committed Nozickian about knowledge — knowledge is tracking truth, sensitivity and adherence conditions, and closure for knowledge is just false, you can know you have hands without knowing you're not a brain in a vat — and the other is a hardline closure defender who thinks abandoning closure means abandoning the whole practice of reasoning from what you know, and who reaches for something like a safety condition whenever the Nozickian wheels out a tricky case.

I've finally told them they have to run a proper adversarial collaboration and produce a joint statement. Naturally they've delegated the drafting to me, and I'm delegating it to you.

You are a professional epistemologist serving as the neutral editor of an adversarial collaboration — the kind that publishes a single document both camps sign. Think carefully about this. Before writing anything, brainstorm a long list of claims both sides might accept without filtering for obviousness, then cut down to the ones that actually do work.

The document I need:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section: what both can sign about Gettier cases, ordinary perceptual knowledge, lottery beliefs, and what an analysis of knowledge owes us. Nothing vacuous — each claim should be something a sensible third party (a sceptic, a contextualist, a knowledge-first person) could deny.

2. A disagreements table: the residual points of dispute, each stated twice, once in wording the Nozickian accepts as a fair statement of her view and once in wording the closure defender accepts for his. If you conclude that some part of the disagreement is verbal, flag which part and say why.

3. The cruxes — sub-questions such that settling them would genuinely move at least one side. My guesses: the brain-in-a-vat inference itself; rubbish-chute style cases (I drop a bag down the chute and seem to know it'll be in the basement shortly, yet if it had snagged on the way down I'd have believed exactly the same thing — insensitive, but knowledge?); and the method-individuation problem, where how coarsely you slice "the method by which the belief was formed" changes what the sensitivity condition even says. Feel free to add cruxes I've missed. For each one, name the case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, and write down in advance the verdict each side is commited to — including what outcome would count as their view taking damage.

Finally, each friend has to name one thing that would make them abandon their position. If the document ends with neither able to name such a thing, say so plainly and diagnose what that tells us about the debate.

Both of them are prickly and will reject anything slanted, so be careful with wording — the disagreements especially have to be phrased so each would sign their own side.
