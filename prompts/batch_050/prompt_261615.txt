So I run this informal philosophy group that meets at my flat every couple weeks, usually 8 to 12 people, and for next month I picked the Dutch book argument — the idea that if your degrees of belief don't line up like proper probabilities, someone can sell you a package of bets you'd happily accept that guarantees you lose money no matter how things turn out. None of us have formal training (I got into this through podcasts and one pop-science book), so a lecture would fall flat. Design an activity of about 75-90 minutes where people actually get stung: they announce their own betting odds on some propositions you set up, the others get to build bets against them, and the people whose odds were incoherent discover they've been booked even though nobody knew the truth of anything. If possible I'd love a second round where the problem isnt incoherent odds at one moment but someone shifting their odds over time in a dodgy way, so the group feels the difference between those two versions of the argument rather than just hearing it. Give me concrete rules, what props or materials I need, a token or points scheme that doesn't use real money but still stings a bit, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what actually happened onto the argument — including how to handle the person who inevitably says "well I'd just refuse those bets," since I gather that objection is both fair and sort of the whole point of a hidden assumption. Also warn me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I suspect anyone who knows probability will just game it) and be honest about which part of the real argument a game like this can't capture.
