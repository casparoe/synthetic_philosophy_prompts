So I got into this long argument with a friend last night about whether "generations" -- Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z, all that -- are real things or just marketing. My position, which I defended maybe more stubornly than I should have, is that they're not real kinds at all. Birth rates are basically a continuous curve, people are born every single day, and drawing a line between someone born December 1980 and someone born January 1981 and saying they belong to fundamentally different types of people seems like astrology with a spreadsheet. The boundaries aren't even stable -- different sources give different cutoff years, nobody agrees when Gen Z starts, and the whole thing seems driven by whoever wants to sell a book about managing Millennials in the workplace. My friend pushed back that cohorts genuinely share formative experiences (growing up with the internet vs. before it, entering the job market during a recession, 9/11 happening when you're 8 vs. 25) and that this makes generations more like real social kinds than I'm admitting, maybe like how "middle class" is fuzzy but not nothing.

What I want to know is: what would count against my view? Not just "here are some differences between cohorts," because I can explain those away with continuous trends plus age effects -- obviously a 70-year-old and a 25-year-old differ, that's just being different ages, not generational membership. So what would evidence or argument have to look like to show that cohorts are a genuine kind rather than an arbitrary slicing of a continuum? Are there observations that would settle it -- like discontinuities in some measurable variable at particular birth years, rather than smooth gradients? And what does my skeptical position actually risk -- is there anything that, if shown, would just flat-out refute me, or have I (without noticing) built my view so that every apparent counterexample gets absorbed as "that's just a continuous trend" or "that's just an age effect"?

(~150 words, an honest verdict on whether my position risks anything and whether that's a defect or just what being right looks like).
