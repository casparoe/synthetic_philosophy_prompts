I am a high school student trying to understand the conceivability argument about philosophical zombies. When I imagine a zombie, I picture a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a conscious person, including identical brain activity and behavior, and then mentally leave out any inner experience. But if the physical description is truly complete, am I genuinely conceiving a different possible world, or merely redescribing the same world while refusing to apply the word “consciousness”? Please explain the strongest version of the argument, where this “stipulation” objection applies, and how a defender might distinguish imagining consciousness absent from a complete physical duplicate from merely imagining water that is not H2O. Also explain what the argument would establish if successful: substance dualism, non-physical facts, or only a limitation of our physical concepts.
