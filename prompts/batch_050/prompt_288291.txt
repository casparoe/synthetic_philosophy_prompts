I am a novelist at work on a book whose central figure — a woman who has tended a remote lighthouse alone for two decades — records a recurrent visual aura in her journal using a sign of her own invention, a device I have borrowed from the diary example in Wittgenstein's Philosophical Investigations. The remark I keep circling is §270, where Wittgenstein imagines the diarist discovering by experience that whenever the sensation occurs, a manometer shows his blood-pressure rising, so that he can report his blood-pressure without any apparatus. Wittgenstein then argues that it becomes quite indifferent whether the diarist has identified the sensation correctly: even a regular mistake would make no difference, which shows, he says, that the supposition of such a mistake was a sham — as if one had turned a knob that looked as though it adjusted something in the machine but was a mere ornament, unconnected with the mechanism.

I would be grateful for a careful explanation of three things. First, what exactly the argument of §270 is: why does the possibility of a systematic, undetectable misidentification show that the notion of error was never genuinely in play, and what does the ornamental-knob image claim about attempts to fix a meaning by private ostensive definition? Second, applied to my character as I have written her — no manometer, no correlation between her sign and anything public, no other speaker — does the sign mean anything at all? Is Wittgenstein's point the familiar one that where nothing could count as getting it wrong, nothing counts as getting it right, or is §270 making a subtler claim about what gives an inner recognition its content? Third, what is the smallest change to her practice that would restore a genuine grammar to the sign: her own tracking of a public correlate, the arrival of a second person who might learn it, or something else entirely? Please distinguish what the argument strictly requires from what would merely make her practice more dramatic, and tell me whether the whole line of thought rests on premises about meaning and verification that a novelist of realist temperament about private experience could coherently refuse.
