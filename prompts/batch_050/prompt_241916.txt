So the Copycat literature has this idea of "fluid concepts" -- even in a tiny microdomain, concepts slip around because the pressure of the situation keeps redrawing where one ends and the next begins. I want a satirical piece that takes this dead seriously in a made-up microdomain: write the internal FAQ (call it v14, implying thirteen failed predecessors) of a fictional company called something like the Office of Lexical Standards, a one-man bureau in a mid-size firm tasked with keeping the company's house vocabulary stable. The vocabulary keeps drifting anyway: "urgent" now means "this week," "final draft" means "unless anyone objects," "small project" means anything the speaker doesn't want to staff. 450-600 words, deadpan bureaucratic voice, no winking.

The comedy has to come from the actual mechanics of conceptual slippage, not just "words change lol": meaning migrating one borderline case at a time (sorites-style), prototypes quietly replacing definitions, family resemblance doing the work once the definition dies, and the fact that every "clarification memo" the officer issues gets absorbed into the drift within a quarter. The FAQ should be structured so its own answers demonstrate the failure -- e.g., definitions that cite examples which have themselves slipped. End with a short, honest author's note (3-4 sentences) saying where the piece exaggerates for effect: which parts of the concept-drift picture are pretty settled among philosophers and cognitive scientists and which are still contested, without dressing a contested reading up as the consensus.
