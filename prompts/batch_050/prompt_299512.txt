Write a chapter from the internal employee handbook of a fictional firm — call it something bland like Meridian Consolidated — that has resolved to take meritocracy literally. Every pay packet, promotion, and desk assignment at the company is justified by an itemized annual Desert Statement, and this chapter, roughly 700 words and formatted as numbered sections, explains to staff how their statements are calculated. The comedy must come from the firm faithfully applying the standard philosophical problems with desert rather than dodging them, with each problem surfacing as a mundane line item or administrative rule. So: the statement includes deductions for advantages the employee did nothing to earn, which means the payroll office must somehow put a figure on inherited temperament; the effort column keeps collapsing into the talent column, since the capacity to make an effort turns out, on inspection, to be another thing nobody chose; and the handbook must specify how far down this regress the auditors are instructed to go before they are told to stop auditing and just issue the number. Include one worked example of a complete statement for a mid-level employee, and one deadpan sentence in the founder's own statement acknowledging that the ledger's baseline may be arbitrary. Work in, as an inconspicuous policy clause, the distinction between being entitled to one's pay because that is what the contract says and deserving it full stop, with the firm insisting it deals only in the second. End with a short FAQ answering the complaint staff actually raise — that they did not choose to be the kind of person who chooses to work hard — with the company's official reply, which should be technically correct and entirely unsatisfying. Keep the tone flat and procedural throughout, with no winking and no character who doubts the system; the target is the practice of justifying reward by appeal to desert, and a reader who knows the luck-based critiques of meritocracy should be able to name the argument behind each joke while a reader who doesn't still finds it funny.
