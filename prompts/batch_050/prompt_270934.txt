- She buys a $2 lottery ticket a few times a year, fully aware the expected value is negative, and says it's worth it for the fun of imagining.
- She pays about $40 a month for flood insurance on her apartment even though the area floods maybe once every few hundred years.
- At a wellness expo she refused to pre-pay $200 to a guy who swore his supplement regimine would add ten years to her life.
- A state-verified charity raffle, $5 a ticket, one-in-a-million shot at a $20 million prize: she declined and said one in a million might as well be zero.

Now the new case. So: what does she do? Which of the plausible rules behind her earlier verdicts is the prediction riding on — something like ignore-anything-below-a-probability-threshold, or a bounded/saturating utility function where astronomical payoffs stop counting, or discount-the-wild-story (which would treat the hospital very differently from the train station guy)? Which of the precedents is the closest analog to the new case? If her record genuinely underdetermines the answer, tell me what principle she'd be committing to with each verdict, and name the future case that would actually settle which rule she's running.
