Here's where I've landed on moral luck, roughly in order of how much I'd fight for each:

(1) I'm committed to some version of the control principle: people are morally assessable only for what was up to them. This is the load-bearing wall of the whole view.

(2) On resultant luck: the two drunk drivers who make the same bad choice on the same night, where one kills a pedestrian and the other makes it home because the pedestrian happened to stay on the curb, are equally culpable. Full stop. BUT I also think it's appropriate -- not just psychologically understandable, but fitting -- that the victim's family resents the killer more, and that our practices of blame legitimately treat the two cases differently.

(3) On circumstantial luck: the kindly shopkeeper who never faced a real test is no morally better than the collaborator who would have behaved identically in the shopkeeper's shoes. What matters is the will, not the world.

(4) More generally I think the quality of a person's will -- ill will, indifference, lack of due concern -- is the basis of blame. This is part of why I can say (2) and (3) with a straight face: outcomes and opportunities don't bear on the will, so they don't bear on blame.

(5) That said, I think it's unfair to fully blame people for callousness that was drilled into them by a brutal upbringing. Someone raised to be indifferent to suffering, who never had a realistic shot at becoming otherwise, gets a substantial excuse -- even when the indifference really is their indifference at the time of action.

(6) I'm against tracing theories as a general requirement: unwitting negligence can be blameworthy even when there's no prior knowing or reckless choice in the agent's history that it traces back to. Sometimes people are just blameworthy for a lapse.

(7) Methodologically: I want a theory that preserves most of our actual blame practices and verdicts in cases. I'm not interested in an error theory that says ordinary moral assessment is systematically confused. If the theory says our practices are rotten, so much the worse for the theory.
