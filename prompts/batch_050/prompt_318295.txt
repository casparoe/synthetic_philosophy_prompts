ok so i'm writing a novel where one of the characters is an overworked junior lawyer at a mid-range hotel chain, and the running gag is that head office keeps making him revise the guest-room cleanliness policy after guests keep disputing the "excessive soiling fee." For one chapter I need the actual prop document: the third revision of the chain's official Room Cleanliness Standard, maybe 400-500 words, deadpan corporate legalese, the kind of thing that gets laminated and stuck to the back of the door.

The comedy is that the policy is trying to legally define "dirty." Revision one just said rooms must be "left in reasonable condition," which lost them every dispute, and revision two tried examples ("stains, odors, general untidiness") which made things worse. So revision three gets quantitative. I want it to walk straight into the sorites paradox without ever naming it. For instance a clause built around crumbs: a room containing no crumbs is clean, and the addition of a single crumb never makes a clean room dirty -- and then you should be able to see the lawyer realize, mid-document, exactly where that principle leads, and panic in the drafting. Then he tries a hard cutoff instead (250 crumbs, exactly, and a fee schedule above that), which just generates fresh fights about what counts as a crumb versus a fragment versus a smear, and whether the 250th crumb is itself contestable. Somewhere in there I want a desperate clause distinguishing "dirty" from "definitely dirty," and a final sad appeal to "the standard of the reasonable guest" as though that settles anything.

Keep it completely straight-faced, no winking at the reader, no footnotes. The humor should come from the lawyer genuinely tripping over the real problem -- a reader who's ever spent an afternoon on heaps and bald men should be able to point at each clause and say exactly which move is being made (tolerance, stipulative sharp boundary, higher-order vagueness) and feel the satire is fair. Don't name any philosophers or use the word "sorites" -- this poor guy has never read the literature, he's just independently discovering it via litigation.
