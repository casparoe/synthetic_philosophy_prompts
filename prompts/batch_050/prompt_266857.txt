A friend of mine in cog sci keeps leaning on the desktop metaphor for consciousness: phenomenal experience is like your computer's GUI. Colors, shapes, sounds, the felt solidity of objects -- those are the icons and windows, simplified renderings that hide the machine code actually running underneath. He uses this to argue there's no hard problem left: once you explain why the brain would evolve a handy interface like that, youve explained consciousness, and asking what it's like to *be* an icon is just confused.

How far does the analogy actually hold? Give me the correspondence table first -- what plays the role of the icons, the files, the OS, the pixels, and crucially the user. Then walk me through where it breaks, esp. the part where a desktop implies someone sitting in front of the screen looking at it, which seems like exactly the thing consciousness is not supposed to have. Which disanalogies genuinely threaten his no-hard-problem conclusion and which are just noise? If the conclusion only survives because the metaphor is doing the work, say so plainly, and offer a better analogy if you can think of one.
