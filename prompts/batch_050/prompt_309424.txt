I'm running a reading group on formal epistemology next week and a friend cornered me with this argument that Dutch book arguments proove way too much, and I honestly can't find the hole. (1) If a set of credences can be Dutch booked -- meaning someone who knows your credences can offer you a package of bets, each one fair or favorable by your own lights, which taken together guarantee you lose money no matter what -- then those credences are irrational. (2) Standard result: any credences that violate the probability axioms can be Dutch booked. (3) Assigning different credences to two logically equivalent propositions violates the axioms, since equivalent propositions have to get the same probability. (4) Every actual human assigns different credences to some logically equivalent pair -- before I saw the proof I had credence 0.5 in some theorem and credence 1 in a tautology it turned out to be equivalent to, and the same is true of everyone for something. (5) So every actual human's credences can be Dutch booked. (6) So by (1), every actual human is irrational. (7) A rationality norm that literally everyone violates no matter what, and that nobody could satisfy without being logically omniscient, isn't a real norm -- ought implies can. (8) So premise (1) is false: being Dutch-bookable has nothing to do with irrationality, and the whole Dutch book tradition rests on a mistake.

But (8) seems nuts. Dutch books clearly diagnose *something* when someone gives P(A) = 0.7 and P(not-A) = 0.5, and I want to be able to say what. So the argument proves too much somewhere between (1) and (8), but each step looked fine when he walked me through it. If I had to keep one thing it'd be (2), since that's just the math, and I'd also hate to give up (7), because "you're irrational for not already knowing all the theorems" really does sound like a bad norm. Which step do I actually reject? Why did that step look fine? And what does rejecting it cost me in the easy cases -- like the person with the lopsided odds on A and not-A -- where the Dutch book diagnosis still has to come out correct?
