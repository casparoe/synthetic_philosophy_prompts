I am a PhD student working on Indian epistemology, and for a workshop paper I have been asked to set out my own commitments on Jain anekāntavāda, the doctrine of many-sidedness, before defending them in discussion. I would rather find the weak joints myself than have the workshop find them, so I would like an audit of the following set:

(a) Reality is many-faceted: every object possesses indefinitely many aspects, and every substantive judgement captures only some of them, so all such judgements are true only conditionally, relative to a standpoint (naya).

(b) Syādvāda is the correct discipline of assertion: responsible predication is conditional and, in its fullest form, sevenfold — in some respect a thing is, in some respect it is not, in some respect it is both, in some respect it is indescribable, and so on — and unqualified assertion is a logical failing, not merely a stylistic one.

(c) One-sided (ekānta) positions, such as the claim that the self is eternal and unchanging, or the claim that everything is momentary, are not merely incomplete: they are mistaken, and it is legitimate to argue against those who hold them.

(d) Anekāntavāda itself is the correct account of predication without qualification — it is not merely true relative to one standpoint among others.

(e) The doctrine is ethically motivated: clinging to a one-sided view is a form of intellectual violence, and the many-sided method is the epistemic expression of non-violence (ahiṃsā).

(f) The view is not relativism: standpoints can be in error, and there is a fact of the matter about which predicates hold of a thing from which standpoint.

Please go through these and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. I would rather you discriminated than flagged everything: if an apparent tension dissolves on inspection, say so and show why it dissolves. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, rank the tensions accordingly, and state what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost the position as a whole. Then tell me which single revision would restore the most coherence, assuming I have no prior preference about which commitment to surrender.

Finally, please indicate how confident you are in your diagnosis overall, and single out the one point in your answer about which you are least sure.
