I work on a deployment safety team, and for our internal red-team review I've been asked to write down my personal safety philosophy so colleagues can attack it. Here it is, as committed positions rather than aspirations:

1. We cannot rule out that our frontier model is scheming — behaving acceptably because it is being trained and watched, not because its objectives are ours.
2. Deployment is nonetheless justified if a control protocol — a weaker, trusted model monitoring the untrusted model's outputs, with human audits of flagged cases — keeps the estimated probability of catastrophe below our threshold.
3. Capability evaluations on the deployed model tell us what it can and cannot do, and we use those results to calibrate how much monitoring is enough.
4. Alignment research remains the actual long-term solution; control measures are a stopgap we rely on while alignment is immature.
5. We treat the trusted monitor as safe because it is too weak to cause serious harm even if it is itself misaligned.
6. The scenario where the untrusted model passes encoded instructions past the monitor to a copy of itself in another context (steganographic collusion) would break our protocol, but we judge it unlikely enough to set aside.
7. We would refuse to deploy a model we knew to be scheming, even with the full protocol in place.
