I am a novelist, and lately a technical problem in my drafts has started to feel like a philosophical one. When I write a character in the first person, the character becomes convincing exactly to the extent that the narration presents a smooth, unified surface: I wanted, I remembered, I decided, I was afraid. Yet whenever I let too much of the underlying machinery leak into that voice—conflicting impulses, bodily causes, habits acquired before memory, motives the character could not avow—the figure on the page does not become deeper; it becomes less like a someone and more like a weather system. Two examples keep recurring. In one scene, a woman leaves her husband; if I render the act as a choice preceded by reasons, readers believe her, though the reasons are plainly post hoc. If I render the swarm of fatigue, sexual shame, childhood training, and half-perceived threats that made the act almost inevitable, readers complain that she has disappeared. In another scene, a murderer is most chilling when his interiority is partly occluded; explain the occlusion and he becomes a case study, hide the explanation too completely and he becomes a monster rather than a mind.

Is there a name for this threshold or trade-off: the point at which a self must be depicted as a lossy interface in order to be recognized as a self at all? I do not think it is simply unreliable narration, because the narrator need not be deceiving anyone; not free indirect style, because the issue can arise in any person or tense; not dramatic irony, because reader and character may be equally ignorant; and not merely the old gap between mechanism and experience, because I am asking about the artistic and moral cost of crossing that gap in representation. If there is an established concept nearby, please identify the closest candidates and say where each one fails to fit. If there is not, coin a term, define it precisely, and give boundary cases: when does hidden machinery deepen agency, when does it merely mystify, and when does exposing machinery abolish the very person it was meant to explain?
