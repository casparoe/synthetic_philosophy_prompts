Write an essay about how you should act when you're not sure which moral theory is right. Here's the setup I want you to use: Maya is 60% confident in utilitarianism and 40% confident in a kantian view where lying is always seriously wrong, no exceptions. She can lie to a stranger to slightly increase the chance (say 5%) that a much bigger donation later goes to an effective charity instead of being squandered. Under utilitarianism the lie is clearly right, under the kantian view its a serious wrong. The essay should weigh at least these three approaches: (1) maximize "expected choiceworthiness" by somehow weighing how wrong each theory says the act is against how confident she is, (2) a moral-parliament style approach where her theories get votes proportional to her credence and negotiate or vote, and (3) just going with whichever theory she thinks is most likely true. The tricky part I want you to grapple with: expected choiceworthiness seems to need a way to compare how bad a kantian lie is against how good extra charity money is, and nobody seems to have a non-arbitrary way to do that -- but the other two options have their own problems (like the parliament letting a view she's barely confident in veto everything, or option 3 ignoring that she's genuinely uncertain). Don't just describe the options and shrug -- pick which one Maya should actually use and defend it against the strongest objection you can come up with.
