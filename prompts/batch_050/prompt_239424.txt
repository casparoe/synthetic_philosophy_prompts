I'm a bio major who ended up in a phil of biology seminar this semester, partly becuase my dad's on the transplant list and I kept turning over what it even means to say a heart is "working properly". Last week a guy in my discussion section walked me into this argument and I can't find the exit:

(1) To give the function of a trait is to say what it does that explains why organisms like us have it.
(2) What explains why we have the traits we have is natural selection acting on our ancestors.
(3) So a trait's function is the effect it was selected for — the thing its ancestral versions did that kept it around.
(4) But selection never acted on my heart. My heart is a brand-new token that never existed in any ancestral population; what explains why *I* have one is my development, not selection.
(5) So strictly speaking my heart has no function. At most, the type "human heart" does, and types don't pump blood or fail.
(6) But function-talk in medicine is about particular organs in particular bodies — my dad's failing heart, not some abstract type. So either that talk is systematically confused, or something in (1)–(3) is wrong.

He says this shows the etiological account of function collapses into type-level talk that can't ground a single actual diagnosis. I feel like something slipped between (3) and (5) but I can't name it. Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost? If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (1) than (6), since (6) is kind of the whole point of the concept for me.
