So my reading group spent last term on the conceivability argument against physicalism -- zombies, the whole setup -- and I watched the same exchange happen about four times. The anti-physicalist says: I can clearly conceive of a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me with nothing it is like to be it, so phenomenal consciousness must be something over and above the physical facts. The physicalist replies: you can't actually conceive that, you only think you can -- you conceive the physical setup while simply not attending to consciousness, and then you mistake that gap in your attention for a genuine absence. The anti-physicalist says the physicalist is just denying the datum, the physicalist says there is no datum, only a failure of imagination dressed up as one, and round it goes. Nobody has moved since the mid-90s as far as I can tell.

My working theory is that "conceivable" is quietly doing two jobs here -- something like "I can't see the contradiction on reflection" versus something stronger, like "an ideal reasoner with full information would still find this possible" -- and the two sides are operating with different success conditions without noticing, so each side's key move looks legitimate from inside and like cheating from outside. But maybe that's too neat. Maybe the real split is about whose intuition gets to count as the data, or about whether imaginability is evidence of possibility at all, or it's just bedrock and nothing would move anyone.

One thing that made me suspicious: when I asked both camps what would change their minds, both said a complete neuroscience of consciousness would vindicate *them*. That made me think the dispute can't be empirical in any normal sense.

So: diagnose it. What kind of disagreement is this actually -- verbal, about the standards conceivability has to meet, about which intuitions count as evidence, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? Ground the diagnosis in what each side actually says rather than in caricatures, tell me whether my two-senses hypothesis holds up or whether its something else, and say what, if anything, would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a sharper distinction, a shift in what the question even is, or an honest admission that nothing will move it and why.

Answer in British English please, and I'd rather have a real diagnosis than a balanced survey of positions.
