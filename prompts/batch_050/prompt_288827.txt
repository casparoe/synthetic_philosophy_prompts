I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine with a satire page, and I want a piece built on a passage I keep returning to: the opening of the second section of Nietzsche's On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life (1874), where he writes that history serves the living person in three respects -- as one who acts and strives, as one who preserves and reveres, and as one who suffers and stands in need of liberation -- and matches these needs to his three kinds of history, monumental, antiquarian, and critical.

Write a deadpan trend piece of about 600 words, in the voice of our lifestyle desk, reporting on a booming wellness company that has turned this taxonomy into a product: three subscription tiers for managing one's relationship to the personal and collective past, plus a premium unhistorical detox modeled on the contented grazing animal of the essay's first section. The comedy has to come from fidelity rather than invention: each tier should sincerely deliver its mode's promise while quietly exhibiting the failure Nietzsche assigns it -- monumental history flattering the striver and manufacturing usable greatness, antiquarian history preserving everything so indiscriminately that nothing new can grow, critical history liberating its client by condemnation and leaving no ground to stand on. Include customer testimonials, a company spokesperson, and one dissatisfied subscriber whom each mode has failed in its characteristic way. Stay deadpan throughout, with no winking: a reader who knows the essay should recognize the triad and its dangers rendered exactly, while a reader who has never opened it should still be able to reconstruct them from the parody alone.
