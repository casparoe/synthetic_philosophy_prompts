Write the official FAQ page for the (fictional) Argent Prize Foundation, a billionaire-funded outfit that runs the following offer: at midnight tonight, if you can verifiably *intend* to drink a small vial of toxin at noon tomorrow — a toxin that will make you violently ill for about a day but causes no lasting harm — you receive one million dollars. The money is wired at midnight, before anything is drunk, and the payout depends only on the intention, never on the drinking. Whether you actually drink the vial at noon is, as the Foundation is at pains to point out, "entirely your own buisness." The Foundation has also developed a neural Intention Verification Scanner that can tell whether you really intend something as opposed to merely hoping, wishing, or planning to plan.

The FAQ should be written in the Foundation's deadpan, slightly defensive corporate voice and run about 600 words, as a series of questions with answers. The questions applicants ask should be exactly the ones the philosophical puzzle makes urgent: Can I intend now and then just... not drink? What if I intend to drink only because I know I won't have to? Does the scanner catch the difference between intending to drink and merely being confident my future self can be bullied into it? Can I use a hypnotist, a self-deception coach, or a pre-commitment device (e.g., signing over my house to a charity unless I drink)? What if I form the intention sincerely but I *know* tomorrow-me will see no reason to follow through — does that knowledge undermine the intention at midnight? Can two people apply jointly? Does intending to intend count? What about people who would drink the toxin anyway?

The humor should come from the Foundation taking the puzzle completely seriously and trying to administrate it: a refund and clawback policy for failed intentions, eligibility rules that quietly concede deep points (e.g., the scanner can verify resolve but not rational justification; the Foundation refuses to say whether a rational person can even win), an increasingly frazzled legal disclaimer. A philosopher who knows the toxin literature should laugh in recognition; a lay reader should still find it funny and come away basically understanding why the puzzle is hard. Don't name any real philosophers or papers — the Foundation acts as if it invented all of this.

End with a short out-of-character note (3 sentences max, marked as an author's note) saying where the piece takes liberties with the actual philosophy — e.g., what the scanner would really have to detect, or which contested claim about intention and rationality the FAQ is silently taking a side on.
