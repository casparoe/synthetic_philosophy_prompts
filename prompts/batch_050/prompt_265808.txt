"This is Simpson's paradox, where a trend in the aggregate data reverses in every subgroup. The data is not actually contradictory, because a paradox like this is only an apparent contradiction, not a formal logical one -- the inequalities can all hold at once. The reversal happens because of confounding: doctors gave B to more of the easy small-stone cases and A to more of the hard large-stone cases, so the overall comparison is unfair to A. Since Simpson's paradox is always produced by a confounding variable, the fix is always to disaggregate and condition on it, so the doctor should recommend A, which wins in both subgroups. Randomized trials avoid this problem entirely, so in any randomized study you can safely condition on whatever variables you recorded and the paradox can't arise. My recommendation of A depends only on the success rates, not on anything about the causal story."
