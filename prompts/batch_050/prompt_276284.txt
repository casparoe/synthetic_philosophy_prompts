Write a villanelle about Dennett's two coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn. Both have come to dislike their coffee, but Chase swears the taste is exactly what it always was and only his attitude toward it has soured, while Sanborn insists his palate and standards haven't budged -- it's the taste itself that's gone off. The two refrains should be their two claims, and the form should do the argument's work: the refrains keep returning on alternating stanzas, each guy sure there's a fact of the matter he's tracking, until they land side by side in the final quatrain, and by then the poem should make it feel like you cant actually pull them apart -- no memory of how it used to taste, and no fresh sip either, settles whether the quale changed or the judge did. A reader who knows the paper should be able to tell which refrain belongs to who, and to see why their collision at the end is the whole point: the conviction that one of them must be right is exactly what's being quined. Start with the poem itself, no preface, no notes after.
