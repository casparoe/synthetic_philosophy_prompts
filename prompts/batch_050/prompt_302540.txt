So I'm a backend dev at a small company that makes a habit-tracking app, and last sprint I got pulled into a design review where we argued for like an hour about whether a feature we shipped was "manipulative" or just "good retention design," and I realized I have no actual principle here, just vibes. So here are my vibes, and I want to know what they add up to. Defaulting new users into a weekly summary email they can switch off in settings: fine. Making the unsubscribe link tiny and light gray: not fine. A streak counter that celebrates when you hit 30 days: fine. A push notification at 11pm saying "you haven't logged today, don't lose your streak!" timed specifically because our data shows thats when willpower is lowest: not fine, even though everything in the message is true and the habit is one the user chose. Telling users "1,200 people completed this habit this week" when the number is real: fine. Sending that same message worded as "people like you usually quit by day 12" because it tested better in the A/B: not fine, and I can't fully say why since it's also true. Auto-renewing after a free trial with a reminder email two days before: fine. Auto-renewing silently: not fine. Using the exact same streak mechanic in a gambling app: not fine, even though the mechanic is identical to the one I said was fine for us.

The pair I keep going around in circles on is the truthful-but-timed notification vs. the truthful social proof stat -- both accurate, both designed to push behavior, but one feels predatory and the other feels like marketing, so "is it true" clearly isn't my line, and "does it serve the user's own goals" doesn't obviously work either because the 11pm nudge arguably does serve the user's stated goal of building the habit. Is there one principle, stated precisely enough that I could actually apply it in a design review, that captures all or most of these? If there are two or three candidate principles, give me them and tell me which verdicts each one forces me to overturn, because I'm open to some of my gut calls just being wrong. Then apply whatever survives to the case we're literally debating at work right now: an email that says "your trial ends tomorrow and you'll lose your 58-day streak," where the streak was genuinely built to help users, the facts are all true, but the loss framing is doing a lot of heavy lifting. And flag which step of your principle is a judgment call I shouldn't pretend is mechanical.
