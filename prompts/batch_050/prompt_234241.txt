ok so I'm drafting a position statement on self-locating belief for a workshop and before I send it out I want to know if the package actually hangs together. Here's what I hold:

(1) I'm a thirder on the classic Sleeping Beauty case -- on being woken, Beauty should be at 1/3 that the coin landed heads.

(2) When Beauty wakes on Monday she gets no new information. She knew on Sunday she'd be awakened with everything looking exactly like this, and nothing about the waking experience discriminates between Heads-Monday and the Tails awakenings.

(3) Centered content is real: there are genuinely self-locating propositions not reducible to uncentered ones (Lewis's two gods who know all the same world-facts but don't know which god they are convinces me of this).

(4) The self-indication assumption is false. The Presumptuous Philosopher case is, to my mind, a reductio of it -- you shouldn't be able to get near-certainty about cosmology from the armchair just by reflecting on your own existence.

(5) Whatever principle governs self-locating credence should be one unified principle that handles Beauty, the Doomsday argument, fine-tuning, and simulation-type cases alike. I'm not willing to go case-by-case with a patchwork of verdicts.

(6) In the variant where Beauty is told, right after waking, "it's Monday," her credence in heads should stay at 1/3 -- being told which day it is can't possibly bear on how a fair coin landed.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and where the real problems are, what's the least costly revision for each? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally bad, and if one single change would clean up the most, say which one. Also: I suspect (1) and (2) clash but I'm not sure -- if they don't, explain why not, because I keep going back and forth on it.
