Write a dialogue (in British English) between two fact-checkers, Maya and Tom, who run a small misinformation site and have to agree on a label for a viral post claiming that the current King of France has endorsed a brand of supplements. Maya wants to mark it simply FALSE, following Russell's theory of descriptions: the post says there is a present King of France and that he endorsed the product, the first conjunct fails, so the whole claim is false and readers deserve a clear verdict. Tom, following Strawson, thinks labelling it FALSE is actively misleading — the claim presupposes there's a King of France, and when a presupposition fails the statement doesn't get to be false, it just fails to get off the ground; stamping FALSE on it wrongly implies there's some king out there who didn't endorse the supplements. This is for a zine i'm putting together, so it should read like an actual argument, not a lecture.

Neither should be a pushover and they must engage with each other's actual points. Have them stress-test each other with harder cases: what to say about the bare claim "the present King of France exists" (isn't that just false even for Tom?), whether "it's not the case that the King of France endorsed the product" comes out true or also undefined (and if Tom says undefined, is he stuck saying the same about its negation?), and what label they'd give a claim about a real person that contains a false presupposition about them. The dialogue should end with them either hammering out a labelling policy they can both live with or identifying precisely the point where their disagreement bottems out.
