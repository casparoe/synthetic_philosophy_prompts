I just got rotated onto the IRB at the academic medical center where I work, and my first real fight is already waiting for me. There's a Phase 1 trial up for a vote at next month's meeting — healthy volunteers, no expected benefit to them, real but modest risks — and the sponsor wants to pay $4,500 as a flat completion payment. Our commitee's standard practice has always been to prorate payment per visit, on the theory that a big completion bonus is an undue inducement: people enroll who wouldn't otherwise, or hide disqualifying conditions to get the money. But the PI is arguing that prorating is actually the ethically worse option, because it pressures people to stay in a study they've soured on, and the freedom to withdraw at any time without penalty is supposed to be a cornerstone of valid consent. So we've got two consent-protecting rules that collide: don't let the money distort the decision to enroll, vs. don't let the money distort the decision to continue.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting. I want a concrete recommendation on the payment structure for this trial (flat, prorated, some hybrid — pick one and defend it), plus a fair treatment of whichever side loses. And the thing I most want the memo to actually sort out: is "undue inducement" even a consent problem at all? An offer isn't a threat. If someone freely takes a deal just because the money is good, and later says the money clouded their judgment — is that their complaint to make, and is it the kind of complaint an ethics board should be protecting people from? Don't just gesture at the standard answer here, argue it.
