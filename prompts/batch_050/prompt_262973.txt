My take is that gauge transformations are obviously just redundancy in our description -- like, two potentials related by a gauge transformation describe literally the same physical situation, the surplus structure is an artifact of the math, and people who think otherwise are confused. His response was to bring up the Aharonov-Bohm effect and say that if gauge potentials were pure redundancy you couldn't get observable phase shifts from a region with zero field, so something gauge-ish must be out there in the world. I said no, the AB effect just shows you need *holonomies* (gauge-invariant stuff), not the raw potential, so it's still evidence for my side. He said I'm just relocating the surplus structure, not eliminating it. Then we got into whether diffeomorphism invariance in GR is the same kind of thing or a different kind of thing and it all fell apart from there.

Here's what bugs me: we both know the same physics. Neither of us learned a single new fact in three hours. So what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a verbal dispute about what counts as "real" or "the same physical situation"? Or is it something sneakier -- like we're both assuming that a theory's formalism comes with a fact of the matter about which parts represent and which parts are scaffolding, and maybe that assumption itself is the problem?
