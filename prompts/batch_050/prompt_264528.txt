Having spent forty years as an experimental physicist, I have lately become preoccupied with how much of what I once called my knowledge rested on testimony -- the word of technicians, collaborators, referees, and textbook authors, almost none of whose chains of evidence I ever checked. This has led me to the philosophical dispute between reductionists and anti-reductionists about testimony, and I would like you, acting as an expert epistemologist with a reputation for scrupulous fairness to both sides, to help me.

Draft the joint statement that a Humean reductionist (who holds that testimony-based belief is justified only insofar as the hearer possesses positive, non-testimonial grounds for regarding testimony as reliable) and a Reidian anti-reductionist (who holds that testimony, like perception or memory, confers default justification absent defeaters) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Please include: (1) a numbered list of claims both parties accept, including what each grants the other's position gets right; (2) a precise statement of their residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording -- I am particularly suspicious that part of the dispute concerns whether the reductionist's demand for independent grounds must be met by each hearer individually or only by the community; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a case or consideration both parties agree bears on it (the testimony of young children, and the epistemics of scientific collaboration, strike me as candidates), with each side's prediction recorded.

Before drafting, think carefully about where the two views actually diverge in practice rather than in slogan; a brief brainstorm of candidate cruxes, from which you select the strongest, would be welcome. If you conclude that some portion of the debate is merely verbal, identify which portion and explain why.
