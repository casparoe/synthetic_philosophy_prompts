my psych friend has this line he uses every time religion comes up: belief in gods is basically a smoke detector going off at burnt toast. evolution tuned our agency detection to be jumpy because mistaking the wind for a predator costs you nothing and mistaking a predator for the wind costs you everything, so we get floods of false alarms, and gods are just the biggest false alarm. he uses this to argue religion was "explained away" and anyone still taking the claims seriously hasn't kept up.

i want an actual audit of the analogy, not just a vibes check. give me the correspondence table -- what plays the role of the detector, the smoke, the burnt toast, the asymmetry in costs -- and then pull apart where it breaks. two things that bug me: false alarms are supposed to be cheap, but religion is insanely expensive (temples, tithes, celibacy, martyrdom, weird dietary rules), which seems backwards for a false alarm; and smoke detectors going off at toast don't usually organize the people in the house into a community with rules and shared stories, so is the false-alarm picture even the right category. also: suppose the analogy held perfectly, that the belief started as a misfiring detector -- does the debunking conclusion actually follow, or is my friend committing some version of the genetic fallacy? if a better analogy than the smoke detector exists for what the by-product theory is claiming, tell me what it is and what it buys.
