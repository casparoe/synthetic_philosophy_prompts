I'm taking a philosophy course on the meaning of life this semester and we've spent like four weeks circling the same debate and honestly going nowhere. The objectivists keep saying meaning requires connecting your life to things of objective value (so the engaged artist beats the guy counting blades of grass, no matter how fulfilled the grass-counter feels), the subjectivists keep pushing back with some version of "but if someone is genuinely, stably fulfilled by something, who are you to say their life lacks meaning," and then the absurdists in the back of the room insist the whole debate is a sideshow because nothing grounds objective value anyway, so at best we're ranking subjective fulfillments with extra steps.

My hypothesis is that the debate has stalled because the sides aren't really disagreeing about lives at all — they're disagreeing about what a theory of meaning in life is even supposed to do. Is it supposed to vindicate our intuitive rankings of paradigm lives, explain why the question feels so urgent to people, or give actual guidance to someone deciding what to do with themselves? Maybe those jobs pull apart and each side has quietly picked a different one.

So: diagnose the disagreement. Is it factual, semantic, a difference about what counts as data for a theory, or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? Use what each position actually says as your evidence, not a survey of the literature. And then tell me, given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a better distinction (meaning vs. wellbeing vs. worthiness?), a sharper account of the phenomenology of "finding something meaningful" — or whether nothing can move it and why. Diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right.
