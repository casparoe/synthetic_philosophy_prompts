Write a deadpan FAQ page, around 600 words, for the Office of Fictional Verisimilitude, an imaginary regulatory body that licenses authors to depict deviations from actuality in their fiction. The FAQ should explain, in patient bureaucratic prose, why no permit is required for faster-than-light travel, time loops, talking animals, or worlds where water is not H2O, whereas a special waiver must be obtained before any work in which the narrative voice asserts, flatly and without irony, that cruelty for its own sake is admirable or that promise-keeping is contemptible. Include: the fee schedule; the appeals process for authors whose waiver applications failed because readers "could not go along with it"; a clarification that placing the deviant morality in a character's mouth rather than the narrator's counts as a lesser infraction; a note that framing the deviance ironically or as parody may qualify the work for expedited approval; and a consumer-complaints section for readers who report that an author "asked them to imagine something they simply could not," even though the same readers had no complaint about the dragons two chapters earlier. The humor must come from the asymmetry itself: the Office never remarks on it, never suggests anything is philosophically puzzling, and never winks; it treats the licensing distinction as obvious administrative common sense and grows slightly condescending when applicants ask why the rules are this way. By the end, a reader who has never encountered the puzzle of imaginative resistance should have tripped over it, and a reader who knows the literature should find the Office's taxonomy uncomfortably accurate.
