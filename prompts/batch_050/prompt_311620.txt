Field biologists and cognitive scientists routinely describe animal signals and simple internal states in normative vocabulary. A vervet monkey that gives the leopard alarm call when no leopard is present is said to have produced a false alarm; a honeybee's waggle dance can be described as misindicating where the nectar is; a frog that snaps at a small thrown pellet is said to have mistaken it for a fly; a rat that freezes at a harmless sound is described as having made an error. Yet these same researchers typically deny that the creatures in question possess language, concepts, or beliefs in anything like the human sense. Write an essay reconstructing what theory of content, if any, this practice presupposes.

Structure the answer as follows. First, explain why the vocabulary of correctness and error appears to need a standard that a purely causal or informational account arguably cannot supply: if a state's content is simply whatever reliably triggers it, then the leopard call triggered by a rustling antelope is just an antelope-rustle signal, and nothing has gone wrong. Second, assess whether the practice therefore presupposes something in the family of teleosemantics — the view that a state's content is fixed by what it was selected to do, whether by natural selection or by learning, so that a particular token can fail to do it — or whether rivals can license the same talk: a deflationary reading on which "mistake" is mere shorthand for statistical mismatch, with no genuine normativity; an informational account supplemented with a clause about ideal or normal conditions; or a learning-based teleology covering cases without evolutionary history. Third, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with: identify which features of actual scientific usage, if any, would discriminate between these reconstructions, and where the practice is genuinely underdetermined such that several theories would license it equally well. Fourth, test your reconstruction against a hard case: an artificial image classifier, trained entirely on synthetic data, that labels a photograph of a cat as "not a cat". Does error-talk apply to it, and does your verdict reveal that the biological practice was committed to more, or less, than it appeared?

Throughout, be candid about the load-bearing steps of the argument. Conclude by stating what would change your mind: which consideration, if established, would show that the scientists' vocabulary presupposes substantially less — or substantially more — than your reconstruction claims. Write in British English.
