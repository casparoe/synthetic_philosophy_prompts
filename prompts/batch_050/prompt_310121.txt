I'm a backend engineer at an agtech startup. Our main product is basically a control system for automated insect farms -- crickets and mealworms raised for protein. My code decides temperature, lighting, feeding schedules, stocking density, and when to harvest (which for the insects means being shredded or frozen). We're talking billions of individual insects per customer per year, way more animals than all the factory farms for chickens and pigs combined.

The company recently set up an "ethics advisory board" (three engineers, one ops person, and a guy from legal) and the first agenda item is me. Some customers are asking for "welfare features" because their own buyers are asking -- stuff like lower stocking density, gentler harvest methods, environmental enrichment, whatever that means for a mealworm. These features cost money and reduce yield. Legal says there's zero regulatory requirement here. Our CEO wants a recommendation from the board before the next quarterly planning meeting: do we build a welfare tier into the product or not?

My honest position: I have no idea if a cricket can suffer. It seems pretty unlikely that something with 100,000 neurons has anything going on inside that matters. But also... it's billions of creatures, and I've been wrong about simpler things. If there's even a small chance they feel something, the math gets weird fast. My co-worker on the board says this is Pascal's wager nonsense and that you can justify literally anything by multiplying a tiny probability by a huge number, and that if we take this seriously we'd have to shut down the whole company over dust mites.

Write me a short memo I can present to the board. It should: recommend a concrete decision (build the welfare tier, don't build it, or some defined middle option -- but pick one and defend it); lay out the reasoning in a way that takes seriously both the tiny-probability-times-huge-scale thing and my co-worker's objection to it; and say what we'd actually be assuming about insect minds if we went with the recommendation, since nobody on this board has any idea what the science says. Plain language, no jargon I can't explain to the CEO, around a page.
