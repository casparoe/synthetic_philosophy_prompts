My roommate has gotten really into the sphex wasp thing — the digger wasp that drags a paralyzed cricket to its burrow, sets it at the entrance, goes inside to check things out, comes back and drags it in, and if a researcher nudges the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp just repositions it and runs the whole inspection again, over and over, apparently dozens of times without ever skipping the check. She's started using "sphexish" as a general term and her rough gloss is that a system is sphexish when its behavior is really being run by the enviroment rather than by the system.

Her verdicts so far, as I understand them:

- The wasp: sphexish, obviously, it's the namesake.
- A tick that sits on a twig for months and drops onto anything warm with the right smell, including a warm dummy a researcher waves under it: sphexish.
- Skinner's pigeons, still doing their little trained dances after the food dispenser has been unplugged: sphexish.
- Our robot vacuum, which re-plans its whole route when we move the couch instead of bumping into it forever: NOT sphexish.
- Google Maps, which reroutes the second you blow past your turn: NOT sphexish.
- A chess engine that's superhuman at chess and literally cannot do anything else: NOT sphexish. "Narrow isn't rigid," she says.
- My dog: not sphexish, though I think she's being generous.

So here's the new case. Last saturday I got in the car to go to the hardware store and came to in the parking lot at my office. I didn't crash, I handled a lane closure and a jaywalker fine, I just... executed the work drive. She laughed and said she needed to think about it, which tells me it's actually hard for her. Where do you think she lands given her track record? And for a second data point, what would she say about a current LLM chatbot — handles basically any topic you throw at it, but you can jailbreak it, and it almost never notices on its own that it's started talking nonsense?

What I actually want is: which rule she's most plausibly running on the earlier cases (I can think of at least two or three that all fit them, and they come apart on me and on the chatbot), your best guess at her verdicts with the reasoning, and one further case — real or invented — that would settle which rule it is. She's also drafting a blog post arguing that sphexishness, not determinism, is what free will worriers should actually be scared of, so if her verdict on my drive makes ordinary adult human action come out sphexish, tell me whether that guts her thesis or is secretly the point of it.
