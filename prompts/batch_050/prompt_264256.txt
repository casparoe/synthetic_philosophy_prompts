At Thanksgiving my uncle shut down an argument about whether it's ok to publicly criticize the war while it's still going on by saying "my country, right or wrong," and everyone at the table kind of nodded like that settled something. It's been bugging me for weeks because I genuinely can't tell if he said something obviously true, something obviously awful, or nothing at all.

Do a proper disambiguation job on that sentence. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, as a menu. I can already see a few candidates to get you started: something like "don't abandon your country just because it's screwing up" (which sounds like a platitude, roughly the advice you'd give about a friend going through a bad phase); something like "when push comes to shove, back your own side against outsiders" (a real claim, but maybe a monstrous one); and maybe something about how allegiance to a country isn't supposed to be conditional on your own moral judgment in the first place, the way love for family supposedly isn't -- i.e. the slogan as a refusal to even do the right/wrong appraisal. There's also apparently a reading where it means close to its opposite, "if wrong, set it right," which if accurate would be funny given how my uncle used it. Add any readings I'm missing.

For each reading: state it precisely enough that two reasonable people could actually disagree about whether it's true, then give a verdict -- platitude, substantive and defensible, substantive and false, or so strong that nobody really holds it when pressed. Also flag any reading that only survives by sliding between "my country" = the people and place and "my country" = whatever government happens to be in charge right now, because I suspect that slide is doing a lot of work in casual use.

Then the part I actually care about: which of these readings, if any, would support the move my uncle made, i.e. show that criticizing the war now is somehow disloyal or out of bounds? My hunch is the readings that support his move are the indefensible ones, and the defensible readings don't support it, but tell me if that's too tidy and there's a respectable reading that genuinely gets him what he wants.
