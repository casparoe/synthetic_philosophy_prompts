I work as a software engineer at a mid-sized company, and for the past year my team has been building and fine-tuning models that assist with infrastructure work: writing database migrations, generating deployment configs, refactoring legacy code, that kind of thing. We've split into two camps over how to supervise the models, and our arguments keep going in circles, so we've agreed to try an adversarial collaboration and produce a joint statement. The process camp wants to supervise the model's reasoning step by step: reward legible intermediate steps, have humans or trusted models review plans before execution, and treat an unreviewable chain of reasoning as a defect even when the final artifact works. The outcome camp wants to supervise only checkable end results: does the migration run, do the tests pass, does the config deploy cleanly in a sandbox, and to let the model use whatever internal reasoning gets it there, however opaque. Each side has a favorite horror story. The process camp points out that pure outcome rewards select for reward hacking the moment our checkers have gaps, and that a model that learns to pass tests rather than to be correct will eventually pass them in ways we regret. The outcome camp replies that grading reasoning steps mostly trains models to produce reasoning that looks good to reviewers, that human reviewers of technical plans are themselves unreliable and expensive, and that a legibility requirement imposes a tax that hobbles the model exactly where it would otherwise outperform us.

Draft the joint statement that both camps could actually sign. I want: a numbered section of agreed claims, covering what both sides accept about the failure modes of each method, about the dependence of the answer on how verifiable the task domain is, and about what current evidence can and cannot show; then a disagreements section in which each remaining dispute is stated precisely, with each side's position phrased in words that side would accept as fair; then a cruxes section identifying the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp, with, for each crux, the test, observation, or deployment experiment both sides agree would bear on it and each camp's prediction recorded in advance. One crux I suspect is whether the two camps actually disagree about supervision or only about the reliability of current checkers, i.e., whether the dispute would dissolve if outcome verification were perfect. Another may be whether process supervision is just outcome supervision applied to steps. If any part of our dispute is verbal rather than substantive, the statement should say which part and why.

Where the two camps cannot converge, I don't want the document to paper it over, but I also don't want it to end in a shrug. Please have the statement commit: it should take definite positions on which formulations both sides sign, rule on which of our recurring arguments each camp should retire as weak, and conclude with a single concrete supervision policy the joint statement recommends for our specific situation (a team shipping code with decent but imperfect automated checkers, limited reviewer time, and tasks of mixed verifiability), with the dissenting camp's signed reservation attached rather than left as an open question. If the honest verdict is that one camp is mostly right for our case, the document should say so and give the reasons, not split the difference for the sake of harmony.
