For years I've used the label "Moloch trap" loosely for perverse collective dynamics, and I'd like to know what principle, if anything, my usage is actually tracking. My gut verdicts: researchers cutting corners on rigor to avoid being scooped — trap. Concertgoers each standing to see over the person ahead, until everyone stands and sees no better — trap. Elk evolving ever heavier antlers across generations — trap, even though nothing deliberated. Two firms locked in a price war — not a trap, just ordinary competition, since customers gain and the losers can exit. Two collectors bidding a rare stamp far past its appraised value — not a trap; one side's loss is the other's gain, and the winner got what he wanted. An arms race between rival powers — trap. A used-car market where the impossibility of distinguishing lemons drives good cars out — I call it a trap, but a friend insists it is a mere information failure rather than an incentive race, and I cannot articulate why I disagree. City tenants bidding rents upward year over year — I'm genuinely torn; it feels Molochian, but then so might any scarcity. Is there a single principle that sorts these the way I do, or a small set of candidates? For each candidate, state it precisely, say which of my verdicts it saves and which it overturns, and give its verdict on the two cases I flagged as contested. Finally, name the one further case — real or hypothetical — that would best discriminate between the two strongest principles.
