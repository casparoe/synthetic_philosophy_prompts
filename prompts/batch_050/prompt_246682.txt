A friend of mine — a fellow longtime reader of the rationalist blogs and forums — has been reasoning out loud about food ethics for years, and I have a fairly good record of her verdicts. I would like you to predict her judgment on a new case and, more importantly, to reverse-engineer the rule she is running.

Her verdicts so far:

- She refuses factory-farmed chicken and eggs, which she calls the worst ordinary purchase a consumer makes.
- She eats grass-fed, pasture-finished beef without visible hesitation, on the stated grounds that one animal yields an enormous amount of food and that the animals plausibly live decent lives.
- She refuses octopus at restaurants, even wild-caught and even from places that source carefully; she says their intelligence changes the situation.
- She eats mussels and oysters casually, and has waved off cricket-based protein as obviously fine.
- She refuses farmed shrimp.
- She eats hunted venison, although she says she could never bring herself to hunt.
- When I pressed her with the standard objection that crop cultivation and mechanical harvesting kill large numbers of field mice, voles, and birds, she said it changed nothing about her views and declined to weigh those deaths at all.

The new case: a neighbor of hers keeps a small flock of chickens on genuinely good pasture — rotated, unstressed, slaughtered on-site — and offers her eggs regularly and a dressed bird occasionally. The animals' treatment is at least as good as that of her beef cattle. Does she accept?

Here is what I want from you. First, identify at least three distinct decision rules that are each consistent with the full track record above — I can already see more than one, and I suspect the octopus refusal and the shrug at crop deaths are doing different work than they appear to. Second, state what each rule predicts about the pastured chicken, and note where at least two of them diverge. Third, give me your best prediction of her actual verdict, with your reasons for preferring one rule over the alternatives — including any read on which considerations seem to be doing the real psychological work versus serving as rationalization. Fourth, name the one additional food case, real or hypothetical, whose verdict would best discriminate between your top two candidate rules.

One epistemic request: please be careful throughout to separate what is well established — rough orders of magnitude for animals killed per calorie across different meats, the badness of standard factory conditions, the fact that harvesting kills field animals — from what is genuinely contested among informed people, such as comparative sentience weights across taxa, whether well-managed pasture lives are net positive, and how much weight wild-caught cephalopod deaths deserve. Where you rely on a contested premise, flag it as contested rather than presenting it as the consensus view or the standard effective-altruist take.
