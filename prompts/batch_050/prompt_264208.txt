I am taking a final-year course on the philosophy of artificial intelligence, and for several weeks our seminar has been stuck on whether a language model's chain-of-thought can be treated as a faithful account of how it arrived at its answer. What frustrates me is that everyone seems to accept the same evidence and yet nobody has moved. One side points out that the reasoning text is causally load-bearing: corrupt it, truncate it, or force the model to answer without it, and performance on multi-step problems collapses; so the tokens are doing real work, not decorating an answer produced elsewhere. The other side points to experiments in which models follow unacknowledged hints, sycophantic cues, or answer-order biases: the output changes, while the stated reasoning never mentions the factor that actually drove it. Their conclusion is that chain-of-thought is post-hoc rationalisation, structurally similar to human confabulation in split-brain and social-psychology studies, where people sincerely explain choices they were covertly caused to make.

My own hypothesis is that the two sides are answering different questions under one label: one asks whether the reasoning text is causally involved, the other whether it is a complete and accurate report, and both are calling their answer a view about 'faithfulness'. A second suspicion is that there is a hidden normative question about the standard: if human introspective reports also routinely omit the real causes of judgement, is the bar 'as faithful as human self-explanation' or 'faithful enough to rely on for safety monitoring', and are these wildly different bars?

Am I right about the structure of this disagreement, or is there a substantive empirical question underneath that could actually settle it? I want a diagnosis rather than another survey of the results: is this verbal, substantive, or a mixture; is there a concealed disagreement about what would even count as evidence; and what, concretely, would constitute progress -- a principled definition, a mechanistic test of which internal states the text tracks, a disaggregated notion with different grades of faithfulness -- or should we expect the impasse to persist because the very idea of a system 'reporting its reasoning' is ill-formed?
