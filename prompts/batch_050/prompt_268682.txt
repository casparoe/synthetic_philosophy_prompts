So I've been thinking about when a change that makes everyone involved better off is actually unproblematic -- call it a "safe" Pareto improvement. Here are my gut verdicts, and I want to know what principle, if any, is behind them:

1. Two rival countries stuck in an arms race sign a verifiable treaty to both cut military spending 40%. Both prefer the post-treaty world. Verdict: totally fine, obviously safe.
2. Two firms secretly agree to fix prices. Both firms are better off than under competition. Verdict: clearly not safe, ban it.
3. A mugger says "your wallet or your life"; handing over the wallet leaves both me and the mugger better off than the shooting outcome. Verdict: that transaction is in no sense a good improvement, even though it's Pareto against the alternative the mugger is offering.
4. Two AI systems playing an iterated game against each other modify their own code so both commit to cooperate instead of defecting. Verdict: fine, same as case 1 basically.
5. A dictator offers to step down in exchange for a golden parachute and immunity; him staying is worse for literally everyone including him (he's miserable and paranoid). Verdict: I wince, but I think this one is actually a safe improvement?
6. Everyone in a town votes unanimously to fund a fireworks show through a mandatory tax. Verdict: fine, even though it's coercive in form, because it was unanimous.

What bugs me: 1 and 2 look structurally identical (parties to the agreement all benefit, outsiders lose), yet my verdicts differ. And 3 vs 6 both involve extracting something under pressure, but I flip-flop. Is there a single principle -- maybe about which baseline counts, or about who's a party vs a bystander -- that gets all six? Or do I have to give one up? If my verdict on 5 is the one that has to go, say so plainly.

Please give me your answer as a JSON object with these fields: "principle" (the best candidate, stated in one or two sentences), "case_table" (an array of objects with fields "case", "your_verdict", "captured_by_principle" true/false, and "why"), "verdicts_to_abandon" (array of case numbers), and "new_case" (a fresh case the principle delivers a clear verdict on, plus that verdict).
