So I've been reading LessWrong and SSC since like 2013 and have absorbed the whole Chalmers vs Dennett thing mostly through osmosis, zombie arguments, fading qualia, the works. Last weekend my housemate (who's read none of this) finally agreed to watch Black Mirror with me and I put on White Christmas, the Jon Hamm one with the cookies. Afterwards she said something that stopped me: the whole episode only works as horror if the cookies are concious, and "that's just an assumption the show makes, you could imagine them being empty." And I realized she's basically right, the episode takes a side in the hard problem fight without ever arguing for it, and I want to think through how it does that and whether it cheats.

So: read White Christmas through the hard problem of consciousness, properly. Some things I want the reading to actually engage with, not just gesture at. The scene where Matt breaks Greta's copy by running her through months of simulated solitary in seconds until she agrees to be her original's assistant — that scene is horrifying exactly to the extent that behavior implies experience, and the episode treats the copy's pleading as settling the matter. Is that just functionalism smuggled in through casting, since they put the same actress on screen? Nobody in the episode ever raises the zombie option, and I keep thinking about how the plot reads if the cookies really are p-zombies — then Matt is basically yelling at a tape recorder and the cops at the end are vandalizing a hard drive, and the episode becomes a dark comedy about category errors. Does the episode know it's betting everything on substrate independence? Also the ending, where they leave Joe's cookie running at a massively accelerated rate with the Christmas music on loop — that's only a punishment if suffering scales with subjective time, which is its own assumption worth pulling apart.

And push back where it's warranted: I suspect a Searle-type or an illusionist would say the episode is a 70-minute intuition pump that begs the question, and I'd want to hear that case made honestly, including which scenes it best explains. But also — is there anything the episode shows about this debate that papers can't? My half-formed thought is that none of the characters need the metaphysics settled to know that what's happening to the cookies is wrong, and maybe that's itself a datum about how the moral question relates to the hard problem. Reference specific scenes, no need for quotes or anything, I just watched it.
