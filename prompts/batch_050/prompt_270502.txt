I spent forty years in a biochemistry lab, and since retiring I've been working my way through philosophy of social science the way I once worked through review articles, and I've gotten stuck on the social construction of kinds. Here's my problem. In my old field, a "real" kind earned its keep by supporting induction -- you learn something about one sample of a protein and it generalizes. Now I keep reading that race, gender, money and so on are "socially constructed," but plenty of constructed things seem to generalize just fine, so construction alone can't be the interesting claim. I want help building a case that separates the standard frameworks, because the textbook examples (money, marriage, quarks vs. dollars) feel worn smooth.

Construct a thought experiment about a socially constructed category that meets these specs: (1) the category must support genuinely robust prediction and explanation, on par with a decent biological kind -- so nobody can dismiss it as a mere label; (2) awareness of the classification must feed back on the people classified, so Hacking's looping effect is in play; (3) it must NOT be race, gender, money, or mental illness -- invent something like an educational tracking category, a credit-worthiness tier, or an occupational licensing class, so the case has to stand on its own legs; (4) realistic, present-day, and under a paragraph for the scenario itself. The twist I want engineered in: a descriptive analysis (Searle-style institutional facts, or Boyd-style homeostatic property clusters) should deliver the verdict that the kind is perfectly real and stable, while an ameliorative analysis (Haslanger-style: what kind should we have, given what it's for) delivers the verdict that the kind is defective and ought to be re-engineered or scrapped -- and the two verdicts must come apart *because of* the loop, not despite it.

After the scenario, show me the reasoning for each of the three frameworks -- Searle, Boyd, Haslanger -- and confirm each spec is met, i.e. that the verdicts actually fall out of the theories rather than being stapled on. Then tell me: which single feature of your case is doing the work of prying the descriptive and ameliorative verdicts apart, and what happens to each framework's verdict if you delete the feedback loop -- does the case collapse into something boring where everyone agrees? Finally, steelman the response of someone who says my whole setup is confused because "what should the category be" just isn't a question metaphysics answers. If that's right I'd like to know before I embarass myself at the reading group I joined at the library.
