I'm one of five members on an expert panel advising our national nuclear waste agency on the marker for a deep geological repository that has to stay sealed for at least 10,000 years, probably longer. We're stuck on what is honestly a philosophical problem, and I want to bring something sharper than our usual engineering-speak to next month's session, so I need a short written opinion I can circulate beforehand.

Here's the setup. The agency's current plan is a big granite monolith field with warning text carved in seven languages plus pictograms showing sickness and death. Our semiotics consultant has been pushing a framework (I think she got it from Hofstadter) that splits any message into three layers: the inner message, the actual content ("danger, don't dig here"); the frame message, the structural cues that tell you this thing is a message at all and roughly how to start reading it; and the outer message, the shared code or language you'd need to actually decode it. Her argument: over 10,000 years the outer message is guaranteed to die, since no language or symbol system survives that long, so we should stop trying to transmit an inner message and put everything into the frame level. Make the site unmistakably *deliberate*, an artifact that screams "humans built this on purpose, figure out why," and trust that future archaeologists or whoever treat legible intent as a reason for caution.

The problem is a dissenting member of the panel pointed out that a strong frame message is exactly what a treasure map has. "Humans built this on purpose, something important is buried here" is an invitation, not a deterrent — see every Egyptian tomb that was both elaborately marked and thoroughly robbed. His proposal: build no marker at all and rely on the geology, since the only winning move is making the site forgettable. The consultant replies that forgetting is not a policy, it's just hope, and that a site stumbled on by accident with no frame at all gets read as "natural cave system" or "mining opportunity," which is the worst outcome of all.

So: draft me the opinion. Pick one of the three positions on the table (monolith as planned / frame-only marker / no marker) or some hybrid, recommend it as our official advice to the agency, and give the reasoning the way a panel document would. Whatever you recommend, take the strongest objection from the losing side seriously rather than strawmanning it — especially the tomb-raider problem if you come down in favor of any marker at all, because that's where our discussions keep going in circles and I want to break the loop. Also say something concrete about what the recommendation implies for the pictogram budget line: if we abandon the inner-message layer there's no defensible reason to keep paying for it, and I'd rather the memo bite that bullet itself than have the agency's accountants notice first.
