I am a novelist drafting a book whose three central figures each perform an act of serious sacrifice: a man who donates a kidney to a stranger, a woman who raises an orphaned niece at the cost of her own ambitions, and a soldier who throws himself on a grenade. I want my readers' moral reactions to these figures to track genuine distinctions in moral theory rather than my own instincts. Please construct a decision tree that takes an action like these and sorts it according to the main positions on supererogation: the commonsense view that such acts are praiseworthy precisely because they go beyond what duty requires; the denial of supererogation, on which the best available act is always obligatory and apparent heroes merely do their duty; anti-supererogationism, on which an unrequired sacrifice is positively wrong; and the prerogative view, on which an agent may permissibly decline the best act without its ceasing to be best. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the labels: each branch node should be a question about a concrete case that someone could answer without knowing which position it leads to (for instance, whether morality may demand unlimited sacrifice, or whether declining the best act is blameworthy or merely less good). Then locate the positions in that space, note any unoccupied combination of answers and briefly diagnose why it is vacant, and flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot genuinely separate. Keep the tree to roughly six decision nodes; I need a working instrument, not a survey of the literature.
