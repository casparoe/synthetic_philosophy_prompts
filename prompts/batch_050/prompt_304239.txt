My oldest friend and I argued past midnight last weekend about my uncle, who over the past few years has sunk into a fringe worldview: he believes the municipal water supply is being deliberately contaminated, that the local paper is paid to conceal it, and that several of his neighbors are in on the deception. My friend's verdict was blunt: the man is simply wrong about nearly everything he says, and honesty requires telling him so. I objected that this description cannot be literally correct. To know that he is wrong about the water, we must already take him to be right about what water is, what pipes are, what a council is, that people act from motives, that documents can be forged or genuine — right, it seems to me, about nearly everything he talks about. My friend replied that hearing his words in whichever sense makes them come out true is a courtesy I may extend or withhold, and that nothing forces it upon me. We left it there. Which of us is correct? And if understanding someone's words really does require finding them right about most of the world, what exactly is my uncle wrong about — and where, in an actual conversation with him, can disagreement even begin?
