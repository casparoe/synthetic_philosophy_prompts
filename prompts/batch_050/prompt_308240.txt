The Judy Benjamin problem seems to have settled into a permanent stalemate and I want a diagnosis, not another proposed solution. Quick recap of where things stand as I understand them: Judy is dropped in territory split into Red and Blue regions, each split into First and Second Company sectors, with priors like P(Red) = P(Blue) = 1/2. She gets one radio message: if she's in Red, the probability she's in Second Company sector is 3/4. Van Fraassen's line is that the problem is underdetermined -- the message doesn't fix the full conditional distribution she should adopt, so multiple updates are rationally permissible. The maxent/KL-minimization crowd says no, there's a unique answer, you minimize information gain subject to the constraint. And then there's a third current that says the puzzle is just ambiguous about what the message *is* -- learning the conditional isn't the same event as conditionalizing on it, and once you specify the evidential situation properly the disagreement evaporates.

Thirty-plus years on, nobody has moved anybody. My hunch is that this isn't a formal dispute at all -- the math on both sides is fine -- but a disguised disagreement about whether rationality comes with a uniqueness constraint or is permissive about priors and update rules, and that's a much more general fault line that Judy Benjamin just happens to expose. Is that right, or is there a hidden substantive question doing the work (e.g. about what constraints an observed conditional really imposes)? Use what each side actually argues, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a formal result, a distinction, a change of question -- or argue honestly that none should be expected.
