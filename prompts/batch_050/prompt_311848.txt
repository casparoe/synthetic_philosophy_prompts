I work in model validation for a trading firm and am fluent in the vocabulary of estimands and estimators, integrability, truncation, regularization, convergence in probability and almost surely, Monte Carlo error, and model-risk approval. I also use an internal discipline I call audit-realizability: a valuation claim is meaningful only if it specifies a well-defined estimand, a finite implementable procedure for estimating or reproducing it, a stopping rule, and criteria under which the result would be rejected.

Please restate the Pasadena paradox in that vocabulary rather than merely explaining it. The game is this: a fair coin is flipped until the first heads appears. If the first heads occurs on flip n, which has probability 2^-n, the payout is (-1)^(n-1)2^n/n. The absolute expected payout is infinite, while the naturally ordered signed contributions have a conditionally convergent sum; different orderings of those contributions can produce different finite or infinite totals.

Translate the following claims as faithfully as possible:

1. Orthodox expected-utility theory is silent here because the payoff has no expected value.
2. The natural-order proposal assigns the game a value by summing its contributions in increasing n.
3. A finite-truncation proposal values the game by stopping after N flips and letting N increase.
4. Bounded-utility, payout-cap, and discounting proposals avoid the paradox.
5. The game is nevertheless decision-relevant: rational agents still need policies concerning whether to accept, reject, or pay for it.

For each claim, distinguish carefully among the random payoff, the estimand, the estimator, the truncation scheme, and the decision policy. Explain what a Monte Carlo simulation would and would not establish, why ordinary standard errors and confidence intervals may be misleading, and whether a rearrangement of an infinite series has any genuine counterpart in an implementable sampling procedure. Also state whether caps, bounded utilities, and discounting repair the original valuation or simply replace it with a different model.

Then provide a residue report in a table with columns for the original claim, its closest audit-realizable translation, any distortion required, and what has no expression in the target vocabulary. Pay particular attention to whether identical game survives translation, whether rational requirement can be reduced to convergence failure, and whether a completed infinite total has an operational meaning. Conclude by judging whether the residue shows that my model-validation framework lacks a needed normative concept, or whether some of the philosophical claims are underspecified because they never identify an estimand and a realization procedure. Write the answer in American English.
