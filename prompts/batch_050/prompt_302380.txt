In 2017 the Whitney Biennial in New York showed Open Casket, a painting by Dana Schutz, a white artist, based on the famous photograph of Emmett Till's mutilated body in its coffin — the photograph his mother insisted be made public in 1955. It kicked off a huge row. A protestor stood in front of the painting to block people from seeing it, an open letter circulated demanding not just that the painting be taken down but that it be destroyed, Schutz defended the work by saying she'd approached it through empathy as a mother and promised never to sell it, and the Whitney left it hanging.

Analyse this episode through the main philosophical accounts of cultural appropriation in art. I'm interested in how the case stresses each theory rather than a generic survey, so at minimum:

1. Harm-based accounts (appropriation is wrong when it exploits, silences, or offends members of the source community, or profits from pain that isn't the artist's). Does Open Casket count as appropriation on this view at all? Till's murder isn't a style, motif, or cultural practice that got "borrowed" — it's an event. Say whether that matters or whether the theory needs a wider notion of what's being appropriated (e.g. grief, collective memory).

2. Accounts that locate the wrong in speaking over or misrepresenting others — the idea that Schutz lacked the standing to aestheticise Black suffering. Who exactly is the wronged party here, and can the theory say without collapsing into "only paint your own group"?

3. Sceptical views that the whole category of cultural appropriation is incoherent or that policing it does more damage to art and cross-cultural solidarity than it prevents. Does this episode vindicate the sceptics (the demand to destroy the painting looks like a reductio) or is it actually the strongest case against them?

Also run the analysis in the other direction: what does the episode show about the theories? Does Schutz's "empathetic mother" defence do any real work, or does it reveal something the standard accounts have no resources to evaluate? And is there anything here — like the destruction demand, or the fact that the photograph was deliberately circulated by Till's mother as a political act — that none of the standard theories anticipated? Say where each theory simply goes silent rather than forcing a verdict out of it.
