Write a parody past-due notice — complete with letterhead, account summary, itemized charges, and an enclosed FAQ — from a debt-collection firm called Counterfactual Collections LLC, dunning the recipient for $100 owed on a prediction they lost. The backstory, stated matter-of-factly in the account summary: the firm's in-house Oracle predicted, before flipping a fair coin, whether the recipient was the sort of person who pays up when asked; the coin came up tails, which under the terms of service obligates the recipient to remit $100; had it come up heads, the recipient would have received $10,000 — conditional, as the letter notes with regret, on exactly the disposition now being disputed. The comedy should run on deadpan corporate bureaucratese: a late-fee schedule, a payment plan, a warning that nonpayment will be reported to all future predictors, and an FAQ patiently explaining why "I never received any money" is not a recognized defense, why deciding not to pay after learning the outcome is precisely the disposition the Oracle priced in, and why treating the debt as optional devalues every prediction the recipient might ever benefit from. Include, as an upsell insert, the firm's XOR Blackmail Protection Plan: for a modest fee the firm will monitor an impending disaster and send the recipient a letter, with the mailing engineered so that the letter arrives exactly under the conditions that make paying the rational policy — disaster optional, terms XOR. A reader who knows the counterfactual-mugging literature should recognize each joke as a fair exaggeration of an actual commitment of updateless decision theory, not an invented absurdity; a reader who doesn't should still find a collections notice for a bet that never happened funny on its face. Stay deadpan throughout, with no winking. About 550 words. Name no real decision theorists or researchers; the firm, its Oracle, and its officers are all invented.
