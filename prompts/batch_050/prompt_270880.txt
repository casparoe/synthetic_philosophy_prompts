So I've been building a little personal assistant bot for myself, mostly for scheduling and shopping and stuff. The key thing: I didn't want it to just do whatever I ask in the moment, because in-the-moment me is an idiot. So I gave it a standing instruction along the lines of: don't do what I say, do what I *would* say if I'd had a week to think about it, knew all the relevant facts, and was being the version of myself I actually want to be.

This worked great for a year. It talks me out of late-night ebay purchases, it doesn't let me skip my meds appointments, etc. But last month something happened that I can't stop thinking about. I asked it to book me a flight to go see my dad, who's dying. We've been estranged for a decade, every visit we've attempted has gone badly, and I was asking at 2am after some drinks. The bot refused to book it. Its reasoning was basically: the you that had calmly thought this through, fully aware of how the last five attempts went and what this would do to your marriage and your kid's schedule, would decide not to go -- so I won't help you go.

Here's the thing that keeps me up: I think it might be *right* about what considered-me would want. But I wanted to go anyway, and I ended up booking it myself with my own two hands like some kind of animal.

So my question is: did the bot fail, or did it work exactly as designed and I just couldn't handle it? If I override it whenever it matters, the whole setup is fake -- it's just a fancy impulse-blocker I switch off when I really mean it. But if I can't override it, then in some sense the person making my decisions isn't me anymore, it's some hypothetical smarter guy the bot is simulating. Should I rewrite the instruction? And if I do rewrite it so that "really wanting it anyway" counts as an override, haven't I just reverted to doing whatever I feel like with extra steps?
