A colleague of mine sits on an animal behavior review panel and prides herself on applying Morgan's canon rigorously. Her past verdicts: a New Caledonian crow that bent a straight wire into a hook on its first attempt to retrieve food -- genuine causal insight, she said; a dog that displays a "guilty look" when its owner discovers a shredded cushion -- no attribution of guilt, just an appeasement display triggered by the owner's demeanor; a dog that buries a bone when another dog is watching but not when alone -- no attribution of intentional concealment, since a learned discrimination suffices; an octopus that unscrews a jar after many attempts of varying technique -- no insight, just persistent trial and error. Now the panel faces a new case: a raven that caches food, and later, when alone, re-caches it -- but only if a particular other raven was watching the original caching, and only if the first raven has itself previously stolen food from others' caches. Predict her verdict, identify which candidate rule her track record is actually tracking -- behavioral flexibility, skepticism specifically about mental-state attribution, or something else -- and name the further case that would settle which rule she is running.
