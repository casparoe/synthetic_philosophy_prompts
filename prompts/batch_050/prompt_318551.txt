I co-run the philosophy club at my school (like 12 people, mostly juniors and seniors, 50 minutes after school on tuesdays) and last week we read a chunk of Locke's Second Treatise of Government, the chapter "Of the Extent of the Legislative Power," and got totally stuck on one sentence in section 135: "The obligations of the law of nature cease not in society, but only in many cases are drawn closer, and have by human laws known penalties annexed to them, to inforce their observation." We got into a genuine argument about the "known penalties" part. One kid said a penalty being known is just a practical convenience, like it makes enforcement easier but has nothing to do with whether the law is legitimate. Someone else said a rule you couldn't have known about in advance basically isn't a law at all, it's just the ruler doing violence and calling it law afterwards. I kept pushing on what "known" even requires — known to who, announced how, known when — and we ended up listing like five seperate ways a penalty could fail to be known: it gets made up after you act, it's kept secret, it changes every week, it contradicts another rule so you can't follow both, or it's so vague nobody can tell what counts. Then our faculty advisor mentioned that this guy Lon Fuller wrote a whole book (The Morality of Law I think?) about a bad king named Rex who fails at making law in exactly these ways, and said we should do something with that instead of just talking.

So here's what I want: design an activity or game I can run at the club that makes everyone FEEL why Locke's "known penalties" thing matters, without me lecturing first. Like, I want them to actually live under rules that fail in these ways for half an hour and get genuinely annoyed, and only afterwards find out the names for what went wrong. I'm imagining something like one person playing a Rex figure who makes and enforces the rules of some simple game while everyone else tries to get points or whatever, with the failures rolled out one at a time, but you can definately come up with something better as long as it's runnable with 12 people, one facilitator (me), and stuff we have in a classroom. Please give me the actual rules of the activity, how to stage each failure so people notice it themselves, and a debrief that maps each moment onto one of the ways a penalty stops being "known" and onto what that does to people trying to plan their lives (my advisor says this planning thing is a big deal in the rule of law literature, Raz or someone). Also tell me what's going to predictably go wrong when I run it — like if people figure out the trick too early, or the Rex person is too nice to be arbitrary, or everyone just laughs instead of getting frustrated — and what I should do about it. And be honest about what a game with fake points can't capture about living under actual bad law, since I don't want to oversell it in the debrief. One last thing for the debrief: do you think "known penalties" is really one requirement or a bundle of different ones, and would Locke's sentence actually rule out all five of the failures we listed, or only some of them?
