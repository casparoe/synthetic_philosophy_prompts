I run a small outdoor adventure company. A couple months ago I got our gear supplier's annual safety certification, read through it carefully, and told my business partner I know their harnesses meet the standard. We ordered a batch for our regular adult courses and I didn't think twice about it.

Now we're setting up a program for kids with disabilities, and suddenly the exact same certificate doesn't feel like enough. I want to pay for independent testing before we use that gear in the new program. My partner thinks I'm being flat-out inconsistent: either I knew the harnesses were safe or I didn't, and if extra testing makes sense now, it made sense then too.

But that seems off to me? My evidence hasn't changed one bit. What changed is how much rides on being right. So which of us is confused here? If ordering the tests is the right call for the kids' program, does that mean I never actually knew the gear was safe back when I signed the first order — I just thought I did? And here's the part that really bugs me: if whether I know something can depend on what's at stake for me, does that mean knowing isn't really about having the truth nailed down at all?
