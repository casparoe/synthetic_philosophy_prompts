A friend of mine did a PhD in metaphysics and has extremely settled intuitions about dispositions -- finks, masks, mimics, the whole menagerie. Over the years I've collected some of her verdicts, and now I want to triangulate her on some new cases. Her track record:

- A delicate wine glass shipped inside a big styrofoam block, so that while packed no ordinary knock could possibly break it: still fragile, she says. The packing is just a mask, the glass keeps its disposition.
- The same glass hooked up to a little device that detects any incoming blow and, in the instant before the blow lands, rearranges the glass's molecular structure into something sturdy: not fragile anymore, she says. That's a classic fink and it genuinely removes the disposition.
- A sturdy concrete block hooked up to a device that would weaken its internal structure the moment anyone struck it, so that if struck it would in fact crumble: she says this one IS fragile. I pushed back on this and she didn't budge -- her view was roughly that what matters is what happens when you actually hit the thing.

Now the new cases I haven't asked her about yet:

(a) A glass rigged to a device that does nothing at all until the glass shatters, at which point it instantly fuses the shards back into a seamless whole, so fast that no observer ever sees broken glass. Fragile or not?
(b) A glass with the hardening device from case two, except the device is unreliable and only kicks in about half the time -- a coin flip on each strike. Fragile or not?
(c) A glass with the hardening device, plus a second device whose only job is to disable the first device whenever the first one activates. Fragile or not?

What I'd like from you: predict her verdict on each of (a)-(c), and say how confident you are in each prediction seperately. More than that, I want to know what rule you think she's running, and what rival rules would fit her three original verdicts just as well as your favored one. For each rival, say which of the new cases it diverges on, and if her verdicts so far genuinely underdetermine (a), (b), or (c), say so rather than forcing a prediction. Also: what's the single further case you could put to her that would do the most to pin down her actual rule? And finally, tell me where your reconstruction of her view is most likely to go wrong -- what would change your mind about which rule she's following?
