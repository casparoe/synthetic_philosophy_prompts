Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a methodological individualist and an explanatory holist who are trying to explain one invented but realistic case: “permit-line drift,” where a city moves permit applications online, each applicant becomes faster and better informed, no staff are cut, yet average resolution time worsens and applicants increasingly describe the office as hostile and arbitrary. The individualist insists the explanation must bottom out in incentives, beliefs, bottlenecks, and aggregation effects; the holist insists that queue norms, institutional tempo, and legitimacy cascades can be causal in a way not reducible to a sum of individual states. Produce the document they could both sign: (1) 6–10 numbered agreed claims about the case and about explanation; (2) a table of residual disagreements, each phrased so both sides accept the wording of their own view; (3) exactly three cruxes, and for each a concrete observation, natural experiment, or intervention both agree would bear on it, with each side’s prediction recorded in advance. Keep it neutral, separate what is settled from what remains genuinely contested among competent social scientists, and do not present a contested stance as consensus or invent citations. End with a final line beginning “Summary:” that names the largest remaining crux in one sentence.
