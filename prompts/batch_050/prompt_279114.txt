My best friend is getting married next weekend and I'm the best man, so I have to give the toast at the rehearsal dinner. Small problem: he's a decision theorist (PhD and everything) and roughly a third of the guests are his old grad school crowd who will heckle any sloppy philosophy. The rest are family and the bride's college friends who mostly just want to hear something nice.

Here's the hook I want to build the toast around. Whenever anyone jokingly asks him "so what are the chances you actually go through with it," he refuses to give a number, and he's got this whole thing about why: while you're still genuinely deliberating about what to do, you can't treat your own upcoming choice as just another event to be predicted from the outside, because the moment you see yourself as predictable the deliberation is basically over. Assigning yourself odds is something you can only do about other people, or about yourself once you've quietly stopped deciding. So on his view, saying "I'm 99% sure I'll show up tomorrow" would actually be a confession that he's already made up his mind and is just going through the motions of choosing.

Write me a ~4 minute toast that takes this idea seriously rather than mocking it, but still lands for the non-philosophers. Specifically, I want the speech to earn its way to the point where his refusal to quote odds about himself turns out to be the most romantic thing about him: a marriage vow isn't a forecast about your future self ("I'm predicting I won't leave you") but a commitment, and you don't love someone the way you predict the weather, you love them by choosing them, fresh, again and again. But it has to be funny on the way there, and the philosopher hecklers should feel like they've gotten a respectful nod instead of a lecture. End on the actual toast line.
