So I'm a retired biochemist (40 years in protein science, mostly) and I've gotten into philosophy of science in my retirement. The local philosophy department let me sign up to give a colloquium talk, and my thesis is that Lakatos's picture of research programmes fits the prion story in my own field almost embarrassingly well: Prusiner's "protein only" hypothesis was a hard core that survived a long stretch of anomalies and failed detection attempts, the protective belt kept getting revised (scrapie agent as virus, then as virus with a protein coat, and so on), and the whole thing only looked "progressive" once the novel facts finally showed up. Basically I want to argue the critics who called it degenerating in the 80s were not irrational, and neither were the people who stuck with it, and Lakatos explains how both can be true.

Problem: the audience will be mostly philosophers of science who know Lakatos way better than I do, plus a couple of Kuhn people and one formal epistemologist who I gather dislikes anything without a probability in it. Prepare me for the Q&A.
