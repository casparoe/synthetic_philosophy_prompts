Here's my opening move. First a definition so we're talking about the same thing: call it a "commitment race" when each side of a dispute can make an early, hard-to-reverse commitment -- a public vow, a self-executing contract, an automated device -- and committing first dumps the whole burden of backing down onto the other side. Now the challenge that stumped my students. Your advice is public by now; everyone has read the book. So in any dispute, both sides know that whoever commits first grabs the leverage, which means both sides race to commit, and they commit hastily and maximalist, before anyone has properly thought it through -- and when both commitments land, nobody can climb down, and you get exactly the collision your strategy was supposed to prevent. Doesn't the whole approach eat itself the moment both players have read the same book you wrote? And don't dodge by saying "just don't race" -- rational agents don't get to opt out of the incentives in front of them.
