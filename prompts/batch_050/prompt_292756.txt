Write an Onion-style local news article -- headline plus roughly 500 words -- reporting on the Mytilene debate of 427 BC as if it were a municipal news story in a present-day paper, complete with quotes from 'concerned citizens' interviewed outside the Pnyx. The actual history should be doing all the comedic work: the Assembly votes on Cleon's motion to kill every adult male in rebellious Mytilene and enslave the women and children, sends the trireme, then holds the *same vote again the next morning*, Diodotus talks them out of it on grounds of cold imperial self-interest rather than mercy, and a second trireme has to race the first one across the Aegean with the rowers eating and drinking at their oars because the crew of the first ship is, understandably, not in a hurry. Keep the voice completely deadpan -- the humor is that the reporter treats 'body reverses decision to commit genocide within 24 hours, passes' as routine city-council coverage, like a zoning variance being revisited. No winking, no 'can you believe these people.'

Also include, as a final 'In other Assembly news' roundup of two or three short items, the Sicilian expedition (voted for by people who couldn't place Sicily on a map, its loudest champion recalled mid-voyage to stand trial over the mutilated herms, its most skeptical general left in charge) and the upcoming trial of Socrates -- again written as neutral briefs, letting the reader connect the dots about what the world's most famous democracy keeps voting for.

Ground rules: every joke has to be a joke *about* something Athens actually did, not a generic 'politicians are dumb' gag, so someone who's read Thucydides should recognize each bit and someone who hasn't should still get the satire of democratic mood-swings. Paraphrase Cleon and Diodotus's arguments rather than quoting them. And keep the modern slang out of the historical content itself -- the anachronism should live in the journalistic framing, not in ancient people saying 'vibes.'
