My town (about 40k people) spent eight years under a local government that was basically a racket: contracts steered to cronies, loud critics harassed by inspectors, a couple of people beaten up by a "security firm" on the town payroll. That crowd got voted out two years ago, and now the new council has a mess on its hands. Most municipal staff -- clerks, inspectors, cops -- were around for the racket; lots did small dirty work, a few did serious stuff. The council's proposal: anyone who comes forward within six months and gives a full, honest account of what they did and saw keeps their job and can't be prosecuted for it; anyone who stays quiet and gets found out later is fired and charged. Victims' families are furious -- the guy who beat up your brother just gets to keep his pension because he talked? The council says without the deal we'll never learn where the money went or who ordered what, and prosecuting everyone would take a decade and gut the town's services. I'm genuinely torn. Is trading punishment for the truth defensible here, or is it just letting guilty people haggle their way out? Does it matter what someone confesses to -- should the beater get the same deal as the clerk who stamped the paperwork? And what is this deal even supposed to accomplish -- truth, healing, deterrence -- because it seems like it might fail at all three?
