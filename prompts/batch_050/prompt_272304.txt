I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or physics, and I would like a disciplined analysis of an episode from recent physics through the lens of a thesis I keep encountering in my reading: that mathematical elegance is evidence of physical truth. The thesis is associated with Dirac, who held, in effect, that beauty in one's equations can be a better guide than agreement with experiment, and its supporters usually rest it on cases like his own: his relativistic equation for the electron, arrived at with substantial guidance from mathematical aesthetics, implied an antiparticle that was duly detected in cosmic rays a few years later.

The episode I want analyzed is supersymmetry and the Large Hadron Collider. Here are the salient facts as I understand them; correct me where I am wrong. Supersymmetry posits a partner particle for every known particle. Its motivations were largely aesthetic rather than strictly empirical: it protects the Higgs mass from enormous quantum corrections that would otherwise require implausible fine-tuning (the naturalness or hierarchy problem), it makes the three gauge couplings nearly unify at high energy, it yields a dark matter candidate, and its defenders note that it is, in a precise sense, the unique consistent extension of the symmetries of spacetime. Many physicists reportedly expected superpartners to show up at LHC energies. Instead, the LHC found the Higgs boson in 2012 at about 125 GeV and, across two runs, no trace of any superpartner. The community's response was mostly not to abandon the framework but to push the partners to higher masses — in some versions accepting precisely the fine-tuning the theory was invented to eliminate.

Please run the analysis in both directions. First, be careful about what the elegance thesis actually claims, because I suspect it bundles at least three theses: (i) simplicity as fewer moving parts — the version I recognize from engineering practice and from formal notions like minimum description length; (ii) symmetry and mathematical inevitability; (iii) naturalness, the absence of unexplained fine-tuning. Supersymmetry is a poor exemplar of (i) — it roughly doubles the particle census, and its minimal model carries on the order of a hundred free parameters — but a strong exemplar of (ii) and (iii). So: which version does the Dirac episode actually support, which version was the SUSY community relying on, and does the LHC null result tell against the same thesis the positron confirmed, or against a different one? Second, run the case back against the theory. Is the null result a straightforward anomaly for the elegance thesis, or is the thesis constructed so that no empirical outcome could count against it — if elegance is a heuristic for generating theories rather than a source of evidence about them, say so explicitly. Critics reply to the Dirac story that we remember the beautiful theories that worked and forget those that failed: the steady-state universe, committed to an aesthetic of perfect temporal uniformity, was killed by the cosmic microwave background, and Einstein spent his last decades on beautiful unified field theories that went nowhere. Is there a principled way to tally this track record, or is the thesis insulated from track-record evaluation altogether? And what does the community's actual response — retreating to heavier superpartners rather than reconsidering the aesthetic — reveal about the role elegance was really playing: evidence, prior, or professional identity?

Finally, tell me what would change your verdict: which consideration, formal argument, or conceivable future result would lead you to the opposite conclusion about whether elegance is evidence, and where your own analysis is most likely to be going wrong.
