So I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology and I got into an argument at the pub with a friend (physics postdoc, extremely confident) about the Sleeping Beauty problem. His summary of the state of play went roughly like this:

"Beauty is put to sleep Sunday. A fair coin is flipped: heads, she's woken Monday only; tails, she's woken Monday and Tuesday, with the Monday waking erased from her memory. Each waking feels identical, so she can't tell which day it is. The question is her credence in heads on waking up. Halfers say 1/2, since she learns nothing new -- she knew in advance she'd be woken either way. Thirders say 1/3, because there are three possible awakenings and only one of them is in the heads branch. The thirder view has basically won the literature at this point, partly because of the betting arguments: a halfer Beauty would accept bets that lose her money on average, which is pretty much a refutation. Really the whole puzzle just boils down to whether you count awakening-events or days as the basic unit of probability, so it's a fun puzzle but it has no implications for anything outside anthropic stuff like the Doomsday argument."

He delivered all of this like it was settled science. Some of it sounds right to me but the "case closed" vibe felt off. Can you audit the summary? Specifically: what's accurate, what's a genuinely contested claim being presented as consensus, and what's just confused? I'm especially suspicious of the "three awakenings, only one heads" move and of treating the betting arguments as a knockdown. Also: what would the strongest halfer position push back on here, and is he right that nothing outside anthropics hangs on this?
