Had a long dumb argument with a friend last night about illusionism — the view (Frankish, Dennett-ish) that phenomenal consciousness is a kind of introspective illusion, that it seems to us like there's something it's like to see red, but the seeming is all there is and nothing further needs explaining. She says the view is self-refuting, because an illusion of experience is itself an experience. I say that's exactly what's being denied — the "seeming" is just how the brain represents its own states, and asking for more is refusing to take no for an answer. We went in circles for hours. Every functional story I gave about why a brain would misrepresent itself this way got met with "but the redness!", and every "but the redness!" got met with me accusing her of just asserting the very thing in dispute.

So diagnose this for me rather than picking a winner. Is this a real substantive disagreement, or is something else going on — her smuggling in a definition of experience that begs the question, me changing the subject from explaining consciousness to explaining our reports and intuitions about it, or two sets of bedrock intuitions neither side can argue *for*?
