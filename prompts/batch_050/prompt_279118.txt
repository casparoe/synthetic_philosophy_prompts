I am a distributed-systems engineer, fluent in deterministic state machines, snapshots, append-only event logs, hash chains, replay, replicas, compaction, garbage collection, and rollback, but I have only an informal understanding of statistical mechanics. Translate the low-entropy-boundary account of the direction of time into that vocabulary as rigorously as possible.

Use the following deliberately constructed analogy. A LedgerWorld is a finite universe whose microstate is a complete snapshot. Its transition function is deterministic and reversible: it bijectively maps every microstate to a successor microstate. A macrostate is what an operator can observe after discarding most snapshot details, and its entropy is the logarithm of the number of microstates compatible with it. At the initial checkpoint, LedgerWorld occupies an unusually small macrostate. Later apparent “histories” are produced through snapshots, replicated event logs, hash-linked records, compaction, and garbage collection.

First, restate each of the following claims in LedgerWorld terms:

1. Entropy’s tendency to increase is statistical rather than a fundamental law: from an atypically low-entropy initial condition, most microstates evolve toward macrostates with more compatible microstates, though fluctuations and recurrence remain possible.
2. The thermodynamic arrow explains why reliable records and memories concern the past rather than the future.
3. The apparent asymmetry of intervention—agents can influence the future but not the past—is derivative rather than primitive.
4. Practical irreversibility can arise even when the underlying transition function is perfectly reversible.

Then give a residue report. Which parts translate cleanly into snapshots, log replication, replay, and information loss through compaction? Which require distortion—for example, because an append-only log, a causal message order, or the distinction between checkpoint creation and replay already smuggles in a temporal direction? Which parts have no genuine counterpart in this systems vocabulary? Pay particular attention to whether “forgetting” in garbage collection is really analogous to entropy increase, and whether a hash chain establishes a thermodynamic arrow or merely represents one.

Conclude by diagnosing the residue: does the failure lie in LedgerWorld’s vocabulary, especially its presupposed notions of observation, control, and record creation, or does it reveal ambiguity in the statistical-mechanical account itself? Do not treat every engineering asymmetry as a successful translation.
