I recently took over sourcing for my family's small clothing brand and I've been touring factories and reading up on the ethics of it all, and I keep getting stuck on one thing. The economists I read say sweatshop jobs are mutually beneficial -- workers take them because they beat the alternatives (subsistence farming, scavenging, whatever), and the company benefits too. Fine, I buy that. But then philosophers call this "mutually beneficial exploitation," and honestly that seems like a straight-up contradiction? If both sides are better off than they'd be otherwise and nobody's being forced into anything, where's the exploitation? Exploitation has to mean someone ends up worse off, otherwise it's just an emotional label for a transaction people find icky. So the activists calling for boycotts and factory closures are basically just confused about this, right? What am I missing -- how is "mutually beneficial exploitation" even a coherent concept in the first place?
