So I fell down a rabbit hole about this endurantism vs. perdurantism debate, i.e. whether objects persist by being wholly present at each time or by having different temporal parts strung out like a worm. And I think I've found a fatal problem for the perdurantists. If I'm just a chain of momentary person-stages -- me-typing-this, me-eating-breakfast, me-asleep -- then strictly speaking no single one of those stages ever keeps a promise, holds a job, or stays married, because each stage vanishes after an instant and gets replaced by a numerically different one. Worse, the argument for perdurantism itself refutes itself: the stage that reads the first premise is gone by the time the conclusion shows up, so no stage ever believes the whole theory, and thus nobody could ever rationally accept it. Isn't this basically a knockdown refutation that the perdurantists have just been ignoring? Please answer in American English.
