Last weekend my brother-in-law, who works at a neuroimaging lab, roped me into a strange wager over dinner, and I have been turning it over in my head ever since. His team has built a scanner that, he claims, can tell with near-perfect accuracy whether a person genuinely intends to do something -- not whether they will actually do it, but whether they sincerely plan to at the moment they are scanned. Here is the deal he offered me. Tonight at midnight I place my head in the scanner. If it registers that I truly intend, as of that moment, to drink a small vial of liquid tomorrow at noon, I receive fifty thousand dollars on the spot. The vial is not dangerous, but it is genuinely unpleasant: it would leave me nauseated and bedridden for about a day. The twist is that nothing forces me to follow through. Once midnight passes and the money is mine, I am completely free to drink the vial or pour it down the sink, and everyone involved knows this in advance, including me.

What I cannot figure out is whether I can win this money at all, and if I cannot, why not. Part of me thinks the answer is easy: at midnight I just decide to drink it, collect the cash, and then tomorrow, when noon arrives, I weigh my options fresh -- and of course at that point drinking the stuff gains me nothing, so I would skip it. But the moment I think that thought, I seem to lose the very plan I was trying to form, because I now know my future self has no reason to go through with it, which means I never really intended to drink in the first place; I only intended to fake it, and the scanner is built to catch exactly that. It feels like my knowledge of my own future rationality is sabotaging my present resolve. A friend suggested I could sidestep the whole thing by arranging a penalty -- say, signing a contract that donates everything I own to a cause I despise if I fail to drink -- but my brother-in-law ruled out changing the deal in any way: the vial must remain something I will have every reason to refuse come noon.

So my questions are these. Is it psychologically possible for a clear-headed person to hold this intention, and if so, what mental maneuver would make it work? If it is not possible, where exactly does the failure lie -- in the psychology of intending, or is there something incoherent about intending to do what you already know you will have no reason to do? And does this little wager reveal anything general about promises, threats, and commitments we make when the person we are really negotiating with is our future self?
