Retired chemist here, got into philosophy of mind late in life and keep tripping over something I don't have a word for. In the lab I was happy with the idea that causation happens at the bottom: molecules bump, everything else follows. But now I read about brains and computers and I keep meeting cases where the macro-pattern seems to do the pushing. Two examples. A traffic jam crawls backwards down the motorway while every car in it drives forwards, and the jam genuinely makes people brake, so does the jam cause anything? And when my chess computer beats me, the best predictions of its next move come from thinking about its strategy, not its transistors -- and the same strategy could run on completly different hardware.

So: is there a name for the position that higher-level patterns (strategies, jams, thoughts, symbols) genuinely cause things at the lower level, without any extra spooky force being added to physics? I gather some say pattern-talk is just a handy summary with all real causation at the bottom, and others wave at emergence, but I want the middle carving: how would a defender of pattern-causation answer the charge that the pattern must be causally redundant, since the parts already suffice? If nothing established fits exactly, coin a term and give me the boundary cases. Also, say how confident you are in each part of the answer and flag the point you're least sure of.
