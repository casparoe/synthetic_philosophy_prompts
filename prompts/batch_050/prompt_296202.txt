Write an essay of approximately 1,500 words on the following problem.

A standards laboratory must decide whether to certify the generalization that every pressure gauge produced at the Harrow plant passes tolerance test T. The plant's warehouse holds a mixed population: gauges from Harrow, gauges from other manufacturers, and unrelated items such as lamps, tools, and packaging. Every object in the warehouse, whatever its kind, has been scanned and labeled either OK or FAULTY. Three technicians gather evidence under three different protocols.

Technician A samples uniformly at random from all objects in the warehouse. Her first draw is a lamp labeled FAULTY, and she records: a non-gauge that is FAULTY, hence no counterexample to the generalization. Technician B, considering A's procedure absurd, samples uniformly at random from among the non-gauges only; her first draw is likewise a lamp labeled FAULTY, recorded in the same words. Technician C samples uniformly at random from among the FAULTY objects only; her first draw is a lamp, recorded as: a FAULTY object that is not a gauge. By sheer coincidence, all three may have drawn the very same lamp. The question is whether their observations have the same evidential bearing on the generalization.

The essay should accomplish four things. First, analyze each draw in Bayesian terms: for each technician, state whether the observation confirms the generalization, disconfirms it, or leaves it untouched, and exhibit the likelihood comparison that settles the matter. (You may assume that the fault rate among non-gauges is independent of the quality of Harrow's gauges; say what happens to your verdicts if that assumption fails.) Second, connect the analysis to Hempel's raven paradox: identify which draws instantiate Nicod's criterion, and explain how the equivalence condition — evidence that confirms a hypothesis confirms anything logically equivalent to it — generates the appearance of paradox, given that the generalization is logically equivalent to the claim that every FAULTY object is a non-gauge. Third, adjudicate Hempel's own diagnosis of the paradox. Hempel held that an observation like the FAULTY lamp genuinely does confirm the generalization, and that the contrary intuition arises because we tacitly hold background knowledge fixed rather than assessing the evidence statement on its own. Take a position: does the protocol-sensitivity exposed in the first part vindicate this diagnosis, or does it instead show that confirmation cannot be the purely syntactic, two-place relation between an evidence statement and a hypothesis that Hempel's formal criteria presuppose — since A's and B's draws issue in the same evidence statement yet differ in evidential import? Fourth, reach a practical verdict: may the laboratory count B's and C's draws toward its certification requirement, and what does the answer imply about what an observation must be taken to include for purposes of confirmation theory?

The essay should defend a definite conclusion about whether the raven paradox is dissolved — a pseudo-problem with no lessons for confirmation theory — or instructive, for instance as a demonstration that the unit of evidence includes the sampling procedure, so that confirmation is at least a three-place relation among hypothesis, datum, and method of generation. A conditional verdict (dissolved under one construal of evidence, instructive under another) is acceptable if argued for rather than merely asserted. Engage Nicod's criterion and the equivalence condition by name; refer to positions and arguments in the literature as needed, but paraphrase rather than quote, and do not supply citations.
