I chair the ethics commitee of a small hospice charity. We've been left about £2 million in the will of a man who made his fortune in payday lending; the money would fund a new wing, and the only string attached is a small plaque with his name on it. We hired a well-regarded moral philosopher to advise us, and she's told us firmly that accepting the money would be wrong -- but, unusually, she says her judgement rests on twenty years of casework and pattern-spotting that she can't fully turn into arguments, so her written note is pretty thin. Half the committee wants to just defer: we'd take an engineer's word on a load-bearing wall, so why not a moral expert's? The other half says moral deference is different, and that we can't defend a decision to our donors and staff on the basis of someone else's say-so when none of us can see the reasons ourselves. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote: recommend whether we should take the money, and give the strongest losing argument a fair hearing, including whether our having to publicly defend the decision changes anything.
