A friend and I have spent much of the past year arguing, mostly over email, about puzzles involving forgetting and self-locating uncertainty — neither of us has any formal training, but we got hooked through a podcast and have been keeping score of each other's intuitions ever since. She has now committed to three verdicts, and I want to test how well I understand her mind. (a) In the classic Sleeping Beauty setup — fair coin; one awakening on Monday if heads, two amnesia-inducing awakenings on Monday and Tuesday if tails — she says Beauty's credence in heads on awakening should be one-third. (b) If Beauty is instead told upon awakening that it is Monday, my friend says the credence in heads should rise to two-thirds, by ordinary conditionalization. (c) In the absent-minded driver problem — the driver passes two indistinguishable intersections, cannot recall whether he has passed one already, and wants to exit at the second — she says the driver at an intersection should simply execute whatever mixed strategy was optimal when planning the trip, on the grounds that arriving at an indistinguishable intersection gives him no information that could license departing from his own plan.

Here is the new case I want to put to her, which I call the forgetful courier. A courier flips a fair coin before a delivery. If heads, her route has exactly one intersection, and the delivery address lies just past it. If tails, her route has two intersections that look and feel exactly alike; the delivery address lies just past the second one, and after passing the first she is given an amnesia pill so that arriving at the second feels subjectively identical to arriving at the first. Crucially, whether she ever reaches the second intersection on the tails route depends on her own behavior at the first: if she exits there, she goes home and never reaches the second. She must choose, and can commit to, a probability of continuing past any intersection she encounters. The questions I plan to ask her: given her chosen continuation probability, what credence should she assign, upon finding herself at an intersection, to the coin's having landed tails, and should she follow her pre-planned strategy or recompute at the intersection?

Please act as an expert on the epistemology and decision theory of self-locating belief, and think carefully and precisely before answering — I want to catch my friend in an inconsistency if there is one to catch, not to be handed a vague summary of the literature. I would like your reply as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: item 1 should state one rule that reproduces all three of my friend's verdicts; item 2 should state a second, genuinely different rule that also fits all three verdicts but treats strategy-dependent awakenings differently; item 3 should say what each rule predicts for the forgetful courier case, covering both the credence question and the follow-the-plan question, and making explicit any dependence of the credence on the courier's own continuation probability; item 4 should give your best guess at which verdict she would actually deliver, with your reason drawn from the pattern of (a)–(c); and item 5 should describe one further case, different from all the above, whose answer would discriminate between the two rules.
