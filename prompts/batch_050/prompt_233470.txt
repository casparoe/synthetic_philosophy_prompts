Years of reading LessWrong and its orbit have left me with a reflexive habit: whenever a debate gets slippery, I retreat to dispositional talk. A system is "agent-like" if it would steer toward outcomes across a range of counterfactual circumstances; a belief is "real" if it would manifest in behavior under the right conditions; an argument is "load-bearing" if its conclusion would survive removal of the surrounding rhetoric. But it recently struck me that I have no idea what I'm entitled to demand from an analysis of dispositions themselves — the fragility-and-conductivity literature that sits underneath all of this. So: before I read another conditional analysis or any of its rivals, I want the specification first. Write the desiderata for an adequate philosophical theory of dispositions: what such a theory must deliver in order to count as adequate for someone who wants disposition ascriptions to ground genuine counterfactuals rather than merely summarize observed behavior.

Give me the motivating case behind each requirement — the stock cases are fine (finkish dispositions, where a sorcerer stands ready to strip a glass of its fragility the instant it is struck; masks or antidotes, where a live wire runs through a sheath that would neutralize the current only if it flowed), but also test the requirements against a case I have not seen in the standard lineup: a fragile glass guarded by two demons, where the first demon would remove the glass's fragility the moment it was struck, and the second demon would disable the first demon's power the moment it activated, but the second demon's countermeasure is keyed to the first demon's act rather than to the striking. Is the glass fragile? An adequate theory should either deliver a principled verdict here or explain why no verdict is required — and the desiderata should say which. Where the disposition's fink is itself masked, and the mask is itself finked, where does the chain bottom out, and what feature of the theory decides that?

For each desideratum, mark whether you take it to be genuinely non-negotiable or merely the prejudice of one camp (I suspect, for instance, that the demand that dispositions rest on intrinsic categorical bases owes more to Humean taste than to any case that needs getting right). Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — my guess is that wanting disposition ascriptions to entail their associated counterfactuals and wanting finked objects to retain their dispositions is such a pair, but tell me whether that survives scrutiny — and identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidates. Then, briefly, score the main contenders against the list: the simple conditional analysis, reformed conditional analyses built to handle finks, views that locate dispositions in intrinsic causal structure, and full powers ontologies. Keep the scoring short; the criteria and their cases are the deliverable.

Open with your verdict in the first sentence — either the desideratum you judge most exclusionary or the pair you judge jointly unsatisfiable — then give the list and the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
