Last weekend I got into a long, unresolved argument with a friend from my old reading group, and it left me with the seed of a research project I want to develop properly. The fight was about the unilateralist's curse: the idea that when any one of many actors can carry out some irreversible initiative on their own, and each decides based on their private estimate of whether the initiative is good, the person most likely to act is the one with the most optimistic estimate -- so the initiative tends to happen exactly when the average judgment says it shouldn't, and rational actors should therefore deflate their own enthusiasm before acting. My friend conceded the basic selection effect but insisted the argument is self-defeating: once everyone knows about the curse and corrects for it, she claimed, the selection effect just reappears at the level of who corrects least, so awareness provides no stable resting point. I claimed that sufficiently thorough correction must converge on some equilibrium where the curse is neutralized. Neither of us could settle it.

Please write a proposal for a one-semester independent research project (roughly a 7,000-word paper) that turns this into a tractable question. My half-formed idea is to model agents who are all aware of the curse but differ in how aggressively they discount their own estimates, and to ask whether there is a rational equilibrium level of correction -- and whether making the correction itself common knowledge reproduces the curse one level up, dissolves it, or yields some mixed result. The proposal should: state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the informal treatments of the curse (which generally assume naive or uniformly correcting agents) leave it open; describe the method concretely (I imagine a simple Bayesian signaling or auction-style model, but tell me if there is a better tool and why); lay out the possible conclusions and what model features would decide between them; say what a negative or inconclusive result would still teach us; and give an honest account of what could make this too ambitious for a semester and what I would cut first. Please do not include citations or a bibliography -- I will supply those. Write the proposal in American English.
