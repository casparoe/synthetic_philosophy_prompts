I've been collecting my gut verdicts on which so-called generations are real kinds and which are just labels, and I want the principle underneath. Boomers feel like a real kind in the US, but the same 1946-1964 bracket applied to a country with no postwar birth spike feels like an empty borrowing. Millennials feel real to me, yet the people at the two ends of the bracket seem to have nothing formative in common (someone who was 20 on 9/11 vs someone who was 2), so whatever makes them one kind can't be shared experience. The 2008-2010 graduating class feels like a genuinely real cohort even though it has no catchy name and cuts across the official generation lines. "Xennials" feels fake, but I can't point to anything about its boundaries that's more arbitary than the boomer ones. The class of 2020 (covid graduations) feels real. "Gen Alpha" currently feels like pure marketing but I suspect that will change as they age into it. Is there one principle -- demographic discontinuity, shared formative events in some critical age window, or social uptake where a label becomes real by being used (looping-effect style) -- that captures all or most of these, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Also say what it predicts about cases I haven't listed: could there be a real generation nobody has ever named, and does your principle say Gen Z is one kind worldwide or only in countries that went through the smartphone transition together?
