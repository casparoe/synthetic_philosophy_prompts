I'm TAing an undergrad epistemology seminar this semester and the KK principle came up last week — roughly, the idea that if you know something, you're in a position to know that you know it. Half the class found it obviously true, half found it obviously false, and I realized I couldn't hand them a clean counterexample that wasn't just Williamson's anti-luminosity argument in disguise (the slowly dimming light, the tree whose height you can't judge to the centimeter, margins for error, all that). They've all seen the dim light case already and it didn't move the KK-lovers at all, since they just say the subject never really knew in the first place.

So here's what I want: construct a thought experiment in which a subject genuinely knows p at the first order but does not know that she knows p, and where the failure of KK is not explained by limted discriminative capacities or margins for error — the case shouldn't collapse into a luminosity argument. The higher-order ignorance has to come from somewhere else: misleading evidence about her own reliability, a reasonable but false belief about her epistemic situation, social/predictive circumstances, whatever actually works. Constraints: one ordinary-life scenario (office, kitchen, classroom — no demons, no evil neuroscientists, no perfectly reliable predictors), one paragraph, no sorites setup, no fake barns, and don't cheat by making her merely believe p rather than know it. Also don't just stipulate the verdicts; the scenario has to generate them.

After the scenario, do the verification work: state exactly what she knows and by what method, then walk through why she fails to know that she knows it, and name the single structural feature doing the work. Then tell me what happens if that feature is removed — does the case collapse back to KK holding, or does she lose the first-order knowledge too? Finally, steelman the attack: how would someone who thinks every KK failure ultimately traces back to a luminosity or margin-for-error failure try to redescribe your case as one of those, and does the redescription stick?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — basically whether such a case is buildable and what makes it work — then give the scenario and the analysis, no preamble and no restating my question.
