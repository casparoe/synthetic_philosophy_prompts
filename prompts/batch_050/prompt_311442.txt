So I'm giving a talk at our local rat meetup (mostly ACX/Astral Codex Ten people, a couple physics PhD students, one philosophy grad student who did her masters on the Doomsday argument) defending a position on Boltzmann brains, and I want a proper Q&A prep sheet.

My thesis, roughly: Boltzmann brain arguments are standardly taken to be a constraint on cosmological models -- if your model predicts that most observer-moments are fluctuated brains with scrambled memories, the model is cognitively unstable and should be rejected. I'm arguing this is overrated. My move is what I call the agency-weighted measure: instead of counting raw observer-moments, weight them by how much causal influence their decisions have on future observer-moments. Boltzmann brains have basically zero downstream influence, so they get ~zero weight, and the cognitive instability argument collapses without needing to reject the cosmology. I know this smells like an ad hoc patch cooked up to save big cosmologies, and yes, it has a flavor of "just define the problem away," but I think it's actually principled: it's the same intuition behind why we don't weight the simulations of us in alien laptops equally with ourselves in anthropics.

Prepare me for the question period. Give me maybe 8-10 objections, ordered by how likely they are times how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way the specific person would phrase it (the physics students will come at the measure problem in de Sitter space and proper-time cutoffs; the anthropic-reasoning crowd will hit me with SSA/SIA baggage and "isn't this just the xerographic distribution move again"; the philosophy grad student will probably press on whether causal influence is even well-defined for observer-moments). For each objection: what I should concede, what I should hold, a two-minute reply, and where the reply is soft. Also flag the weakest point in the agency-weighting idea itself -- the thing I'm quietly hoping nobody notices -- and tell me honestly whether you'd advise me to present it as a solved problem or as a promissory note. Definately don't pull punches on that last part.
