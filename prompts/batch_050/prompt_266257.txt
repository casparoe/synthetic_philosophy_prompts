In a popular book on the philosophy of time, the author dismisses the ancient worry about tomorrow's sea battle with a single image: the future is like a film that has already been shot and sits in its can. The frames are all there; our experience is merely the projector reaching them one at a time. On this picture, the claim that a sea battle will occur tomorrow is straightforwardly true or false now, because the relevant frame exists in the can just as much as the ones we have already watched, and the sense that the future is open is only the projectionist's ignorance of what is coming. The author uses the image to conclude that anxiety about fatalism is a confusion: nothing about the film being finished settles anything we should care about.

A colleague in theology objects with a rival image: the future is like a choose-your-own-adventure book, already printed, in which the reader's decisions at each juncture determine which page comes next. She uses this to conclude that the future is genuinely open while remaining intelligible, and that neither bivalence nor divine foreknowledge is threatened, since God can know the whole book, branches and all, without writing the reader's choices.

Audit both analogies. For each, give the correspondence table: what plays the role of the film stock, the projector, the present moment, the battle, the branches, the reader, the author. Then say where each mapping breaks. Consider in particular: whether the film reel conflates the fixity of what exists with the determinacy of what is true; whether "already in the can" borrows a tensed notion of existence that the argument was supposed to establish rather than presuppose; whether the adventure book, being printed in advance, smuggles in exactly the fixity it was enlisted to deny, and whether knowing a book cover to cover is even the same kind of knowing as knowing what will happen. Distinguish the disanalogies that matter for the conclusions each thinker draws from those that are merely decorative. Finally, state whether either conclusion survives the audit, and if a better analogy for future contingents is available, supply it.
