You are advising a three-person ethics research team that has twelve months to produce a framework for a national emergency-response agency. The agency is designing a rescue-allocation algorithm for disasters in which responders can save one of several mutually exclusive groups but cannot save everyone. Its director wants the framework to take seriously Taurek’s challenge to the idea that saving the greater number is automatically the morally required choice, without simply presupposing either Taurek’s conclusion or unrestricted aggregation.

Identify and rank the four or five open questions from the debate about whether the numbers count that this team should prioritize. Possible candidates include: whether equal claims can be summed across persons; whether numbers may break ties even if they cannot otherwise justify sacrifice; whether claims differ in strength because of differing risks of death or chances of rescue; whether an ex ante policy should be evaluated differently from a one-off rescue involving identifiable people; whether partial aggregation can preserve the separateness of persons; and whether the debate’s conclusions change when an algorithm must apply a stable rule across many cases. You may replace or reorder these if another question is more important.

For each item in your ranking, explain why it deserves priority using at least three considerations: how many other policy questions depend on it, how tractable it is for philosophers within twelve months, and how much its answer would alter the agency’s eventual decision rule. Also discuss neglectedness where relevant. Include at least one question or alleged implication that the team should deprioritize despite its prominence, explaining whether it is downstream, not decision-relevant, or mistakenly treated as equivalent to the numbers question.

The deliverable should be a defended research agenda, not a general survey of the literature. Make clear what argument or result during the year could change your ranking. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list.
