I've been persuaded by the Hartz-style story of American exceptionalism: roughly, that the US's durable weirdness (no serious socialist labor party, high religiosity, anti-statism, weak welfare state) traces to starting as a settler society with no feudal past, lots of cheap land, and an already-liberal founding stock, so the usual European class-politics pressures never got traction. What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I especially want you to use the comparison cases the theory itself invites -- Canada and Australia are settler societies too, and Canada even shares the frontier and the liberal founding, so tell me exactly which divergences (single-payer healthcare, stronger unions, a parliamentary left) the theory has to explain away and which it can genuinely absorb. Also: what would the theory predict that rival explanations (racial fragmentation, presidentialism, religion-driven) don't, and has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability -- e.g., is "America was exceptional but converging after 1980" a prediction or a patch? Finally, be honest about whether evidence against the historical thesis would even touch exceptionalism as a normative claim or as ideology, or whether those float free of the facts.
