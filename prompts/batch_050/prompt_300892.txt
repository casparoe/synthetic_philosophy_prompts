So I'm on the hiring committee at my job (small company, three of us run the final round) and we just landed in a genuinely weird spot. We agreed beforehand that to make an offer, a candidate has to clear two bars: strong technical skills and good communication. Yesterday we interviewed a guy and scored him seperately on each. I thought he was great on both, so I'd hire him. My coworker Ana thought he was technically strong but a rough communicator — she'd pass. Our third member, Luis, thought he communicated well but wasn't technical enough — also a pass.

Here's the problem: if we tally by criterion, he clears both bars (me and Ana on technical, me and Luis on communication), so by our own rule we should make the offer. But if we just take a straight up-or-down vote on hiring him, it's 2 to 1 against. Same three people, same opinions, and the outcome flips depending on how we count. The candidate is waiting and HR wants an answer by end of day.

Which way should we actually decide this? And more than that — why does the answer flip at all? It feels like one of these counting methods has to be the "correct" one, but I can't figure out how you'd even argue for one over the other. Also, should we be worried this happens again? Is there a way to set up our process so it can't, or is this just a thing that can bite any group that votes on stuff with multiple conditions?
