Working particle physicists now treat essentially every quantum field theory as an effective field theory: they truncate the Lagrangian at some order in an expansion in powers of energy over a cutoff scale, organize the discarded terms by operator dimension, and trust their low-energy predictions — cross-sections, anomalous magnetic moments, the chemistry underlying everyday materials — without knowing, or needing to know, what happens near the Planck scale. The justification offered is the decoupling of scales: heavy physics leaves only suppressed traces at accessible energies, so each level of description is largely autonomous from the ones above it. Yet the same community spent four decades arguing that the smallness of the Higgs mass relative to any plausible ultraviolet cutoff was unnatural, since radiative corrections drag the mass up to the cutoff unless unknown new physics near the weak scale cancels them — and this argument was widely used to predict new particles at the LHC, which have so far failed to appear. Meanwhile the standard is applied selectively: the electron's tiny mass in Planck units provokes no anxiety, because setting it to zero enlarges the theory's symmetry and so is deemed technically natural, while the cosmological constant, though vastly more unnatural by the same style of reasoning, has not been taken to require new physics at the corresponding scale.

What view of the relationship between theories at different scales does effective-field-theory practice presuppose — about explanation, about fundamentality, about what it would mean for one level of description to be genuinely autonomous from another? And what view of probability or typicality does naturalness reasoning presuppose, given that it seems to require a measure over possible parameter values that nobody possesses? Are the two practices coherent in combination, given that the first asserts that low-energy predictions are insulated from unknown ultraviolet physics while the second appears to let unknown ultraviolet physics dictate what we should expect to find? Distinguish what each practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, note where the practice is philosophically underdetermined — where several rival accounts of inter-level relations would license the same methodology — and say what would concretely have to change in working physics if naturalness reasoning were abandoned wholesale, or if the autonomy of scales turned out to fail.
