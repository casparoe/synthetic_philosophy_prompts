Ok so at a BBQ last weekend two of my friends got into this whole argument about memes, not internet memes exactly but the bigger idea that memes are basically like genes, little replicators that spread between brains and get selected for. It started cause Dan had watched some video about it and was going on about how memes "use" us to copy themselves, the way a cold virus uses you to spread, and Marta was having none of it. But honestly they both made points that sounded right in the moment and I cant figure out who actually won. I'll try to reconstruct it.

Dan's opening move was: some ideas spread even when they're bad for us, like chain letters ("send this to 10 people or bad luck"), earworm songs you can't get out of your head, doomscrolling bait that makes you miserable. If we were just rationally picking good ideas, that shouldn't happen. So the meme's-eye-view explains stuff the "people choose ideas" view can't -- the meme isn't there for you, you're there for it.

Marta said that's just relabeling. "This idea spread because it's good at spreading" is circular, it explains nothing, you could say the same about anything popular. Psychology already explains why chain letters work (superstition, guilt) without inventing a new creature that "wants" to replicate.

Dan: but that's what people said about genes too, "this organism survives because its traits are good at surviving" -- and that turned out to be the most productive idea in biology. Circularity isn't the problem if the framework generates predictions.

Then Marta made what I thought was her strongest point: genes actually get copied, like physically duplicated. Ideas don't. When I tell you a story, you don't download my copy, you rebuild your own version from scratch, and by three retellings it's a different story. So there's no replication, just reconstruction, and without replication the whole Darwinian machinery doesn't get going.

Dan came back with two things. One: gene copying isn't perfect either, that's where mutations come from, so fidelity can't be the dealbreaker. Two: sometimes ideas DO get copied exactly. He gave this example that I actually found kind of creepy -- he collects these old chain emails and he showed us how the same typo, like a specific misspelled word, shows up in copies from years apart, and you can trace lineages of the letter by which typos they carry, exactly like biologists trace species by shared mutations. If people were "reconstructing from scratch" you couldn't do that.

Marta said fine, chain letters are a freak case where the whole point is to copy verbatim, you can't generalize from a case designed for copying. And she added: look at misheard song lyrics. Half the time the wrong version spreads better than the real lyrics. That's not mutation-plus-selection, that's every listener generating their own wrong version independently, which proves her reconstruction point.

Dan said that IS selection -- lots of wrong versions get generated, the catchiest wrong version wins and spreads, and the fact that everyone converges on the same mishearing is selection pressure, not a coincidence. At which point they were kind of talking past each other and someone changed the subject to the potato salad.

Is the copying-vs-reconstruction thing the fatal objection Marta thinks it is, and does Dan's typo-lineage example actually defuse it or is Marta right that it's a special case? And the misheard lyrics -- whose side does that support? Also curious if theres something both of them are missing, some assumption they shared that they shouldve been fighting about instead.
