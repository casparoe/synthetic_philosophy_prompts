I'm in a philosophy of mind course this semester and we've spent the last two weeks on Kim's causal exclusion argument, and I can't shake the feeling that the way it's usually framed makes it look way more threatening than it actually is. As I understand it, the argument goes roughly: every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause (the closure principle), so if my decision to raise my hand is something over and above the neural cause of my hand going up, then either my decision is causally idle, or every single action is systematically overdetermined, which is absurd. So the mental gets excluded. The crucial step, it seems to me, is that the mental cause and the physical cause have to be two genuinely seperate things — otherwise there's no competition at all, just one cause getting described in two vocabularies.

But here's where I get confused about who the argument is even aimed at. If you're an identity theorist — you think pains just are C-fiber firings, decisions just are certain neural events — then the whole problem seems to evaporate: my decision causes my hand to rise because it is the physical cause, and closure is satisfied trivially. So the exclusion argument only appears to have teeth against dualists, who are a pretty small minority these days. Yet my textbook presents it as a major problem for physicalists, specifically the mainstream nonreductive kind, and I honestly don't see how. If the nonreductive physicalist would just admit that the mental is identical to the physical, wouldn't the problem dissolve on the spot? (A guy in my discussion section also claimed overdetermination isn't even that weird — firing squads overdetermine deaths all the time — so maybe the whole worry is overblown, but that seems too quick to me.) What am I missing about why mainstream physicalism is supposed to be in real trouble here?

Feel free to be blunt: if my reconstruction of the argument is off, or I'm conflating two things that shouldn't be conflated, I'd rather have that pointed out directly than get a polite answer to a confused question.
