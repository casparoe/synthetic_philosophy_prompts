I'm a hobbyist screenwriter and I've spent the past year working on a script about a real person — a fairly obscure labor organizer from my hometown in the 1930s, not famous at all, nobody's ever made anything about her. I got hooked on her through old newspaper clippings in the local archive. Here's my problem. The documented record is thin and honestly kind of flat at exactly the point where the story needs a climax. So I invented one: in my script she secretly informs on a rival organizer to protect her own position, and then lives with the guilt. It works dramtically, it ties the whole third act together. It also makes her look pretty bad, and there's zero evidence anything like it happened — if anything the surviving letters point the other way.

A producer friend of mine says this is completely standard, every biopic does it, audiences know not to take "based on a true story" literally, and she'd probably be flattered anyone remembered her at all. I can't shake the feeling that something's off, but I can't articulate what. She died in 1978, so it's not like I can hurt her feelings or ruin her career. Her grandkids are alive, though — they were sweet when I interviewed them and they trusted me with her personal letters. Does that change things, or is the issue (if there is one) independent of who's left to be upset? And here's a wrinkle I keep circling: because she's obscure, my film would effectively BECOME the public record of her. If I made stuff up about Lincoln, my version would be one of fifty and nobody would mistake it for the truth. For her, whatever I put on screen is just what people will think happened. Should the obscurity change the ethics, or is that just me being self-important about my own influence?

One more thing that's been bugging me: would it be equally fine — or equally not fine — if I'd gone the other direction and invented something that made her look heroic when she wasn't? Everyone seems to think flattering inventions about the dead are harmless, and I'm not so sure. So: if you think my unease tracks something real, give me the best account of what exactly I'd be doing wrong, and who I'd even be wronging — her, the grandkids, the audience, the historical record itself? And if you think the producer friend is right, tell me where my intuition goes wrong, because I've been stuck on this for weeks and it's blocking the rewrite.
