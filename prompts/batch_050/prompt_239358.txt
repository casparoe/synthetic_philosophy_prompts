Please take on the role of a professional philosopher specializing in moral responsibility and Strawson's account of the reactive attitudes. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming the principles that could unify the case record below before committing to an answer. Please write in American English.

The question has become personally pressing for me. My younger brother developed an opioid addiction that began with a series of fully voluntary, much-warned-against choices in his twenties, and he has since been stealing money from our parents to pay for pills. When he takes the money he is often in the grip of craving and barely thinking about us, though he retains his ordinary intelligence and self-control in other domains. I cannot tell whether my resentment toward him is fitting, or whether I ought to relate to him the way one relates to a storm -- something to be managed rather than blamed.

A close friend of mine, who has thought about excuses and exemptions for years and whose judgment I usually trust, has given me these verdicts over time:

- A colleague who snapped at her after thirty-six hours without sleep, days after a bereavement: excused.
- A charming con artist who defrauded elderly people and appears constitutionally incapable of caring: she does not resent him; dealing with him, she says, is like dealing with bad weather.
- A rested, competent executive who quietly signed off on misleading accounts because he simply did not care about the people affected: full blame.
- An adult with profound intellectual disability who deliberately smashed a family heirloom in a fit of frustration: no blame.
- A driver who struck a pedestrian while suffering an unforeseeable first seizure: no blame.

Please predict her verdict on my brother, identify which earlier case is the closest analog, and say which principle she is implicitly running -- a rule keyed to the agent's capacities at the moment of action, a rule keyed to the quality of will the act expresses, a rule that traces blame back to earlier responsible choices, or some hybrid. If her record genuinely underdetermines the case, say so honestly and state what principle she would be adopting under each possible verdict. Finally, describe the single further case that would most cleanly reveal which rule she is actually using.
