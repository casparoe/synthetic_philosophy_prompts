I became interested in Maxwell's demon through my work on reversible computing and data compression, and I have been trying to understand the modern resolution of the paradox. My reading is that the debate has more or less stalled, and I cannot tell why. The standard story I encounter runs: Szilard reduced the demon to a single particle in a box and located the entropy cost in measurement; Landauer then argued that logically irreversible operations, above all erasing a bit, dissipate a minimum of kT ln 2 of heat per bit; Bennett completed the exorcism by showing the demon can measure reversibly but must eventually erase its memory to return to its initial state, and that erasure pays the full thermodynamic bill. On this view, information is physical and the second law is saved by the physics of information processing.

But there is a dissenting tradition, associated in my mind with John Earman and John Norton, which holds that this resolution is circular: the claim that erasure necessarily costs kT ln 2 is typically derived using the very second law it is supposed to protect, so the demon is not defeated but merely absorbed into the assumptions. The rejoinder from the Landauer-Bennett side seems to be that the cost of erasure is established independently by statistical-mechanical reasoning about phase-space compression, so there is no circle. And there the matter appears to rest. Both sides are competent, both understand the physics, and yet after several decades the dispute about whether the demon has truly been exorcised keeps recurring in new forms.
