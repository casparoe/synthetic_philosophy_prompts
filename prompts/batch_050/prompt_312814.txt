so rousseau's social contract has always bugged me, specifically the general will stuff -- the idea that the general will is always right and aims at the common good, that it's different from just adding up what everyone happens to want (the will of all), that individuals can be mistaken about what their own general will actually wills, and of course the infamous move where someone who refuses to obey it gets, in rousseau's cheerful phrase, forced to be free.

write me a parody of a corporate HR employee handbook, maybe 600-700 words, for a company that has restructured itself around the general will. deadpan corporate-speak throughout, formatted like a real handbook with numbered sections and maybe a FAQ at the end. stuff it could cover: the difference between what employees actually voted for at the all-hands meeting (mere aggregate preference, please see your manager) and what the General Will decided; the onboarding session where new hires are shown that their true will was always to work here; a disciplinary procedure explaining that being escorted to mandatory team-building is not coercion but liberation from your merely particular self; the ban on factions/departments forming sub-groups with their own interests; maybe the mysterious founding CEO who designed the whole structure and then had to leave the company because, as the handbook puts it, the legislator can have no part in the general will they instituted. the jokes should come from getting the rousseau right -- the satire should work because this IS what the view says, transplanted into a context where it sounds horrifying, not because you made up a strawman. a political theorist should be able to nod along going "yep, that's the legislator, yep, that's the will of all distinction."

stay fully deadpan, no winking, the handbook should genuinely believe in itself.
