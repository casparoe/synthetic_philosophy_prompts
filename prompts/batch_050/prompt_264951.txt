I'm refereeing a dispute about Skolem's paradox and I want the standard of adequacy nailed down first. Write the list of requirements a genuine resolution of the paradox has to meet, with the consideration motivating each one: what it must explain, which theorems it has to stay consistent with (downward Löwenheim–Skolem at minimum), and what it's not allowed to just assume. A few things that have to be on the list: it needs to say something about both first- and second-order set theory, since full second-order semantics dissolves the paradox but arguably just moves it somewhere else; it has to take a stand on whether "countable from outside, uncountable from inside" is a real relativity in the concept of cardinality or just sloppy talk about models; and it has to deal with the worry that appealing to the "intended interpretation" to pin down set-theoretic vocabulary begs the question, since you can only specify that interpretation in some language, which itself admits unintended models. Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are really just demands of the platonist camp that a formalist can shrug off, and if some subset of the desiderata turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, flag that — it's the part I care about most.
