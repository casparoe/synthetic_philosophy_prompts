I was recently in a reading group on philosophy of mind where someone brought up an old thought experiment of Douglas Hofstadter's called the Careenium, and it has been nagging at me ever since. As it was explained to me: imagine a vast frictionless billiard table populated by two kinds of objects -- tiny marbles, called simms, and enormous balls, called simmballs, each simmball so large that it dwarfs the simms. The simmballs bash into one another in lawful, patterned ways, and because their collisions are so regular and their configurations so rich, an observer can read them as symbols: a given arrangement of simmballs means something, and the sequence of collisions looks, from the god's-eye balcony, like a computation or a train of thought unfolding at the symbol level. The simms, meanwhile, dart around underneath it all, and at the fine-grained level every motion of every simmball is nothing over and above the aggregate motions of simms shoving and being shoved. Hofstadter's question, as it was put to me, was: who is shoving whom around in there? My reading-group friend takes the Careenium to show that downward causation is perfectly respectable -- that the symbol level genuinely pushes the substrate around, so that explaining an event by citing what a simmball-configuration means is just as legitimate as, and sometimes more explanatory than, citing the simms. My other friend, a physicist, says the Careenium proves the opposite: nothing in the device ever moves except in accordance with substrate-level physics, so symbol-talk is a compressed redescription of the causal story, never a rival to it, and the feeling that symbols "push" anything is an artifact of standing on the balcony.

I'd like you to audit this analogy and the argument my first friend builds on it. Start with the correspondence table: in the intended application to minds, what plays the role of the simms, the simmballs, the symbol-level patterns, the observer on the balcony, and the shoving itself? Then identify where the mapping breaks or quietly does unearned work. Some candidate pressure points, which you should evaluate rather than simply accept: the Careenium builds in a stipulated clean separation between two levels (there really are two discrete sizes of ball, with no mid-sized entities blurring the hierarchy), whereas brains may not come pre-sorted into a neat symbol level and a neat substrate level; the simmballs' symbolic meaning is conferred by an external observer, so it is unclear whether the symbols do anything in the system or only for the balcony; and the substrate in the story is causally closed and fully deterministic, which may make the "symbol level pushes back" reading available only in a trivial sense that any coarse-grained redescription of anything would enjoy. For each disanalogy you find, say whether it is fatal to the intended use or merely cosmetic.

Finally, adjudicate. If downward causation in the Careenium amounts only to "a useful higher-level description of a process entirely carried out at the lower level," does that give my first friend the genuine mental causation she wants, or does it give my physicist friend everything he needs? Consider what would have to be true of the Careenium for symbols to push the substrate in a non-trivial sense -- some slack, indeterminacy, or multiple realizability at the lower level, perhaps -- and whether adding that feature to the device rescues the stronger reading or simply relocates the mystery. If neither friend is right, say what the Careenium actually shows, and whether a better analogy for the relation between thoughts and neurons is available.
