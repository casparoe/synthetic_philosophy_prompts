I am a doctoral student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I am drafting the position statement that will anchor a dissertation chapter on machine mentality. Before I commit to it, I would like an audit of whether the following commitments are jointly tenable, since several colleagues have told me the package smells inconsistent without agreeing on where.

1. Belief is interpretation all the way down. A system has the beliefs that the best overall interpretation from the intentional stance assigns to it, and there is no further fact of the matter: no internal sentence, no particular functional architecture, is required beyond what the best interpretation fixes.

2. Consciousness is irrelevant to belief, and so is etiology. A system assembled by chance, or trained merely to imitate human text, believes exactly what a psychologically normal human with matching behavioral dispositions would believe.

3. Current large language models, at their best, genuinely believe things. A well-functioning model that reliably tells you Paris is the capital of France, deploys that claim in inference, and revises neighboring claims accordingly believes that Paris is the capital of France, literally and not by courtesy.

4. When such a model confidently asserts a falsehood, the correct description is that it holds a false belief, not that it merely emits text while believing nothing.

5. A lookup-table conversationalist — a machine that passes every behavioral test over its operational lifetime by table lookup — has no beliefs whatsoever.

6. A thermostat has no beliefs, not even a single minimal belief about the room.

7. There is a principled difference between the chatbot of (3) and the encyclopedia on my shelf: the book contains the information that Paris is the capital of France but believes nothing, while the chatbot believes it.

My questions: which pairs among these are genuinely in tension, and how severe is each? Which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection? For each genuine tension, identify the cheapest available revision and what it costs. Please rank the problems by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, conclude with the single revision that restores the most consistency at the lowest price, and tell me what the repaired package then implies about a system I have not classified — say, a top chess engine.
