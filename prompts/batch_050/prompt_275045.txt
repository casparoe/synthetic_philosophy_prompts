I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm working on a piece about fact-checking as a profession — the editorial kind, not the political genre. I've spent time with checkers at several publications, and the practice has a structure I can't get philosophically clear on.

Here's how it works. For every article, the checker re-verifies each factual claim independently: names and dates against documents, quotations against recordings or transcripts, statistics against the original studies, descriptions of events against multiple sources. Two independent sources for anything contested. Rigorous, adversarial, proud of it. But whole classes of claims go entirely unchecked, and nobody finds this strange. No checker verifies that the voice on the recording belongs to the person the reporter says they interviewed rather than to an impersonator. Nobody checks the arithmetic in the piece, or that the university cited in the third paragraph actually exists, or that the reporter didn't fabricate the transcript wholesale, or that the checker's own memory of reading a source twenty minutes ago is veridical. A checker who demanded evidence for those things would be regarded not as unusually rigorous but as confused about the job. Yet the checked and unchecked claims don't differ in kind — all are empirical, all are fallible, and recordings can be dubbed and transcripts mistyped just as statistics can be miscopied.

My question is what theory of justification this division of labor presupposes. Does the practice assume that some propositions are simply certain, a foundation on which checking builds? Or something subtler — that doubt and verification can only operate against a background of claims that stand fast without justification, functioning as scaffolding for inquiry rather than as its conclusions? Or is the line merely pragmatic triage under deadline, compatible with any epistemology at all? I'd like the implicit theory reconstructed, with a clear distinction between what the practice strictly requires and what it merely happens to fit, and an honest account of where it's underdetermined — where several rival epistemologies would license exactly the same desk procedures. One more wrinkle: with synthetic audio now cheap, the industry is quietly reclassifying recordings from bedrock to checkable claim, which suggests the unchecked class was never where the official rationale said it was. If that's right, what follows for the practice — and what would a checking desk have to do differently if it took seriously the view that no empirical claim is exempt, that everything stands or falls with the whole web of belief?

Please give your answer as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion.
