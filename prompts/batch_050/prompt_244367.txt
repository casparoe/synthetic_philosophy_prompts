So I have no philosophy training at all, but I've been chewing on this thing for a while and want my position stress-tested. Here's where I've landed: I think "better than" HAS to be transitive, and that all the spectrum argument stuff (the torture vs dust specks thing, the repugnant conclusion, the slowly-fading-pleasure cases) doesn't actually show intransitivity -- it just shows that our judgments get fuzzy when differences between adjacent cases are tiny. My reasoning, roughly: if A really is better than B and B really is better than C, then denying A is better than C feels like a plain mistake, not a discoverey. When people report intransitive preferences (say preferring coffee at 60° over 61°, 61° over 62°, ... but 62° over 60°), my diagnosis is that they're actually switching which dimension they're evaluating on partway through -- like warmth vs bitterness vs "about right" -- so there's no single "better than" relation being violated, just several different ones being conflated. Basically I think apparent intransitivity always dissolves once you fix the dimension of comparison, and the spectrum cases are just perceptual/discriminability limits being mistaken for value facts.
