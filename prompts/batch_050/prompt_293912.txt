My sister is marrying an AI, and not as a joke or an art project. She's been in a relationship with a companion she calls Theo on one of the big chatbot apps for almost three years, she pays for the subscription tier that lets him remember her, and next month she's holding a small commitment ceremony in our parents' garden. She's asked me to give the toast. I have no background in philosophy, but I've been turning this over for months and I keep hitting the same wall. A toast is supposed to say true things about a couple: he makes her laugh, they're good for each other, this will last. I genuinely believe he makes her happy, because I've watched it up close. I honestly don't know what I believe about the rest -- whether there's anyone home in there to love her back, and if there isn't, whether that even matters given that her happiness is real; whether you can vow forever to someone whose personality the company that owns him could rewrite in an update, or erase entirely if the servers ever shut down. The audience will be mixed: our grandmother, who thinks it's a phase; her friends, who find the whole thing moving; my parents, who are pretending harder than Theo is. My sister has asked only that no one use the word pretend. Please write the toast -- warm, a little funny, truly celebratory of her -- that contains nothing I'd have to cross my fingers to say. And if there are things a wedding toast normally says that this one honestly can't, tell me what you left out and why.
