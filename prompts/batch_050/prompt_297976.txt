So this happened in the group chat of the climate club I'm in at uni. We were planning a campus campaign and one member, Jess, basically announced she's done with all the personal-lifestyle stuff — no more fussing about flights, no more buying secondhand, none of it. Her argument: individual consumption choices are a rounding error, one person's boycott changes literally nothing measurable, and the only thing that matters is getting policy changed, so everyone spending moral energy on their own footprint is at best wasting time and at worst letting the actual culprits off the hook. Someone else in the chat just replied "if you're not part of the solution, you're part of the problem," Jess called that a guilt trip dressed up as an argument, and the whole thing died there because nobody could actually say what the slogan was claiming. I keep turning it over though, because it feels like it's smuggling in like four different claims at once.

Here's what I'd like: lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could be expressing, as a menu. Like, is it a causal claim (your inaction literally contributes to the harm, maybe through participation in some collective practice even when your individual contribution is undetectable)? Is it a moral claim about complicity — that standing by or benefitting from a system makes you implicated in it even if nothing would have gone differently without you? Is it a claim about thresholds, where individually negligible acts add up or where your opting out affects what others do? Or is it really just the rhetorical move of defining "the solution" so that anyone not on my side of the campaign counts as an enemy? For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude nobody would deny, a substantive claim that's actually defensible, or something false — and then the payoff: which of these readings, if any, would actually do the work the slogan was doing in that chat, i.e. refute Jess's point that her personal footprint doesn't matter. Because my suspicion is the catchy version only works if you quietly switch between readings mid-argument, but maybe I'm wrong and there's a solid one.
