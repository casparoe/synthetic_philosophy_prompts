I'm a high school student preparing a presentation for my school's ethics club on the "numbers problem": when you can save either one group of strangers or another, but not both, should you simply save the greater number? Please build me a decision tree of six to eight branch nodes that sorts a person into a position on this question based on their answers to concrete cases, not on labels they already know. Each node should be phrased so a classmate could answer without any philosophy background — for instance, a choice between rescuing one person from one island or five from another, a fair coin flip for a scarce rescue spot, or a choice between curing many people's mild pain and saving one person's life. The leaves should include at least: straight aggregation (always produce the greatest total benefit), Taurek-style non-aggregation (give everyone an equal chance, since each person's claim is separate and claims don't add up), a proportional lottery (chances weighted by what each person stands to lose), and pairwise comparison of individual complaints. For every combination of answers that ends up unoccupied, add one sentence on whether it's incoherent or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't genuinely separate.
