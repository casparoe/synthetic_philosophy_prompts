I'm putting together a proposal for a summer research grant (about 6 weeks of funding) in philosophy of art, and I need help turning a half-formed idea into a one-page pitch. The idea: the ontology-of-music debate I know treats musical works as types that get tokened in performances, and then fights about what kind of type they are. But it seems to me a lot of music made in the last fifty years was never performed at all -- it was assembled in a studio, track by track, and the only thing that ever gets "played" is a recording. The recording isn't a performance of the work, and it isn't just a document of one either. So my hunch is the field needs a second basic category, something like works-for-playback alongside works-for-performance, and that once you have it, a bunch of edge cases (remasters, remixes, live versions of studio tracks, covers that change the arrangement) sort themselves differently. Write the proposal: state the question precisely, explain why the standard positions on what musical works are leave it unanswered rather than answered, say what I'd actually do over six weeks (what cases I'd work through, in what order), what a positive result would look like, and what would count as the project failing -- e.g., if the playback cases turn out to be handled fine by the existing framework, I want to know that too.
