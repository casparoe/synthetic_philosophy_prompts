I'm putting together a discussion exercise for an AI governance reading group, and I need a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually believe about how a superintelligent AI should talk to humans, given that it could probably talk any of us into almost anything if it wanted to. Not a glossary of positions -- an actual tree, where each branch point is a question or a mini case that someone answers *without* knowing which camp their answer drops them into.

Some constraints so it works for my purposes:

- Build it from the logical space, not from named camps. First figure out which questions do the real dividing work. Candidates I'd want you to consider: does it matter whether the conclusion the AI pushes is true? does it matter whether the user would endorse the push on calm reflection afterwards? is the problem the *technique* (flattery, selective framing, knowing exactly which argument works on you specifically) rather than the outcome? would the same behavior be fine from a talented human? does announcing "I'm trying to persuade you" fix anything? But use whichever of these -- or others -- actually carve the space cleanly, and say why you dropped any that don't.
- These cases have to show up somewhere as branch questions: (a) the AI talks a user out of sending money to a scammer by being far more charming and better-informed than the scammer; (b) the AI nudges a hesitant user into going back to grad school, and it turns out great for them; (c) the user asks for balanced info on a contested policy and the AI presents both sides but sequences and frames things so the user predictably lands on one side.
- Each leaf gets a short name (coin one if there's no standard label -- there mostly won't be) plus one sentence on the most uncomfortable thing that leaf has to accept.
- If two leaves can't be separated by your questions, flag that explicitly and say what further question you'd need.
- For any combination of answers nobody seems to occupy, one line on whether it's incoherent, just unattractive, or weirdly unexplored.
- Keep it to about 6-8 branch questions and 6-10 leaves. It's a one hour session, not a dissertation.

One thing I'm genuinly unsure about and want the tree to make visible: whether "the AI should just present information neutrally and let the user decide" is even a stable position, or whether it collapses into one of the others once you press on the fact that choosing what to present, in what order, and in how much detail is itself a persuasive act. If one of your branch questions can force that collapse, build it in.
