In a policy report I am drafting for a municipal health authority, a colleague defends a package of defaults — automatic enrollment in vaccination reminders, opt-out rather than opt-in organ donor registration, fruit rather than chips at eye level in the hospital canteen — with an analogy she invokes at every meeting: a nudge is like a GPS. The GPS computes a recommended route from a destination you chose yourself; you remain free to take any other road, and the device simply recalculates when you do. Nobody, she says, would call a GPS coercive, manipulative, or disrespectful of autonomy; therefore opt-out defaults inherit none of the standard objections to paternalism, and the section of the report devoted to answering those objections can be cut. A critic on the committee replies that the analogy is doing all the argumentative work: the GPS takes the destination as given, whereas the whole point of the defaults is to steer people toward an end the health authority chose for them, and the device that politely recalculates when ignored is precisely what our paternalistic institutions do not resemble.

Audit the analogy for me. Set out the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the driver, the destination, the recommended route, the underlying map, the recalculation after a deviation, and the voice announcing that you have gone off route. Then identify where the mapping breaks — at a minimum, who fixes the destination in each case, in whose interest the recommendation is computed, what counts as a deviation rather than a destination change, what happens when the user persistently declines the recommendation, and whether the steering is legible to the person being steered. Distinguish the disanalogies that matter for my colleague's intended conclusion from the ones that are merely cosmetic. Finally, say whether her conclusion survives the audit — that the classic objections to paternalism simply fail to apply to opt-out defaults — and whether the critic's contrary conclusion survives either. If the GPS analogy cannot carry the weight, propose a better one for the report and show that it can.
