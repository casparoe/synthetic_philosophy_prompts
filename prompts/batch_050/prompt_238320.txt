So I moved to Munich last year for work (I'm a lab tech, nothing fancy) and when I got my first proper payslip there was this line item, Kirchensteuer, taking a chunk of money on top of my regular taxes. I asked around and apparently because I got baptized Catholic as a kid back in the States, the German tax office just... assumes I'm a member and collects money for the church out of my wages. I havent been to mass since I was like twelve. My coworkers say I can go to some office and formally declare I'm leaving the church, but then there's a fee just to do the paperwork, and a colleague warned me that if I ever want a church wedding, or my parents want a church funeral for me someday, that could get complicated or cost extra. Which honestly feels a bit like blackmail?

Anyway I'm torn and I keep going back and forth on it. On the one hand, why should I pay for an organization I don't belong to in any real sense? On the other hand, my mom would be sad, and I can't rule out wanting the big church wedding someday, which makes me feel like leaving would be kind of hypocritical -- like I want the benefits without contributing. But then I think, is it even right that the state is doing the collecting in the first place? Where I grew up everyone made a big deal about the government staying out of religion and religion staying out of government, and here the tax office is basically the church's collection agency and my German friends just shrug like it's totally normal. And it bugs me that apparently only some religions get this arrangement -- a coworker who's Muslim said his community doesn't.

So two questions I guess. First, what should I actually do -- stay registered and pay, or formally leave? And second, stepping back from my own wallet: is this system defensible at all? Is there a version of it that's actually fair, maybe even fairer than the American way, or is my gut right that something is off when the state treats being Catholic as a default setting on your payslip? I'd love an answer that takes the awkward in-between case seriously -- people like me who are neither devout nor committed atheists -- instead of just telling me it's a personal choice.
