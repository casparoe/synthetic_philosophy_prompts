so I'm writing up my stance on well-being for a reading group and I want a consistency audit before I embarrass myself. context is Nozick's experience machine and what follows from it. here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'd refuse to plug in even if the machine life were stipulated to be more pleasant than any real life available to me, and I don't think this is just status quo bias or me doubting the engineers. stipulate perfect reliability, perfect tailoring, whatever — the intuition survives.
2. Because of 1, I take the machine case to refute hedonism about well-being, full stop. how things feel from the inside is not all that matters to how well a life goes.
3. My positive view is desire satisfactionism: your life goes well in proportion to how many of your suitably informed desires get satisfied, and it makes no difference whether you ever find out they were satisfied. that's why I think the standard deceived-spouse case comes out right: she's worse off being cheated on even if she never suspects a thing.
4. But I don't think anything can be good or bad for you once you've ceased to exist. if my rivals trash my life's work the week after I die, that's unfortunate, maybe tragic, but it's not bad *for me*. the dead can't be harmed.
5. I also think some people would be right to plug in. someone in unrelievable misery with no real prospects isn't making a mistake about their own good if they choose the machine — I'd refuse, but that's me.
6. relatedly: caring whether your experiences are real is just one preference among others. someone who geniunely doesn't care whether they're in a simulation isn't irrational, and I don't want a theory that says they are.
7. none of this means pleasure doesn't matter — I think it's a huge part of most good lives, I just deny it's the whole story.

which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I want the tensions ranked by severity, each with the cheapest fix and what that fix costs me. don't just flag everything as a problem — I suspect at least one or two of these are only apparent tensions, and I'd like to know which. and if there's a single revision that would clean up the most, name it.
