Last night a friend and I argued for hours about the old puzzle of how purely instrumental music can be sad. My friend defended the view that a slow, descending melody literally resembles the voice and movement of a sad person, so we hear sadness in the music the way we see a face in a cloud. I maintained that music is sad because it tends to make us sad, and we project the emotion back onto it. We got nowhere, and my suspicion is that the wider debate is stalled for the same reason we were: the two sides may not even be answering the same question — one about what emotion music expresses, the other about what emotion it causes in listeners. Diagnose the disagreement rather than settle it. Is it substantive, verbal (perhaps about what 'sad' means when predicated of a sound), or a disguised empirical question about the psychology of musical response that armchair argument cannot resolve? Given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress — a conceptual distinction, a finding from music psychology, an agreed test case — and whether anything could actually end the dispute. Please answer in American English.
