I chair the human-subjects review board at a research hospital, and we must rule this week on a protocol that has split the board. The investigators are testing a memory-blocking sedative, and their information sheet for volunteers quotes the following passage from Adam Elga's "Self-Locating Belief and the Sleeping Beauty Problem" (Analysis, 2000), a setup their design deliberately replicates:

"Some researchers are going to put you to sleep. During the two days that your sleep will last, they will briefly wake you up either once or twice, depending on the toss of a fair coin (Heads: once; Tails: twice). After each waking, they will put you back to sleep with a drug that makes you forget that waking. When you are first awakened, to what degree ought you believe that the outcome of the coin toss is Heads?"

The disputed feature: at each waking, subjects may wager up to half their study payment on the coin outcome at odds the investigators fix in advance; the researchers describe these bets as a behavioral measure of confidence under amnesia. One board member argues the wagers are harmless because a fully informed subject learns nothing new on waking and should remain at one-half, in which case fair odds can be offered and no one is exploited. A second counters that a subject who reasons that way will predictably lose money twice in the tails branch while the corresponding winnings occur only once — so the design either profits from subjects' self-locating uncertainty or must take a stand on the very question Elga's passage poses — and she wants the consent form to tell subjects that the correct credence on waking is one-third. A third member objects that putting any answer in the consent form both contaminates the measurement and has the board endorsing one side of an unresolved dispute in epistemology.

Draft the board's decision memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. It should state exactly what the consent form may or must say about what a subject ought to believe on waking, whether the wagering component can be retained and on what terms, and what the quoted passage does and does not settle about these questions — giving the strongest version of the losing side's position before answering it.
