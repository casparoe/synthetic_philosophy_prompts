Afterward he published an account of what he claims he experienced while clinically dead: an intensely bright, painful red light which he somehow sensed was responsible for the government of the universe, two ministers in charge of space and time who had neglected their duties, and a river which he felt he had crossed and could cross back over. Crucially, he did not convert to anything. He stayed an atheist and a humanist, and said only that the episode had slightly weakened his conviction that his genuine death would be the end of him -- while adding, characteristically, that he continued to hope it would be.

Professor Ayer: in Language, Truth and Logic you held that a statement has factual meaning only if some possible experience could bear on whether it's true, and you spent your whole career denying that any experience could settle the question of survival after death -- whatever someone seemed to see on the other side would always admit of a naturalistic explanation, so the hypothesis stays untestable either way.
