I'm a senior in high school and I run our school's philosophy club. Next month we're holding a session on what instrumental music — a string quartet, a symphony, a piano étude, anything without lyrics — is about, if it is about anything at all, and I want to open the meeting with a sorting quiz that places each member into a position on that question. Before I describe the quiz, some context that should shape its design.

In the preface to The World as Will and Idea (Vol. 1), translated by R. B. Haldane and J. Kemp, Arthur Schopenhauer writes: "According as we consider the different aspects of this one thought which I am about to impart, it exhibits itself as that which we call metaphysics, that which we call ethics, and that which we call æsthetics; and certainly it must be all this if it is what I have already acknowledged I take it to be."

I chose this passage because of the puzzle it sets for our session. Schopenhauer's own account of music, developed later in the same work, is the clearest case of the single-thought structure he describes: on his view, music matters because, unlike the other arts, it does not copy the world of appearances or the ideal forms of things but directly presents the striving, tension, and release that he takes to be the inner nature of everything — which is why, he thinks, it can move us so deeply without depicting anything. That theory is not something you could adopt while rejecting the rest of his philosophy; it only makes sense as part of the package. So the passage gives us a live question: is he right that a view about an art form is inseparable from one's metaphysics and ethics, or can a theory of music stand alone? (Please paraphrase rather than quote his music chapter anywhere in your answer; the passage above is the only text I have verified word for word.)

With that in mind, here is what I need:

1. A decision tree, or equivalently a quiz with a scoring key, built from 8 to 10 questions. Every question must present a concrete musical situation with two or three answer options — for instance, being moved to tears by a piece with no words, a film score that makes a chase scene feel urgent, a Baroque fugue that seems to be about nothing beyond itself, a symphony's storm passage. No question may mention a philosopher, an "-ism," or ask flat-out what music "expresses"; the sorting must happen entirely through reactions to cases, so that members are classified by what they think rather than by which label sounds appealing.

2. The first branch of the tree should operationalize the passage: a question that separates members who feel a satisfying account of music must connect to their broader picture of what the world and human life are like from members who feel a theory of music can be assessed entirely on its own. Add one paragraph explaining how this branch interprets the passage and whether you think it is a fair operationalization of what Schopenhauer meant.

3. The leaves should include at least: Schopenhauer's own view, placed on the tree so that reaching it requires accepting that a theory of music carries metaphysical commitments, exactly as the passage suggests; a formalist position on which music has no subject matter beyond its own structure; the view that music expresses emotions the composer felt; the view that music arouses emotions in listeners without expressing anything; a representational or pictorial view; and a narrative view on which listening to music is like following a story. Give each leaf a name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

4. A scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves, with any pair of leaves your questions cannot reliably pull apart flagged as such rather than papered over — I suspect the expression and arousal views will be hard to separate, and I'd rather know that than pretend otherwise.

5. A brief note on combinations of answers that no real position occupies, with your diagnosis of each gap: is it incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored?

The quiz will be taken by teenagers who have read no philosophy, so keep the wording plain and the situations vivid. The whole instrument should fit on two printed pages.
