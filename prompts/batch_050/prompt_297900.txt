A friend and I argued late into the night over whether republican freedom — liberty as non-domination — is a genuinely third concept of freedom or merely negative liberty with extra conditions attached. I am an economist by training and most at home in the vocabulary of choice sets, feasibility constraints, and counterfactuals about what others would do if I acted otherwise. Restate each conception — negative liberty as absence of interference, positive liberty as effective self-mastery or realized capacity, republican liberty as independence from another's discretionary power, even power never exercised — in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then report what failed to survive the translation. Give your answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: one item per conception translated, one item on the claims that had to be distorted or cannot be expressed in the economist's vocabulary at all, and a final item giving your verdict on whether the residue exposes a gap in my framework or shows that the threefold distinction my friend defended is less substantive than it appears.
