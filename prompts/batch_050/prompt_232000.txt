I'm a journalist who covers AI and ideas, and I'm drafting a long essay on what it would mean for advanced AI systems to be "aligned." Before I commit to my framing, I'd like my position audited for internal consistency. Here is roughly where I've landed:

1. A system is aligned just in case it reliably tries to do what its operator intends — alignment is a matter of fidelity to intent, not of which intentions are being served.

2. Refusal behavior in today's chatbots (declining requests the user sincerely intends, on grounds set by the developer) counts as genuine alignment progress, not a bolt-on or a reputational patch.

3. The core danger from a misaligned AGI is that it would pursue goals its designers never intended; if we could guarantee intent fidelity at scale, the control problem would essentially be solved.

4. Reward hacking and deceptive mesa-optimization are the same basic failure: in both, the system ends up optimizing something other than what was intended.

5. Whether a system is aligned cannot depend on the moral quality of the operator's intentions — a well-built instrument is well-built whoever picks it up.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which pairs only look like they do? Please rank any genuine tensions by severity, say for each one which commitment would have to give and what the cheapest revision costs me, and identify the single change that would clean up the most. I don't have a preferred candidate to sacrifice, so don't pull punches.
