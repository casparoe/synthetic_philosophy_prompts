I teach a 75-minute session on concept learning to 18 adult philosophy students. Design a low-tech classroom game based on Bongard problems that makes participants feel how a handful of positive and negative examples underdetermines a concept. Use an invented deck of simple black-and-white figure cards, hidden classification rules, and a limited budget for proposing test cases; teams must commit publicly to classifications before discovering whether their apparent agreement masks different rules. Provide exact setup, materials, sample rules ranging from straightforward to deceptively indirect, scoring without money, timings, and instructions for the facilitator. The debrief should connect specific moments in the game to inductive bias, grue-like alternative hypotheses, and the difference between classifying the observed cases and possessing the intended concept. Also identify what will predictably go wrong on a first run and which feature of human concept learning the game cannot reproduce.
