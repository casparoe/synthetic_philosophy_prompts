You are a moral philosopher specializing in promises, obligations, and normative powers. Think carefully before answering, and begin with a brief conceptual map of the relevant distinctions before developing your analysis.

A seminar handout reduces the question of why promises bind to two competing accounts:

The Reliance Account: A promise binds because the promisor deliberately invites the promisee to expect performance and rearrange plans accordingly. Breaking the promise wrongs the promisee by frustrating those expectations and undermines the valuable social practice that makes coordination possible.

The Normative-Power Account: A promise binds because communicating an intention to undertake an obligation exercises a distinctive power to give the promisee a claim against the promisor. Expectations and reliance may explain why promises matter, but they do not create the obligation; the promisor’s voluntary undertaking does.

Compare these accounts as explanations of promissory obligation, not merely as descriptions of why people usually keep promises. In particular:

1. Explain what each account takes to be the source, object, and direction of the obligation. Who possesses the relevant claim, and who can waive or release it?
2. Test both accounts against the following cases: a promise kept secret so that the promisee never forms expectations; a promise the promisee initially doubts but later relies upon; an insincere promise that the promisee reasonably believes; a promise extracted through an illegitimate threat; a promise to help commit a minor wrong; a promise whose intended beneficiary dies before performance; and an attempted promise in a community whose members have no established practice of promising.
3. Distinguish carefully between doing something wrong and wronging the promisee, between having an obligation and having a reason, and between a promise being binding and its enforcement being justified.
4. Assess whether the Reliance Account can explain obligations that arise before reliance, and whether the Normative-Power Account covertly presupposes the value of the social practice it claims to make unnecessary.
5. Consider a hybrid view on which promising exercises a normative power, while expectations and social coordination explain why creatures like us possess that power. Determine whether this is a genuine synthesis or merely relabels the disagreement.

Conclude by identifying which account, or which version of the hybrid, better explains the cases as a whole. Do not provide citations or a bibliography; focus on conceptual analysis and clearly stated examples.
