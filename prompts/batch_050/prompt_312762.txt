I'm a journalist working on a column about whether history gets "locked in" or whether the best option wins in the end, and my editor's go-to example is VHS beating Betamax in the 1980s. The standard pop version goes: Sony's Betamax was technically superior, but through some early luck and network effects VHS pulled ahead, and than it snowballed -- video rental stores stocked VHS tapes, so consumers bought VHS players, so stores stocked more VHS, and Sony was done. I want to stress-test this as a case of genuine path dependence. Do the mapping for me: what exactly is the increasing-returns mechanism here, what would count as the "accident" that got amplified, and at what point (if ever) was the outcome locked in? Then tell me where the path dependence reading strains: Betamax tapes could only record about an hour at launch while VHS did two, which mattered a lot for recording movies off TV, and Sony kept its format closed while JVC licensed VHS to basically everyone. That sounds less like lock-in through historical accident and more like losing on the merits plus worse strategy. So: run the counterfactual -- would VHS have won anyway, accident or no? -- and then go the other direction and tell me what this episode shows about the theory itself. Is "lock-in" here doing real explanatory work, or is path dependence just a story we tell after the fact that no evidence could have refuted?
