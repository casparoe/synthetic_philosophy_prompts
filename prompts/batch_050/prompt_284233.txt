I'm on a panel at work about women in STEM next month and I've been writing down what I actually believe about this stuff so I don't contradict myself mid-discussion. Can you check whether these hang together?

(a) The so-called gender-equality paradox is basically right: countries that score highest on gender-equality measures tend to have *more* gendered patterns in occupational choice, not less, and I don't think it's a measurement artefact.
(b) In genuinely free and prosperous societies, people's choices express what they actually want — so the Nordic pattern is best read as revealed preference, with constraints stripped away.
(c) Preferences formed under socialisation can't be taken at face value as evidence about what people really want; you can't just read authenticity off choices that were shaped from birth.
(d) The gender pay gap is a real injustice that calls for policy intervention, not just something to shrug at.
(e) The state shouldn't be in the business of re-engineering people's values — nudging women out of caring professions they chose would be overreach.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and where's the cheapest revision? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and don't just tell me what a good liberal is supposed to say — I want to know which commitment I'd least regret dropping.
