I never studied math formally but I got hooked on the story of the four-color theorem and the Kepler conjecture and how people argue about whether computer-checked proofs really count as proofs. I've been chewing on a distinction I haven't seen anyone talk about (maybe it has a name and I just don't know it). It seems like there are actually three different worries lumped together: (1) the computer might have a bug, (2) no human could ever check the whole thing even in principle, and (3) a human could check it in principle but it would take longer than a lifetime so nobody ever will. My gut says these are really different situations. A cosmic ray flipping a bit feels like a practical annoyance, but case (3) seems like it changes what a proof even is, since the "anyone can verify it" property becomes sort of fictional.

So here's my question: if you had to pick which of these three worries actually deserves the most philosophical attention -- say you're advising someone who wants to write one solid piece on computer-assisted proof and can only dig into one -- which would you pick and why? And more broadly, what do you think are the live open questions in this area that aren't just rehashing "but is it really a proof?" I'd like a defended ordering, not just a list.
