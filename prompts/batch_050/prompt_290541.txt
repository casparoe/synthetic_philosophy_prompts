Exam question: Brandom's inferentialism is often described as reversing the traditional order of explanation that treats representation as primary and inference as derivative. Explain what this reversal amounts to, including the role Brandom assigns to material inference and to the practice of scorekeeping. Then assess whether an inferential role semantics can account for the representational dimension of language -- the fact that our claims are answerable to how things objectively are with particular objects.

Student's answer: Brandom reverses the standard order of explanation by holding that conceptual content is constituted by inferential role rather than by representational relations to the world. On the representationalist picture, we first explain what it is for a state to represent an object or fact and then explain inference as a truth-preserving transition between such states; Brandom instead starts with the practice of giving and asking for reasons, in which assertions are moves that undertake commitments, and treats representation as something to be explained in terms of that practice. Central to this is the notion of material inference: inferences that are good in virtue of their inductive reliability rather than their logical form, such as the inference from "it is raining" to "the streets will be wet," which is licensed by a defeasible statistical generalization. Grasping a concept, on this view, consists in knowing the rules that govern its correct application and its inferential connections. Scorekeeping is the practical activity in which speakers track one another's commitments and entitlements, attributing and undertaking commitments as conversation proceeds. Regarding objectivity, Brandom argues that the representational dimension emerges from the structure of scorekeeping itself: because scorekeepers can attribute commitments de re as well as de dicto, and because anaphoric chains link tokenings back to the objects they purport to be about, the distinction between what is said and what is actually the case is internal to the practice rather than imposed from outside. Since Brandom regards truth-conditional semantics as fundamentally mistaken and treats talk of truth as a dispensable façon de parler, the objectivity of content is secured entirely by these intra-practical norms rather than by any word-world relation.
