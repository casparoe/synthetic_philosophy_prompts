The topic I got assigned is impact measures / "low-impact" AI -- the whole idea of getting an agent to accomplish its task while minimizing side effects on the world, without having to fully specify human values. Things like attainable utility preservation, relative reachability, reversibility-based penalties, that cluster of ideas.

Here's what I want: one essay-style exam question that you can't answer just by regurgitating the standard summary of "penalize change, watch out for the agent turning everything off / offsetting its own actions." The crux I want students to hit is roughly this dilemma: either you measure impact relative to some inaction/default baseline (in which case, what counts as the default, and does the agent get penalized for stuff other agents and nature were going to do anyway -- which perversely incentivizes it to *stop* other people from doing things), or you penalize deviation from whatever would have happened, in which case the agent can just offset its own effects (keep the vase on the conveyor belt, un-do its changes) and "low impact" becomes meaningless. And then the deeper question of whether ANY definition of impact needs a choice of which state variables/descriptions matter, and whether that choice smuggles back in the partial value specification the whole approach was supposed to avoid. So the question should push students to construct the dilemma themselves and take a position on whether there's a way out (distinguishing effects "through" the agent's actions? coarse agreement on valuable features being enough?), not just describe it.
