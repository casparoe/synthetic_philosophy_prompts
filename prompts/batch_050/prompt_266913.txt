Here's roughly where I've landed on the nudge stuff and I want to know if it hangs together or if I'm fooling myself:

(1) The state shouldn't be in the business of changing what adults want. Respecting autonomy means taking people's preferences as given and just helping them act on them.

(2) Opt-out organ donation registration is a fantastic policy and way more countries should adopt it. The places that have switched have seen consent rates go from a minority to nearly everyone.

(3) Sin taxes on cigarettes are fine, but those soda-size-ban style proposals are objectionable. There's a real line between charging people for a choice and taking the choice off the menu.

(4) Mandatory calorie labels on menus are obviously fine, since it's just information.

(5) What actually bothers me about nudges is when they're covert. Choice architecture you don't know about is manipulation; if people can see what's being done to them it's fine.

So: is this a consistent package? I suspect (2) and (5) are doing something funny to each other since most people never find out what their default even is, and maybe (4) isn't as innocent as I think, but I'm not sure where the real problem is vs. where I'm just being paranoid. Tell me which commitment has to go if it comes to that, and rank the tensions by how bad they actually are. Under 600 words please, I don't want an essay.
