ok, consistency check request before I post my "philosophical profile" in a thread about thought experiments. Here's where I've landed on intuition pumps:

(1) Intuitions are evidence — defeasible, roughly like perceptual appearances, but you don't get to just ignore them.
(2) Trolley-style dilemmas are basically worthless as philosophy. The literature on framing and order effects (push vs. switch, which case you see first, etc.) shows these judgments track presentation, not moral structure, so trolleyology tells us about psychology, not ethics.
(3) I trust the intuition that I survive Parfit-style teletransportation more than I trust any abstract argument, and it's what actually grounds my reductionism about personal identity.
(4) A thought experiment only misleads when it smuggles in irrelevant details or connotations; a properly constructed one just isolates the feature you're trying to test.
(5) I treat the repugnant conclusion as a reductio of total utilitarianism, i.e., the intuitive verdict against it is load-bearing.
