So I spent 40 years in a chemistry lab and now in retirement I've been reading philosophy, most recently Frankfurt's little book on bullshit. His point as I understand it is that the bullshitter isn't trying to deceive you about the facts like a liar does, he just doesn't care whether what he says is true, only what it does for him. Here's what's been bugging me. When an LLM hallucinates, it clearly isn't lying, since lying requires knowing the truth and it doesn't know anything. But it also can't be bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, since it has no motives at all, nothing the speech is doing *for it*. So by elimination it's just error, same as when my old spectrometer drifted out of calibration. But then why does everyone, including plenty of philosophers I see, insist on calling it bullshit? If the taxonomy really forces hallucenation into the error box, is the whole debate about lying vs bullshit just people misapplying Frankfurt, or is there something about producing confident fluent text that changes which category fits? And if it really is just error, does that mean I should treat a hallucinating chatbot the way I treated a miscalibrated instrument -- fixable, trustworthy once recalibrated -- rather than the way I'd treat a bullshitting colleague, who I'd never trust again?
