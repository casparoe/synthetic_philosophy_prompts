in my corner of twitter people keep settling the "do LLMs have beliefs" debate by reaching for Dennett's thermostat. the move goes: Dennett said we happily adopt the intentional stance toward a thermostat -- we say it "wants" the room at 20 degrees and "believes" it's currently colder -- and nobody thinks that's a deep metaphysical commitment, it's just a useful predictive shorthand. and an LLM is obviously a way better candidate for that treatment than a thermostat, so attributing beliefs to it is cheap, unremarkable, and definitely not evidence of anything spooky going on inside. case closed, apparently.

can you audit this for me? give me the explicit mapping first: what plays the role of the thermostat's setpoint, its sensor, its single "belief", its single "desire", when we port the analogy over to a language model in conversation. then the places it breaks -- i have a few suspicions (the thermostat never models *me* back, its behavior is fully predictable from the design stance so the intentional stance is pure laziness, whereas with an LLM the design stance is available in principle but practically useless, and the LLM's "beliefs" seem to shift depending on what i say to it in a way that feels different from the thermostat's belief changing when the room warms up) but i want the full list and i want to know which of the disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion being drawn, versus which are just "well of course an LLM isn't literally a thermostat."

and then the verdict: does the "so belief-talk about LLMs is as innocent as belief-talk about thermostats" conclusion survive, or was the thermostat analogy smuggling in the conclusion by picking the most boring possible example of stance-adoption -- i.e. would the argument look different if they'd started from Dennett's treatment of, say, a chess-playing computer or a corporation instead of a thermostat? if the thermostat was the wrong anchor, what's the right one?
