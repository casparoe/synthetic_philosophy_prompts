Act as an expert in philosophy of mind and consciousness science who's also built these Buzzfeed-style-but-rigorous quizzes before.

The set-up: after commissurotomy, you get the classic results -- the patient names objects shown to the right visual field but not the left, the left hand picks things up for reasons the patient can't report and then confabulates, the two hands occasionally work at cross purposes, and there's all the cross-cueing cleverness where one hemisphere tips off the other via head movements or tongue taps. Everyone in my group has a strong take on how many subjects there are in that skull, and I want to sort them by their verdicts on cases rather than by which label they've latched onto.

Brainstorm a bit first (privately, not in the final output) about what the actual logical space of positions is, then write me the quiz. Requirements:

- Nine to eleven questions, each a concrete scenario or verdict with two to four answer options. Use at least these: whether it's wrong not to anaesthetise the mute right hemisphere during an operation the patient consented to; what to say when the left hand does something the patient sincerely disavows; whether the right hemisphere has the kind of perspective you could in principle adopt or trade places with; and what to make of the fact that outside the lab these patients seem largely unified and functional.

- Build it from the logical space first: show me the questions-as-dimensions, the combinations of answers, and then locate the named positions in that grid. Explicitly flag any unoccupied combinations and diagnose briefly whether they're empty because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored.
- If two positions on my list can't be separated by any case-based question, say so honestly and tell me what would separate them instead (maybe nothing behavioural can?).
- No question should mention position names or authors -- it has to sort a first-year and the neurologist equally well.

Write the whole thing in American English (our web host is American and it drives me mad, but there we are). Casual tone for the quiz text itself is fine, but the scoring key and the logical-space bit should be tight.
