I am a novelist drafting a book in which a character's brain is surgically removed, kept alive in a vat, and continues to control his former body by radio link; in later chapters the body is destroyed while the brain lives on, a second body is built for it, and for one disastrous week the link is split so that the brain steers both bodies at once. I am loosely working from Dennett's brain-transplant thought experiment in "Where Am I?", but my plot keeps outrunning my grip on the underlying philosophy. I want each of my characters (the narrator, his wife, his lawyer, the surgeon) to hold a different, internally consistent answer to where he is and whether he survives each stage. Please write me a decision tree I can use as a casting instrument for these views: start from the shared premise that the remote-controlled body moves and speaks fluently, make each branch node a concrete question about one of my plot events (where is he during the radio-link chapter; does he survive the body's destruction; what happens to him during the two-bodies week; does the seat of a person follow the brain, the point of perception, the locus of control, or the whole organism), and make each leaf a named position (brain criterion, animalism, psychological continuity with and without a no-branching clause, a scattered-object view, and Dennett's own deflationary answer about points of view). Phrase each node so a character could answer it without knowing which label it leads to, note any combination of answers that no actual position occupies and why (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored), and flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot keep apart -- I would rather know that now than discover in chapter twelve that my lawyer and my surgeon secretly agree.
