Right, I want a referee for a long-running pub argument. A friend of mine (biology postdoc, endlessly patient with me) and I got onto the what-is-life question after I brought up the NASA working definition at a dinner party, and since then she's given me her verdicts on a string of borderline cases. For the record: bog-standard E. coli in a dish, alive, obviously. A flu virus, not alive. A bacterial spore that could sit dormant in salt for centuries, alive, because all the machinery is still in there. A mitochondrion plucked out of its cell and kept ticking over in a dish, not alive, just a component. A mule, alive, sterility is neither here nor there. A candle flame, not alive, even though it takes in fuel, grows, maintains its shape, and can send sparks off to start daughter flames. A prion, not alive, even when I pointed out that prion strains adapt under selection. A sterile worker ant, alive. And a sterile hybrid plant that only ever propogates because gardeners take cuttings, alive, though she admitted that one took a second.

Now the new cases I want to put to her. First, the artificial life setup I run on my laptop: self-replicating programs in a simulated world that mutate, compete for CPU time, and evolve tricks nobody coded in. Second, a hypothetical bit of lab chemistry: a droplet system with a membrane that takes in fuel, repairs itself, sits far from equilibrium, but has no genome, no offspring, no lineage whatsoever, it was built once and just keeps going. Third, a human embryo vitrified in perfect suspension, metabolism not slowed but at zero, indefinitely.

What I'd like: predict her verdict on all three, but more importantly reverse-engineer her. Give me at least two different rules that each fit her entire track record so far, show me where they part ways on the new cases, and tell me which one she's most likely actually running and why. One thing that trips me up: whatever patch excludes the flu virus (the copying is done by the host's machinery rather than the virus) seems like it might exclude my digital replicators too, since the CPU does the actual copying work, or maybe it excludes everything, since ribosomes do the work of copying DNA. So part of the job is telling me how that clause should be cashed out. Also tell me which of her existing verdicts is doing the most work in pinning the rule down, and if the two candidate rules genuinely diverge, what's the one further case you'd put to her to settle it. Part of me suspects the honest answer is that life is a cluster concept and there's simply no fact of the matter here, but she won't wear that, she gives crisp verdicts every time.
