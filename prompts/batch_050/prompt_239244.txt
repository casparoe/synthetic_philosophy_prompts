We're doing Dennett's "Quining Qualia" next week, and I want the handout to be a decision tree that sorts the positions on what his famous coffee-taster case actually shows. Quick recap so we're on the same page: Chase and Sanborn are professional coffee tasters who've both come to dislike the coffee. Chase says the coffee tastes exactly the way it always did, he just can't stand that taste anymore. Sanborn says no, the coffee genuinely tastes different to him now, his palate has drifted. Dennett's point is that nothing, not even perfect neuroscience plus perfect introspection, could settle which of them is right, and he thinks that deflates qualia talk generally. His stated strategy is: "I want to shift the burden of proof, so that anyone who wants to appeal to private, subjective properties has to prove first that in so doing they are not making a mistake."

Here's the thing I want the tree to turn on: that burden-shift is itself a move people can resist, and I suspect where you end up on qualia mostly tracks whether you accept it. So make the very first split whether Dennett gets to declare qualia-talk guilty until proven innocent, or whether that demand smuggles in a verificationism about meaning that a realist should just reject. From there, build the tree down to maybe 8-10 leaves: there should be room for the flat-footed qualia realist who insists there's a fact of the matter about Chase vs. Sanborn, the person who redefines qualia functionally so the question dissolves, the eliminativist, the illusionist who says we only seem to have the seemings, and whatever else the space genuinely supports.

Rules for the tree: every branch gets labeled with the claim or case that splits it, phrased so someone can answer without knowing any of the jargon (ask about the coffee case, inverted spectra, whether two people could function identically while experiencing differently, etc. -- never "do you believe in intrinsic properties?"). Every leaf gets a position name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. If some combination of answers is unoccupied, note it and say whether it's incoherent, just unpopular, or unexplored. And flag any pair of positions your tree can't actually separate, rather than pretending the questions do more work than they do.
