I'm a journalist who covers science and big ideas, and I'm working on a long piece about whether Darwin-style thinking has overreached itself -- you know, the whole cranes-not-skyhooks picture, where mindless bottom-up processes (selection, gradual accumulation, trial and error) are supposed to explain everything that looks designed, with no miraculous top-down "skyhooks" allowed. At a conference last month I interviewed a philosopher who gave me an argument she called the crane-collapse argument, and it's been bugging me ever since because the premises all sound like the standard sensible picture and the conclusion is nuts. Roughly as she laid it out: (1) Anything complex that appears designed is either the product of an intelligent designer or the product of a mindless, gradual crane process like natural selection -- there is no third option, because skyhooks don't exist. (2) Invoking a designer doesn't actually terminate the explanation, since the designer's own intelligence is itself complex and apparently designed, so the same either/or applies to it in turn; and to avoid an infinite regress of unexplained explainers, every explanatory chain has to bottom out somewhere in mindless processes. (3) So everything that appears designed -- organisms, tools, proofs, poems, scientific theories -- is at bottom the product of mindless processes. (4) A belief produced by mindless processes is at most accidentally true when it's true; genuine justification requires that beliefs be formed by mechanisms responsive to reasons, not merely shaped by whatever happened to replicate or spread. (5) So our mathematical and scientific beliefs, being products of mindless neural selection and memetic competition rather than skyhooks, are never justified in the way we take them to be. (6) But the reasoning in steps (1) through (5) is itself one of those beliefs, so either this argument gives us no reason to believe its conclusion, or its conclusion is false.

Now, I definately don't want to give up (1) through (3), because as far as I can tell that IS the crane insight and it's the whole reason the piece exists. And (6) strikes me as a reductio of something, not a result anyone should accept -- if the anti-skyhook picture really committed us to global skepticism about our own reasoning, including the reasoning that got us the picture, that would be a self-own of historic proportions and somebody would have noticed. So presumably something in the middle breaks. Is it (4)? That premise sounded plausible when she said it fast, but it seems to trade on something -- like, the fact that a process is "mindless at bottom" doesn't obviously tell you what the things it builds are like, since selection famously builds organisms with minds that track reasons. But then does that rescue justification, or does it just relocate the problem, since the reason-tracking itself was shaped by selection for survival and not for truth? And is (2) doing more work than it looks like -- does "bottoming out in mindless processes" mean every link in the chain is mindless, or only the first one? Walk me through where exactly this goes wrong, what each candidate repair costs (including for the original point about organisms and artifacts, which I need intact), and whether there's a version of the argument that survives as a genuine problem for some narrower thesis -- say, about moral beliefs rather than mathematical ones -- even if the general version collapses.
