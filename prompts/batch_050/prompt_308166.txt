After a career in experimental particle physics, I have spent my retirement reading philosophy of physics, and I have tried to set down, as precisely as I can, my present commitments on the ontological status of the wavefunction. Before I build an essay around them, I would like the set examined for internal consistency.

(1) Largely on the strength of the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem, which, assuming that separately prepared systems possess independent physical states, rules out models in which the quantum state represents only an observer's knowledge, I adopt a psi-ontic view: the wavefunction is an objective physical state of the system itself.

(2) I nevertheless cannot bring myself to regard a mathematical object living on a 3N-dimensional configuration space as a literal physical field; what is physically real, I am inclined to think, resides in ordinary three-dimensional space, and configuration space is a calculational device.

(3) I take the collapse of the wavefunction to be a real physical process, along the lines of spontaneous-collapse models of the Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber type: definite outcomes have to come from somewhere, and the unitary dynamics alone does not supply them.

(4) I accept that decoherence, the suppression of interference through entanglement with the environment, explains the classical appearance of the macroscopic world, though not by itself the uniqueness of the outcome we observe.

(5) I regard Lorentz invariance at the fundamental level as among the most securely established features of modern physics.

(6) Bell's theorem has convinced me that nature is nonlocal, and I am untroubled by this, provided that no controllable superluminal signaling is permitted.

Please answer with exactly five numbered items, each of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: order any genuine tensions you find from most to least severe, stating for each which commitments are at odds and what the least costly revision would be, and if the genuine tensions number fewer than five, devote the remaining items to pairs of commitments that look inconsistent but in fact hold together.
