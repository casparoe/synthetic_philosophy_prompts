Here was the exam question: "In 'Freedom and Resentment,' Strawson distinguishes participant reactive attitudes from the objective attitude. Explain this distinction, including the difference between excusing and exempting conditions, and then reconstruct Strawson's reply to the incompatibilist (the 'pessimist') who argues that if determinism were true, our practices of holding people responsible would be unjustifiable. Would Strawson's reply succeed in vindicating blame as we ordinarily practice it?"

And here's the student's answer:

"Strawson distinguishes between two broad kinds of stance we take toward people. The participant stance involves the reactive attitudes -- emotional responses like gratitude, resentment, and forgiveness that arise when we engage with others as fellow members of a moral community. Resentment, for instance, is what I feel when I witness someone wronging a stranger, while indignation is the more personal version I feel when the wrong is done to me. The objective attitude, by contrast, treats a person as something to be managed, treated, or worked around -- the way we regard someone when we suspend ordinary interpersonal engagement.

Strawson notes that we modify or withdraw reactive attitudes in two kinds of cases. Excusing conditions (he was pushed, he didn't know, he couldn't help it) show that the agent's will wasn't ill in this particular instance, while leaving our general participant stance toward them intact. Exempting conditions (he's a small child, he's schizophrenic) invite us to see the person as someone toward whom the objective attitude is appropriate more globally.

Against the pessimist, Strawson argues that the truth of determinism would not give us reason to abandon the reactive attitudes. His argument is essentially psychological: these attitudes are so deeply rooted in human nature that we simply could not give them up even if we wanted to, so the pessimist's demand is idle. Since we cannot help blaming, blame is justified. Strawson's own view is that we should therefore extend the participant stance even to those we currently exempt, since determinism excuses everyone equally. Overall his reply vindicates blame as we ordinarily practice it, because it shows that ordinary blame requires no metaphysical foundation at all."
