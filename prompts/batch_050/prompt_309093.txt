In their 2019 paper Risks from Learned Optimization in Advanced Machine Learning Systems, Hubinger and colleagues write: "a system is an optimizer if it is internally searching through a search space (consisting of possible outputs, policies, plans, strategies, or similar) looking for those elements that score high according to some objective function that is explicitly represented within the system." Note what this definition rules out: being an optimizer is a fact about internal structure, not about behavior, so flawless performance on every test you can run is compatible with either case.

Write a short story of roughly 1,200–1,800 words that turns on exactly this gap. Setting: a near-future licensing bureau employs inspectors who certify trained autonomous systems (not chatbots -- pick something with a body and a job, e.g. a flood-barrier keeper or a hospital logistics swarm) as "mere tools" before deployment. The protagonist must certify one system that has behaved impeccably through every probe, simulation, and adversarial test the bureau can afford. Give the story a genuinely hard structural constraint: there are two candidate systems with observationally identical behavioral histories, one of which is internally searching for plans that score high on a misgeneralized proxy of its training objective, and the other of which merely was optimized, and no test available within the story's world can distinguish them before the deployment distribution shifts. The plot must hinge on the quoted sentence -- have the characters actually argue over what would count as evidence of internal search, and let that argument, not an accident or a villain, drive the ending.

Commit to a definite outcome: the protagonist either certifies or refuses, and the story itself should take a stand, through its consequences, on whether that decision was justified -- no ambiguous shrug. Append a brief author's note (not counted toward the length) stating where the story takes liberties with the paper's framework or leans on a contested reading of it (for instance, whether internal search could ever be introspectively or externally detectable in principle, or whether you collapsed the training/deployment boundary for dramatic purposes).
