Since Skolem’s paradox apparently shows that a countable model of ZFC can contain a set it proves is uncountable, doesn’t Cantor’s theorem lose its intended force? If the model has only countably many elements available from the outside, then its “real numbers” can be enumerated, so why isn’t this a refutation of the claim that there are more real numbers than natural numbers? Should we conclude that first-order ZFC cannot express uncountability at all, or is there a principled way to reconcile the model’s internal judgment with the external enumeration?
