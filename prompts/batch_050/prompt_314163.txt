I run the discussion notes for a small philosophy of physics reading group, and next month it is my turn to circulate a position statement on Zeno's paradoxes of motion for the others to attack. Rather than defend a single view, I want to check whether the handful of convictions I have accumulated actually sit well together. One anchor for the statement is Aristotle's rejoinder to the Dichotomy in Book VI of the Physics, where (to paraphrase rather than quote) he says that Zeno's argument rests on a false assumption: it supposes that what is moving cannot pass over infinitely many things in a finite time, but length and time are each called infinite in two ways — in respect of divisibility and in respect of extent — and while a finite time cannot cover what is infinite in extent, it can cover what is infinite in divisibility, because the time is itself infinitely divisible in just the same respect as the distance. My first commitment is that this reply is essentially correct as far as the Dichotomy goes, and that nothing in the modern treatment of convergent series really goes beyond it.

The rest of my commitments: (2) completed infinities are perfectly legitimate — I accept the standard Cantorian picture on which the continuum is an actual infinity of points, and I take it that the walker genuinely does pass over infinitely many of them on the way to the door; (3) supertasks are impossible in principle, on the grounds that nothing can carry out infinitely many distinct operations in a finite time, and I take Thomson's lamp to show that the very idea is incoherent, since there is no determinate fact about the lamp's state at the end; (4) the Arrow is fully dissolved by the definition of instantaneous velocity as a limit, with no need for any further impulse or intrinsic state of motion at an instant; (5) space is genuinely infinitely divisible, with no smallest distance, whatever speculative physics might eventually say.

What I want from you is an audit, not an exposition of the paradoxes: tell me which of these five commitments genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to, and where the appearance comes from. Rank any real tensions by severity, and for each say precisely which commitment would have to give and what giving it up would cost the overall position. If one revision would restore consistency at the lowest total cost, identify it. Please keep the whole reply to no more than 450 words.
