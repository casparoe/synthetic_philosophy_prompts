My flatmate and I have been bickering all week about a thought experiment I half-remember from Hofstadter: the careenium, a machine full of tiny marbles called simms that crash into each other at crazy speed, and whose collisions clump together into big slow-moving "simmballs" — and the trick is that the simmballs ARE the machine's symbols, so at one level of description it's just physics, marbles shoving marbles, while at another level the symbols are clearly doing the thinking and shoving things around by virtue of what they mean. My flatmate insists the simmballs "don't really push anything" because only the simms have causal powers, and I think that's a level-confusion, and we agreed to settle it by whoever could make the other laugh hardest. So I need you to cheat on my behalf.

Write me a satirical open letter, 500-600 words, addressed to the Department of Fundamental Simm Dynamics (the smug reductionists of this world), written by an outraged simmball — give it a pompous name — who is fed up with being told it "does no causal work" and is "merely a façon de parler we use for bookkeeping". Deadpan throughout, no winking. You're a satirical columnist with a proper background in philosophy of mind, so brainstorm a couple of angles before you start and then pick the one where the jokes do the most philosophical work. The humour has to come from getting the actual issue right: levels of description, whether downward causation is real or just a handy way of talking, the awkward fact that every furious sentence of the letter is itself nothing but simms colliding, and the epiphenomenalism worry lurking under all of it.

Two constraints that matter. First, internal consistency: the simmball can be indignant but it can't cheat — no claiming spooky extra forces the setup forbids, and if it starts smuggling in magic causation, that slip itself has to be part of the joke rather than an accident. Second, the best jokes should cut both ways, so that a reader finishes the letter genuinely unsure whether symbol-level causation has been vindicated or beautifully undermined, and ideally a careful reader can tell which side you secretly take from the final line without you ever stating it. Avoid just listing philosophy buzzwords; the simmball should have a life, grievances, maybe a rival simmball it keeps getting bumped into.
