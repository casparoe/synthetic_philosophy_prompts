For a graduate seminar on pragmatics, I have to submit a short statement of my working commitments about Grice's account in "Logic and Conversation" so the group can audit them before we meet. I am confident about each of these individually, but I suspect the set does not hang together, and I would like to know where before I defend it.

One thing to keep in view while assessing the set: Grice's official test for conversational implicature is not merely that a hearer could infer the further content, but that the inference must in principle be reconstructible as an argument running from what was said, plus the Cooperative Principle and its maxims, plus background knowledge. On his view, an intuitive grasp of what the speaker meant that cannot be backed by such an argument does not qualify as a conversational implicature at all; it would have to be conventional.

My commitments:

1. What a speaker says is exhausted by the conventional meaning of the words uttered, plus whatever contextual fixing of reference and resolution of ambiguity is needed to arrive at a truth-evaluable proposition. Everything else the speaker conveys is implicature.
2. Something counts as conversationally implicated on an occasion only if the hearer actually works it out at the time; a hearer who takes the utterance at face value has missed nothing that was, strictly speaking, implicated.
3. Conversational implicature is essentially occasion-sensitive: the very same sentence, in different settings, implicates different things.
4. Cancellability is the decisive test for implicature status: if a piece of conveyed content can be explicitly cancelled without contradiction or self-stultification, it is no part of what was said.
5. Case verdict: a speaker who says "Some of the students passed" in a context where she knows all of them passed has said something false, and not merely something misleading.
6. Generalized conversational implicatures are carried by default, without any special features of the conversational setting being needed to generate them.

Please audit the set rather than lecture me on Grice. Identify which pairs of commitments genuinely conflict, which pairs only appear to, and how each genuine tension relates to Grice's reconstructibility requirement above. Then propose, for each genuine tension, the cheapest revision that restores consistency, and name the single revision that cleans up the most. The asker should not be able to tell from my list which commitment I would give up, so treat all six as equally up for revision.
