ok so i'm writing up my position on personal identity for a reading group and i want a consistency audit before i let anyone else poke at it. here's where i've landed:

(1) numerical identity is not what matters in survival; what matters is Relation R — psychological continuity and connectedness — with the right kind of cause.

(2) any reliable cause counts as the right kind. so routine teletransportation (scan, destroy, rebuild on mars) preserves what matters fully, and i'd step in without a second thought. but a freak one-off reconstruction, like a lightning bolt happening to assemble a copy of me in a swamp, wouldn't count, because the cause isn't reliable.

(3) whether i have special reason for concern about some future person should depend only on the relations between me and that person, not on extrinsic facts like whether other copies of me exist or what machines were doing elsewhere. that's exactly why fission doesn't make things worse for me even though identity fails: two successors, twice what matters.

(4) since identity isn't what matters, whoever steps out of the teletransporter after me inherits my obligations — debts, promises, the marriage. there's no further fact about identity that could block that.

(5) in the combined spectrum, where my cells and psychology are gradually swapped for replicas of someone else's, there has to be a sharp cutoff where i stop surviving. at every point either i survive or i don't, and vagueness about my own existence is intolerable.

which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and what's the cheapest revision? rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give. then finish with \answer{...} containing just the number of the one commitment i should revise first.
