I'm a philosophy PhD student and next week I'm presenting at our department's political theory workshop, defending prioritarianism as a complete theory of distributive justice rather than just a principle for a benevolent planner. My main move against the leveling-down objection is going to be that it equivocates between "better in one respect" meaning better for someone and meaning better simpliciter, and that once you seperate those two readings the intuition that leveling down is in any way good falls apart.

The audience is mostly Rawlsians, two or three luck egalitarians who take the leveling-down objection to be decisive against any view that denies equality has intrinsic value, and one total utilitarian who thinks prioritarianism is an unmotivated fudge between maximization and equality.

Write my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd specifically (not the generic seminar version), ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would actually put it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Where my reply has to change depending on whether the egalitarian is running a telic, deontic, or pluralist version of the objection, say so and give both versions. My known soft spot is inequalities that don't register in anyone's welfare -- status, recognition, political standing -- where my view seems to have nothing to say: tell me whether to defend that silence or concede and restrict the view's scope, and what each costs me. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
