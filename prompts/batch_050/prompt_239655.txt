i want a sestina for the last meeting of my feminist philosophy reading group, where we've spent the semester on beauvoir and are ending on her claim that one is not born but becomes a woman. the thing i want the FORM to carry: a sestina takes six end words and makes them come back six times in a fixed permutation that the poet doesn't choose after the first stanza is set — which is more or less her picture of how the vocabulary that turns someone into a woman works. so: the six end words should be six mundane gendered labels or injunctions that get handed to girls early (stuff like sweet, careful, pretty, quiet, helpful, lady — pick better ones if you can, but keep them everyday, no slurs, nothing dramatic), and each time a word recurs it should mean something a bit different because of everything that's accumulated around it in the intervening stanzas. same word, different weight — that's the becoming. somewhere in the middle stanzas the poem should register, without naming it, her distinction between the body you happen to have and the situation built up around it, because that's the load-bearing part of the argument. the final full stanza is where the speaker stops just receiving the words and starts doing the rearranging herself, and the envoi should put all six words in an order the sestina's own rules never permitted — landing her actual point, which is that becoming was never about escaping the six words, it's that they were materials all along and never a destiny. dont make it a greeting card or a rally speech. someone whos actually read her should be able to tell you understood the claim rather than just the slogan.
