I'm TAing a metaphysics seminar and next week we hit the Special Composition Question: under what conditions do some things compose a further thing? Instead of a balanced survey I want to hand out a tier list of the main answers, ranked by how well each survives the pressure points people actually press. The candidates: (1) nihilism — nothing ever composes anything, there are only simples arranged tablewise; (2) universalism — any plurality composes something, my left shoe plus the moon included; (3) organicism — composition happens exactly when the parts' activity amounts to a life (van Inwagen's own answer); (4) brutalism — composition happens in some cases and not others but there is no informative criterion, its just a brute pattern; (5) deflationism — the whole dispute is a pseudo-question, the sides mean different things by "there is an object" and every answer comes out true in some equally good language. The pressure points: (a) vague composition seems to entail vague existence, which lots of people think is impossible, so fuzzy criteria are in trouble but a sharp cutoff looks arbitrary; (b) the possibility of gunk, matter divisible forever with no simples at the bottom; (c) the fuzzy line between life and non-life (viruses, a cell mid-death) threatening to infect existence itself on (3); (d) Moorean pressure — there are tables, come on. Give me the tier list with a sentence or two of justification per entry, say which single pressure point does the most damage and to whom, and tell me whether (5) even belongs on this list or is playing a different game entirely — and if so, how I should present it instead.
