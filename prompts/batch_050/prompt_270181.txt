An encyclopedia article on mereology glosses Peter van Inwagen's special composition question, and his answer to it, as follows:

"The special composition question asks: what conditions must some objects satisfy in order for there to exist a further object that they compose? Van Inwagen argues that any precise, principled answer would be arbitrary, and so concludes that when composition occurs is largely a matter of how we conventionally describe the world. He adopts the convention that simples compose something just when their activity constitutes a life. It follows that, strictly speaking, there are no tables or chairs -- there are only simples arranged tablewise or chairwise -- though ordinary sentences like 'there are two chairs in the kitchen' still come out true on his view, because the simples arranged chairwise do all the work chairs were supposed to do. He presents this as the only answer that avoids both nihilism (nothing ever composes anything) and universalism (any things compose something)."
