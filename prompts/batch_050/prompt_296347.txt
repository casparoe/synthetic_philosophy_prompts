So I did jury duty earlier this year (assault case, won't go into details) and something has been bugging me ever since. When the police did the lineup, they had the witness say right then and there how sure she was, in her own words, and they wrote it down. She said she was pretty sure but not positive, like 70 or 80 percent. My cousin's a cop and he says that's standard now, they always record the confidence at the moment of the ID because it supposedly matters a lot later. But then at trial the whole thing felt completely different. The lawyers kept pushing us to either believe her or not believe her, and the judge's instructions were all about deciding whether her testimony was credible, full stop. Nobody ever told us what to actually do with the 70 percent.

What I keep wondering is what the first practice assumes about how evidence even works. It seems like it only makes sense if you think that learning something from an experience doesn't have to mean becoming certain of anything -- the witness saw the guy under a streetlamp at midnight, and the honest output of that seeing is a "probably," not a yes. And if that's right, the police form seems to be assuming there's some rule for how the rest of us should take that in, something like: shift your belief by an amount that weights how sure she was, instead of going all the way or not at all. Is that a coherent picture of what learning is? And does the courtroom practice assume the opposite -- that testimony is the kind of thing you accept or reject, and that being unsure is a flaw in the witness rather than information in its own right?

If the two practices really do presuppose different theories of evidence, which one is the law actually committed to, deep down? And what would trials have to look like if we took the sliding-scale view seriously all the way through -- would jurors get asked to put numbers on how much they believe each witness, and would that wreck something like beyond a reasonable doubt? Seperate question: is the confidence-recording practice maybe compatible with a few different views and it just sits most naturally with one of them? I'd like you to sort out what's genuinely assumed versus what's merely a nice fit.
