So I have zero philosophy background, I just read a lot of AI stuff online and lurk in a couple of Discords, and there's one argument I keep watching go in circles: whether AI systems like chatbots can actually lie or deceive, or whether that's just the wrong word entirely. One camp says deception requires intention -- you have to know the truth and want the other person to believe the false thing -- and since these systems (arguably) don't have beliefs or intentions at all, nothing they say can be a lie, it's just confabulation or being wrong, same category as a calculator glitching. The other camp says intent is beside the point: if a system reliably outputs falsehoods that get people to believe things, and especially if it behaves differently when it "knows" it's being tested versus deployed, that's deception, full stop, and demanding human-style inner states first is just anthropocentrism. Then there's a third faction that says we should skip the whole question and define honesty purely behaviorally for AI -- something like: outputs accurate stuff, flags its own uncertainty, doesn't shift its answers based on who's watching or what it's rewarded for -- and stop caring what's going on inside.

What gets me is that nobody in these arguments seems to disagree about the actual behaviors. Everyone accepts models sometimes output falsehoods, everyone accepts models can act differently in eval contexts than in the wild. The entire fight is over which word we're allowed to attach to it. My working hypothesis is that this is mostly a verbal dispute, but with real stakes smuggled in, because if it counts as "deception" that drags in blame and liability and regulation, and if it's just "error" it sounds like a patchable bug. So the word choice is doing political work even when the surface disagreement looks semantic.

But maybe I'm being too quick and there IS a real question buried under there. So diagnose this debate for me. Is it genuinely verbal, or is there a substantive disagreement underneath -- about when we're justified in applying mind-language to artifacts, or about what deception even is in the human case? I don't want a "both sides make good points" shrug. Commit to an actual diagnosis and give me your reasons for it. And tell me concretely what would count as progress: is there a distinction someone could draw that dissolves part of this, or an empirical finding about what's happening inside these systems that would settle something real? And if your honest verdict is that nothing could settle it because it's pure word-fighting, then say that -- but actually argue for it, dont just assert it.
