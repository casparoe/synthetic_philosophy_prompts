I'm preparing a handout for a philosophy-of-mind reading group that is working through the typogenetics material in Gödel, Escher, Bach and the surrounding "strange loop" discussion of self-replication, and I want a diagnostic instrument rather than another summary. Write a decision tree that takes a reader from a single shared starting commitment -- "a sufficiently rich formal system can, at least in principle, contain descriptions of itself and operate on those descriptions" -- to one of five named positions on how deep the analogy between DNA self-replication and Gödelian self-reference actually runs:

1. The strong isomorphism: the mapping between the DNA/ribosome machinery and the string/proof machinery of formal systems is a genuine structural correspondence, so cellular self-reproduction and the Gödel sentence's self-reference are two instances of one abstract scheme.
2. The heuristic analogy: the parallel is real at the level of mechanism, but the formal system is a model we impose on the chemistry, so the isomorphism licenses pedagogy rather than metaphysics.
3. The disanalogy of causation: Gödelian self-reference is achieved by syntactic arithmetization, DNA self-replication by physical machinery, and conflating code-hood with causal efficacy makes the isomorphism misleading rather than deep.
4. The selective-correspondence view: some parts of the mapping survive scrutiny (code duality, where a string is both instruction and operand) while others do not (no biological analogue of the diagonal construction, or of consistency), so the honest position is a partial isomorphism with an explicit list of failures.
5. The eliminativist shrug: self-reference talk in biology is wholly metaphorical, and typogenetics is a toy notation rather than evidence about how actual self-reproduction works.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the labels. Use five to seven branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete question a reader could answer without knowing which position it feeds -- for instance, questions about whether code duality requires an external interpreter to count as self-reference, whether a mapping devised by an outside observer can do the relevant work, or whether typogenetics' deliberate simplifications (a fixed catalog of enzyme operations, no mutation, no metabolism) undercut the analogy or are the analogy's entire point. Each leaf should name its position and give one sentence stating its strongest argument. Flag explicitly any pair of positions your tree cannot cleanly separate, and identify at least one coherent combination of answers that none of the five positions occupies, with a sentence diagnosing whether that slot is empty because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Begin the response with the tree itself, with no preface or commentary.
