I am a journalist covering science and ideas, currently reporting on a dispute over whether a mass atrocity should be officially named genocide. At a memorial event, the keynote speaker invoked the slogan "never again," and in the days since I have heard it used for strikingly different purposes: to argue for immediate military intervention, to argue that the label itself carries binding legal consequences, and to insist that the phrase commits us to nothing more than continued vigilance. For a piece unpacking how this phrase does argumentative work, I need a rigorous disambiguation: what are the distinct, precise claims that "never again" could be expressing -- a moral vow, a prediction about deterrence, a legal duty of prevention, a statement about collective memory, a warning to would-be perpetrators -- and for each, which are near-platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false or empty? For each reading, tell me whether it would actually support the specific use I saw it put to, and which reading the speakers most plausibly meant. Lay the readings out as a menu I can work from rather than settling on one interpretation.
