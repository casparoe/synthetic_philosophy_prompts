My officemate and I have been arguing all semester about the units of selection debate, and I've started keeping score of her calls because she swears she's not some gene's-eye-view zealot, but im not so sure. Her track record so far: meiotic drive genes (segregation distorters) — obviously selection acting at the gene level, she says, the organism is just the arena it happens in. Camouflage evolving in moths — individual level, no question. Army ant colonies outcompeting neighboring colonies — there the colony is the unit, she calls it a superorganism and gets visibly annoyed if you try to "reduce" it. But then sterile worker ants, the classic case, she says is gene-level all the way down, Hamilton settled it, and all the colony-level talk is just bookkeeping. And cancer she describes as selection gone rogue below the level of the individual — cheater cells, same basic logic as the drive genes.

So here's what I actually want. First: what rule is she running? Because I can think of at least three different principles that generate exactly those five verdicts — one that tracks where the fitness conflicts are, one that tracks bottlenecks and policing and what counts as a genuine "individual," and one that's basically pluralism (the unit is whatever level the best causal explanation of the trait cites) — and they all look identical on her record so far. Spell out the candidates properly, don't just gesture. Second: the case we fought about last night is the holobiont, an organism plus its microbiome (we were talking about the bobtail squid and its glowing vibrio, but take the human gut if you'd rather). Will she call the holobiont a unit of selection or not? Give me your predicted verdict, which candidate rules deliver that verdict and which deliver the opposite, and which rule you'd actually bet on her running, with reasons. Third: name the one further case, real or invented, that would most cleanly split the remaining rules apart, and tell me what each rule says about it. I've been pushing Wolbachia on her — maternally inherited, so it rides through the host's bottleneck, but it kills male hosts and generally acts against the host's interests — because I think it breaks her whole system, but maybe it doesn't.
