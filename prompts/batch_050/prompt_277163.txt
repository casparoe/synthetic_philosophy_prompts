ok so i'm helping run a reading group on the philosophy of time and we just finished the augustine session -- book XI of the confessions, the distentio animi stuff, the threefold present (present of things past in memory, present of things present in direct attention, present of things future in expectation). i want to write them a sestina as a sort of parting gift and i'm stuck, so: write it for me.

the reason it has to be a sestina specifically is that the form seems weirdly perfect for augustine's picture and i want the poem to actually cash that out rather than just mention it. the six end-words keep cycling back in a fixed order, never escaping, which is basically the human condition on his view -- the soul stretched out (distentio) across a past that's gone and a future that doesn't exist yet, with only the present of attention actually real. and the way the same words recur but mean something slightly different in each stanza could do the work of showing how past and future only exist as present memory and present expectation -- same "words," different tensed roles.

so: six end-words that should be (or clearly evoke) memory, attention (or sight/gaze), expectation, past, present, future. the poem should track augustine's actual puzzle about measurement too -- we measure time, but how, if the past no longer exists and the future doesn't yet and the present has no extent? his answer that what we really measure is something in the mind, an impression that passing things leave, should show up somewhere, ideally around the envoi where the form tightens.

one more thing: there's a contested reading issue. some people read augustine as a full presentist, others as saying something more subtle where only the mind's stretching gives time any reality at all. i dont want the poem to hedge. commit to one reading -- i'd lean toward the stronger mind-dependence reading since it makes the distentio/intentio contrast hit harder, but if you think the text supports the other one better, go with that. either way, add a short note after the poem saying which reading you committed to, why, and where the poem takes liberties with what augustine actually says (e.g. if you import anything about eternity that hes less clear on). the note can be a few sentences, casual is fine.
