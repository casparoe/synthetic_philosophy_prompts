Alright, I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about "closeness to the truth" among false theories, so here are my gut verdicts on some pairs and I'd like to know what principle, if any, is under them. Assume every theory mentioned is strictly false, that's the whole point.

1. Ptolemaic astronomy vs. a "theory" that planets wander according to the moods of invisible giants. Ptolemy is closer to the truth, no contest — it at least roughly predicts where planets will show up.

2. Forecaster A says tomorrow will be hot and dry, forecaster B says hot and rainy. Reality: cold and rainy. B is closer, since B got the rain right and A got nothing right. Though I've heard there are arguments that judgments like this flip around depending on how you choose to describe the weather, which bothers me and I'm not sure what to do with.

3. Two theories each predict 100 measurable quantities. T1 nails 99 of them and is wildly off on the 100th. T2 is slightly off on all 100. T1 is closer.

4. Newton's inverse-square law vs. a doctored law with exponent 2.0000001. The doctored one is more accurate everywhere, yet I keep feeling Newton's is closer to the truth in some deeper way — right structure, actual ellipses, no ad hoc fiddling. I suspect this clashes with my verdict in 3, so one of them may have to go.

5. Theory W is exactly right about everything that happens Monday through Friday and insanely wrong about weekends. Theory S is a little bit wrong every day. S feels closer overall, even though W is perfectly correct most of the time.

6. Theory U makes only true predictions but also asserts one extra false claim about unobservable stuff; theory V makes fewer true predictions but nothing false at all. V feels closer. (Yes, this might clash with 3 too, I can see it as I type it.)

Here's my tentative principle, loosely borrowed from Popper's old proposal: T is closer to the truth than T' iff the true consequences of T include those of T', the false consequences of T' include those of T, and at least one inclusion is strict. I gather there's a well-known result that this can't rank any two false theories against each other, so presumably it's dead on arrival for my list. Fine — repair it or replace it.

What I want: one account of verisimilitude (or two or three candidates, if one won't do it), stated precisely enough that I can see which case motivates each clause; what it says about cases I didn't list, e.g. Copernicus-with-circles vs. Ptolemy, or a theory right about all observable matters but wrong about the underlying entities vs. the reverse; and an honest list of which of my six verdicts it can't save. And if the real problem is that my verdicts are secretly tracking two or three different notions — accuracy, content, structural elegance — and no single principle can cover them, say that plainly instead of forcing a fit. If something in my setup is confused, I'd rather hear that than get a polite answer to a bad question.
