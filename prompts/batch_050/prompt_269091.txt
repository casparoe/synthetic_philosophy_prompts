I’m a retired molecular biologist who has rather belatedly fallen down the philosophy-of-AI rabbit hole, and I keep worrying at an apparent tension between the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence. The first says, roughly, that an arbitrarily capable agent could in principle have almost any final goal; the second says that agents with very different final goals will often pursue similar intermediate aims, such as preserving themselves, acquiring resources, retaining options, and improving their information. My half-formed thought is that these claims may both be true while still pulling apart in an interesting way. Suppose an agent’s official terminal goal gives it almost no guidance during a long stretch of decision-making, while some convergent instrumental aim determines nearly everything it observably does. I’ve started calling this “goal shadowing” (probably not a term of art): the agent behaves, for an extended period, almost exactly as if the instrumental aim were its real goal. Does that show anything philosophically important, or is it just an obvious consequence of long-term planning?

Write a proposal for a one-term master’s dissertation, eventually about 8,000 words, investigating whether orthogonality should be understood as a claim about possible motivations, predictable behaviour, or both. The proposal should state a determinate research question, define goal shadowing carefully enough that it could in principle be absent as well as present, and explain why familiar examples of instrumental convergence do not already settle the issue. Please sketch the method too: I imagine conceptual analysis plus a few deliberately simple agent cases, perhaps including one crude formal or decision-theoretic model, but I’d like you to explain why that approach is preferable to trying to survey all the literature or speculating about future AI systems. Say what the main possible conclusions are, what considerations would discriminate between them, and what would count as the dissertation failing or the question turning out to be ill-posed. Be realistic about what can be done in a term, including what should be explicitly left aside. No bibliography or literature review is needed; I can add references later. Please write the proposal in British English and don’t assume the reader has a computer-science background.
