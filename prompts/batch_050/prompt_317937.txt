I’m putting together a handout for an upper-level philosophy seminar on Frankfurt cases, and I keep finding taxonomies that just list the usual labels without showing what actually separates the views. Please make me a decision tree of 8–10 diagnostic questions that sorts readers by their intuitions about direct moral responsibility for an action. Don’t begin with “Do you believe in free will?” or “Are determinism and responsibility compatible?” Each question should present a concrete case or an answerable claim about one, so that someone can work through the tree without already knowing the terminology. The relevant target is ordinary moral blameworthiness, not legal liability, punishment, or whether the person has a bad character.

Please build the tree from cases like these, using at least five and changing the details if that makes the distinctions cleaner: a chip would have forced Dana to leak a document if she had shown the earliest sign of deciding not to, but she leaks it entirely on her own and the chip never activates; Dana has exactly the same psychology in a deterministic world but no chip exists; her only physically possible way of avoiding the act is a random neural blip or some other “flicker” that she could not choose for a reason; a device leaves her a 10 percent, 50 percent, or 90 percent chance of avoiding it; yesterday she freely installed the device that removes today’s alternatives so she cannot back out; she could not have acted otherwise in the exact circumstances, although the same decision-making mechanism would have responded differently if new reasons had appeared; and her choice is partly produced by an undirected quantum event that gives her no extra control. The branching questions should distinguish, where possible, robust alternatives from mere flickers, alternatives from sourcehood, present control from tracing to an earlier act, and categorical responsibility from responsibility that comes in degrees.

At the leaves, locate at least these positions if the tree can separate them: a robust-leeway defense of the principle of alternative possibilities, a flicker-of-freedom version, source incompatibilism, semi-compatibilism, a reasons-responsive actual-sequence view, hard incompatibilism, and one plausible but unnamed hybrid position. If two named positions give the same answers, put them at the same leaf and explain what extra issue would be needed to split them rather than pretending the tree is more discriminating than it is. Also include a compact answer key, one sentence at each leaf describing its most awkward commitment, and a short note on any coherent-looking combinations of answers that have no named defender. Please write the whole thing in American English, and don’t turn it into a history-of-the-literature survey or a bibliography.
