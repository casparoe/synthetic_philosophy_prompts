Last night, over too much wine, a friend and I fell into an argument about whether psychopathic offenders deserve blame, and she cornered me with a line of reasoning whose conclusion I cannot accept but whose steps I cannot yet fault. I have reconstructed it as faithfully as I can:

1. It is appropriate to blame someone for an act only if, at the time of acting, they knew the act was wrong.
2. One knows that an act is morally wrong only if one possesses the concept of moral wrongness.
3. Possessing that concept requires grasping that moral transgressions are wrong regardless of what rules or authorities decree — for this, she insisted, is precisely what separates the moral from the merely conventional.
4. Psychopaths do not reliably grasp this: on the standard moral/conventional task, they characteristically treat a serious assault and a breach of classroom etiquette as wrong for the same sorts of reasons, namely that some rule forbids them — much as young children do.
5. Therefore psychopaths lack the concept of moral wrongness (from 3 and 4).
6. Therefore they cannot know that what they do is wrong (from 2 and 5).
7. Therefore blaming them is inappropriate (from 1 and 6), and our practice of holding psychopathic offenders criminally responsible rests upon an error.

To her credit, my friend accepted that the argument excuses small children as well, and regarded this as a feature rather than a defect. She baulked, however, when I suggested that premise 3 appears to prove too much: anyone raised to regard morality as essentially a matter of divine command would, by her lights, also fail to possess the concept of wrongness, which threatens to excuse rather a large proportion of humanity, past and present.

My question: where, if anywhere, does the argument go wrong? If a premise must be rejected, tell me which one carries the least cost. I should like to keep premises 1 and 4 if at all possible, so if the fault lies in 2 or 3, I need to understand exactly what I am giving up — for instance, whether rejecting 3 commits me to saying that one can possess a concept whilst holding an entirely false theory of what it marks out. Please keep your diagnosis under 350 words.
