I am a novelist at work on a book about a disputed manuscript, and I want the plot to turn on a philosophical point I can currently state only loosely, drawn from Hofstadter's discussion in Gödel, Escher, Bach of the frame message, the outer message, and the inner message. I would like you to construct a thought experiment that puts that scheme under controlled pressure, since the plot will stand or fall with whether it survives.

Here is the case I need. An artifact passes from a sender to a community of receivers. The frame message succeeds: every receiver recognizes the artifact as a message and correctly identifies the kind of thing it is. The outer message succeeds: every receiver possesses and correctly applies the decoding mechanism; not a word is misread. The inner message succeeds by the only criterion that seems available: every receiver recovers exactly the content the sender encoded, and all of them recover the same content. Yet the communication fails — the receivers come away understanding something the sender did not mean, or failing to understand what she did mean — and the failure must not be locatable in any of the three layers as Hofstadter characterizes them. I want the case engineered so that this residue is visible rather than quietly patched over.

Constraints. First, no exotic devices: no alien jukeboxes, no universal decoding machines, no information-theoretic machinery beyond what an educated reader already carries in her head. The artifact should be something I could plausibly put in my novel — a letter, a diary, a book, an inscription. Second, the receivers' misunderstanding must be reasonable, ideally inevitable, given everything they could in principle know from where they stand; I do not want a story about careless or provincial readers. Third, the failure must hinge on a single isolable feature of the case: there should be one alteration which, if made, restores successful communication, and you must tell me what it is. Fourth, give me one scenario worked out in detail, not a catalogue of candidates.

After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it meets the specification. Proceed layer by layer, showing that the frame, outer, and inner messages each function exactly as required, and then say where the failure actually lives if not in any of them — whether it forces a fourth category, shows that what mattered was never inside the artifact in the first place, or instead exposes an ambiguity in how the three layers are individuated to begin with.

Finally, the epistemic bookkeeping. Tell me which further alteration to the case, beyond the single feature already identified, would rescue the three-layer scheme and thereby defeat the thought experiment's point; identify where your construction is most likely to have cheated — for instance, by quietly enlarging the outer message or the frame until nothing could possibly fail outside them; and state plainly what consideration or counterexample would change your verdict about where the failure resides. I would rather have an honest account of the case's weakest joint than an airtight one.
