So a few months back I got diagnosed with a chronic pain condition (nothing dramatic, think constant moderate joint pain, worse some days) and it's turned me and my friend Mara into late-night philosophy debaters about whether pain and happiness can actually be traded off against each other. She has very strong intuitions and I've been keeping a list because she's weirdly consistent, or so I thought.

Her verdicts so far:
1. Trading a month of annoying daily headaches for a genuinely wonderful year: take it, easy call, mild pain is just a cost like any other.
2. Enduring ten minutes of something torture-level in exchange for a lifetime of extra joy afterwards: absolutely not, no amount of happiness pays for that.
3. A million people each getting a paper cut to spare one person from being tortured: prevent the torture. The paper cuts "don't add up to that," her words, no matter how many you pile on.
4. Ten minutes of torture-level pain vs. fifty years of a constant moderate chronic ache: she'd take the ten minutes. She says the fifty years is worse, and she said this BEFORE my diagnosis, so it's not just her being nice to me.

Now here's the new case we hit last night. Suppose you could either let one person go through two weeks of severe, torture-level pain, or let ten thousand people live out whole lives with the kind of moderate chronic ache I have, everything else about their lives being normal. Which do you pick?

I honestly can't tell what she'll say. Case 4 suggests she thinks long moderate pain can pile up into something worse than short severe pain, but case 3 suggests she thinks mild suffering just doesn't aggregate across some threshold, and I'm not sure my ache counts as "mild" or "severe" on whatever scale she's running. Predict her verdict, tell me what rule (or rules) she seems to be running that fit all four verdicts, and if more than one rule fits, tell me which of the cases so far actually pins it down -- or what case I'd have to put to her to tell the rules apart.
