I cover science and big ideas for a magazine, and I've been circling a piece on consciousness for months without finding my angle. Here's what's bugging me. The hard problem got its name thirty years ago and the debate has basically calcified: physicalists say phenomenal experience will eventually yield to the same kind of explanation that dissolved the mystery of life, dualists and panpsychists say no pile of functional or structural description adds up to what it's like, and illusionists say the whole problem is a cognitive artifact -- we systematically misdescribe our own introspective states. Same neuroscience, three camps, zero convergance.

My working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about consciousness but about what counts as an explanation. The physicalists think a good explanation is one that lets you predict, intervene, and build; their opponents think an explanation of experience has to make the experience intelligible from the inside, and neither side can argue for its own standard without begging the question. Is that right, or is there a genuine empirical question buried in there -- say, whether the meta-problem (why we *report* a hard problem, why we say the things we say about experience) can be fully solved in functional terms, which illusionists seem to think would defuse the rest?

What I want is a diagnosis with reasons, not a tour of the positions. And be straight with me: is there anything -- a formal result about what structure-and-function explanations can in principle deliver, a decisive experiment, a distinction nobody's drawn yet -- that could actually end this? Or should I write the piece as a story about a disagreement that has no possible settlement, and explain why that is?
