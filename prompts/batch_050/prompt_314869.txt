I'm in a second-year metaphysics module and we just finished the personal identity unit (Locke's memory theory, Reid's objection, all the fixes). Our exam lets us bring a one-page sheet, so I want to make a flowchart that sorts the positions we covered. Here's the bit I want the chart to hang off. It's from Locke's An Essay Concerning Human Understanding, Book II ch. 27, section 3 -- I'm copying from an old edition so the punctuation is a bit weird:

"an oak growing from a plant to a great tree, and then lopped, is still the same oak ; and a colt grown up to a horse, sometimes fat, sometimes lean, is all the while the same horse"

What got me in seminar: here Locke says the horse stays the same horse because it's the same continuing *life*, even with total turnover of matter. But ten sections later he says a person stays the same because of same *consciousness*, not same life. So the first question on my tree is basically: does whatever keeps the colt the same horse also keep you the same you? If you answer yes you end up somewhere near animalism, if no you're off into the consciousness branch and then Reid's officer case is waiting for you.

So: build me a decision tree, 6 to 8 branch questions, that takes someone from that root question to a named position. Rules:

- Every node is a concrete question or case you can answer without knowing any labels (no "do you endorse psychological continuity?"). Cases that have to show up somewhere: Reid's brave officer (general remembers being the officer, officer remembers being the flogged boy, general doesn't remember the boy), the prince and the cobbler, a fission/teleporter-split case, a brain transplant, and total amnesia about something you definately did.
- Leaves: the direct-memory view (with the officer problem still attached), the overlapping-chains fix, the no-branching version, the "identity isn't what matters anyway" exit, animalism, and the Butler/Reid "identity is simple and unanalyzable" dead end. Add bodily continuity if it's not redundant with animalism.
- Mark any combination of answers nobody could coherently hold (I think direct memory plus transitivity is one?) and say why it's empty.
- Flag any two leaves your tree can't actually seperate with the questions given.

Keep each leaf to one sentence on its most awkward commitment -- it has to fit on one page.
