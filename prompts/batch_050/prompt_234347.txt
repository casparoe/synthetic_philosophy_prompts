Okay so I'm a novelist working on a first-contact trilogy that leans hard on dark forest logic -- the idea that the galaxy is silent because every civilisation that reveals itself gets destroyed, so the rational move is to stay hidden and shoot first. Liu Cixin built the famous version on two axioms (survival is the primary need, resources are finite) plus chains of suspicion and the tech-explosion problem. Fine. But I keep running into places where the hypothesis has loose threads, and I can't resolve all of them in one book, so I need to triage.

Here's my list of the ones bugging me, feel free to add your own: (1) the detection asymmetry problem -- can a civilisation actually hide, given waste heat, industrial byproducts, accidental leakage, and the fact that any telescope good enough to strike is good enough to spot you; (2) whether "shoot first" survives contact with game theory -- iterated encounters, the option value of waiting, and the possibility that a strike reveals *you*; (3) deterrence-by-broadcast, i.e. the idea that you can hold a rival hostage by threatening to publish their coordinates to the wider forest, and whether that's a stable equilibrium or just mutual suicide with extra steps; (4) a thing I invented for the book where a paranoid civilisation seeds thousands of *fake* coordinates -- broadcasts pointing at empty systems or at third parties -- to turn the forest's hunters against each other, and whether that actually improves their safety or just raises the ambient trigger-happiness; and (5) what the hypothesis implies about the *first* move, since someone had to strike before deterrence norms existed.

Which of these matters most for me? Prioritize for a specific purpose: three books, one main character who's a game theorist, and I need the world's logic to hold up under a smart reader poking at it, not under a physicist. For each item, tell me why it's upstream or downstream of the others -- like, does the fake-coordinates scheme even make sense if detection asymmetry resolves the wrong way? Which ones can I paper over with authorial handwaving and which ones will blow up the whole plot if I get them wrong? Rank them, defend the ranking, and flag any place where the "obvious" resolution that fans online seem to assume doesn't actually follow. Also if there's an open question I'm not seeing that you'd put above everything on my list, say so and argue for it.
