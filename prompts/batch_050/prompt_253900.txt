Over the past year or so I've become something close to a convinced tranquilist about wellbeing -- the view, associated with Lukas Gloor, that nothing is good in itself except states free of craving, and that what we call pleasure is really just the relief or temporary silencing of some craving, not a positive ingredient added on top of neutral experience. Pleasure is the water, craving is the thirst; the water has no value beyond what it does for the thirsty.

What I want from you is: what would count against this view? Don't give me the standard objections to negative utilitarianism warmed over -- tranquilism isn't classic NU, and I allready know how I'd answer the pinprick-style stuff. I want a real map of the view's exposure. Specifically:

- Which intuitions or cases should actually move me? E.g., pleasures that seem to arrive with no prior craving at all -- an unexpected smell of rain, music that lands out of nowhere when you were perfectly fine a second ago. Does the tranquilist reply (cravings can be faint and only noticed in their removal, like a thirst you only register when you drink) survive scrutiny, or is it a patch that renders the view unfalsifiable?
- Are there defeaters lurking in population ethics? Tranquilism seems to imply that adding a perfectly content, craving-free person to the world makes nothing better, and I honestly can't tell if that's a bullet to bite proudly or a reductio.
- Does the view collapse into a rival? If on close inspection tranquilism turns out to be antifrustrationism (Fehige) or preference-based NU in new vocabulary, is that a refutation, a vindication, or neither? And who gets to say?
- The meta-question: tranquilism is an axiology, so no experiment can refute it. What kind of thing could even count as evidence here -- phenomenology, coherence with adjacent views, intuitive costs? If nothing could in principle dislodge it, is that a defect in this view or just what being an axiology is like?

Sort the defeaters from "fatal, abandon ship" to "awkward but survivable," and for each say whether a committed tranquilist would themselves concede it counts against the view or dismiss it as question-begging. For calibration: I find phenomenological arguments far more persuasive than intuition pumps, and bullet-biting defenses embarrass me less than they embarrass most people.
