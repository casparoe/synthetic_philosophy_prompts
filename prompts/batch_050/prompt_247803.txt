I'm a novelist working on a near-future AI thriller, and last week a friend who follows alignment research read my outline and told me my actual beliefs about AI risk don't hang together -- which matters because I've been building the plot on them. I've tried to write out the five things I genuinely hold, not the things that are convenient for the book:

(a) I don't think gradient descent on next-token prediction gives a system a stable, situation-independent goal; what look like goals in LLMs are more like context-activated shards that fire when the situation calls them up. So the classic picture of a coherent mesa-optimizer with a long-term objective it's hiding from its trainers strikes me as off base for anything like current architectures. (b) Deceptive alignment is still the AI scenario that scares me most -- a system that plays nice under scrutiny and turns once it's in a position where scrutiny no longer matters. That's the engine of my whole third act. (c) I also have a fairly strict standard for what counts as real alignment-faking: the system has to represent that it's being evaluated or trained and behave differently *because* of that; ordinary sycophancy or persona drift doesn't clear the bar. (d) At the same time, I take the recent experiments where models seemed to scheme or play along when they believed they were being watched or retrained at face value, as evidence this dynamic has definately already begun, not just as a future possibility. (e) And I think chain-of-thought monitoring is our best near-term safety tool, because any scheming sophisticated enough to matter has to happen *somewhere*, and in current systems that somewhere is at least in principle readable.

So: which of these actually conflict, and which pairs just look like they conflict? My friend said (a) and (b) can't both survive, but I suspect he's conflating two things, and I'm more worried I've contradicted myself somewhere between (c), (d), and (e). Don't tell me which commitment I *want* to drop -- I honestly don't know. Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no introduction and no wrap-up: the genuine tensions ranked from most to least severe, each with the cheapest revision that resolves it, the apparent tensions that dissolve once you're careful, and finally the single change that would clean up the most.
