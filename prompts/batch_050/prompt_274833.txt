I'm a third-year undergraduate taking a seminar on individual ethics and collective harms, and our final project is a classification instrument for the positions we've encountered on the so-called inefficacy objection -- the worry that since no individual purchase, flight, or ballot makes any causal difference to factory farming, climate change, or an election, there is no moral reason to act otherwise. For the project I want to build a decision tree that takes a classmate from the shared starting point -- "I accept that the aggregate harm is real and that my individual contribution is causally negligible" -- to a named position, but our instructor keeps insisting that the standard trichotomy of consequentialist, deontological, and virtue-ethical responses is too coarse, because those families disagree about things that cut across the labels (for instance, whether unknowable production thresholds matter, and whether participation itself can be wrong without causation).

Please construct the tree so that:

1. Every branch node is phrased as a concrete question the respondent answers about a case, not as a question about a doctrine. To keep the cases fresh, use these three rather than the usual chicken and trolley examples: a consumer in a country where meat is restocked by weekly order volume rather than by individual purchases, so it is genuinely unknown whether any given purchase triggers a production response; a frequent-flyer program member deciding whether one more flight per year matters given that the plane flies regardless; and a voter in a safe seat whose constituency's median-voter dynamics mean her ballot could matter only under turnout conditions she cannot detect. Where a node would receive different answers depending on which of the three cases is in view, say so explicitly and note what the divergence reveals about the position.

2. The leaves must include at least: expected-value reasoning based on the possibility of being the marginal or threshold-triggering agent; a complicity or participation-based view on which one can do wrong by contributing to a collective outcome without causing it; a Kantian universalization response; a virtue- or integrity-based response; a view on which individual consumption duties dissolve and are replaced by duties of political or institutional action; and the deflationary leaf on which the inefficacy objection simply succeeds and there is no individual duty at all. If your tree's questions cannot separate two of these, flag that rather than forcing a split, and explain what further case would be needed.

3. Where the logical space permits a combination of branch answers that no position I know of occupies, leave the leaf in the tree, mark it unoccupied, and give a one- or two-sentence diagnosis of whether it is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

4. End with a short note on which single node does the most work -- i.e., which question, if answered differently, reroutes the largest share of positions -- since I have to defend that choice in the project presentation.

The tree should be usable by a classmate who has done the seminar readings but has not memorized anyone's taxonomy, so please avoid letting the wording of any node tip off which leaf it leads toward.
