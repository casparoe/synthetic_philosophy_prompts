Here's my working principle about manipulation, and I want it stress-tested. I think persuasion is legitimate when it works by giving the person reasons (or evidence, or experiences) they can then weigh for themselves, and manipulative when it works by triggering some psychological mechanism that operates *around* their judgment instead of through it. But my own verdicts on cases don't seem to line up with it:

- A charity leads with one named, photographed child instead of statistics about millions, because they know donations double. Fully honest info, nothing hidden. Verdict: fine.
- A different charity mails me a little gift (address labels) before asking for money, banking on reciprocity. Also nothing hidden, I know exactly why they sent it. Verdict: manipulative.
- My therapist asks questions she's carefully chosen so that I'll end up stating the reasons to quit drinking in my own words, which she knows makes them stick better. Engineered as hell. Verdict: fine.
- During a fight, my partner cries. The tears are 100% genuine, but she's aware they're making me cave and doesn't exactly fight them. Verdict: not manipulation, or at least not blameworthy.
- A supermarket puts the expensive brand at eye level. Verdict: manipulative, mildly.
- A negotiator opens with an extreme first offer purely to anchor me, knowing I'll discount it. Verdict: fine, standard practice.

So: my principle handles the anchoring case okay (an anchor isn't a reason), but it seems to flunk the two charities -- both levers bypass judgment equally, and knowing about the mechanism doesn't obviously neutralize either one. And eye-level shelving engages my *judgment* in some sense, I do see the product, so why is that one out? Either repair the principle so it delivers most of these verdicts (tell me exactly which clause does which case), or convince me one or two verdicts have to go. Also: what does your best version say about a politician who's genuinely furious about an injustice and lets it show in a speech, partly because he knows genuine anger is contagious?
