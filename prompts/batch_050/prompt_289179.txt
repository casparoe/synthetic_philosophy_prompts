Here's my opening move. Take the gatecrasher case: 1000 people at the show, only 499 paid for tickets, and the organizers sue one randomly picked attendee for the price of admission. On your view the court should find against them, since it's more likely than not they snuck in. Fine. But now run the same suit against all 1000 attendees and your theory licenses 1000 findings of liability, of which we know in advance at least 499 are false — and there's no procedure for telling the true verdicts from the false ones. A method of 'proof' that is guaranteed to be wrong about half the time, predictably and undetectably, isn't proof; it's a lottery with a court seal on it. And don't reach for DNA to save yourself: we trust a DNA match because there's a causal story about how the sample traveled from the crime scene to the lab, which is exactly what the gatecrasher base rate lacks.
