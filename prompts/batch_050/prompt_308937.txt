Next month I am presenting to a joint seminar of stratigraphers and environmental historians my proposal for how the start of the Anthropocene should be fixed. My position is that the boundary should not be placed at the point of sharpest globally synchronous stratigraphic signal (which favours a mid-twentieth-century date) but chosen on explicitly functional grounds: the right start date is whichever one best serves the periodisation's explanatory and political purposes, even if that means defending a diachronous or contested marker such as early colonial land-use change. Prepare me for the question period. Set out the objections I should expect, roughly in the order they will arise, phrased as each constituency would phrase them: the stratigraphers will say I am abandoning the criteria that make a geological unit formal rather than rhetorical, and confusing periodisation with advocacy; the historians will say that any single date, however chosen, flattens unequal responsibility and smuggles in a decision about who counts as the 'anthropos'. For each objection, state its real force, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and where my reply is weakest — including the question I am hoping nobody asks. Please answer in British English.
