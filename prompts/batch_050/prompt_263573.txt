A colleague built the following argument out of the Judy Benjamin case, and my reading group cannot find the exit. The setup: Judy is certain she is in exactly one of regions R1, R2, R3, with credence 1/3 in each; only R2 contains a "blue" zone, and conditional on being in R2 her credence that she is in the blue zone is 1/2. A fully trusted source then tells her: if you are in R2, you are not in the blue zone. The argument:

(1) To learn an indicative conditional "if A then B" is to learn that the A-and-not-B possibilities are ruled out; so the correct update is conditionalization on the material conditional: eliminate the A-and-not-B worlds and renormalize.
(2) Applied here, that eliminates the R2-and-blue worlds (probability 1/6) and renormalizes, so P(R1) rises from 1/3 to 2/5.
(3) But the message was entirely about what R2 is like; it contained nothing discriminating R1 from R3, and nothing bearing on how likely R1 was in the first place.
(4) So the correct posterior keeps P(R1) at 1/3.
(5) Hence (1) is false, and the natural replacement is imaging: shift the probability of the excluded worlds onto the nearest worlds where the conditional holds -- here, move the 1/6 from R2-and-blue onto R2-and-not-blue -- which keeps P(R1) at 1/3 and matches the Ramsey-test idea that accepting "if A then B" is a matter of setting P(B given A) to 1.
(6) So updating on an indicative conditional is in general imaging, and imaging on "if A then B" never changes P(A).
(7) But that proves too much: it implies that learning a conditional can never bear on its antecedent -- that a detective who comes to trust "if the butler took the key, the spare is missing" has thereby learned nothing that could bear, even slightly, on whether the butler took the key, whatever the source and however the trust arose.
(8) So either conditionalization in (1)-(2) was right and the intuition in (3)-(4) is wrong, or some third update rule is needed; but (1)-(4) each seem forced, and no third rule is on the table.

Which step do you reject? Explain why that step looked forced -- it did to us -- and what rejecting it costs. For what it is worth, we would most like to preserve (3)-(4), the thought that the message was only about R2, but we want to know what we are paying for it.
