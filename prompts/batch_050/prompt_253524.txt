I've been going back through the old mentalese debates and trying to put together a complete list of the main arguments for the language of thought hypothesis, roughly in the Fodor tradition. Here's what I have so far: (1) productivity — we can think an unbounded number of novel thoughts, which is hard to explain without recombinable parts; (2) systematicity — anyone who can think 'the dog chased the cat' can also think 'the cat chased the dog', which suggests thoughts share a common syntax; (3) the concept-learning argument — learning a concept looks like forming and confirming hypotheses, which presupposes some medium to state hypotheses in, so you can't learn your first language; (4) the inference argument — thoughts stand in rational relations to each other (believing p and believing if-p-then-q leads to believing q) and those transitions look structure-sensitive, like derivations; (5) the cognitive science argument that the only worked-out story we have of a physical system doing rational stuff is computation over representations, so thought presumably works like that too.

Is there a sixth argument I'm missing?
