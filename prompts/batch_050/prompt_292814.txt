I teach undergrad bioethics and I'm trying to sort out my own views on the therapy/enhancement line, so I went by gut on a bunch of cases. Here's where I land:

- LASIK for a commercial pilot who wants slightly better than 20/20 vision: fine.
- A healthy surgeon taking modafinil before a 12-hour operation so she stays sharp: makes me queasy, even though it seems parallel to the pilot case.
- Growth hormone for a kid who's just short (no hormone deficiency, parents want him taller): not fine.
- Growth hormone for a kid with diagnosed growth hormone deficiency: obviously fine.
- A cochlear implant for a deaf kid so he can hear: fine.
- A hypothetical implant giving a hearing kid perfect pitch and the ability to hear dog-whistle frequencies: not fine, though I'm less sure here.
- Antidepressants for someone who's sad but not depressed, like after a normal breakup: not fine.
- Caffeine before the bar exam: completely fine, nobody blinks.
- Hypothetical memory-chip implant so law students can skip memorizing for the bar exam: feels wrong, even though it's caffeine with extra steps.
- Screening embryos for a variant linked to better memory: nope.
- Vaccines: totally fine, and nobody calls them enhancement even though they make your immune system better than nature made it.

So my instincts are a mess. The pilot/surgeon pair bugs me, and the caffeine/memory-chip pair bugs me even more, and vaccines seem to break whatever rule the rest of my gut is following. Is there one principle that gets most of these? Or two or three candidate principles that each get different subsets? I want this stated precisely enough that I could defend it to my students, not just "restoring normal function is ok, going beyond isn't," because I already know that slogan doesn't survive contact with vaccines and caffeine.

Format-wise, give me the response in this order: (1) "The menu" — two or three candidate principles stated precisely, one short paragraph each; (2) "Scorecard" — for each candidate, which of my eleven verdicts it captures and which it breaks, maybe a half-page total; (3) "The tie-breakers" — how each principle rules on cases I didn't list, like beta-blockers for stage fright or cosmetic surgery to look younger for a job hunt; (4) "My recommendation" — which principle you'd actually go with and which of my gut verdicts I should be willing to give up, one paragraph. Keep the whole thing under ~900 words and don't turn it into a literature survey.
