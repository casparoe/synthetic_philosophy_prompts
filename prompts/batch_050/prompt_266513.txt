A bit of context first. I run a graduate seminar on paradoxes of rational choice, and this term the self-torturer case (Quinn's one, with the dial strapped to the arm that pays out at each notch and where adjacent settings are indistinguishable in felt pain) produced the liveliest discussion we've had in ages. One of my students pushed a thought I can't shake: everyone argues about the climb up the dial, but almost nothing gets said about the way back down. Once you're at the top, each single notch downward looks just as attractive as each notch upward did on the way in — a tiny, imperceptible relief, again and again. So a rational agent seems able to walk itself all the way back to the start, ending up exactly where it began, minus its winnings, with nothing to show for the round trip. That strikes me as less a puzzle about intransitive preferences and more a genuinely self-inflicted money pump, and I suspect that reframing changes what a solution has to deliver: it's not enough to explain why stopping somewhere is rational, you have to explain why the full cycle is irrational even though every individual step passes the standard tests.

So here's what I need. I'm applying for our faculty's small internal research grant (one term of teaching relief, decision in November) and I want a one-page proposal for a single paper on the downward leg of the self-torturer and what it shows about rational preference. The proposal should state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the usual moves in the literature — intransitivity-friendly views, vagueness or epistemicist treatments, resolute-choice style answers — don't already settle the reversal case; lay out the approach I'd actually take and why that approach rather than the obvious alternatives; and say honestly what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing. Please don't include a bibliography or any named citations beyond what's needed to identify the positions — I'll fill in the references myself, since I know roughly who holds what here.

Two further requests. First, commit. I don't want a menu of possible projects to choose between: pick the single angle you judge most promising, build the proposal around it, and give reasons — including a sentence or two on why the strongest runner-up loses out. If you think my money-pump framing is wrong and the downward leg collapses back into the familiar puzzle, that's a position too, but then argue for it rather than hedging. Second, the whole proposal must come in under 500 words, because the committee genuinely will not read past a page.
