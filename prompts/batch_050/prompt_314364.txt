Here's my opening move: in the image-scanning experiments, people take longer to shift attention between two landmarks on a memorized map the farther apart those landmarks are, and the response time scales pretty cleanly with the actual distance. If the image were just a list of propositions -- "the hut is 4cm from the tree," stored as symbols -- then distance would be an arbitrary number in a description, and there'd be no reason for retrieval time to track it so smoothly. The fact that time tracks distance looks like the representation itself preserves metric space, not just facts *about* space. How do you explain the linear distance-time function without admitting the format is depictive?
