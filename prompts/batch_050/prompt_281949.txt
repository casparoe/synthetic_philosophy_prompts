I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over luminosity and the KK principle, using only the setup below plus standard, non-specialist knowledge of those notions. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or quotations.

Setup. Treat an agent’s mind as a black-box system with a query interface. A first-order query Q(x) asks whether condition x obtains, where x may be an external fact, a bodily sensation, an occurrent emotion, an intention, or a belief-like commitment. The interface returns success/failure, a coarse confidence, and sometimes a margin value estimating how far the world is from the nearest case in which the answer would flip. Define luminosity for a class C as: whenever a condition in C obtains, the agent is in a position to know that it obtains. Define KK as: if the agent knows p, the agent knows that the agent knows p. Camp L, the bounded luminists, defend luminosity for some self-directed conditions and a restricted KK for successful self-ascriptions under ideal attention. Camp A, the margin-for-error anti-luminists, deny luminosity for any non-trivial condition with a vague or noisy boundary and deny KK generally, while allowing reliable self-knowledge without iterability.

Produce four parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section that both sides could sign: claims must be specific enough to constrain a simulator, not platitudes, and should cover at least single-case fallibility versus threshold fallibility, the difference between being in a state and having an exact discriminative representation of its boundary, the possibility of reliable self-ascription without KK, the behavior of repeated self-queries, and the role of margins near sorites-like boundaries.

Second, a disagreements table with columns: issue; Camp L formulation in words Camp L would accept; Camp A formulation in words Camp A would accept; genuine or partly verbal; if partly verbal, the disambiguating distinction that removes the verbal residue. Include at least these issues: whether knowledge requires a safety margin or merely benefits from one; whether a successful self-query creates a new iterable fact or only re-presents the old one; whether confidence calibration is evidence of knowledge; whether luminosity fails because of noise, vagueness, finite attention, or the metaphysics of mental kinds; whether KK is a norm of assertion, an architectural invariant, or an idealized limit.

Third, a cruxes section. For each crux, state an operational test implementable in the toy query model, record each camp’s prediction in advance, and say what result would move which camp and how far. Cover at minimum: behavior as the margin approaches zero; whether one extra introspective step increases reliability, merely adds noise, or changes the state; whether there is a stable fixed point after finitely many iterations; and whether errors cluster at boundaries uniformly across mental kinds or differ for pain, intention, belief, and emotion.

Fourth, end with a joint engineering specification: a minimal state-machine semantics for Q, margins, confidence, and higher-order queries on which both camps can agree as a common testbed, while explicitly marking the parameters about which they still disagree. Keep the tone formal and constructive; do not adjudicate the winner; if part of the dispute dissolves under the model, identify exactly which part and why.
