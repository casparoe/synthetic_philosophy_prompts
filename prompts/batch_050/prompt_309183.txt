In my philosophy of probability seminar we've just covered the Carter–Leslie doomsday argument, and I've come to accept a fairly strong version of it: given only my position in the human birth order, and absent other evidence, I should shift my credence substantially toward humanity's total population being small relative to its being astronomically large. Please treat this as my position and tell me what would count against it. In particular: which auxiliary assumptions does the argument actually stand or fall with -- the choice of reference class, the shape of the prior over total population, the principle used to update on my own indexical information -- and for each, what would the world (or the surrounding theory) have to look like for that assumption to fail in a way that defuses the argument rather than merely weakening it? I'd like defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, including cases where accepting the doomsday reasoning apparently forces me to accept parallel reasoning I find implausible (say, about my being early in some other sequence). Also say which concessions defenders of the argument have themselves already built in -- for instance, whether the argument is best read as merely adjusting a prior that other evidence then overwhelms -- and whether a position qualified that far still risks anything at all, or has become unfalsifiable in a way that should make me suspicious of it.
