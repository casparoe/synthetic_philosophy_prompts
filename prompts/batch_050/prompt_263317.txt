The received answer to the old tautology objection — the charge that selection theory is circular because fitness is defined in terms of actual survival and reproductive success — is the propensity interpretation, which defines fitness instead as a probabilistic disposition to leave offspring, so that the fitter organism can be unlucky and the less fit one lucky. My half-formed worry is that this fix relocates the problem rather than solving it. A propensity attribution is only ever supported by actual or hypothetical reproductive outcomes, and only relative to some specification of the environment and the reference class of trials. If the theorist individuates environments finely enough to accommodate whatever outcomes occurred, fitness slides back toward actual success and the circle closes; but if the propensity is cut loose from any outcome pattern, claims that selection explains trait frequencies start to look empirically unconstrained — explanation by a hidden variable that no evidence could disturb. Turn this into a proposal: state the question precisely (can the propensity theorist specify what makes fitness attributions explanatory rather than redescriptive, without either collapse?), explain why the standard defenses leave it open, set out the method — I plan to distinguish a causal-dispositional, a statistical-mathematical, and an ecological reading of fitness claims and test each against drift-dominated cases in small populations and against rapidly changing environments — and say how the project bears on, without simply replaying, the debate over whether selection theory is a causal or a purely statistical theory. State what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing, including the possibility that the problem dissolves into semantics.
