A friend of mine from the old LessWrong days and I have been arguing about what Occam's razor actually cashes out as, and I've started keeping a record of her verdicts because I suspect she's running a specific formal rule without having said so. Here are the cases she's ruled on so far. One: a coin comes up HTTHTHTTHH; she says a fair coin plus noise is a better explanation than a program with the sequence hard-coded, even though the second fits perfectly. Two: she dismisses Last-Thursdayism outright — the world being created last Thursday with all memories and records intact — as worse than the standard past, on the grounds that the fabricated records are extra specification. Three: given ten data points fit exactly by a ninth-degree polynomial and approximately by a straight line, she takes the straight line. Four: she prefers many-worlds to collapse interpretations, her stated reason being that a collapse rule is additional code while unitary evolution is not. Five: asked how much more likely a hypothesis is if its shortest program is ten bits shorter than a rival's, she said roughly a factor of a thousand, not ten, not a million. Six: asked whether the choice of universal machine matters, she said it can shift things by a constant but shouldn't change her verdicts in cases like these.

Here's the new case I want to put to her. Two deterministic programs both exactly reproduce a long observed string. Program A is 200 bits and computes the string directly in a few million steps. Program B is also 200 bits, but it works by simulating a simple cellular automaton for 10^20 steps and reading the string off a pattern in the ash. By pure universal-prior weighting they're equals; by anything that charges for runtime they aren't. And here's the follow-up I want answered too: does your prediction change if Program B is 199 bits instead of 200? What I'd like from you is a prediction of her verdict on both versions, a diagnosis of which rule she's implicitly running — Solomonoff's universal prior, a two-part minimum-description-length criterion, a speed prior, or some hybrid — and an honest accounting of which of these rules fit all six of her earlier verdicts equally well. If more than one rule survives, tell me what single additional verdict I could elicit from her that would most cleanly separate the survivors, and say what each rule would predict about it.
