At a department happy hour two history grad students got into a long fight about why the men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 became mass killers, and they asked me to settle it, which I can't. (1) Mara took roughly Browning's line: these were middle-aged reservists, not hand-picked fanatics, their commander even offered to let them sit out the first massacre and almost nobody stepped forward, so the explanation has to be situational -- conformity, deference, not wanting to look weak in front of your comrades, gradual escalation. (2) Dev took roughly Goldhagen's: situation can't explain the zeal. These men weren't reluctantly complying, they killed face to face for months, hunted down escapees on their own initiative, and some brutalized victims when nobody ordered them to. Peer pressure explains going along, not volunteering for extra cruelty. (3) Mara said cruelty is exactly what routinization and dehumanization produce once killing becomes your day job, and anyway the eliminatonist-antisemitism story can't explain why similar massacres happen in totally different cultures, or why plenty of non-Germans collaborated eagerly. (4) Dev said fine, but Browning can't explain variation: units in near-identical situations behaved differently, some commanders made opting out easy and some made it hard, and the men could feel that difference -- so something about culture and character has to be in the story, otherwise the whole theory reduces to 'people do whatever the guy next to them does.' (5) Mara then proposed a test: imagine you could swap in equally ordinary middle-aged men from a country without Germany's antisemitic tradition. Browning predicts they mostly kill too, Goldhagen predicts they mostly don't. Doesn't that settle it? (6) Dev said the swap is rigged, because ordinary men bring their formation with them -- you can't hold the person fixed and change only the surrounding culture, so the thought experiment can't discriminate between the theories at all. (7) Mara shot back that if no comparison could ever discriminate, then Goldhagen's account is unfalsifiable and stops being an explanation at all. Dev said that's a philosopher's parlour trick; historical explanations are judged by comparative fit with the evidence, not by lab-style falsification, and the comparative evidence (enthusiasm, initiative, cruelty beyond orders) fits his side better. By that point everyone was tired and nothing got resolved. Can you adjudicate this move by move -- say who's right on each specific point, flag where either of them is overclaiming, and say what, if anything, they're both missing?
