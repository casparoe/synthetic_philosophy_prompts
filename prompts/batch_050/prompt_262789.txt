I'm a PhD student working on distributive justice and I'm putting together a paper on the Wilt Chamberlain argument from Anarchy, State, and Utopia. My supervisor keeps pushing me to find an objection that isn't just the usual greatest hits, so here's where I'm at. The objections I already have, and don't need re-explained to me: first, the initial acquisition objection, i.e. that the argument starts mid-stream and even perfectly just transfers can't launder unjust initial holdings, so everything hinges on the Lockean proviso stuff. Second, Cohen's line that Nozick just equates liberty with private property rights, so taxing Wilt restricts one person's freedom to dispose of money but can expand other people's effective freedom, and "liberty upsets patterns" only sounds devastating if you've already decided whose liberty counts. Third, the point that Nozick slides between prohibiting transfers and taxing them: nobody is banning capitalist acts between consenting adults, you can still give Wilt your quarter, the state just skims some afterwards, so the pattern is disturbed by adjustment rather than by interference with choice. Fourth, the aggregation objection, where each individual transfer is fine but the cumulative outcome erodes the background conditions (equality of opportunity, fair value of political influence, whatever) that made the exchanges legitimate in the first place. Fifth, the loaded-example objection: Nozick picks the most sympathetic possible case, a beloved athlete getting small voluntary payments, and then generalizes to inherited fortunes and windfalls and monopoly rents.

So what I want is one more objection that is genuinely not on this list, not a rewording of one of them, and not just two of them stapled together. It can target the transfer principle, the way the example is set up, the move from the one-step case to a whole society, whatever -- just not a variant of the five above. Also say what would change your verdict -- like what consideration or counterexample would make you conclude the objection actually fails, or where your own reasoning for it is most likely to fall apart, because I'd rather know that now than in my defense. One more thing: please write the whole thing as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just like you're writing me a long email.
