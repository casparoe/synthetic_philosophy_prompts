I'm preparing a short position statement for a graduate seminar on epistemic rationality, and before circulating it I'd like a consistency audit. Here are my commitments, stated as plainly as I can:

(a) Evidentialism: what it is rational for a person to believe is fixed entirely by their total evidence, nothing else.

(b) Higher-order evidence is ordinary evidence. Evidence that I have reasoned well or badly — that I was tired, that peers disagree with me, that my track record on this kind of question is poor — gets integrated with the first-order evidence like any other consideration. There is no special bracketing or insulating of first-order judgments.

(c) The enkratic requirement: epistemic akrasia is always irrational. It can never be rational to believe p while believing that one's evidence does not support believing p.

(d) Conciliationism about peer disagreement: when someone I know to be my epistemic peer firmly disagrees with me about p, I am rationally required to move substantially toward their view, roughly splitting the difference.

(e) Conciliationism applies across the board: to disagreement about the epistemology of disagreement itself, and to disagreement about my own reasoning competence in the case at hand.

(f) A case verdict: suppose my first-order evidence clearly supports p, but I acquire strong yet misleading evidence that my faculties are impaired — a physician I trust tells me I've been dosed with a reasoning-scrambling drug, when in fact it was a placebo. My judgment is that rationality requires me to retain my belief in p.

Please tell me whether this set hangs together. Identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; for each genuine tension, explain what forces it, rate its severity, and state the least costly revision that would resolve it, including what that revision would sacrifice. Rank the tensions from most to least serious rather than treating them as equal, and conclude by identifying the single change that would restore the most coherence. Keep the entire audit under 400 words.
