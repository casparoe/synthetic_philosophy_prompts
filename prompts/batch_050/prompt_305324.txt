I just joined my university's IRB and the first protocol on my desk is already a headache. A psych lab wants to run a study on why people fall for misinformation. Participants get told its a study about memory for news stories, but the real point is which fake stories they later believe -- so there's deception built in. They recruit through a gig-work app and the pay is $12 base plus up to $20 in performance bonuses, which to me looks like it might nudge people who need the money into staying when theyd otherwise quit. And the lab specifically wants a sample that skews older, 65+, since that's who they say shares misinformation most, so presumably some participants will have mild cognitive stuff going on. The PI's cover letter insists the deception is "standard practice" and the debrief handles it.

I have to write the short opinion I'll circulate to the board before we vote. Please draft it: a definite recommendation -- approve as-is, approve with specific modifications, or reject -- with the reasons, treating the consent issues (the deception, the pay structure, the older sample) as the heart of it rather than as checklist items. Take an actual position and defend it; don't hand me a menu of options. And whatever you recommend, give the strongest argument on the other side a fair run before knocking it down, because someone at the meeting will raise it.
