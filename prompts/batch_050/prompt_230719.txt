I am trying to work out what, if anything, fundamentally distinguishes the past from the future, and I want the question settled through my own considered verdicts on cases rather than through a survey of positions in the literature. Here are the verdicts, stated as I actually hold them.

1. A film of a porcelain cup falling and shattering, run in reverse, is immediately recognizable as unphysical; a film of two billiard balls colliding, run in reverse, is indistinguishable from a lawful process. I take the first asymmetry to be real and the second process to be genuinely time-symmetric.

2. A droplet falling into a still pool produces expanding circular ripples. The time-reverse — ripples converging from the edge of the pool, meeting at a point, and ejecting the droplet — violates no law of mechanics, yet I would stake anything against ever observing it, and I do not think this reluctance is merely a psychological fact about me.

3. The stars radiate spherical waves of light outward into space. The time-reversed process, in which exquisitely tuned incoming waves converge on each star and are absorbed, strikes me as a genuine asymmetry of the world but not of the laws: I want to say it reflects the contingent absence of suitable absorbers rather than anything built into electrodynamics itself.

4. The very early universe appears to have been in an extraordinarily special, low-entropy state. I am inclined to regard this as a brute boundary condition to be accepted rather than a regularity crying out for derivation from a deeper law, and I find myself suspicious of proposals to explain it.

5. I possess detailed records and memories of the past and none of the future, but I do not regard this as an independent arrow of time: whatever explains the shattering case should, I think, explain this one as well.

6. Certain rare particle decays, in the neutral kaon system, are not invariant under time reversal even after the standard CPT compensations. I concede this is a bona fide time asymmetry in the fundamental laws, but I deny it has anything to do with why cups do not reassemble — the effect seems far too small and too parochial to underwrite the macroscopic arrow.

7. A gas confined to one corner of a box, once released, expands to fill it and never returns. If the box were observed for astronomically long times, recurrence considerations say it would return; I nonetheless treat the expansion as irreversible for all explanatory purposes and do not feel the recurrence threatens the arrow.

8. Gravitating systems behave oppositely to gases: a smooth distribution of matter clumps into structure, and the clumped state is counted as the higher-entropy one. This makes me uneasy about glossing entropy increase as mere spreading out, and I suspect my verdict 4 smuggles in assumptions about gravitational entropy that I could not state precisely if pressed.

The tension I keep circling is this. Verdicts 2, 3, and 4 together push me toward the claim that all temporal asymmetry in physics derives from a special initial condition conjoined with time-symmetric laws. But verdict 6 locates an asymmetry in the laws themselves, and verdict 8 leaves me unsure whether the notion of a low-entropy early universe is even well-defined. Meanwhile verdict 5 commits me to the records asymmetry being derivative, though I could not produce the derivation on demand.

Is there a principle, stated precisely, that systematizes these verdicts — one that says which phenomena genuinely mark a direction of time, which merely reflect statistics and boundary conditions, and how the two classes stand to one another? If no single principle accommodates all eight, tell me which verdict is best sacrificed and why, what the most defensible revised set looks like, and what the surviving principle predicts about cases I have not listed — in particular, whether a universe contracting toward a low-entropy final state would appear to its inhabitants to be running backward in time, and whether the presence of perfect absorbers everywhere would force me to revise verdict 3.
