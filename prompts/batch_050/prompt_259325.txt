I work as a software engineer and have no formal training in philosophy, but over the past year a colleague and I have had a running disagreement that I would like to see written up properly. My colleague holds a fairly standard total utilitarian view: what matters is the sum of welfare across everyone, suffering counts negatively and happiness counts positively, and in principle enough happiness can compensate for any amount of suffering. I lean toward a suffering-focused or negative-leaning view: I doubt that happiness and suffering trade off against each other so cleanly, and I think preventing severe suffering has a kind of priority that merely creating additional happy experiences does not. We have mostly talked past each other, so I want us to do an adversarial collaboration and produce the joint statement it should yield. Please draft that document for us.

Make sure it contains the following, written so that each of us could honestly sign our own side's wording:

1. A numbered section of claims we should both be able to agree on -- for example, about what suffering and happiness are like as experiences, about our shared uncertainty regarding consciousness in animals and future minds, and about what neither view is committed to (I suspect my colleague thinks my view implies we should painlessly end the world, and I think his implies we should tile the universe with barely-content minds; please sort out which of these implications actually follow and which are caricatures).

2. A disagreements table with three columns: the precise point of disagreement in neutral wording, my side's position as I would phrase it, and his side's position as he would phrase it. Flag any row where, after careful formulation, the disagreement turns out to be about words rather than substance, and say why.

3. A cruxes section identifying the two or three sub-questions such that resolving them would actually move one of us. For each crux, describe a concrete hypothetical or piece of evidence we would both accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each of us predicts our own view would say about it. As one test case you may use this scenario I invented for our arguments: a "calibration chamber" in which, before running a simulated civilization, you can inspect its complete future and learn that it contains a trillion lives of modest contentment and one life of unrelenting agony; you may run it, run a variant with the contentment halved and the agony removed, or run nothing. Record what each side predicts the correct choice is and what each side would count as a reductio of the other side's answer. Include at least one further test case of your own construction rather than recycling well-known examples like the experience machine.

Keep the whole document under 1,100 words. Begin with a one-sentence statement of what the document is and who its two signatories are.
