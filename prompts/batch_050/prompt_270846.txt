Two friends of mine have been bickering about low-impact AI for months and I've voluntered to referee. Write the joint statement they'd produce if they sat down and did a proper adversarial collaboration.

Priya thinks you can define "don't cause side effects" without first telling the agent what humans actually value. Her favoured family of ideas: penalise the agent for making the world harder to steer back from, or for shrinking what a wide range of possible future goals could still achieve — so switching off a stranger's life support scores as huge impact, tidying your own desk scores as roughly nothing, and no one had to write down the true utility function. Marcus thinks this is hopeless. Which changes count as big, he says, depends entirely on what you care about: unplugging a freezer is a catastrophe if you care about the ice cream and a non-event if you don't, so any impact measure is just values smuggled in through the back door, badly.

I want the document they could both sign: first a numbered list of agreed claims (about what the naive versions get wrong, about cases where any half-reasonable impact penalty fires and cases where none does, about what would and wouldn't count as actually solving the problem); then a disagreements table, each row phrased so the person holding that view would accept the wording of their own position; then the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete scenario or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If part of their fight turns out to be about words — say, what "side effect" means, or what "without specifying value" is even claiming — say which part and dissolve it.

One thing I care about alot: don't launder a contested claim into the agreed section. Keep what's genuinely settled among people who've thought hard about this (e.g. that a fixed penalty gets Goodharted, the doing-vs-allowing asymmetry, offsetting behaviour) separate from what's still up for grabs, and flag the places where even the experts are basically guessing.
