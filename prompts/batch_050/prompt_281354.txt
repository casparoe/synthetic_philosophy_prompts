So I wrote a novel a few years ago and it's done okay, but lately it's been having this second life online where a big chunk of readers have latched onto an interpretation of the ending that I honestly never meant at all. Without spoiling it: the book is narrated by a guy who gradually reveals himself as selfish and deluded, and I meant the ending as him getting away with it and the reader being left squirming. But a whole community of readers reads the same ending as him finally being redeemed, and they read my ambigous last chapter as the proof. They've built this whole moving reading of the book as a story about forgiveness, and people write to me saying it helped them through stuff.

Here's my actual problem. A podcast wants me to come on and talk about the ending, and I know they'll ask me which reading is right. My agent says I should just say the redemption reading is valid, it's good for sales and the readers clearly love it. Part of me wants to say no, I wrote the book, the ending means what I meant it to mean, and the squirming version is the real one. But when I try to actually defend that I get tangled up. The words on the page genuinely do support their reading better than I realized when I wrote it. And if I die tomorrow, does the meaning die with whatever was in my head, or does the book just keep meaning whatever it best supports? Also, does it even matter what's true here -- is there maybe a difference between what the book means and what I should say about it in public?

So: when I get asked on the podcast, what should I actually say, and why? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning -- no preamble, no restating my question back at me. And I don't want generic "all interpretations are valid!" fluff, I want to know whether my intention counts for anything here and if so how much, and whether the nice thing my agent wants me to say could be true even if my intention counts for a lot.
