(1) Frankfurt cases work — Jones is blameworthy for what he did even though Black's device meant he couldn't have done otherwise, so the principle of alternative possibilities is just false. Responsibility doesn't require alternatives.

(2) The "flickers of freedom" reply doesn't save PAP either. If the prior sign (the blush, the twitch, whatever) reliably shows what Jones will do, then it only rules out alternatives by assuming his decision was already fixed, which stacks the deck; and if the sign is unreliable, the leftover possibility is too thin to be the kind of alternative that responsibility was supposed to need.

(3) Still, I'm a libertarian about free will. Determinism is incompatible with it, and what compatibilists offer (reasons-responsiveness, mesh accounts, whatever) doesn't get you real desert-entailing responsibility — you need to be the ultimate source of your action, and that requires genuine indeterminism.

(4) The consequence argument is sound.

(5) I also accept "ought implies can" — you can't be obligated to do something it's impossible for you to do.

(6) And I think standard excuses like coercion or seizures exculpate because in those cases the behavior isn't really coming from the agent in the right way.
