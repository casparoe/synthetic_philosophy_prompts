So my reading group is doing Brandom (the Making It Explicit-era stuff, meaning as inferential role) and my flatmate, who's sat in on exactly one session, reckons he's found a fatal flaw. He's got this argument that inferentialism makes disagreement impossible, and I've been going round in circles on it for two days. The conclusion obviously can't be right but I can't find the move I hate. Here it is as he gave it to me:

(1) To grasp the meaning of an expression is to master its inferential role: which claims license it as a conclusion, and what follows from claims containing it.

(2) Inferential role is holistic — the role of "dog" in your idiolect includes every inference involving "dog" that you accept, because post-Quine there's no prinicpled line between the inferences that are constitutive of the meaning and the ones that are just things you happen to believe.

(3) No two speakers accept exactly the same inferences. I infer "Fido is an animal" from "Fido is a dog"; my flatmate accepts that one too but, being a vet student, happily infers all sorts of things I'd never dream of (stuff about distemper, hip dysplasia, whatever), and presumably rejects or suspends some I'd endorse.

(4) If two tokens of a word have different inferential roles, they have different meanings — that's just what inferentialism claims.

(5) So "dog" in my mouth means something different from "dog" in his.

(6) But two people can only genuinely disagree if some expression in their mouths means the same thing; otherwise they're like one person saying "banks are financial institutions" and the other saying "no they're not, banks are where the fish are" — homophones, no contradiction.

(7) So my flatmate and I have never actually disagreed about anything, and by generalisation, neither has anyone else.

(8) But people plainly do disagree — he's disagreeing with me about whether this argument works right now.

(9) So inferentialism is false: meaning cannot be inferential role.

He calls this "Fodor and Lepore's point made personal" or something like that. I'd very much like to keep both inferentialism and the existence of disagreement. So: which step do I actually reject? I keep suspecting something dodgy happens around (2)–(4), but every time I try to say what, I either end up reinstating the analytic/synthetic distinction (which I thought we weren't allowed to do) or saying "the shared inferences are the ones that matter", which just begs the question. And if I try to fix things by saying inferential role is about what you're *committed* to rather than what you'd actually say in the pub, does that genuinely save disagreement, or does it just relocate the problem to two people who endorse really different norms — say a classical and an intuitionist logician arguing about excluded middle? Tell me which premise or step has to go, why it looked so plausible, and what each of the live repairs would cost me.
