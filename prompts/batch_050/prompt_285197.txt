A friend and I recently got into an argument we couldn't resolve, and I'm hoping there's a named concept for the thing we were circling. The pattern is this: someone wants another person's agreement to something -- to go on a date, to lend money, to volunteer for a committee, to sleep with them -- and the other person declines. The asker doesn't threaten anything, doesn't lie about anything, and doesn't have any power over the other person. They simply ask again. And again. Each individual request is perfectly polite, takes the last refusal on board ("I know you said no last week, but..."), and could not reasonably be objected to on its own. But the requests keep coming, and each refusal gets a little more socially expensive -- a little more awkward, a little more hurtful-seeming, a little more requiring of fresh justification -- until eventually the person says yes, partly just to make the asking stop. My friend says this is obviously just ordinary persistence and that adults can hold their ground; I say the resulting yes doesn't count for much, but I can't point to any single moment where something wrong happened, since no individual request was out of line.

I've run into this shape in several places now. A charity fundraiser who calls every month, graciously accepting each refusal, until the donor gives in to end the calls. A child who asks for a puppy forty times over a summer, never punished for asking, until the parents cave. A colleague who keeps proposing that you take over his administrative duty, each time with a new small reason, until saying no feels like a bigger social act than saying yes. What strikes me is the sorites-like structure: if any one of the requests in isolation is innocent, and consent given after request one would clearly be valid, it's hard to say at which repetition the validity drains away -- and yet consent at request forty seems clearly compromised in a way consent at request one was not.

Is there an established name for this specific thing? I know it's somewhere in the neighborhood of coercion, but coercion standardly involves threats, and here nothing is threatened -- the only "cost" of refusing is that you'll be asked again, which the asker is arguably entitled to do. It's also not deception, and it doesn't fit the usual manipulation cases I can think of, since everything is out in the open. If there is a named concept, I'd like to know exactly how it differs from coercion, from nagging in the morally innocent sense, and from legitimate persuasion-by-persistence (surely some re-asking is fine -- where's the line, and what determines it: the number of asks, the asymmetry of what refusal costs each party, the asker's knowledge that wearing down is working?). And if there's no established name, please coin one with a careful definition and a few boundary cases on each side, because my friend and I clearly need the vocabulary before we can even state what we're disagreeing about.
