my roommate is in one of those phases. he read a pop-science book on Gödel over the summer and now everything comes back to it. his favorite move: whenever we argue about anything -- religion, politics, whether materialism is true, whatever -- he leans back and says Gödel proved that no system of thought can establish its own consistency from within, so every worldview, science included, ultimately rests on unprovable axioms, which means every belief system is at bottom an article of faith and his agnosticism about everything is the only rational position. he also sometimes adds that Gödel showed there are truths no system can ever prove, so there are truths no amount of scientific progress can ever reach, which he takes to vindicate some vaguely mystical stuff he's into.

I took a logic course a couple years ago so i have a vague sense something is off here, but when i push back he just says "no, the theorems are quite general, they apply to any sufficiently expressive system" and i can't articulate what the gap is.

So: treat his use of the incompleteness theorems as an analogy -- formal axiomatic systems on one side, worldviews/belief-systems/science as-a-body-of-thought on the other -- and audit how far it holds. Lay out explicitly what would have to correspond to what: what plays the role of the axioms, the rules of inference, the recursively enumerable set of theorems, consistency, the Gödel sentence. Then the disanalogies: which conditions of the actual theorems (recursive axiomatizability, enough arithmetic strength to encode syntax, the derivability conditions for the second theorem, the fact that "undecidable in the system" doesn't mean "unknowable full stop" since you can prove the sentence in a stronger system) have no clear counterpart when the "system" is a worldview, and whether worldviews are even the kind of thing that has a well-defined consistency property in the relevant sense. Distinguish the disanalogies that actually sink his conclusions from the pedantic ones that don't matter for what he's trying to get out of it. And finally: does anything in the neighborhood of his conclusion survive -- some deflated point about unprovable starting assumptions, say -- and if so, does Gödel's theorem do any of the work in establishing it, or was the theorem just decoration on a much older skeptical point? end with a verdict on whether his agnosticism-about-everything move is licensed or whether he's been laundering it through technical vocabulary.
