i'm in a philosophy of mind seminar this term and we just spent two weeks on Ryle, category mistakes, the ghost in the machine, all that. instead of a final response paper our prof said we could bring in something "creative" to the last session, so i want to write a villanelle, and i need help getting it right. the speaker should basically be the visitor from Ryle's own example: the person who gets shown around the campus, sees the colleges, the library, the labs, the playing fields, and then at the end of the tour still asks to be shown the university, as if it were one more building he hadn't gotten to yet.

heres the shape i want. the two refrains of the villanelle should be the two versions of that same question: one is the visitor asking where the university is after being shown all its parts, the other is the same visitor, now standing in front of brain scans or watching neurons fire in some neuroscience lab, asking where the mind is. the whole point of the form is that the refrains keep coming back in alternation, so the reader slowly realizes its literally the same mistake happening twice -- the university isn't a missing building hidden somewhere on campus, and the mind isn't a missing organ hidden somewhere among the gray matter. but i don't want the poem to just state the moral. in the final quatrain, where the refrains meet, the visitor should make the slide Ryle diagnoses: because he can't point to the mind the way he points to a college, he concludes it must be there anyway, only hidden, immaterial, a ghost tucked inside the machine. so the poem ends with him committing the Cartesian version of the error rather than being cured of it, which is funnier and sadder.

someone whos actually read the Ryle chapter should be able to recognize the category mistake in how the refrains repeat, not just catch the phrase ghost in the machine being name-dropped. and someone who hasn't should still get a decent creepy-funny poem about a very stubborn tourist. 19 lines, proper villanelle scheme, and please keep the visitor's voice a little pompous and bewildered throughout.
