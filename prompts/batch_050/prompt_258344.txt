write me a parody excerpt from a self-help book, except the book is Spinoza's Ethics rewritten as a wellness manual while keeping the full geometric apparatus -- definitions, axioms, propositions with proofs, scholia. give it a title like "The Ethics of Self-Care, Demonstrated in Geometrical Order." so: the Definitions section defines things like affect, conatus, and bondage in wellness-manual terms (bondage = our lack of power to moderate our feelings about our ex, or however you want to do it; maybe define "toxins" too), the propositions should be recognizably Spinozist moves translated into the genre -- desire as the very essence of the person, joy as a passage to greater perfection, the mind's power over the affects consisting in knowledge -- and the proofs should actually mimic the stlye, citing earlier props and ending with Q.E.D.

the two things i most want in there: (1) a proposition where the highest form of self-care turns out to be the intellectual love of God, presented as the premium tier of the wellness program, and (2) at least one proof that leans on necessitarianism -- the wellness industry's "everything happens for a reason" taken dead seriously, so that radical acceptance means understanding that your breakup, your layoff, and your knee pain all follow from the necessity of the divine nature with the same force as the rest of the universe. that joke should run through the piece without anyone ever flagging it as a joke.

the scholia are where the comedy breathes -- deadpan practical recommendations, e.g. a scholium endorsing breathwork strictly on the grounds that an affect ceases to be a passion once we form a clear and distinct idea of it. the satire should cut both directions and be fair to both targets: wellness culture really does promise what Part V of the Ethics promises, and Spinoza really did write a manual for emotional freedom, he just did it with axioms. a specialist should be able to tell the author has read Spinoza; a non-specialist should still find it funny.

about 600 words, maybe 6-8 propositions plus scholia. never explain the joke or step out of the voice. just begin with the piece itself, no intro, no commentary before or after.
