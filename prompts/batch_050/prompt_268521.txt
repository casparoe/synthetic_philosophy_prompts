A colleague who runs a mindfulness-and-leadership seminar gave me this gloss of Daoist wu-wei, and I want an audit before I reuse it:

“Wu-wei is the Daoist ideal of effortless action: the sage stops interfering and lets things unfold by themselves. Because deliberate effort is the problem, the path is to stop trying to be good at anything until spontaneity replaces intention; this is why the famous ‘paradox of trying not to try’ is only apparent, since the solution is simply to care less about outcomes. In practice wu-wei is closest to improvisation: a jazz musician, athlete, or cook in flow acts without rules, plans, or self-monitoring, and the Daoist texts treat ritual training and explicit moral instruction as mostly obstacles to that state. So wu-wei is not a technique for acting better but a recommendation to withdraw from striving, politics, and judgment whenever engagement would require strain.”
