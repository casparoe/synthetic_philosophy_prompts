Is there a name for this pattern, and can someone explain to me why it isn't just ruled out by decision theory? Three examples from my own life. A friend of mine trades stocks for a living and he told me he deliberately checks his portfolio less often during rough weeks than during good ones, same account, same information, he just looks less when he suspects the news is bad. My aunt was offered a free genetic test for a condition there's no treatment for, and she turned it down, she said she'd rather not know. And I sometimes avoid looking at my bank balance after an expensive month, not because looking changes anything, it obviously doesn't, I'd just rather stay in suspense a bit longer.

So the pattern seems to be: information is free, sometimes even pushed on you, and people will pay (in effort, in mild dishonesty with themselves, in actual money in the genetic case) to NOT recieve it. But I dimly remember there's a theorem, I think due to I.J. Good somewhere in the 60s, saying the expected value of free information can never be negative, because if you don't like what it tells you, you can always just ignore it and act as you would have anyway. If that's right, all three of us are being irrational, or else the theorem's setup doesn't apply to these cases somehow.

What I'm after: does this phenomenon have an established name, and if it's been studied, what makes the standard theorem fail to cover it? Please don't just say "risk aversion" because none of these people are avoiding a risky choice, they're avoiding a free signal. Also don't just say "people dislike bad news" because that's a restatement, not an explanation -- the theorem already accounts for the fact that you might not like what you learn, you can disregard it. So which assumption is actually breaking: is it something about people not being able to commit to ignoring what they'll learn, or about knowledge itself entering what you care about rather than just guiding your actions, or something else entirely? And is there a clean limiting case, maybe where the information isn't even about the avoider themselves, that shows where the boundary of the phenomenon lies? I don't just want a label, I want to know exactly what cases fall inside and outside it.
