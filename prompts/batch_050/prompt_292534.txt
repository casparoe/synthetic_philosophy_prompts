OK so I watched this youtube video about Chomsky and why he thinks language is innate, and it opened with a bit on Plato that went something like this: "Plato was the first nativist. In the Meno, Socrates proves that learning is really just remembering. He questions a slave boy who has never been taught any geometry, and the boy works out a geometric proof on his own — so Plato concludes the knowledge was already inside him, put there before the boy was born. For Plato, all genuine knowledge is innate, and teaching is just a way of jogging the soul's memory. Two thousand years later, Chomsky makes essentially the same argument: kids hear too little language, and language that's too messy, to learn the rules of grammar from scratch, so those rules must be built in from birth. Plato's theory of recollection is basically the original poverty-of-the-stimulus argument, and Chomsky is just updating it."

That sounded neat — maybe too neat — so I actually tracked down the Meno, and right after the whole recollection discussion Socrates says this to Meno (86b-c, Jowett translation): "Most of the points I have made in support of my argument are not such as I can confidently assert; but that the belief in the duty of inquiring after what we do not know will make us better and braver and less helpless than the notion that there is not even a possibility of discovering what we do not know, nor any duty of inquiring after it—this is a point for which I am determined to do battle, so far as I am able, both in word and deed."

Which does not exactly sound like a guy who thinks he just proved something. So: how fair is the video's summary? Go through it claim by claim — which parts would a Plato scholar sign off on, and which are overstatements or conflations? Like, is "all genuine knowledge is innate" actually Plato's position in the dialogue, did the boy really work the proof out "on his own" given how the scene goes, and is recollection really the same move as the poverty of stimulus argument, or is it answering a different problem (I'm thinking of the paradox about how you can even search for something you don't know)? And what should I make of the quoted passage — is Socrates taking back the "proof," or is this just routine Socratic modesty that leaves the doctrine standing? I'm inclined to think the video is mostly fine as pop philosophy, but tell me if I'm being to generous.
