**Exam question:**
State the paradox of blackmail in its classic form — the puzzle about how two individually legal acts can combine into a crime. Then consider the reply that there is no real paradox, because threatening to reveal the information in exchange for money is itself wrong. Assess whether that reply resolves the puzzle.

**Student's answer:**
The paradox of blackmail is that the law criminalizes a transaction whose component parts are each perfectly legal. If I know something embarrassing about you, I am legally free to reveal it, and I am legally free to ask you for money. But if I offer to stay silent in exchange for payment, I have committed blackmail. Two acts that are each within my rights become a crime when combined, and it's hard to see what justifies that.

One proposed solution, defended by Robert Nozick, is that blackmail is wrong because it is coercive: the blackmailer forces the victim into a choice no one should have to face, and it is the coercive character of the threat that makes the transaction criminal. On this view the two acts aren't genuinely independent — combining them changes the moral character of each one.

The reply that the puzzle dissolves because threatening to reveal information for money is itself wrong does resolve the paradox. Since the threat is morally wrong, the law is justified in forbidding the combination, and the appearance of arbitrariness disappears. The paradox only arises if we insist on evaluating each act in isolation from the other, and we have no reason to do that.
