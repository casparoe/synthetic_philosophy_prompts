I've noticed a pattern in my own thinking that I don't have a word for, and I'd like to know whether people who study this sort of thing have one. I have no training in probability or philosophy, so I'll describe it in plain terms, with the cases where I keep running into it.

If a friend offers me a bet on a fair coin flip at even odds, I'll cheerfully take either side. But there are other propositions where I find myself unwilling to take either side at odds that, by any number I'd write down, ought to be attractive. If someone asks me how likely it is that a couple I know will still be married in ten years, or whether a small business I'm familiar with will survive the next five, I can't settle on a figure. It's not that my figure is low. If you force me to write down a single number, whatever I write feels like a lie: 40 percent implies a confidence I don't have, and 60 percent would feel like exactly the same lie in the other direction. So I try a range instead, say somewhere between a third and two thirds. But the range has its own problem: its edges feel arbitrary, and I couldn't defend why it ends where it does rather than a little wider or narrower.

Here's the instance that convinced me something real is going on. Suppose I'm told a jar holds a hundred balls, fifty black and fifty white, and I'm offered even odds on drawing black. Easy bet, either side. Now suppose I'm told only that a jar holds a hundred balls in some unknown proportion of black and white, mixed by a stranger whose methods I know nothing about. If I had to put a number on drawing black, it would be 50 percent in both cases. Yet I'll take either side of the first bet and neither side of the second. Whatever my mental state is in the second case, the number 50 percent does not describe it, because that same number describes the first case, and the two cases feel nothing alike. I've also noticed that learning vague new information sometimes makes me less willing to bet rather than more, which seems backwards.

So my questions. Does this state of mind have an established name? I suspect it's related to simple risk aversion, but it can't be the same thing, because a merely cautious person refuses unfavorable bets, whereas I'm refusing both sides of a bet that looks fair by my own best estimate. I also suspect it's related to just not having enough information, but that can't be the whole story either, since there are things I know little about where I'd still happily bet at even odds. If there is a name, I want more than the label: I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is taken to be, what it is not, where its edges are, and whether it's regarded as a defect in my reasoning or as a legitimate state that single numbers simply cannot represent. And if it's legitimate, is there any principled way to make decisions while in it, or does the refusal to commit to a number leave you paralyzed whenever a real choice is forced? If there's no established concept for exactly this, please coin one, define it precisely, and mark out its boundary cases.
