Next month I'm giving a talk at a small workshop on the history of analytic philosophy and I need to not embarass myself in the Q&A. My thesis, roughly: the standard revenge story about Two Dogmas of Empiricism is wrong. The revenge story, as I understand it, goes like this — Quine's attack only looks decisive if you accept his behaviorism about language; once you drop that, Carnap's later moves (meaning postulates, the internal/external framework stuff from Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology) show you can have analyticity without reductionism, so the critique missed its target and the two of them were mostly talking past each other. I want to argue the opposite: the second half of Two Dogmas, the attack on radical reductionism, doesn't lean on behaviorism at all, and the verificationist picture of meaning it goes after is precisely what the analytic/synthetic distinction was supposed to rest on. On my reading, meaning postulates don't answer Quine, they just write the disputed synonymies into the language by fiat and hope nobody asks where they came from.

The room: mostly Carnap scholars and neo-Carnapian philosophers of science — people who work in the archives, think the Aufbau got a raw deal, and treat the whole "Quine versus Carnap" framing as itself a historiographical mistake. A couple of elderly Quineans who will be on my side but might "help" in ways that hurt. And two or three younger metaphysicians who think two-dimensional semantics and the revived a priori already settled all of this and will look visibly bored until suddenly they aren't.

Write my prep sheet. I want the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this specific crowd — not generic philosophy-class objections to Quine, but what these people will say, in their vocabulary (internal versus external questions, linguistic frameworks, the principle of tolerance, definition versus explication, the Grice and Strawson line, and so on). Order them by how much damage they'd do if I fumble them. For each: how they'd actually phrase it, a ninety-second reply, what I should concede up front versus hold the line on, and where the reply is soft. Also flag the weakest joint in my own reading — the spot where a Carnap person who really knows the texts could hurt me — and end with the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
