In the literature on the ethics of blame, much attention has been paid to hypocrisy and standing — the idea that a person who is guilty of the very fault he condemns thereby forfeits the right to condemn it. I am interested in a configuration that seems to fall between that idea and its neighbours, and I would like to know whether it has an established name.

Two cases. First: a man who publicly and harshly condemns draft evaders, whose own lottery number was never called, and who concedes, when pressed, that had his number come up he would very likely have fled too. Second: a senior academic who leads the denunciation of a junior colleague caught padding her CV, whose own early career never presented an occasion on which padding would have been both tempting and undetectable, and who admits as much. In both cases the blamer has committed no wrong. There is no hypocrisy of the ordinary sort, since the blamer is not guilty of the fault in question. And yet the blamed party's retort — your clean record is luck, not virtue — has real force: more force than a mere change of subject, though less obvious force than you did it yourself.

My questions are these. First, does this configuration — blame issued by someone whose innocence is untested rather than counterfactually robust, challenged on precisely that ground — have a name of its own, or is it treated simply as a limiting or degenerate case of the hypocrisy objection? Second, if it is not hypocrisy, what exactly is the complaint: that the blamer lacks some credential that standing requires, or that the blame tacitly claims a virtue the blamer has never had occasion to demonstrate? Third, how does the configuration relate to moral luck, which is usually discussed as a problem about the outcomes of things people actually do rather than about wrongs they would have done but never did? Fourth, does the blamer's open concession of the luck improve matters or worsen them — does candour restore standing, or does it aggravate the fault by adding self-awareness to the presumption?

If no established term fits, propose one, define it, and give boundary cases that distinguish it from ordinary hypocrisy, from a bare tu quoque dismissal, and from moral luck proper. A bare label without the carving will not satisfy me.
