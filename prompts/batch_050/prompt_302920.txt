I run a small philosophy meetup at a pub, 10-14 people, mostly not academics, we get about 90 minutes. Next month's topic is advance directives -- whether your earlier self should get to bind your later one. The case that's been eating at me: my gran signed papers twenty years ago saying if she ever got severe dementia she wouldn't want to be kept going, but now she has it and seems genuinely happy most days and gets upset if anyone mentions the papers. So whose call is it?

Design an activity or game we can actually run that makes people feel the pull of both sides before we ever name the issue. Give them a decision to lock in early that they badly want to take back later, or make them rule on someone else's locked-in choice -- small real stakes are fine (snacks, tokens, who does cleanup), just no money. One thing I'd love them to stumble onto themselves: nobody ever fights about a directive the person still agrees with, so is the document ever really doing the work, or is it always the current self deciding?

Include the rules, a timeline, how to run it with no lecture up front, and a debrief mapping what happened onto the gran problem. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time, and be honest about which part of the real problem the game cant reproduce.
