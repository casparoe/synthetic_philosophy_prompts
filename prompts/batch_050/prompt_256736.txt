i sit on the outcomes committee for a small funder of pain research, and basically my job is to decide what our grantees have to measure for a treatment to count as a success. this keeps blowing up on us. last cycle we funded a trial where patients on the drug said the pain was still there, it just didn't bother them anymore, and half the committee said thats a clear win and half said the treatment did nothing. then someone brought up pain asymbolia, the lesion condition where people report pain but show no distress or avoidance, and we realized we don't actually have a view on what makes pain bad, so we can't say what would count as making it less bad.

so before i read any more philosophy of pain, i want the spec first. what would a theory of the badness of pain have to get right to be usable for us -- where usable means it lets us decide, for a given intervention, whether it reduced the bad thing, left it alone, or merely hid it from the patient? give me the desiderata: the cases it has to handle (asymbolia and cingulotomy-type reports, morphine-style dissociations where sensation and bother come apart, the athlete or masochist who says it hurts but they don't mind, congenital insensitivity to pain and what it does or doesn't show, nonhuman animals and neonates if those need separate treatment, plus whatever obvious case i'm leaving out), the distinctions it can't collapse (intensity vs unpleasantness vs suffering, bad-for-the-person vs just bad, the pain itself vs the desire to be rid of it), and any findings from pain neuroscience it isn't allowed to contradict. for each desideratum, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether you think it's genuinely required or just one camp's hobbyhorse. and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, because if the list is inconsistent i want to know that now, before we build it into grant language.

then briefly score the main contenders against the list -- roughly: the badness is wholly in how pain feels; the badness is in what pain motivates or makes you want; and hybrid views. keep the scoring short, the list is the deliverable.

last thing: tell me how confident you are in all of this, and single out the one desideratum or claim you're least sure of.
