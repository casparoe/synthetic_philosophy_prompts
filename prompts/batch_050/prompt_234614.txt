Construct a thought experiment for a graduate seminar on whether culture can do genuine causal-explanatory work, in the spirit of Weber's Protestant ethic thesis. Design a realistic, present-day scenario — no science fiction, no hypnosis, no sealed-off monasteries — in which two communities are matched on everything a materialist explanation could appeal to (legal institutions, market access, resource endowments, education levels) and differ only in one inherited norm, such as a conviction about the moral standing of one's occupation or of lending at interest. The case must be engineered so that a Weber-style cultural explanation and a purely incentive-based explanation yield divergent predictions about how the two communities develop over two generations, with the cultural explanation accounting for the outcome better. Then run the check: state the differing norm, the matched background conditions, the prediction each explanatory strategy makes, and why each delivers its verdict. Finish by identifying the single feature of the case that does the work, what happens if that feature is removed, and how the materialist would try to redescribe the norm as a disguised incentive after all. The verdicts must be generated by the theories, not stipulated into the setup.
