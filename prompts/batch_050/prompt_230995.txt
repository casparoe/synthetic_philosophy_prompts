Hey! So I'm a junior in high school and I'm in our schools philosophy club (yes we have one, it's tiny lol). This semester we got obsessed with this idea called acausal trade — the thing where two agents can end up "cooperating" with each other even though they can never communicate, never meet, and can't causally affect each other at all. It supposedly works because each of them can just reason about what the other one would do, so their choices get correlated through logic instead of through any actual signal. We also got into related stuff like superrationality and programs that cooperate in the prisoner's dilemma by basically reading each other's source code.

For the club's end of year newsletter I want to include a joke about this. Please take on the role of a professional comedy writer who also happens to have a PhD in philosophy and knows decision theory inside and out.

Some rules for the joke:
- The punchline has to actually depend on the acausal part. If the joke would still work unchanged if the two characters could just text each other, it's not what I want. The funny bit should be that coordination happens (or goes horribly wrong) with literally zero communication, purely through each side modeling the other side.
- I'd love it if the joke used the really weird implication that you can't even cheat in acausal trade — since the other agent's model of you already includes whatever sneaky thing you're planning, being predictably honest is somehow the smart move even when nobody is enforcing anything. (If you have a different angle that's just as specific, go for it, but it should be at that level of in-the-weeds, not just "two nerds cooperate lol".)
- Someone who has never heard of acausal trade should be confused by the joke. Someone who has should actually laugh. Please dont aim for the middle.
- Setting is up to you, but bonus points if it's something mundane, like a group project or a shared office fridge, instead of aliens or superintelligences.

Before writing the final joke, brainstorm 3 different possible angles or punchlines, say in one sentence each why they'd land for someone who knows the theory, then pick the strongest one and write out the full joke. Think carefully about the logic of the scenario — if the "trade" in your joke could've just been arranged by talking, you messed up. Also keep it short, it's going in a newsletter.
