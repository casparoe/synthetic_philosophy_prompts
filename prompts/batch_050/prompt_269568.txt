I've been asked to circulate a short statement of my views before our philosophy of mind workshop, and I want a consistency audit before it goes out. Here is where I've landed:

(1) Eliminative materialism is correct about beliefs: the ontology of a mature cognitive science will not contain belief-like states with propositional content, and positing them now is a placeholder at best.

(2) Folk psychology is nonetheless an extraordinarily successful instrument for predicting human behavior, and it will remain practically indispensable in everyday life whatever the science says.

(3) I am a scientific realist in general: the predictive success of a mature theory is best explained by its being approximately true, which is why we should believe in its central posits.

(4) I accept the classic eliminativist charge that folk psychology is explanatorily stagnant -- it tells us little about sleep, learning, mental illness, or memory, and it has not developed in millennia -- and that this stagnation is good evidence against its posits.

(5) It is rational for me to deliberate about my own future conduct in belief-desire terms -- weighing what I want against what I take to be the case -- and I intend to keep doing so.

Please tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to, ordered by severity. For each real tension, state exactly what would have to give on either side and what that concession would cost the overall position. I want a committed verdict rather than a menu: identify the single revision that restores the most consistency at the lowest cost, and defend it against the next-best alternative.
