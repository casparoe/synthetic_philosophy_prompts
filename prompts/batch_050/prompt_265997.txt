So I've been reading LW and the adjacent blogs forever, and one thing thats always bugged me: Chesterton's fence and the precautionary principle feel like the same move pointed in opposite directions. CF says don't tear down an old rule you don't understand, because its existence is evidence it's doing work you can't see. PP says don't deploy a new thing whose risks you don't understand, because absence of a track record isn't evidence of safety. But what happens when the status quo *is* the new risky thing -- fossil fuels, algorithmic feeds, LLMs themselves lol. Compare the two principles properly: what failure mode is each one actually designed against (reformer hubris vs innovator hubris?), what account of where our knowledge comes from does each one smuggle in, and is there a unified principle underneath both -- something like "prefer the option whose failure modes are better characterized" -- or does every attempt at unification just collapse into either status quo bias or novelty bias? If you think they genuinely conflict on some real case, pick one and say which principle should win and why.
