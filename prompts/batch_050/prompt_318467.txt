So I'm a novelist, mid-draft on book three, and last week at our writing group my friend Maya (who writes thrillers, very plot-first) told me to stop obsessing over whether the big death scene is plausible. Her line was: reading fiction is basically like dreaming. You know perfectly well it's not real, same way you eventually know a dream wasn't real, and yet your heart pounds, you cry, you're furious at people who don't exist. So according to her there's no special craft problem about earning emotion -- readers will feel stuff anyway, believable or not, because that's just what the dream-machine does. She was using this to tell me I could have my character's wife die in a freak scaffolding accident in chapter 2 with no setup and readers would still bawl at the funeral.

I'm suspicious but I can't articulate why. Can you audit the analogy properly? Like, actually do the work:

- Give me the correspondence table. What in dreaming maps to what in novel-reading -- the dreamer, the dream's events, the waking up, the lingering mood, all of it.
- Then the places it breaks. One that nags at me: while you're actually in a dream, don't you usually *believe* it? Whereas my reader holding the paperback never believes for a second. Does that difference wreck the whole analogy or is it beside the point? Also things like: dreams aren't authored, you can't reread a dream, dream-feelings evaporate by breakfast but people carry fictional grief around for years and it changes how they treat real people.
- Separate the disanalogies that matter for Maya's actual conclusion (skip the setup, emotion comes free) from the ones that are just pedantic.
- Does her conclusion survive once the breaks are on the table? I have a feeling the analogy might be assuming the very thing we're arguing about, i.e. that emotion doesn't need anything like belief, just vividness.
- And if the dream analogy can't carry it, what's the better comparison for what's going on when a reader cries over my dead wife character -- kids playing pretend? being scared by your own hypothetical at 3am? something else?

Basically: should I write the scaffolding accident or does the emotion have to be earned after all.
