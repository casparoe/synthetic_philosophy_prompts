I am preparing a short position paper for a philosophy of mind and cognitive science reading group, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency audit of the claims I intend to defend. The theme is the idea, traceable to Darwin and to Turing, that the order of explanation can be inverted: instead of assuming that competence must flow from prior comprehension, one shows how competence can arise without any comprehension at all. Here are my five commitments:

1. Darwin's inversion is real and complete for biology: natural selection generates genuine design and functional competence without any comprehending designer, and there is no residual sense in which biological adaptation secretly depends on understanding.

2. Turing's inversion is parallel, and current large language models are its clearest demonstration to date: their linguistic competence emerged from training on next-token prediction over text, not from anything like grasping meanings, so they exhibit competence without comprehension in just the relevant sense.

3. Where genuine comprehension exists — for instance, in a human mathematician who follows a proof rather than merely producing its steps — it makes a real causal and explanatory difference to the competence it accompanies. Understanding is not epiphenomenal decoration laid over competent machinery; citing it genuinely helps explain why the mathematician gets things right.

4. The characteristic failures of large language models — confident fabrication of facts, brittle arithmetic, derailment under minor rephrasing — show that these systems lack genuine linguistic competence, not merely comprehension. They do not really have the skill; they mimic its surface.

5. Human intelligence is itself, at bottom, competence assembled by processes that comprehend nothing; what we call understanding is a late, layered product of such processes rather than their foundation.

Please audit this set. For each pair of commitments that is in tension, state the tension precisely, rate its severity, and identify the cheapest revision that resolves it and what that revision costs. For pairs that merely appear to be in tension but dissolve on inspection, say so explicitly and show the dissolution — I want an audit that discriminates, not one that flags everything. Rank the genuine tensions from most to least severe. I have no preferred revision in mind, so do not assume one; tell me which single change would clean up the most.

Also, hold yourself to the same standard: after the audit, say which argument, example, or consideration would most plausibly change your verdict, and where your own reasoning in the audit is most likely to fail.

End your response with a final line of the form \answer{X}, where X is the number of the single commitment whose revision would do the most to restore consistency, or the word none if you find the set fully coherent.
