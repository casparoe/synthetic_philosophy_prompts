ok so I'm a math grad student who fell down the constructive logic rabbit hole last year, and my reading group wants everyone to write up their position so people can attack it. Before I hand mine in, can you check whether it actually hangs together? Here it is:

1. Intuitionistic logic is the correct logic for mathematics. Excluded middle isn't valid in general, because mathematical truth just is provability-in-principle — there's no Platonic realm out there that settles every statement one way or the other. So I'm an anti-realist about math, roughly in Dummett's camp.

2. The big practical payoff of all this is computational. Constructive proofs contain algorithms (proofs-as-programs), which is why I think constructive math is the real thing and classical existence proofs that don't compute anything are basically second-rate promissory notes.

3. Relatedly, I'm happy to adopt Church's thesis as an axiom: any function on the naturals that a constructive mathematician can genuinely define is computable. If you "constructed" it, you had a method, and a method is an algorithm.

4. I'm also drawn to the Brouwerian stuff — I want the fan theorem on board so I can prove that every total function on [0,1] is uniformly continuous. That's the kind of theorem classical math can't even state properly, and it feels like the real vindication of intuitionism.

5. At the same time, the standard calculus results — intermediate value theorem, every continuous function on a closed bounded interval attains its maximum — should be provable constructively as stated, without weird extra hypotheses. Every undergrad relies on them, so if constructive math can't deliver them that seems like a mark against the whole project.

6. I steer clear of choice principles in all forms, because constructively the axiom of choice implies excluded middle, and I'm not about to reintroduce classical logic through the back door.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I'd like the real tensions ranked by how bad they are, with what I'd have to give up for each, and then one recommendation for the single cheapest revision that gets me to a coherent position. Please be concrete about why things clash rather than just telling me "some people would disagree".
