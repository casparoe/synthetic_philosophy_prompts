Consider a proposal I will call clinical abolitionism: a society that has abolished every practice of moral blame, indignation, and resentment, and responds to all wrongdoing, from everyday betrayals to serious crimes, exclusively with forward-looking measures such as treatment, containment, and prevention. In this society, wrongdoers of every kind are regarded in roughly the way Strawson says we already regard small children and the seriously deranged: as objects of social policy to be managed, rather than as participants in moral relationships who can be owed good will and from whom it can be demanded. Please argue that, on Strawson's account of the reactive attitudes, such a society would not merely have made a costly policy choice but would have lost access to a dimension of human relationship that its members could not recover simply by deciding to value it again, and that this fact bears on whether the proposal is even coherently available to creatures constituted as we are. In developing the argument, address the two strongest objections to it: first, that Strawson's claim that we cannot wholly suppress the reactive attitudes is an empirical conjecture which a sufficiently disciplined or differently socialized population could in principle falsify; and second, that the objective attitude is not an impoverished or inhuman stance but simply a different one, so that nothing is lost which a humane and reflective person should grieve. Throughout, distinguish carefully between what is settled in the interpretation of Strawson's argument and what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who work on moral responsibility — for example, whether his case is best read as a transcendental argument about the conditions of intelligible interpersonal life or instead as a naturalistic claim about the fabric of human relationships — and do not present a contested reading as the scholarly consensus. Write the entire answer as continuous prose, in the manner of a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
