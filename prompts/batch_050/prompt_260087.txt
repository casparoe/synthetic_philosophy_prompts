I'm a retired molecular biologist (worked on auditory physiology for years, ironically enough) who has gotten into philosophy in the last couple of years, and I'm stuck on the question of how music can be sad or joyful or yearning when it's literally just pressure waves. I've read a bit of the standard back-and-forth -- does music express emotion, arouse it, resemble it, etc. -- but everyone seems to argue by pointing at their favorite piece. Before I read any more of it, I want the spec first: what would a theory of music and emotion actually have to do to count as adequate? Give me the desiderata as a requirements list, with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark which ones you think are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are just the hobby-horses of particular camps. A table is fine.

To make this concrete, here are some cases I made up (or noticed) that I think a theory has to handle, but feel free to add your own: (a) a piece that sounds sad to a listener who knows nothing about the composer, the era, or any lyrics -- pure audio, no backstory; (b) a piece that only becomes sad once you learn the backstory, so the same audio file produces a different emotion; (c) genuinely upbeat music in a minor key and miserable-sounding music in a major key, which seems like it should embarass any theory that keys everything to mode; (d) the fact that some animals respond to tempo and pitch but presumably don't get nostalgia from a string quartet; and (e) the weird fact that people voluntarily seek out sad music and enjoy it, which you almost never see with actual sadness. Tell me which of these cases are real constraints and which are red herrings -- I have a suspicion at least one of mine is confused, but I can't see which.

Then the part I care most about: do any pairs of desiderata on your list look jointly unsatisfiable, i.e. a theory can meet one only by failing the other? And which single requirement, if enforced strictly, knocks out the most of the popular approaches in one blow? Score the main contenders against the list only briefly if at all -- I mostly want the criteria themselves, since that's where I think the actual disagreement lives.
