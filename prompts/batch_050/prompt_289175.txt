I'm writing a short piece on griefbots -- chatbots trained on a dead person's texts, emails, voice memos etc. that let the bereaved keep "talking" to them. I've got four objections to the practice and I want one more before I finalize. What I have so far: (1) the consent problem -- the dead person never agreed to be simulated, and probably couldn't have anticipated what it would be like; (2) the distortion problem -- the bot will confidently say things the real person never would, and over time the smooth interactive caricature crowds out your actual memories of them; (3) the grief problem -- the bot lets you indefinitely postpone accepting the death, which is arguably what grief is supposed to accomplish; (4) the exploitation problem -- a company monetizing your mourning has every incentive to keep you hooked rather than to help you heal. What's a fifth objection that's genuinely different from these -- not a rewording, not a special case of one of them (e.g. don't just say "the bot might malfunction and say something hurtful," since that's just 2 again)? Bonus if you can say whether your new objection also applies to older practices like keeping someone's letters or commissioning a portrait of the deceased, and what that shows about it.
