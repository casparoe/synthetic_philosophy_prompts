ok so i'm a junior at Tanner High, which is named after Hollis Tanner, a mill owner who donated the land for the school back in the 1910s. Next month the school board votes on whether to rename it, and i'm speaking at the public forum the night before the vote. Some background: Tanner spent years lobbying to keep the children of his own workers (mostly immigrant families) out of the town's schools, he straight up argued that educating them was a waste of money. And people objected to the name at the dedication itself, letters to the editor and everything, so the usual "he was a man of his time, nobody knew better" defense doesn't really hold. Problem is, two rename campaigns here already failed because they were basically "he was a terrible person" campaigns, and the whole town dug in. His great-grandkids still live here and will be sitting in the front row, plus a bunch of alumni who are super sentimental about the name.

So i want a different angle. I want the speech to just grant the other side everything -- fine, we won't put a dead man on trial by today's standards -- and still land on rename. The idea i want it built around: the name over the door isn't a history lesson, and it's not a verdict on him either. It's something the school actively does, in the present tense, to us. We write it on college applications, it's across our jerseys, we chant it at games. Remembering someone is what museums and history class are for; a name on a school is more like a gift we keep handing him over and over. Write me a 3-4 minute speech built on that spine that also handles the obvious comebacks (it's just a name, you're erasing history, where does it stop) without getting smug about it, and ends by inviting the alumni in instead of telling them they should be embarassed. And don't make him a cartoon villain -- partly because his family is literally right there, but mostly because the argument should still work even if he wasn't one.
