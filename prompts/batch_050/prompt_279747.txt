I’m on the grants commitee of a small private foundation, and we can fund only one of two final proposals. Proposal A would prevent several hundred deaths over the next five years by expanding a proven malaria-treatment program. Proposal B would preserve a collection of climate records, endangered-language recordings, and seed samples in a facility designed to remain useful for centuries, though its value depends on later generations continuing the work.

One trustee argues that Proposal B matters because of something like Samuel Scheffler’s “afterlife conjecture”: many of our projects get part of their point from humanity continuing after we die, so mere survival isn’t the only thing at stake. Another thinks that this is too hazy to justify passing up lives we can save now.

Draft a decision memo of about 900 words for the board. Pick one proposal and give an actionable recommendation, including any conditions on the funding. The memo should explain how much weight, if any, the board should give to humanity’s collective future beyond the particular deaths each option prevents. It should also deal seriously with uncertainty, opportunity cost, and the objection that caring about future “meaning” could become a blank check for prestige projects. Don’t make it a bibliography or a book report on Scheffler; make it something an actual board could use.
