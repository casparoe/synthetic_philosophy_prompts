I'm putting together my "philosophy of physics profile" for a workshop where each of us circulates a page of commitments and everyone else tries to break them, so I want a stress-test before I hand it in. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) Noether's first theorem gives a genuine *explanation* of why energy, momentum and angular momentum are conserved in classical mechanics: time-translation, space-translation and rotational symmetry are the explainers, not mere correlates.

(2) Gauge symmetry is not a real symmetry of nature. Two field configurations related by a gauge transformation are one and the same physical state described twice; gauge "symmetry" is just surplus representational structure, an artefact of the formalism.

(3) All the same, the gauge argument is a good explanation of why interactions like electromagnetism exist: demand local phase invariance of the matter field and you're forced to introduce a connection field with the right coupling. The requirement of gauge invariance does real explanatory work.

(4) Energy is conserved in general relativity too, and it's conserved for the same reason as in (1), namely time-translation symmetry.

(5) Explanation by symmetry is non-causal, but that's no objection, since explanation never required causation in the first place.

(6) In a Lagrangian framework a continuous symmetry and a conserved quantity are interderivable (given the converse of Noether's theorem), and I take interderivability claims like that at face value rather than reading either direction as metaphysically prior.

I have a nagging feeling that at least two of these can't be jointly maintained, and that at least one other pair only *looks* like a problem and isn't. Audit the set: for each genuine tension, say which commitments are in conflict, how severe it is, and what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me.
