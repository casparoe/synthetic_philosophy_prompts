*Bertrand's paradox. **The student's answer:**

Bertrand's paradox, introduced in Joseph Bertrand's Calcul des probabilités (1889), asks: given a circle with an inscribed equilateral triangle, if a chord of the circle is chosen at random, what is the probability that the chord is longer than a side of the triangle? Bertrand showed that three natural selection procedures give three different answers.

(1) Random endpoints: fix one endpoint of the chord on the circumference; by symmetry the other endpoint is uniformly distributed around the circumference, and the chord is longer than the triangle's side iff the second endpoint falls in the half of the circumference opposite the first, giving probability 1/2. (2) Random radial point: choose a radius, then a point uniformly along it, and draw the chord perpendicular to the radius through that point; the chord exceeds the side iff the chosen point lies within the inner third of the radius, giving probability 1/3. (3) Random midpoint: choose a point uniformly in the disk and take it as the chord's midpoint; the chord exceeds the side iff the midpoint lies inside the concentric circle of half the radius, which has one quarter of the area, giving probability 1/4.

Bertrand concluded that classical probability theory is internally inconsistent, since one and the same well-defined event is assigned three different probabilities, which is a contradiction. The paradox therefore refutes the principle of indifference outright, and the principle has no legitimate use in modern probability.

Jaynes, in his 1973 paper on well-posed problems, argued that the paradox dissolves once we make the problem's implicit symmetries explicit. Since all three procedures are rotationally symmetric, rotational invariance cannot discriminate between them; Jaynes showed that the discriminating requirement is translation invariance of the chord distribution, which only the radial-point procedure satisfies, selecting the answer 1/2. This vindicates the principle of indifference: once its symmetry requirements are fully spelled out, it yields a unique correct answer.
