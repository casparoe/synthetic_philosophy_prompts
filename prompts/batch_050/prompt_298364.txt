My flatmate engineers records and hit me with this after a long night; it feels like it proves way too much but I keep missing the dodgy step.

(1) A musical work is repeatable: the same piece can turn up on Tuesday in Leeds and next year in a car, and if literally nothing of it could recur we would say it had been lost or never quite existed.
(2) What repeats is the audible content. If two events would be indistinguishable to ideal listeners with all the time and kit they want, then musically there is nothing more to one than the other.
(3) A good enough capture of an ideal performance preserves everything hearable about that performance, so it is not merely evidence of the work but a full presentation of it.
(4) A score is only a recipe, and a live performance is only one perishing attempt; neither can be the repeatable thing itself unless its content is fixed somewhere hearable.
(5) So the work just is the recording-type: the master/file/stream that can be played again, with scores and gigs as backstage instructions or adverts.
(6) Therefore before there was sound recording there were no musical works in the strict sense, only performance-practices and notation; and if every copy of a track is wiped before anyone has memorised or notated it, that track never existed, however many people once danced to it.

(6) is obviously crackers, but saying “recordings are just recordings” feels like name-calling rather than an arguement. If I reject (2), I seem to say music has invisible essentials that no listening could reveal, which is spooky. If I reject (3), I owe a story about what a perfect capture fails to capture. If I reject (4), I have to explain how a perishing Tuesday gig can be the same thing as next year’s car singalong without smuggling in a recording-like fix. If I reject (5), I need to say what the repeatable work is made of, and why deleting every copy is theft or vandalism rather than metaphysical murder.

Which premise or move should I give up, why did it look harmless, and what does each escape cost for ordinary cases: cover versions, remasters, live improvisation, DJ edits, MIDI mock-ups no human could play, and a symphony known only from a mouldy score? I’d rather keep (1) and (2) than keep my common sense, but I suspect that’s exactly the trap.
