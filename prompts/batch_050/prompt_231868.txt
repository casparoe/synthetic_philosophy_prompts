A colleague sent me the following summary of teleosemantics from an unnamed philosophy survey:

“Teleosemantics explains mental representation through biological function: an inner state means whatever its tokening mechanism was selected to indicate. A frog’s visual state caused by a thrown pellet still means FLY, because the relevant mechanism evolved to respond to flies; the pellet therefore produces a false representation rather than an ambiguous one meaning FLY, SMALL DARK MOVING THING, or FOOD. Since content is fixed by the mechanism’s causal history, its present dispositions and downstream inferential role are irrelevant. For the same reason, a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a thinker formed without an evolutionary history would have no representational states. Teleosemantics thus solves both the problem of error and the disjunction problem, showing that content is objective, determinate, and wholly independent of how the representation is used.”

I suspect that this gets the basic appeal of the theory right while quietly collapsing some important distinctions. Is it a fair summary? Please audit it sentence by sentence, separating what a careful teleosemanticist would accept from what is a simplification or misrepresentation. In particular, distinguish:

1. producer-centered selected-effect accounts from consumer-based accounts;
2. misrepresentation from mere causal deviance or malfunction;
3. the disjunction problem from the underdetermination of content by selection history;
4. dependence on history from the claim that present dispositions are irrelevant;
5. evolutionary selection from learning, development, or other possible content-fixing histories.

Also assess whether the Swampman-style conclusion is a straightforward consequence of teleosemantics or a controversial commitment of only certain versions.

Finally, test your corrected account on this case: in one lineage, a mechanism was selected because it reliably detected midges, but its only downstream consumer uses its output as a generic danger signal that triggers the same evasive behavior for midges, shadows, and falling leaves. Does the output represent MIDGE, DANGER, neither, or something else, and what further facts would decide? No bibliography or citations are needed; I want the conceptual analysis.
