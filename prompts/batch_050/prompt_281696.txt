My metaphysics lecturer does this thing where she rules on puzzle cases at the end of each seminar and never explains herself. Her verdicts so far:

- A lump of clay gets modelled into a statue on Monday and squashed flat on Tuesday: the statue is gone by Tuesday, the lump is still there, and no, the statue was never just identical to the lump.
- A sculptor gradually reshapes a standing figure into a seated one over a month, same clay throughout, no interruptions: same statue all along, just different-looking.
- A statue is melted down into a puddle, and a year later the sculptor uses the very same clay to build a perfect copy of the original: that's a new statue, not the old one back again, and the fact that it's the same clay and looks identical doesn't change that.
- A statue has a cracked arm replaced with fresh clay: same statue. Then, over years of restoration, every last bit of the original clay is gradually swapped out: still the same statue.
- All the discarded original bits from that restoration are later collected and reassembled into something statue-shaped: that's a replica, not the original.

Predict her verdict, and tell me which rule she's actually running -- strict spatiotemporal continuity of the statue-shaped object, some constitutionalist story about lumps, a four-dimensionalist worm thing, or something about the restorer's ongoing activity like a watch surviving a trip to the repair shop. I can see at least two rules that fit all five verdicts but pull apart on this one, so tell me what each of them says, which one you'd bet on given her track record, and what the closest analog among the earlier cases is (I keep going back and forth between the melt case and the gradual restoration case). And if the new case genuinely underdetermines it, name the further case that would settle it once and for all.
