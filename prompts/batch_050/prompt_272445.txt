I retired a couple of years ago after thirty-some years in condensed matter physics, and like a lot of retirees with too much time on my hands I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole. Lately it's panpsychism — the idea that experience, or proto-experience, is a basic feature of matter present all the way down. I can half-follow the metaphysical arguments, but what actually grabbed me is the ethical spin some writers put on it: if sentience is everywhere, maybe suffering is too, and maybe boiling a kettle or crushing a rock is, on some microscopic scale, a moral event. Part of me thinks this is nonsense — everything I know about nociception and neural integration suggests suffering requires the right kind of organized processing, not just any old physical goings-on. But another part of me suspects that's anthropocentrism with extra steps rather than a principled answer, and I don't like leaving it there.

So here's what I actually want. Rather than telling me whether panpsychism is true (I gather nobody knows), tell me which open questions in this area matter most for deciding whether the ethical implications deserve to be taken seriously, and rank them. I'm thinking of candidates like: does micro-level proto-experience even combine into unified subjects capable of suffering (the combination problem, which I believe goes back to William James in some form); would suffering require valenced states, and can valence exist without anything like behavior or report; does moral weight, if there is any, scale with complexity so the totals could ever add up to something a policy should care about; and is any of this empirically tractable at all, or permanently insulated from evidence. Feel free to add anything obvious I've missed. For each one, say why it is or isn't upstream of the ethical conclusions, how tractable it looks for someone with my background (physics and math, no formal philosophy training untill recently), and what new argument or evidence would move it up or down the ranking. I care much more about the reasoning behind the ordering than about the list itself — lists I can find on my own.

Two small requests. First, please answer in British English, since the friend I'll be arguing about this with is a Brit and I enjoy annoying him. Second, end with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing just a short label for the single question you rank first, so I can paste it into the running tally we keep of our disagreements.
