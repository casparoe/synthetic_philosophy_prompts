Three months ago I joined an industrial robotics team that trains manipulation and locomotion policies with deep reinforcement learning. At a recent team dinner, a colleague watching a training dashboard joked that we were running a tiny hell, since the curriculum had held one policy in a high-penalty regime for roughly two million updates. It was a joke, but I have not been able to put it down, and I want our lab's reading group (about ten people, half engineers and half philosophy students, meeting for one evening) to work out what, if anything, each of us actually believes about it.

Please design a decision tree that takes a participant from a shared starting point — "I understand, at least in outline, how an actor-critic agent is trained, and I agree that humans and many animals can suffer" — to a position on whether RL agents can suffer and, if so, where in the system that suffering would live.

Requirements:

- Eight to twelve branch nodes, each phrased as a question about a concrete case rather than an ism. Work at least these four probes into the tree: (1) a tabular Q-learner that receives −1 reward at every step of a maze it has not yet solved; (2) a model-based agent that plans detours to avoid a penalty region it has never entered; (3) an actor-critic agent whose advantage signal is broadcast to gate plasticity across the whole network; (4) the same architecture as (3), but trained purely by imitation, with no scalar reward anywhere in the pipeline.
- Additional nodes should probe whatever distinctions the tree needs to separate the leaves: whether a subject with no phenomenal experience can fare badly at all; whether the candidate bearer of valence is the externally supplied reward, the learned value function and its prediction errors, or the agent's counterfactual avoidance dispositions; whether the magnitude of a signal tracks the intensity of anything; whether one training run over billions of episodes counts as one subject or as many.
- The tree must reach at least these leaves, coined where no settled label exists: pure-arithmetic deflationism; reward literalism (negative reward just is suffering, and magnitude is intensity); a prediction-error functionalist view; a motivational-profile view on which the planning avoider of probe (2) is the strongest candidate; an architecture-gated view requiring a self-model or global integration; precautionary agnosticism; and an illusionist leaf on which the question dissolves even for humans. Locate these in the logical space of possible answers rather than presenting a list, mark any combination of answers that nobody seems to occupy, and say briefly whether each gap looks incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- Flag any pair of leaves that answer all four probes identically and therefore cannot be separated by this tree.
- Every question must be answerable both by an engineer with no philosophy background and by a philosopher whose RL knowledge stops at the starting commitment. Prepend a five-line glossary of the RL terms the probes rely on (reward, value function, advantage signal, model-based planning, imitation learning), phrased neutrally enough that the glossary itself does not smuggle in a position.
