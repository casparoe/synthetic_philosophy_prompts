For context: my father-in-law, a retired wheat farmer, recently heard a popular podcast claiming that the adoption of agriculture was the worst mistake in human history, and he has taken it as a personal slander against his life's work. We end up debating it every time we visit, and at Christmas I offered to write down my own position in full so that we could argue from a text rather than from half-remembered statistics. Before I hand it over, I want to know whether the position actually hangs together. Here is what I find myself committed to:

(1) The "original affluent society" picture is broadly correct: hunter-gatherers typically worked fewer hours, ate more varied diets, and were healthier than the early farming populations that displaced them, so the first farmers got a markedly worse deal than the foragers they replaced.

(2) Agriculture at its origins was not imposed by conquest; it spread because individual households rationally chose to adopt it, which means adoption must have looked like an improvement to the people making the choice.

(3) The steep inequalities of agrarian civilization — hereditary elites, coerced labor, rigid hierarchy — trace back to storable and confiscatable surplus and the property regimes it made possible, and would not have arisen in a world that stayed with foraging.

(4) The claim that the agricultural revolution was, on balance, a mistake for humanity is a meaningful and defensible thesis, not merely an incoherent nostalgia.

(5) Every human alive today exists only because agriculture happened, and one cannot coherently regret, or judge a mistake, an event that is a precondition of the existence of everyone doing the judging.

(6) Whatever forager affluence there was, we are plainly better off now: modern medicine, sanitation, and material abundance outweigh anything lost in the Neolithic.

Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision overall? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say specifically what would have to give. Please respond as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: list genuine tensions first, from most to least severe, each naming the cheapest fix, and if there are fewer than five genuine tensions, use the remaining items for apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, briefly saying why they dissolve.
