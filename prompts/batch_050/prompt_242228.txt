I teach a course on collective decision-making and want a fun reading to pair with the week on Hanson's "vote values, bet beliefs" slogan. Write me a parody FAQ, roughly 600-700 words, issued by the administration of a small liberal-arts college that has just switched all its governance to futarchy: which courses run next semester, who gets tenure, the cafeteria menu, whether to keep the football coach. It should be an in-universe document, addressed to skeptical faculty, and completely deadpan throughout. The jokes have to come from the real mechanics of decision markets and their known failure modes -- thin markets where nobody's trading, wealthy traders' prices counting more, manipulation being cheap when liquidity is low, the awkward fact that bets in the rejected policy branch just get refunded so the incentives there are mushy, the dean quietly trading on information the rest of the college doesn't have. Someone who knows the futarchy literature should nod along rather than wince. The FAQ-writer must never break character or admit there's a problem: the standard objections (who's subsidizing liquidity and why, whether markets track what's good or just what's priced, what happens to people who can't afford to bet) should land entirely through the cheerful bureaucratic answers, never by being stated as objections.
