Okay here's a thing that keeps bugging me and I want to know if philosophy of mind has a name for it or if it's just me being confused.

Two cases. First: I drove home from work last week, twenty minutes, and when I pulled into my driveway I realized I had zero memory of the whole drive. Not in a scary way -- I clearly stopped at lights, changed lanes, didn't hit anyone. Information about the road was obviously getting in and steering my behavior. But was I *experiencing* the road? It honestly feels like the answer is no, and yet something was doing the seeing. Second case, kind of the mirror image: I had a headache all afternoon once and only noticed it existed when it went away and I felt this wave of relief. While it was going on I was just irritable and distracted, I wasn't attending to it at all. But I'd say the headache was conscious the whole time, unlike the drive. So in one case there's a perceptual state doing real work that I'd call unconscious, and in the other a state I was ignoring that I'd still call conscious, and the difference isn't about whether I was *thinking about* either of them.

What I'm groping at is the pattern where a mental state's consciousness seems to come apart from my awareness of it, in both directions. I've half-absorbed from podcasts that there's a family of views where being conscious of something requires your mind to represent the state itself, not just the world -- like a thought about the thought, or a perception of the perception -- and these cases seem like exactly the stress test for that. The autopilot drive looks like the theory's best case (no second-level representation, no consciousness, checks out) but the unnoticed headache looks like a problem for it (conscious pain, no attention paid to it whatsoever). Unless noticing isn't the same thing as representing, which is where I lose the thread.

So: does the general phenomenon -- a state that's clearly doing experiential work while the person has no awareness of having it, discovered only in retrospect or inferred from behavior -- have an established name? And how does the headache case get handled by the represent-your-own-states theories, because it seems like a counterexample but presumably smart people have noticed headaches exist. If there's no settled term for the pattern itself, feel free to just propose one with a clean definition. Don't just give me a label though, I want to know where the edges of the phenomenon are -- like, does it cover blindsight-type cases too or is that a different animal.

Also: please say how confident you are in each part of the answer, and flag the single claim you're least sure about, since I know this corner of the literature has a lot of internal disagreement and I'd rather know where the shaky ground is.
