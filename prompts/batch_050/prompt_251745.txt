ok so last night I had this massive arguement with my friend Tomas (he's a Harsanyi guy, I'm more sympathetic to Rawls) and he laid out this thing that I can't crack. he numbered it and everything, the smug bastard. here it is, roughly as he gave it:

(1) Behind the veil of ignorance you have no probabilities about which social position you'll end up in — that's the whole point of the veil.
(2) When you genuinely lack probabilities, the rational rule is maximin: pick the option whose worst possible outcome is least bad. That's what Rawls has the parties do, and it's what gets you the difference principle instead of average utility.
(3) So maximin is the rational policy for choice under ignorance, full stop.
(4) But in ordinary life we basically never have real probabilities either. Nobody knows the actual probability that THEIR startup fails, THEIR marriage lasts, THEIR flight goes down. Actuaries have stats about populations, sure, but nobody has a probability for your specific case.
(5) So ordinary life is also choice under ignorance, and maximin governs it too.
(6) Maximin compares worst cases. The worst case of quitting your job to start the company is ruin; the worst case of keeping the job is a tolerable floor. The worst case of getting on the plane is death; staying home has nothing like that.
(7) So rationality demands near-total paralysis: never quit, never fly, never have kids, never move cities.
(8) (7) is insane, so something upstream has to die, and Tomas says the natural casualty is the idea that ignorance rationally demands maximin — i.e. exactly the machinery Rawls needs in the original position — which hands the whole Rawls vs Harsanyi fight to Harsanyi.

I feel like (4) is doing something sneaky but I can't pin it down, and honestly (6) smells too. Which step do I actually reject, why did it look fine when he said it, and what does rejecting it cost me? Specifically: if I save maximin for the original position but deny it in daily life, am I just gerrymandering the rule to protect Rawls, or is there a principled difference between the two kinds of ignorance?

Two constraints. First, if I have to choose, I'd rather keep the veil plus maximin for the original position than accept (7) — but tell me what I'm paying for that. Second, at some point Tomas tried swapping in minimax regret instead of maximin and got different verdicts on the same cases, and neither of us could even say which rule was in play anymore — so explain what each rule is actually responding to and whether his fallback ("no probabilities just means equal probabilities, so maximize expected utility, welcome to average utilitarianism") really follows if I give up (2) or (3).
