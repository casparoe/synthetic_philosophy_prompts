So I just got out of a genuinely exhausting argument with my friend Marcus (like four hours, started at dinner, ended on his porch) about whether having kids is morally neutral. My position is roughly Benatar's asymmetry thing: the presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure is good, but *absent* pain is good even if nobody exists to enjoy it, whereas absent pleasure is only bad if someone exists to be deprived of it. So coming into existence is always, on net, worse than never existing, even for a happy person, and procreation always needs justifying rather than being the default. Marcus kept doing the move where he says his daughter is obviously glad to be alive and waves his hands like that's checkmate, which I think conflates the value of a life once it exists with whether starting it was a benefit, but I admit I got a bit tangled when he asked me who exactly is harmed in the non-existence scenario. Which, fine, that's the whole crux.

Anyway, the reason this matters beyond my friendship: in two weeks I'm presenting this view at our department's grad colloquium, and the audience is not Marcus. There's two people who work on population ethics and know the non-identity literature cold (one of them has said in seminar that asymmetry views just push the repugnant conclusion around rather than solving anything), a Kantian who thinks the whole framing is a category error because you can't do welfare comparisons with non-existence, and then a chunk of non-specialists, at least two of whom are recent parents and will hear this as a personal attack no matter how I frame it. I want a prep sheet, not a pep talk. Give me the objections I'll actually get, ordered by how much damage they do, phrased the way these specific people would phrase them (the population ethics person will say things like "comparative evaluation across non-existence," the Kantian will not), each with a reply I can deliver in about two minutes and a note on where the reply is soft. I especially need help on the "who is made worse off" objection, since Marcus already drew blood with it, and on whether my view really does imply that creating a life with one single pinprick of pain was wrong, because I can feel that one coming from the back row.
