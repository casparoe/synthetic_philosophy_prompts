Longtime reader of the rationalist blogs, first-time house bureaucrat. I share a twelve-person community house in Manchester, and after one too many dreadful meetings I’ve been asked to rewrite our house charter before Monday.

The fight is about how we’re allowed to talk. Some people constantly say things like: the house wants quieter weekdays, the house is drifting towards being a party venue, or the house punished Sam by not renewing his room. They’ve built a genuinely useful dashboard from rent payments, cleaning rotas, guest logs and complaint messages, and it predicts recurring blow-ups better than any individual’s impressions. On their view, house-level talk is just a compressed model of a stable pattern.

Two of the more engineerish residents think this is becoming mystical nonsense. They say there is no house, only twelve people, a lease, some radiators, several WhatsApp groups and bad incentives. Their proposed rule is that every charter claim must name an actual person or mechanism: Alice failed to buy bins, the deposit rules reward over-reporting damage, and so on.

The practical problem is that both versions misbehave. The house-level version lets people dodge responsibility — nobody decided to lock the thermostat, the house merely prefers 17 degrees. But the mechanism-only version becomes absurdly long and sometimes worse at prediction. Saying the house has become conflict-averse really did forecast that three seperate disputes would be settled by quietly freezing out the unpopular person.

I’m tempted by a principle I’ve been calling abstraction debt: a useful high-level shorthand may stay in the charter only while it saves more confusion than it creates, and it has to be paid down whenever someone uses it to assign blame, rights or duties. Is that a sensible rule, or does it smuggle in the mistake it’s meant to prevent?

What should I put in the charter? I don’t just want a taxonomy. Give me a test for when this shorthand is tracking something real, when we should drop to intentions and incentives, when to drop further to mechanisms, and how to stop a handy description from quietly turning into a fictitious moral agent that everyone can hide behind.
