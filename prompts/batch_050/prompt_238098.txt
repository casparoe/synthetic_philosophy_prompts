I'm a junior in high school, and over the past year I've fallen into an argument with my best friend that neither of us can settle, so I'd like an outside perspective. After watching a documentary about global poverty, I started tutoring younger kids for money and donating nearly everything I earn, minus bus fare and school supplies. I've worked out roughly how much good each dollar does, and once you see the exchange rate between my small luxuries and what the same money accomplishes elsewhere, it's hard to unsee. So I skipped our school's spring trip and donated what it would have cost, I stopped going to movies and buying new clothes, and I've started thinking about choosing a college major purely on the basis of which career would let me earn and give away the most. None of this feels like a sacrifice exactly; it feels like finally being consistent.

My friend says I've become a worse person to be around, not a better one. Her argument, as I understand it, has three parts: that I'm visibly less happy and more tense than I used to be; that the way I live implicitly condemns everyone who doesn't live that way, so hanging out with me now feels like being graded; and that a life organized entirely around one project of helping, with no room for music, goofing off, or interests pursued just for their own sake, isn't a good human life even if it's a morally productive one. She says nobody should aspire to become that kind of person, and that a world where everyone did would be unbearably dull even if it were fairer. My parents separately worry I'll burn out before I'm twenty. I think all of them are dodging the real point: people are suffering, my sacrifices are small by comparison, and "I would find that life unpleasant" isn't a moral argument. But I'll admit I can't articulate why her third point feels wrong to me, rather than merely inconvenient for my view.

Can you help me think through who's right? I don't want generic advice about finding balance or being kind to myself. I want to know whether the kind of life I'm aiming at -- maximal moral effectiveness, with everything else treated as optional -- is actually an ideal worth aspiring to, or whether there's a principled objection to it that isn't just comfort-seeking, and what the strongest version of that objection would look like. If such an objection exists, I'd also like to know whether it implies I should simply dial things back a little, or something more interesting than that.
