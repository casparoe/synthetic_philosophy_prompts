Ok so context: I'm a philosophy prof and in about three weeks I'm giving a talk at a small workshop on ethics and cosmology. Audience is maybe twenty people. Mostly moral philosophers, specifically the population ethics crowd (Parfit readers, people who've spent years on the repugnant conclusion and mere addition), plus two cosmologists and one formal epistemologist who works on decision theory with unbounded utilities. Nobody openly hostile, but the population ethics folks have very strong priors and the physicists will pounce on anything sloppy I say about infinity.

My thesis: total utilitarianism can be saved from infinitarian paralysis — the problem that in an infinite universe every action plausibly has the same infinite expected value, so no choice matters more than any other — by restricting moral comparison to the causal future of the agent. On the view I'm defending, which I call horizon-restricted aggregation, two actions differ morally only insofar as their consequences *inside our future light cone* differ; anything outside it (other galaxies we'll never reach, other Everett branches, whatever) just cancels out. I argue this keeps the spirit of total utilitarianism, escapes paralysis, and doesn't smuggle in discounting by time or distance within the cone.
