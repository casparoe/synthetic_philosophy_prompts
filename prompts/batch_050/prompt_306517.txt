I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, and I've gotten interested in sortition -- selecting political officials by lottery -- partly because random sampling is something we actually use in my field (load balancing, A/B testing, sampling from streams) and partly because it seems to cut against the intuition that legitimacy requires consent expressed through voting. Before I read any more proposals for citizens' assemblies or "lottocratic" legislatures, I want to know what I should be asking of a theory here.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of legitimate political selection by lot has to deliver. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it. Some things I'd want addressed, though you should reorganize, extend, or reject these as you see fit:

- It must explain why Athenian selection of magistrates by lot and modern jury selection seem (to many) defensible, while selecting the head of a nuclear regulatory agency by lottery seems reckless -- or else deny that intuition and own the consequence.
- It must handle the statistical worry that a randomly drawn body will sometimes be wildly unrepresentative by pure bad luck, and say whether stratified sampling (which we use constantly in engineering) fixes this or betrays the point of randomness.
- It must say whether equal probability of selection is itself a form of political equality, or whether it actually conflicts with one-person-one-vote equality, since in an election everyone affects the outcome but in a lottery almost nobody does.
- It must address competence and filtering: whether screening the pool (education requirements, opting in, exclusion of the clearly unqualified) undermines whatever made the lottery legitimate in the first place.
- It must distinguish descriptive representation (the body looks like the population) from the claim that the selected actually make good decisions, and say which one the legitimacy rests on.

Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- that's the part I most care about -- and briefly note how the main contenders (pure sortition, mixed sortition-plus-election systems, and lottocracy as a full replacement for elections) fare against the list. A table for the scorecard is fine. Please write in American English.
