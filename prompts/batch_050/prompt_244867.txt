Just spent the whole evening in an arguement with my mate Dan and I need someone to settle it. He manages a little electronics shop and last month someone nicked a load of phones while Dan was in the stockroom. He watched it happen on the CCTV monitor, live, and told the police he saw the guy do it. At the pub I said he didn't see anything — he saw a screen, and if the feed had been pre-recorded or doctored he'd have sworn to something that never happened. He reckons that's mad: by my logic nobody's ever seen anything through a telescope or binoculars, I didn't see the cup final cos I watched it on telly, and a keyhole surgeon operating via camera never sees the patient. Fair enough, but then where's the line? Does it matter that his feed was live? Would a twenty-second delay change anything? If all he had was one still frame of the guy mid-theft, does that count as seeing the theft, or does it have to be moving footage? What if the camera quietly AI-sharpens the image, like some of them do now? And obviously a police sketch artist's drawing doesn't mean the artist saw anything — but why not, if a photo does? I still feel there's a real difference between seeing the man and seeing a picture of him, but every line I draw Dan pokes a hole in. So who's right? And if neither of us is, what would a principled answer even look like?
