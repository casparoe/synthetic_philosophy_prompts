I chair the label-review committee at a mid-sized natural history museum, and we're deadlocked over the wording of species descriptions on exhibit signage. The disputed labels are generics: "Penguins live in Antarctica," "Mosquitoes carry disease," "Ducks lay eggs." The scientists on the committee object that each is false of plenty of individuals (chicks, the vast majority of mosquito species and individuals that transmit nothing, males) and want every label hedged with qualifiers like "most adult females of some species." The educators object that signage bristling with hedges teaches visitors nothing and that qualified generics like "some mosquitoes carry disease" are actually misleading in the other direction, since they suggest rarity where the risk-relevant truth is that the dangerous bite is common enough to act on. Draft the memo I'll circulate before our next meeting: it should recommend a concrete policy for when exhibit generics may stand unqualified and when they must be hedged, state the criterion in a form staff can apply to new labels without re-litigating the philosophy, and take seriously the strongest argument on the side the policy rejects. At the end, say how confident you are that the recommended criterion is the right one, and name the single point in the memo you're least sure of.
