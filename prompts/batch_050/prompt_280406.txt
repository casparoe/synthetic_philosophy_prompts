Suppose an interdisciplinary research group has three years to investigate the symbol grounding problem in artificial systems. Its aim is not to improve benchmark performance, but to determine when an internal state or output token genuinely refers to something in the world rather than merely participates in correlations that are useful to us.

The group can build and manipulate three kinds of systems:

1. A model trained first on text and later connected to cameras, microphones, and robot actuators while different parts of its original network are frozen or allowed to change.
2. An agent trained from the beginning through sensorimotor interaction, including the ability to experiment on its environment rather than merely receive sensory input.
3. A text-trained model given detailed records of other agents’ interventions and observations, but no direct sensors or actuators of its own.

The investigators can inspect and alter internal activations, construct counterfactual environments, control what information caused which representations to form, and test whether representations remain stable under changes in sensors, goals, linguistic conventions, and downstream tasks.

Which unresolved questions should they put first? Consider at least the following, though you may argue that some are badly framed or replace them with better ones:

- What is the relevant bearer of grounding: a token type, a particular token use, an internal state, a distributed pattern, or the system as a whole?
- Should grounding depend primarily on causal history, present covariation, counterfactual responsiveness, successful intervention, or some combination of these?
- What must be true for a system not merely to classify reliably but to misrepresent—so that there is a fact about what it meant independently of whether its output is useful?
- How does grounding survive composition, abstraction, metaphor, and reference to absent or unobservable things?
- Are agency and embodiment constitutive of grounding, important routes to it, or contingent aids?
- What would count as evidence for grounding in an opaque system, and how can that evidence be separated from human interpretive projection?

Produce a dependency-aware research agenda for the three years. State your criteria for prioritization—such as how many other questions a question is upstream of, its tractability, and whether answering it would alter the experimental design—and defend the resulting order. Identify at least one question that sounds central but should be deferred because its answer would not yet change what the group does. Include the decisive experiments or conceptual results that could move an item up or down the ranking. Do not provide a survey or reading list; provide a defended plan of work.
