I've been going back and forth about quantum suicide and quantum immortality with people on the forum and I've realised half the arguments are just people quietly disagreeing about five different things at once (MWI vs collapse, whether anthropic reasoning over branches even makes sense, what "you" surviving means, etc.). So write me a decision tree I can link people to before these threads start. It should take someone from "I'm genuinely unsure between many-worlds and collapse" to a named position on whether quantum suicide works and whether quantum immortality follows from it.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names. I want branch nodes like: does MWI hold at all; given MWI, is self-locating uncertainty over branches a coherent notion or is the whole question malformed; does your survival require *some* branch where you live or enough Born-measure of branches where you live (this is where the "you always find yourself alive" view comes apart from the "you almost certainly die, just in a multiverse" view); and whether death-in-the-experiment is instantaneous, since that's where the classic objection about gradual degradation and aging does its work. Phrase each node as a question someone could answer without already knowing which position it sorts them into, and put a concrete case at each node where the positions genuinely diverge (the idealised instantaneous-death machine, the botched version that mostly leaves you maimed, ordinary aging).

Leaves should get position names where they exist and "unnamed" where they don't, plus one sentence each on the leaf's most awkward commitment (e.g. the immortality view seems stuck saying you'd survive aging; the Born-measure view has to say something odd about why low-measure survival doesn't count). Note any empty combos and whether they're empty because incoherent or just undefended, and flag any pairs of leaves the tree can't really tell apart -- I suspect the anthropic-reasoning variants (SSA-flavoured vs SIA-flavoured self-selection) collapse into each other at some point but I'm not sure where.

Two asks on how you pitch it: write the answer in British English please, and be upfront about which nodes rest on settled physics (decoherence, the Born rule as used in practice) versus which are genuinely contested among people who actually work on this (anthropic reasoning, personal identity across branches, whether measure-based survival is survival). Don't present a contested anthropic view as the multiverse consensus.
