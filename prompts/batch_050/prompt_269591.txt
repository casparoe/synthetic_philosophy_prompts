I hold a narrow person-affecting view of ethics: an action cannot be morally wrong unless it is worse for some person who does, did, or will exist. Accordingly, I bite the bullet on the nonidentity problem. When a couple conceives a child now rather than waiting, knowing the child conceived now will have a painful but clearly worth-living life, the choice is regrettable but not wrong, since no one is made worse off than they would otherwise have been. I take the same line on policy: if our energy choices today determine which people will exist in three centuries, then leaving a degraded planet to those future people wrongs no one.

What would count against this position? I want a map of its exposure, with defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Please cover at least the following, and add what I have missed.

First, consider what I call identity laundering. A polluter can release a toxin into a water supply either before or after a particular child is conceived; the dose, the suffering, and the child's life are identical either way, but my view condemns only the later release, since the earlier one helped determine that this child, rather than another, exists. If the wrongness of producing a fixed amount of suffering should not depend on the causal routing that produces it, this is pressure against the view. How much pressure, and is the routing intuition itself stable under reflection?

Second, assess the standard challengers: the intuition that deliberately selecting a disabled embryo over a healthy one is wrong even when the disabled embryo's life is worth living; the procreation asymmetry (we seem forbidden to create miserable lives but not obliged to create happy ones); and whether the view can underwrite any duties regarding climate or long-term policy.

Third, address the defensive maneuvers. Wide person-affecting variants say an act is wrong if it is worse for whoever would exist under each alternative. Does that rescue survive, or does it collapse into impersonalism in disguise? Has my position been qualified into something that no case could touch -- and if so, is that a defect for a view of this kind, or the natural resting state of a metaphysical commitment?

Finally, distinguish what defenders of the view would themselves concede counts against it from what only its critics regard as damaging.
