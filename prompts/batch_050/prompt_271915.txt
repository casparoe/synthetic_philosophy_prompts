For a graduate seminar, I want the requirements a theory of habitat destruction and wild-animal welfare would have to meet before it could tell us whether destroying habitat reduces suffering overall. Don’t just assume wild lives are bad or that less habitat automatically means less suffering. Distinguish harms to animals already alive from the prevention of future lives, short-term from long-term effects, total from average welfare, and suffering from mere population size. The list should also cover population turnover, trophic effects, the relevant counterfactual, uncertainty about welfare, and when destroying habitat creates new niches. Explain the case behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable versus which look like welfarist or environmentalist prejudices, and flag any requirements that seem jointly impossible to satisfy. If one requirement rules out most simplistic answers, call that out too. A table would be fine.
