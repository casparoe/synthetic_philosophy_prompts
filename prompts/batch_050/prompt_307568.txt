So I'm in an experimental philosophy seminar this term and we just did the Knobe effect (the chairman harm/help cases where people say he "intended" the harm but not the help, even though he didn't care about either). Our final paper has to take a position on what the effect actually shows, and I want to argue for my own reading instead of just summarizing the debate. Write an essay for me that does the following.

First, lay out the two main families of explanation: (a) the effect reveals that our concept of intentional action is genuinely moralized, so whether something counts as "intentional" partly depends on whether it's good or bad, and (b) the effect is a pragmatic artifact, i.e. people say "intentional" to blame the chairman, but their underlying judgment is just about foresight and not about intention at all.

Then here's the part I want the essay to actually engage with: in class I proposed a variant case that (as far as I know) nobody has run. Imagine the chairman's program both harms the environment AND harms the company's long-term profits, and he says "I don't care about either, I just want the quarterly numbers up." Both outcomes are bad, but only the environment one is *morally* bad -- the profit harm is just him being bad at his job. My prediction is people would still say he intended the environmental harm but NOT the profit harm, even though he foresaw both equally and both are bad outcomes. If that's right, I think it splits the difference between the two families in a specific way: it shows intentionality judgments track *moral* badness specifically, not just blameworthiness or badness generally, which the pragmatic story has trouble with (why would people want to "blame" him for the environmental outcome more when he's equally at fault for both?), but which also means the concept isn't just sensitive to generic norm violations.

The essay should test my variant case against both families of explanation, consider the strongest objection (maybe: the profit harm is weirdly self-directed, so it doesn't even fit the structure of Knobe cases, and my case fails to isolate moral badness after all), and then reach a definite conclusion about what the Knobe effect shows about the concept of intentional action. Don't just survey the positions and shrug -- commit to a view, even if it's a modified version of one of the existing ones, and give actual reasons for why the alternatives fail. If you think my variant case doesn't do the work I think it does, say so directly and argue for what the effect shows instead. Around 1200 words.
