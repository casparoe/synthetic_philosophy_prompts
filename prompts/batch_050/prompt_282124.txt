Write a dialogue between two people arguing about a case I've been chewing on. Case: Maya's smart-fridge app tells her there's milk in the fridge. The app is usually reliable and she has no reason to doubt it, so she believes there's milk. Turns out the app glitched -- it was showing a cached inventory from three days ago, when the fridge did have milk. But by pure coincidence her roommate bought milk this morning, so there IS milk in the fridge right now. So: justified belief, true belief, but it sure doesn't feel like knowledge.

Character one thinks the fix is easy: knowledge is justified true belief plus some extra condition that rules out cases like this (like "your belief can't essentially depend on a falsehood" or "no luck"), and we just need to find the right condition. Character two thinks the whole project is backwards -- you can't build knowledge out of belief plus ingredients, knowledge is the basic thing and belief should be understood in terms of it, and piling on conditions will always spring a leak somewhere.

Have them actually engage with each other, not give speeches. Character one should try at least two different extra conditions and character two should show how each one either fails on Maya's case or invites a new counterexample. Character two should have to answer the obvious pushback: if knowledge can't be analyzed, how do you explain WHY the milk case goes wrong? Neither of them should be a dummy. Medium length, natural speech.
