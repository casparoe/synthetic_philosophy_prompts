In the Definitions at the start of the Principia, Newton writes: "The vis insita, or innate force of matter, is a power of resisting, by which every body, as much as in it lies, endeavours to persevere in its present state, whether it be of rest, or of moving uniformly forward in a right line." This is the notion doing the real work in the bucket argument a few pages later -- the water climbing the sides is supposed to show an "endeavour" to recede from the axis that no merely relative motion can explain. I'm organizing a semester-long reading group for philosophy grad students (a couple have some physics background, most don't) on the absolute vs. relational space debate, and we can only do justice to maybe two or three of the live open questions. Given that Newton's whole case rests on treating inertial effects as windows onto something beyond relative motion, wich questions most deserve our sessions, and in what order? The candidates on my list: whether a Mach-style relationism can actually recover inertial effects without positing absolute structure; whether the modern reframing in terms of spacetime structure (neo-Newtonian or Newton-Cartan geometry) shows the fight was never really about "substance" at all; whether the hole argument in general relativity sinks substantivalism or only a naive version of it; and whether the entire dispute is underdetermined by physics and therefore partly verbal. I care less about the list itself than the reasoning: for each question, how upstream it is of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers in one semester without producing new physics, and what developments -- in philosophy or in physics -- would move it up or down the ranking. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating the question.
