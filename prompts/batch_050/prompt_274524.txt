So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, and I got nerd-sniped by a blog post about deflationary theories of truth and ended up skimming Frege's essay Der Gedanke (1918) in translation. Near the beginning he makes this point that sticking "it is true that" in front of a sentence doesn't change what you're asserting -- saying it is true that sea water is salty is just saying that sea water is salty, full stop. The extra words add nothing.

Ok so from where I sit, that makes truth a no-op. It's the identity function. `true(p) == p`. And we all know what you do with a function that's literally just `return x`: you inline it everywhere and delete it. Dead code.

So my questions:

1. If the truth predicate contributes nothing, why can't we just refactor it out of the language entirely? I tried this mentally and hit a snag with stuff like "everything in section 3 of the RFC is true" or "whatever the error log says is true", where there doesn't seem to be a sentence to inline back in. Does the deflationist just... not have those sentences? That seems like a pretty big feature to lose, so I assume I'm misunderstanding something.

2. Bigger confusion: if the predicate is redundant, doesn't that mean truth is just a grammatical convention with nothing underneath? Like, we agreed as a speech community to have this word, and that's all there is to it? I keep wanting to conclude from Frege's point that there's no objective truth about anything, that "truth" is just a flag we flip on sentences we like. But people who hold this view seem to think they're NOT relativists, and I can't see how they pull that off. If asserting p and asserting "p is true" are the same move, then how is truth any more objective than assertion is?

3. Also, is this redundancy thing actually what Frege believed? The blog called him an ancestor of deflationism, but the essay also seemed to say truth can't even be defined, which sounds like the opposite of "truth is trivial". I might be conflating two seperate points he makes.

Basically: is truth a no-op, and if so why can't I delete it, and if I can't delete it then in what sense was it ever a no-op?
