A hospital deploys an AI chatbot to handle patient correspondence. In the course of an exchange, the chatbot writes: "If you submit the missing form by Friday, I promise we will process your application by Monday." The patient submits the form on Friday; the application is not processed on Monday.

Please defend the following position: the chatbot's utterance was a genuine promise, and it binds the hospital, despite the chatbot's lacking beliefs and intentions. In doing so, work explicitly through Austin's felicity conditions for performative utterances — the A conditions (an accepted conventional procedure invoked by appropriate persons in appropriate circumstances), the B conditions (correct and complete execution of the procedure), and the Γ conditions (requisite intentions and subsequent conduct) — and explain why apparent failures here should be classified as abuses, which leave the act in force, rather than as misfires, which render it void. Address the strongest version of the objection that promising essentially requires an intention no such system can have, and clarify whether the resulting promise, if genuine, belongs to the chatbot, to the hospital, or somehow to both. Conclude by stating which consideration would most plausibly overturn your verdict: what would have to be true of promising, or of systems like this chatbot, for the opposite conclusion to follow, and where your argument is most vulnerable to that consideration.
