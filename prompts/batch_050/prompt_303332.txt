I am a PhD student in philosophy of action helping design the graduate comprehensive exam in free will and moral responsibility. Write one essay question on Galen Strawson’s Basic Argument that could be answered in 90 minutes but not by merely reciting the regress argument. The question should center on an original case in which adults receive three opportunities during their lives to revise one fundamental value or character trait, while each revision is necessarily made from the evaluative standpoint they already possess. Candidates should have to determine whether such partial self-revision breaks the regress and whether responsibility for action requires responsibility for the standpoint from which one acts. The question should separately test their understanding of: (1) determination by one’s present character, (2) control over the formation of that character, (3) sourcehood versus reasons-responsiveness, and (4) whether responsibility can be genuine without being ultimate.

Provide a model answer of 700–900 words and a 25-point marking scheme organized around five must-hit points, specifying the marks for each, the misunderstanding each point is intended to catch, and the specific moves distinguishing an excellent answer from a merely competent one. In the model answer, distinguish what is broadly settled—the structure of Strawson’s regress and its focus on an especially strong, ultimate form of responsibility—from what remains genuinely contested, including whether responsibility requires self-creation, whether the regress is vicious, and whether compatibilist accounts successfully reject its ultimacy condition. Do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus.
