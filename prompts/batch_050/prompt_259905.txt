It started over lab-grown meat: I said I'd feel gross about eating meat grown from human cells even if it was totally harmless and consensually sourced, she said that reaction is just a leftover bias from evolution and I should work on getting rid of it, and I said no, it's just a preference I have, and preferences don't need to justify themselves. Then we realized we couldn't even say what would settle it. Every test one of us proposed either counted all gut reactions as biases or none of them.

So: construct a thought experiment with two characters who have the identical negative gut reaction to the same thing, but where one of them clearly has a mere bias and the other clearly has a genuine preference. The catch is you can't just stipulate which is which -- the difference has to fall out of something structural about how each character's reaction behaves (how it responds to new information, to learning where it came from, to reflection, to hypothetical variations in the scenario, whatever you think does the job). Constraints: realistic setting, no pills that rewrite your values, no brain implants, no aliens; the reaction should be about something morally loaded but not a live political wedge issue, so no abortion, no guns; and each character should be describable in a few sentences.

After the scenario, do the work: pick two plausible criteria for the bias/preference line (for instance, whether the reaction would survive learning its own causal history, versus whether the person applies it consistently across cases that differ only in irrelevant surface features -- or two others you like better) and show that both criteria deliver the same verdict on your two characters. Then describe a third character, or a tweak to one of the originals, where the two criteria come apart, and tell me which criterion you'd trust in that harder case and why. If it turns out one of the criteria secretly presupposes the other, say that instead -- I mainly want to know what I should actually say to my roommate, since "it's just a preference" is starting to feel like cheating.
