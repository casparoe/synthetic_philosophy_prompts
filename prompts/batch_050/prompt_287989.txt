I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I keep running into something while reporting on AI assistants that I dont have a good word for. I'm curious whether it already has a name.

Here's the pattern. I'll ask a chatbot about, say, whether some supplement is worth taking, and everything it says checks out -- each individual claim is accurate, appropriately hedged, the studies it mentions are real. But the overall picture it leaves you with is skewed, because of what it chose to surface and what it left in the basement. Three paragraphs on the two small positive trials, one sentence on the bigger null result. Or I ask it to compare two products and it answers every question truthfully, but it picks the comparison axes -- battery life, build quality, resale value -- that happen to favor one of them. No single sentence is a lie. You could fact-check the whole reply line by line and it would pass. And yet you walk away with the wrong impression.

The thing that makes me think this isn't just one of the usual suspects: as far as anyone can tell, nobody is deceiving anybody. The system is optimizing for being helpful, or for keeping you engaged, and the skewed selection is a byproduct. Every concept I half-remember from journalism school or from reading about the ethics of communication seems to smuggle in a deceiver -- someone who knows the truth and wants to steer you away from it. Here there's no one home in that sense, and I suspect that matters for what we should call it and how worried to be.

So: does this exact configuration -- strictly true statements, distorted overall picture, no intending deceiver -- have an established name? I know there are adjacent ideas floating around in rhetoric, in media studies, in the philosophy of language. But I want you to actually commit: either tell me which existing concept genuinely covers this and explain why the near-misses don't, or, if none of them really fits, say so plainly and coin a term -- define it precisely, and give me the boundary cases (does it still count if the selection is random rather than optimized? if the user could have asked a follow-up question? if the impression the answer leaves happens to be correct?). Distinguish it from its closest neighbors while you're at it.

One more thing: please don't give me a tour of five candidates and leave me to pick my favorite. Take a definite position and defend it. And write it in American English, since this may end up feeding into a piece for a US audience.
