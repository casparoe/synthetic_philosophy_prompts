Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers in my department who've been going at it all semester. One is a continuist about moral status: sentience comes in degrees, moral status roughly tracks those degrees, so a fruit fly plausibly has a tiny but nonzero claim on us, and there is no sharp line anywhere between insects and thermostats, only steadily diminishing amounts of whatever makes something matter. The other is a thresholder: moral status exists only where there is genuinely valenced experience, something that is good or bad for the subject, and below that bar there is not a small amount of status but none. On her view, talk of faint status just confuses the probability that a system has status with a degree of status.

The document they should produce, and that I want you to write for them, has four parts:

1. A list of at least four claims both can honestly sign. They should be substantive, not vacuous. Things in the neighborhood of: nociception by itself isn't sufficient for suffering, behavioral complexity by itself isn't evidence of valence, our evidence about insect minds is genuinely thin. Find the real shared ground, don't fake it.

2. A numbered list of the remaining disagreements, each worded neutrally enough that both parties would except the statement of their own position. Where a disagreement looks like it might be purely verbal (I suspect some of the degree talk vs probability talk is), flag it as such and say what the non-verbal residue is, if any.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that if one side were convinced, they'd actually have to move. I suspect one crux is whether being a subject of experience can itself come in degrees — if subjecthood is all-or-nothing, the continuist's smooth continuum has gaps in it. Another is whether low credence in insect sentience spread across vast numbers of insects aggregates the same way certain-but-faint status would, since the two views seem to come apart there. And a third might be whether the thermostat case is a bullet the continuist is genuinely willing to bite, or whether their view secretly has a floor too, in which case the dispute is about where the floor is rather than whether there is one.

4. For each crux, a concrete case, thought experiment, or empirical finding that both parties agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. For example, if it turned out that insect nociception is handled entirely by distributed ganglia with no central integration anywhere, what would each of them say follows for their view? The predictions need to be specific enough that you could later say who, if anyone, got surprised.

Just give me the document itself, starting from its title or first section heading — no preamble, no commentary about what you're doing.
