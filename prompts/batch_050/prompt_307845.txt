I'm a software engineer (backend, mostly) with zero philosophy training, but I listened to a podcast about how people categorize stuff -- prototype theory, exemplar theory, something called "theory theory" -- and it maps weirdly well onto fights my team keeps having about our product taxonomy. So I wrote down my actual gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and I want to know what they add up to, because right now they feel inconsistent.

- An ostrich is definately a bird. Doesn't fly, looks nothing like a sparrow, doesn't matter, still 100% a bird.
- A robot that walks, barks, fetches, and cuddles exactly like a golden retriever is not a dog. Not even a little bit, no matter how good the fur gets.
- I was completely fine with Pluto getting demoted from planet once astronomers explained the reasoning. Didn't feel like losing anything, felt like fixing a mislabeled enum.
- A tomato is a vegetable. I know the botany answer and I don't care -- in the kitchen and the grocery store it's a vegetable, full stop. But if a biologist calls it a fruit in a paper, that's also correct and I wouldn't say she's wrong. Both, somehow.
- My concept of "chair" is not just a list of chairs I've sat on, because I saw this bizarre art gallery thing last month, looked like a crumpled sheet of metal, and I instantly knew it was a chair because it was clearly built for sitting.
- But my concept of "dog" is honestly anchored to my childhood dog specifically. There's no checklist in my head, he's the standard and everything else is doggish to the degree it resembles him.
- A taxidermied duck is still a duck (a duck, deceased). A perfect wooden decoy never was one, even though the whole point of decoys is that actual ducks can't tell.
- When a junior dev names a class UserAccount that also handles billing, my objection isn't "this doesn't resemble a typical user account," it's more like "your underlying picture of what a user account IS is wrong." Not sure what that distinction even is but it feels real.

So my questions: is there one coherent theory of concepts behind all of these, or am I just running different theories in different domains and that's the honest answer? Which verdicts would a prototype person say I have to abandon, which ones sink exemplar theory, and can the theory theory actually make sense of the tomato thing being context-dependent instead of just declaring one side correct? Give me your best reconstruction, tell me which of my gut calls it forces me to drop, and then tell me what it predicts for the case I genuinely cannot settle: is a hot dog a sandwich? My answer flips depending on whether I've been thinking about menus or about ontology, and I'd like to know if that instability is itself data or just me being confused.
