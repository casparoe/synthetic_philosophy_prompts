I just had a long arguement with a friend about Wittgenstein’s On Certainty, and we got stuck on what a hinge is supposed to be. My view was that things like there being an external world, or my not having been switched with a stranger in my sleep, aren’t super-confident empirical beliefs. They’re the background conditions that make doubting, testing, and giving evidence possible, so calling them knowledge sounds like a category mistake. My friend said that makes hinges sound weirdly untouchable. On her view, they’re just deeply entrenched beliefs that normally go unquestioned, but they can become ordinary hypotheses when practices change. I said that if something is actually being tested, it isn’t functioning as a hinge anymore; she said that was a no-true-hinge move unless I could explain the difference without simply defining hinges as untestable.

Please compare our two accounts and adjudicate the disagreement. In particular: are hinges beliefs, something more practical, or something else? Can a hinge be true or false, known or mistaken? And can the same proposition be a hinge for one person or community while being an empirical question for another?
