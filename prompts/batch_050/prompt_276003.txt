Having spent my career in molecular biology, I have only recently taken up philosophy, and I am struck by how much of the bioethics literature presupposes a boundary between therapy and enhancement without agreeing on where it falls. Before reading further, I would like the specification first. Write the list of desiderata that any proposed criterion for drawing the therapy/enhancement distinction must satisfy in order to be adequate for practical use — say, in deciding what a health system must fund or what an athletic federation may prohibit — with the hard case motivating each requirement (vaccination, cochlear implants, growth hormone for a child of normal but short stature, stimulants taken by a healthy student, statins for the merely at-risk, and the like). For each desideratum, give the consideration behind it and say whether it is a genuine constraint or merely a preference of one camp — those who anchor the line in normal species functioning, those who anchor it in the concept of disease, and those who deny that any principled line exists. In particular, tell me whether any subset of the requirements — for instance, that the criterion track something of genuine moral weight, that it be statable without a controversial theory of human nature, and that it be administrable by clinicians and committees rather than philosophers — is jointly unsatisfiable, since that would itself be the interesting result. A brief verdict on how the main contenders fare against the list is welcome but secondary; the criteria, and their costs, are what I am after.
