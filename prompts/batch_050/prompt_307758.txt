Okay so here's the situation. I'm on an advisory group for a small intentional community (long story, don't ask) that's rewriting its conflict-resolution charter, and the debate has stalled on a weird question: should the community's whole sanction system be built around eliciting guilt or around eliciting shame? Some members want private confession-and-repair rituals (very guilt-coded), others want public acknowledgment ceremonies where the person faces the community (very shame-coded), and everyone keeps citing pop-psych at each other. I told them we're putting the cart before the horse: before picking an emotion to institutionalize, we need to know what a good theory of moral emotions would even have to deliver for this question to be answerable.

So write me that: the desiderata for a theory of guilt, shame, and their moral standing, given that the theory's job is to tell a group whether it can choose its dominant moral emotion and what it would be buying. Some things I already suspect belong on the list: it has to give a principled way to draw the guilt/shame line that survives hard cases (e.g. survivor guilt, where there's no deed; or shame about your body, where there's no victim); it has to explain why guilt seems to push toward repair and shame toward hiding without making that a definitional cheat; it has to be consistent with the psych findings that shame-proneness tracks worse outcomes than guilt-proneness, and explain whether that's about the emotions themselves or about the people prone to them; and crucially it has to say something about the "shame culture vs guilt culture" framing from anthropology -- whether a group-level emotional regime is a real lever you can pull, or whether you just get individual dispositions and the culture stuff is storytelling on top.

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration motivating it, and mark whether it's genuinely non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp (I suspect e.g. "must make guilt come out looking good" is a prejudice of the therapy literature, not a real requirement). The part I most want: if some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable -- like, if you can't simultaneously hold that the emotions are natural kinds with fixed action tendencies AND that a culture can meaningfully choose between them -- say so explicitly, because that result would basically settle our charter fight on its own. If you have energy left at the end, briefly note how a couple of the standard philosophical accounts would fare against your list, but the list itself is the deliverable.
