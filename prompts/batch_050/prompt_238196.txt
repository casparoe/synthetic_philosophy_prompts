So I've been on a bit of a philosophy of math kick lately (hobbyist, not a mathematician, bear with me) and I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a word for. I'd like you to answer as a professional philosopher of mathematics who also knows the history of the field well. Think carefully about this, and maybe start by brainstorming candidate terms and then narrow down, rather than jumping to the first label that comes to mind.

The pattern: a field of math gets hit by a contradiction (or something contradiction-adjacent) at its supposed foundations, there's a brief panic, and then it turns out that almost none of the actual results were lost. The day-to-day practice was never really standing on the shaky foundation in the first place, and the repaired foundation gets reverse-engineered to license what practitioners were already doing anyway. Two instances: naive set theory blows up via Russell's paradox, and ZFC basically keeps everything working mathematicians cared about; infinitesimals get mocked as logically incoherent, and epsilon-delta (and later Robinson's nonstandard analysis) retroactively vindicates nearly all of the old infinitesimal reasoning, including arguments by people who had no coherent story about what they were doing.

Is there an established name for this phenomenon -- the way a body of results turns out to be largely independent of its alleged foundations and survives their replacement? And here's the part that really interests me: people say that if Peano arithmetic turned out to be inconsistent tomorrow, everything would collapse, because from a contradiction anything follows. But given the historical pattern, that prediction seems empirically shaky -- plausibly number theorists would just find which arguments used the inconsistent principle and cordon it off, like they always have. Does *that* sub-phenomenon (the prediction of collapse being at odds with how math has actually behaved) have a name or a literature?

Please don't just give me a bare label. Tell me how what I've described differs from nearby things I half-know about: Lakatos's monster-barring (that seems to be about definitions, not foundations?), paraconsistent logic (that's about tolerating contradictions rather than surviving foundation swaps?), and ordinary scientific theory change, where revolutions actually do throw out practice. And if nothing established fits, coin a term, define it precisely, and give me some boundary cases -- situations that look like my phenomenon but aren't, and vice versa. Also happy to be told I've misdescribed the history somewhere.
