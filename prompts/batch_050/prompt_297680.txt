You are a moral philosopher with expertise in the ethics of communication. Think carefully through the case materials below before answering.

A colleague of mine who works on the ethics of deception has consistent verdicts about cases, and I want to know what she'd say about a new one. Her verdicts so far: a politician who flatly asserts something false to deflect a scandal has done something seriously wrong; a job candidate who says, truthfully, "I've always been passionate about this field," intending the interviewer to draw a stronger conclusion than the words support, has done something mildly wrong at most; a witness who answers a different question than the one asked, hoping the questioner will stop digging, has done something mildly wrong; a person who stays silent while a friend forms a false belief they could easily correct has done something less wrong than any liar. So she clearly grades some deceptive-true-speech cases as far less blameworthy than outright lying.

The new case: a doctor tells a patient's anxious family, truthfully, "the operation went as planned," knowing that in context they will immediately and naturally conclude the patient is fine, when in fact the patient has developed a complication the plan didn't anticipate. The inference is one virtually every hearer would draw; the doctor exploits it deliberately to delay a hard conversation.

My questions: What is her likely verdict, and which principle is she most plausibly running — that lying is wrong because of what is asserted while misleading only invites an inference the hearer co-authors, or that what matters is the speaker's intent to produce a false belief regardless of means, or something else that also fits her track record? Identify at least two principles consistent with all four earlier verdicts that diverge on the new case, and describe one further case that would discriminate between them. Finally, say briefly whether you think the lying/misleading moral distinction she appears to rely on is defensible, or whether it collapses under cases like this one.
