I'm the stats person (by default, not by training) at a small ag-tech company, and I end up refereeing the same fight almost every week: someone fits a curve or builds a little predictive model, someone else adds more parameters/terms/features so it fits better, and then both sides yell "Occam's razor" at each other and mean different things by it. Our datasets are usually small (n in the low hundreds), decisions are things like which sensor model ships or whether a treatment effect is real, and I get maybe 30 minutes per dispute before the team lead has to sign off.

What I want: turn the actual theoretical content behind the razor -- Kolmogorov complexity, minimum description length, the Solomonoff-style prior that penalizes longer programs -- into a procedure I can actually run in that 30 minutes. It obviously can't involve computing Kolmogorov complexity (uncomputable, I know), so tell me what the legitimate proxies are and which common ones are cheating (e.g. is "number of parameters" a defensible stand-in or not, and when). The procedure should say what to do about the fact that description length depends on your choice of language/encoding, how it relates to the things my team already half-uses (AIC/BIC, cross-validation) and when those actually approximate the principle vs. when they don't, and where it has to stop and hand things back to judgment.

Then run it on two of my real recurring cases: (1) choosing the degree of a polynomial for a dose-response curve with n=80, and (2) whether to add a bunch of interaction terms to a churn model that gains a little on held-out data. Include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer people won't like, and be honest about what it can't settle.
