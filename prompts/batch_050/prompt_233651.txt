Two historians I know, a committed Great Man theorist and a diehard structuralist, have been arguing for years about the counterfactual: without Hitler, would Germany still have ended up with something like the Third Reich? I want them to stop rehashing and produce an adversarial-collaboration statement, and I'm asking you to draft it for them. It should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign -- e.g., about Versailles, the Depression, the fragility of Weimar institutions, the fact that völkisch and antisemitic movements predated Hitler, and the availability of other far-right figures (Strasser, Röhm, Ludendorff, Hugenberg) -- worded so neither side can complain of slant; (2) a table of their remaining disagreements, each formulated so that both would accept the wording of their own position as well as the description of the other's; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that resolving them would actually move one or both of them (I'd guess these include: how to read the NSDAP's performance in periods when Hitler was sidelined, whether "Hitler was necessary for Nazism's rise" and "Hitler was necessary for its specific character, e.g. the Holocaust and the timing of the war" are really one question or two, and how much weight to give contingency in the backroom maneuvers of late 1932/early 1933); and (4) for each crux, a piece of historical evidence or a comparison case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about it stated in advance. If some of their disagreement turns out to be verbal -- say, over what "inevitable" or "caused" mean in a counterfactual claim -- flag which part and why. Write it in American English, and keep it to something they could actually co-sign, not a transcript of their feud.
