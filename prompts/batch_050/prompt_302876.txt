i sit on the board of our city's community trust, an endowment of about 40 million created when the city sold the old harbor land. the charter just says the money must serve the residents of the city, present and future, and we have to set a spending policy this quarter. one faction wants a spend-down: distribute nearly everything over the next ten years to the city's poorest neighborhoods, since current residents are measurably worse off than future ones are projected to be. the other side wants the standard 4-5% annual draw forever, which means much smaller transfers now but (on their projections, which assume the fund keeps compounding) substantially larger transfers to future low-income residents.

a board member keeps invoking Rawls: he says we should decide as if none of us knew which generation we'd be born into, and that inequality between generations is permissable if it makes the worst-off generation better off. the spend-down people reply that the worst-off people are alive right now, and the difference principle says maximize their position, full stop. there's also a measurement fight underneath: our grants mostly flow through established neighborhood nonprofits, and some board members argue the truly worst-off residents never see the money anyway, so who even counts as the least advantaged for purposes of this decision is up for grabs.

draft the memo i'll circulate before the vote. it should recommend one concrete policy with actual parameters (a floor, a cap, a review trigger, whatever you can defend), explain what each side gets right and wrong in its use of Rawls -- including whether deciding behind a veil of ignorance is even a workable exercise when everyone at the table knows exactly who they are -- and engage the strongest version of the losing side's case rather than the weakest.
