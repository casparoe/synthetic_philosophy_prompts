For my ethics class, we're studying the idea of mutually beneficial exploitation -- the claim that a transaction can leave both parties better off than they'd otherwise be, and still count as exploitative because of how the gains are divided or what circumstances made one party desperate enough to accept. Write a short story (around 800-1,200 words) that dramatizes this puzzle through a concrete situation: for example, a factory owner in a poor region who offers wages far above any local alternative, and a worker who is genuinely glad to take the job. The story should make it impossible for the reader to settle the question too easily. In particular, I'd like it to do three things:

1. Make the mutual benefit vivid and real, not a technicality -- show what the worker's actual alternatives are, and let the improvement in her life be genuine, so that a reader can't dismiss the arrangement as simply bad for her.
2. Give the owner a moment, even a small one, where he knowingly uses the workers' lack of alternatives as leverage -- so the reader has to confront whether the deal is unfair even though it's welcomed.
3. End without a verdict. The last scene should leave two reasonable interpretations standing side by side: that the owner did something wrong by taking more of the surplus than fairness allows, and that condemning the arrangement would leave the worker worse off than either of her real options.

Please don't have any character state the philosophy directly or mention philosophers by name; the tension should come out of the events and dialogue themselves. Ideally, two thoughtful readers could finish the story, disagree about whether the owner acted wrongly, and both be able to point to specific scenes as evidence.
