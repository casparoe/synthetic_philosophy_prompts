I am a high school student preparing for a biology-and-philosophy seminar, and I built an argument that now seems to prove too much. I would like a diagnosis, not a verdict about evolution in general.

Definitions I am using: call a trait design-explained if its current prevalence is mainly explained by selection for its current effect; call it carried if it persists because of linkage, pleiotropy, developmental constraint, phylogenetic inertia, drift in a small population, or because it is a byproduct later put to use. Call a criticism function-locking if it says: once you cannot prove a selected function, you must stop giving adaptive explanations. Call a theory evidentially indebted if every apparent success for it can be redescribed as an unexamined alternative, so it can never lose.

Argument: (1) A trait may be design-explained, carried, both, or neither; these categories exhaust the options. (2) An adaptationist explanation for a trait is justified only if there is positive evidence that selection favored the trait because of its present effect, not merely that the trait is useful, common, old, or intuitively well engineered. (3) For many traits, the available evidence underdetermines whether they are design-explained or carried: usefulness is compatible with exaptation, commonness with drift plus constraint, and fit with environment with later co-option. (4) The spandrels-style critique says adaptationists too often move from plausible use to selected-for use without ruling out carried explanations. (5) If (2) and (3) are true, then for any particular adaptive story, a critic can always demand one more excluded alternative: another pleiotropic pathway, another ancestral constraint, another demographic history, another developmental correlation. (6) A demand that can always be repeated, no matter what evidence is produced, is function-locking. (7) Function-locking is evidentially indebted, because it makes the anti-adaptationist side incapable of being wrong: every failed adaptive prediction is a carried trait, and every confirmed adaptive prediction is only a case where the carried explanation has not yet been found. (8) An evidentially indebted critique proves either too little, because it never tells us which traits are adaptations, or too much, because it implies no trait is ever knowingly design-explained. (9) Therefore the spandrels critique collapses into a choice between explaining nothing and becoming the adaptationism it opposes.

I dislike the conclusion. I want to keep both that selection is a real and powerful explainer and that not every bump, gap, blood color, chin, or behavioral quirk is for something. But I cannot locate the exact break. Is the problem a quantifier shift in (5) from some cases to every case, a confusion in (7) between high standards and impossible standards, a false dichotomy in (8), or a premise I should simply surrender? Please name the step you reject, explain why it looked compelling, and state what rejecting it costs for someone who still wants a principled way to tell adaptations from spandrels.
