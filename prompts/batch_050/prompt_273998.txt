I am developing a position on Kavka’s toxin puzzle and want a consistency audit of the position itself, not a survey of the literature.

Use the following version of the case. At 23:59, an agent is offered a very large prize if and only if a perfectly reliable intention-certification procedure determines at midnight that she sincerely and unconditionally intends to drink a toxin at noon the next day. The prize is paid irrevocably at 00:05. Neither the payment nor any later benefit depends on whether she actually drinks the toxin. Drinking it would cause two days of severe illness but no permanent injury. The agent knows all of this in advance, and the prize is large enough that acquiring the midnight intention would clearly be worth the expected cost if acquisition were possible. By sincere intention, I mean a settled plan to drink, not pretending, hoping, making a conditional plan, or merely intending to make oneself the sort of person who would drink.

Consider two variants:

1. Public Registry: the certification and the agent’s eventual nonperformance are made public. If she fails to drink, she will predictably lose future opportunities whose value exceeds the cost of the illness.
2. Governor: certification arms a device. If the agent does not voluntarily drink by noon, there is a 25 percent chance that the device will administer a substantially worse toxin. The expected harm of abstaining is therefore greater than the harm of voluntarily drinking.

My current commitments are:

1. In the original case, the agent has a decisive reason at 23:59 to try to acquire the midnight intention.
2. If she succeeds, the midnight intention itself can be fully rational: an intention’s rationality can be inherited from the reason for having that intention.
3. Once formed, an intention always generates a genuine though defeasible reason to perform the intended action.
4. Nevertheless, at noon in the original case, the health-related reasons decisively outweigh whatever reason is generated by the midnight intention, so the rational action is not to drink.
5. A fully informed and practically rational agent can know points 1–4 in advance, know that her future self will act rationally at noon, and still sincerely intend at midnight to drink.
6. Sincerely intending to act does not require high confidence that one will act; an akratic agent can sincerely intend while assigning performance a low probability.
7. There is no bootstrapping of practical reasons: the fact that having an intention would be valuable cannot, merely in virtue of that value, create a reason to perform the intended action.
8. Public Registry makes drinking rational by adding an act-relative penalty for nonperformance, so it is no longer the pure toxin puzzle.
9. Governor is relevantly similar to Public Registry: if its expected penalty makes voluntary drinking rational, it also converts the puzzle into a self-binding case.
10. Practical rationality should be time-neutral: merely asking what to do at 23:59 rather than at noon cannot change the answer, although changes in evidence, options, or payoffs can.

Please determine whether these commitments hang together. Identify each genuine tension, specify exactly which commitments generate it, and explain why the obvious distinctions do not immediately remove it. Also identify apparent tensions that dissolve once the relevant distinctions are drawn. In particular, separate the rationality of causing oneself to intend, the rationality of the resulting intention, and the rationality of the intended action; distinguish intending from predicting; and explain whether an intention-generated reason is compatible with the anti-bootstrapping commitment.

Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each, state the least costly revision and what theoretical or intuitive cost it carries. Finally, identify the single revision that would resolve the greatest portion of the package while preserving the pure puzzle, and say whether Public Registry and Governor really belong in the same category. Do not resolve the case by assuming scanner error, self-deception, compartmentalization, or a merely conditional intention.
