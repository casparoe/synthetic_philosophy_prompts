I'm a high school student, and I keep noticing a pattern in how groups of people decide things, and I want to know if it has a name. Two examples. First: two food trucks park outside school selling nearly identical food. On the first day, the first few students pick one truck for basically arbitrary reasons, and everyone after them sees the longer line and figures it must be the better one, so they join it. Within a week, that truck is packed every day, even though lots of students have privately tried the other truck and actually preferred it. Second: a rumor spreads that a teacher grades unfairly. Almost everyone who had the teacher privately thought the grading was fine, but since everyone else was repeating the rumor, each student assumed the others must know something, so they repeated it too. Then one senior publicly said the rumor started as a joke, and the whole consensus collapsed within a day. What strikes me is that in both cases everyone can see what others are doing but not why they're doing it; everyone is being individually sensible, not copying out of fear of disapproval; and the resulting consensus looks rock-solid but is actually carrying almost no real information, which is why it shatters so easily. Is there an established name for this, and how exactly is it different from peer pressure, conformity, or groupthink, since none of those seem to capture the "everyone is reasoning correctly from what they can see" part? If there's no exact name, could you define the phenomenon precisely enough to say where its edges are — for instance, whether it still counts if people can openly share their reasons, and whether it counts if the first movers' choice happened to be the right one?
