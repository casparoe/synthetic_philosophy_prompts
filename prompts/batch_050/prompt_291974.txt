For a graduate seminar on the epistemology of rational belief, I've been asked to circulate my commitments on the lottery and preface paradoxes in advance so the other participants can stress-test them. Here they are:

(1) Rational credences must satisfy the axioms of probability.
(2) Rational outright belief is closed under conjunction: if I rationally believe p and rationally believe q, I rationally believe their conjunction.
(3) Rational outright belief in a proposition requires only that it be sufficiently probable on one's evidence — high probability short of certainty is enough.
(4) In the preface case, the author is rational to believe each individual claim in her book and also rational to believe that at least one of those claims is false.
(5) In the lottery case (a fair lottery with a million tickets and one winner), it is not rational to believe, of any particular ticket, that it will lose.
(6) The lottery paradox and the preface paradox are structurally the same problem, so any adequate solution must treat the two cases uniformly.
(7) It is never rational to hold a set of beliefs that one oneself believes to be jointly inconsistent.

I suspect this package has problems but I'm not sure where. Please audit it: identify which commitments are in tension, distinguishing genuine tensions from merely apparent ones that dissolve on inspection, and order the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say precisely which commitment would have to be given up or weakened, and at what cost to the rest of the package. Finally, tell me which single revision would restore consistency at the lowest overall cost.
