ok so I keep ending up in arguments where I wheel out the same cluster of views about coordination failures, and I want to check before I embarass myself at a workshop whether the package is actually coherent. roughly where I stand:

1. The badness of things like the attention economy, academic overwork, doping in sports, arms races etc. is structural: everyone's behaving locally rationally and the outcome is bad for all of them. Because of this, moralizing at individuals (put down your phone, just work less, just don't take steroids) is basically a category error -- if it's really a trap, unilateral restraint just costs the restrainer and changes nothing.

2. The fix for genuine traps is coordination machinery with enforcement -- treaties, norms with teeth, rule changes that bind everyone at once -- not trying to change what people want. The preferences are usually fine; the incentive gradient is the problem.

3. That said, I personally use a dumbphone, deleted my social accounts, and tell younger academics to refuse to do unpaid peer review. I think these individual moves are genuinely worthwhile and I recommend them without feeling like a hypocrite.

4. I'm against strong global governance. A world state or even a powerful global regulator would be unaccountable, impossible to exit, and a single point of failure for civilization; decentralization and jurisdictional diversity are safety features.

5. I think the AI capabilities race is exactly the kind of trap where we need a binding international treaty with inspections, verification, and sanctions for violators -- voluntary commitments from labs won't cut it.

6. And for the record I'm totally fine with ordinary market competition -- supermarkets undercutting each other on price isn't some Molochian horror, it's just how you get cheap groceries.

Which of these are actually in tension and which only look like it? I'd like the genuine tensions ranked by how bad they are, with what the cheapest fix would be in each case and what that fix costs me. And if one of the apparent tensions dissolves once you state the view more carefully, show me the restatement rather than just declaring it fine. I don't have a preferred revision in mind, so don't pull punches about which commitment is the weak link.
