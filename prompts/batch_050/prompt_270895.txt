our CMO defends the whole segmentation strategy by saying a generation is like a tree ring: the conditions during your formative years get laid down as a permanent band you carry around for the rest of your life, so Boomers, Millennials and Gen Z are durable, targetable types with real shared traits. a sociologist friend says that's exactly backwards -- generations are like zodiac signs: arbitrary cutoffs that people then read personalities into, and the supposed traits are mostly stereotype plus the fact that a 25-year-old and a 60-year-old obviously behave differently regardless of when they were born. audit both analogies for me. give the mapping explicitly (what plays the role of the drought years, the saw that decides where one ring ends and the next begins; what plays the role of the stars, the horoscope writer), say where each one breaks, and flag what each smuggles in -- about how much variation exists inside a cohort, about whether shared events even arrive on a neat generational schedule, and about what happens to people once the label itself is circulating. then the actual question: does the tree ring picture survive the audit well enough to justify the CMO's segmentation, or was it doing all the work? and if neither analogy fits, what's a better one for whatever a generation actually is?
