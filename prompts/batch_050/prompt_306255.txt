You're a philosopher of logic who moonlights as a fiction writer, and I want a short story (600-900 words) about dialetheism -- the view that some contradictions are genuinely true -- and the paraconsistent reasoning you'd need to live with that.

Quick setup so the logic is load-bearing rather than decorative: in classical logic, one true contradiction "explodes" -- from A and not-A you can derive literally anything, so a contradictory database or legal code is useless. Paraconsistent logics block this: they let a sentence be both true and false without letting everything become true. I want a story set somewhere characters have to reason this way as a practical skill -- e.g. a detective whose witnesses give genuinely contradictory testimony that is (in the story's world) all true, or a magistrate working under a legal code containing a true contradiction, where if her reasoning "exploded" everyone would be guilty of everything.

Before you write, brainstorm briefly in a few bullet points about where the true contradiction in your story should live -- a self-referential statute ("this law is not valid")? the borderline of a sorites case? someone who both is and isn't the murderer? -- and pick whichever option lets the paraconsistent reasoning do the most work. Then write the story.

The hard part, so don't fudge it: the characters must NOT treat the contradiction as a puzzle to resolve or an error to fix. They accept it and keep reasoning carefully anyway, and the reader should feel both why explosion would be catastrophic and why their reasoning still has rules -- it is not "anything goes." No lecturing, though: nobody gets to say "as Graham Priest argued..." or explain the logic textbook-style. It should show up as a discipline they've mastered. And end the story on the one thing paraconsistent reasoning can't give them: a way to force a verdict when the contradiction IS the whole case.
