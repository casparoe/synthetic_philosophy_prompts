I do research support for an international mediation NGO and we keep getting asked to weigh in on unilateral secession claims -- usually a few a year, sometimes with real tension on the ground. Right now every analyst just wings it with their own intuitions and we get wildly inconsistent internal assessments. I want a reusable decision procedure any staffer can run on a claim in an afternoon, built out of what the philosophy of self-determination actually says (remedial-right-only views, primary-right/plebiscitary views, and the big problems with each -- who counts as 'the people', why remedial theories struggle with cases where the grievance is real but moderate, etc.). It should be an ordered set of questions with what counts as a passing answer to each, where the procedure halts with a no, and where it has to punt to a human because the question is contested theory rather than fact. Then run it on three stylized cases: a Quebec-style case (no oppression, functioning democracy, clear regional majority), a Kosovo-style case (recent serious persecution, no negotiated exit possible), and a case where a rich region mainly wants to stop subsidizing poorer co-nationals -- including flagging if the procedure gives an answer I'd find uncomfortable there. Keep the whole thing under 700 words.
