I host a philosophy-of-mind podcast, and next month's guest is a philosopher who defends a thesis in the spirit of Aunt Hillary, the conscious ant colony in the Ant Fugue chapter of Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach: she holds that some ant colonies are, literally, minded subjects -- that the colony itself, and not merely its individual ants, is a bearer of beliefs, goals, and at least a rudimentary form of experience. Her argument rests on the functional organization of the colony: the fact that information is distributed, integrated, and used to guide flexible behavior at the colony level, even though no single ant represents any of it.

Please draft nine interview questions that engage the actual machinery of her position rather than inviting her to restate it. In particular, I want:

- One question pressing on the boundary problem: if colony-level functional integration is sufficient for a mind, ask her to state the principled criterion that grants a mind to the colony but withholds one from the whole forest, a nation's economy, or the internet -- and a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply, that only the colony's organization is autonomous and self-maintaining.
- One question on the speed objection: colony-level "signals" propagate at the pace of ants walking, orders of magnitude slower than neural firing. Ask why processing speed should not bear on whether a system is a subject, and follow up on her likely response that speed is irrelevant to the abstract causal structure, by asking whether a colony simulated one ant-step per century would still experience anything.
- One question distinguishing mere interpretability from mindedness: a skeptic says we can adopt the intentional stance toward the colony without the colony really representing anything. Ask what makes colony-level content real rather than projected, with a follow-up for the reply that the colony's states are causally efficacious in their own right.
- One question about phenomenal consciousness specifically: even granting functional minds, ask what could settle whether there is something it is like to be the colony, given that no individual ant has access to colony-level states and the colony cannot report in anything like our sense.
- One question on fission and fusion: when a colony buds into two, or two merge, what happens to the subject -- with a follow-up pressing her on whether her answer is a discovery or a stipulation.
- One question asking what observation or experiment would disconfirm her view, and a follow-up if she answers that the view is a conceptual claim and therefore not empirically refutable.

For each question, write both the main question and the follow-up in a form I can read aloud, and keep each under sixty words.
