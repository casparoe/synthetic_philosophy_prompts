So my philosophy club has been going in circles for like a month on this: some music is obviously sad, but where is the sadness? One side says the sadness is a property of the music itself, because sad music shares stuff with sad people -- slow tempo, low pitch, drooping melodies that fall like a sad voice does. The other side says come on, sounds are just air vibrations, they can't literally be sad, so really the music makes us feel sad or we project our feelings onto it. But then someone pointed out that tons of people listen to sad music without becoming sad at all, they actually enjoy it, which seems really bad for the "music makes you sad" side. And the resemblance side gets asked stuff like, ok, a willow tree looks sad too, is the tree sad? What kind of property even is "looking sad"?

My working hypothesis is the two sides just mean different things by "the music is sad" and once you split the meanings the whole arguement evaporates. But I'm not fully convinced, because it feels like there's something left over, maybe a disagreement about whether hearing sadness in music is literal perception or some kind of imagination thing, and I don't know what that leftover part even is. So my question: what kind of disagreement is this actually -- verbal, empirical (could a psychology experiment settle it?), or a clash of starting intuitions neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would progress look like -- a better definition, an experiment, or just admitting the question was badly put?
