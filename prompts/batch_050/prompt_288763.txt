My mom died about a year ago, and I was talking about it with my older brother, who works in physics. He told me I should think of it like this: mom isn't gone, she's just located in the part of time where she lived, the same way Paris still exists even though I'm not in Paris. The sixty years she had are permanently on the record, so to speak, and nothing about her dying erases a single one of them. Her wedding, my childhood, all of it is still there, just not where I'm standing.

Honestly I found this really comforting for about a week. But the more I sit with it, the less I can tell whether it's an actual thought or just a nice-sounding metaphor. My girlfriend thinks it's cope: if a person isn't around anymore, then pointing at the calender and saying "but 2019 still has her in it" is just refusing to use the word gone honestly. And there's a darker side I keep bumping into. If the good times are permanently there, then so are the bad ones, including her last few months, which I'd very much rather think of as over and done with.

So a few things I want sorted out. Is my brother's picture saying something that could actually be true, and if so, what exactly would have to be true about time for it to work? Was the comfort I got from it tracking something real, or does it fall apart the moment you press on it? If he's right, does he have to bite the bullet that her suffering is still sitting there too, or is there a principled way to keep the comfort without the horror? And maybe most of all: is the disagreement between him and my girlfriend even a real disagreement about how the world is, or are they just using words like "still exists" in two different ways and talking past each other?
