Write a short story that plays with the heterogeneity problem for hedonism.

Context: I'm a software engineer (recommendation systems, nothing fancy) and this came out of an argument at work about whether "pleasure" is even one thing you could put on a single axis and optimize for. What does the pleasure of finally tracking down a heisenbug at 2am have in common with a hot shower, or your grandma's soup, or scratching a mosquito bite, or that weird bittersweet feeling when a song reminds you of someone? They feel completely different. But utilitarians and recsys people both seem to need some common currency, so either there's something shared underneath or the whole project is confused.

The setup I want: a near-future startup ships a headband-ish device (name it whatever) that can both read a user's moment-to-moment wellbeing and directly induce it — basically a dial that turns up "the good feeling" without needing any of the usual sources. The twist: the device works flawlessly for some pleasures and keeps failing or producing something eerily hollow for others, and the engineers slowly realize it's because their model assumes all pleasure is one kind of stuff that varies only in intensity. Take it wherever you want from there.

Things the story should actually grapple with, through the plot rather than dialogue-lectures:
- If pleasures have nothing in common except that we pursue them, is hedonism a theory about the experiences or about us?
- Is the "pleasure" of solving a hard problem even a feeling, or is it relief, or the removal of frustration, or something that doesn't recieve a clean name at all?
- Maybe: a character gets unlimited dial access and slowly discovers which of their favorite experiences survive when you isolate "the pleasure part" — and which ones turn out to have never been about pleasure in the first place.

~800-1500 words. Show don't tell, no character who conveniently explains the philosophy out loud, and please no ending where someone learns that the real happiness was friendship all along unless you can genuinely earn it.
