So I'm in the second year of a PhD on consciousness and cognitive architecture, and at our reading group last week we did the old neuron-by-neuron replacement thought experiment -- your biological neurons get swapped one at a time for silicon functional duplicates until your whole brain is artificial, and the question is whether you're still you at the end, and whether you'd notice anything. I raised what I thought was the obvious worry: even granting the swap works functionally at each step, it's not clear the person who survives is me rather than a successor who thinks it's me. The postdoc running the session just waved this off with 'you're the pattern, not the atoms', which I've now realised people in my department say constantly -- I've heard it used against substrate chauvinism, in favour of uploading, against cryonics sceptics, basically whenever anyone expresses unease about substrate stuff.

Here's my problem: I've started to think this sentence is doing at least four different jobs depending on who says it and when, and that the reading that would actually defeat my objection is way stronger than the reading that's obviously true. What I'd like is a breakdown of the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, laid out as a list, roughly ordered from trivial to radical. For each one, say whether it's a platitude, a substantive but defensible thesis, or actually false, and flag which philosophical commitments it smuggles in (e.g. does it need functionalism? does it need a particular theory of personal identity over time?). Then -- this is the bit I actually care about for next week's session -- tell me which of those readings would genuinely support using the slogan to dismiss the 'successor who thinks it's me' worry in the gradual replacement case, because my suspicion is that the people saying it are relying on a weak reading (something like multiple realisability) while the arguement actually needs a strong one (something like: preservation of functional organisation is sufficient for numerical identity of the person across arbitrary substrate change).

One more wrinkle I'd like addressed: the same people deploy the slogan about scan-and-copy uploading, where there's no gradual continuity at all, just an information transfer. Does the reading that works for gradual replacement also cover that case, or does the slogan quietly switch meanings between the two? If it switches, I want to be able to point at exactly where.
