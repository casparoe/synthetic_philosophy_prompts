Just got done arguing with my roommate about Nozick for like two hours and I want to settle this. He read some pop-political-philosophy book on the plane and summarized the Wilt Chamberlain argument to me like this:

"Nozick says: imagine society starts out with a perfectly fair distribution, whatever you think fair is. Now a million fans each voluntarily pay Wilt Chamberlain 25 cents to watch him play, so he ends up way richer than everyone else. Since every single transfer was voluntary and nobody was wronged, the new unequal distribution has to be just too. So Nozick proved that egalitarianism is self-contradictory, because the only way to keep a patterned distribution in place is to constantly seize people's legitimately earned property, which means taxation is basically theft. And since the fans started from a fair distribution, the argument doesn't depend on any controversial assumptions about property or self-ownership — it works against any theory of distributive justice that says inequality is bad."

I told him I remember it differently — that the argument is about patterns needing constant interference with liberty, not a proof that redistribution is theft, and that he seems to be smuggling in a bunch of stuff about taxation that isn't actually what the example shows. But it's been a few years since I read Anarchy, State, and Utopia so I can't fully back that up. Which parts of his summary are accurate, which are the standard overreach people make, and which are just wrong?
