Okay here's a thought experiment that's bugging me. You know Hofstadter's Careenium setup, roughly: a big vat where zillions of tiny "sims" knock around, and riding on them are big "simmballs" whose motions implement symbol-level cognition, like the tokens of a thinking system. The usual fight is about whether the symbol level actually causes anything or whether all the real pushing happens down at the sim level, with the symbol story just a gloss.

Now add my twist: the Lazy Careenium. The vat has an output device -- say a robotic arm that waves when the system has "made a decision." But the trigger on the arm is sloppy: it fires whenever the simmballs settle into ANY configuration that counts, at the symbol level, as a completed plan. Lots of wildly different sim-level microstates satisfy this; there's no single physical description of the trigger condition that isn't a gigantic disjunctive mess, while the symbol-level description ("it finished planning") is short and clean.

Please argue that in the Lazy Careenium the symbol level genuinely does causal work -- that symbols really push the substrate around, at least in this setup -- against the objection that the sim-level microdynamics do 100% of the pushing and the trigger's "sloppiness" is just our descriptive convenience.
