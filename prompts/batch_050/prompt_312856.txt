I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a feature about the Sleeping Beauty problem. I keep coming back to a variant I haven't seen discussed, and I want you to write an essay that works through it and actually takes a stand.

Quick recap so we're on the same page: Beauty agrees to be put to sleep Sunday. A fair coin is flipped. If heads, she's woken Monday only. If tails, she's woken Monday and Tuesday, with her memory of the Monday awakening erased in between. Every awakening feels identical, and she knows the whole setup. The classic fight is between halfers (credence 1/2 in heads on waking, since she's learned nothing new) and thirders (credence 1/3, since two of her three possible awakenings are in the tails world).

Here's my twist. There's a lab technician, call him Ray, who also knows the full protocol. He has Monday off, no contact with the experiment. On Tuesday he strolls into the lab to grab something, glances through the window, and sees Beauty awake in her room. He has no idea whether it's Monday or Tuesday — the room looks identical either way, he hasn't checked a calendar, nobody's told him anything. (If the coin came up heads, what he's actually seeing is Monday's awakening; if tails, Tuesday's. He knows all this.)

So: what should Ray's credence in heads be when he sees her through the window? And here's the part that I think makes it interesting — Beauty, on that very awakening Ray is looking at, has her own credence in heads. Do Ray and Beauty have to agree? If thirders are right about Beauty, it seems like they might assing different credences to the same coin flip while both being fully rational and knowing everything relevant about the setup. That smells wrong to me, but I can't pin down exactly where the smell is coming from. Maybe Ray's glimpse counts as genuine evidence in a way Beauty's awakening doesn't? Or maybe the whole notion of "matching credences" breaks down once self-locating uncertainty is in play?

The essay should:
- Lay out the halfer and thirder cases fairly (no strawmen — both camps have smart people in them)
- Work out what each position implies for Ray specifically, including any weird consequences (e.g., does his answer depend on whether he'd have seen her awake in *every* branch, vs. only some?)
- Say whether Beauty and Ray can rationally disagree, and what that means for the idea that rational people with the same evidence converge
- Reach an actual conclusion about Ray's credence and about the original Beauty case, and justify it. Don't just survey the landscape and shrug.

At the end, add a short note on how confident you are in your verdict, and single out the single step in your argument you're least sure of — I want to know where the weak joint is before I build a piece on this.
