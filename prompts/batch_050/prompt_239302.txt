For my English class I'm writing a term paper on AI safety, and I've hit a conceptual snag I can't sort out on my own. From what I've read, the alignment problem is the problem of getting an AI system to do what its operators actually intend, rather than what they literally typed — closing the gap between the spirit of an instruction and its letter. That much makes sense to me. But the same sources then discuss "value alignment" as though it were a further, distinct, and apparently harder problem, and I genuinely cannot see what work is left over. If an AI reliably did what its operators really intended in every situation, wouldn't it thereby do the right thing by definition? It seems to me that "value alignment" must be either redundant with intent alignment, or else a way for researchers to smuggle their personal moral opinions into what is supposed to be a technical research agenda. Am I missing a real distinction between the two problems — and if so, could you explain what goes wrong even in the best case, where an AI perfectly fulfills its operators' intentions — or is the field simply using two names for one problem?
