I'm TAing a philosophy of mind seminar and grading the midterm take-homes. The question I set was:

"Explain what Dennett means by the 'Cartesian theater,' why he thinks it survives even in materialist theories of mind, and how his multiple drafts model handles apparent temporal anomalies like the color phi phenomenon."

Here's one of the answers:

"Descartes pictured the mind as a spectator sitting in a little theater inside the brain, watching the show the senses put on, and he even located the interface at the pineal gland. Dennett's point is that this picture doesn't die with dualism: materialists keep assuming there's some place in the brain where 'it all comes together,' where unconscious processing finishes and conscious experience begins, with a definite moment when each content arrives onstage. But a central audience like that is just a homunculus, and the homunculus would need its own inner theater, so you get a regress.

The color phi experiment shows why this matters. A red dot flashes, then a green dot flashes nearby, and subjects report seeing one dot move and turn green partway across — before the green dot had even appeared. Dennett gives two possible explanations. The Stalinesque one: the brain concocts the motion-and-color-change sequence in advance and only the fabricated version ever reaches consciousness, like a show trial. The Orwellian one: the subject really experiences the dots as they are, but the memory gets rewritten immediately afterward, like the Ministry of Truth, so they misremember having seen the motion. Dennett argues that we need more refined experiments to determine which of these explanations is the correct one, since both are consistent with the behavioral data we have.

The multiple drafts model instead denies that there's a determinate moment when a content 'becomes conscious.' Contents get fixed in parallel all over the brain, at different times and scales, and what counts as 'the experience' at a given time is just whichever draft happens to get probed — by a question, a report, a button press. So asking what the subject really saw before the probe is a confused question. Dennett concludes from this that consciousness is an illusion: there are only judgments and reports, and nothing it is like to see the dot at all."
