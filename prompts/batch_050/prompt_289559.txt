I am an instructor grading short-answer responses in an undergraduate philosophy of mind course. Below is the exam question, followed by one student's answer. Please assign a grade out of 20 and give the specific comments that justify it. I want a point-by-point assessment: identify everything the answer gets right, flag each conceptual error or unsupported step precisely (saying what the correct or defensible claim would be), and note where partial credit is warranted because an error is embedded in otherwise correct material. Do not grade on prose style, and do not penalize the student for failing to cite the literature.

Exam question:

In his work on the evolution of minds, Daniel Dennett describes a tower of generate-and-test, with each floor corresponding to a kind of creature. Darwinian creatures are shaped only by natural selection across generations: variation is generated genetically and tested by differential survival and reproduction, so no learning occurs within an individual lifetime. Skinnerian creatures can be modified within a lifetime by reinforcement: behaviors are generated more or less blindly and the ones followed by reward become more likely. Popperian creatures can represent candidate courses of action and evaluate them internally, so that bad options can be rejected in imagination rather than suffered in the world. Gregorian creatures, named after the psychologist Richard Gregory, can import mind-tools from their culture -- language, counting systems, diagrams, methods of inquiry -- that were built up by others and that restructure the creature's own thinking.

(a) For each of the four kinds of creature, state where the generation of variation and the testing of it take place, and give one plausible example of an organism or behavior fitting that kind. (b) Explain what changes at the transition from Skinnerian to Popperian creatures. (c) Critically discuss: when a higher floor of the tower appears, does it displace the floors below it, and can a Gregorian mind-tool ever make its bearer worse off?

Student's answer:

(a) In Darwinian creatures, variation is generated by mutation and recombination and tested by the environment over many generations; the individual is a fixed product of that process, as in the hard-wired web-building of a spider. In Skinnerian creatures, variation is generated by the individual trying out behaviors, and testing occurs when the environment delivers reinforcement or punishment; a rat in a maze gradually settling on the rewarded route is the standard case, and this is the first floor at which genuine learning appears. In Popperian creatures, the generate-and-test process moves inside the organism: candidate actions are generated as internal representations and tested against an inner model of the world, so the creature's hypotheses can die instead of the creature itself; a predator that sizes up two routes to its prey and rejects one without running it fits this floor. In Gregorian creatures, variation is generated by the individual inventing mind-tools -- words, numerals, diagrams -- and tested by whether those tools succeed in practice; a human using long division or a map is the example.

(b) The transition from Skinnerian to Popperian is the point at which testing stops happening in the world and starts happening in the creature's head. A Skinnerian creature must actually perform a behavior to find out whether it works, which means its mistakes are paid for in the currency of real consequences. A Popperian creature can pre-select among imagined options, so selection acts on representations rather than on trials. Because the internal test is faster and safer than the external one, a Popperian creature's choices will generally be the correct ones.

(c) Each new floor effectively replaces the one beneath it, since a creature with a cheaper and safer generate-and-test mechanism has no further use for the older, costlier one. Adult human behavior is therefore essentially Gregorian rather than Skinnerian: we act on what we have reasoned and been taught, not on what happens to have been reinforced. Gregorian tools are a special case of this displacement. Because a mind-tool is adopted only after proving its worth, and because tools like arithmetic and literacy plainly raise their bearers' fitness, Gregorian mind-tools always work to their bearers' advantage; a tool that made its bearer worse off would simply be discarded in the same way a bad hypothesis is discarded by a Popperian creature.
