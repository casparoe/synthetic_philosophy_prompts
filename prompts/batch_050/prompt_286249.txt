Alright, here's my situation. I spent forty years as a working biochemist and only got into philosophy after I retired, so maybe I'm missing something obvious. Lately I've been reading about the definition of death -- the whole brain death versus circulatory death fight -- and I cannot figure out what the disagreement is even supposed to be about anymore.

Here's what puzzles me. Everyone seems to agree on the biology. The brain death folks know that a body with a destroyed brain on a ventilator can still heal wounds, fight infections, gestate a fetus, even go through puberty if it's a kid -- which sure sounds like what a living organism does, and is exactly what the critics keep pointing out. The critics know that these bodies nevertheless can't do much without massive ongoing support. On the circulatory side everyone knows about auto-resuscitation worries and the arbitrary-looking waiting periods (two minutes here, five there), and now there are protocols where they declare you dead because your circulation stopped, then restart regional circulation to keep the organs perfused -- which, forgive me, looks an awful lot like undoing the thing that was supposed to make you dead. So nobody's facts are in dispute, as far as I can tell.

And yet the debate has been stuck for decades. My suspicion, as an outsider, is that this was never really a scientific question at all -- that "death" is doing moral and legal work (when can we take the organs, when can we stop paying for the ventilator) and both sides are reverse-engineering a biological-sounding definition that lands the organ procurement question where they want it. In other words it's a values argument wearing a lab coat. But maybe that's too cynical and there's a genuine conceptual question buried in there about what an organism is, or a genuine empirical one about integration, that could actually be settled.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Diagnose it for me -- verbal, factual, conceptual, or politics in disguise -- using what each side actually argues, not a textbook survey of positions. And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: is there a distinction, an experiment, or a conceptual result that could move it, or should I expect this to stay stuck forever because of what kind of question it is?
