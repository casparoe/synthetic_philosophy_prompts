In my medieval philosophy seminar we've just finished Confessions XI, and the study guide a classmate circulated summarizes Augustine's position like this:

"Augustine begins from the puzzle that the past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist, while the present has no duration, since any stretch of it can be divided into past and future parts. From this he concludes that time, strictly speaking, is unreal: nothing temporal genuinely exists, and our talk of past, present, and future describes an illusion produced by the mind. What we call the past is present memory, what we call the future is present expectation, and what we call the present is present attention. Augustine therefore holds that time is nothing more than a distention of the soul, by which he means that time is purely subjective -- a feature of how things appear to us rather than of the world. As for eternity, Augustine describes God's eternity as an endlessly long duration, a present that stretches infinitely far in both directions. When he considers the question of what God was doing before creating the world, he simply rebukes the questioner for being curious, treating the question as an impertinent joke rather than answering it."

I'm fairly sure parts of this are off, but I can't pin down exactly where it departs from the text we read. Could you go through it and tell me which claims a specialist on the Confessions would accept as a fair, if compressed, account, which ones subtly distort Augustine's position (for instance, whether "unreal" and "purely subjective" are fair renderings of the distention-of-the-soul doctrine, and whether his account of eternity is really a matter of infinite duration), and which are just wrong? In particular, if the summary mischaracterizes how Augustine handles the "what was God doing before creation" question, I'd like to know what his actual response is and why it counts as an answer rather than a brush-off. Finally, my classmate goes on to claim that because Augustine reduces time to features of the soul, his view is essentially a form of idealism about time in the later Berkeleyan sense. If the summary of Augustine needs correcting, does that further claim survive the corrections, or does it depend on the very misreading you're identifying?
