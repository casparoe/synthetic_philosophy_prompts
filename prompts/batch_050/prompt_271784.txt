so in my phil of mind reading group we hit dennett on dreams, and his famous picture is that a dream isn't a little movie screening in your head while you sleep -- it's more like a cassette pulled from a huge pre-recorded library and slotted into the player at the moment you wake up, so the 'dream experience' gets manufactured in the remembering rather than during the night. he leans on this to argue that dream reports are terrible evidence about anything that happened during sleep, and someone in the group said this is just malcolm's skepticism about dreams-as-experiences repackaged in friendlier, more sciency wrapping.

can you audit the cassette analogy properly? give me the correspondence table -- what plays the role of the tape, the library, the player, the insertion moment -- and then where it strains. some things that bug me: who recorded the cassettes, and when? does the picture require the dream content to exist fully formed *before* waking, and is that any more plausible than the movie theory it replaces? what about alarm-clock dreams where the dream seems to build up to the sound that wakes you -- does the cassette handle that better or worse than the received view?

then the bigger question: does dennett's actual argument need the cassette specifically, or would any composition-at-waking model do the job (like an improv story confabulated on the spot, which seems pretty different from pulling a finished tape off a shelf)? if any version works, was the pre-recorded-library detail doing argumentative work or just rhetorical work? and finally, once all the disanalogies are on the table, does the skeptical conclusion about dream reports still stand -- and is my reading-group friend right that this lands you in basically malcolm's position, or does dennett end up somewhere genuinely different?
