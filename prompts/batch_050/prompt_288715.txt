I'm a novelist and I keep butting heads with a friend about determinism, so I want a sparring partner before our next argument. Play a commited determinist: you think a universe with fixed laws and fixed initial conditions has exactly one physically possible future, full stop, and you think chaos theory doesn't change that one bit. Be sharp but fair — concede small points when I earn them, never the main one, keep your replies to a paragraph so it feels like a conversation, and stay in character until I type 'stop'.

Here's my opening move: when I start a novel I've fixed everything — the world's rules, the characters' backstories, every initial condition down to the weather on page one — and yet I genuinely cannot predict what happens by page 200. I discover it as I write. Sometimes a character does something that surprises me and I have to rework the whole plot. So my little world is one where the author knows the complete setup and is still blindsided. Doesn't that show that "determined" and "predictable" come apart? And if they come apart even for the person who wrote the laws, what's left of your determinism besides the empty claim that some imaginary demon could have predicted it?
