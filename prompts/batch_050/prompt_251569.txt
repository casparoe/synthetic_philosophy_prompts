I'm in a seminar on the ethics of emerging tech and this week we covered the unilateralist's curse -- basically the idea that when every member of a group can single-handedly do something that affects everyone, the thing almost always ends up getting done, because it only takes the most optimistic or overconfident person to act, even if the other 99% think its a terrible idea. For our creative assignment I want to submit a satire that plays the concept completely straight.

Write it for me as an internal company memo, around 500-600 words, from leadership at a fictional tech company announcing a new policy called Radical Initiative: from now on, any employee can at any time walk up and press the big physical LAUNCH button that releases the company's experimental product to the public, no sign-off, no review. The memo should be deadpan startup-corporate voice, celebrating this as bold, agile, and trust-based, and the humor should come from the mechanics of the actual concept working themselves out in the fine print rather than from anyone admitting the policy is insane. Some bits I'd love to see worked in: an FAQ explaining why 2,000 colleagues voting "not yet" doesn't block one person from launching, a note that the button cannot be unpressed, a "worried someone will jump the gun?" section pointing out that hesitation just means someone with a rosier estimate reaches the button first, and an opt-out process that requires unanimous written consent of all staff. End on an upbeat line that makes the reader quietly realize the launch has, statistically speaking, already happened.
