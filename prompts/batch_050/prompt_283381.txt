So I just got out of a genuinely annoying two-hour argument with my friend Marcus about AI assistants, and I want to settle something that came up in it. His position was basically that using AI for everything is no different from using calculators or GPS, tools have always changed what we bother to learn. My position is that something different is going on, and I kept gesturing at a pattern that I don't have a word for, which made me lose the argument because he just kept saying "you're describing calculators again." So here's the pattern, with the two examples I gave him. First one: a junior programmer I know uses an AI for basically all her coding, and she's fast, genuinely productive, but last month she told me she can no longer tell, reading the AI's output, whether it's right -- she has to run it and see. She lost the skill that would let her check the tool, *because* of using the tool. Second: my uncle has used GPS for fifteen years and now can't navigate his own city without it, but crucially he also can't tell when the GPS is being dumb, like routing him through a flood, the way a local driver instantly would. In both cases the reliance itself quietly destroys the very capacity you'd need to notice the reliance has gone wrong, so the whole thing ratchets -- the worse your own judgment gets, the more you need the tool, and the more you need it the worse your judgment gets.

Marcus says this is just "skill fade" and it's boring and economists have known about it forever. But I think the loop structure is the important part, not the fading: a calculator doesn't erode your ability to check calculators, because you were never checking them by hand anyway, whereas these tools erode exactly the faculty that would be your safety check on them. Also, in the AI case there's a second-order thing where you stop even *wanting* to understand, because the answer is right there and understanding feels like homework. So: does this configuration have a name? I've heard people throw around "automation bias" and "extended mind" and something called the Google effect, but from what I can tell those are all about neighboring things and none of them is specifically about reliance destroying its own error-correction. If there's an established term I should be using, tell me and explain how the thing I'm describing does or doesn't fit it. If there isn't one, tell me that honestly rather than squashing my thing into the nearest bin, and maybe propose what it should be called and where its boundaries are -- like, does it apply to relying on a knowledgeable spouse for all car problems, or only to opaque machine systems?

One more thing, because Marcus accused me of motivated reasoning and he wasn't entirely wrong: I'd like the answer to be upfront about what's actually established here versus what's still contested among the people who study this. Like, if there's real evidence that offloading atrophies the checking skill, say so and say how solid it is, and if the scary ratchet version is mostly an extrapolation that smart people dispute, say that too. Don't present my side as the consensus just because I lost the argument and would like to win it retroactively.
