A colleague cornered me after our seminar with an argument that ends somewhere I refuse to go, but I can't find the weak joint. Here it is as she gave it:

(1) It can be wrong to impose a risk of serious harm even when the harm probably won't materialize -- you may not drive drunk just because you'll most likely get home fine.
(2) Uncertainty about which moral theory is true works the same way: if you place substantial credence on a theory according to which some act is seriously wrong, then performing that act imposes a risk of serious moral wrongdoing.
(3) So it can be wrong to perform an act when a theory you credit condemns it, even if your own best guess says the act is permissible.
(4) The graver the wrongdoing at issue, the less credence it takes to make running the risk impermissible.
(5) There are theories you cannot responsibly dismiss -- theories with serious defenders and no obvious refutation -- on which eating meat, or keeping most of your income rather than donating it, are grave wrongs.
(6) So intellectual honesty about your credences forbids all of these things, and a great deal more besides.
(7) Worse, the demands collide: the same reasoning that forbids eating meat forbids, on some creditable theory, refusing your grandmother's cooking; the reasoning that demands you donate forbids, on some creditable theory, impoverishing your own family. A decision procedure that sometimes commands both A and not-A is broken. Since (6) is already absurd and (7) shows the method issuing contradictory orders, the whole project of letting moral uncertainty guide action is incoherent, and we should simply act on whichever theory we judge most likely true.

She presents (7) as the coup de grâce and her final sentence as the only way out. I'd rather keep (1) through (3) than accept that moral uncertainty makes no practical difference, but I can't say which step I'd surrender or what surrendering it buys me. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In answering, please separate what is genuinely settled in this literature from what remains live and disputed, and don't present a contested position as the consensus.
