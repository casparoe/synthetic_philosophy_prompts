I'm a philosophy grad student TAing a course in philosophy of biology, and next week we're doing the adaptationism debate. In office hours I tried to sharpen the Gould–Lewontin spandrels critique into a general argument against adaptationist explanation, and I think I accidentally built an argument that proves too much, but I can't locate the slip. Here it is, as I wrote it on the whiteboard: (1) For any trait you pick, some adaptationist story can be told about it, and if one story fails a test, another can always be devised in its place -- so no possible observation about a trait could ever refute adaptationism. (2) If no possible observation could count against a hypothesis, then the absence of such an observation provides no evidence in its favor; unfalsifiable hypotheses gain no support from surviving tests. (3) So the fact that a trait fits an adaptationist story provides no evidence that selection produced that trait. (4) Fit between trait and story is the only evidence adaptationist explanations ever rest on. (5) Therefore we never have evidence that natural selection produced any trait whatsoever. (6) But we plainly do have such evidence -- eyes, wings, camouflage, lactation -- so something in the argument must be wrong, and the spandrels critique, taken at full strength, seems to refute itself. A friend who sat in on the session says the argument is fine and I should just accept that adaptationist explanation is never warranted, full stop; I'd rather not.

A few things that might matter for your diagnosis: I set this up partly as a test of how far the spandrels critique can be pushed before it collapses into a blanket skepticism no biologist -- including, I suspect, Gould and Lewontin themselves -- would accept, so I'd like the answer to say something about what the critique legitimately establishes versus what my formalization smuggles in. If I have to give something up, I'd much rather abandon the Popperian-flavored premise (2) than accept (5), but I want to know whether that's even the premise doing the damage, and what the cheapest revision is that keeps the critique's real teeth: namely, that particular adaptationist stories are often held more loosely than the evidence warrants.

Please give your diagnosis as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: identify the step you reject, explain why that step looked plausible, state what rejecting it costs, state what the best alternative repair would cost instead, and close with a one-line verdict on whether the spandrels critique survives your repair.
