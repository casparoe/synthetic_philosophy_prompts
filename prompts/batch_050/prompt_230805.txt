I've been reading LessWrong and its surrounding blogs since before GPT-3, and I have now watched the same argument recur for years: whether current language models genuinely understand anything, or whether they are merely very large next-token predictors. Both camps cite the same evidence -- benchmark saturation, mechanistic interpretability results, the same embarrassing failure modes -- and nobody moves. My working hypothesis is that the impasse comes from both sides implicitly assuming Daniel Dennett's Tower of Generate-and-Test, from Kinds of Minds: the ladder from Darwinian creatures, shaped only by selection on their genes, through Skinnerian and Popperian creatures, up to Gregorian creatures that import thinking tools from the surrounding culture. The disputants seem to be arguing about which rung the models occupy. But Dennett's ladder presupposes a learner whose variation and selection happen during its own lifetime, whereas a trained model's generate-and-test loop ran offline, in gradient descent, before it ever answered a question -- the tower, if it applies at all, is built sideways. So: what kind of disagreement is this, really? A factual dispute about which rung is occupied, a verbal dispute about the word understanding, or two camps answering different questions under one heading? Give me a diagnosis with reasons, and tell me what -- a distinction, an experiment, a formal result -- would count as progress, or argue honestly that none should be expected.
