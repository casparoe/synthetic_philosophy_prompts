I am a doctoral student in metaphysics preparing a piece for a department colloquium. Please write a parody, roughly 700 words, of an official municipal Unified Objects Code issued by a city council that has adopted a restrictive answer to the special composition question: several material things compose a further object only when their coordinated activity constitutes a life.

Maintain the deadpan voice of municipal legal prose while specifying inspection and permitting rules for borderline cases: planks fastened shipwise in a backyard, a clock whose parts cooperate without metabolizing, a recently deceased animal awaiting burial, and an art installation in which two chairs touch for one minute each day. Include procedures for certifying when an object comes into existence or ceases to exist, penalties for discarding a table that was allegedly never there, and an appeals process for residents who claim that part replacement has produced an unauthorized successor organism.

The satire should draw its humor from genuine difficulties faced by the restrictive view—especially the vagueness of life, the difference between causal coordination and functional organization, and the instability of ordinary judgments about persistence. Do not name philosophers, summarize scholarly debates, or break the bureaucratic voice; the ordinance should present its metaphysical commitments as obvious matters of public safety and zoning compliance.
