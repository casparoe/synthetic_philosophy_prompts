So my department's hiring committee has turned into my favorite real-life judgment aggregation puzzle and I want to sanity-check my thinking on it. Three people on the comittee, call them Alice, Bob, and Carol. Every candidate gets a binary thumbs up/down from each member on three criteria: research, teaching, and "fit" (which basically means collegiality / will this person answer emails). The official rule in the faculty handbook is that we make an offer iff the candidate clears all three bars. But the handbook never says how the individual votes get combined into a committee verdict, and as far as I can tell nobody has ever noticed this gap. Out of curiosity I pulled the vote patterns from the last few searches (don't tell HR):

- 2021 hire: all three members passed the candidate on everything. Offer made.
- 2022 search 1: everyone failed the candidate on teaching, mixed on the rest. No offer.
- 2022 search 2: Alice failed them on research but passed teaching and fit; Bob and Carol passed them on everything. Offer made.
- 2023: Alice and Carol both failed the candidate on fit, everything else clean across the board. No offer.

Now this year's finalist: Alice passes him on research and teaching but fails him on fit. Bob passes him on research and fit but fails him on teaching. Carol passes him on teaching and fit but fails him on research.

Questions: (a) Predict what the committee actually does when they meet. Do they even notice there's a problem, or does someone just say "well, nobody voted for him" and move on? (b) More interesting: what *should* they do to stay consistent with their own track record? As far as I can tell every past case is compatible with at least two different aggregation rules -- tally the votes on each criterion first and then apply the handbook's all-three-bars rule to the tallies, versus have each member apply the handbook rule privately and then majority-vote the verdicts. This candidate is exactly the case where those two rules split: no individual member wants to hire him, but every single bar is cleared by a 2-1 majority. Lay out the candidate rules that fit the history, say which one better rationalizes it (or whether the history genuinely underdetermines the answer), and what principle the committee would be committing itself to either way -- like, is the committee a body that has views about criteria, or a body that has views about candidates? (c) If I could dig up one more past search, what vote pattern would settle which rule they've implicitly been running all along?

Also, side question: is there even a fact of the matter about what "the committee thinks" here, or is asking that a category error? This feels adjacent to the Condorcet jury stuff that comes up on LW every so often but I can't quite make the mapping precise myself.
