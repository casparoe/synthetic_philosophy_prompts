Ok so I went down a rabbit hole about ring species last night, like those salamanders in California where each group can breed with the neighboring groups all the way around the mountains, but the two groups at the ends of the chain can't breed with each other -- and apparently they're still all considered ONE species because the chain connects them.

Which got me thinking. If being connected by a chain of interbreeding is enough to make things one species, doesn't that prove literally everything alive is one single species? I can't breed with a chimp, sure, but if you trace the chain backwards, my ancestors could breed with their ancestors, and their ancestors could breed with the ones before them, and so on all the way back. There's an unbroken chain of interbreeding between me and a chimp, me and a banana, whatever. So either the biological species concept says all life on earth is one species, which seems wrong, or there's some rule about where the chain stops counting -- but nobody ever mentions this rule when they explain ring species. What am I missing here?
