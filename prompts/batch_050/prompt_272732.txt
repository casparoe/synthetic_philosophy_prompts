So I'm drafting the "here's where I stand" section of a paper on induction for my grad seminar and before I commit I want to know if the package is actually coherent. Here's roughly what I want to defend:

(1) Hume's problem has no solution. There's no non-circular justification of induction and we should just accept that rather than keep trying.

(2) Goodman's grue puzzle is a genuinely new problem and not just Hume dressed up. It's about which predicates are projectible, not about whether the future resembles the past.

(3) The reason grue is defective is that it's positional: it smuggles in a reference to a specific time t, and legit predicates don't do that.

(4) Goodman's own entrenchment answer is a failure because it just ratifies whatever vocabulary we happen to have inherited, which is history, not epistemology.

(5) The right fix is that some predicates carve nature at its joints and others don't. Naturalness is out there in the world, it's not something we project onto it, and grue fails because it's gruesomely unnatural, so to speak.

(6) What a body of evidence confirms is always relative to a language: state the same observations in a different vocabulary and you get confirmation of different hypotheses. There's no language-independent fact of the matter about what the evidence supports.

Some of these feel like they should fit together (1 with 2, 4 with 5) but I'm less sure about how the whole set hangs. Which pairs are actually in tension vs just look like it, ranked by how bad the damage is, and for each real tension what would I have to give up? And is there one commitment whose removal fixes the most? Be concrete, dont just say "it depends on how you cash out naturalness."
