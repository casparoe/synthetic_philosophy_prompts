So I'm giving a talk at our department's value-theory workshop in a couple weeks and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The talk defends a specific answer to the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: my claim is that there's no shared feeling or phenomenological quality running through all pleasures (the taste of coffee, solving a proof, warm sun on your back, hearing good news), and that hedonists should stop looking for one. What unifies pleasures, on my view, is an attitude -- roughly, that the subject welcomes the experience and would have it continue for its own sake. So pleasure is attitudinal, not a felt quale, and hedonism survives the heterogeneity problem by relocating what pleasures have in common.

The audience is what makes me nervous. It's mostly well-being people, and the split matters: about half are desire-fulfillment folks who will be fine with the attitudinal move but will pounce on whether my view is still *hedonism* at all or just their view in a trench coat; a few are moral psychology types who think pleasure is basically a natural kind with a common neural signature and will bring up the wanting/liking stuff; and there's one Aristotelian emeritus who shows up to everything and thinks the whole question of "what do pleasures have in common" is malformed.

Give me the prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from *this* room (not from a generic philosophy audience), roughly ordered by how damaging they'd be, each phrased the way that specific person would phase it. For each: my two-minute reply, what I should concede up front vs. hold firm on, and where the reply is soft. Also flag which objections have real force against the view itself versus which are really just terminological disputes about who gets the word "hedonism," because I suspect half the room's fight is about that. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks -- I have my own guess about what it is (it's about whether attitudinal pleasure can handle pleasures you're ashamed of), but tell me if you think the real nightmare question is something else.

Two more things.
