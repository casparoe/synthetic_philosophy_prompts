I am a doctoral candidate in political economy, and for a paper on the limits of causal inference in development I want to use the Argentina–Australia divergence as a disciplined test case, analyzed through the institutions-versus-geography-versus-culture debate. The broad facts are well known: around 1900 both were temperate-zone, European-settler, commodity-exporting economies with among the highest per-capita incomes in the world; over the twentieth century Argentina fell steadily down the income rankings while Australia remained rich. Please proceed in four steps, and hold yourself to them.

First, work out what each of the three grand explanations actually predicts for this pair. Geography does badly on its face, since the two countries resemble each other on most of the variables geographic determinists emphasize, but consider whether a more sophisticated geographic story -- distance from markets, commodity composition, terms-of-trade exposure -- can be salvaged, and at what cost to the theory's distinctiveness. Culture and institutions both seem to fit, but note that Argentina inherited its institutions and its culture from largely the same colonizing and immigrating populations, so ask whether the comparison can even in principle separate them, or whether the two theories are observationally equivalent here.

Second, take the identification problem seriously rather than gesturing at it. With a sample of perhaps ten comparable settler economies, no random assignment, and 'institutions' standing for a shifting bundle of property rights, electoral rules, labor regimes, and policy choices, what would a genuine causal claim even be? Is 'inclusive institutions' a well-defined treatment, or a residual category that absorbs whatever worked?

Third, run the analysis in the other direction: what does this episode do to the theories? Does Argentina vindicate the institutional account, or does it instead reveal that the account is flexible enough to explain any outcome after the fact, since any failed policy can be redescribed as an extractive institution? Say plainly where each theory is confirmed, where it is embarrassed, and where the case is simply one it never anticipated.

Fourth, conclude with what evidence -- if it were obtainable -- would actually discriminate among the three explanations in a case like this, and what it implies for the broader debate if no such evidence exists for historical divergences of this kind.
