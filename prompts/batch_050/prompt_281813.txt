I run a philosophy of physics reading group, and for most of the past year two of our members have been going around in circles over the hole argument in general relativity. The first — call her the hole-argument literalist — takes the standard Earman–Norton style presentation at face value: because the theory's equations permit models that agree everywhere outside some region and differ inside it only by a diffeomorphism, general relativity is indeterministic unless we abandon a robust realism about spacetime points and their identities, and she regards that conclusion as a genuine discovery about the theory rather than an artefact of presentation. The second — call him the sophisticated substantivalist — holds that diffeomorphically related models are merely redundant representations of a single physical possibility, that the argument dissolves once this is recognised, and that accepting it costs the substantivalist nothing, since spacetime points can still be individuated by their qualitative and metrical relations within a model.

I would like you to draft the joint statement these two could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, so I can hand it to them as a model of what they should have produced themselves. It should open with a numbered section of agreed claims: what both accept about the mathematics of the construction, about what determinism means as a property of a theory versus a formulation, and about which responses to the argument (for instance, appealing to the empirical content of spacetime being exhausted by point coincidences) settle less than their proponents suppose. Then give the disagreements, phrased in a table with a neutral formulation of each dispute in one column and, in the other two, the wording each camp would accept as a fair statement of its own position — I want at least one row on whether the literalist's notion of a distinct physical possibility is even coherent, and one on whether the substantivalist's move is relationalism under another name.

Close with a section on cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect one crux is whether the redundancy the substantivalist invokes should be treated uniformly with gauge redundancy in other theories, such as electromagnetism, or whether spacetime diffeomorphisms are a special case; another is whether the whole dispute survives once both sides agree on what the formalism says and are merely contesting what counts as a genuine possibility. For each crux, specify a case or argument both parties would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side predicts the examination of that case will show. If you judge that some part of their disagreement is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and explain why both should concede that it is. Please write the whole document in British English, and keep the tone that of a document the two of them could genuinely put their names to, not a referee's verdict.
