Here is the classmate's answer:

(a) A dialetheist holds that some contradictions are true, where a contradiction is a sentence of the form A and not-A. The stock examples are the semantic paradoxes, such as the liar. Trivialism is the view that every sentence whatsoever is true; dialetheism is strictly weaker, and no dialetheist in the literature defends trivialism. The dialetheist needs a paraconsistent logic because classical logic validates ex contradictione sequitur quodlibet: from A and not-A, any arbitrary sentence B follows. A dialetheist who retained classical logic would therefore be committed to the truth of everything, that is, to trivialism. Because paraconsistent logics invalidate explosion, a dialetheist who adopts one is protected against triviality no matter how many contradictions turn out to be true.

(b) The liar sentence L says of itself that it is false. If L is true, then what it says is the case, so L is false. If L is false, then, since that is precisely what L says, L is true. So classical reasoning delivers both L and its negation. The gap response, associated with Kripke's theory of truth, holds that L is neither true nor false, thereby blocking the reasoning. The dialetheist's reply, pressed by Priest, turns on the strengthened liar, the sentence that says of itself that it is not true: if the gap theorist calls this sentence not true on the grounds that it is gappy, that is exactly what the sentence says of itself, so it must be true, and the paradox returns. Priest concludes that the liar is a truth-value glut, both true and false. Like Kripke's theory, however, Priest's glut theory can remain non-trivial only by restricting the T-schema, so both approaches give up something classical.

(c) The objection charges that anyone who rejects explosion has simply redefined negation, so that the dispute is verbal. This assumes that the classical inference rules, explosion included, are constitutive of the meaning of negation. The dialetheist can respond that this is question-begging: whether explosion is constitutive of negation is precisely what is at issue. If meaning is given by inferential role, then since the dialetheist accepts most of the classical rules governing negation, their negation is close enough to the classicist's for the disagreement to be genuine rather than a change of subject. This reply seems to me largely successful, though it leaves open whether anything could count as arguing with, rather than merely disagreeing with, a classicist who takes explosion to be analytic of negation.
