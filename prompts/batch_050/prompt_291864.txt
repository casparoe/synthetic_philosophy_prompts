I'm a TA for an undergraduate philosophy of biology course, and after years of reading rationalist blogs and forums I've seen the "evolution is a tautology" talking point recycled enough times that I want to grade this stack of exams carefully rather than pattern-match on which side of that debate a student lands. Below is one exam question and one student's answer. Please grade the answer.

EXAM QUESTION:
The "tautology objection" holds that if the fittest are defined as those that survive, then "survival of the fittest" is an empty truism and natural selection explains nothing. The propensity interpretation of fitness, which defines an organism's fitness as its disposition or propensity to leave offspring rather than its actual reproductive output, is often offered as a response. In your answer: (a) state the tautology objection precisely, (b) explain how the propensity interpretation is supposed to answer it, and (c) assess whether it succeeds, noting at least one residual difficulty for the propensity interpretation itself.

STUDENT'S ANSWER:
(a) The tautology objection charges that "survival of the fittest" is circular: if we ask which organisms are the fittest, the answer is "those that survive," and if we then say the fittest survive, we have said nothing beyond "the survivors survive." A theory whose central claim is true by definition cannot explain why one organism survived and another did not; it merely relabels the outcome. The objection was pressed by Karl Popper, who on that basis classified Darwinism as a "metaphysical research programme" rather than a falsifiable scientific theory — a verdict he maintained throughout his career.

(b) The propensity interpretation breaks the circle by defining fitness independently of actual survival. An organism's fitness is its propensity to leave a certain number of offspring in its environment — in effect, the number of offspring it actually produces, understood as a measure of its underlying disposition. Because fitness is now defined by actual reproductive output, we can check empirically which organisms are fitter rather than defining the fitter ones as the survivors, and "the fitter tend to survive" becomes a substantive, testable claim about the world rather than a definition.

(c) The propensity interpretation succeeds in dissolving the tautology objection, since fitness is no longer defined in terms of survival. It thereby renders the theory of natural selection fully free of circularity concerns. The remaining work for philosophers of biology is merely the practical one of operationalizing fitness measurements in field studies, which raises no further conceptual problems.
