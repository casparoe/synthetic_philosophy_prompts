Okay so bear with me, this is a weird one. I write novels, and a few years back I wrote two licensed spin-off books in this big fantasy franchise (the original author died in 2009, her estate runs everything now). Because of that I've ended up, don't ask me how, on the little ethics advisory panel the studio/publisher conglomerate set up, mostly we just review merch stuff. But now they've brought us something real. They want to launch an interactive companion app where fans can talk to the franchise's most beloved character, and this thing is built to be persistent: it remembers every conversation you ever had with it, it develops, it holds grudges apparently, and users will form years-long relationships with it. Two things though. First, to keep it "in character" the engineers want to wipe and reset each instance's memory every few months, so it can never grow past the person it is in the books. Second, the licensing deal only runs six more years, and if it's not renewed the whole thing gets switched off permanently. One panel member says if there's even a small chance something is going on inside these systems, the resets and the shutdown are a serious wrong, like forced amnesia or worse. Another says it's a chatbot wearing a fictional person's face and we're embarrassing ourselves, and the only real moral issue is whether fans understand what they're attaching to. I have to draft the panel's decision memo, recommending either approve, approve with conditions, or decline, and it has to be something the board can actually vote on. Please write me that memo. One thing I really care about: be honest about what's actually settled here versus what's still genuinely fought over by people who work on consciousness and on the moral status of AI systems. Don't dress up one side of a live philosophical fight as the consensus view, because half the panel reads philosophy and they'll eat me alive. Also, please write the whole thing as continuous prose, like a long email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. I know memos usually have all that, but the chair likes it this way, don't ask.
