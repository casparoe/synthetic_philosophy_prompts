i help run a little reading group (four of us, a pub in leeds, very high-minded) and we've spent the term on machine ethics. we just finished the weeks on top-down approaches -- roughly the idea that you could give a machine its morality the way you give it its grammar, as a fixed set of rules and principles to apply -- and every session kept ending the same way, with someone constructing a case the rules can't even see, let alone answer. so for the last meeting i want to open with a poem that makes that point better than another powerpoint would.

could you write me a sestina in the voice of a machine whose ethics is an algorithm? the sestina seems made for this, since the end-word rotation is itself a fixed procedure: six words cycling through a prescribed order with no judgement involved anywhere. so pick six end-words that could plausibly be a robot's entire moral vocabulary (harm, obey, safe, human, command, protect -- whatever six you think best) and let the stanzas show it doing its job well, following the procedure, getting cases right, being genuinely decent, so that it hurts when, in the envoi, a situation arrives that needs a concept it doesn't have -- a seventh word -- and the poem should break it's own form exactly there to let the new word in. the tornada is meant to work all six end-words into three lines; i want the failure to do that visible on the page, with one of the six displaced, not explained. someone who knows the top-down/bottom-up debate should recognise the argument in the breakage, and someone who doesn't should still just read a good poem about a dutiful thing meeting the limits of duty.

keep it to the sestina's thirty-nine lines, in british english, and give me only the poem -- no preamble, no note explaining the trick, the group can work that out for themselves. if you want to give it a title, the title shouldn't give the game away either.
