So I'm in a third-year philosophy of technology seminar and we just spent two weeks on deepfakes and testimony, and there's one idea I can't stop thinking about: what our prof called the epistemic backstop. Roughly the thought is that recordings have functioned as the fallback of last resort in public disputes -- when testimony conflicts and institutions disagree, "the tape" settles it -- and the worry is that once any video can plausibly be dismissed as a deepfake, that fallback disappears even for footage that's completely real. There's also the flipside people call the liar's dividend, where the mere existence of deepfakes lets actual wrongdoers wave away genuine evidence against them.

I need to write a term paper (~15 pages) and I can only do justice to two or three questions. Which open questions in this area matter most, and in what order? Some candidates I've been toying with:

- whether the backstop argument romanticizes how much trust video ever deserved in the first place (surveillance footage, selective editing, context-stripping have misled people forever, long before generative AI)
- whether the real harm is people believing fake things or people losing the ability to settle true things -- i.e. misinformation vs. erosion of a shared fact-settling mechanism, and which of the two policy should actually target
- whether technical fixes like content provenance and watermarking even touch the philosophical problem, or just relocate where trust gets invested (now you have to trust the camera manufacturer, the platform, the standard body...)
- whether the backstop logic generalizes beyond video -- text, audio, eventually real-time video calls -- or whether video is a genuinely special case and why

Feel free to add a question I missed or argue that one of these is overrated. The thing I care about most is the reasoning behind the ordering: which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable for someone with a philosophy background and no ML expertise, and what developments over the next few years (detection arms races, platform adoption of provenance standards, some big public deepfake scandal) would move a question up or down the list.
