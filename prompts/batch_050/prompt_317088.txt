I work as a software engineer, and I've been pulled into drafting moderation rules for a marketplace platform — specifically, how our trust-and-safety team should decide whether a message between users counts as extortion. I have no philosophy background, but the legal folks mentioned in passing that this touches a known puzzle: a threat to do something you have every right to do, paired with a demand you have every right to make, somehow adds up to a crime, and nobody seems to agree on why. I tried sorting cases by gut and now I want to know what principle my gut is actually following.

Here are my verdicts:

1. A seller messages a buyer: pay me an extra $500 or I'll post the screenshots of our private negotiations. Clearly extortion; ban.
2. A buyer messages a seller: refund me in full or I'll file a complaint with the consumer protection agency, and my complaint is completely legitimate. Feels fine; this is just how disputes work.
3. An ex-employee messages the company: pay me a severance I have no contractual right to, or I'll tell a journalist about the safety violations I saw. My gut says this is wrong, even though reporting the violations would be legal and even praiseworthy, and even though the parallel case where she just reports them is obviously fine.
4. An ex-employee messages the company: reinstate me, or I'll report the wage-and-hour violations that cost me my job in the first place. This one feels okay to me, or at least much less bad than #3, though I can't fully justify the difference.
5. A user messages another: apologize for what you said in the group chat or I'll send your boss the screenshots. Feels like extortion, even though no money changes hands and both halves are legal.
6. A user messages another: stop using my copyrighted artwork as your avatar or I'll report you to the platform. Obviously fine, and yet structurally it looks identical to #5: do X I want, or I'll do Y I'm entitled to do.
7. A journalist messages a politician: give me an exclusive interview or I run the story without your side. Feels acceptable, though I notice it's a threat-plus-demand combo again.

Cases 3 and 4 are the pair I keep going back and forth on, since the demands differ but the threats are similar, and cases 5 and 6 bother me because my verdicts differ while the structure matches. My tentative principle is that a threat-plus-demand is permissible when the threat is a genuine remedy for the same wrong the demand is about, and extortion when the threatened act is just leverage rented from an unrelated grievance — but case 7 makes me suspect the principle needs a clause about whose interests the whole arrangement serves, and case 5 makes me suspect money isn't the right dividing line at all.

In your response, state your bottom line in the first sentence — the single best principle, or the verdict that my intuitions can't be captured by one — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the cases. I want: the principle stated precisely, with the case that motivates each clause; a direct explanation of how it resolves the puzzle that two separately legal acts become wrong in combination; its verdicts on two cases I haven't listed (one involving a public-interest whistleblower who asks for a reward, one involving a threat to do something illegal-but-trivial, like jaywalking, in exchange for money); and an honest list of which of my seven verdicts your principle has to overturn, with a defense of each overturning. If no principle survives all seven, say which one you'd sacrifice last and why.
