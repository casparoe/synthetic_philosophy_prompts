So the reading for my historiography seminar this month is the Fischer controversy and the whole war guilt question about WWI, and honestly I'm stuck on what kind of dispute this even is. It's been going for over a century: Fischer's claim in the early 60s that Germany deliberately pushed for war as a grab for world power, the furious response from German historians who said he was basicly handing Versailles propaganda a second life, then decades of archival work, and now the more recent sleepwalkers-style take that everyone stumbled into the war together and the blame gets spread around. What's weird is that the sides mostly seem to agree on the documents by now — the blank cheque to Austria-Hungary, the mobilization timetables, Bethmann Hollweg talking about calculated risk, the Septemberprogramm. Nobody is really disputing the archieve record anymore, and yet nobody has moved an inch.

My working theory: this stopped being a purely empirical disagreement a long time ago and is really three different disagreements stacked on top of each other that everyone keeps treating as one. Roughly: (1) a factual one about intentions and escalation in July 1914, (2) a philosophical one about whether a nation or state is the kind of thing that can be responsible for anything at all, or whether only individuals can, and (3) a moral-political one about what hangs on the answer — reparations, national identity, the whole postwar order. Am I right that the deadlock comes from people arguing at different levels without noticing? Or is there still a genuinely historical question buried in there that new evidence could settle?

Give me a diagnosis with reasons, engaging what the positions actually say rather than a survey of every historian's view. And given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like — a conceptual distinction from philosophy of action, a new kind of archival finding, or is this one of those debates that can't end because it isn't really one debate? Also, an honest answer on whether "who is to blame for WWI" is even a well-posed question. Please answer in American English.
