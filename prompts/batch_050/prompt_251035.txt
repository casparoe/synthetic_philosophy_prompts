So my mate Zeph and I are in this little decision theory reading group (we meet above a pub, there are five of us, two of them are basically just there for the crisps) and we've developed this running game where one of us invents a weird Omega-style scenario and the others have to give their verdict before anyone's allowed to argue. I've been quietly logging Zeph's answers all year because I have a pet theory that he's secretly consistent and just doesn't know it. His track record so far: bog-standard Newcomb with a near-perfect predictor, he one-boxes without hesitation. Smoking lesion, he smokes -- says the lesion's doing the damage and he's not going to "manage the evidence about himself" like some kind of PR department. Parfit-style hitchhiker, the version where he's already safely back in town and the driver's prediction was the only reason he got rescued: he pays the £100, says he'd be a fool to have been the sort of person who wouldn't. And counterfactual mugging, Omega's coin came up the bad way, he hands over the £100 as well, though he grumbled about it for a full ten minutes.

Here's my problem. I've realised his four verdicts don't actually pin down what he's doing, because I can think of at least three different rules that generate all four. Maybe he's running proper functional-decision-theory style reasoning, where what matters is what his decision subjunctively depends on, including the predictor's model of him. Maybe he's just got a personal policy of being the kind of agent predictors reward, adopted years ago after reading too much LessWrong, which is a different beast from deriving it fresh each time. Or maybe he's a patchwork: raw one-boxer intuition on Newcomb, bog-standard causal reasoning everywhere else, and the two payments are just ordinary promise-keeping and gratitude dressed up in Omega costumes. Next month I'm going to hit him with the transparent version of Newcomb -- both boxes see-through, and he can plainly see the million sitting in the big one. Does he take the opaque box too, since the money's already there whatever he does, or does he still leave the thousand on the table?

So: predict his verdict, tell me which of those three rules (or some fourth one I've missed) his track record best supports, and give me your reasoning rather than just a guess. If you think two of the candidate rules survive the transparent case as well, name the single follow-up scenario you'd put to him to prise them apart -- I've got him for at least another year of pub nights, so I can afford to play the long game. And please be honest about how confident you are: tell me which part of your reasoning is most likely to fall over, and what Zeph could say in the pub afterwards that would make you bin your whole diagnosis.
