Here's a proposal I keep running into in debates about influence: an attempt to change someone's mind or behavior counts as manipulation iff its effectiveness depends on the target not fully noticing or understanding how the influence is working on them. Call it the scrutiny test -- legitimate persuasion would still work if the target saw exactly what was being done to them, manipulation wouldn't. Write an essay evaluating whether this test actually carves the distinction at the right place. Stress-test it against awkward cases: a friend who knows guilt trips work on you and deliberately leans on one; a debater who openly announces she's about to use a framing trick and then does (and it works anyway); subliminal advertising; a doctor who presents a surgery's survival rate instead of its mortality rate, knowing full well you'd notice the switch if it were pointed out. Some of these seem to pass the test while still feeling slimy, others fail it while seeming fine. Does that show the test is too wide, too narrow, or tracking the wrong thing entirely? If it's broken, say what should fix or replace it -- and say whether passing whatever test you end up with settles whether the influence is morally ok, or whether that's a seperate question. Reach a verdict, don't end on a shrug.
