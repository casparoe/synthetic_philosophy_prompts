A workshop handout I’m using summarizes hermeneutical injustice as follows: “It occurs when a group’s socially significant experience has no established public concept, so its members cannot adequately understand or communicate that experience. The wrong is therefore a kind of testimonial injustice: hearers discount what speakers say because the needed words are missing. It counts as injustice only when dominant groups knowingly suppress a vocabulary that would otherwise be available; where the conceptual gap is nobody’s fault, there may be confusion but not injustice. Supplying the missing term remedies the hermeneutical problem, although lingering credibility deficits must be treated separately.” I suspect the second and third claims conflict with what I have heard about structural injustice, but I am not sure. Audit the summary sentence by sentence: which parts accurately state the standard account, which conflate hermeneutical and testimonial injustice, which overstate the role of individual blame, and whether the proposed remedy follows. Where a point is contested rather than simply false, say so.
