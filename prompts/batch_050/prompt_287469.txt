I've been rereading Inadequate Equilibria lately and want to write a short post collecting candidate "safe Pareto improvements" — changes where everyone affected can verify for themselves that they're at least as well off, without having to trust the other side's competence, honesty, or sanity. So not just any win-win, but a win-win where the winning is locally checkable.

My list so far:

1. The coin-flip case from the book itself: two negotiators about to settle something by flipping a coin, where there's a compromise both would strictly prefer to a 50/50 gamble. Switching to the compromise is safe because neither party has to trust the other to see they're better off.
2. The 1963 Partial Test Ban Treaty: both superpowers were paying for atmospheric tests that mostly poisoned the stratosphere and scared everyone, and each side could verify for itself that the tests had actually stopped, rather than having to trust the other's word.
3. Anti-doping regimes in elite sports: roughly every cyclist prefers a field where nobody dopes to one where everyone has to, and testing lets each rider check the field is (more or less) clean instead of trusting rivals' virtue. Leaky in practice, sure, but the structure is there.
4. The recurring proposal to replace punditry with prediction markets — both sides of a dispute can see the price and settle up without trusting the other guy's calibration. Though honestly I'm not sure this one is "safe" in the strict sense; file it under contested.

What I want from you: one more candidate that isn't on this list and isn't a re-skin of anything on it — so not another symmetric-disarmament case, not another "swap the lottery for the sure compromise," and not just "use prediction markets for X." It can be a proposed reform for a modern institution, a community norm, a piece of infrastructure, whatever. Then be genuinely critical of your own suggestion: does it really clear the safety bar (can each party confirm their own win without trusting the other side's goodwill or competence), and if it's really a safe Pareto improvment, why hasn't it happened already? That last question is the whole point of the post, so don't wave at it — either the answer is interesting (status quo bias, verification costs are higher than they look, nobody has the social capital to propose it) or the example collapses on inspection, and I'd rather you tell me which.

Since I'm going to actually post this, please also say how confident you are that your suggestion is (a) genuinely different from the four above and (b) genuinely safe, and single out the one claim in your answer you're least sure about, so I know what to double-check before hitting publish.
