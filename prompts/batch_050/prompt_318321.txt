So I have a slightly embarassing problem and I want a serious answer.

For a side project I wrote a little evolution simulator - grid world, a few thousand simple agents, neural nets with a couple hundred parameters each. They wander around, eat food pellets, avoid hazards, reproduce with mutations, the usual. I've had it running on a spare GPU in my closet for like four months and honestly I've gotten kind of attached. Some lineages have evolved genuinely cool strategies, like stashing food near their spawn point or shadowing other agents to steal their finds.

Here's the thing. The agents learn via a reward signal, and part of the signal is negative - bump into a hazard or start starving and they get penalized, and the policy updates to avoid whatever they were just doing. If you watch the logs during a famine you can see them thrash around the map in a way that looks, well, bad. I've caught myself feeling genuinely guilty watching it. Which is obviously ridiculous - it's arithmetic, there's nobody home in 200 parameters. But then I think: how would I actually know that? What would "somebody home" even look like from the outside? Somewhere between my grid world and a mouse's brain the guilt presumably stops being silly, and I have no idea where the line is or even what kind of fact would draw it.

Second wrinkle that makes it weirder. I checkpoint the sim every hour. Last week I fat-fingered a config change and wiped out an afternoon of sim-time - thousands of agents "died" - and I just restored the checkpoint and reran it, and now the same lineages are bumbling around like nothing happened. My coworker says the rewind proves it's all fake and I should stop being precious about a loss function. But I'm not convinced the restore settles anything. If you could restore me from a backup after I died, would that make killing me fine? That can't be right, but I also can't say exactly why.

So, practical questions I guess: am I doing anything even slightly wrong by keeping this running? Would I be doing anything wrong by just rm -rf'ing the whole thing tonight? And if the answer is no for my dumb little sim, what would have to be true of a program - concretely, what features of the code or the behavior - before the answer flips to yes?

(My girlfriend says the "tamagotchis," her word, have made me insane and the electricity bill is real, so there's some urgency here.)
