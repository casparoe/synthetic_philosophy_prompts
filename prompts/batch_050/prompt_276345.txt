Here's the situation as I understand it. In Book 3 Schopenhauer argues that the arts generally work by presenting the Platonic Ideas, i.e. the adequate objectifications of the will at its various grades, and that aesthetic contemplation is a release from suffering precisely because in it we stop being striving, willing individuals and become what he calls the pure subject of knowing. But then when he gets to music he does something weird. In the passage I'm working from (§52, paraphrasing into my own words here, please check this paraphrase for me!) he says that music is not, like the other arts, a copy of the Ideas, but rather a copy of the will itself, and that this is why its effect on us is so much more powerful and penetrating than that of the other arts. He also develops this whole analogy where the definite grades of the will's objectification in nature correspond to elements of music, so the ground bass is like inorganic nature, the lowest grade, and then the harmony rising above it is like the plant and animal worlds, and the melody up top is like the highest grade, the intellectual life and striving of man, with all its delays, detours, and final resolutions. My essay's central claim is that this creates a serious problem for Schopenhauer that commentators don't push on hard enough: if aesthetic experience is supposed to be valuable because it silences the will and gives us peace from striving, then music should be the worst art, not the best one, because it's the one art that doesn't even bother with the Ideas and instead feeds us the will neat, undiluted. I argue that his only way out is to say that music gives us the will as representation, at a distance, so we contemplate the inner nature of willing without actually being dragged through our own willing, and that this is a kind of knowledge by acquaintance with the thing in itself that the other arts can't give. Then in the last section I try to test this against the melody analogy: I claim that when he says melody mirrors human striving, with its digressions and its return to the keynote like a satisfied wish, he's basically admitting that listening to a symphony is a simulation of the whole will-driven human condition, suffering included, and that we enjoy it because for once the striving isn't ours.
