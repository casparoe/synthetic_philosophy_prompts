Remember the MU-puzzle from Gödel, Escher, Bach -- start with MI, apply some string-rewriting rules, try to reach MU? Heres a variant system. The axiom is XY, and the rules are:

1. If you have a string consisting of X followed by some string w, you may produce X followed by two copies of w. (So from XYZ you could get XYZYZ.)
2. If a string ends in Y, you may append a Z.
3. Any occurrence of five Ys in a row (YYYYY) may be replaced by a single Z.
4. Any occurrence of ZZ may be deleted.

Argue that you can never derive the string XZ in this system. I want a real invariant argument, not handwaving or just trying a bunch of derivations and giving up. Two extra things: first, the famous invariant from the original MU-puzzle (the relevant letter count is never divisible by 3) does not work here as-is, so explain exactly why it fails and what invariant does the job instead. Second, say a couple sentences about in what sense your argument involves jumping out of the system rather than working inside it, and why no amount of forward-deriving strings from the axiom could ever settle the question on its own.
