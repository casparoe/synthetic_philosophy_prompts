Two people in my reading group have been arguing for months about the gender-equality paradox -- the finding that wealthier, more gender-equal countries often show more gender-segregated career choices, not less. One of them thinks it's basically nature: once economic necessity and external pressure recede, average differences in interests just show up in the data. The other thinks it's still culture: richer societies express gender norms differently, e.g. through ideas about free choice and self-fulfillment that let people act on stereotypes while feeling like they're just being themselves. Write the joint statement the two of them would produce after a genuine adversarial collaboration: the claims about the cross-national data and it's limits that both can sign; the exact disagreements, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own position; the cruxes; and for each crux, the evidence or study design both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Also: somewhere in the document, say explicitly which parts of their fight are empirical (what causes the pattern) and which are actually about justice (what, if anything, anyone should do about it) -- I suspect they keep sliding between the two without noticing.
