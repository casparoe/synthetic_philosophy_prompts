Okay so I help run a local rationality-ish reading group (we've drifted from actual LW sequences into general philosophy of mind / ethics, ~14 regulars, mix of grad students and software people who read SSC back in the day). We meet for about 90 minutes. Next session is on intuition pumps -- Dennett's term for thought experiments as tools that steer you, and the general LW-adjacent worry that trolley problems, Chinese rooms, Mary's room etc. work by getting you to feel something rather than showing something.

I want an in-person activity that makes the group *feel* how easy it is to steer an intuition before we ever use the phrase "intuition pump." Design it for me. Here's the shape I'm imagining, tell me if it works or what to change:

Something where some participants get to be pump-builders and others are the pump-targets, and the builders are secretly handed knobs to turn -- vivid irrelevant details, order of presentation, whether the case is framed as action or omission, named characters vs anonymous ones, scale of stakes -- and the targets' gut verdicts actually move when the knobs turn, even though the underlying dilemma is held fixed. Ideally there's some small stake (chocolate, points, bragging rights) so the builders try to win rather than aiming at "what's philosophically interesting," because I want the steering to be *strategic*, the way it is in published thought experiments where the author wants a particular verdict.

What I need from you:

1. The actual setup: how many builders vs targets, what materials, what the fixed dilemma should be (pick one that isn't the trolley problem -- everyone's pattern-matched that to death and they'll just recite the literature), and the concrete list of knobs the builders are allowed to turn. The knobs should be ones that arguably *shouldn't* matter to the right answer, and a couple of edge cases where reasonable people disagree about whether the knob is actually morally relevant -- that's a fight I want to have in the debrief.
2. A scoring/payoff scheme that incentivizes the builders to move verdicts without being able to just lie about the case, and that keeps the targets honest (no reward for being stubborn).
3. A timeline for the 90 minutes, including when the reveal happens (targets find out which knobs were turned on them) -- I'm guessing mid-session, not at the end, so we get post-reveal rounds where everyone knows the game and we can watch whether knowledge protects them.
4. The debrief: map specific moments onto the mechanisms (framing, concreteness, the way a thought experiment smuggles in its conclusion by choosing which details to make vivid). Also: how do I handle the person who'll inevitably say "my intuitions didn't move, this proves nothing" -- and what if they're right and the effect is weak in a smart forewarned group?
5. What will predictably go wrong the first run, and the one thing this activity structurally can't show about real intuition pumps that I'll have to just say out loud.

Keep it runnable off one printed page per participant.
