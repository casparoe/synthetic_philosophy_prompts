So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background at all, and I got sucked into this after a dumb argument in our team Slack about whether "the platform" we run — like 40ish microservices spread across three cloud regions — is actually one thing or just a label we slap on a bunch of machines for billing and dashboards. Googling around at 1am I found out philosophers apparently have a name for this, the "special composition question," i.e. when do a bunch of things add up to one further thing. So I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and honestly they dont line up as cleanly as I expected. Here's where I land: my coffee mug — one thing, obviously. Same mug after I dropped it and it's in five pieces on the kitchen floor — not one thing anymore, just shards. Two lego bricks snapped together — one thing. Same two bricks pressed together really hard with my fingers — not one thing. A jigsaw puzzle with the last piece just placed — one thing, even though nothing's glued, it's just friction, which I realize sits weirdly next to the lego verdict. A swarm of bees — kind of one thing? I'd say "the swarm is angry," they move and react as a unit. But a concert crowd doing the wave — not one thing, even though they're arguably coordinating just as much, so that pair bugs me too. And my grandparents' dining table, where over the decades literally every part has been repaired or replaced — still one thing, still the same table.

What I want: a principle (or two candidate principles, if one clean one won't do it) that captures most of these verdicts, stated precisely — not "things compose when they form a unity" hand-waving, but something I could actually apply to a new case and get a verdict out. Tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, if any, and don't hide it if the price of a tidy principle is overturning one of my gut calls. And apply it to the case I genuinely can't decide: my car is at the mechanic right now with the engine sitting on a bench next to it and two wheels off — is it one thing at this exact moment, or a pile of parts that happen to be in the same building? Max 350 words.
