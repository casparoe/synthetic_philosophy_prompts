I am a retired condensed matter physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has been working my way through the philosophy of mind and modality with a reading group of other latecomers to the subject. We have agreed to circulate short written statements of our positions for mutual criticism, and before I embarrass myself I would like an audit of mine. You are a professional philosopher with expertise in modality and the conceivability literature; please think carefully, begin by briefly restating each commitment in your own words so I can check you have understood me, and only then proceed to the analysis. Here is where I currently stand: (1) Conceivability is a fallible but genuine guide to possibility — if a scenario survives clear and distinct reflection, on ideal consideration, without contradiction, that is defeasible evidence that it is metaphysically possible. (2) Philosophical zombies — beings molecule-for-molecule identical to conscious humans but lacking all inner experience — are conceivable in this demanding sense, and I conclude on that basis that they are metaphysically possible. (3) I accept the standard post-Kripke view of natural kinds: "water is H2O" is necessary though a posteriori, and what one imagines when one imagines water without H2O is strictly a superficially similar substance, so no genuine conceivability of the impossible occurs there. (4) Likewise in mathematics: if Goldbach's conjecture is true it is necessarily true, and my ability to picture a counterexample being announced on the news shows nothing about its possible falsity. (5) Throughout my scientific career "possible" simply meant consistent with the actual laws of nature, and I do not believe there are several disconnected kinds of possibility floating around; there is one modal notion, and philosophical claims invoking it must ultimately mesh with the scientific usage.

What I want from you is not a survey of the literature but a consistency audit. Which of these commitments pull against one another, and how severely? Please rank the tensions rather than treating all candidate conflicts as equally serious, and in each case state precisely what would have to give — which commitment must be weakened, restricted, or abandoned, and at what cost to the overall position. I would also like you to distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones: I have a suspicion that at least one pair in this list looks incompatible but dissolves once a distinction I have already half-granted elsewhere in the list is applied consistently, and I would like to see whether you identify the same pair. Conversely, I worry that I am helping myself to something in one commitment that another commitment quietly rules out, in a way I have not noticed. Finally, if the set is not jointly tenable, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether the resulting position is one a scientifically minded person could hold without embarrassment.
