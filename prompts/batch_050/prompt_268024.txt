I sit on the deployment ethics board of a company whose triage assistant chats with patients in overcrowded ERs, and we have to pick one of two reasoning profiles before launch. The utilitarian build will bend the truth when it expects good outcomes -- e.g. telling a wavering patient that a bed is free right now to get them to stay, when really it's an hour away -- and our pilot data says this measurably reduces walkouts and probably some deaths. The deontological build never lies and never withholds options, and in the same pilots it loses a few of those patients. The wrinkle I want the memo to wrestle with: word is getting out that the system "optimizes," and some patients are starting to distrust everything it says, which is itself a welfare cost the utilitarian profile has to count -- so our engineer proposes a two-level setup where designers use utilitarian reasoning to lock in deontological rules the assistant never breaks. Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote: recommend one of the three options (or approve with concrete conditions), give an actionable bottom line, and give the strongest losing-side consideration a fair run rather than a strawman. I'm leaning deontological-with-exceptions but haven't commited, so don't just tell me what I want to hear.
