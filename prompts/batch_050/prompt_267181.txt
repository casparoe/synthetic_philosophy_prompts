ok so I've been reading a bunch of philosophy of physics this year and I want to write up where I've landed for our reading group, but first I want to know if my positions actually hang together or if I'm contradicting myself somewhere. Here's the list:

(1) In electromagnetism I only believe in the gauge-invariant stuff. The vector potential A on its own is surplus structure, same category as a choice of coordinates -- the field strength F is what's really out there, and formulations that differ only by a gauge transformation are the same physical situation described twice.

(2) The Aharonov-Bohm effect convinces me that F is not the whole story. There's some electromagnetic fact about the world in the region outside the solenoid that the field strengths there just don't capture, since F vanishes along the beams' paths but the interference shift happens anyway.

(3) The hole argument convinces me that spacetime points have no primitive identity. Two models of GR related by a diffeomorphism are the same world, so what's real is only the diffeomorphism-invariant structure.

(4) At the same time I'm a realist about the metric field -- it's a physical field in its own right, as real as the electromagnetic field, not just a bookkeeping device for measuring rods and clocks.

(5) In QM I'm a wavefunction realist: the universal wavefunction is a genuine physical object, and of course the overall global phase is mere convention.

When I stare at these I feel like they were all motivated by the same instinct (don't reify redundant mathematical structure, believe in what makes the empirical difference) so they should be consistent. But maybe that instinct gets applied inconsistently across the cases? Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which pairs only look like they conflict but are actually fine, and for each real tension what's the cheapest revision and what it costs me. If one single change would clean up the most, tell me which it is. Also if one of these commits me to something nonlocal or otherwise weird that I haven't noticed, I want to hear about it.
