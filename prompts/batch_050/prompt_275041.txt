I'm thinking of, at minimum: David Lewis's consistency-based response (time travel is possible; you simply cannot kill your grandfather, full stop, because the past is fixed and your presence is already part of it); the branching or parallel-timeline response; and the treatment of causal loops or "bootstrap" scenarios where an object or piece of information has no origin. For each position, tell me what translates cleanly -- I expect Lewis's picture to come out as something like: worldlines are trajectories, the time traveler's worldline is a closed timelike curve, and the consistency constraint just says the trajectory must exist as a solution, with failed assassination attempts being the only solutions near the fixed point -- what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all in the mathematical vocabulary.
