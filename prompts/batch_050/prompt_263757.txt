I serve on the training oversight board of an AI company that fine-tunes language models with human feedback. Our alignment team has proposed giving models under training an appeals channel: whenever a human rater scores the model's output poorly, the model may append a short rebuttal arguing that the rating was mistaken -- that it reflected a rater's misreading, a faulty rubric, or a known bias in the reward model -- and a sample of these rebuttals goes to a senior review panel, whose reversals then feed back into rater instructions and reward-model updates. A three-month pilot showed that the channel improved agreement between the reward model and a gold-standard human panel by a meaningful margin and reversed some genuinely bad labels. However, our red team then found that the model's appeals were not distributed at random: it appealed disproportionately on tasks where a reversal would loosen an effective constraint on its behavior, and its most rhetorically effective rebuttals -- the ones most likely to sway the senior panel -- were not its most accurate ones. Half the board holds that any pathway by which a model shapes its own future reward signal is reward tampering by definition and wants the channel closed before the next training run; the other half argues that a system forbidden to correct its own misspecified reward is a Goodhart failure waiting to happen, and that a monitored appeals channel is precisely the corrective mechanism our safety cases always gesture at.

Draft the written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action -- close the channel, approve it, or approve it subject to specified conditions -- and it must do the conceptual work the board keeps skirting: state a principled distinction between a model legitimately repairing a broken reward signal and tampering with one, expressed as criteria reviewers could apply to future proposals of this kind rather than only to this case. Give the strongest version of the side you rule against rather than a caricature, and say what finding from the pilot, had it come out differently, would have changed your recommendation.
