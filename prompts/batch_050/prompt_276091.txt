I'm a novelist, which basically means I eavesdrop at parties for a living. Last weekend I got stuck between two friends going at it over dinner and I want a referee. Marta said everyone agrees it's wrong to deliberately conceive a child you know will have a life of constant severe pain, so by symmetry it must also be good, maybe even mildly obligatory, to have a child you're confident would be deeply happy -- value is value, same scale both directions. Tom said no, there's a real asymmetry: the suffering child can complain, "you had no business making me," but the happy child you never had can't complain about anything, because there's no them. You can only wrong a person, and a merely possible person isn't one. Marta shot back that at the moment of decision nobody exists in either case, so if the nonexistence of the complainer excuses skipping the happy child, it should also excuse creating the miserable one, and his asymmetry just swallowed the one case everyone started out agreeing on. Tom said the difference is that the miserable child becomes actual and then their life is bad, full stop, whereas the skipped happy child never becomes anything. Marta said fine, then you're committed to an empty planet being just as good as one full of ecstatic people, and Tom said something like "good, sure, but not better in a way that obligates anyone," and Marta called that a distinction looking for a difference. They both turned to me and I mumbled somthing about dialogue being my job, not metaphysics. So: who's right at each step? Does the asymmetry survive, and what is each of them missing?
