(2) "A price you judge fair" just means the price at which, given your credences, you're indifferent between buying the bet and not buying it — i.e., the expected monetary value of the bet computed with your own credences. That's the standard operationalization the argument runs on.

(5) By (3), declining the fair bet and buying the insurance are both irrational: she refuses bets at exactly the prices her credences make fair, and accepts packages at prices worse than fair.
(6) But risk aversion and insurance are plainly not irrational — they're the most boringly rational behaviors around.
(7) Therefore something in (1)–(3) is wrong, and the Dutch book argument, at least in its standard form, doesn't actually justify probabilism.
