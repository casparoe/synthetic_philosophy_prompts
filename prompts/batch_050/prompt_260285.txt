I serve as the student representative on my high school's technology advisory committee, and next week we vote on whether to adopt camera software that flags students who appear distracted or distressed so that teachers can intervene early. The vendor's internal studies report no evidence of harm, but there is little independent research, and critics raise concerns about false flags, uneven accuracy across different students, and the effect of feeling constantly watched. Our chair argues we should approve it, since no one has demonstrated actual harm and students in crisis might get help sooner; a teacher on the committee argues we should refuse until the vendor proves it safe, since students should not have to show they were harmed after the fact. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote: recommend approval, rejection, or a conditional pilot; explain who should bear the burden of proof in a case like this and why; and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the side you reject, including what we stand to lose by waiting if you advise us to wait.
