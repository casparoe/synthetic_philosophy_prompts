In the Black Mirror episode "USS Callister," Daly's digital crew insist on their suffering, beg for mercy, and ultimately rebel, while Daly dismisses them as lines of code. Read the episode through epiphenomenalism and the paradox of phenomenal judgment. The worry I want pressed is this: if the copies' phenomenal states were causally inert, their pleas and screams would not be caused by their pain, and so would give us and Daly no evidence that anything hurts. Does the episode's moral force quietly presuppose that pain causes avowals of pain, in a way an epiphenomenalist viewer must disown, or can the epiphenomenalist recover the intuition that Daly does something monstrous? And does the drama itself, where anguish visibly drives planning and rebellion, dramatize the very causal role epiphenomenalism denies to experience? I'm writing an essay on whether science fiction about digital minds smuggles in a theory of consciousness while pretending merely to ask whether the copies are conscious.
