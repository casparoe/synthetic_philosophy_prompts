So I'm teaching a unit on moral psychology and I keep going back and forth on this myself, so I'd like to see it argued out properly. Write a dialogue between two philosophers (give them names, make them both sharp, no strawmen) disagreeing about whether psychopaths actually *know* that what they do is wrong.

Some ground rules for what I want in the dialogue:

- One of them defends the view that psychopaths don't genuinely possess moral knowledge -- that when a psychopath says "stealing is wrong" it's more like a foreigner reciting the local customs, correct words without the real thing. The other defends the view that psychopaths know full well what's wrong and just don't care, and that this tells us something important about the connection between moral judgment and motivation.
- They should get into the actual evidence, but keep it at the level of well-known findings rather than citing specific papers: the fact that psychopaths are shaky at picking out which transgressions are wrong in a way that doesn't depend on rules or authority (vs. just wrong because you'd get punished), the fact that some studies seem to show they *can* draw that line when asked carefully and just don't behave accordingly, and the contrast case of people who suffer certain kinds of brain damage as adults and end up able to pass every moral test you give them while their actual conduct falls apart. The second character should use that contrast against the first, and the first should have a real answer to it, not a deflection.
- They should actually engage each other -- each response should pick up on what the other just said, concede points where they have to, and the disagreement should get narrower and more precise as it goes, ending on the crux rather than one of them "winning."
- No mention of the names of real philosophers' positions by label (no name-dropping isms), I want it in plain terms.

Maybe 12-16 exchanges. After the dialogue, give me your own take as the author on which of them has the stronger position and why, in one sentence, as the last line, formatted exactly like this:
