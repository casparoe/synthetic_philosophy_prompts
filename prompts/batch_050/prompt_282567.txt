You are a metaphysician who has published on the ontology of propositions. Think carefully, and reason from what the parties to this debate actually argue rather than from textbook summaries.

Since Bradley pressed it in Appearance and Reality, the regress problem about the unity of the proposition has generated a standing literature: if a proposition's unity consists in a relation holding between its constituents, that relation must itself be related to those constituents, which requires further relations without end, so either propositions are not genuinely unified or unity cannot be a relation. The standard replies are all long on the table -- Frege's doctrine that concepts are unsaturated and complete themselves without glue, Russell's relating relations, appeals to a non-relational tie, and deflationists who hold that the very demand for a unifier is the mistake. Yet no side has ever converted the other; each regards the others' moves as simply declining the problem.

My working hypothesis: the debate has stalled because it is really a disagreement about what may legitimately be demanded of an explanation -- the regress-mongers treat unity as a fact requiring a ground, the deflationists treat a's being F as explanatory bedrock -- while both sides share, without noticing, a prior assumption that a proposition is a complex assembled from parts, so the real fork sits upstream of everything they actually argue about.
