My gut verdicts on who should pay for university, and I want to know what principle (if any) they're all instances of:

- Poor student studying engineering, charged full tuition with loans: feels wrong.
- Same engineering degree, rich parents paying full freight: feels fine, even good.
- Med student racking up $200k in debt: feels okay-ish, she'll earn it back.
- Fine arts student racking up the same debt: feels bad, like a trap.
- Nursing degree at moderate tuition: feels wrong — we desperately need nurses and then underpay them.
- Germany charging basically nothing to everyone, funded out of general taxation: feels right to me.
- But: a factory worker's taxes paying for a rich kid's free philosophy degree: feels unfair.
- Someone who drops out with debt and no degree: the worst case of all.

Is there one princple that delivers all of these, or do I have to drop one? My hunch is something like "charge when the degree is a private investment, subsidize when it's a public service," but that doesn't obviously handle the fine arts case (it's neither lucrative nor socially essential, and I still think charging full price for it is predatory), and I suspect my verdicts on the German system and the factory worker pull apart somehow. Also say what your principle implies about master's programs and second degrees, where I keep flip-flopping.
