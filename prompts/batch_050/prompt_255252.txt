I direct a four-person risk office at a regional public-health agency. Roughly twice a month we must decide whether to act on preliminary evidence: issuing a warning about a possibly contaminated water source, restricting a supplement flagged in a single adverse-event cluster, pulling a product after an unconfirmed lab result, or instead saying nothing while further tests run. Every decision is mine to sign, reviewable afterward by the agency director, and we typically have days, not months, before the evidence either firms up or the question resolves itself.

I want to ground our decisions in the ethics of belief dispute between William James and W.K. Clifford -- Clifford's rule that it is wrong always and everywhere to believe on insufficient evidence, against James's claim that on genuine options (forced, living, momentous) awaiting full evidence is itself a choice and belief may legitimately run ahead of proof. Please turn that dispute into a decision procedure my office can actually run on a case: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and a definite rule at each branch for whether Clifford's discipline or James's license governs. The procedure must at minimum handle the distinction between questions where waiting leaves the evidence available and questions where waiting destroys the option; between beliefs that mainly expose the believer to error and beliefs that expose third parties to harm; and between hypotheses we could in principle test within our deadline and ones we cannot. Mark explicitly the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and the procedure is judgment dressed up as rules. Where the two philosophers genuinely conflict rather than merely emphasize different cases, take a side and defend it -- do not leave me a menu.

Then run the procedure on three of our actual recurring cases: (1) a warning about possible contamination of a town well based on one unconfirmed positive sample, where a false alarm costs the water utility heavily; (2) whether our office should internally adopt the belief that a widely used supplement is unsafe, where confirmation trials will take two years and meanwhile we must write guidance; (3) a case where the procedure gives an answer I will dislike, and say why I will dislike it. End with a short statement of what the procedure cannot settle. The entire response must be under 750 words.
