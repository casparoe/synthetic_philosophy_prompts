so i'm prepping the last meeting of a reading group on political obligation and we spent the whole term circling around thoreau's essay Civil Disobedience (the one he first published as "Resistance to Civil Government"). i want to close with a poem built on this passage, which honestly caused the most argument all semester:

"It is not desirable to cultivate a respect for the law, so much as for the right. The only obligation which I have a right to assume, is to do at any time what I think right."

write me a villanelle that takes this seriously. heres the thing i want the form to do: one refrain line should belong to the respect-for-law side and the other to the respect-for-right side, so the way villanelle refrains keep dragging the poem back to the same two lines enacts the citizen getting pulled between the two demands over and over. but the poem has to notice something our group almost missed -- thoreau doesn't say obeying the right is a duty laid on you, he says its the only obligation he has a *right to assume*, i.e. the obligation is self-assigned, not handed down by anyone, not even by morality-as-an-authority. the closing quatrain should land on that, not on some generic "follow your conscience" sentiment.

so: standard 19-line villanelle, no more, and then at most two sentences after it telling me what you did with the refrains. someone whos actually read the essay should recognize the argument in it, not just the vibe.
