I'm in a grad seminar on social epistemology and we just spent two weeks on deepfakes. What grabbed me wasn't the obvious worry -- that people will get fooled by fake videos -- but the flip side our prof kept pushing on: once everyone knows convincing fakes are possible, anyone caught on genuine footage can just shrug and say it's AI. She called this stuff the erosion of recording as an "epistemic backstop" and the "liar's dividend," and the whole seminar kept joking that the real business opportunity isn't in making deepfakes, it's in selling doubt. Nobody's actually written that satire as far as I know, so I want to do it for the creative assignment that's due instead of a final paper.

Write me the client-facing services brochure (with a short FAQ at the end) for a fictional crisis-communications consultancy -- you can invent the name, something blandly corporate -- whose flagship offering is essentially deniability management for public figures. The firm's whole pitch is that it never fabricates anything. Not one pixel. Its product is purely ambient: it works to keep the public vaguely aware that video evidence can't be trusted in general, so that when authentic footage of a client surfaces, the client can dismiss it without the firm ever having to claim anything specific. Around 600-700 words, deadpan LinkedIn-corporate voice, upbeat and full of the usual consultancy jargon.

The humor should come from getting the epistemology exactly right, not from exaggerating it into nonsense. Some beats I'd love to see worked in: a tiered pricing structure (the expensive tier somehow involves less visible activity, not more); the firm's strict internal policy against ever producing an actual fake, because an exposed fabrication would "re-authenticate" everything else and ruin the market; the fact that the firm proudly sponsors media literacy campaigns teaching the public that any video could be generated, since awareness is literally the product; and an FAQ answer explaining why their worst-case scenario is a piece of client footage getting formally verified as real. The piece should never use phrases like liar's dividend or epistemic backstop -- the reader should recognize the concept purely from the business model. Keep the irony totally legible but never wink at it: the brochure should read like the firm genuinely believes it's a normal, even socially responsible, service. Sustained deadpan the whole way through, and end on the FAQ.
