Right, so I've just been picked for one of those citizens' assemblies the council is running on local planning (letter came last week, proper lottery-style selection, nearly binned it thinking it was a scam) and I've gone down a rabbit hole trying to work out what the actual point of the thing is meant to be.

Everyone involved keeps saying the assembly is justified because it's a kind of portrait in miniature of the area -- we statistically mirror the population, so what we conclude carries the weight of what the public would conclude. But the organisers also keep banging on about the expert briefings we'll get, the facilitated discussion, the weekends to mull things over, the whole point being that we end up with views *better* than whatever the man on the street currently thinks. And these two stories seem to pull against each other, no? If the magic is that we mirror the population, then everything that makes us *unlike* the population (getting briefed, deliberating, the self-selection of who bothers to show up) seems to erode the justification. But if the magic is the deliberation, why select at random at all -- why not just recruit thoughtful people, or indeed have elected councillors do it, since they deliberate too and at least someone voted for them?

So what I'd like explained, at a conceptual level: are these genuinely two different justifications for sortition that can come apart, or one justification with two aspects that I'm wrongly splitting? And where does the "nobody can vote you lot out" objection fit in -- is that a third, seperate problem, or is it secretly just the mirroring argument again wearing a different hat? I'm not after a reading list or citations, just help getting the logical structure of the debate straight before I have to sit in a draughty community hall and actually do the thing.
