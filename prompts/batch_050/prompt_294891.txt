I've been going down the population ethics rabbithole again (the usual ACX-adjacent stuff, Parfit, Benatar, the whole asymmetry debate) and I want to figure out what principle my gut is actually running on, because it doesn't feel consistent. Here are my verdicts:

1. A couple knows any child they conceive has a 50% chance of a condition involving chronic severe pain and an early death. They conceive anyway, even though they could have adopted. Verdict: clearly wrong.

2. A different couple decides not to have kids at all, even though any child of theirs would very likely have a great life. Verdict: not wrong. Not even a little bit wrong. Nobody failed to do their duty here.

3. Someone has a kid largely because they want to bring one more happy person into existence, and the kid does in fact turn out very happy. Verdict: fine, obviously, but not praiseworthy. They don't get moral credit the way someone who donates a kidney to a stranger does.

4. A rich eccentric offers to fully fund ten additional children, raised by willing families who were on the fence, with every indication the kids will be well off and glad to exist. Verdict: this seems genuinely good, maybe even a real missed opportunity if he keeps his money. Which I'm pretty sure clashes with 2 and 3.

5. Two healthy survivors of some catastrophe could restart humanity, and there's decent reason to think the descendants would mostly have lives worth living. Verdict: I feel a real pull toward "they should," which clashes with 2 even harder than 4 does.

6. A farmer who treats his animals well decides whether to breed another generation of pigs. Verdict: feels completely neutral, like choosing what crop to plant, even though it's the same "create happy beings or don't" structure as 4 and 5. That one I can't even tell wether it's a tension or just a fact about me.

So: is there one principle that gets me most of these? I know the standard move is some person-affecting view (you can wrong existing people, but merely possible people have no claims), but I don't see how that delivers 4 and 5, and I don't see how a simple total-view delivers 2 and 3. Give me the best candidate principle or two, stated precisely, tell me which of my verdicts I'd have to give up to hold it, and then -- this is the part I actually care about -- tell me what it predicts for cases I haven't listed: terraforming Mars and filling it with happy colonists at trivial cost, and creating a large number of sentient digital minds whose lives would be excellent. The digital minds case is where I suspect my intuitions will just lie to me, so say it straight.
