My brother-in-law cornered me with this at a barbecue and it's been eating at me for weeks. He says you can prove that a great life is no better than a horrible one, and I think the argument is airtight except for the fact that it obviously can't be.

Here's how it goes. Imagine two lives. Life A is 80 years of genuine happiness -- love, work you care about, good health, the whole thing. Life Z is 80 years of constant severe pain, nothing else. Surely A is better than Z. Now here's the move:

(1) You can build a chain of lives from A down to Z where each life differs from the one before it by only a tiny change -- like, one headache on one Tuesday afternoon becomes slightly sharper, or one good nap gets cut short by a minute.

(2) For any two neighbors in the chain, neither is better than the other. A life with a slightly worse Tuesday nap is not a worse life. Nobody could even notice the difference from the inside.

(3) So by walking down the chain, A is no better than its neighbor, which is no better than the next, and so on, until A is no better than Z.

(4) But A is obviously better than Z. So something in (1)-(3) has to go.

(5) (1) is just true -- you can always make the changes small enough. (2) also looks undeniable, because if you couldn't tell the difference even living both lives, on what basis could one be better? (3) is just logic. So he says the real conclusion is that "better than" is an illusion, or at least that it can't survive scrutiny, and we only think A beats Z because we're lazy and don't inspect the chain.

Now obviously I refuse the conclusion. But honestly I can't find the bad step. The one thing I keep circling is (2): it feels like there's a difference between "you couldn't notice the difference" and "there's no difference in how good the life is," but when I press on it I can't say what grounds the second one if not the first. And I really don't want to say that some microscopic change you couldn't possibly detect makes a life better, because that seems crazy too.

Please don't just give me a menu of the standard options and let me pick -- I want you to actually take a side. Tell me which specific step you'd reject, why it looked so plausible (because it did, to me), and what I have to swallow if I reject it -- like, what other innocent-seeming claims I now can't hold. And if you think the chain reasoning should be kept and something else has to give instead, say that and defend it.
