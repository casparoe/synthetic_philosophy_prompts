As I understand it, Löb's theorem shows that no consistent formal system capable of basic arithmetic can assert its own soundness. It seems to me this settles a question about AI: a proof-based agent can never rationally trust an exact copy or successor of itself, since trusting the successor just is trusting itself, which the theorem forbids — and since recursive self-improvement requires each generation to be trusted by the last, intelligence explosion through self-modification must be mathematically impossible, not merely difficult. Am I right that this is a hard no-go result? If so, is there any known workaround engineers could use to build genuinely self-trusting agents despite the theorem — say, by weakening the logic, adding randomness, or keeping a human in the loop — or do such moves amount to cheating by making the successor no longer a true successor?
