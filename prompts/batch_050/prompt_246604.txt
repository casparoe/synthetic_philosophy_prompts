Construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. I am interested in the value-of-information theorem associated with I. J. Good: roughly, that for a Bayesian agent facing a decision, who will update by conditionalization on whatever they learn and then choose the act that maximizes expected utility given their new beliefs, the expected value of learning the answer to a relevant question for free is never negative — so such an agent should always accept cost-free information. Build a single-agent decision problem in which a Bayesian agent would nonetheless be better off not learning the answer to a question relevant to a decision they face — better off in the strong sense that they would, if offered the choice, pay a small positive sum to remain ignorant.

Constraints: (i) The information is genuinely free: no cost of acquisition, transmission, storage, or delay. (ii) There is no second agent whose behavior depends on what the first agent knows, and no one observes whether the agent has inquired; that route to negative value is well understood and I am not interested in it. (iii) The scenario should be realistic — a decision an ordinary person might actually face — and fit in a single paragraph. (iv) The verdict must be generated by the setup rather than stipulated: no announcing that the agent simply prefers ignorance.

After the scenario, do the work. State the decision, the question on offer, and the computation of expected utility with and without the information; show that remaining ignorant carries strictly higher expected utility. Then identify exactly which assumption of the theorem your case relaxes, since a theorem is a theorem — a genuine counterexample must be a violation of some assumption in disguise — and explain why that relaxation, rather than an error in the arithmetic, produces the negative value. Finally, say why a skeptic could not reclassify your case as smuggling in a second agent or a hidden processing cost, and what happens to the verdict if the assumption you relaxed is restored.
