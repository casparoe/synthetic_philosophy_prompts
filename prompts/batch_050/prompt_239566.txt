The two finalists are: (1) a really polished charcoal portrait that almost everyone loves, super realistic, parents always stop and look at it, and (2) this huge messy abstract canvas with jagged black and red shapes that a lot of students straight up say is ugly or that "my little cousin could do that." Here's the thing though -- Ms. R says the second one is by far the more serious work. Idk if that solves anything though bc Ms. R would obviously count as one of those judges, and she'd pick the abstract one, but my friend would say Hume was just an art snob from the 1700s and that a hallway in OUR school should have what the students actually like looking at every day.

- give real reasons, and take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side instead of just dunking on it
- deal with the underlying question of whether "trained" judgment should outweigh popular opinion in a case like this, without just repeating "it's all subjective" or "the expert is always right"

Keep it like a page or less since nobody on the committee will read more than that lol. Also don't just say "compromise!" -- if the answer is a compromise, explain why that specific one is fair and not just a cop-out.
