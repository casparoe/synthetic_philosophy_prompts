I'm a novelist and I've somehow agreed to give the opening address at a small literary festival whose theme this year is 'Minds on the Page'. The room will be roughly half fiction writers and half cognitive science students, and I'd like to give them something with real bones instead of the usual misty stuff about the mystery of the creative act. So write the speech, built around the idea that minds come in grades: creatures that just get shaped by selection (Darwinian), creatures that can also learn from reward within a lifetime (Skinnerian), creatures that can try out plans in their heads and let the bad ones die there instead of dying for real (Popperian), and creatures that hand part of their thinking over to tools, language and other minds (Gregorian). The ladder has to do actual work, not just be a nice metaphor: I want it to settle what can honestly be said about my own characters, who don't think at all and yet make readers weep, and about the chatbots the audience will definately ask me about afterwards. Around ten minutes, funny in places, and it mustn't turn into a lecture for either half of the room -- the writers shouldn't glaze over, the scientists shouldn't wince. British English please.
