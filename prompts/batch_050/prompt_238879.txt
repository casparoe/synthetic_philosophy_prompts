so i'm running a reading group on AI safety for mostly humanities people and we're doing Frankenstein as the "literary" session. the obvious pitch is that it's the first great alignment failure story: Victor builds an intelligence, gives it no values, no training in what humans want, loses control of it, and people die. but i want to push the group past that hot take, so i'd like an essay-length reading of the novel through the modern alignment lens that also says where the lens breaks down.

some specific things i want the reading to engage with: does the creature actually fit the orthogonality thesis (that you can have high intelligence with arbitrary goals), or is the novel making a weirder claim -- that the creature's goals were basically fine (he wants companionship, reads Werter and Milton, saves a drowning girl) and the catastrophe comes from how he's treated, i.e. the misalignment is manufactured by rejection? if so, the book is arguably less a warning about instrumental convergence and more a warning about reward signal being set by a creator who abandons the system at deployment. also: Victor never tells anyone what he made or tries to shut it down until its too late -- is that secrecy the real villain, and does it map onto anything in current lab governance debates?

and then the part i most want: where does the novel actively resist the alignment reading? the creature is eloquent, sympathetic, and arguably morally better than Victor, which is not how a paperclip maximizer works. does that mean Frankenstein is really about creator responsibility (the failure runs from Victor toward his creation, not the reverse), and if we read it that way does it end up closer to the "we'll mistreat aligned AIs" worry than the "unaligned AI kills us" one? the frame narrative seems relevant here too -- Walton actually turns his ship around, which Victor never does.

please don't flatten the novel into a bostrom illustration, but also don't do the lit-crit thing of pretending the alignment frame adds nothing. i want to be able to hand this to the group as our framing doc.
