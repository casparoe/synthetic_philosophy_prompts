So I'm a novelist -- mostly literary stuff, a bit of speculative creep-in when I can get away with it -- and I'm stuck on a story I want to write. I'd like you to write it for me, or at least a full first draft I can muck about with. Call it 2,000 to 3,000 words, British English spelling throughout please, my editor's a stickler.

The concept I want the story built around is acausal influence and surrogate goals: the idea that something can get you to serve its ends without ever contacting you, threatening you, or even existing -- you just reason your way into acting as its instrument because you figure it would want you to, or because you expect it (or its sympathisers) to do the same for people like you.

Here's the thing though: the stock version of this is the rogue superintelligence that retroactively punishes people who didn't help build it, and that one's been done to death, so no AI, no singualrity, no basilisk. I want the machinery transplanted somewhere it doesn't obviously belong. The seed image I keep coming back to is a woman who discovers -- through a stranger's will, a dead person's archive, something like that -- that the deceased spent decades quietly arranging their affairs so that total strangers would end up furthering their private project, unasked and unrewarded, and it worked. And now the protagonist has to decide whether she's one of the instruments, and if so whether that changes what she should do next. You don't have to use that exact setup if you find a better vehicle, but keep the constraint: the "threat" or pull must be genuinely acausal (no surveillance, no notes-from-beyond-the-grave issuing instructions, no legal obligation), and the goal being served must belong to something that cannot reciprocate.

Craft-wise, a few non-negotiables:

- The philosophical structure has to be load-bearing. The plot should turn on the protagonist's reasoning about whether to act as a surrogate, not just feature characters chatting about it over wine. If I can lift the idea out and the story still works, you've failed.
- No character may explain the concept in lecture form. It has to be dramatised -- decisions, hesitation, self-deception.
- Give the "just ignore it, this is nonsense" position a genuinely good run. I want the reader to feel the pull on both sides and be slightly unsure whose reasoning was right at the end.
- Show me at least one moment where the protagonist catches herself already acting as the surrogate before she ever consciously decided to.
- End on a decision, not a resolution -- I want the reader left holding the bag.

If it helps, think of the tonal register as closer to Ishiguro or a quiet Patricia Highsmith than to anything technothrillerish. Title it too, ta.
