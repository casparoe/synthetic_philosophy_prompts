I am a novelist working on a utopian book, and I have noticed a pattern in my own drafts that I suspect philosophers have already studied and named. Please answer as a moral philosopher with particular expertise in value theory. Think carefully: begin by briefly brainstorming the candidate concepts this might be, then settle on the best fit.

Here is the pattern. My first draft described a small society, a few thousand people whose lives are rich, deep, and vividly happy. In revision I kept enlarging the world: another valley, another city, each new community somewhat less flourishing than the last, but still made up of people who are, on balance, glad to be alive. At every individual step, adding the new community seemed to make the fictional world better: more lives worth living, and no existing life made worse. Yet after enough rounds of this I arrived at a teeming world of billions whose lives are thin, gray, and only barely worth living, and the manuscript now feels to me, and to my early readers, like a failure: somehow worse than the small society I began with, even though I reached it through a chain of steps each of which seemed to be an improvement. Two things interest me. First, the tension between stepwise improvement and the endpoint's apparent badness. Second, I genuinely cannot tell whether my unease is a judgment about which world is morally better, or merely about which world makes a better novel: the small society is simply more vivid on the page, and perhaps my readers would rank the real populations differently from the fictional ones.

Does this phenomenon have an established name and literature? If so, tell me which of the nearby concepts fits my case best, and how my case as described differs from each of the others. If the case, including the moral-versus-aesthetic ambiguity, does not quite match anything established, say so plainly and coin a term for it, with a precise definition and some boundary cases. A bare label will not satisfy me; what I want is the carving.

Close with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing the name you judge the best fit, whether established or coined.
