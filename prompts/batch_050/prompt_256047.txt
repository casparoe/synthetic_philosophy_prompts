So I was reading about futarchy, the Robin Hanson thing where you vote on values but bet on beliefs, and I think I get the gist but something's bugging me. The idea is you set up prediction markets on whether some policy will work, like "if we pass this tax bill, will GDP be higher in 5 years", and then you pass whichever policy the market is most optimistic about. But here's my problem: a prediction market only ever settles after the fact, right? You find out who won the bet years later when GDP numbers come in. So the market can't actually tell you anything before you implement the policy — it's just a fancy way of scoring politicians after the fact. At that point how is futarchy any different from just voting normally and then checking the statistics afterwards to see if things worked out? Everyone says markets "aggregate information" but the information literally doesn't exist yet when you need to make the decision. Am I missing something or is the whole idea kind of circular?
