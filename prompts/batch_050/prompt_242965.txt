Write me a short story built around the Orwellian-vs-Stalinesque puzzle about when experiences happen. Setup: it's the near future and there's a consumer gadet that records and replays your own memories with perfect fidelity. The protagonist replays some small moment — say, an LED that seemed to drift across a dark room and switch color halfway, color-phi style — and the replay shows something that shouldn't be possible on either account of how that illusion works. Make the plot turn on the difference between the two options: either the experience was staged before it ever "aired" (the Stalinesque show trial — the brain fakes the color change and backdates it), or it was genuinely lived one way and then rewritten in memory a split second later (the Orwellian revision, Ministry-of-Truth style). Bonus points if the story lands on the really disturbing bit: even perfect memory replay can't settle which one happened, because the replay is itself just more record-keeping. Please don't have characters lecture the philosophy at each other — let it come out in what they do and argue about. Then, after the story, add a short author's note saying where you took liberties with the ideas — especially whether you treated Dennett's point as "there's no fact of the matter about which happened" versus "there's a fact but it's forever out of reach" — and what consideration would make you rewrite the ending the other way.
