I'm putting together a long post for my little sociology blog pushing back on all the "ok boomer" / "Gen Z is killing X industry" discourse, and I want to end it with a flowchart-style decision tree readers can walk themselves through. Basically: you start with whatever you currently think when someone says "Millennials are entitled" and end at a named position on what generations even ARE.

Here's what I need from you:

Build the tree from the questions, not from a list of positions. I want maybe 4-5 branching questions, each phrased so a reader can answer it without knowing any of the labels -- so concrete claims or cases, not "do you believe generations are natural kinds?" Good candidate splits (feel free to reword or replace): (a) when we say a generation is different from the last one, are we usually just describing people at the same age in different eras (a 25-year-old today vs a 25-year-old in 1990), so it's a stage-of-life thing wearing a cohort costume; (b) do the boundaries between cohorts track anything real (a war, a recession, a technology arriving in your teens) or are the cutoff years basically arbitrary and could've been drawn five years earlier; (c) can the label do any explanatory work -- does being a Millennial cause anything, or does it just sit next to correlations that income and region explain better; (d) does it matter whether members of the cohort themselves feel like a unit, or can a generation be real even if nobody in it identifies with it.

Then locate the actual positions at the leaves. I want these to show up at minimum, even if under your own labels: the full skeptic (generations are arbitrary bins and horoscope-tier discourse), the realist who thinks shared formative events genuinely forge cohorts with a distinctive outlook (I associate this with Mannheim but don't name-drop in the tree itself, my readers won't know him), the statistical skeptic who accepts cohorts exist as demographics but thinks generation talk mostly confuses age effects, period effects, and cohort effects, and the person who thinks "generation" is only legit as a family-kinship term (grandparents/parents/kids) and the pop usage is just borrowing the word. If some combination of answers has no defender, still leave a leaf there and tell me briefly why it's empty -- incoherent, unappealing, or just nobody's bothered.

Two more asks. First, if any two of the positions above can't be separated by my questions, say so honestly rather than inventing a fake distinguishing question -- that actually helps my post. Second, each leaf should get one sentence on the most awkward thing that position commits you to (e.g. the full skeptic has to explain why 18-year-olds in 2008 really do seem marked by graduating into a recession).

Keep it in American English, and casual enough that my readers (mostly not academics) can follow it. Oh and the post is called "There Is No Such Thing As Gen Z" so the tree should still work for readers who end up at the realist leaf and feel betrayed by my title -- a one-line note to those readers at the end would be great.
