I run a small reading group that has spent the autumn on panpsychism, and we have reached the point where the literature seems to us to offer a fairly closed menu of responses to the combination problem — the question of how, if fundamental physical entities have experience, many small experiences could yield a single unified experience like yours or mine. For our final session I want to present the menu and then ask whether it is really exhaustive. The four options we have catalogued are these. First, constitutive subject-summing: micro-subjects with micro-experiences literally add up to, or together constitute, a macro-subject with a unified experience; this is the view that inherits William James's worry that a crowd of feelings does not become a felt unity just by being crowded together. Second, panqualityism in Coleman's style: the fundamental things are qualitative but not subjects at all, so there are no small subjects needing to combine, and subjecthood appears only at the macro level; this dodges combination at the cost, some of us think, of merely relocating the hard problem and of ceasing to be recognizably panpsychist. Third, cosmopsychism: reverse the direction of explanation, take the universe as a whole to be the one fundamental subject, and treat ordinary minds as decompositions or limitations of it; this converts the combination problem into a decombination problem, and we are unpersuaded that decombination is any easier. Fourth, emergent or fusion views, including phenomenal bonding: micro-experiences enter into a fundamental bonding relation that generates a novel macro-experience over and above them; this looks to us like naming the gap rather than closing it, since the bonding relation appears to be brute and otherwise undetectable.

What I would like from you is a fifth response to the combination problem that is genuinely off this menu — not a rewording of one of the four, not a special case of one, and not a hybrid of two of them. A couple of moves I would count as failing this test: panprotopsychism of the sort where the fundamental properties are merely proto-phenomenal strikes me as a cousin of panqualityism rather than a new option, and simply denying that macro-experience needs to be constituted out of anything is a refusal of the problem rather than a response to it, unless you can show the refusal itself does explanatory work. If you can give me a genuinely distinct fifth option, state it as precisely as you can, say which of the standard objections it escapes and which it inherits, and give your honest assessment of whether it is any good. If, after trying, you conclude that the menu is in fact exhaustive — that every possible response collapses into one of the four, or into rejecting the problem — make that case instead and say where the impossibility comes from.
