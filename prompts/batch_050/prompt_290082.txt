I'm in the philosophy elective at my high school, and we've just spent two weeks on hedonism. The basic idea that pleasure is the one thing good in itself seemed almost obvious at first, but then our teacher posed what she called the heterogeneity problem: what do all the different pleasures actually have in common that makes them all pleasures? She told us to go home and collect our own intuitions about which experiences count as pleasures before trying to answer. I've done that, and my verdicts don't seem to hang together, so I'd like help figuring out what principle, if any, captures them.

Here are my cases. The cold water I drank after soccer practice when I was desperately thirsty: definitely pleasure. The strange glow I felt for a whole evening after a teacher praised my essay in front of the class: pleasure too, even though it felt nothing like the water. The state I get into when I'm deep into a difficult piano piece and lose track of time: I'd call it one of the best things in my life, yet when I introspect I can't locate any positive feeling at all, so I'm genuinely unsure whether it counts. The moment a bad headache finally fades: it feels wonderful, but part of me suspects it's just the absence of pain rather than a pleasure proper. The burn of extremely spicy food, which I actively seek out and would miss if it were gone: it literally hurts, but I want to count it as pleasure. The small satisfying feeling when a classmate who was rude to me trips in the hallway: it undeniably feels good in the moment, but I'm reluctant to call it a pleasure because I'd be embarrassed to admit I felt it. And nostalgia, the warm ache I get looking at photos from elementary school: it seems to be partly pleasant and partly painful in a way I can't separate, so I don't know whether to count it once, twice, or not at all.

Is there one account of what pleasure is that delivers all or most of these verdicts? I've heard of two candidate styles of answer: one says all pleasures share some common felt quality, however subtle; the other says pleasure isn't a feeling at all but rather an attitude we take toward whatever we're experiencing, like wanting it to continue. I'd like you to state one or two such principles precisely, show which of my verdicts each clause handles, and be honest about which verdicts each principle has to overturn. In particular, please tell me what your favored account predicts for the flow state and for nostalgia, since those are the cases I keep changing my mind about, and whether resolving them cleanly is worth giving up my verdict about the headache relief, which is the one I feel most confident in.
