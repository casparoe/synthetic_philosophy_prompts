I'm in a philosophy of biology reading group and we've now spent three sessions on the function debate and honestly nothing is moving. The setup is the standard one: the etiological or selected-effects people say a trait's function is whatever it was historically selected for (the heart's function is pumping blood because that's why hearts are still around), and the causal-role people say a function is just whatever a part actually does that contributes to some capacity of the system it's in (so the heart's noise-making is as much a "function" as its pumping, if you're analyzing the right system). Both sides have their killer cases memorized — vestigial organs and malfunctions on one side, the Swampman duplicate and the sheer messiness of selection history on the other — and both sides just... keep presenting them.

My working hypothesis, which I want tested rather than adopted, is that this stopped being one disagreement a long time ago: the etiological camp is really asking "why is this trait here" and the causal-role camp is really asking "how does this system work," and "function" is being asked to name the answer to both. If that's right the debate is a turf war over a word, and the resolution isn't an argument but a divorce. But maybe that's too easy — maybe there's a genuine fact of the matter about what function-talk is *for* in biology that one side is right about, or maybe the real dispute is empirical (about what working biologists actually need the concept to do) and neither camp has done that work.

So: diagnose the disagreement. Which kind is it — verbal, substantive, two questions under one name, a hidden empirical question, or bedrock intuitions that can't be argued for? Support the diagnosis with what each side actually commits to, not the cartoon version. And tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a concept-splitting, an empirical study of biological practice, a formal result, or an argument that the debate should just dissolve. If you think my two-questions hypothesis is wrong, say so directly.
