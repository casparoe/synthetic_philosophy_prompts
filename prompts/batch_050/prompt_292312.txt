Draft the joint statement that could result from an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who disagree about Avicenna's flying man argument. The first holds that the thought experiment — a person created fully formed, suspended in mid-air, limbs apart, with all sensory input removed, who would nonetheless be aware of their own existence — establishes that the self is not identical with the body, or at least that awareness of oneself is independent of awareness of one's body. The second holds that it shows at most an epistemic gap between self-concepts and body-concepts, fully compatible with physicalism.

I want: a numbered section of claims both could sign, including what the thought experiment genuinely demonstrates, if anything; a table of residual disagreements, each worded so that both parties would accept the formulation of their own position; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party; and for each crux, the argument, case, or distinction both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction of what that consideration would show written down in advance. Where an apparent disagreement turns out to be verbal — for instance, over what awareness of oneself must involve, or whether the suspended person truly lacks all bodily representation — identify it as such rather than papering over it. Consider in particular whether the analogy with the knowledge argument is a genuine crux for both parties or a distraction. Do not adjudicate the debate: the deliverable is a document both philosophers could put their names to, with the surviving disagreement made exact.
