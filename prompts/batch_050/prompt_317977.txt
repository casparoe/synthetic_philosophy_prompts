Here's a debate that seems to have gone nowhere in decades: how can purely instrumental music sound sad? No one is sad, nothing sad is being depicted, there's just sound. The standard moves are all on the table — the music resembles the vocal/gestural profile of a sad person, the music disposes *us* to feel something that we then hear as sadness, the music expresses the emotion of an imagined persona, the music just *is* sad in some sui generis way we should stop trying to analyze. Everyone knows all the arguments and objections (resemblance seems too thin to explain the depth of the experience, arousal can't explain why we hear the emotion *in* the music rather than just feeling it, the persona seems like a philosopher's invention nobody actually hears), and nobody's position has budged. My suspicion is that the sides aren't disagreeing about the music at all but about what an explanation of the phenomenology has to deliver — one side wants a mechanism, the other wants a description of the experience that leaves it intact — and they're talking past each other under one question. Diagnose it: is that right, or is there a real substantive disagreement here, and what would actually count as progress given your diagnosis? I want you to commit to a definite reading of what kind of impasse this is, not a survey of the options. Lead with your verdict in the first sentence, then give the reasoning — no preamble, no restating my question.
