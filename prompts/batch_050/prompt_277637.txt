So I'm a third-year PhD student and my supervisor has basically given me free rein to spend my remaining three-ish years on hinge epistemology, the whole cluster of work that's grown out of Wittgenstein's On Certainty (Moyal-Sharrock, Wright, Pritchard, Coliva, the extended-cognition people, whoever). The problem is the field feels sprawling and I can't work on everything, so I want help building an actual research agenda rather than a survey. Here's how I see the candidate problems, though tell me if I'm miscarving things: first, the constitutive question of what hinges even are -- propositions we're certain of, rules, ways of acting, animal certainties -- since every answer seems to change what the rest of the view can say. Second, the epistemic status question: whether hinges can be known or justified at all, or whether they're arational but not irrational, and what that does to closure and to skepticism. Third, the disagreement problem: if two communities run on different hinges, is rational resolution even possible, or does the view collapse into relativism, and does the answer change if the hinges are held non-propositionally. Fourth, applications: deep disagreement and conspiracy belief, testimony and trust, religious belief, and recently people stretching hinges to cover things like our reliance on instruments, algorithms, and now AI systems, which feels promising but also maybe a stretch too far. Fifth, the revision question that I almost never see treated head-on: hinges plainly do change over time (Wittgenstein's own river-bed image suggests as much), but how does a hinge get revised from the inside, by a believer who can only reason from within their own hinges? Which of these should I prioritize, and in what order? I want a defended ranking, not a menu: pick the one or two questions that are genuinely upstream of the others, in the sense that getting them right would settle or reshape the rest, and be honest about which ones you'd drop entirely because they're either intractable with the tools we have or because their answer wouldn't actually change much downstream. Also tell me what would make you reorder the list, since I want to know which assumptions the ranking rests on. Please be committed -- I'd rather get a definite agenda I can push back on than a balanced lay of the land. And write it as continuous prose, like an email from a well-read friend: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
