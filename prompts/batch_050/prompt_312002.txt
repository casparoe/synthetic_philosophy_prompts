Ever since I started maintaining a small open-source project last year, I've been faintly baffled that strangers turn up and fix bugs for free — no pay, no CV line anyone will ever check. I've fallen down the evolution-of-cooperation rabbit hole as a result, and I'm tired of surveys that list every mechanism as if they all mattered equally. I want actual bets.

Here's the usual menu: kin selection, direct reciprocity (repeated tit-for-tat style interaction), indirect receprocity via reputation, costly punishment of defectors, and group or multilevel selection. For everyday cooperation among unrelated humans — contributors, neighbours, colleagues, donors, not bees — what fraction of the action does each mechanism account for? Give me five fractions that sum to 1. Note that kin selection is on the list even though my question concerns non-relatives, so don't just copy textbook weightings; tell me what you actually think it earns here.

Then, briefly:
1. One sentence each defending your two biggest fractions.
2. Your smallest fraction, plus one concrete setting where even that mechanism still does real work.
3. Which mechanism you consider most overhyped relative to the evidence, and which most underrated.

Please write the answer in American English — I'm aware of the irony of requesting that in this spelling.
