I'm prepping for a philosophy of physics reading group where everyone has to circulate a one-page "position statement" beforehand, and mine is on whether beauty is evidence in math and physics. Before I send it around I want someone to stress-test it, because I suspect I've contradicted myself somewhere but I can't see where. Here's what I hold. First, that elegance is a genuine guide to truth and not just a labour-saving device: when two theories fit the data equally well, the more beautiful one really is more likely to be true, and physicists are right to bet on it. Second, I'm a pretty strict Bayesian about evidence -- something only counts as evidence for a hypothesis if it's more likely given that hypothesis than given its rivals, and a warm feeling of rightness is a psychological event in me, and no fact about my psychology can by itself make one theory of the external world more probable than another. Third, on cases: I think the history shows aesthetic judgment is fallible but better than chance -- Kepler's Platonic-solids cosmology was gorgeous and false, the simplest grand unified theories were elegent and got ruled out, but general relativity and the Dirac equation were backed partly on beauty and won, and on balance following your taste pays off. Fourth, I endorse what Poincaré says in his essay Mathematical Discovery, which I take to be that the aesthetic sense works as a kind of filter, picking which of the countless unconsciously generated combinations of ideas get to rise into consciousness, and I think this account of his actually supports my first claim that beauty tracks truth. Fifth, I don't think the universe was guaranteed to be beautiful -- no metaphysical aesthetic realism, beauty is a fact about us, not about the deep structure of reality.

What I want from you is an audit, not a survey of the literature. Go through these five commitments and tell me which pairs are in real tension, which pairs only look like they clash but are fine once you're careful, and rank the real tensions by how serious they are. For each genuine tension, say exactly what I'd have to give up or weaken to get out of it, and what that costs the overall picture. Then end by committing to a verdict: which single revision would make the whole set coherent at the lowest cost to what I care about, and why that one rather than the alternatives. Please actually pick one -- I don't want a menu, I want to be told where the body is buried. And if you think one of my five claims is being leaned on to do work it can't do (say, a claim about how we discover theories being used to support a claim about which theories are likely true), call that out explicitly, since I have a nagging feeling that's where the problem is but I'd rather you reached it yourself than took my word for it.
