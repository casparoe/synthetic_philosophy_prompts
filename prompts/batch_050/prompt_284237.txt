I have no training in philosophy or politics, but I have been chewing on a distinction I cannot find a word for, and I would like to know whether there is an established concept for it — and, just as much, where its boundaries lie.

There are situations in which nobody is actually stopping me from doing anything, yet I feel distinctly unfree. My previous employer never once overruled me, but he could have reversed any decision of mine without giving a reason, and we all knew it. A friend is married to a kind, generous man who would never dream of restricting her — yet every account is in his name, and she mentions running purchases past him "just in case". Nothing is prohibited in either case; something is clearly amiss.

On the other hand, there are situations in which I really am obstructed — the flight is cancelled, the motorway is closed, my knee will not manage the stairs — and although this is frustrating, I do not feel unfree in anything like the same way. No one is doing it to me.

So the pattern seems to be: what matters is not whether I am in fact being interfered with, but whether somebody could interfere at will and face no consequences for it. Three questions. First, does this notion of unfreedom — exposure to another's unaccountable power rather than actual obstruction — have a name, and is there a recognised tradition behind it? Second, how does it differ from the more familiar idea of freedom as simply not being prevented from acting? Third, where are its edges: does it stretch to cases where the constraint comes from something with no will at all, such as market forces or an automated system, where a person is hemmed in but nobody is standing over them?

A bare label will not satisfy me. I would like the carving itself: what the concept includes, what it excludes, and which of my examples fall inside and outside it. Please answer in British English.
