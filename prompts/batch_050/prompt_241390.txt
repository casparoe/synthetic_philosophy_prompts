Construct a thought experiment in which tranquilism — the axiological view that only the absence of craving and suffering has value, so that any state entirely free of craving is exactly as good as any other, however much or little pleasure it contains — delivers a verdict opposed both to total hedonistic utilitarianism and to ordinary person-affecting intuition. Constraints: the case must involve no death, no pain, and no one being made worse off in any everyday sense; it should be realistic and present-day (a policymaker, a physician, or a prospective parent — not a utility monster, a world-destroying button, or a drug that erases all desire); the scenario itself should be statable in one paragraph; and the divergence must follow from tranquilism's indifference among craving-free states rather than from any distortion of the view. After presenting the scenario, state the verdict each of the three views gives and why, identify which single feature of the case is doing the work and what happens if it is removed, and explain how a defender of tranquilism would respond to the charge that the verdict your case forces on them is absurd.
