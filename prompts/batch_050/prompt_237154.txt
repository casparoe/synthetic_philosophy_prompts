I use a myoelectric prosthetic hand and recently joined the design team that maintains its control software. Last month, an update began misclassifying the muscle pattern for a precision grip as the pattern for a power grip. Until then, I had rarely thought about the hand while using it; now I am aware of its latency, confidence thresholds, calibration routine, and error messages almost every time I reach for something. This has made Heidegger’s account of equipment feel less like intellectual history than like a design problem.

I am fluent in the vocabulary of human-computer interaction and control systems: affordances, feedback, latency, mode errors, automation transparency, user models, cognitive load, embodiment, trust calibration, and graceful degradation. Restate Heidegger’s account of readiness-to-hand, breakdown, conspicuousness, and presence-at-hand in that vocabulary as faithfully as possible. Include the idea that a tool belongs to a wider network of tasks, other tools, purposes, and social roles, not merely the point about tools becoming noticeable when they malfunction.

Then give a residue report, organized as:

1. A precise mapping between Heideggerian notions and HCI or control-system notions.
2. Cases where the translation works only with distortion, especially the difference between an object of attention and an object contemplated as merely present.
3. Parts of Heidegger’s account that have no real counterpart in the design vocabulary.
4. Features of my prosthesis case that strain both frameworks, particularly the unstable boundary between body, tool, interface, and environment.
5. A judgment about whether the residue exposes a limitation of HCI’s vocabulary, an unhelpful vagueness in Heidegger, or both.

Please do not reduce the account to the slogan that good design is invisible. I want to know exactly what kind of non-invisibility is involved in ordinary skilled use, what breakdown reveals that routine use conceals, and whether a device can remain ready-to-hand for one activity while becoming conspicuous within a larger activity. Lead with your bottom-line answer in the first sentence, then give the reasoning with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
