So here's roughly where I've landed on hypocrisy and who's allowed to blame whom, and I'd like an audit of whether the package hangs together:

(1) Hypocrites lose their standing to blame others for the very thing they're guilty of themselves -- someone having an affair can't call out their partner's affair, and the partner can rightly say so.
(2) But what a hypocrite says can still be true and worth taking seriously; the message and the messenger are seperate things.
(3) Nobody needs special credentials or a clean record to blame in the first place -- any member of the moral community can call out wrongdoing, not just victims or the innocent.
(4) It's totally legitimate to respond to hypocritical blame with "look who's talking" and just dismiss it on that basis.
(5) Blame should be judged on whether it's accurate and proportionate, not on features of the person delivering it.

Is this consistent? If not, tell me exactly which commitments pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict but don't, and what's the cheapest fix and what it costs me. Don't just flag everything.
