So I'm a retired chemist, 68, and I've been reading philosophy for fun lately, which is maybe why I'm overthinking a family situation. My mother is 91, lives alone two states away, and is starting to genuinely need help -- bills, doctor visits, probably a move within the year. My brother's position is simple: we owe her this, full stop, because of everything she did raising us. Here's the thing though. She was a decent parent, never cruel, but we were never close, and honestly if she were a neighbor I'd met last year I wouldn't fly out monthly to sort her paperwork. I do feel like something is owed, I just can't tell whether its grounded in the past (she sacrificed, so now it's payback) or in whatever relationship we actually have now, which is pretty thin. My brother's debt framing feels off to me but I can't articulate why, and the alternative -- that the obligation tracks how good the relationship is -- seems to have a nasty consequence, namely that you could wriggle out of it just by drifting apart, which can't be right either. So what does an adult child actually owe an aging parent in a case like this? And please tell me how confident you are in your answer, and flag the single point you're least sure about.
