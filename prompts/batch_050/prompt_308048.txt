My sister-in-law teaches in a primary school and we got into one of those long pub arguments about diagnoses in kids, and it's left me with a pattern I can't find a word for. Say a label starts circulating -- something like "highly sensitive" or a mild flavour of attention disorder. The thing I keep noticing is that the label seems to change the child *without the child ever knowing about it*. The parents read the book, the teacher goes on the training day, and suddenly ordinary fussiness gets read as sensory overwhelm, so the kid gets moved to a quieter corner, excused from the noisy assembly, spoken to in a special calm voice, never pushed to just get on with it. Ten years on the kid genuinely is more withdrawn and noise-averse than they'd otherwise have been -- but not because they absorbed some idea of themselves from the label, since nobody ever told them they had it. The environment did all the work.

I know there's a literature on how being classified changes people -- I read Hacking years ago and mostly remember the broad idea that people come to fit the descriptions they're given. But the cases I remember involve the labelled person reacting to their own label, either growing into it or kicking against it. Here the person is oblivious; it's everyone *around* them who changes, and the change in the person is downstream of that. I also know the old finding that teachers' expectations shape outcomes, but that seems to be about the label changing how others judge or grade the kid, not the physical and social environment rearranging itself around them in ways that then manufacture the trait for real. And I don't think it's quite a self-fulfilling prophecy in the ordinary sense either, because nobody involved is predicting the kid will be difficult -- they're often being kind and accomodating, which is what makes it sticky.

So: is there an established name for this specific route -- label changes third parties, third parties change the milieu, milieu makes the label true -- as distinct from the standard looping story? If there is, I want to know how it's distinguished from Hacking-style looping and from plain labelling theory, not just the bare term. If there isn't, coin one, define it properly, and tell me where it stops applying: for instance, does it still count if the accommodations never actually produce the trait but only make a pre-existing one more visible, or if the kid catches on later and starts using the label themselves?
