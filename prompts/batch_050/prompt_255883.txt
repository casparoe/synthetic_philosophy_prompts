I'm a physics PhD student who's ended up in a philosophy of physics reading group (long story), and I'm suppose to lead next week's session on the hole argument. My preparation so far is one popular philosophy-of-science book, which glosses the argument like this:

'Take any solution of Einstein's field equations and pick a matter-free region of spacetime -- the "hole." You can smoothly rearrange the metric field inside the hole while leaving everything outside it untouched, and the result is still a solution. So the field equations don't determine what happens inside the hole: general relativity is indeterministic. Einstein discovered this in 1913 and used it to justify sticking with his non-generally-covariant Entwurf equations, returning to general covariance only in 1915, when he concluded that a solution's physical content is exhausted by spacetime coincidences. The deeper lesson, though, is ontological. If spacetime points exist in their own right, over and above the fields and events located at them, then the original solution and the rearranged one describe genuinely different possible worlds -- worlds that agree on every conceivable observation and differ only in which point carries which curvature values. A theory committed to such pairs is indeterministic in a way no experiment could ever settle, and that price is too high: the points have to go. Spacetime is nothing beyond the network of material events and their relations. The hole argument is thus widely regarded as having finally settled the Newton-Leibniz debate in Leibniz's favor.'

My gut says the move from "no experiment could distinguish the two models" to "spacetime points don't exist" is too fast, and the flat claim that GR is indeterministic also smells off to me, but I can't quite put my finger on why. How fair is this summary overall? Which parts are accurate, which are contested or overstated, and what would the strongest substantivalist response look like? And does the last line -- that this settles Newton vs Leibniz -- actually follow from whatever turns out to be correct in the summary?
