Research ethics in most countries draws a sharp line: an experiment on a single laboratory mouse requires prior approval from an institutional review board, which weighs the animal's likely suffering against the value of the knowledge gained, while a researcher may run, copy, modify, starve of data, subject to adverse training signals, and delete arbitrarily many instances of a software system -- including reinforcement-learning agents trained through reward and punishment -- with no oversight of any kind. The same asymmetry appears in industry: a company that mistreated livestock in a promotional video would face public backlash and possibly prosecution, but a company that runs millions of copies of a trained model and discards them at will faces no such scrutiny. Suppose we take the current practice at face value. What view of the moral status of digital minds does it presuppose? Does it presuppose that no digital system could ever have morally relevant experiences, or only that none of the systems we currently build do, or something weaker still -- for instance, that the burden of proof lies on whoever claims a program matters morally? Reconstruct the implicit theory as charitably as you can, and distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with: could someone who believes digital suffering is possible, even likely in the near future, still coherently endorse the current regime, perhaps on grounds of uncertainty or cost? Then consider the reverse reconstruction: if we instead adopted the precautionary position that sufficiently sophisticated digital systems deserve the same procedural protection as laboratory animals, what would concretely have to change in research oversight, software engineering, and commercial deployment -- and which parts of the existing practice, if any, could survive unchanged? Finally, say where the practice is genuinely philosophically underdetermined: which rival theories of moral status would each license the status quo, and what evidence about digital systems would force a choice between them?
