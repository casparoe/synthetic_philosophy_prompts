i'm a novelist working on a book where the plot hinges on this idea (which i picked up from Dennett and cant stop thinking about) that there can be free-floating rationales -- genuine reasons for something to be the way it is, without any mind ever having entertained or understood the reason. my main character inherits a weird old house full of design choices nobody can explain, and i want the reader to slowly realize the house has *reasons* even though nobody ever had them. to get my head straight on the concept i've been collecting cases from other domains. here's what i have so far:

1. evolution, obviously -- the peacock's tail has a rationale (mate choice dynamics) that no peacock or peahen has ever represented to itself. the reason floats free of any reasoner.
2. the bitter cassava thing -- amazonian groups have elaborate multi-day processing rituals for cassava that remove cyanide, but nobody performing them knows that's what the steps are for, and the knowledge is distributed across a tradition rather than any head
3. hayek's markets -- prices compress and transmit information about scarcity that no single buyer or seller possesses, so the economy "knows" things nobody knows
4. chesterton's fence -- norms and rules that look pointless but encode solutions to problems the people following them have forgotten existed
5. grammar -- the rules of english syntax have a kind of functional rationale that native speakers follow perfectly and couldn't state to save their lives

i need one more case that isn't just any of these in a trenchcoat. in particular it shouldn't be another "tradition encodes hidden wisdom" case, since 2 and 4 already have that covered, and it shouldn't be natural selection wearing a hat. ideally something where the rationale is floating free in a *different structural way* -- different mechanism, different location for the reason -- because i want to steal the structure for a subplot. also tell me whether you think the case actually works or whether it collapses into one of the five on closer inspection, because i've been fooled before.
