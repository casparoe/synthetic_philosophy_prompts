Construct a thought experiment for the socialist calculation debate, set in a near-future municipal microgrid during a five-day heat dome. Use these working definitions, not as quotations but as the positions to be operationalized: Mises says rational allocation of producer goods needs genuine money prices generated by exchange; Hayek says the decisive problem is dispersed, tacit, time-sensitive knowledge that cannot be fully uploaded to a center; Lange says a planning board can mimic a market by announcing parametric shadow prices, observing shortages and surpluses, and adjusting by trial and error. Specifications: the scenario must be realistic, at most 350 words, with no perfect forecaster, no black-box superintelligence, and no stipulated verdicts; include one allocation problem that is computationally clean, such as scheduling rooftop batteries across feeders, and one that turns on tacit local knowledge, such as which aging transformers can tolerate a phase imbalance without a lineworker smelling hot insulation. Ex ante, have the Lange board and a decentralized spot-price market predict the same clearing prices and the same outages; after a specified shock, they must diverge, but not because the center lacks arithmetic power or because prices are literally absent. Then show, theory by theory, what Mises, Hayek, and Lange would say failed, identify the single structural feature that makes the board peel off despite matching the market ex ante, state what happens if that feature is removed, and give the strongest reply by the side that comes out worst.
