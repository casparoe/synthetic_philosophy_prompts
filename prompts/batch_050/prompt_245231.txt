I run a small ethics-advice channel, and a recurring theme in the letters I receive is whether someone is in a position to criticise at all: the parent who smokes warning a teenager off cigarettes, the reformed heavy drinker confronting an old friend, the colleague reporting misconduct they once quietly overlooked. I would like to give my readers a decision tree that sorts them into a philosophical position on when a person lacks the standing to blame, based on their actual judgements rather than on labels they may never have heard.

Please build the tree from the questions upwards, not from a list of named theories. Six to eight branching questions, each answerable by someone with no philosophy background, so ask about concrete cases and gut reactions rather than using words like 'hypocrisy' or 'complicity' in the question text. No question should simply ask whether the reader thinks hypocrites may blame; the tree should trap that commitment out of them indirectly. Among the leaves I need at least: the view that nobody ever loses standing to blame, since true blame is true whoever speaks it; the view that hypocrites lose standing because blame presumes an equality between blamer and blamed that the hypocrite denies; the view that what matters is whether the blamer is genuinely committed to the norm they are invoking; and the view that standing turns on the wrong being one's business in the first place. Each leaf should carry one sentence on its most awkward consequence.

For any combination of answers that no actual position seems to occupy, note it briefly and say whether it looks incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. If two of the positions above cannot be separated by any question you can include, flag that honestly rather than inventing a sham question to split them. Finally, test the finished tree by running two harder cases through it: a collective (a company or a nation) blaming another for something it also does, and blame directed at someone who has since died.
