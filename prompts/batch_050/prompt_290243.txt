I host a monthly rationality meetup — about a dozen people, mixed math backgrounds, 75 minutes. Design an in-person game that makes participants feel in their wallets the difference between maximizing expected value and maximizing geometric growth: the Kelly insight as an experience, not a lecture. Constraints: repeated multiplicative bets on a favorable but noisy gamble, no stakes beyond tokens, and the rules must be arranged so that the intuitively correct strategy — bet big on positive-EV propositions — visibly ruins the players who follow it while a boring fixed-fraction bettor quietly finishes ahead, all before anyone names the criterion. Include: the exact rules and payout scheme, a record-keeping mechanism that needs no spreadsheets, and a debrief mapping specific moments (the early leader going broke, the timid bettor winning) onto ergodicity, log utility, and absorbing states. Then tell me how the game predictably derails the first time — a lucky all-in player winning the session and poisoning the lesson, participants suspecting rigged coins, ordinary risk aversion masquerading as insight — and how to handle each. Close with an honest note on what twenty rounds of play cannot demonstrate about the long run, and what the facilitator has to supply by hand.
