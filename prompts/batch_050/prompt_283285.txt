ok so a few months ago I started volunteering at a wildlife rehab center and last week we had to decide whether to put a fox through roughly six painful weeks of treatment and cage rest or just euthanize her, and it definately broke my brain a bit about how pain and pleasure are supposed to trade off. Since then I've landed on something like a lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism: suffering and pleasure trade off against each other normally at low intensities, but past some "red line" of agony, nothing on the pleasure side can compensate. Roughly my commitments:

(1) Below the red line, pain and pleasure are commensurable. A mild headache can be worth enduring for a great concert; there's some rough exchange rate between small hurts and small goods.

(2) Past the red line, no amount of pleasure, to anyone, justifies the suffering. Torturing one creature to delight a billion others is wrong, full stop.

(3) Numbers matter below the line. A thousand moderate pains can be worse than one, and enough mild headaches could add up to something worse than a single very bad (but sub-red-line) pain.

(4) Species is morally irrelevant to the weighting: a fox's suffering counts the same as a human's at equal intensity, and the red line sits at the same intensity for both.

(5) There's no duty to create happy beings. The never-conceived aren't deprived of anything, which is why I'd rather fund anesthesia access than, idk, amusement parks -- relieving suffering beats manufacturing joy.

(6) It was right to treat the fox, because her future pleasures genuinely count: six weeks of significant pain is a fair price for years of good fox-life afterward.

(7) Decisions should go by expected value: probability-weight the bads and goods, sum them, with the red line acting as a hard veto on any outcome past it.

Does this package actually hang together, or am I helping myself to something? Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one tell me specifically which commitment has to give and what that revision costs -- don't just flag everything as equally problematic. Also, if the red line is doing contradictory work somewhere in here, I want to know, because it's doing a lot of work in this view.
