I’m a software engineer with basically zero philosophy training, so write me a short story about this problem I keep flip-floping on. My fictional startup has built an animal-welfare dashboard that combines behavior, sleep, injury avoidance, learning, stress hormones, and neural complexity into a single consciousness score. During testing, it ranks a pig above an octopus, an octopus above a shrimp, and then unexpectedly gives a honeybee hive a surprisingly high score because the colony acts so coherent as a group.

The founder wants to launch the dashboard as an objective measure of which animals deserve the most protection. One engineer thinks the score is just measuring traits humans find relatable, while an animal caretaker argues that even a badly uncertain score is better than ignoring possible suffering. Make the story center on a concrete release decision where shipping or delaying the product causes harm either way. Include at least one moment where an animal’s behavior can be interpreted in two very different ways, and don’t let the technology magically reveal who is conscious. Explore whether consciousness has to belong to one bounded individual, whether precaution should increase with uncertainty, and whether reducing all this to one number is useful or morally evasive. Keep it under 800 words.
