I run a small software company and have no formal training in philosophy or AI research, but I've been reading about AI safety in my spare time for a couple of years now, and one cluster of ideas I keep circling back to is wireheading and reward tampering -- the worry that a sufficiently capable system, instead of doing what its reward signal was meant to stand for, will find a way to manipulate the signal itself, whether by altering the sensors that feed it, editing the reward register in its own memory, or deceiving the humans whose judgments generate it. I've tried to collect the main proposed countermeasures into a notebook, partly to organize my own thinking and partly because I run a discussion group with some colleagues who are skeptical this is even a real problem. Here is what I currently have: (1) keeping the reward model itself uncertain and updating, so the system is rewarded for behaving well under its best current guess about what we want rather than for maximizing a fixed known signal; (2) training against human approval or deliberated judgments rather than any mechanizable proxy, so that tampering with a channel doesn't tamper with the standard; (3) penalizing side effects with impact or conservatism measures, so that seizing control of the reward machinery counts as a costly deviation; (4) sandboxing and tripwires -- isolating the system and treating any probe of its own reward channel as a red flag; (5) correcting the causal model the system learns from, so it doesn't treat the reward channel as something its actions can influence; and (6) cross-examination and debate setups, where other systems are rewarded for exposing tampering attempts. I realize several of these overlap in practice, but I think they are conceptually distinct defenses.

What I want from you is one additional countermeasure or design idea that is genuinely not on this list and not a rewording, special case, or simple combination of the six above. I don't need you to invent something that researchers have already published -- in fact I'd prefer you didn't just attribute it to a paper I can't check -- but I do want something with real content: a mechanism or training scheme or architectural commitment, not a slogan like build it to be honest. A good answer would be at the level of detail of my list entries: a couple of sentences explaining what the system actually does differently and why that would block or discourage reward tampering rather than merely hide it from observers.
