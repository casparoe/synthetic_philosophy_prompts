Last weekend I was out on my cousin's boat with two of his buddies — one keeps reef aquariums for a living, the other is a grad student who studies ant colonies — and we drifted past a Portuguese man o' war, and the two of them got into an argument about whether it's "one animal" that has been bugging me for days, partly because I've been asked to write the captions for a little nature photo zine my cousin is putting together and I have no idea what to call this thing. Here's how it went, as best I remember it. (1) The aquarium guy said it's obviously a single animal: it sails around, catches fish, digests them, and functions as one coherent unit, and if we pulled it aboard nobody would say we'd caught dozens of animals. (2) The ant guy said no, it's a colony — it's made of specialized zooids, where some are basically just a stomach, some are just tentacles, one is just the gas float, and calling it one animal is like calling an ant colony or a beehive one animal just because the members cooperate really tightly. (3) The aquarium guy pushed back that worker bees and ants can at least in principle survive and reproduce apart from the colony, while the zooids can't exist on their own at all, and anyway by his logic my body is a "colony" of cells, some of which (like neurons) can't survive independently either, so he has to pick: either humans are colonies, or "the parts can't leave" is the real test of being one organism. (4) The ant guy said fine, but then consider a lichen — a fungus and an alga locked together, neither of which can be a lichen on its own, and they're not even genetically related — or the bacteria in my gut that I literally can't digest certain food without. If mutual dependency is what makes something one organism, then me plus my microbiome is a single organism, which he said is obviously absurd, so dependency can't be the test either.

Please adjudicate this move by move: rate each of the four moves on a scale from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a sentence or two justifying each rating, and then tell me what, if anything, both of them are missing that would actually settle it. Lead with your overall verdict in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning — no preamble and no restating the argument back at me.
