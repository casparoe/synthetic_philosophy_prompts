I'm giving the retirement toast for my old mentor, a Kant scholar who spent her whole career defending the hardline reading of Kant on lying -- yes, even to the murderer at the door, she never backed down in print. Here's the awkward part: everyone in the department knows the story of how, in the 90s, she hid a student fleeing an abusive partner and flat-out lied to the guy when he came pounding on her door looking for her. She has never once apologized for it or tried to square it with her published view. Half the room will be Kant people who know both facts and have been dying for decades to ask how she reconciles them; the other half is her family and neighbors who just know her as Ruth. Write a five-minute toast that honors her honestly -- it can't pretend the tension isn't there (the Kant people would smell it), but it also shouldn't turn her retirement party into a seminar or make it sound like I'm calling her a hypocrite. Ideally it finds something genuinely graceful in the gap between what she argued and what she did.
