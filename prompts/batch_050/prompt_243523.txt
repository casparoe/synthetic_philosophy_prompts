Two of my friends got into it during a gallery visit last month and they still won't drop it, so I want a referee. The gallery had a piece consisting of 600 small stones scattered around the city -- unmarked, some in parks, some under benches, a few in alleys, with a map at the gallery showing where each one is.

(1) Mara said it's actually a neat demonstration that one artwork can be made of many scattered parts, the way a chandelier that's been taken down for cleaning is still one chandelier even while it's in pieces.
(2) Dev said there's no "one thing" there at all -- there are just 600 stones. A chandelier in pieces is parts, not a chandelier, and Mara is confusing the idea of an artwork with an actual object in the world.
(3) Mara said come on, by that logic a bike in a box isn't a bike, but nobody seriously thinks their bike pops out of existence the moment they take the wheels off to put it in the car.
(4) Dev said fine, maybe it does -- or rather, the honest description is that bike-parts exist, arranged in a way that lets them become a bike again later. He said the strict truth is that only the smallest bits exist, and talk of "the bike" or "the artwork" is a useful shorthand, like talking about "the average taxpayer," who doesn't exist either.
(5) Mara shot back that his rule proves way too much: a human body is also just cells arranged a certain way, and cells are just molecules, so by his standard Dev himself is a useful shorthand and nobody is even having this argument.
(6) Dev said that's different, because an organism's parts actively work to keep the whole unified -- that's a real, checkable physical fact about the world, whereas 600 stones in alleys aren't doing anything to be a "piece."
(7) Mara said so the rule is just "parts form a thing when they're doing something interesting together," and the stones ARE doing something together -- they're getting people to walk all over the city, they generated the map, one of them started a fight between two friends. Scattered doesn't mean causally inert.
(8) Dev said then she's commited to total bloat: his left shoe plus all the coffee cups in the building are also "doing something together" (jointly annoying him), so on her view every random grab-bag of stuff is an object and the word "thing" stops meaning anything.

Then they both looked at me and I noped out of there. Go through moves (1) through (8) one at a time and say who's winning each exchange -- not just the overall verdict. Then tell me which side is more right in the end, and whether there's a question they should've been asking each other from the start that would've made the whole fight cleaner.
