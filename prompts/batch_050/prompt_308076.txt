I run a small independent cinema, and each month I have to commit to a program of eight films for the following quarter. I can personally watch perhaps fifteen of the hundred-plus candidates; for the rest I rely on other people's judgments -- trusted critics, festival reports, my two part-time programmers, audience scores from earlier screenings elsewhere. I keep running into a philosophical problem that I've learned has a name: the question of whether aesthetic testimony can justify belief, or whether the so-called acquaintance principle is right that genuine aesthetic judgment has to rest on first-hand experience of the work itself.

I want you to take a definite position on that debate and build it into a decision procedure I can actually run each month. Specifically:

1. State, in a paragraph or two, the position you're taking on whether and when I can rationally form a justified aesthetic judgment of a film I haven't seen, and why -- engage the strongest considerations on the other side rather than just noting them, and be clear about what your position implies for the legitimacy of my entire enterprise (a program necessarily built largely on second-hand judgment).

2. Translate that position into a procedure: the questions to ask about each candidate film in order; how testimony should be weighted differently depending on what kind of claim it supports (e.g., "this film is competent" vs. "this film is great" vs. "this film will repay this particular audience"); how to handle agreement and disagreement among my sources; and where the procedure must stop and require that I actually watch the thing before booking it. Be explicit about which steps are genuine rules and which are judgment calls the procedure cannot eliminate, and say what the procedure cannot do.

3. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical cases: (a) a documentary with uniformly strong reviews from critics I trust; (b) an experimental film one programmer calls a masterpiece and the other walked out of; (c) a crowd-pleaser with mediocre reviews but enormous audience enthusiasm at every festival it's played. Make sure at least one application yields an outcome I won't like -- a case where following the procedure means booking or rejecting against my own inclination -- and explain why the procedure bites there.

Please be committed throughout: don't present the menu of positions and leave me to choose. Where the philosophy is genuinely unsettled, pick a side, give your reasons, and flag where a reasonable person running my cinema could defensibly configure the procedure differently.
