Next week I'm presenting at my lab's weekly meeting and I need to survive the Q&A, so help me prep. The talk: I claim that getting letter-string analogy problems right (abc goes to abd, so what does iijjkk go to?) tells you almost nothing about whether a system has fluid concepts, and that the actual signature to look for is *spontaneous slippage* -- whether the system's own working concepts (successor, leftmost, sameness-group) get reshaped by pressure from the problem itself, the way "take the successor" has to slip into "take the alphabetically first" once you hit xyz with a cyclic alphabet. I've built a new problem family designed so that no single rigid rule cracks every instance -- you have to let one description give way to another mid-solve -- and I claim current LLMs only *parody* slippage: they'll do it fluently and charmingly if you tell them to, but left alone they either lock onto one rule early or produce an answer first and reverse-engineer a slippage story after the fact. I'm proposing a metric I call the "slippage budget": how much genuine conceptual restructuring a system does per problem, measured by having it narrate its reasoning and counting where the narrative's own categories shift.

The audience: two cognitive scientists raised on Copycat -- they know the slipnet, codelets, the temperature parameter, all of it -- and they think most "AI solves analogy" work tests whether you can find an answer, not whether you got there the way a mind does; one of them loves pointing out that microdomains get "solved" in ways that leave the interesting phenomenon untouched. Then three NLP engineers who think letter-strings are a toy domain and will only care whether my metric predicts anything on real tasks. What will each camp hit me with, roughly in the order it'll come, phrased the way they'd phrase it? What should I concede right away (I suspect the narration-based metric is my soft underbelly -- why trust the story the model tells about itself?), what should I hold firm on, and whats the objection I'm secretly hoping nobody raises?
