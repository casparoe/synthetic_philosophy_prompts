I'm drafting a seminar paper on whether time is "real" in any objective sense, and I keep going back and forth on how much weight to give Gödel's 1949 rotating-universe argument -- the one where solutions to Einstein's field equations contain closed timelike curves, so no global temporal ordering exists in those worlds, and Gödel infers that if time can fail to exist in a physically possible universe, it can't be part of the objective furniture in ours either (the modal step is the controversial bit, plus the reply that Gödel universes aren't physically realistic since our universe isn't rotating like that).

Before I commit to a position I want the spec, not the verdict. Write the desiderata any adequate philosophical account of time must meet specifically in light of this argument -- which intuitions about becoming and temporal direction it can sacrifice and which it can't, what it has to say about the modal inference (does possibility of timeless worlds really imply ideality here?), whether it's allowed to lean on the physical unrealisticness of Gödel's solution or whether that's dodging the point, and what it owes to relativity generally.

Format it like this:

- The requirements list -- 6 to 9 desiderata, each with the consideration behind it and a tag of non-negotiable vs. negotiable, ~400-500 words total
- The tension report -- which pairs or subsets of your desiderata can't be jointly satisfied, and where Gödel's argument is doing the work vs. where ordinary relativity already causes the trouble, ~200 words
- Quick check -- one short paragraph each on how the B-theory/block view, presentism, and a "time is local not global" view fare against the list. Keep this part short, the list is the delivarable.
