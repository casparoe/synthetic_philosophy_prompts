Is there a name for this? I'm a retired physicist (forty years in spectroscopy, mostly) and since hanging up my boots I've been reading philosophy, which is humbling. There's a phenomenon I keep noticing that feels like it should have a name, but I can't find one, and I'd like to know whether philosophers have got there first.

Three instances. First, the everyday case: when I look at the apple tree outside my window, what I'm presented with is the tree — leaves, light, the works. Nowhere in the experience is there anything like "a viewing of a tree, brought to you by a nervous system." If I try to attend to the seeing itself, I just end up looking harder at the tree. Second, my telescope. A beginner at the eyepiece notices the circle of the field, the vignetting, the chromatic aberation on bright stars. After a few sessions all of that drops out of attention — you stop noticing the optics entirely, you're just looking at Saturn. The fringing is presumably still there optically, but it's no longer part of what you're presented with. Third, the exceptions, which interest me as much as the rule. In dreams everything seems solidly, unquestionably real — until the occasional lucid dream, where the whole thing suddenly presents itself as a dream, and it's as if a layer becomes visible that wasn't there a moment before. And once, during a bad migraine with aura, the visual field felt for a few minutes less like the world and more like, I don't know, a screen. Then it passed and the world went back to just being there.

So the general thing I want named: whatever does the representing seems constitutionally unable to appear within what it represents, and that's why the world strikes us as simply given, unmediated, right there — except in rare moments when the mechanism shows through for a second.

I've found "naive realism," but that's a doctrine about what's true, not a description of what experience is like. And I've seen "transparency" used for language — a word being something you read through to the meaning — which is nearby but not the thing itself. If there's an established concept, give me the nearest candidates, say how what I've described differs from each, and which fits best. If nothing fits cleanly, coin a term and define it, and work some boundary cases: does an afterimage count as a glimpse of the layer, or is it just one more object in the world? And tell me whether this is best understood as an all-or-nothing feature with occasional breakdowns, or as a matter of degree, because my examples seem to pull in both directions and I can't settle it.

One request about how you answer: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables — continuous prose only, the way you'd write a letter. I read these over coffee and formatting gets in the way.
