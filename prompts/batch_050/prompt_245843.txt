At our local rationality meetup last night two friends of mine got into a fight about morality and I still cant decide who was being less wrong. Friend A's whole thing was: morality is nothing but adaptations for kin selection and reciprocal altruism, full stop. When you feel like lying is Really Wrong, that's just your genes talking, and anyone who thinks there's something left to explain is confused. Friend B said this proves way too much — by that logic arithmetic is "just neurons" and evolution is "just chemistry" — so moral truth has to come from somewhere outside the whole causal story that produced our brains, like reason just latching onto it directly, otherwise you're only ever describing your own programming and not actually being moral. A called that magic, B called A a nihilist, downhill from there.

The weird thing is I feel like there both making the same mistake in opposite directions, but I can't put my finger on what it is. I also worry I'm just pattern-matching to "the truth lies in the middle," which is usually a cope. So: what's actually wrong with each of their pictures, and is there a non-fake middle position here, or do I have to pick a side? Please don't give me a general survey of metaethics — I want a diagnosis of these two specific failure modes and what assumption they share.
