I am applying for a one-year humanities research fellowship whose outcome would be a single journal article, and I need the two-page proposal written up. The project concerns Hume's standard of taste in his essay Of the Standard of Taste, and I want you to develop the following half-formed thought into the proposal.

Hume's true judges are constituted partly by practice and comparison: they have surveyed a great many works and can situate any new candidate against acknowledged models of excellence. But this machinery appears to presuppose a settled canon, since comparison requires comparanda. So consider works in genuinely novel genres or media, where no acknowledged models yet exist and the seasoned judges' practice has all been elsewhere. Two readings seem available, and both look bad. On the first, the standard simply goes silent at the frontier of art, in which case it cannot explain how canons ever get started or revised — it ratifies taste but cannot generate it. On the second, the standard extrapolates from established excellences, in which case it seems structurally guaranteed to underrate whatever breaks with tradition; the well-known pattern of hostile first receptions of works later admitted to the canon looks like exactly this failure mode. Meanwhile Hume's own fallback test — durable admiration across ages and nations — is diachronic and therefore useless in the present: by the time it can be consulted, the verdict is no longer needed. My suspicion is that this temporal blind spot reveals something about what a standard of taste could even be for, and that the right response is not to patch Hume but to reconceive the standard as conservative by design.

Write the proposal document. It should include: (1) a precise statement of the question, framed so that it has a determinate answer — something like: on the best non-circular interpretation of Hume's criteria, does the standard yield any verdicts at all for genuinely novel art forms, and if not, is that a repairable limitation or a consequence of what kind of thing a standard of taste is; (2) why the standard objections in the literature — the circularity worry about identifying true judges without already knowing good taste, the elitism worry, the ideal-observer readings — leave my question untouched rather than answering it; (3) the method, stated concretely: close conceptual analysis of the criteria of delicacy, practice, comparison, and freedom from prejudice, asking of each whether it can operate without a prior canon, plus at most two schematic case studies (e.g., the reception history of a now-canonical nineteenth-century movement and of a contemporary form such as video games) used only to test the analysis, not as historical scholarship; (4) the possible conclusions — that the blindness is a defect to be repaired from within Hume's own sentimentalist resources, that it is a feature because standards are inherently backward-looking and verdicts on novelty simply must wait, or that the standard should be read as a regulative ideal rather than a decision procedure — and which considerations would decide among them; (5) what would count as the project failing; and (6) an honest note on scope, given one year and one article. Do not include a bibliography or any citations; I will add those myself.
