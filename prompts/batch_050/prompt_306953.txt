So I've been a LW/ACX reader for years and my group chat has been stuck in a two-week argument about moral uncertainty ever since I finally read the MacAskill/Bykvist/Ord book and started sending them passages at 1am. The parliamentary model people and the maximize-expected-choiceworthiness people are at each other's throats, and I want to write something for my little newsletter that defuses it by making everyone laugh instead. So here's what I want: a satirical agony-aunt column. The columnist, call her Dr. Ought, is a completely sincere believer in the parliamentary model of moral uncertainty, and she answers ordinary reader dilemmas -- whether to ghost a bad date, whether to tell your brother his novel is bad, a dispute with a neighbor over a fence line, whatever you like -- by convening the moral parliament in her head and reporting back how the vote went. The utilitarian bloc wants one thing, the deontologists keep raising points of order, the virtue ethicists abstain on grounds that the question is malformed, and the contractualists hold out for a deal nobody can quite state.

The important part: the humor has to come from the real problems in the moral uncertainty literature, visibly happening, without ever being named. So the intertheoretic comparison problem should just occur on the page -- Dr. Ought confidently weighing the utilitarians' numbers against the Kantians' outrage with some normalization scheme she treats as obvious and never explains. The my-favorite-theory problem should show up as the reader letters getting suspiciously convenient answers. Maybe one party's stakes are so astronomically large that the whole parliament gets steamrolled by a single fanatical delegate. Dr. Ought should never notice any of this; she thinks this is simply how a reasonable person lives. Keep it deadpan, no winking, and it should be funny to someone who's never heard of any of this while a reader who knows the lit recognizes exactly which objection each gag is. 600 words max, formatted as two or three reader letters with Dr. Ought's replies.
