So a bit of background: I spent forty years as a research ecologist (freshwater fish populations, mostly) and since retiring I've been plowing through political philosophy on my own, which is humbling at my age. Last month I got through Nozick, and then last week my grandkids made me watch The Lorax with them, and the two things collided in my head. Am I crazy, or is the Once-ler basically Wilt Chamberlain in work gloves? Every Thneed sale is a voluntary exchange -- nobody is forced to buy one, people really do want them once the advertising gets going, and the money piles up exactly the way Nozick says it will when people are free to do what they want with their holdings. Liberty upsets patterns, fine. And yet the story obviously wants us to think something has gone badly, badly wrong. So I'd like a careful reading of The Lorax (the book, or the 2012 film -- they're close enough for my purposes, though the film adds that bottled-air businessman O'Hare, which might matter) through Nozick's entitlement theory. Things I keep circling on: Does Seuss actually land a blow on the Wilt Chamberlain argument, or does the story sort of cheat, because the real wrong was never in the transfers at all but in the Once-ler's original appropriation of the truffula forest -- i.e. the Lockean proviso fails at step one and everything downstream is moot? Does it matter that the buyers can't see the harm their purchases add up to (externalities, we used to call them, and Nozickians seem to treat those as someone else's department)? And the ending, with the last seed handed off to a kid: is that Nozick's principle of rectification in picture-book form, or is rectification supposed to work differently? Finally, where does all this leave the Lorax himself -- he claims to speak for the trees, but if every individual transaction was just, does anyone actually have standing to complain in Nozick's framework? I'd like your answer as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, with no introduction and no conclusion -- just the five points.
