Got into an arguement with a coworker last week that ended with him basically refusing to continue. We'd been talking about airport security: I said that even if you add up a 40-minute delay across millions of travelers a year, it feels wrong to just sum all that into one big number and weigh it against a small number of deaths or serious harms from laxer screening -- a million people getting mildly annoyed isn't the same kind of thing as one person getting seriously hurt, and adding identical tiny harms together doesn't change their kind. He said 'shut up and multiply' and treated that as game over.

I've seen that slogan around rationalist-adjacent internet for years and I've never been sure what exactly it's claiming. Can you lay out the different precise theses it could stand for? I'm imagining a spectrum from something pretty tame (like: actually do the arithmetic before trusting your gut about quantities, since people systematically underweight tiny harms spread over huge populations) through something stronger (like: for any serious harm, there exists some number of trivial harms that morally outweighs it -- no lexical thresholds, ever) up to something maximal (like: aggregation under expected value is all of ethics, and anyone who resists a conclusion like 'one person tortured beats 3^^^3 dust specks' is just being squeamish or innumerate). For each reading: is it a platitude, a live substantive position, or actually false? And which of them does my coworker need to be true for 'shut up and multiply' to actually end our specific argument, as opposed to just reminding me to check my math? Bonus points if you address whether the dust-speck-style thought experiments, where the tiny harms are stipulated to be identical and independent, tell us much about real cases like mine where the harms are heterogeneous and interact with each other.
