I am a novelist at work on a literary novel whose plot turns on a variant of Nozick's experience machine, and I find that I cannot write the central chapter -- the one in which my protagonist decides whether to plug in -- until I have settled the underlying philosophical question to my own satisfaction. My variant differs from the standard one in two respects, and I would like the essay to engage the variant rather than the textbook original. First, the machine does not offer a life of uniform maximal pleasure; it offers a life the subject would, on reflection, find narratively satisfying -- with struggle, loss, and accomplishment included, because the machine's designers discovered that purely blissful simulations produce boredom and a creeping sense of weightlessness. Second, the subject knows she will be plugged in only for the remainder of her natural life, but her real body is tended by the machine, her real relationships lapse, and the people she loves inside the simulation are, unbeknownst to her, simulations of actual people who are themselves living on without her.

Please write an essay on whether my protagonist -- a talented but chronically dissatisfied painter -- should plug in. The essay should examine what each of the major theories of well-being (hedonism, desire-satisfaction theory, and objective-list theories) says about her choice, paying particular attention to the wrinkle my variant introduces: if even simulated flourishing requires real-seeming struggle and genuine narrative structure, does Nozick's intuition that something is lost by plugging in survive, or does it rest on an unexamined assumption that meaning requires mind-independent reality? I would also like the essay to consider whether the protagonist's obligations to the real people she would leave behind are a separate question from her well-being, or whether a sufficiently rich theory of well-being already folds them in.

Do not end on a shrug. Reach a conclusion about what she should do and justify it, and indicate which theory of well-being your verdict commits you to -- or, if your verdict can be reached from several theories, explain why the convergence itself is philosophically interesting. I will adapt whatever argument persuades you into the novel's climactic scene, so clarity about the decisive consideration matters more to me than comprehensiveness.
