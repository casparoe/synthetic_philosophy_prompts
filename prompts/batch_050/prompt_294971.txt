Is there a name for this? My grandmother can vividly remember being my mother's age, raising kids, going to work. My mother can vividly remember being my age, sitting in the same kind of classroom I sit in now. But my grandmother insists she cannot remember being fifteen at all -- she knows facts about her teenage years the way she knows facts about history, without any feeling of having been there. So the chain goes: my grandmother connects directly to middle-aged-her, middle-aged-her connects directly to teenage-her, but my grandmother does not connect directly to teenage-her. Everyone still says the grandmother and the teenager are the same person, and I agree they are, but the link between them only exists through the middle.

A second instance, closer to home: a friend of mine got in trouble last year for something mean he supposedly did in sixth grade, which he honestly does not remember. His defense was basically that the kid who did it is a stranger to him now, and nobody accepted that defense.

We touched on Locke in philosophy club, and his idea seems to be that you are whoever you remember being. But taken literally that produces something broken in my grandmother's case: the grandmother is the same person as the middle-aged woman, the middle-aged woman is the same person as the teenager, yet by the memory test the grandmother is not the teenager -- so the same person both is and is not herself. I assume someone has analyzed this exact chain structure, where identity runs through overlapping links instead of one continuous thread. Does it have an established name? How does it differ from the simple memory view it is trying to rescue, and does chaining actually fix the contradiction or just spread it out? And where are the edges -- if a middle link is destroyed, say by total amnesia in midlife, does the person at the end snap in two? I'd rather have the carving than just the label.
