So I have to write a paper for my philosophy elective and I want to do something more specific than the generic "what is art" essay everyone else is doing. My idea: use the urinal as a test case. The story as I understand it — in 1917 Marcel Duchamp took a porcelain urinal, signed it with the fake name "R. Mutt," and submitted it to an exhibition run by the Society of Independent Artists in New York, which had bragged it would show anything from anyone who payed the entry fee. They refused to display it anyway, and it only later became like the most famous artwork of the 20th century.

Here's what I actually want: analyze this episode through the institutional theory of art (roughly: art is whatever the "artworld" confers the status of art on — Dickie and Danto are the names that keep coming up in the stuff I've read). Concretely:

1. What does the institutional theory say happened here, step by step? Who in this story counts as "the artworld"? And at what exact moment did the urinal become art according to the theory — when Duchamp signed it, when he submitted it, when it got written about, or only decades later when museums wanted it?

2. Where does the theory struggle? The whole point of the episode is that the institution that was supposed to rubber-stamp everything rejected it. If the artworld's verdict is what makes something art, how does a rejected piece become art? Does the theory have to bite the bullet and say it was NOT art in 1917 and only became art later? Is that a devastating problem for the theory or actually a feature (like, it shows the status can be conferred retroactively)?

3. Now run the analysis in the other direction too: what does the episode show about the theory? Is it a confirmation (art status really does depend on institutions, not on skill or beauty, and look — the urinal proves it), or an anomaly (the institution failed to do its job and art happened anyway, so the theory misdescribes what happened), or is the whole thing just a case the theory was never built to handle? If the theory is silent on something here, say so instead of forcing it to give an answer.

4. Brief comparison with one rival: formalism, the view that what makes something art is its form or aesthetic properties rather than its institutional status. What does formalism say about the urinal, and does the episode hurt formalism worse than it hurts the institutional theory?

Don't present a contested position as the consensus — tell me which of the moves in your answer most philosophers would accept and which ones are your own take or a minority view.
