I work in operations and over the years (school district admin first, now a hospital network) I've run into the same weird failure twice, and I can't figure out if it has a name.

The pattern: there's some outcome leadership cares about but can't observe directly, so they pick a measurable number to stand in for it. Fine so far. Then they start holding people to the number, and people adapt -- they optimize the number. Standard story so far, I know. But here's the part that gets me: the number was also the organization's only real window into the outcome. So once the number gets optimized, leadership doesn't just have a bad number, they've lost the ability to even notice that something's wrong, becuase the thing that would have told them is the same number that's being gamed. The dashboard stays green while the building burns, and there is no second alarm.

Two cases I've lived through. First, the school district: test scores were used to judge teaching quality, which is normal enough, but they were also how the district noticed a school going downhill. After a few years of very intense test prep the scores looked fine, better than fine, and when parents started complaining about what their kids actually couldn't do, the district had no instrument left to confirm or deny it. The complaints were "anecdotes," the scores were "data." Second, the hospital: ER wait times became the tracked number, staff got creative about what counted as the start of the wait (quick triage hello, then back to the waiting room), and again -- the wait time dashboard was how the network detected overcrowding. Once the number was being managed, overcrowding became invisible to the people upstairs. Nobody was exactly lying. But the sensor and the report card were the same object, so breaking the report card broke the sensor.

Whenever I describe this, people say "that's just Goodhart's law" or "specification gaming," but I don't think either fits. Goodhart, as I understand it, is about the measure decoupling from the goal under pressure. Specification gaming is about the cleverness of the optimizer finding loopholes. My thing is a second-order effect: the decoupling makes itself undetectable, because your detection channel and your optimization target are one and the same. You could imagine Goodhart-style decoupling where everyone can still plainly see it happening -- that's NOT my cases. I also recently came across "surrogation," where people start treating the metric as the goal itself, and that's closer but still not it: in my cases even the people who know better can't see the gap, because their eyes were the metric.

So: does this specific configuration have a name of its own? If so, what's the concept and how is it distinguished from plain Goodhart, from specification gaming, and from surrogation? And if there isn't an established name, coin one, define it precisely, and walk me through the boundary cases. For instance: does it count if there's still some weak second channel for noticing (annual audits, whistleblowers), and how weak is too weak? Does it require deliberate gaming, or can it happen when everyone is honestly adapting to the incentive? What makes an organization structurally vulnerable to it?

Don't just hand me a label and stop -- I want to know where the edges of this thing are.
