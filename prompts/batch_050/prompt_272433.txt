I have no academic background, but I've been reading on my own about how we should reason from the sheer fact that we exist, and I've run into a dispute I can't shake. As I understand it, one school of thought says you should reason as though you were a random draw from all the observers who actually exist, wherever and whenever they are. The other says that a theory predicting more observers gives you more chances to exist at all, so your existence itself counts as evidence in favor of more populous universes — which apparently leads to a worry sometimes called the presumptuous philosopher problem: someone in an armchair could conclude, before any telescope is built, that a universe containing a trillion trillion observers is vastly more likely than a small one, and could then shrug off actual astronomical findings as barely moving the needle either way.

Write me a satirical piece of about 600–700 words in the form of a press release from a fictional Institute of A Priori Cosmology announcing that, having fully committed to the second way of reasoning, it is shuttering its observatory, auctioning its telescopes, and retraining its astronomers as "existence consultants," since observation can now only ever refine what armchair reasoning has already settled. The humor should come from taking the assumption seriously and following it further than its defenders would like — have the institute apply the same logic to hiring (candidates from larger applicant pools deserve the job more, since their existence was less surprising), to grant funding, and to why it confidently expects an imminent announcement of alien life. Keep it deadpan throughout, with no winking at the reader, and make sure the standard objection to this style of reasoning comes through purely from the content rather than being stated outright. Please write the piece in British English.
