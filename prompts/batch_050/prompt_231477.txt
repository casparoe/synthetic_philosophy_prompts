I'm designing the unit on cosmology for my grad seminar in philosophy of science this spring, and I want to front-load the Boltzmann brain week with a spec before we read any actual proposals. Here's what kicked this off: in office hours last week one of my sharper students insisted the whole problem is just brain-in-a-vat skepticism wearing a physics costume, and another fired back that no, it's a strictly empirical problem about which cosmological measures predict the wrong ratio of fluctuated observers to ordinary ones. I suspect they're both half right, and I want the seminar to have the criteria in hand before they start judging Carroll-style solutions, or the "cognitive instability" dismissal, or the various typicality principles people wheel in.

So: write the desiderata that any adequate resolution of the Boltzmann brain problem has to meet, with the consideration motivating each one. I want it to be genuinely discriminating, not a laundry list. A few things it definitely needs to engage with: the distinction between rejecting a BB-dominated cosmology because it's false and rejecting it because it's self-undermining (if I take myself to probably be a bare fluctuated brain, my memories and evidence are noise, which undercuts the very reasoning that led me to the cosmology) — are those two different objections, and does a good solution need both? The demand that the fix not prove too much: plenty of respectable cosmologies predict a heat-death future where BBs eventually outnumber us, and a criterion that kills all of those has arguably just refuted cosmology. Whether the solution is allowed to lean on a typicality or xerographic-distribution assumption, and if so what work that assumption must do versus smuggle in. And the asymmetry point that it's thermodynamically vastly cheaper to fluctuate one brain than a whole observed universe, which is presumably load-bearing somewhere.

Also flag the stuff I'd otherwise miss: which desiderata are non-negotiable versus which are just preferences of the physics camp or the epistemology camp, and whether any subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable — if it turns out you can't simultaneously have a principled (non-ad-hoc) measure, keep standard eternal-inflation cosmology alive, and avoid collapsing back into global skepticism, that's exactly the kind of result I want on the board before anyone defends a solution. If you think my student's "it's just skepticism" line is right after all, the desiderata should make that visible rather than assume it away. Skip the scorecard of existing proposals; I'll grade those in class.
