Tennis coaches tell players to stop thinking about their stroke mid-rally, insomnia advice tells you to stop trying to fall asleep, acting teachers say to stop "acting," and the whole flow-state industry treats deliberate effort as the enemy of peak performance. What view of action does this cluster of practices presuppose? It seems to assume that some things can only be done non-deliberately, which is basically the Daoist wu-wei idea -- but the same practices then try to *teach* you this, with drills and steps and techniques, which looks a lot like trying to try not to try. Reconstruct the implicit theory of how deliberate effort relates to spontaneous skilled action, and say whether these practices are coherent or just pragmatically useful despite resting on a confusion. Also: do they rule out the rival view that expert action is really just very fast, well-trained deliberate control? And tell me what would change your verdict here -- which argument or bit of evidence about how skill works would most plausibly break your reconstruction.
