Here are the five phenomena I care about:

1. Graded membership: a robin feels like a better bird than a penguin, and the system should reproduce that gradient rather than a hard in/out boundary.
2. Conceptual combination: whatever rules govern "pet" and "fish" separately, they don't obviously generate "pet fish", whose stereotypical member (a goldfish or guppy) closely resembles neither parent's stereotype.
3. Ad hoc categories: people instantly form coherent categories that were never stored in advance, such as "things to take out of a burning house".
4. Knowledge-driven reclassification: experts sometimes classify against surface similarity because they know the underlying mechanism, as when a biologist insists a whale is a mammal despite its appearance.
5. Borderline indeterminacy: cases like "is a company's group chat a community?" where competent users simply disagree, with no fact of the matter obviously available.
