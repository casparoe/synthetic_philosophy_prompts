So I just spent like three hours arguing with my friend Dana (she's in law school, I'm not, which she mentioned roughly nine times) about when statistical evidence is enough to pin something on someone. She kept saying a probability is a probability and if 80% is good enough in one place it's good enough everywhere. I kept insisting that's obviously wrong but I couldn't articulate why, and now it's 1am and I'm annoyed, so I want to actually figure out what I think. Here are my gut verdicts on the cases we batted around.

A woman is sideswiped by a blue bus that drives off; the Blue Bus Company operates 80% of the blue buses in town and that's literally all the evidence: she should NOT win against the company. A stadium seats 1000 people, only 400 tickets were sold, and the organizers sue one random attendee for the ticket price because he probably (60%) hopped the fence: also NOT a win, that feels outrageous. A DNA profile from a crime scene matches the defendant and the random-match probability is one in ten million: convict, totally fine even if there's not much else. An eyewitness picks the defendant out of a lineup and witnesses in those conditions are right about 80% of the time: honestly I'd convict on that alone, which I realize sits weirdly with the bus case since the numbers are the same. A prison riot where 100 prisoners were in the yard and a guard was definitely killed by one of them but we can't say which: we can't just jail everyone at 1% each or pick one at 1%, agreed. And the one Dana and I never resolved: same blue bus facts, except now there's also a shaky eyewitness who glanced up and thinks it "looked like a company bus" -- she says this changes nothing since the total probability barely moves, I say it changes everything but I can't say why.

So: is there a principle that gets all or most of these? I'd want it to explain why the identical 80% in the bus case and the lineup case feel so different (or tell me one of those verdicts has to go, and which), why DNA gets a pass when the gatecrasher logic doesn't, and it should give me a verdict on the bus-plus-shaky-eyewitness case and on one we didn't get to: convicting someone purely on a facial recognition match that's right 99.5% of the time. If the answer is that some of my intuitions are just incoherent and I have to abandon one, tell me which one is cheapest to drop.
