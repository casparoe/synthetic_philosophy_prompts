I'm a novelist, and I'm stuck on something that's become more philosophical than I expected. The book I'm writing has a character, Walter, who's in the early stages of dementia, and he keeps everything in a little leather notebook he's carried for thirty years. Anyway, in trying to write him convincingly I've ended up with a bunch of gut verdicts about when something outside your head counts as part of your mind, and they don't seem to sit together. I read a bit of philosophy ages ago — there was an example I half-remember about a man with memory loss and a notebook, Otto somebody — so I know clever people have thought about this, but I want to know what *my* intuitions actually commit me to.

Here are my verdicts, such as they are:

Walter's notebook, which he consults automatically, trusts completely, and would be lost without: the stuff in it is part of his mind. When he "remembers" his daughter's birthday by looking it up, I want to say he genuinely remembered, just differently.

My own card index of plot threads, which I shuffle through every morning before I write: also part of my thinking, honestly. When I plan a chapter I'm really planning with the cards.

A calculator I use for working out royalty splits: no, that's just a tool. Nobody's mind includes a calculator.

My phone, with Google on it, which I check more often than Walter checks his notebook: no. Definitely not part of my mind, even though I use it constantly and trust it. This one's starting to bother me, because I can't say what separates it from the notebook.

A couple I know who've been married fifty years and remember things jointly — she supplies the names, he supplies the dates, neither could recall the whole thing alone: I'd say they have a genuinely shared memory. It's lovely, and I think it's real.

A chess player I know who's "in the muscle" memorised thousands of positions: that's plainly in his head, but weirdly it doesn't feel like knowledge in the way the notebook's contents feel like Walter's knowledge. I can't justify that.

So: is there a principle here? Something like "it's part of the mind if you reach for it automatically and trust it without checking" gets me the notebook and maybe the marriage, but it also gets me my phone, which I've said isn't, and possibly not the card index, since I have to actually sit down and work with it. I suspect one of my verdicts has to give, and I have a nasty feeling it's the phone one, which I'm emotionally attached to because I don't want my mind to be partly owned by Google.

Also — what would whatever principle you land on say about a case I haven't listed: a deaf character I'm drafting whose signing feels, from the inside, like it *is* her thinking rather than a translation of it? Does the principle even touch that, or is that a different question entirely?
