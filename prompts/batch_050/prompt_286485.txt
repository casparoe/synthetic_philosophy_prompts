Write a dialogue between two characters who've just gotten back the results of a cross-cultural study they ran together: a philosopher who's a moral objectivist and an anthropologist who thinks morality is a set of local conventions all the way down. Their study asked people in a dozen very different societies to judge a set of scenarios (harm, fairness, hierarchy violations, purity violations) and -- here's the thing they both have to reckon with -- the results are awkward for both of them. Harm and fairness norms showed up everywhere but with wildly different scopes (who counts as someone you can harm, what counts as fair depends heavily on kinship structure and whether the society is WEIRD), while purity and hierarchy norms vary enormously in content but show up in nearly every society in some form.

The objectivist tries to argue that the universal *structure* (every society has norms in these categories) plus the near-universal harm norm is convergence enough to support objectivity, the same way you'd explain away disagreement about the shape of the earth as error. The anthropologist pushes back that what actually does the moral work in each society is the stuff that varies, and that the objectivist is just declaring the invariant thin core "real morality" and everything else noise. Neither of them should strawman the other: the objectivist has to grapple seriously with the point that the scope differences (e.g. whether outsiders, animals, or ancestors count as moral patients) aren't peripheral details, and the anthropologist has to grapple with the question of why there's convergence at all if it's all just convention, and whether her own discomfort with, say, cruelty to children is itself just WEIRD parochialism.

Make it feel like a real conversation -- interruptions, one of them conceding a point grudgingly, someone noticing mid-argument that they're both assuming something they should examine. It should end with them identifying the exact point where they still disagree, not with one of them winning or a mushy both-sides conclusion. Aim for something like 1200-1500 words.
