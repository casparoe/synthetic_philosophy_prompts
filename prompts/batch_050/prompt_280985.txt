So I'm helping run a reading group on AI alignment-adjacent philosophy, and next month we're doing the cluster of ideas around "don't tell the AI what to value, have it figure out what we'd value if we were better in some way." People keep using terms like idealized deliberation, coherent extrapolated volition, ideal advisor theories, and the whole debate/amplification family interchangeably, and it's driving me nuts, because they're clearly different proposals with different failure modes. I want to hand out a decision tree at the start of the session that sorts people: you begin at something like "what matters is what a suitably improved version of us would want or endorse," and by answering a series of questions you end up at a named position.

A few requirements. Each branch node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing the jargon -- e.g. something like "does the idealization have to bottom out in procedures actual humans could in principle run, or is a god's-eye idealization fine even if no one could ever perform it?" rather than "are you a proceduralist?". The positions I want as leaves include at least: CEV-style extrapolation of a whole population, an individual-level ideal advisor view, a "deliberation must stay close to actual human cognition" view (the debate/iterated amplification end of things), and a deflationary position that says the whole indirect-normativity move just relocates the problem rather than solving it. Add others if the space calls for it. Where two leaf positions can't actually be distinguished by any diagnostic question you can phrase neutrally, say so explicitly instead of fudging it.

Also, build the tree from the logical space first -- identify the axes of disagreement (whose volition gets idealized, how idealized, whether the idealization is defined by a procedure or by its output, what happens if idealized versions disagree with each other, etc.), note which combinations of answers are unoccupied by any actual position, and briefly say why for each (incoherent? just unattractive? nobody's bothered?). Aim for maybe 6-10 questions on a full path, and flag the one branch point you think is doing the most philosophical work.
