So I just spent three hours arguing with my friend about tranquilism — the view that wellbeing is basically just the absence of craving, disturbance, frustration, and suffering, not the presence of pleasure or desire-satisfaction — and I’m still annoyed. I’m interviewing an unnamed philosopher next week who defends a careful version of this, and their favorite move is to say every alleged good is just relief from some lack, then accuse critics of confusing stimulation with happiness.

Act as a philosophy-of-wellbeing producer and think carefully; brainstorm privately, then give only the final plan. No citations or name-dropping. Make the questions in-the-weeds but fair: press on whether contentment after a satisfied desire is positive or merely zero, whether never existing beats a calm life, whether intense bliss has no value if it later produces craving, how tranquilism avoids praising numbness, coma, or death, and whether it collapses into negative utilitarianism or a sophisticated status quo bias.

Format, in this order: “Frame” 3 sentences max; “Warm-up” 2 short questions; “Main sequence” 6 numbered questions, each with the likely tranquilist reply and one follow-up that boxes it in; “Escalator” 3 questions that get progressively harder to deflect; “Closer” 1 question asking what observation would make them abandon the view.
