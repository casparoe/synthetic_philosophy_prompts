Defend the claim that binary referendums are a structurally defective instrument for deciding questions on which the change side bundles several mutually incompatible policies — as when a single Leave option in a Brexit-style vote covers arrangements ranging from continued regulatory alignment to a clean break. The thesis to argue for is the strong one: the defect is intrinsic to the binary format, not a contingent failure of campaigning, turnout, or voter information that better procedure could cure. Your argument should exhibit an explicit preference profile over the status quo and three change options in which the change side wins the binary vote while the status quo beats every change option in pairwise majority comparison; it should explain why the resulting mandate therefore need not correspond to any policy that a majority actually prefers to the status quo; it should rebut the objection that voters dissolve the ambiguity by voting for whichever implementation they expect; and it should show why adding a second-stage vote on the concrete terms of change does not repair the defect. Present the argument as exactly five numbered steps, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
