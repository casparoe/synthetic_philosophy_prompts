I help run a student philosophy club at my university and next month it's my turn to lead a session. We get a real mix of people: a bunch of biology students, some CS people, a couple of humanities regulars who mostly come for the argument afterwards. I want to do the session on Dennett's idea of a "strange inversion of reasoning" -- the thing he claims Darwin and Turing both pulled off, where the pre-theoretic assumption was that competence has to flow from comprehension (you make something well because you understand what you're doing), and both of them flipped it: Darwin showed you can get brilliant biological design from a process that understands nothing, and Turing showed you can get arithmetic done by a mechanism that has no idea what numbers are. The slogan version is competence without comprehension.

Here's my problem: I've tried explaining this to friends twice now and both times it collapsed. The biologists nod along at the Darwin half and then treat the Turing half as a random bolt-on about AI, and the CS people do the reverse. Nobody seems to leave with the thought that it's one move made twice, which is the whole point as far as I'm concerned. So I don't want you to explain the idea to me -- I've read Dennett on this -- I want you to plan the session. What's the dependency structure: what has to be in place before the inversion even registers as an inversion (I'm guessing they first need to feel the pull of the comprehension-first view, the "a watch implies a watchmaker" instinct, otherwise Darwin's move lands as just... a fact about finches?). Which single example should carry the session: my instinct is to anchor on natural selection since more than half the room has it already, but I worry that makes the Turing half feel bolted on again, so maybe there's a better common anchor.

Also map the misconceptions for me, in the order this specific audience will generate them, and tell me exactly where in the sequence to head each one off. Ones I'm anticipating: "so evolution has no foresight, big deal, everyone knows that" (the deflationary reading where the inversion loses all its bite), "so you're saying ChatGPT is basically evolution" (the CS people will go straight here), "this is just emergence dressed up," and someone will definately say competence without comprehension just means the process is random, which it doesn't. And give me one closing question I can pose that would tell me whether they've grasped the shared structure of the two inversions, rather than just having two separate fun facts about Darwin and Turing. If there's something I should explicitly leave out at this level -- I suspect getting into whether LLMs actually comprehend is a tarpit -- say so and say why.
