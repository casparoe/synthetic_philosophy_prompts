I am a PhD student in philosophy of science working on the foundations of machine learning, and I am drafting a section of my dissertation on the epistemological significance of the no-free-lunch (NFL) theorems for supervised learning. A claim I keep encountering in the literature is that these theorems settle Hume's problem of induction in the skeptic's favor: since, averaged uniformly over all possible data-generating distributions, no learning algorithm outperforms any other — including deliberately anti-inductive algorithms that predict the opposite of past patterns — any preference for induction over counter-induction must rest on an arbitrary, unjustifiable assumption about the world's actual distribution.

I am skeptical of this skeptical reading, and I would like you to argue for the position I am developing: that the NFL theorems fail to vindicate inductive skepticism because the appearance of neutrality in "averaging uniformly over all possible target functions" is an illusion. Please make the case that the theorems' result depends essentially on a particular choice of measure over the space of possible worlds or functions, that this choice is no more neutral or innocent than a simplicity-favoring prior — much as Bertrand-style paradoxes show that there is no unique way to be "uniform" over a continuous space of possibilities — and that, consequently, the NFL theorems do not prove that inductive priors are unjustifiable, but merely that they are unprovable from within the formalism, which is a far weaker and less interesting conclusion than the skeptic claims. Your argument should explain where exactly the skeptical inference from the NFL results goes wrong, address the obvious rejoinder that simplicity priors are equally arbitrary, and clarify what, if anything, the theorems do legitimately teach us about the status of induction.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — stating whether the NFL theorems vindicate inductive skepticism — and then develop the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
