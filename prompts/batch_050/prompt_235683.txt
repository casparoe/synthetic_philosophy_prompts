I've been chewing on a pattern I keep seeing at work and I can't find the right word for it, if there is one. I work in a university department, and there's a senior guy here — grew up rich, elite schools, won every prize going — whose entire professional persona is built around attacking elitism. He denounces competitiveness, mocks the word "excellence," and visibly enjoys taking ambitious younger colleagues down a peg in meetings; "careerist" is his favorite insult. Here's the thing though: nothing was ever denied to this man. There's no wound, no failure, no grapes out of reach that he then called sour. And I catch a milder version of it in myself — I grew up comfortable too, and the righteous little hit of pleasure I get when someone "privileged" gets humbled online is real, but I can't locate any injury it's compensating for. The inversion seems to live in the water supply, not in anybody's psyche.

I know this is somewhere in the neighborhood of Nietzsche's ressentiment, the slave revolt in morality and all that — the weak inverting the values of the strong because they can't take revenge directly. But every summary I read makes that sound like a psychology: actual powerlessness, actual bottled-up vengefulness, and then the "priestly" types who channel it into new values. What I'm describing doesn't seem to have the psychology at all. The people enforcing the inverted values are often the biggest winners of the old order, and as far as I can tell they're completely sincere — they didn't fake these values, they inherited them, the way you inherit table manners. It's also not hypocrisy, and I don't think it's just "luxury beliefs" or virtue-signaling either, because my colleague pays real costs for his crusades and would keep paying them with no audience at all.

So: does this configuration have a name of its own? Is it still ressentiment once you subtract the powerless, wounded individual, or does Nietzsche's account actually need that individual to get going — or can the herd and the institutions carry the inversion on their own, no resentful psyche required? Where are the edges of the concept — what would not count as this, and would Nietzsche himself recognize it as his phenomenon or as something else entirely? And a seperate worry I keep having: even if the lineage story is right, does it bear at all on whether the values are true or worth keeping, or is that just the genetic fallacy talking? One request on how you answer: please keep clear what's actually settled — what Nietzsche wrote, how interpreters standardly read it — from what's genuinely contested among people who study this stuff, and flag when you're extrapolating or making an educated guess rather than reporting a consensus, because I've noticed these terms get thrown around very loosely on the internet.
