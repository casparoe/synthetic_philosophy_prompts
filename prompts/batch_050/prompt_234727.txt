So for history class we read Nietzsche's essay about the uses and disadvantages of history, the one where he talks about monumental, antiquarian and critical history, and how too much history can basically crush life. It got me thinking: what would Nietzsche say about the internet? Like, nothing ever gets deleted anymore. Old tweets from 10 years ago can ruin your life, Google remembers everything, wikipedia has an article on every tiny event, and my phone keeps showing me "memories" from years ago that I didn't ask to see. On one hand that sounds like his worst nightmare, the past never letting go of us. But on the other hand, people today barely seem to know any actual history at all, they just have infinite storage and zero memory. So which is it in his terms -- are we drowning in history (like the antiquarian mode gone totally out of control), or are we weirdly ahistorical despite having all of it saved? And what do you think he'd say we should actually do with all this stored past, since he clearly didn't think forgetting was the whole answer either?
