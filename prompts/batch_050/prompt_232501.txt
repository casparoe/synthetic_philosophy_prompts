I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I sit on the staff council that must vote next month on a proposal to replace our standard contract with a 35-hour week plus an enforceable right to ignore email after hours. Management accepts the cap in principle but has tabled an amendment: any employee could opt out and work unlimited hours by signing a waiver, on the ground that our own engagement surveys rank 'a sense of purpose at work' as staff's leading source of meaning in life -- so, they argue, a universal cap protects a good (leisure) that many here do not actually want more of. The union delegation opposes all waivers, pointing to karoshi, Japan's officially recognized category of death from overwork, and arguing that the survey data are untrustworthy evidence: if something like the Protestant work ethic has trained generations of workers to locate their worth in their jobs, then people's reported preferences are part of the pathology, not a verdict on the policy. Draft the council's decision memo recommending one of three outcomes -- universal cap, cap with the waiver, or rejection of the cap. The memo should state plainly which view of work's place in a good life each option commits the company to, give the strongest version of management's preference-based argument before answering it, and close with whatever conditions or review mechanisms the council could realistically administer.
