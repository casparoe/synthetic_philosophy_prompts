My friends are getting married in a decommissioned grain elevator turned workers co-op, and I’ve been asked to give the short ceremony speech, like 6-7 minutes. One bride is basically Randian: she thinks altruism as a moral duty is poison, love should be selfish in the proud sense, promises are chosen values not debts, and she visibly flinches at words like sacrifice, duty, selflessness, and giving yourself away. The other comes from a mutual-aid / Catholic Worker-ish crowd where unpriced care is the whole point of love, and her grandmother has already warned me not to turn marriage into a contract negotiation. The room is half philosophy-adjacent people who will definately notice if I flatten Rand into “greed is good,” and half relatives who just want to cry.

Write the speech. The hard constraint: Rand’s view has to do structural work, not be a name-drop. It should rule out some normal wedding lines -- no now-you-exist-for-each-other, no love means never counting, no give till it hurts -- force a different account of why devotion can be total without being self-erasure, and still let the mutual-aid side hear that mercy, care, forgiveness, and help nobody can repay are real rather than weakness.

Build it around one concrete image: a shared ledger for the marriage where some entries are priced (chores, money, who moves cities, who apologizes first after fights) and two pages are intentionally left blank for gifts that must never be totaled. Include exactly one joke both halves of the room can laugh at, and end with a vow-like charge neither bride could honestly call a demand for sacrifice. After the speech, add five bullet notes on which lines would make each side bristle and why you kept or changed them.
