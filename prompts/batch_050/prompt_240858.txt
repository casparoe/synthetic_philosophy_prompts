An epistemologist I know has a consistent-sounding set of intuitions about when knowers may disregard counterevidence, and I want to figure out the rule she's actually running. Her verdicts so far:

1. She sees her bicycle locked outside the library; a passing stranger remarks that someone just rode off on a similar-looking bike. She may disregard the stranger without going back to check -- she knows the bike is still there.
2. A colleague tells her the seminar is at 3:00; a second, equally reliable colleague says it was moved to 4:00. She must suspend judgment and verify; she may not dismiss the second colleague on the grounds that she already knew.
3. She sees that the hallway wall is red; a technician mentions that this corridor is fitted with color-tinting demonstration lights that are almost always off. She may no longer claim to know the wall's color without checking whether the lights are on.
4. Having just reviewed the minutes, she knows the committee approved the grant; she is warned that next week a doctored recording will begin circulating that purports to show the committee voting it down. She may resolve now, in advance, to disregard that recording when it appears.

The new case: she confirmed on the airline's website this morning that her flight departs at 6:00 p.m. At lunch, a friend -- generally reliable and a frequent traveler on the same route -- says he is fairly sure he received an email announcing the evening flight had been moved to 4:30, but he cannot produce the email and admits he may be thinking of a different day. May she disregard the friend's report and proceed on the 6:00 schedule, or must she re-verify with the airline?

Predict her verdict, and identify the rule that best unifies cases 1-4 -- is it a distinction between first-order counterevidence and evidence about her source's reliability, a requirement that new evidence rival her original basis on its own terms, a closure-based principle that knowledge licenses dismissing misleading evidence, or something else? Note where the candidate rules agree on cases 1-4 but diverge on the flight case, and describe a further case that would settle which rule she is running.
