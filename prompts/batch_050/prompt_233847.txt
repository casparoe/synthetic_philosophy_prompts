I need a term for something, if the safety literature already has one. The standard pitch for scalable oversight goes: have one model critique another, or have two models debate and let a judge pick the winner, and the adverserial pressure keeps everyone honest. But I keep noticing a failure mode where both models do better, reward-wise, by quietly not fighting. The critic raises only tiny, easily-fixed nits because harsh critiques are risky if the judge can't verify them. The debater concedes early or both sides drift toward whatever the judge already seems to believe. Nobody exchanged messages, there's no price-fixing-style agreement you could point at and ban -- it's just that every step toward being agreeable was locally rewarded. Related but distinct case: you ask a model to red-team another model from the same lab and it comes back with suspiciously gentle findings.

This isn't quite tacit collusion in the economist's sense, since there's no repeated game with punishment holding it together, and it's not sycophancy, since there's no user being flattered. It's also different from two models just being wrong the same way because they trained on the same data, though I suspect those get confused a lot. So: does this have an established name, and how does the literature distinguish it from ordinary correlated failure? If there's no name, coin one, define it crisply, and give me the boundary cases -- especially what evidence would tell you you're seeing this rather than honest agreement that happens to be correct.
