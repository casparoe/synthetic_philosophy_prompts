I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. At a design review last week, my team spent over an hour arguing about whether our new event pipeline provides "exactly-once delivery." Each side kept producing scenarios the other had to concede, but the disagreement never moved, and our manager eventually closed the meeting by saying, "This is just semantics — pick a definition, document it, and move on." That settled the meeting but not my discomfort: whatever was at stake didn't feel resolved, only renamed.

This phrase — "it's just semantics," or "you're merely arguing over words" — gets used constantly in engineering culture to end disputes: whether a system is "really" serverless, whether a datastore is "truly" ACID-compliant, whether a component is "actually" a microservice. I'd like to understand what it's claiming when it's deployed this way. Could you lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise theses someone might be expressing when they call a dispute "just semantics" or "merely verbal"? For each thesis, say whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or actually false, and note what picture of language or meaning it quietly presupposes. I'm especially interested in whether any of these readings conflates "a question is about words" with "a question is unimportant" or "has no right answer," and whether that conflation is doing illegitimate work when the phrase is used to shut down an argument.

Then apply the menu back to my meeting. Which reading, if any, would have justified my manager's move of stipulating a definition and moving on? Was the exactly-once disagreement plausibly verbal in the relevant sense, or substantive disagreement wearing verbal clothes? And is there a general diagnostic an engineer could run — something involving stipulated vocabularies, or asking what each party would predict, build, or monitor differently — to tell genuinely verbal disputes apart from real ones before dismissing them? Finally, if philosophers who work on this disagree among themselves about when a dispute counts as merely verbal, I'd rather know where the live disagreement lies than walk away thinking "just semantics" is a settled technical verdict.
