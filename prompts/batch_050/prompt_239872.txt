I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I keep running into a heuristic that works suspiciously well in my field and apparently in physics too: "elegance is evidence." In code review, an elegant solution is treated as more likely to be correct; in physics, people talk as though the beauty of a theory is a reason to believe it before the data arrive. But I've been thinking about my own case, and I'm no longer sure the inference is clean: elegant code might correlate with correctness only because careful engineers write elegant code, or because short, uniform code has a smaller space of places for bugs to hide, or because we retroactively call whatever survived testing "elegant." None of those mechanisms have anything to do with the code being true of the problem in some deeper sense.

Take the slogan "elegance is evidence" and lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing. I want a menu, not an essay defending one reading. For each candidate reading, do three things: state it precisely enough that I could tell what would count against it; classify it as trivial, substantive-and-plausible, or probably false; and say what mechanism would have to hold in the world for it to work (for example, something about the prior probability of simple hypotheses, something about how human aesthetic responses got calibrated, or something about selection effects in which theories get remembered). Cover at least the following, plus any I've missed: elegance as a proxy for simplicity or compression, elegance as a symptom of unification or explanatory reach, elegance as a social signal among practitioners, and elegance as a post-hoc label applied only to confirmed results. Then tell me which readings, if any, would actually justify the physicist's practice of trusting a beautiful theory ahead of the evidence, and which would justify only the weaker practice of investigating beautiful theories first.

Format: a one-sentence bottom line up front, then the menu as a numbered list with a verdict for each item, then the closing comparison. Maximum 650 words.
