You know how in the Laws, Plato surprisingly recommends regulated wine-drinking as a kind of training ground for virtue — the idea being that drink loosens our self-control, so practicing self-restraint while tipsy, under the eye of a sober and respected drinking-master, is great practice for practicing self-restraint in the rest of life. He even suggests wine can be a test of character: get someone drunk and you see who they really are.

Now imagine Plato is alive today and someone shows him the following modern things: (1) cheap, accurate breathalyzers that tell you exactly how drunk you are; (2) hangover-prevention pills that cancel out most of the physical costs of a heavy night; (3) 'sober bars' that mimic the whole social vibe of a bar but serve only non-alcoholic drinks; and (4) the modern legal practice where courts order repeat offenders to attend mandatory alcohol-education classes.

What would Plato make of each of these? Would he welcome the breathalyzer as the perfect impartial 'drinking-master', or would he think outsourcing self-monitoring to a device defeats the entire point of the exercise? Would the hangover pill make wine-training more attractive to him (pure moral exercise, no bodily damage) or less (the hangover being part of nature's discipline)? And would he see the court-ordered education classes as vindicating his approach, or as a degenerate version of it? Please reconstruct his likely verdicts, and say where you think his reasoning would actually lead him astray, if anywhere.
