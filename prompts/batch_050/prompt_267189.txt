Camp two (call them the absence camp) thinks the whole point of grief is learning to relate to someone who can no longer surprise you, correct you, or be wronged by you, and that the griefbot's core trick is manufacturing fake surprise and fake correction, so it doesn't continue the relationship, it dissolves the thing that made it a relationship. Give me the claims both camps would actually sign: about what makes a relationship with a person different from a relationship with a text or a photo, about how memory itself is already reconstructive and unreliable, about what the grieving person does and doesn't know about what they're doing. Then the residual disagreements, each phrased carefully enough that the side being described would accept the wording as their own view, not a strawman. Then the cruxes: which sub-questions, if settled, would move at least one camp.
