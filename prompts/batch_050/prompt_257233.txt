So I've been reading about the School of Names and the white horse dialogue (the "a white horse is not a horse" thing attributed to Gongsun Long), and I've talked myself into a reading of it that I want stress-tested. My position: the whole dialogue stops looking like a sophism once you take "horse" (ma) in classical Chinese to work more like a mass term or kind-term than like a count noun picking out individual horses. On this reading, "horse" refers to horse-stuff or the horse-kind as a whole, and "white horse" picks out a proper part of that -- so "a white horse is not a horse" comes out literally true in a boring, correct, mereological way, the way a slice of cake is not the cake. The paradox dissolves; Gongsun Long isn't tricking anyone with an ambiguity, he's making a clean point about part and whole that his interlocutor keeps missing.

What would count against this? That's what I want from you. Not generic "some scholars disagree" stuff -- actual levers. Like: does anything in the dialogue's own argument structure break the reading (I'm thinking of the passage about going to a stable and asking for a horse vs. a white horse, where yellow and black horses count for the first request but not the second -- does that exchange fit the mass-noun reading or undermine it)? Is there anything about how 非 (fei, "is not") works in classical Chinese -- identity vs. class-membership -- that would show the dialogue is really trading on an equivocation after all, in which case my "no sophistry" claim collapses even if the mereological point stands? Would it matter if other School of Names paradoxes (like the hard-and-white one) clearly *are* sophistical -- does guilt by association count as evidence about authorial intent here, or not? And what about the fact that basically everyone in the later Chinese tradition treated the dialogue as a notorious piece of clever trickery -- is the near-unanimous verdict of the tradition a defeater for a modern reconstruction, or is that exactly the kind of thing a revisionist reading is licensed to ignore?
