I'm a cellist in an amateur orchestra and last week at the pub after rehearsal our conductor shut down an argument about whether film scores "mean" anything by declaring that, as Schopenhauer said, music is a copy of the will itself — so asking what a piece is about is already a mistake. Our first violinist (who does philosophy at uni) said that's not what the slogan means at all. I looked it up and it's apparently from The World as Will and Representation, where Schopenhauer seems to think the other arts copy Platonic-ish Ideas of particular things, while music skips all that and relates to the world roughly as a depiction relates to what's depicted, expressing emotions in the abstract rather than anyone's actual sadness or joy.

Can you unpick the slogan itself — "music is a copy of the will itself" — as a menu of distinct claims it might be making? Like, does "copy" mean a picture, a symptom, a causal product, a language? Does "the will" mean my individual wanting, some metaphysical world-will, or just human striving in general? For each reading, say whether it's trivial, substantive-but-defensible, or probably false, and how it sits with his claim that music somehow ranks above the other arts. Most importantly: which readings, if any, actually support the conductor's move — i.e. the conclusion that it's confused to ask what a film score is about? I suspect he was using a metaphysical sledgehammer to win an aesthetics arguement, but I can't show it.
