Me and a friend keep arguing about why human extinction would be bad over and above all the deaths it involves. His verdicts on cases so far:

- Painless universal infertility, last generation knows, everyone lives out their natural lives: still a catastrophe, nearly as bad as everyone dying outright.
- Everyone alive today dies painlessly tomorrow, but humanity (kids raised by relatives, etc.) carries on for millennia: bad, but nowhere near extinction-level bad.
- Extinction, but aliens later find our archives and take up our science and art as their own: takes a real chunk out of the badness, maybe half.
- Extinction, but (unknown to anyone) the sun was going to explode in 150 years anyway, wiping out any possible continuation: the extinction happening now is still much worse than just letting the 150 years run.
- Humanity survives forever but frozen -- no new ideas, no new art, same routines maintained indefinitely: bad, but not extinction-level bad.

New case: humanity doesn't die out, but within 200 years it drifts into a civilization that repudiates basically everything we value -- abandons our science as a dead end, finds our art ugly, remembers us mainly as a cautionary tale -- and then thrives on those terms for a million years. Predict his verdict comparing this to straightforward extinction next century, say which rule he's implicitly running (lost future value? something Scheffler-ish where the meaning of present lives depends on an afterlife? truncation of ongoing projects?), and tell me which further case would settle it between the rules.
