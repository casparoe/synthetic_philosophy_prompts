So I play guitar (badly) and do a bit of woodworking (slightly less badly), and there's a thing I've noticed in both that I can't find a proper word for, and it's driving me a little nuts. When things are going well, the tool sort of... isn't there. I don't mean I'm ignoring it, I mean my experience just doesn't contain a guitar. There's the song, there's what I'm trying to do, and the pick and strings are transparent, like the glass in a window you're looking through. Same with the hammer: I'm aware of the nail, not the hammer. But then something goes wrong -- a string slips out of tune, the hammer head wobbles -- and suddenly the tool is ALL I can see. It's this clunky object with weight and flaws, and the activity itself falls apart for a second. The whole situation seems to flip into a different mode.

Here's the part that I think makes it interesting, or at least harder to google. There seem to be two roads into that second mode. One is breakage, like above. But the other road is just paying attention. If, mid-song, I start thinking about what my left hand is doing, the skill evaporates and the guitar becomes an object again, even though nothing broke. Nothing failed except that I looked at it. A friend of mine who did some philosophy in college says this whole thing is in Heidegger, Being and Time, the ready-to-hand stuff, tools withdrawing into use and then showing up when they break. Fine. But when I skimmed the bits he pointed me to, the breakdown talk all seemed to be about equipment failing or being missing or in the way -- not about looking too closely at something that's working perfectly. So either Heidegger covers the attention road somewhere and my friend pointed me at the wrong passage, or the attention road is a genuinely different phenomenon that just arrives at the same place.

So my actual questions: does the first state (tool transparent, absorbed in the task) have an established name, and does the flip have one, and is the attention-triggered version of the flip the same phenomenon as the breakdown-triggered one by the lights of whatever theory handles this, or merely a lookalike? Also how is any of this different from plain old choking under pressure, which seems related but not identical, since stage fright can hit before you've played a note? I don't want just a label, I want the carving -- what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, where the edges are. If the two-roads structure itself doesn't have a name, coin one and define it well enough that I'd know whether, say, a fly landing on my hand counts as road one or road two or neither.
