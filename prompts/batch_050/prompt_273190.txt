Me and my brother keep having the same dumb argument every time we watch the news. A politician does something grim for the greater good — orders a strike knowing some civilians will die, lets one person get framed to stop a riot, whatever — and my brother says if it was the right call then that's it, case closed, nobody should lose any sleep. And I keep insisting it can be the right call AND the person should feel wrecked about it for the rest of their life, and that the feeling wrecked part actually matters morally, it's not just therapy stuff. Neither of us has the vocabulary to pin down where we actually disagree, we just go in circles.

So I want a decision tree (or sorting quiz, whichever works better) that takes someone from the starting point "leaders sometimes have to do things that would be monstrous for a normal person" down to a named position on what to say about that. Some rules:

- 6 to 8 branch questions, each one a concrete scenario or a yes/no claim someone could answer without knowing any philosophy. No question like "are you a consequentialist." Cases could include the ticking bomb torture one, lying to voters because they'd vote wrongly otherwise, breaking a promise to an ally, that kind of thing.
- The leaves have to include at least: the my-brother view (if it was the right action there's nothing left to regret, full stop), the absolutist view (some things are just never allowed no matter what hangs on them), the view that the action was right but genuinely wrong anyway and guilt is the fitting response, and the view that political roles change the moral rules so what's dirty for a private person isn't dirty for a president (or the reverse, if anyone holds the reverse).
- Here's my own half-baked position that I want located on the tree too: I think the wrongness doesn't disappear, it gets sort of spent, like a debt that somebody still owes even though spending it was justified — and that a decent leader should do something about the debt afterward (apologize, resign, make reparations) rather than just feel bad. If my view is actually one of the standard ones under a name I don't know, say so. If it's a weird hybrid, say which two leaves it's glued together from.
- For any combination of answers nobody seems to defend, put an empty leaf there and say in one sentence why nobody lives there (incoherent? just unpopular?).
- Flag any two leaves your tree can't actually tell apart.

Keep the whole thing under 800 words, tree and notes included. Oh and give a one-line answer for where my brother lands based on what he said above.
