I have spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, and I keep encountering the suggestion that maximum entropy can dissolve the problem of priors. Please argue that it cannot, at least when maximum entropy is presented as a way to derive a uniquely rational prior from ignorance alone.

Use the following case. An agent knows that some generator produces independent Bernoulli trials with an unknown constant success parameter \(p \in [0,1]\). One analyst applies maximum entropy directly to \(p\) and obtains the uniform density \(f(p)=1\). A second analyst parameterizes the same hypothesis space by \(q=p^2\), which is the probability of obtaining two consecutive successes, and applies maximum entropy to \(q\), obtaining \(g(q)=1\). Transformed back to the \(p\)-parameterization, the second prior is \(f(p)=2p\). Thus, for example, the two analysts assign different probabilities to the same event \(p \leq 1/4\), despite beginning with the same evidence and differing only in how they describe the parameter.

Argue that this redescription problem shows that maximum entropy cannot by itself identify a rationally mandatory prior: some further principle must privilege a parameterization, reference measure, symmetry, or set of constraints. Address the following attempted replies:

1. The probability \(p\) is the natural parameter because it directly represents the success rate, whereas \(q\) is an artificial transformation. Explain why calling \(p\) natural is not enough unless one supplies an epistemically non-arbitrary reason for privileging it, especially given that both parameterizations describe the same Bernoulli family.

2. Maximum entropy is meant to incorporate all background knowledge, and knowledge of the Bernoulli likelihood already breaks the symmetry between \(p\) and \(q\). State precisely what additional structure would have to do this work, and why the mere existence of an equivalent likelihood expressed in either coordinate does not settle the matter.

3. Prior disagreement is unimportant because enough evidence will make the two posteriors converge. Distinguish asymptotic convergence from rationality under finite evidence, and explain why decision problems can remain sensitive to the difference even when both priors assign nonzero density throughout \([0,1]\).

The conclusion should not be that maximum entropy is useless. Distinguish maximum entropy as a conditional procedure—given a chosen state space, base measure, and explicitly justified constraints—from the stronger objective-Bayesian claim that ignorance plus maximum entropy uniquely determines what one ought to believe. End by stating what kind of independently justified symmetry or invariance principle could still support rational constraints on priors without pretending that maximum entropy alone supplied the justification.

Write a direct philosophical argument with the necessary algebra, not a historical survey or literature review. No citations or bibliography are needed.
