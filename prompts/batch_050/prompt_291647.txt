so my mom died in october and somehow i've ended up agreeing to give the memorial remarks at our town historical society's winter dinner, which she basically ran for forty years. she was the town historian, and the thing she was famous for -- locally famous, which is its own weird category -- was spending decades proving that our town's whole founding story never happened. the thing on the plaque downtown, the thing in the fourth grade pageant, the thing the gift shop sells ornaments about. she showed it was a story some minister made up in the 1890s. half the town adored her for it and half never forgave her, and she genuinely loved both halves.

anyway the society president called me and hinted, not subtlely, that it would be nice if i said she'd "made her peace with the legend" toward the end. she did not. i was there. but going through her desk i found an index card taped inside her field notebook with this copied out, from Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War, book 1:

"far from permitting myself to derive it from the first source that came to hand, I did not even trust my own impressions, but it rests partly on what I saw myself, partly on what others saw for me, the accuracy of the report being always tried by the most severe and detailed tests possible."

and then right after that bit he says eyewitnesses contradict each other because of imperfect memory or partiality for one side.

heres the thing thats messing with me. a eulogy is exactly the kind of first-source-that-comes-to-hand he means. whatever i say about her is what people will repeat, and the room is full of people who WANT to remember her as softening at the end, because it lets them keep the plaque. so write me a speech, maybe five minutes, that uses the Thucydides passage to say what she was actually doing all those years -- not debunking for fun but something more like respect -- while being honest that the speech itself is partial testimony from a grieving son and shouldn't get a free pass on her standards. it has to land for the gift shop half of the room without lying to them, and for her two actual historian friends who will know if i flinch. don't let it turn into a lecture about historiography, its still a eulogy, but i want the passage doing real work, not just read out as decoration.
