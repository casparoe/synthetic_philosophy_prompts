In March 1989, the electrochemists Martin Fleischmann and Stanley Pons announced at a University of Utah press conference that they had sustained a nuclear fusion reaction at room temperature by electrolyzing heavy water with a palladium electrode. Within weeks, major laboratories reported failures to replicate the excess heat and the expected neutron flux; within months, mainstream physics had moved on, yet a dedicated community of researchers continued to insist the effect was real, explaining away each null result by appeal to uncontrolled variables: the palladium had not absorbed enough deuterium, the electrolysis had not run long enough, the metallurgy of the electrodes differed, the calorimetry of the replication attempt was faulty.

Analyze this episode through the lens of the Duhem–Quine thesis about the underdetermination of theory by evidence. I do not need the textbook statement of the thesis; I want a disciplined mapping. Which proposition in the affair plays the role of the hypothesis under test, and which propositions function as the auxiliary assumptions that a negative result can be deflected onto? The defenders' moves look, on their face, like exactly what Quine's holism permits: any statement may be held true come what may, if we make drastic enough adjustments elsewhere in the web of belief. So explain why the broader scientific community nonetheless treated the case as closed within a year or two, and whether that closure shows that actual scientific practice is governed by considerations the bare logical thesis ignores — and if so, which considerations, exactly, did the work.

Then run the analysis in the other direction. Does cold fusion confirm underdetermination, since committed researchers really did exploit the logical slack for decades? Or does it pressure the strong version of the thesis, since the slack turned out to be socially available but epistemically inert — exploited, yet carrying no conviction outside a shrinking circle? Finally, propose a criterion, drawn from the episode rather than imported wholesale from a philosopher, that would distinguish a legitimate revision of an auxiliary assumption from a merely face-saving maneuver, and test your criterion against one or two of the specific defenses the cold fusion community actually offered.
