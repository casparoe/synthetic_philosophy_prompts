Longtime LW/SSC reader here, and I've been chewing on Dennett's color phi stuff for years (the dot that changes color halfway across the screen, and you *see* it change before it could have gotten there). Dennett's move is to argue there's no fact of the matter between the brain filling things in beforehand ("Orwellian revision," the experience itself gets doctored after the fact) vs. inserting a false memory of having seen it ("Stalinesque revision"), and I've never been fully sold on that dissolution.

So here's what I want: a list of desiderata that ANY theory of the timing of conscious experience has to meet, in order to even be in the running. Not a tour of the contenders -- I know the multiple drafts pitch already. I want the spec. E.g.:

- which phenomenological facts it has to take at face value vs. which it's allowed to explain away (and what the criterion for that split is)
- how it handles apparent retrocausation cases like color phi and postdiction effects generally
- what it says about the distinction between the time of a representation and the time represented (confusing these seems like a classic failure mode)
- whether it's allowed to invoke an "inner audience" that observes at a determinate time, and if not, what replaces it
- what it commits to empirically -- like, what would neural data about when perceptual processes complete even show, given the theory

For each desideratum give the consideration motivating it, and flag which ones you think are genuine constraints vs. which are just Cartesian Theater assumptions smuggled back in. And the thing I most want to know: is there a subset of these desiderata that's jointly unsatisfiable? My suspicion is that "there's a determinate time at which experiences happen" and "all the phenomenology is veridical" can't both be kept, and the interesting question is which one has to go. Tell me if that's right or if the conflict is somewhere else.
