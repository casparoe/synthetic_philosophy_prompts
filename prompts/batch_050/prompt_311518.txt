So I'm in year 12 and I run a little philosophy discussion group at school, and this term we've got into this concept called the unilateralist's curse. Quick version in my own words: imagine a group of people who can each independently take some big, hard-to-reverse action, and the action happens if even ONE of them thinks its worth doing. Since everyone's judgement is noisy, the keenest person's estimate is probably an overestimate, so the thing ends up happening far more often than it should — even when the group as a whole, on a sober view, would have judged it a bad idea.

For next week's session I'm compiling real-world situations where the curse plausibly bites, and I'd love one more entry. Here's what I've got so far:

1. Gain-of-function research on dangerous pathogens — any single lab can go ahead or publish even if most of the field thinks it's reckless.
2. Geoengineering — one country, or conceivably one very rich individual, could start spraying aerosols into the atmosphere on everyone's behalf.
3. Messaging extraterrestrials — anyone with a powerful enough transmitter can effectively speak for all of humanity, no permission slip required.
4. Whistleblowing and leaks — one insider can release information that can never be unreleased, whatever the other 10,000 employees think.
5. Releasing powerful code or models openly — any one developer or lab can push the button, and once it's out there it's out there.

Here's the ask: pretend you're an expert on the ethics of collective action and existential risk, and think this through carefully — feel free to brainstorm a few candidates first and then pick your best one. What's ONE more situation for my list that isn't already on it? It needs to be genuinely new: not a rewording, special case, or mash-up of the five above (so please don't just hand me another instance of "someone leaks info", that's #4 covered). Then tell me two more things: does your case have some feature mine lack — is the curse stronger or weaker there, and why — and, honestly, are there any of my five where the curse doesn't actually apply as cleanly as I've assumed? Please answer in British English, cheers.
