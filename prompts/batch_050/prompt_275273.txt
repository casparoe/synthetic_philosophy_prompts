I drive a delivery route and burn through a lot of history-of-technology audiobooks, and over the last year or so I've accidentally assembled a whole worldview about this stuff. Before I inflict it on my brother-in-law again at Thanksgiving I want to know if the pieces actually fit together. Here's what I believe: (1) Some technologies have politics baked right into the hardware — a nuclear power plant needs armed guards, secrecy, and a central chain of command no matter what country builds it, and you just can't run one as a decentralized hippie co-op, whereas a rooftop solar panel doesn't force any of that. I also read somewhere about low bridges on Long Island allegedly being designed so city buses couldn't get to certain beaches, and whether or not that specific story is true, that kind of thing is definitely possible. (2) At the same time, whether a technology ends up freeing people or controlling them depends on the society that gets hold of it — the same internet is an organizing tool in one country and a surveillance machine in another. (3) Nobody ever gets to vote on whether a technology arrives: nobody asked us if smartphones should reorganize childhood, they just did, and everyone scrambled to adapt after the fact. Technology drives history more than history drives technology. (4) And I think cities are right to ban things like facial recognition, and generally we should have democratic say over which technologies get rolled out instead of leaving it to whichever company ships first.

Some of this has to be in tension and I can feel it but can't pin it down. (1) and (2) seem fine to me because the internet really does get used differently in different places, but maybe I'm dodging something there. My brother-in-law goes after (3) and (4) — he says if technology just happens to us, banning facial recognition is King Canute yelling at the tide — and I don't have a great answer. And honestly I can't tell whether (1) is an argument for (4) or against it. So: audit the whole set. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only look like they conflict but turn out fine, how severe is each real one, and what's the cheapest revision that fixes the most? If one commitment flat-out has to go, tell me which and what it costs me. Please answer in American English.
