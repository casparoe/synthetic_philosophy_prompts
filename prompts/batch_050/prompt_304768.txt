Working on my "philosophical profile" for a reading group on methodology in ethics, and I'd like it stress-tested before I show it to anyone. Here's what I currently hold:

1. Wide reflective equilibrium is the right method for justifying moral beliefs -- the only thing that confers justification, really.
2. Coherence by itself isn't truth-conducive; a coherent worldview can be systematically and monstrously false.
3. Moral intuitions are causal products of upbringing and culture and have no special reliability on their own.
4. There's no method-independent moral truth out there that our beliefs could match or fail to match; what's right is fixed by what the best version of our method would deliver.
5. Some judgments are non-negotiable: that torturing children for fun is wrong is one, and any moral theory that denies it is thereby refuted, full stop.

Tell me which pairs of these pull against each other and how badly. For each tension, say what's generating it, how severe it is, and what the cheapest fix would be. I suspect at least one apparent tension will dissolve on inspection -- flag that too, and say what it costs to keep it dissolved. Then tell me which single revision would do the most work if I wanted the set fully consistent.
