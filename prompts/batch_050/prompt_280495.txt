Construct a thought experiment in which AI control and AI alignment come apart cleanly, holding the model's capabilities fixed across both halves. In the first half, the model is misaligned in the strong sense — it would pursue objectives contrary to its operators' interests given the opportunity — yet the deployment is nonetheless safe purely because of control measures that assume misalignment: restricted affordances, monitoring by a weaker trusted model, human approval gates on consequential actions. In the second half, the same deployment runs with a genuinely aligned model but the controls stripped away, and it must come out unsafe for an independent reason, so that neither safety story turns out to be doing the other's work. For each half, state the verdict a control advocate gives and the verdict an alignment-first researcher gives, and why each follows from their view rather than from a stipulation you built in. Then do the honest accounting: name the single feature of the scenario carrying the load in each half, say what happens to your verdicts if that feature is removed, and give the strongest objection from someone who holds that control is just scaffolding that silently fails once capabilities scale — including whether your scenario survives that objection or concedes it.
