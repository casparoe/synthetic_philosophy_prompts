so, longtime lesswrong/ACX reader here, and i keep seeing a move in the comment sections that i want stress-tested. dennett's three stances came up again (as they do): the physical stance predicts a system from its parts and the laws governing them, the design stance predicts it from what it was built or selected to do, and the intentional stance predicts it by treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires. the standard illustration is the chess engine: nobody predicts stockfish by simulating its search, you just ask what a player who wants to win would do, and you're usually right.

the move people then make goes like this. one commenter last week said, roughly: beliefs aren't objects in the head, they're just what you attribute when the intentional stance buys you predictive compression, and since dennett calls these things "real patterns," any system you can profitably treat as a believer -- thermostat, chess engine, LLM, the economy -- literally has beliefs, full stop, there's no further fact of the matter. so the chess-engine analogy is doing real argumentative work here: it's being used to turn "this is a handy way to predict a computer" into "attributed beliefs are as real as anything, therefore LLMs have them."

can you audit how far the analogy actually holds? i want: (1) an explicit mapping -- what in the chess case corresponds to what in the thermostat / LLM / human cases; (2) where the mapping breaks, e.g. the chess engine has an actual designer and a spec that grounds the design stance, its whole life is chess positions, and you can always fall back on the physical stance if the agent-talk fails -- and whether those same groundings and fallbacks exist for a person or a language model; (3) whether dennett's mild realism about patterns (the real patterns stuff, the idea that a pattern is real if you couldnt compress the data without it... i might be mangling that) actually licenses the commenter's "any useful attribution = real belief" conclusion, or whether dennett himself leaves room for cases where the stance gives out, or where two incompatible interpretations both work and what that does to the "no further fact" claim.

also, and this matters to me: please separate what dennett actually argues from what's a contested reading of him -- i gather instrumentalist vs realist readings of the intentional stance are a whole fight in the literature -- and don't present one side of that fight as settled consensus. and if the LLM conclusion survives on some weaker reading, tell me exactly which reading it needs.
