So here's where I'm at: I've basically been talked into ante rem structuralism about arithmetic. The Benacerraf point -- that 2 being a member of 4 on one set-theoretic reduction but not another shows numbers can't be sets, just positions in a structure -- strikes me as decisive, and I've started telling people so.

Now I want to stress-test that before I embarass myself further. What would count against it? Not the standard "but what are positions, metaphysically?" hand-wringing -- I mean specific things: which intuitions about number would the view have to flatly deny, which possible cases it can't describe, which formal results would sink it or show it collapses into the set-theoretic realism it was supposed to replace.

Two worries I already have, and I'd like you to tell me whether they actually bite or whether I'm confused:

1. Categoricity. The structuralist needs the natural number structure to be uniquely characterized, and as I understand it that requires full second-order semantics, which arguably presupposes the set theory structuralism was meant to escape. If that circularity worry holds up, is it fatal or just a footnote?

2. Indiscernible positions. Complex numbers bug me: within the structure there's an automorphism swapping i and -i, nothing structural distinguishes them, so on a purely structuralist picture it seems like there can't be a fact about which one is i. If structures can have non-trivial automorphisms, does the "positions are individuated only by their relations" line just fail for them -- and does arithmetic survive even if it does?

Beyond those, I want the full map of the view's exposure: the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and for each one whether a committed structuralist (say a Shapiro-type) would actually concede it counts against the view or would dismiss it as missing the point. Also tell me what the view predicts or rules out that its rivals (set-theoretic realism, nominalism, in re structuralism) don't -- i.e. what it's actually risking, if anything. And be honest if the conclusion is that it's so flexible it risks almost nothing, and whether that's a virtue or a tell.

Give me the reasoning in prose first. Then at the end, summarize the defeater map as a JSON object with this schema:

- "position": string, one line
- "defeaters": array of objects, each with:
  - "name": string (short label)
  - "kind": one of "intuition", "possible-case", "formal-result", "collapse-into-rival"
