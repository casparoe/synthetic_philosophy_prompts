Okay so I'm a novelist, mid-draft on a book that's turning out to hinge on something I didn't plan: whether two characters ever actually decided something together, or whether each just drifted into it and retroactively narrated it as a joint decision. In the book it's a married couple who, over about eight years, let the wife's dementia-slowed mother move in, and later there's a blowup where the husband claims "we chose this" and the wife claims "you never agreed to anything, you just stopped objecting." A friend of mine who does philosophy of action read the chapter and said oh, you're writing about shared intention, there's a whole literature, and now I've fallen down a hole of papers that all seem to presuppose what I want to have explained.

So before I read another word of it, I want the spec first. What would a theory of shared or joint intention actually have to deliver for it to be adequate -- not adequate for winning arguments at conferences, adequate for a novelist trying to write the moral psychology of two people doing something together without cheating? Like, which cases does it absolutely have to get right (I'm guessing: two people carrying a couch vs. two strangers both pushing a broken-down car at the same red light), which distinctions can it not collapse (doing the same thing side by side vs. doing it together, agreeing reluctantly vs. never agreeing but going along), which facts about ordinary life can it not contradict (small groups don't sign contracts before making dinner; big orchestras seem to act together without anyone knowing everyone)? And the thing I most want flagged: which of these requirements pull against each other, because I have a suspision that the same feature that makes a theory cover orchestras breaks it for marriages, and vice versa.

Give me exactly seven desiderata as a numbered list, each one a sentence or two with the case or consideration motivating it, no introduction and no conclusion. Mark each one non-negotiable or negotiable-but-common, and if two items on the list look jointly unsatisfiable, say which and why inside the items themselves. At the end, one extra numbered item (item 8) telling me which single desideratum you think knocks out the most existing theories, since that's probably the chapter where my characters' marriage actually lives.
