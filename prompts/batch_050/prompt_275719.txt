For a seminar on rational choice under multiplicative uncertainty, I want a specification written before we read anything: the desiderata that any adequate theory of rational wager sizing — how much of one's capital to stake on a favorable but risky opportunity — must meet to count as a serious account. At minimum, the specification should take a position on each of the following: what the theory owes us about a single, never-repeated bet as opposed to an indefinitely repeated sequence; how it should adjudicate between maximizing expected monetary value and maximizing the expected logarithm of wealth (the Kelly prescription), and whether appealing to long-run growth rates to justify the latter is legitimate or quietly circular when the long run never arrives; whether the theory must accommodate agents with genuine ruin constraints or intolerance of deep drawdowns, and if so whether that accommodation is rationality or mere preference; and how geometric rationality should be situated relative to orthodox expected utility theory — rival, special case (logarithmic utility), or something that answers a different question entirely. Each requirement should come with the consideration or case that makes it a genuine desideratum rather than a prejudice of one camp, and the finished list should be strong enough to pronounce on invented decision rules, such as a rule that maximizes expected log wealth minus a penalty proportional to the probability of finishing below half one's current capital — say what such a rule gets right and where it falls short.

Present the response as a numbered list of exactly six items, each no more than three sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: the first five items should state the five most important desiderata in descending order of how much theoretical work each does, giving the motivating consideration for each, and the sixth item should identify any pair of the five requirements you judge to be jointly unsatisfiable (for instance, justification purely from asymptotic growth versus applicability to one-off decisions) and name the single requirement that eliminates the most positions actually defended.
