So I work in ML and there's something about how we do evaluation that I keep turning over and can't quite square. The standard practice is: you train a model on one chunk of data, keep another chunk locked away, and then you test on the held-out chunk and treat a good score as evidence the model has actually learned the task -- not just memorized, but gotten something general. Whole careers and product launches ride on this. Test accuracy on a benchmark gets reported as if it tells you what the model will do on data it's never seen, from the "real world."

Here's my problem. Everyone in the field also knows the no-free-lunch results: averaged over all possible data-generating distributions, every learning algorithm does exactly as well as every other, including random guessing. There is no distribution-free reason to expect what worked on the test set to keep working. Which seems to mean that when we treat held-out performance as evidence of generalization, we are smuggling in some assumption about which distributions the world actually draws from -- that the real world is a special, friendly corner of the space of all possible worlds. Fine. But nobody ever states what that assumption is. It's just... in the walls of the whole practice.

What I want is: reconstruct the implicit epistemology here. What does the practice of train/test evaluation, as actually done, presuppose about the world and about induction? Is it a commitment to something like a simplicity bias in nature, or to the future resembling the past in some specific respect, or to datasets being "representative" (and what does representative even mean if you can't define it without circularity)? Then tell me: does the practice strictly need that assumption, or would several different views of induction license the same behavior -- i.e., where is it philosophically underdetermined? And finally, what would actually have to change in how we build and evaluate models if we dropped the implicit assumption and took the no-free-lunch result at face value -- does anything in the practice survive, or is it all built on the smuggled premise?
