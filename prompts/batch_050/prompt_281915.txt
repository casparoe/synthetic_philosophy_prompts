I'm putting together a syllabus unit on metaphor for a philosophy of language seminar I'm TAing, and before the students read any of the actual theories I want them to have the standard of evaluation in front of them first. So: write the list of desiderata any adequate theory of metaphor must meet -- the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it can't collapse -- with the case or consideration motivating each one. The cases should include: novel one-off metaphors (I offered the students "the committee's decision was a slow-motion car crash held in a boardroom" as an example), dead or fossilized metaphors like the mouth of a river, metaphors that seem to structure how people reason over entire domains (the way we talk about arguments in terms of combat, or time in terms of money), metaphors that resist paraphrase without it feeling like something got lost, the metaphor/simile contrast, and the awkward cases where the literal reading of the metaphorical sentence isn't false but trivially true. For each desideratum say why a theory that fails it has missed something real, and whether it's genuinely non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp.

The two views I know the students will bring are the Davidson-style line that a metaphor has no content beyond its literal meaning and all the real work is done by what the utterance prompts us to notice, versus the Lakoff-and-Johnson-style line that metaphor is primarily a systematic mapping between conceptual domains and language is just surface evidence of that. Don't quote either, I'm working from memory too, but do briefly run each against the list once it's written -- the list itself is the deliverable, the scorecard is secondary. What I most want flagged: any desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. It seems to me, for instance, that a theory can't both treat metaphorical content as fully capturable conceptual content and explain why good metaphors feel inexhaustible, but maybe I'm wrong. Also say which single requirement knocks out the most candidates, and add at least one desideratum that isn't on my list, with its motivating case.
