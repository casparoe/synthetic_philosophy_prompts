ok so I want a consistency check on my responsibility views before I post a version of this to the open thread. longtime reader of SSC/LW so assume I'm ok with determinism talk. roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a compatibilist. Determinism, if true, changes nothing about whether people deserve blame, praise, punishment, etc.

(2) Strawson's basically right that holding people responsible just *is* participating in the web of reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation — and there's no standpoint outside that practice from which the whole thing could be justified or condemned. The attitudes come first; the theory trails behind.

(3) I think he's also right that the "objective attitude" is the correct response to some people — someone in a severe psychotic episode, say. You don't resent them, you manage them, the way you'd manage a storm or a toddler. And that's the humane stance, not a cold one.

(4) But honestly I think the logic of (3) applies way more widely than we like to admit. If someone's cruelty is well-explained by an abusive childhood plus unlucky neurology — and at some level of detail it's always well-explained by *something* — then resenting them seems about as fitting as resenting the psychotic guy, just less obvious. "There but for fortune" does a lot of work for me here.

(5) Like Strawson, I don't think determinism itself bears on any of this, because excusing is always local — specific excusing conditions, case by case — never something that follows globally from everything having a cause.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and how hard? Are there pairs that only *look* tense but dissolve when you spell them out? Rank whatever tensions you find by severity, and for each one tell me what would have to give. Then: if I can only afford to revise one commitment, which single revision buys me the most consistency?
