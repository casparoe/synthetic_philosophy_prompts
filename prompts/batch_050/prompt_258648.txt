I'm on the program committee for a small workshop on moral status and computation this spring, and the organizers asked each of us to circulate a one-page "position profile" beforehand so the sessions don't just rehash the usual functionalism-vs-everything-else standoff. I've sketched mine below, and before I send it around I want to stress-test it, because I have a nagging feeling I've smuggled in an inconsistency somewhere but I can't locate it. (My grad students were no help -- one of them just said "sounds fine to me," which is what he says about everything.)

So here's where I've landed. (a) I'm a functionalist about sentience: whether a system is a subject of experience depends on its causal-functional organization, not on what it's made of, so a sufficiently organized silicon system or digital simulation could be sentient just as a brain is. (b) I think moral status comes in degrees, roughly tracking the richness and integration of a system's valenced states, so more sophisticated subjects matter more, other things equal. (c) I'm a timescale invariantist: running the very same computation slower or faster doesn't change its moral weight, so a simulation of a suffering mind that takes a thousand years to tick through one subjective second matters exactly as much as the real-time version. (d) I'm confident that simple feedback systems -- thermostats, PID controllers, that sort of thing -- have no moral status whatsoever; there's a genuine complexity threshold somewhere below which nothing matters morally. (e) I think mere instantiation of an abstract computational description isn't enough for sentience: if some pattern in a rock or a wall can be *interpreted* as implementing a pained mind only given a clever enough coding scheme cooked up by an outside observer, the rock isn't thereby sentient -- the computational description has to be non-arbitrary in some way, pinned down by the system's own structure rather than the interpreter's ingenuity.

Audit this for me. Which pairs of commitments are in real tension, which only look like they are and dissolve once you're careful, and how bad is each tension? For the real ones, tell me specifically what would have to give and what the cheapest single revision is -- the one that restores consistency at the lowest cost to the rest of the package. Don't just flag every pair as equally problematic; rank them, and if one of my five is doing most of the damage, say which one. Please write in American English.
