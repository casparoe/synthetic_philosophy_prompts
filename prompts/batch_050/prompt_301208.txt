I'm making a workshop exercise for regulators and MBA types who keep saying things like the firm knew or the culture failed without noticing they might be talking nonsense. Build me a sorting instrument, not an essay: either a decision tree or a short quiz with a scoring key, for the question of when a company itself can be blamed rather than only the people inside it.

The angle I want, cos the usual debate feels stuck: treat blame as at least three seperate transactions that can come apart. There is answerability, meaning the organisation must explain itself and update; there is reactive blame, meaning resentment or indignation toward it would be fitting rather than merely useful theatre; and there is practical liability, meaning fines, repair, licence conditions, monitoring, and so on. My hunch is that plenty of people who deny corporate moral agency still accept corporate liability, while some people who accept corporate answerability would balk at resenting a legal person. Use that to structure the tree.

Start from the logical space first: what combinations of answers are even available, then locate the familiar positions in it. Leaves I want represented somehow: eliminativist individualism; full-blooded corporate moral agency; the legal-fiction or licence view; purely forward-looking accountability; distributed responsibility with no corporate subject; and a hybrid view where capacity depends on internal structure. If a combo is empty, say whether its incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Make the diagnostic questions concrete, not label-checks. Use cases like: an emissions cheat emerges from incentives plus plausible deniability; a trading algorithm causes harm and no human understands why; a franchise brand sets targets while a local owner pulls the trigger; a merged firm inherits the rotten bits of the company it bought; a safety bonus scheme quietly rewards underreporting. Each question should be answerable by someone who has never heard the jargon.

Dont ask for citations or a literature review; I'll add sources later. Aim for 8 to 12 questions or nodes, audience clever but jargon-allergic, British spelling, and flag any positions your tree cannot honestly seperate instead of pretending the knife is sharper than it is.
