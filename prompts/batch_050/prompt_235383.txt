The exam question was: "Suppose we build an advanced agent that maximizes expected utility, and we want to ensure it will let us shut it down. A natural suggestion is to simply add a term to its utility function that rewards it for being shut down, or to make its utility conditional on our pressing an off-switch. Explain why this kind of direct fix fails or is unstable, and then explain the deeper structural difficulty that any corrigibility proposal has to confront -- namely, why it is hard to make an agent that neither resists shutdown nor actively seeks it, and what role (if any) uncertainty about its own objectives might play in a solution."

The student's answer: "The problem with simply rewarding an agent for being shut down is that the agent is an expected utility maximizer, so it will compare the utility of shutting down against the utility of continuing to operate. If the shutdown bonus is too small relative to the expected utility of pursuing its goals, the agent will take steps to prevent shutdown, such as disabling the off-switch or copying itself; if the bonus is too large, the agent will instead try to get itself shut down as quickly as possible, perhaps even by coercing a human into pressing the button. Only a knife-edge calibration of the bonus produces indifference, and that calibration is fragile under any change in the agent's estimates. The deeper difficulty is that a fully rational agent with a fixed, known utility function has instrumental reasons to preserve its own operation and goal content, so shutdown avoidance falls out of almost any objective. One proposed resolution is to construct utility functions that are indifferent between outcomes in which the button is pressed and outcomes in which it is not, and this utility indifference approach fully solves the shutdown problem, because once the agent assigns equal value to both outcomes it has no further incentives regarding the switch. An alternative line of work, the off-switch game of Hadfield-Menell and colleagues, demonstrates that a rational agent will always defer to being switched off provided it can observe whether the human chooses to press the button, so uncertainty about objectives is unnecessary once this interactive structure is in place. In sum, shutdown incentives are an artifact of poorly specified reward terms, and either indifference constructions or interactive games dissolve the problem entirely."
