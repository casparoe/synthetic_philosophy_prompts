I'm writing a novel set in 1874, and my main character is a country parson who's just finished the Descent of Man and is quietly losing his faith over it. I want his crisis to track the actual book, not some hollywood version of it. So: take the main moves Darwin makes about morality in there -- the sympathy-plus-habit account of how the moral sense grows out of social instincts, the claim that tribes whose members are more loyal and self-sacrificing would outcompete other tribes, and conscience as the social instincts winning out over passing appetites after the fact -- and rank them by how hard they'd hit a thoughtful Victorian reader who knows his theology, with a sentence or two each on why it lands where it does and where the weak joints are. Then a second ranking of the same moves by how well they hold up now, and a line on what's changed between the two lists. My parson has to be most shaken by the argument that lands hardest, so this ranking literally decides my plot -- don't cop out with a tie at the top.
