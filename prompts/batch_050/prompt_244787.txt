So I volunteer with our local history society digitizing old parish registers, and because I'm the least-bad with computers I ended up building a little SQLite database to hold it all. No academic background in anything, I just like puzzles. The registers are full of references like "the miller's widow" or "the son of John Carter" or "the curate of St. Bride's" and half the time I can't figure out which actual person in my people table they mean, or whether there even is one. Somebody at the society said this is basically a philosophy problem and pointed me at Bertrand Russell's essay "On Denoting" from 1905, which I found online and have been slowly working through.

Most of it I'm following ok, and there's a bit that made me laugh because it's literally my database problem. He says that "the, when it is strictly used, involves uniqueness" and then admits that "we do, it is true, speak of 'the son of So-and-so' even when So-and-so has several sons, but it would be more correct to say 'a son of So-and-so'."

Here's what I want. Translate his whole theory of descriptions into the vocabulary I actually think in: tables, foreign keys, NULL, queries that return zero rows versus queries that error, unique constraints, WHERE EXISTS, joins that silently drop rows. The three cases he lists at the start (a phrase that denotes nothing, one that denotes one thing, one that denotes ambiguously), the trick where "I met a man" becomes a claim about some x, the uniqueness condition on "the" — put all of it in database terms as honestly as you can.

Then give me the residue report. What translates cleanly, what did you have to fudge, and is there anything in his theory that just has no counterpart in how a database handles references? Like, he keeps insisting these phrases have no meaning on their own, only inside whole propositions — is that even a thing a database could say? And specifically on the passage above: is his fix basically just "add a unique constraint and fail loudly," or is something getting lost when I think of it that way? I want to know if my instinct that this is all just foreign keys and NULL handling is right or if I'm missing the actual point.
