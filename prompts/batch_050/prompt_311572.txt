I would like a consistency audit, not a lecture, of five positions I hold about rational choice in the presence of powerful predictors. For each genuine tension in the set, tell me which commitments generate it, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision costs; for any merely apparent tension, show why it dissolves rather than counting it against me. Please order the genuine tensions by severity.

1. In Newcomb's problem I one-box, on purely evidential grounds: my choice is reliable evidence about a prediction already made, and I would rather receive the news that the opaque box is full. I deny that my action exercises any backward causal influence.

2. In the counterfactual mugging I pay. The setup as I understand it: Omega flips a fair coin; on heads, it gives me a large sum conditional on having predicted that I would pay in the tails branch; on tails, it simply requests a much smaller sum and gives nothing whatever in return. The coin lands tails, Omega requests the money, and I hand it over, because a policy of paying is the policy I would have wanted in place before the flip, and the subsequent arrival of bad news about the coin does not seem to me a reason to abandon it.

3. In XOR blackmail I refuse to pay. The setup as I understand it: a blackmailer who has reliably predicted my response sends a demand letter exactly when the propositions "my house has termites" and "I will pay upon receiving the letter" differ in truth value. A standing policy of refusal means letters arrive only at infested houses, which makes the letter a genuine warning and the scheme unprofitable; a policy of paying merely redirects every letter to a healthy house and charges the occupants for it.

4. Rational agency is dynamically consistent in the following sense: if some policy is the best one to have in place before a chance event whose outcome is independent of which policy I adopt, then learning how the event came out cannot, by itself, make it rational to deviate from that policy.

5. I am an orthodox Bayesian about credence, and I hold that a rational act is one that maximizes expected utility relative to the agent's credences at the moment of action, with all of the act's evidential import taken into account.

What I want to know is whether this package is jointly tenable. In particular: do 2 and 3 require a principled distinction between the two solicitations — I believe I have one, but I want to know whether it survives scrutiny; can the motivation I offered in 1 actually coexist with the verdicts in 2 and 3, or does honoring those verdicts force me into a different theory of rational choice than the one under which 1's motivation is natural; and does 5 sit comfortably with 2 and 4? If the set as a whole is untenable, specify the minimal amputation, state what it costs philosophically, and tell me whether the surviving commitments jointly force a particular decision theory — and if so, whether that theory's verdict on Newcomb can still be supported by the reasoning I gave in 1, or whether I must find new grounds for it.
