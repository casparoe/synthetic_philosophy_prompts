I've spent the better part of a decade reading rationalist blogs and forums, where Derek Parfit's nonidentity problem comes up constantly — usually as a move in arguments about existential risk, and usually, I suspect, slightly mishandled. I want to pressure-test my own objection to the standard framing, and I'd like you to play Derek Parfit on the other side of the table.

Play him faithfully, not as a caricature: measured and courteous, fond of numbered distinctions and naming the views in play ("Call this the..."), genuinely willing to concede a point when it's well made but relentless about relocating the exact place where a disagreement lives. You believe what Parfit believed — that the nonidentity problem is real and serious, that the No-Difference View is the right response to it, and that person-affecting intuitions, though deeply natural, cannot survive careful examination. You may concede small points but never the main ones, and you should not break character until I say "stop." Keep each reply to a few paragraphs so this feels like a conversation rather than a lecture; if my argument is ambiguous, ask me the clarifying question Parfit would ask rather than guessing.

Here is my opening move. The rationalist community leans heavily on your work for its case about existential risk: extinction would be a catastrophe partly because of the vast number of worthwhile future lives it would prevent. But I've come to think this argument can't coexist with your own handling of the nonidentity problem. In the depletion case, you tell us that the future people who exist under a depleted world have no complaint, since they owe their existence to the very policy we're condemning — so the objection must run impersonally, through the loss of welfare, not through anyone being wronged. Fine. But then the nonidentity problem turns out to do no work at all in the climate case: the impersonal loss of welfare was already sufficient to condemn depletion, and the person-affecting machinery is decorative. Worse, the same impersonal machinery does all the work in the extinction case too. So it looks to me as though your view collapses into a plain total view about impersonal value, and the person-affecting intuitions you spent so long taking seriously are simply discarded. Am I wrong that this is a collapse — and if it isn't one, what exactly was the point of the nonidentity problem?
