Is there a name for this? I keep running into cases where somebody believes something is going to happen, their reasons for believing it are genuinely bad or at least pretty shaky, but the belief ends up true *because they held it* — not by coincidence. Some examples:

1. A fund manager puts out a confident call that some mid-size company is undervalued. Her analysis is sloppy — miscounted inventory, wrong comparisons, teh works. But her fund is big enough that buying on her own prediction pushes the price up, other investors pile in behind her, and six months later the stock really is worth what she said it was.

2. A teacher gets it into his head that one particular kid is a late bloomer at math, for no good reason — the kid's scores are honestly mediocre. Because he believes it, he gives the kid harder problems and way more attention, and by the end of the year the kid actually is good at math.

3. Everyone at a startup believes the startup is about to fold, so the best people quietly leave, and then it folds. Darker, but same shape: the belief makes itself true.

Now, I know about those cases where a belief is true and well-supported but only by some fluke — like glancing at a broken watch that happens to show the right time, so you're right but you don't really *know*. This seems different to me: there's no lucky break anywhere, the truth isn't accidental, it's downstream of the belief itself. But the cases don't feel equal either. In case 1 I'm reluctant to say she knew. In case 2 I'm tempted to say the teacher ended up knowing, or at least deserves more credit than the fund manager. Maybe the difference is whether the route from belief to truth is one the believer could in principle endorse? Or whether the believer's own actions are the mechanism?

So: is there an established concept for exactly this — a belief that counts as justified-ish and is true because it's held — and does epistemology have a verdict on whether it's knowledge? I've heard people say self-fulfilling prophecy, but that seems to name the social phenomenon, not the question about epistemic status. If there's no settled term, coin one and give me the edges: what if the belief only *partly* causes its own truth (the kid also had hidden talent all along)? What if the believer knows full well the belief is self-fulfilling — does seeing the loop from the inside change anything? What if they'd have believed it no matter what the evidence said?

And whatever verdict you land on, tell me what would change your mind — what argument or case would flip your answer, and which step of your own reasoning you trust least. I'd rather know where it's wobbly than get a confident-sounding answer that falls apart when poked.
