I am a high school student with no formal training in philosophy, and I have been trying to find out whether a pair of experiences I keep having has an established name, and if so, what exactly the named thing is and is not.

The first experience: our refrigerator hums constantly. I never hear it, except that the moment it clicks off, I am struck by a retroactive certainty that it had been droning the whole time and that I had, in some sense, been hearing it. Yet if anyone had asked me one second before it stopped whether I was hearing anything, I would honestly have said no.

The second experience: last month I was a passenger while my older brother drove a familiar highway route, and at our exit he realized he had no memory whatsoever of the previous forty minutes — none of the lanes, the lights, or the other cars. Yet he had clearly changed lanes, slowed for traffic, and stopped at red lights. He insists he was not "seeing" any of it, but something in him was plainly registering red lights, because he braked for them.

My question is whether there is a name for the state of registering or experiencing something without being aware that you do. I know the term "subliminal perception," but as I understand it that refers to stimuli too faint or too brief to be detected at all, which does not fit my cases: the hum was perfectly audible and the road perfectly visible. What I am after is the difference, if there is one, between information being fully present to your senses and that information being present to you. If the field has established terms for the two sides of that line, I would like to know them — but I would also like the carving, not just the vocabulary. In particular, are my two cases one phenomenon or two? My brother's behavior was guided by what he does not remember; mine was not, until the hum stopped. Should that difference matter to how the cases are classified?

One further wrinkle. In a documentary I watched, a scientist compared consciousness to a theater: everything the senses take in waits backstage, and only what enters the spotlight and gets broadcast to the whole mind at once counts as conscious. I believe the theory was called something like the global workspace. If that picture is right, it seems to imply that I simply was not hearing the hum, and my brother was not seeing the road, and that my conviction that the hum "was there all along" is an illusion my memory manufactured at the instant it stopped. Is that actually what the theory says? And is there a rival view that takes the retroactive conviction seriously, holding that the experience was present even though it never reached the spotlight? Finally, how could anyone ever settle which side is correct, given that the only evidence either way seems to be a report made after the fact? I would rather understand where the edges of the phenomenon lie than receive a bare name for it.
