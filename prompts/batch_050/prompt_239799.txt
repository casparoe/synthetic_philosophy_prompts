So last night my roommate and I got into it again about functionalism -- he's convinced multiple realizability is basically a dead argument, like it was a big deal in the 70s and everyone's just been patching it up since. I disagreed, loudly, wine was involved, and now, somewhat to my own surprise, I've committed to presenting at our department's Friday workshop defending the position that multiple realizability is still a live and genuinely good argument against type-identity theories of mind, once you state it carefully.

My actual thesis, roughly: the multiple realizability argument doesn't need to show that minds could be made of silicon or ectoplasm or whatever -- it only needs to show that psychological states are individuated at a level of description (functional/organizational) where the underlying physical stuff doesn't explain the psychological facts, so type-identity explanations miss the pattern even if they're technically true in every token case. So I'm defending an explanatory version, not the strong metaphysical "a Martian could feel pain" version.

The audience: about 15 grad students and faculty. The people who will actually ask questions are (a) two die-hard enactivist/embodied-cognition people who think talk of substrate-neutral computation is exactly the disease, (b) one computational neuroscientist who thinks multiple realizability is empirically oversold -- brains turn out to be way more convergent than the argument assumed -- and (c) two very analytic philosophers of science types who will grant the premise but think the whole thing is philosophically thin once you press on it.

Prep me for Q&A. I want like 7-8 objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of those people would actually phrase it (the enactivists talk in terms of sense-making and structural coupling, the neuro person talks about connectomes and convergence, the analytic people will say things like "levels of description" and "explanatory pluralism" dismissively). For each: what I should concede up front, what I should hold, a two-minute reply, and where that reply is soft.

Two things I specifically want help with. First, my roommate's best hit last night: he said my "explanatory" retreat concedes everything interesting, because if multiple realizability is only a point about explanation and not about metaphysics, the type-identity theorist can just say sure, psychology is a special science, of course it's not reducible in practice -- that was never the claim. If the embodied people or the analytic people land this, I think I'm in trouble, so I need my best answer and I need to know honestly how good it is. Second, the question I'm praying nobody asks, which is about the grain problem: for ANY two systems you can find a level of functional description at which they're equivalent, so what makes the psychological level non-arbitrary rather than just the description I happen to like?
