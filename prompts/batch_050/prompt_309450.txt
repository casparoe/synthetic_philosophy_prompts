So I'm a novelist -- literary fiction mostly, plus a thriller series I write under a pen name -- and I've been on a Kierkegaard kick lately, specifically his stuff about the press and "the public," and it's started messing with how I think about my own career. I tried writing down my actual positions to see if they cohere and I suspect they don't. Here's where I've landed:

1. Kierkegaard was basically right: "the public" is a phantom. It's an abstraction the press conjures up so that everyone can appeal to it and nobody has to answer to it. Nobody ever meets the public. You only ever meet actual people.

2. I want to be a bestseller. Like, genuinely -- I care about reach, I check my sales figures, and I don't think this ambition is corrupt or embarrassing. Reaching tons of readers is the point of writing novels.

3. Anonymous online reviews are not real criticism. Goodreads, Amazon, all of it -- nobody stands behind a star rating from "bookdragon88," there's no accountability, so it's noise and serious writers should ignore it.

4. I have absolutely changed my writing based on aggregate reader response. Readers hated the ambiguous ending of my last book, the ratings showed it, and the next one wraps things up more cleanly partly because of that. I'd do it again.

5. As I understand it, my position in (2) doesn't actually clash with Kierkegaard, because a novel isn't the press: it reaches people one at a time, each reader alone with the book, so a mass readership is just a lot of individual encounters, not an appeal to "the public" in his bad sense.

6. Pseudonymity is fine even though authenticity and answerability in communication matter to me. I publish thrillers under a fake name and literary stuff under my real one, and I don't think that's hiding -- if anything the guy himself wrote half his work under made-up names, so there's precedent.

Where does this actually break? Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. Also tell me straight up whether my reading of Kierkegaard in (5) actually holds water or whether I'm just telling myself a story that lets me keep (2), (3) and (4) at the same time -- because I honestly cant tell if (5) is a real defense or a rationalization.
