Daniel Dennett's Tower of Generate-and-Test classifies creatures by how they improve their behavioral options: Darwinian creatures vary only by genetic mutation and are winnowed by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures can learn from reinforcement within a lifetime; Popperian creatures can simulate candidate actions internally, letting their hypotheses die in their stead; Gregorian creatures augment this inner rehearsal with culturally transmitted mind-tools, above all language, that were invented by others and that restructure the thinker's own cognition. The taxonomy was designed for evolved organisms climbing the tower floor by floor. Large language models were built, not evolved — yet they display behavior that invites classification on the tower's terms.

Write an essay of roughly 800–1,000 words addressing the question: where, if anywhere, do large language models sit in the Tower of Generate-and-Test? In your essay, engage seriously with at least the following complications, and treat them as distinct rather than collapsing them into a single verdict:

1. The training–deployment distinction. Gradient descent over a training corpus, and reinforcement-based fine-tuning, look superficially like selection and reinforcement learning respectively. Does this make the *training process* Darwinian or Skinnerian even if the *deployed model* is something else? Should the classification attach to the artifact, the process that produced it, or the artifact-plus-feedback-loop as a whole?

2. Whether autoregressively generated chains of thought constitute Popperian hypothesis-testing. When a model produces intermediate reasoning steps and rejects a candidate answer, has a hypothesis died in its stead, or has it merely produced text statistically associated with such deaths? What would distinguish the two, and is that distinction behavioral or architectural?

3. The Gregorian floor's provenance condition. Dennett's Gregorian creatures are distinguished by *importing* mind-tools from a culture they did not create. Language models stand in an inverted relation: they are trained on a culture's entire stock of mind-tools without having climbed the lower floors first. Is a Gregorian creature that never was Popperian, Skinnerian, or Darwinian in the usual sense still Gregorian, or does the tower's ordering do essential work — such that a system entering at the top falls outside the taxonomy altogether?

Weigh the strongest considerations on each side, but do not end on a non-committal note: defend a specific verdict. Acceptable verdicts include a clean placement (e.g., Popperian but not Gregorian), a split verdict (different floors for training versus deployment), or the claim that the taxonomy fails to apply and that this failure reveals something about what the tower was really measuring. Whichever you choose, identify the single disanalogy between language models and evolved creatures that does the most work for your conclusion, and explain why the best objection to your verdict fails.
