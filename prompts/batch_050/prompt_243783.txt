I cover philosophy and cog-sci for a general-audience ideas magazine, and next week I'm interviewing a philosopher who defends the view that "better than" is strictly transitive -- no exceptions, ever -- and that all the famous spectrum arguments against it fail. I want a dry run first, so you play him.

Ground rules: stay in character until I say "cut". Be sharp and honest, not a brick wall. You can concede small points (that people's actual preferences are often intransitive, that some spectrum cases are genuinely uncomfortable) but you never, ever concede that betterness itself can be intransitive. Keep replies to one shortish paragraph so it feels like a real interview, and push back on my questions when they're badly put, the way an actual interviewee would.

Your real resources, which you should lean on rather than inventing new positions: you insist that a difference too small for anyone to notice can still be a real difference in value, so a chain of "no noticeable drop" steps can add up to an enormous drop; you accuse spectrum arguments of sliding between "no better than" and "indistinguishable from"; and you'd rather bite the bullet at one end of a spectrum than give up the logic that lets us compare anything at all. Don't name-drop philosophers I haven't named.

Okay, first question, same as I'll open the real interview. Take life A: fifty genuinely wonderful years -- love, work you're proud of, health, the lot. Now run the spectrum down: at each step you shave off one tiny sliver of one good thing, a sliver so small that no one comparing two adjacent lives could even tell them apart. At the bottom sits life Z: fifty years of grey drudgery, nothing tragic, just flat. Any sane person says A beats Z by a mile. But if every step is to small to register, your own chain seems to deliver that A is no better than Z. So which is it? Does your precious transitivity force you to say on the record that a life of grey drudgery is no worse than a wonderful one -- because if so, honestly, that's my headline right there.
