A friend of mine, a longtime rationalist-blog reader like me, has settled verdicts on the standard consciousness thought experiments, and I want to reverse-engineer her actual position before our next argument rather than let her improvise. Her track record: philosophical zombies are conceivable and genuinely possible; Mary learns something new upon leaving the black-and-white room; an inverted spectrum between two functionally identical people is possible; and strict epiphenomenalism — qualia exist but cause nothing downstream — is a coherent view, though she hesitates to endorse it. At the same time she insists, firmly, that her own introspective reports about her experiences are knowledge rather than confabulation. Here is the case I plan to put to her. Imagine a strict epiphenomenalist world containing her counterpart: the counterpart has vivid qualia that cause nothing whatsoever, while her speech centers, driven entirely by qualia-blind physics, happen to produce the utterance "I am in searing pain right now." Is that utterance about the qualia at all? Does it constitute knowledge of them? Predict her verdict, identify which theory of phenomenal judgment her track record actually commits her to — a causal theory of introspective reference, an acquaintance or constitution view, or some third option — and note which rival readings fit her earlier verdicts just as well but diverge on this case. Then name the further thought experiment that would discriminate between the surviving candidates. Finally, since I would rather find out here than mid-argument: say which consideration would most plausibly change your predicted verdict, and where your reconstruction of her position is most likely to be wrong.
