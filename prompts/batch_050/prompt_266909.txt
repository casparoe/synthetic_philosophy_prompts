After forty years in experimental physics, I have lately been reading philosophy of mathematics, and I should like to have my working position on infinity examined for internal consistency before I embarrass myself at the seminar I have joined. My commitments are as follows. First, Cantor's diagonal argument strikes me as a genuine proof, yielding real knowledge that some infinities are strictly larger than others. Second, Hilbert's hotel convinces me that an actual infinity is logically coherent: the paradoxes are surprising, not contradictory, and so the concept cannot be dismissed on purely logical grounds. Third, I maintain that the physical world contains no actual infinities whatsoever; where our theories produce them (singularities, divergent integrals), this signals idealization or breakdown, never nature herself. Fourth, I regard it as a live epistemic possibility that the cosmos is spatially infinite, with infinitely many galaxies beyond any horizon. Fifth, I hold that the real analysis I used daily in my career is a convenient fiction: talk of continua and limits is shorthand for finite approximations and commits us to nothing. Please audit this set rather than lecture me on the subject: identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear so, order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each state the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision would cost me. I have no prior view about which commitment should go, and I would rather be told plainly where the trouble lies. Confine your reply to five hundred words.
