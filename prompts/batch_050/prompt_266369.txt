I run a weekly ethics seminar for about twenty first-year university students, ninety minutes per session. Last month my elderly father was nearly knocked down by a food-delivery rider running a red light, and I have not stopped thinking about how much of ordinary life consists of imposing small risks on strangers who never consented to bear them. I want an activity for next week's session that makes students feel the force of this before anyone names it. The game should put every participant in both positions at different moments: imposing a risk on someone else in exchange for a benefit, and discovering that a risk has been imposed on them. It needs real (small) stakes, and the rules should be arranged so that the students generate the central puzzles themselves — whether post-hoc compensation can substitute for prior consent, why imposed risks feel different from risks we choose, and how to think about risks so small that no one is ever actually harmed.
