Before I read any more proposals for aligning AI systems through task decomposition — iterated amplification, distillation, recursive oversight, debate — I want the specification first, not another scheme. Write the desiderata that any such scheme must meet to count as solving the problem it advertises itself as solving: namely, producing a system that is both more capable than its unaided human overseer and still aligned, by having weaker-but-trusted agents check and combine the work of stronger ones.

For each desideratum, give the failure mode or argument that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one research camp. Here are the issues I expect the list to engage with, though you should reorganize, add, or cut as you see fit: the amplification step must not lose alignment-relevant information when an overseer aggregates sub-answers it cannot independently verify; distillation must not produce a model whose reasoning exceeds what the amplified overseer can in principle check (the worry that each round of training quietly widens the gap between what the system does and what anyone can supervise); the scheme must remain competitive with unaligned approaches rather than achieving safety only by staying weak; it must tolerate human overseers whose judgments are inconsistent, myopic, or partially corrupted; and it must say something about deception arising inside components rather than only at the top level.

I am especially interested in one structural question: whether a task's decomposition and its values' decomposition come apart. A task can factor into subtasks such that getting every part right guarantees the whole is right; it is much less clear that the value of an outcome factors the same way, since a plan can look fine piecewise while being bad as a whole. If your desiderata imply that decomposition preserves competence but not alignment wherever this asymmetry bites, say so explicitly and say how much of the problem survives.

Take definite positions: state which single desideratum you think eliminates the most existing proposals and why, whether you judge the full set jointly satisfiable, and — if not — which requirement you would relax and what that costs. A table for the requirements is fine; the scoring of particular named schemes is optional and secondary.
