So I'm a novelist, and my current book has dragged me into the personal identity literature in a way I did not plan for, and I want a diagnosis that isn't really about my book. Quick version of the plot: my main character is a retired general on trial for something genuinely awful he ordered as a young lieutenant, and the twist is that a head injury in the intervening years means he verifiably cannot remember any of it — I basically stole Reid's brave officer case and stretched it into a whole third act. Anyway, reading around for this I found Locke's memory criterion and then roughly three centuries of people kicking it around, and what strikes me is that nobody ever seems to concede anything, ever. The memory theorists patch the criterion, the objectors cook up a weirder case (fission, teletransport, memory implants), the patches get re-patched, round and round.

My question: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? My hunch, as someone who thinks about narrative structure for a living, is that "same person" is being asked to do at least three different jobs at once — a metaphysical fact about what persists through time, a legal and moral question about who can be punished or praised, and a storytelling question about what makes a life hang together as one arc rather than a sequence of episodes — and the camps are just optimizing for different jobs while pretending there's a single question on the table. Is that right? Or is there a real factual/metaphysical dispute hiding under there that could in principle be settled, and the cases are genuine evidence for one side? Diagnose it using what the sides actually argue, not a survey of positions, and tell me what progress would even look like given your diagnosis — a better thought experiment nobody's tried, a stipulation everyone could live with, splitting the concept into seperate questions, or is the impasse itself the finding, i.e. the concept is just broken? And sure, tell me whether you think my general is guilty, but honestly I mostly want the diagnosis.
