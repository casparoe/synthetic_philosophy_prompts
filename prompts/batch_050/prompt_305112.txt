I'm in a phil of mind seminar and we just did the paradox of phenomenal judgment — roughly: if qualia are causally inert, then my belief that I'm seeing red isn't *caused* by the redness, so in what sense do I know anything about my own experience at all? For my final paper I want to write up a spec: the desiderata any theory of phenomenal judgment has to meet to count as solving this. So far I've got things like: it has to explain why introspective judgments are usually right; it has to allow for introspective error (change blindness, confabulation cases); it can't make the reliability of our judgments about experience a sheer coincidence; and it has to be consistent with the causal closure of the physical. Help me turn this into a proper list — the motivation behind each requirement, and for each one whether it's a genuine constraint on anyone or just a physicalist prejudice a dualist or panpsychist could reasonably drop. Most importantly: if some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable (I have a hunch closure + realism about qualia + non-coincidental knowledge of them might be), say so explicitly and show why — that's the part I care most about, since it would mean the list itself is the result. No need to score Chalmers, illusionists, Russellian monists etc. against the list unless you can do each in a sentence or two.
