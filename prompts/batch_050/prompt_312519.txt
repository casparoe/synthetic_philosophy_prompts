Research programs in quantum gravity -- string-theoretic holography, loop quantization, causal sets, tensor-network and entanglement-based approaches, asymptotic safety -- routinely advertise that spacetime "emerges" from non-spatiotemporal fundamentals. But the word emergence is doing unexamined work: sometimes it means that a classical limit exists, sometimes that a coarse-graining map exists, sometimes merely that a mathematical dictionary between two theories exists. I am writing a grant-panel report and want the panel to evaluate competing programs against a single explicit standard rather than against each program's own rhetoric.

Write the specification a derivation must satisfy before it earns the claim that spacetime has been shown to emerge, as opposed to the weaker claim that some theory reproduces geometric data under relabeling. Give the requirements list, and for each requirement state the physical or conceptual consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a prejudice of one research community. Among the candidates you should adjudicate:

- Recovery of smooth Lorentzian geometry in some limit (and why, if at all, a Euclidean-signature result falls short).
- Recovery of gravitational dynamics -- Einstein's equation or a justified modification -- as opposed to recovery of the metric alone.
- Locality: how local interactions arise from fundamentals that may be radically nonlocal, and whether approximate locality at low energies suffices.
- Dimensionality: whether deriving 3+1 dimensions is required, or whether taking them as input is acceptable.
- The problem of time: if the fundamental equation is timeless, where does physical time come from, and is an internal/relational clock an answer or a restatement of the problem?
- Black hole thermodynamics: must an emergence story also account for the microstate counting behind horizon entropy, or is that a separate success?
- Background-independent observables: can the theory say what is measurable without smuggling in the very spacetime it claims to derive?
- Empirical distinctness: must an emergent-spacetime theory yield novel, testable deviations from general relativity, or is recovering general relativity exactly enough?
- The metaphysics of the claim itself: must the account specify what kind of emergence is being asserted (reduction, supervenience, something stronger), and what difference the answer makes?

Then do three further things. First, identify any requirements on the list that are in tension with one another, i.e., where satisfying one makes another harder, and say whether any subset looks jointly unsatisfiable. Second, state which single requirement, if enforced strictly, would eliminate the largest share of existing programs, and which requirements you regard as negotiable. Third, apply the list to a constructed case: result (A) is a precise dictionary mapping entanglement structure in a boundary theory to a bulk metric, but bulk locality holds only approximately and fails at scales that are in principle accessible; result (B) derives exact locality from first principles, but only in a model whose limiting spacetime is fixed and non-dynamical. Which result comes closer to demonstrating emergence under your specification, and what would each need to add to overtake the other?
