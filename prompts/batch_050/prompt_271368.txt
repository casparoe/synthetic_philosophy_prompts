Quick recap of the territory. Lucretius's symmetry argument: the eternity of nonexistence after death is mirrorred by the eternity of nonexistence before birth, and since nobody regards the second as a misfortune, dreading the first is irrational. The standard anti-Lucretian reply (Nagel's version of it, anyway) is that the symmetry is fake: you could have died later and gone on living, but you couldn't have been born earlier, because anyone born much earlier than you would simply have been a different person. So death deprives you of life you would otherwise have had, while late birth deprives no one of anything.

Construct a thought experiment that puts maximum pressure on that reply. Constraints: one realistic scenario, no time travel, no reincarnation, no stipulating what the reader's intuitions are, and it can't just be the frozen-embryo-born-decades-late case -- that's the obvious move, I want something less on the nose. The pressure has to come from the structure of the case.
