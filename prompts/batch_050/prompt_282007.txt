I sit on the allocation committee of a small disaster relief foundation, we hand out maybe 60-80 grants a year to fund local response after floods, fires, storms, that sort of thing. The founders left us with a weird founding principle: our mandate is reducing suffering, not creating wellbeing. We've informally run on something like what philosophers call negative utilitarianism with a threshold -- prevent or relieve severe suffering first, and once you're below the threshold of really bad stuff, small comforts and pleasures can also count, but no amount of small comfort outweighs preventing severe suffering.

The problem is this stays purely vibes-based in practice. Every meeting it dissolves into one member going "but isn't preventing the evacuation camp from having a football field for the kids still worth something" while another says "a football field is never on the same scale as someone in untreated pain" and we just vote on gut feeling.

Please write us a decision procedure we can actually run in committee (meetings are 90 minutes, we handle 5-8 proposals, none of us are philosophers): the questions to ask in order for each proposal, what counts as an adequate answer, how to classify a proposed benefit as above/below the severity threshold without a formal scale, what to do when a proposal mixes severe suffering relief with minor comfort (very common), and explicitly mark the points where the procedure has to punt to a value judgement by the committee.

The bit I most need nailed down: the commensurability question. Our threshold rule says e.g. no amount of giving displaced families ice cream nights outweighs leaving one person in severe untreated pain -- but at some point the small things DO seem to add up against mild-but-not-threshold harms. Where in the procedure does that aggregation get allowed and where is it forbidden, and how do I explain the difference to a member who thinks it's either "everything aggregates" or "nothing aggregates"?

Then run the procedure on these three proposals from our current round:
1. Funding a field pain clinic after an earthquake -- serves maybe 200 people with untreated injuries, high cost per person
2. Funding heating + hot meals + activities for a shelter housing 3000 displaced people, nobody in severe pain but everyone uncomfortable and miserable for months
3. A cheap proposal funding painkillers for one hospice patient AND a huge community festival for 50,000 people recovering from a flood

I expect the procedure to give at least one answer the committee won't like, tell me which one that'll probably be. Also tell me where this whole approach breaks down (I suspect comparing mild discomforts across huge numbers of people is a disaster area) so we know when to stop trusting it.
