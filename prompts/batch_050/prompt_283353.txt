Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been arguing about whether the world is getting less violent. On one side, a "declinist" in the Pinker mold: war deaths per capita are down across centuries, homicide rates in Europe have fallen by an order of magnitude since the middle ages, and post-1945 there have been no great-power wars, so violence is declining and this is a robust empirical trend. On the other side, a skeptic who thinks the whole debate is confused: the data on premodern war deaths is way too sparse to support those charts, a single nuclear war would retroactively make the "decline" look like a blip (so you can't infer a trend from a period where the tail risk hasn't yet bitten), and anyway the declinist is smuggling in a contested definition of violence that counts battlefield deaths but not, say, deaths from preventable poverty, colonial extraction, or climate harm caused knowingly.

The deliverable is the document they'd both sign after actually sitting down together. I'd like:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can genuinely endorse -- e.g. what's true about the homicide data for early modern Europe, what per-capita vs. absolute numbers do and don't show, what the sparse ancient data can and can't support. These have to be non-vacuous; no "violence is bad" filler.
2. A disagreements section where each remaining dispute is stated precisely, in wording each side would accept for its own position. In particular, get them to say exactly where they differ on: whether "less violent" is a statistical claim about a rate or a claim about the expected value of future deaths, and whether broadening the definition of violence beyond direct physical killing changes the trend or just changes the subject.
3. A list of cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would force at least one side to update. For each crux, record each side's prediction in advance where that makes sense (e.g. what the skeptic expects from a careful statistical treatment of heavy-tailed war deaths, what the declinist expects).
4. A verdict on which parts of the debate turned out to be verbal -- I suspect the definition-of-violence fight is partly about words and partly not, and I want the statement to say which part is which rather than just gesturing at it.

Format it as an actual joint statement, not a play-by-play of the argument. Keep the tone like two academics who are annoyed with each other but commited to the exercise.
