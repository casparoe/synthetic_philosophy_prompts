Okay so I'm a novelist, and I'm stuck on the philosophy under my current book, of all things. The plot: one of my characters, a French poet, dies and leaves behind a short formal poem, and my protagonist spends the back half of the novel trying to translate it into English. While researching I fell down the Hofstadter rabbit hole — Le Ton beau de Marot, where he gets dozens of people to translate that tiny Marot poem and obsesses over keeping the syllable count, the rhyme, everything — and it's wrecked my brain a little, because now I have strong Opinions about translation and the novel's ending basically depends on them being coherent.

So: put on your hat as a professional philosopher of language who also knows translation studies cold. Think carefully here — I'd actually like you to brainstorm the possible clashes among my commitments first, then do the audit. Here's where I've landed:

1. If you're ever forced to choose, sense beats form. A rendering that keeps the rhyme scheme but twists the meaning has failed as a translation, full stop.
2. A rendering of a formal poem that drops the form — the syllable count, the rhyme — isn't a translation at all. It's an adaptation, a "version." The form is part of what the poem IS.
3. There's no fully determinate fact of the matter about what any word means. Even two native speakers mean slightly different things by the same word; meaning is fuzzy and indeterminate all the way down.
4. For the poem in my novel there is a best English translation, and part of my protagonist's tragedy is that she can never quite be sure she's found it.
5. A great translator is a genuine co-author. The translated poem is a new work of art with two makers, and it's dishonest to pretend otherwise.
6. When my protagonist finally finishes, what the reader of her translation reads is the dead poet's poem — the same poem, not a new one. That's the whole emotional payoff of the book, and I will not give it up lightly.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do at first glance? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally fatal, and for each real one tell me what would have to give and what that would cost. I have a suspicion at least one apparent clash here dissolves if you state it carefully, so don't just flag everything — the point is I want to know what I can walk into the ending with, not a lecture on how translation is hard.
