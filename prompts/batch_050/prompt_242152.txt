I was reading about illusionism about phenomenal consciousness (the Frankish-style view that phenomenal consciousness is an illusion) and honestly I don't get how this position is even coherent. An illusion has to fool *someone* — that's just what an illusion is. So if I'm under the illusion that there's something it's like to be me, then there's something it's like to be under that illusion, and we're right back to phenomenal consciousness. You can't have a mere appearance without an appearance, if you see what I mean. So is the whole view just self-refuting on arrival, like eliminativists about belief telling you they believe there are no beliefs? Or is there a way for illusionists to get this off the ground without smuggling phenomenal properties back in through the "illusion" itself — and if so, what exactly is doing the work that a phenomenal seemings normally does?
