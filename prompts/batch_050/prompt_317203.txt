I'm putting together the final exam for an upper-level undergrad seminar that sits between history and political philosophy, on the question of who was to blame for WWI. The students have done a chunk of Fischer, a couple of the conservative replies (Ritter and friends), and Clark's sleepwalkers book, plus a bit of philosophy of collective responsibility on the side. The problem with how I've examined this before: students either regurgitate the July crisis timeline for two hours or write "it's complicated, everyone bears some responsibility" in fourteen different phrasings.

So write me one essay question, answerable in a two-hour exam, that can't be answered either way. The crux I want it to hit: the Fischer controversy presents as a fight about evidence (the blank cheque, the September programme, what Bethmann Hollweg actually wrote in his diary), but underneath it there's a conceptual dispute about what it even means to blame a nation. Is "Germany willed the war" a claim about a collective agent with intentions, a shorthand for the decisions of a dozen men in Berlin, or a verdict on an entire political culture? And Clark's even-handed sleepwalking metaphor, which feels like the mature position, arguably doesn't distribute responsibility so much as dissolve it. The question should force them to take a stand on what kind of thing national blame is, with the historiography as the test case rather than the answer.

Deliverables: (1) the question itself; (2) a model answer of about 600-700 words; (3) a marking scheme built on five must-hit points with marks for each, totalling 20, and for each point a one-liner on how students typically fumble it. In the scheme, make explicit what seperates a passable "both sides had their motives" answer from one that does the real conceptual work — e.g. an answer that distinguishes blaming a state for its decisions from blaming a nation for its culture, and notices that the Fischer debate looks different depending on which of those you take it to be about. No bibliographies or further-reading lists, just question, model answer, scheme.
