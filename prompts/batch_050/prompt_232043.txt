At a philosophy meetup last night someone ended a long argument about the hard problem by saying consciousness is an illusion — we think there's something it's like to be us, but there isn't. I'm sympathetic to deflationary views but this feels like it might be incoherent: doesn't an illusion have to seem some way to someone, and isn't that seeming already the thing we were arguing about? Can you lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan 'consciousness is an illusion' could be making — anything from 'introspection is unreliable' to 'we systematically misrepresent our own brain states as having properties they don't have' to stronger stuff — and for each reading say whether it's a platitude, a substantive but defensible thesis, or actually self-undermining? Most importantly: which of these readings would actually support using the slogan to dismiss the hard problem, and which ones just relabel the explanandum while leaving the mystery exactly where it was?
