So my uncle died earlier this year and it kicked off this whole thing where I've been lying awake thinking about whether death is actually bad, and I've gotten into some long arguments about it online and with my brother. I never studied philosophy or anything, I've just been reading stuff people link in reddit threads and watching lectures at 1.5x speed, but I think I've sort of settled on a position, or a bundle of positions anyway, and I want to know if they actually fit together before I go arguing with more people about it. Here's where I'm at:

1. Death is bad for the person who dies, when it's bad, because of what it takes away from them — all the good experiences and projects and whatever they would have had. Not because being dead is some horrible state to be in. (I saw this called deprivationism somewhere, that label feels right for what I think.)
2. I don't buy the mirror/symmetry thing — the idea that since I don't care about the eternity before I was born, I shouldn't care about the eternity after I die. The time before I existed doesn't bother me at all and the time after genuinely does, and I think that difference is fine and not just me being irrational.
3. I do think dead people can be harmed. Like if someone spent their whole life building a reputation and after they die someone destroys it with lies, or you promise a dying friend you'll look after their kid and then you just don't — that's a real wrong done to them, not just to their family or whoever's left. Something is genuinely worse for the dead person even though they never find out.
4. Related to 1: how bad a death is depends on how much good life it cuts short. So a child dying is a bigger tragedy than a 95 year old dying, all else equal. This seems obvious to me but I know it makes some people mad.
5. Even though I think death is bad, I think being *afraid* of it is irrational. Fear makes sense for bad experiences — pain, suffocating, whatever. But there's no experience of being dead, nothing it's like. So dreading death itself, as opposed to dreading dying painfully or what happens to your family, is confused.

Number these however you want, but go through and tell me which of these are actually in tension with each other and which only look like they are. Some of my buddies say 1 and 3 contradict each other but I honestly think they're on the same team — am I right about that? And for the real tensions, rank them by how bad they are and tell me what the cheapest fix would be in each case, like which single belief I'd have to drop or soften to make the set consistent, and what I'd lose by dropping it. I don't want a lecture on what famous dead guys thought about death, I want an audit of my five. Also fair warning I'm pretty attached to 1 and 4 so if one of those has to go you're going to have to really make the case.
