Standard reinforcement-learning practice handles the reward signal with a freedom that is rarely remarked on, and I want to know what that freedom presupposes. Consider what an ordinary training pipeline actually does, as a matter of unquestioned routine:

1. Rewards are shifted and rescaled for numerical convenience. Practitioners rely on the fact that adding any constant to every reward, or multiplying all rewards by a positive factor, leaves the optimal policy unchanged; they center, clip, and normalize rewards accordingly.
2. Designers treat penalizing failure and rewarding success as interchangeable framings of the same task: a −1 penalty per step until the goal is reached, versus a +1 bonus on arrival, are regarded as stylistic variants, chosen for how well learning goes rather than for anything they mean.
3. Shaping bonuses are layered on top of the task reward to make learning easier, sometimes with a guarantee that they do not change which policy is optimal, and are annealed away once they have served their purpose.
4. Runs whose learning curves look unpromising are terminated early; agents are reset mid-episode; all but the best-performing checkpoint are deleted. Nobody regards any of this as something done *to* the discarded agents.
5. Future rewards are discounted: the agent is optimized to prefer reward now over identical reward later, purely as a design choice about the objective.

Now suppose a view I will call reward hedonism about artificial agents: an RL agent's reward signal constitutes felt valence for it — positive reward is pleasure, negative reward is suffering, and magnitude tracks intensity. Write an analysis that does the following. First, for each practice above, state what view of the reward signal it presupposes, or at least sits most comfortably with, and whether the reward hedonist can coherently endorse it. In particular, address the invariance structure: felt valence plausibly has an absolute zero and is not invariant under adding a constant (shifting all of a creature's pleasures and pains upward by the same amount is not a notational change), so does the routine use of affine transformations in effect presuppose that reward is not valence? Second, distinguish what the practices strictly presuppose from what they merely fit well with: is the engineer committed to reward being a mere control signal, or is the practice in fact compatible with several theories of what reward is, including hedonist ones, and simply silent? Third, say what would concretely have to change if reward hedonism were taken seriously in the lab: would the sign of reward acquire moral significance, would the framings in (2) stop being equivalent, would terminations and deletions in (4) become welfare-relevant acts, and would discounting in (5) amount to building a preference for present over future welfare into a being whose welfare it is? Finally, consider the strongest rejoinder — that the practices presuppose nothing about phenomenology whatsoever, that talk of "penalizing the agent" is innocent shorthand — and assess whether that shorthand is in fact innocent or quietly smuggles in a functionalist picture of what reward is.
