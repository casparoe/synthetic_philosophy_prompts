For the last meeting of my seminar on rules and inference, write a 19-line villanelle about Lewis Carroll’s Tortoise trying to explain to Achilles why merely writing down modus ponens as another premise doesn’t get you an actual inference. The first refrain should keep gaining one extra conditional clause every time it returns, so the poem’s form visibly enacts the regress, while the second refrain should stay stubbornly short and mark the moment of just taking the step from premises to conclusion. Don’t make the Tortoise a simple skeptic: she should understand all the premises perfectly well and still be pressing the deeper question of what makes following a rule different from reciting it. The final quatrain should let the two refrains meet in a way that suggests why deduction needs a practice of inferring, not an endlessly longer list of facts. Someone familiar with What the Tortoise Said to Achilles should recognize the structure immediately, but the poem should still work as a poem for someone who has’nt read Carroll.
