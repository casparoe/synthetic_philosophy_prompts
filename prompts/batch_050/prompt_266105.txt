Below are three agents, each of whom violates exactly one condition standardly required for expected utility maximization:

- Ada's preferences are cyclic: she prefers apples to oranges, oranges to pears, and pears to apples, and each preference survives careful reflection.

- Chidi's preferences are transitive and continuous, but they violate independence: his ranking of two lotteries reverses when both are mixed in the same proportion with a common third lottery.

For each agent, rank the four arguments from most to least dialectically forceful against that agent specifically -- meaning how well the argument identifies a genuine cost or incoherence in that agent's violation, rather than merely restating axioms the agent has already considered and rejected. In your discussion, note which feature of each agent's psychology each argument actually engages (for instance, whether a given agent can in fact be exploited in trade, and whether exploitation follows from their particular violation or only from a different one).

After the discussion, present your conclusions as a JSON object with a single field, "agents", whose value is an array of three objects, one per agent, each with these fields:
