Write an essay on wether pain asymbolia — the condition where people still report feeling pain, can rank its intensity, but insist it no longer bothers them — shows that the badness of pain consists in its being disliked rather than in how it feels. To keep things concrete, build the essay around this twist: imagine a drug that works in reverse, stripping away the sensory character of a stubbed toe while leaving the aversion fully intact, so the subject is genuinely distressed by something they describe as not feeling like anything at all. Is that case even coherent, and if it is, which of the two conditions involves something bad in itself — the asymbolia patient, the reverse-drug subject, both, or neither? Weigh the main moves on each side (motivational vs. phenomenal accounts of why pain is bad, plus the worry that asymbolia patients might simply be unreliable about their own experience), but don't end on a shrug — defend a verdict about what makes pain bad and what follows for the asymbolia patient.
