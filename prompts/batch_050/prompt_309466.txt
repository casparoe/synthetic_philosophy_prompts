Design me an in-class activity or game where the students actually experience lock-in happening, instead of me describing it to them. What I'm hoping for, roughly:

- Low-tech, no real money, should run in about 40-45 minutes plus time for a debrief.
- Early in the activity some basically random or trivial event should end up mattering a lot later. - There's a distinction my professor keeps hammering that I want the activity to make vivid, so please build around it: (1) the past influences the present (trivial -- everything does), vs (2) the *order* of past events changes which outcome we get, vs (3) we're locked into something demonstrably worse when a better alternative is sitting right there.
