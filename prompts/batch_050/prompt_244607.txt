Every popular account of the arrow of time I have encountered leans on the same analogy: entropy is disorder, a room drifts toward mess and never tidies itself, and that is why the past differs from the future. The analogy is being asked to do real explanatory work: it is supposed to account for why we have records of the past but not the future, why a broken egg unbreaking looks absurd, and why the universe is headed for heat death. Audit it. Give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the messy room, of tidying up, of the messier-than relation. Then the disanalogies that matter for this use, including at least these two: that entropy is defined relative to a choice of coarse-graining in a way room-messiness only crudely imitates, and that for self-gravitating matter a smooth, uniform early gas is the low-entropy state, so clumping into stars and galaxies counts as entropy increase despite looking like a gain in structure and order. Finally, once the disanalogies are on the table: does the disorder picture still support the conclusion that increasing entropy explains the arrow, or does the real explanatory weight fall on the claim that the universe simply began in an extraordinarily special low-entropy state (the Past Hypothesis) — in which case the analogy was only restating the puzzle in tidier language? If you think the analogy survives in weakened form, say precisely what work remains for it.
