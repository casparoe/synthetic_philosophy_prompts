I am preparing a seminar unit on revealed preference and want to use a concrete institutional case rather than begin with abstract choice axioms. Transportation agencies routinely estimate the value of travel time by observing choices between slower, cheaper options and faster, costlier ones: a commuter can take a free road requiring forty minutes or a toll road requiring twenty-five, choose a train over driving despite the fare, or select a longer route to avoid congestion. Analysts infer how much money travelers are willing to pay for saved time, aggregate those amounts across many people, and use the resulting figure when deciding whether a road, rail line, or congestion-pricing scheme passes a cost-benefit test. They often distinguish trip purposes and income groups, and they may discount or correct choices considered uninformed, coerced, anomalous, or distorted by habit. At the same time, the resulting money values are treated as comparable across people even though one dollar does not have the same significance for everyone.

What philosophical conception of utility does this practice presuppose? Does it treat utility merely as a formal representation of stable and coherent choices, as the satisfaction of actual preferences, as access to welfare, or as a measurable quantity that can be traded against money and compared across persons? Reconstruct the strongest version of the implicit theory, identifying which assumptions are strictly required—for example, stability, completeness, transitivity, context-independence, adequate information, voluntary choice, or the commensurability of time, money, safety, and convenience—and which assumptions merely make the practice easier to defend. Please also address the apparent tension between treating observed choices as authoritative and overriding them when analysts judge them biased or ill-informed, and between aggregating willingness to pay while officially remaining neutral about interpersonal welfare comparisons. If several rival theories of utility could license the same calculations, say where the practice is underdetermined rather than pretending it settles those disputes. Finally, explain what would have to change if the agency instead adopted a hedonic, objective-list, or distribution-sensitive account of welfare. Please indicate how confident you are in each major part of the reconstruction and single out the claim about which you are least certain.
