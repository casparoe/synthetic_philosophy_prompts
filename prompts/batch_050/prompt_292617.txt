From what I gather, the New Hume reading says Hume actually did believe in real necessary connections or causal powers out there in nature -- he just thought we can't grasp their nature. But then I don't understand why the problem of induction is still supposed to be a problem. If there really are objective causal powers fixing that the future will resemble the past, then induction is reliable in principle, and Hume's skeptical argument is just about our limited epistemic access to the powers, not about whether induction works. That's a bummer for us cognitively, sure, but it's not a problem for the rationality of science in the way everyone in my field goes on about. So why do philosophers of science keep treating the problem of induction as this live, unsolved thing instead of a footnote about human cognitive limits?

Which one is the actual definition, on the New Hume reading -- presumably the mind-independent one?
