I've been working through the Sleeping Beauty problem by testing my intuitions on variations of the setup, and I'd like to know what principle of self-locating belief, if any, my verdicts amount to. The setup, for precision: Beauty is put to sleep Sunday; a fair coin is flipped; she is awakened Monday in any case, and on Tuesday as well if and only if the coin lands Tails; an amnesia drug prevents her from remembering any previous awakening.

My verdicts:

1. In the classic setup, on awakening her credence in Heads should be 1/3.
2. If instead she is awakened only once either way — Monday if Heads, Tuesday if Tails — then on awakening her credence in Heads should be 1/2, since an awakening was guaranteed and so carries no evidence.
3. If she is awakened on both days regardless of the coin (amnesia still applied), her credence should be 1/2.
4. In the classic setup, upon being told it is Monday, her credence in Heads should rise to 1/2.
5. In a variant with duplication instead of amnesia — no sleep, no memory loss, one copy of her woken in a sealed room if Heads and two copies woken simultaneously in identical sealed rooms if Tails — my intuition says 1/2, not 1/3. This is the verdict I trust least.
6. In the classic setup, her betting dispositions at an awakening should track a 2/3 credence in Tails: she should accept bets on Tails at odds someone with credence 1/2 would refuse.

Is there a single principle that delivers 1 through 4 and 6? If so, state it precisely, indicate which feature of the setup each clause is doing the work for, and tell me what it says about 5 — I suspect 5 and 1 cannot both stand, and I cannot see why the difference between amnesia and duplication should matter, yet the verdicts come out differently for me. Please be explicit about which verdict your proposed principle forces me to abandon, and what it predicts for a case I have not listed: the experimenters do not flip the coin until Monday evening, after the Monday awakening has already occurred.
