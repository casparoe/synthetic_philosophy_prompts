So my friend Priya grades the logic worksheets in our department and she has *opinions* about which conditionals are acceptable and which are nonsense. I've been keeping a list becuase I can't figure out her rule. Her verdicts so far:

1. "If the mail hasn't come yet, it's a public holiday." — fine, since the mail comes every other day without fail.
2. "If the mail hadn't come yesterday, it would have been a public holiday." — garbage, she says; the truck just broke down, and the truck breaking down doesn't make it a holiday.
3. "If you heat that water to 100°, it will boil." — fine.
4. "If you had heated the water last night, it would have boiled." — fine.
5. "If the butler didn't do it, the maid did." — fine, given that we've established it was one of the two of them.
6. "If the butler hadn't done it, the maid would have done it." — garbage; there's no backup murderer waiting in the wings.
7. "If that vase gets knocked off the shelf, it will break." — fine, it's fragile.
8. "If the vase breaks this week, it will have been knocked off the shelf." — garbage, someone could just smash it with a hammer.

New case. Her brother is a detective who swears that whenever a certain suspect lies, he touches his nose — he's got twenty taped interviews backing this up, no exceptions either way. Priya is presented with both of these:

(a) "If the suspect touched his nose in the final interview, he was lying."
(b) "If the suspect had touched his nose in the final interview, he would have been lying."

Predict her verdict on each, and tell me what general rule she's actually running. As far as I can tell there are at least two live candidates: one where both indicatives and counterfactuals just go by whether the consequent is likely enough on the supposition of the antecedent, and one where indicatives go by evidential support while counterfactuals demand an actual mechanism or dependency connecting antecedent to consequent. These seem to diverge on (b) since the nose-touching is evidence of lying without causing it. Tell me which rule best fits her track record, what it predicts, and give me one more conditional I could put to her that would cleanly settle which rule she holds — I'm guessing something like a barometer case would do it but you tell me.
