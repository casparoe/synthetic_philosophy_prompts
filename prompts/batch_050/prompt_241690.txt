Two years ago my partner and I left the city for a smallholding we inherited, and I now volunteer one day a week at a wildlife rehabilitation center. Spending this much time around injured, sick, and dying wild animals has pushed me from abstract agreement that nature contains suffering into actually having to form a view, and I've written down where I've landed so I can pressure-test it. Here is the set.

First, naturalness is morally inert: suffering matters the same whether it is caused by humans, predators, disease, or weather, and the fact that a death is "how things go in the wild" does nothing to diminish its badness. Second, death can be a mercy: I have euthanized animals whose injuries meant a slow, bad end, and I would defend every one of those decisions. Third, predation is off-limits: we should never intervene against predators, and I support reintroducing predators where they have been extirpated, since a landscape without them is impoverished. Fourth, the individual is the moral unit: species and ecosystems matter only insofar as they affect the welfare of the sentient individuals within them, so extinction is bad only through its effects on individuals, present and future. Fifth, I accept the demographic facts as I understand them: most wild animals are born in large clutches, nearly all die young, and many of those deaths are prolonged and nasty; but I reject the conclusion some people draw from this, that most wild lives are not worth living. That conclusion seems to me monstrous, and I won't hold a view that delivers it. Sixth, any interventions we do undertake should be small-scale and reversible; vaccination programs and rehabilitation are fine, but ecosystem-scale redesign is hubris we are not wise enough for.

One case to anchor this: last month I approved surgery, at real expense, on a red-tailed hawk with a shattered wing, knowing full well that a recovered hawk will go back to killing songbirds and rabbits for years, often not cleanly. I would make the same call again.

Please audit this set rather than defend or attack wild animal welfare as a cause. Which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other, and which only appear to? For each genuine tension, tell me exactly what commits me to what, how severe the problem is, and what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. For the apparent tensions, explain briefly why they dissolve. I want a verdict, not a menu: rank the problems by severity, tell me which single change would restore the most consistency, and where you think the hawk case leaves me, commit to that even where you suspect I won't like it. Please write your answer as continuous prose, without headings, lists, bold text, or tables, since I intend to paste it into a letter to a friend.
