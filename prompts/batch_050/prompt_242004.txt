So I was at a pub quiz night last weekend and afterwards two guys from our team (both read a lot of geopolitics stuff, neither of them actually an expert) got into this long argument about neutral countries that I couldn't settle for them. It started when someone mentioned Sweden finally joining NATO, and Mark said that Finland and Sweden dropping their neutrality after Ukraine basically proves neutrality was never a real thing — that those countries were freeloading off the American security umbrella the whole time, enjoying the protection while skipping the bill, and the moment an actual threat showed up they ran to join the alliance like everyone expected. Dave pushed back hard: he said it proves the opposite, that neutrality was a perfectly rational policy that worked for 70+ years, and dropping it the moment the situation changed shows these countries were coldly realistic the whole time, not naive. He said calling it freeloading is like calling it freeloading when you cancel insurance you no longer need. Mark said that's exactly his point — you don't cancel insurance you were never paying for, and the fact that they could flip overnight shows the "armed neutrality" was decorative.

Then it moved to Switzerland and conscription, and this is where it got more heated. Dave argued Switzerland is the only one of the three whose neutrality was ever genuinely credible, specifically because of the militia system — the whole point of conscription is that invading a country where every basement has a rifle and every bridge is wired to blow is a horrible prospect, so credible neutrality basically *requires* mass conscription, and Finland's version too. Mark said conscription is just an economic trick — it's how states get cheap labor and call it virtue — and that a professional army with modern equipment beats a militia every time, so if neutrality depends on conscription then neutrality is dead anyway because conscription is dead. Dave said that completely misses the point, conscription isn't about winning the war, it's about raising the cost of the war so nobody starts it, it's deterrence not warfighting. Mark said deterrence that relies on the enemy believing your citizens will fight a guerilla war from the Alps is a bluff, and bluffs get called eventually, and that's exactly what happened to Sweden and Finland — their deterrence turned out to be worth nothing so they joined NATO. Dave said no, their deterrence was worth plenty until the threat changed, you can't judge a policy by whether it lasts forever. Then Mark said something that I actually found hard to answer: he asked whether a small country's neutrality is ever really a *choice* or just a description of what great powers let you do — Switzerland was neutral because it suited everyone to have a neutral banking hub, not because of the rifle basements. Dave said the rifle basements are exactly *why* it suited everyone. They went in circles from there and I stayed out of it because I genuinely couldn't tell who was winning.
