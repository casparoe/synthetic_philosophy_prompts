I'm designing a handout for an upper-level philosophy of biology seminar on the old charge that natural selection is a tautology — that it says only that the survivors survive, with fitness defined by the very survival it is meant to explain. I want a decision tree that sorts the serious replies by what they take fitness to be, so that students can locate a given paper or textbook passage on the tree without needing to recognize its author's allegiances beforehand.

Please build the tree from questions rather than from a list of named positions. Each node should be a single claim on which the positions genuinely come apart, phrased as a concrete diagnostic on which someone could take a stand without knowing any labels (for example: is an organism that dies in a freak accident despite a superior constitution less fit than the mediocre one that outbreeds it, or merely unlucky?). As a starting menu, the tree must at minimum make use of the following distinctions, though you may reorder, merge, or add nodes if the logic of the space demands it:

- whether fitness is fixed by actual reproductive outcomes or by a disposition that outcomes only imperfectly manifest;
- whether the bearer of fitness is the individual token organism or the trait-type-relative-to-an-environment;
- whether fitness is meant to explain differences in reproductive success, on the model of a causal force, or only to summarize those differences statistically;
- whether fitness can, even in principle, be estimated independently of the survival and reproduction it is invoked to explain.

Each leaf should carry the position's usual name where one exists (the propensity interpretation, for instance), or "unnamed" where the combination is unoccupied; for unoccupied leaves, add one sentence diagnosing whether the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any pair of named positions your tree cannot separate, and say what further diagnostic question would be needed to split them.

Aim for roughly ten to fourteen leaves. Two test cases must appear as worked examples at the relevant nodes: the accidentally sterile organism with an otherwise ideal genotype, and a population in which drift overwhelms selection so that the fitter lineage goes extinct. Where the tree commits to a contested claim — for instance, that a single token organism can bear a propensity that its actual history fails to display — mark the commitment as contested rather than presenting it as settled.

Close with a brief note stating how confident you are that the tree captures the live logical space rather than merely the historically prominent options, and identify the single node or leaf you are least confident about, with a sentence on why.
