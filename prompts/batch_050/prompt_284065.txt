I’ve been thinking about conspiracy theories and why otherwise reasonable people get pulled into them, so I made up a test case.

Suppose a city has a huge blackout. Officials say a substation fire caused it. Over the next month we learn:

1. The mayor initially lied about when she was briefed.
2. The power company had ignored warnings about faulty equipment.
3. Internal emails show officials were mainly worried the outage would become a political scandal.
4. Several independent engineers say the fire genuinely explains when and how the blackout spread.
5. One inspector who questioned the official account later admits he never examined the substation.

People online say this proves the mayor, utility executives, and engineers coordinated a deliberate attack and then invented the fire. To me that jumps from “somebody lied or covered something up” to a much bigger claim about the outage’s actual cause, but maybe I’m missing something.

Please do two things:

- Score each conclusion from 0 to 10 for how justified it is based only on the evidence above:  
  A. The mayor lied about the timeline.  
  B. The company was negligent.  
  C. Officials coordinated a political cover-up.  
  D. The blackout was deliberately caused.  
  E. The official technical explanation is basically correct.

- Rank facts 1–5 by how strongly they support deliberate sabotage specifically, rather than mere negligence, embarrassment, or a cover-up.

0 means ruled out and 10 means established beyond reasonable doubt. Please be brief: maximum 250 words.
