I'm teaching a graduate seminar on decision theory next term and I want to open the acausal trade unit with a sorting quiz, something students take cold so they find out their position by what they say about cases rather than by which label sounds smart to them.

Here's what I need:

- 8 to 10 questions, each a concrete scenario with 2-4 answer options. Work in at least these cases somewhere: a prisoner's dilemma against an exact copy of yourself; an alien civilization you'll never be able to contact where each side could benefit the other cheaply if both "keep up their end"; a counterparty who is a merely possible agent (they exist, if at all, only in some other possible world); a case where you're confident the other agent exists but know almost nothing about what it values; and a case where a superintelligent predictor can hurt you acausally unless you pay up.
- A scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves. The leaves must include: the causalist refusal (only what I can causally affect matters, so there's nothing to trade on), the symmetry-only cooperator (cooperates with literal copies, defects on everyone else), full updateless trade (deal with any agent whose reasoning correlates with yours, even merely possible ones), and the updateless view that trades for mutual benefit but refuses to pay acausal blackmailers. If you think there's another leaf in the logical space someone should plausibly occupy, add it; and for any combination of answers nobody would hold, note it and say in a sentence whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
- The thing I most want the quiz to do is pry apart the metaphysics (do merely possible agents get a seat at the bargaining table?) from the epistemics (can you ever be confident enough about the counterparty's existance and values for the deal to be more than wishful thinking?). At least two questions should have that seperation as their whole job.
- Phrase everything so a student can answer without knowing the terminology -- no question like "do you endorse updatelessness?"
- Flag any pair of leaves the quiz can't reliably tell apart instead of papering over it.

Keep the whole thing to one handout page if you can.
