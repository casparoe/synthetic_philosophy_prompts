You are a moral philosopher with expertise in ancient ethics and moral psychology. Think carefully, and begin by briefly brainstorming several candidate scenarios before settling on the one that best meets the specifications below.

Glaucon's challenge in the Republic asks whether justice benefits its possessor in itself, once every reputational reward and punishment is stripped away. His own device for this, the ring of Gyges, achieves the stripping by magic. Construct a thought experiment that performs the same function without magic. Specifications: (1) the scenario must be realistic -- an ordinary modern setting, no invisibility, no gods, no afterlife, no stipulated guarantees that wrongdoing will never be discovered, only a psychologically and socially credible structure in which the reputational cost of one particular immoral act is in fact zero; (2) the scenario must isolate the intrinsic value of being moral from its consequences, meaning that what the agent stands to gain or lose through others' opinions must be genuinely held fixed, not merely asserted to be fixed; (3) the case must be one on which a psychological egoist and a defender of the view that virtue benefits its possessor give clearly different verdicts about what the agent has reason to do and why.

After presenting the scenario (one paragraph at most), demonstrate that it works: state the verdict each side delivers and the reasoning behind it; identify the single structural feature of the case that does the work of the ring, and say what happens to the challenge if that feature is removed; then give the strongest reply available to the egoist -- the way they would argue that the case either cheats or proves less than it seems to. Close with one sentence on whether your scenario shows the challenge can be posed without fantasy, or instead shows that the fantasy was load-bearing.
