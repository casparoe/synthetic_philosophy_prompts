I've been arguing with a friend about what counts as art and I keep losing, because I can't figure out what principle I'm actually using. So here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases — tell me what principle, if any, unifies them, or tell me which verdict I have to give up.

1. A readymade in a gallery (a urinal on a plinth, that kind of thing): art, obviously. I've made my peace with this.
2. A striking piece of driftwood on a beach that everyone stops to photograph: not art. It's just a nice object.
3. The exact same driftwood, moved to a gallery plinth with a title card: art. The move did the work somehow.
4. A photorealistic forgery of a famous painting, made to deceive buyers: still art. Provenance and intent-to-deceive don't un-art it, they just make it fraudulent.
5. A gorgeous sunset: not art, and anyone who says otherwise is being sentimental.
6. A spectacular diving catch in baseball: not art, even when the broadcasters call it a work of art. Being deliberately performed in front of an institution (a stadium, cameras) doesn't make it art.
7. A pianist sits at the piano for four minutes and plays nothing, as a composed performance: art. Infuriating, but art.
8. An image made by someone prompting a generative AI model, picking between outputs, tweaking the prompt: art. Or at least, I want to say art.
9. The pixel-identical image, but produced by a hardware glitch in an unattended program that nobody ever prompted or looked at: not art. This one I'm shakiest on, because if a curator later found the glitch-image and hung it next to the driftwood, I'm honestly not sure what I'd say.

So the tension you should be able to see: case 3 says selecting and framing a thing you didn't make is enough, case 6 says doing something skillful and beautiful in a framed institutional setting is not enough, and case 9 threatens case 3 if you push on it.

What I want: a candidate principle (or two or three serious candidates) stated precisely, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it. Test each against all nine cases, don't just gesture. Tell me what it says about cases I didn't mention — a child's drawing on the fridge, a ritual mask made by a culture without our concept of art, a proof mathematicians call elegant. And be straight with me about which of my verdicts the best principle can't save, because I suspect one of them has to die.

One more thing: I know aestheticians have actual positions here (intentionalist, institutional, aesthetic-attitude, hybrid views) and I'd like to know where my principle lands relative to those. But please separate what's more or less working consensus in that literature from what's still genuinely contested — don't present one theory of art as if it's settled doctrine when the field is still fighting about it.
