I've been asked to circulate a statement of my views on semantic externalism for a workshop on self-knowledge, and before I send it around I want a consistency check on the package as a whole. Here is what I hold:

1. Putnam's Twin Earth argument goes through. My molecule-for-molecule duplicate on Twin Earth, whose environment contains the liquid XYZ rather than H2O, means something different by the sound he makes when I say "water," and when each of us thinks what he would express as "water is wet," we are thinking thoughts with different truth-conditions. Reference and content are fixed partly by the environment, not by anything internal to the thinker.

2. First-person authority is real. I can know that I am thinking that water is wet without conducting any empirical investigation of my surroundings, and my sincere avowals about what I am currently thinking are normally to be taken as correct rather than as defeasible hypotheses.

3. Slow switching is possible. If I were secretly transported to Twin Earth and settled there, then after enough immersion my word "water" would come to refer to XYZ and the contents of my thoughts would shift accordingly — and at no point in that process would I be able to detect the change by introspection alone.

4. Memory preserves content across such cases. If, years after an unnoticed switch, I seem to remember having thought that water is wet, my memory yields knowledge of the very thought I originally had; it does not silently substitute a new content for the old one.

5. There is no need to posit a layer of "narrow" content that intrinsic duplicates automatically share. Wide, environment-involving content is the only content there is, and it suffices for psychological explanation.

6. Physicalism holds, and the causal explanation of my bodily movements bottoms out in the intrinsic physical states of my brain — yet content-involving explanations of action ("he drank because he believed the glass contained water") remain perfectly legitimate.

Please audit this set. Which commitments genuinely pull against one another, and which conflicts are merely apparent and dissolve on inspection? For each genuine tension, state precisely what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost. Rank the tensions from most to least serious rather than presenting them as a flat list, and conclude by identifying the single revision that would restore the most consistency at the lowest price. I have no strong prior about which item I would surrender first.
