I'm presenting at a grad workshop next week defending a paper on the torture-vs-dust-specks case. Standard setup: one person tortured for 50 years vs a dust speck in the eye for a staggering number of people (3^^^3 in the usual version). Most people pick the specks. My paper argues the specks answer is unstable because it smuggles together two different questions: which outcome is worse, and what it'd be rational to choose given our massive uncertainty about tiny harms. I claim that once you seperate those, torture is worse simpliciter, but picking the specks can still be a defensible decision under uncertainty -- so the dilemma is partly a confusion of axiology with decision procedure, not a clean test of whether you aggregate.

The audience: mostly population ethicists. Two are total utilitarians who'll think my separation move is just evading the repugnant conclusion's neighborhood. One works on lexical threshold views and will be broadly friendly but thinks I don't go far enough. There's also a Kantian who believes the whole thought experiment is corrupt and that you can't compare these harms at all, and a decision theorist who will want my uncertainty claim spelled out properly rather than hand-waved.

Give me my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of them would put it, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Say what I should concede up front vs hold. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
