I'm compiling responses to the tautology objection against natural selection — the charge that "survival of the fittest" is vacuous because the fittest are simply defined as those who survive and reproduce. Here are the responses I already have; please don't repeat them or repackage them:

1. **The propensity response**: fitness is a propensity or disposition to leave descendants, not actual reproductive output. Since actual survival depends on luck as well as propensity, the principle says something substantive — and can even be false in particular cases where drift dominates.

2. **The theoretical-term response**: fitness is defined internally to the formalism of population genetics (as a parameter in selection equations), not by survival. The slogan is an informal gloss on the models, and the models make precise, testable predictions.

3. **The dispensability response**: the slogan is expendable shorthand. Selection theory's real content is the claim that heritable variation in traits correlated with reproductive differences produces evolutionary change, which is plainly empirical and stated without any definition of "fittest."

4. **The operationalization response**: the principle is a schema that is circular only in the abstract; in any actual application fitness is estimated independently (via life-history traits, developmental rates, etc.) and then used to predict outcomes, so no working biologist reasons in a circle.

Is there another response to the objection that is genuinely distinct from these four — not a rewording, a special case, or a conjunction of them? Give the response, state precisely how it differs from each item above, and then give your honest assessment of whether it succeeds, including which of the four (if any) it implicitly depends on.
