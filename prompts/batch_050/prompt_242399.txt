I recently moved from Manchester to Rotterdam for a job, and because I work on a pan-European team I keep hearing stuff like "the Dutch are blunt, the Italians are chaotic, the Germans love a process." Last week two of my colleagues had a proper argument about it in the canteen and I've been going back and forth on who was right.

(1) Joris said generalising about peoples is basically always a mistake: the variation within any country dwarfs the variation between countries, so a sentence like "the Dutch are direct" tells you more about the speaker's small sample of Dutch people than about the Netherlands.

(2) Sofia pushed back that she hires across four countries and she can predict how a new colleague will run a meeting far better from their nationality than from, say, their star sign -- so something real is being tracked. Her line was that even when two distributions overlap heavily, a shifted distribution is still genuine information, and pretending otherwise is just politeness dressed up as statistics.

(3) Joris said what she's actually tracking is institutions and incentives, not character: put the same German in a start-up in Lisbon and the famous process-love evaporates in six months. If the trait vanishes when the context changes, calling it national character is a misdiagnosis of what's doing the work.

(4) Sofia replied that some of it survives the context change -- second- and third-generation diaspora kids keep attitudes their grandparents carried over, about things like family obligation or how openly you disagree with a boss, which shows culture is sticky and not just local incentive structures.

(5) Joris's last move was that diasporas are self-selected so that evidence is dodgy, and anyway even if the group differences are real, they're useless at the individual level: the overlap is so big that knowing someone's nationality barely updates what you should expect from the actual person in front of you, so the practical upshot of his position stands even if Sofia wins the science.

Can you go through the five moves and rate each one from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a sentence or two of justification for each? Then say who's more right overall, and whether there's something important both of them are missing. I'm genuinely torn and don't want to take sides at lunch tomorrow without a better handle on it.
