I retired from chemistry a few years back and have been reading philosophy on my own ever since, and I just finished Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life -- the monumental, antiquarian, and critical modes. Bad timing, or maybe good: I sit on my old university's commemoration comittee, and we have to settle the fate of Vaughan Hall, named for a chemist who made real discoveries and basically built the department from nothing, but who also spent the 1930s actively lobbying for sterilization laws and testifying in their favor -- not just holding the views of his day, acting on them. Three options are on the table: keep the name as is, strip it entirely, or keep it with a substantial contextual exhibit in the lobby. Draft the memo I'll circulate before our vote: recommend one option, but do the analysis in Nietzsche's terms -- which of his three modes of history is actually driving each faction on the committee, which mode a university genuinely needs in a case like this, and whose "life" the history is supposed to serve. Take seriously Nietzsche's own warning that critical history can uproot things an institution needs in order to function, and give the strongest argument for the option you don't pick a fair shake. Open with the recommendation in the first sentence, then give the reasoning -- no preamble, no restating the facts of the case.
