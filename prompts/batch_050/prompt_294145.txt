I help run a local rationality meetup, about 14 regulars, we get a community center back room for ~90 minutes. Problem: this crowd has basically internalized "maximize expected value" as an identity, and I want to spend an evening productively breaking that. Specifically I want them to *feel* the Kelly/geometric-growth point — that for repeated multiplicative bets, the growth-optimal strategy is not the per-round-EV-maximizing one, that the median trajectory of an all-in-EV-maxer goes to ruin while the mean gets dragged up by a couple of lucky outliers, and that "it's still positive EV!" stops being comforting once you're personally bust. I got into this via the ergodicity economics stuff (the coin game where each flip multiplies your stake by 1.5 or 0.6, mean goes up forever, almost everyone goes broke) and I definately don't want to just lecture it at them.

So: design a runnable game for the evening. Give me concrete rules — what the actual bets are (biased coins? dice? a menu of "investments" I offer as the house?), what the stakes are (play money, or points convertible into snacks I bring — budget like $30 total), how many rounds we can realistically fit, whether people pick their own bet fractions or get assigned strategies, rebuy rules when someone hits zero, and a rough timeline for the 90 minutes. The design constraint that matters: the EV-maximizing policy and the growth-optimal policy have to visibly diverge within the number of rounds we can actually run, so the divergence shows up in the room rather than in a spreadsheet. Nobody should hear the words "Kelly criterion" or "geometric mean" until the debrief — if some quant-adjacent regular recognizes it immediately and starts optimizing on round one, tell me how to handle that too.

Then the debrief: map specific moments that will happen in the game onto the concepts (time average vs ensemble average, why log wealth is the thing being maximized, why "converges in the long run" does nothing for the guy who busted at round 12). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this — someone realizing all-in is +EV and YOLOing, people optimizing the points-to-snacks conversion instead of the game, collusion, arguments about whether the coin is actually biased — and how to preempt each. And be honest about which feature of the Kelly story the activity can't reproduce: e.g. the asymptotic almost-sure dominance claim needs way more rounds than we have, and real risk aversion exists independently of growth-optimality so a "log utility" reading of the debrief would be a bit of a conflation — tell me what I have to supply verbally at the end so nobody walks away with the wrong lesson.
