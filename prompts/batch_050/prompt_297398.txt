ok so I'm in a phil of science course and we're doing Bayesian confirmation theory, and I tried to put together an argument for my term paper and it seems to prove something I refuse to believe. Here's the argument, tell me where it breaks:

(1) For evidence E to confirm a hypothesis H for you, learning E has to be what raises your rational credence in H. Confirmation is a diachronic boost: your new credence in H is P(H | E), and E confirms H for you only if that's higher than your old credence.

(2) Simple math: if you already know E for sure, then P(E) = 1, so P(H | E) = P(H). Evidence you've had all along can't give any hypothesis a boost. Old evidence never confirms.

(3) The anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion was measured by astronomers in 1859 and was a famous unsolved problem for decades before Einstein wrote down general relativity in 1915. So for Einstein, and for literally everyone who has ever evaluated GR, the Mercury data was old evidence. By (2), it never confirmed GR for anyone.

(4) Maybe you want to bite the bullet for that one case, but it generalizes. Take any theory we now accept — evolution, germ theory, plate tectonics. All the evidence for it is already in our evidence base, it's all old. So by (2), nothing currently confirms any of our theories, and our high confidence in them is irrational by Bayesian standards. Believing this argument means believing basically none of our scientific knowledge is confirmed.

(5) And the worst part: the case that looks like the most paradigmatic confirmation in the history of science is a scientist realizing that her theory predicts some fact she already knew. That's the Einstein-Mercury case in a nutshell: he worked out that his field equations yield the missing 43 arcseconds per century. But working that out doesn't change anyone's evidence — the astronomical fact was known before and after, and the theory is the same theory. Since rational credence is fixed by your evidence, and the evidence didn't change, the boost he felt was an illusion too.

(6) So Bayesian conditionalization entails that rational confidence can never be increased by evidence you already possess, nor by figuring out what follows from things you already believe. Since those are exactly the cases scientists hold up as the strongest confirmations ever, Bayesianism is refuted as an account of confirmation.
