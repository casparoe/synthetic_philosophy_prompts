Title: The Meteor Objection

Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility (REU) lets rational agents be risk-averse by weighting outcomes according to a risk function r, where worse-ranked outcomes get extra weight. Take the standard set-up: I can have $50 for sure, or a gamble that pays $100 with probability .5 and $0 with probability .5. With a mildly pessimistic risk function r(p) = p², the gamble's REU comes out to 25, so I take the sure thing. So far so good.

But now add a meteor. Consider the same gamble except that with probability .001, instead of the money stuff, a meteor strikes and the outcome is -$1,000,000 (some suitably terrible outcome). Plugging into the REU formula: -1,000,000 + r(.999)(1,000,000) + r(.5)(100) ≈ -1,974. The gamble that was worth 25 is now worth negative two thousand, even though the meteor is a one-in-a-thousand fluke. My objection: the worst outcome enters the formula with an effective weight of 1 - r(1 - p) ≈ 2p when r(p) = p², so mild pessimism systematically doubles the pull of tail catastrophes. Any agent who is even mildly risk-averse in Buchak's sense becomes paralyzed by sufficiently bad far-fetched outcomes, and this strikes me as much worse than anything standard expected utility does.

My proposed fix: agents should truncate their risk function, i.e. just refuse to weight any outcome whose probability falls below some threshold ε. This restores sensible behavior in meteor cases. I argue the cost is small: yes, the theory becomes discontinuous at the threshold, but real agents are lumpy about probabilities anyway.
