I sit on the ethics comittee of a fertility network, and we need a policy before launch. A clinic wants to offer IVF patients embryo screening in two tiers: (A) testing for serious monogenic disease like Tay-Sachs, and (B) polygenic “risk scores” that rank embryos for things like diabetes, depression, maybe even educational attainment, with a glossy dashboard. Some clinicians say refusing B is paternalistic; others say it’s liberal eugenics with worse marketing. Draft the committee’s decision memo: approve A only, approve both, or approve with conditions, and give an actionable bottom line. Use this passage from John Stuart Mill, On Liberty, ch. V as the hinge: “In the first place, it must by no means be supposed, because damage, or probability of damage, to the interests of others, can alone justify the interference of society, that therefore it always does justify such interference.” Say whether possible harm to the future child, to existing siblings, or to society is even the right kind of “damage,” given the non-identity problem, and give the strongest losing argument a fair shake. Keep separate what’s settled among people who know this literature from what’s genuinely contested, and don’t dress up a contested view as consensus. After the memo, add a JSON object with fields: recommendation, conditions, settled_points, contested_points, strongest_objection, reply_to_objection, mill_passage_role.
