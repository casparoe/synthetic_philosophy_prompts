So I've been getting into film photography this past year -- my grandfather left me his old Leica and I've been slowly learning to develop my own rolls in the bathroom -- and somewhere along the way I fell down the rabbit hole of the philosophy literature on whether photographs are "transparent," i.e. whether, when I look at a photo of my grandfather as a young man, I literally see him (indirectly, the way I see things through a telescope) or whether I just see a picture that represents him, the way a painting does. Kendall Walton kicked this off in the 80s with the transparency thesis, and people have been going back and forth ever since: some say photos are counterfactually dependent on the scene in a way paintings aren't because of the mechanical causal chain, others point out that mirrors and live CCTV feeds have the same causal structure and yet the debate just... absorbs those cases without anyone conceding anything.

Here's what bugs me. Everyone in this debate agrees on the relevant facts: how cameras work, how painting works, how mirrors work, what our intuitions about the cases are. The arguments all seem to be on the table and have been for decades, and yet nobody has moved. My half-formed hypothesis is that the dispute is really about which ordinary-language reactions get to count as data -- some philosophers treat "I'm looking at a photo of my grandmother, so in some sense I'm seeing her" as a datum to be accommodated, others treat it as loose talk to be explained away -- and that this is the kind of thing that can't be settled by more argument. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real disagreement buried in there, e.g. an empirical question about how ordinary viewers actually conceptualize photographs, or a substantive question about what perception essentially requires.

So: diagnose this debate for me. Is it verbal (two senses of "see" talking past each other), substantive (a real disagreement about the nature of perception), or a case of the two sides answering different questions under one heading? Support the diagnosis with what each side actually says rather than just surveying positions, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a conceptual distinction, an experimental philosophy study, a new test case nobody's tried -- or argue honestly that no progress is possible and why. End with a last line beginning "Diagnosis:" followed by a one-to-three word label (e.g. "verbal dispute", "substantive", "two different questions") and one sentence of justification.
