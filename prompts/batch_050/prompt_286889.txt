My ethics professor's verdicts on standing to blame so far: a man currently cheating on his own wife blames his brother for cheating -- no standing; a former tax evader who confessed and paid restitution blames a current tax evader -- standing; a compulsive liar blames a colleague for plagiarism -- standing, though it rankles; a man who helped plan a cruel prank blames the friend who carried it out -- no standing; a manager who privately mocked an employee for years blames a coworker for mocking the same employee at a party -- no standing. Next week's case: an executive who years ago committed expense-account fraud, was never caught, has made no disclosure or repayment to the company, but genuinely condemns herself for it, holds herself to the very standard she invokes, and quietly donated the equivalent sum to charity -- she now blames a junior colleague for the same fraud. Predict her verdict, say which principle she is implicitly running -- unrepented commission of the same wrong, complicity in the wrongdoing, failure to apply the standard to oneself, or something else -- and identify the further case that would distinguish the two strongest candidates.
