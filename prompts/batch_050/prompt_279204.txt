Consider the so-called gender-equality paradox: across countries, higher scores on standard indices of gender equality are associated with lower female shares among STEM graduates, so that the more egalitarian the society, the more gendered some educational choices appear. Give me your credence that this pattern is primarily explained by relatively stable average differences in occupational interests between men and women, which wealthier and freer societies give more room to express, rather than by measurement artifacts or by egalitarian societies merely licensing gender stereotypes in subtler forms. Decompose the estimate: the probability that the correlation survives scrutiny of how both gender equality and STEM participation are measured; conditional on its being real, the probability that the interest-expression mechanism, rather than rival cultural mechanisms, does the main explanatory work; and any further terms you think belong, each with a number and one line of justification, then how they combine. State which term you are least confident in and what evidence would shift it most. Finally, say briefly what your conclusion implies for whether the remaining gap is properly a problem of justice or merely a divergence of taste — and how your credence on that normative question relates to the empirical one.
