In the Third Definitive Article of Perpetual Peace, Kant grounds cosmopolitan right in a strikingly physical fact: the earth is a closed, finite sphere, so human beings can't scatter away from each other forever and are eventually forced to live side by side, which is what obliges us to work out terms of peaceful coexistence (hospitality rather than conquest) rather than just flee. The confinement does real argumentative work for him -- it's not just scenery. Or would he instead say that the obligation was never really contingent on geography, and that rational beings remain bound to each other across any distance, in which case colonization just extends the domain of cosmopolitan right to a multi-planetary federation?
