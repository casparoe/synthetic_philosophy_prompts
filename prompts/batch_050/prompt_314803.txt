2. In Newcomb's problem I two-box, and the "why ain'cha rich" line leaves me cold: rationality evaluates acts, not dispositions that predictors happen to reward.
3. In the original Death in Damascus setup — perfect predictor, appointment booked for wherever Death foresees you'll be — staying and fleeing are both perfectly rational. The merchant is doomed and will regret whatever he does, but that makes the story tragic, not paradoxical.
