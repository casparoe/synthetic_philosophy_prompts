So a friend and I have been arguing about death for weeks and she says my position is a patchwork of things that don't fit. Before I concede anything I want a second opinion. Here's what I actually think:

1. Something can only be good or bad *for you* if it enters your experience somehow. Unfelt stuff that never touches your consciousness can't harm you. Call it the experience requirement, I'm pretty committed to it.
2. Given that, the Epicureans are basically right: my death won't be bad for me, because once I'm dead there's no me around having bad experiences, and being dead isn't a state I'm in.
3. That said it's obviously rational for me to prefer living to 90 over dying at 50, and to spend money and effort avoiding an early death. I don't see this as in conflict with 2 at all.
4. Killing a happy person painlessly in their sleep, in a way nobody ever discovers, with no grieving relatives and no knock-on effects on anyone else's behavior, still seriously wrongs *the victim* -- it's not just that it would be a bad rule or that it might get found out.
5. Lucretius-style symmetry arguments work: the eternity before I was born wasn't bad for me, and that's genuinely good evidence that the eternity after I die won't be bad for me either.
6. And it makes perfect sense that I'm glad I exist -- having been born is better for me than never having existed at all.

She says at least two of these can't stand together and one of them isn't doing the work I think it is. Which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like it? Rank them by how bad the damage is, say what would have to give in each case, and tell me the single revision that fixes the most with the least collateral. I'd rather drop one commitment than quietly weaken three of them, so be blunt about which one is the problem.
