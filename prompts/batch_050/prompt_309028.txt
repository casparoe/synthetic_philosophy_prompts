I'm in a political philosophy reading group and we've been stuck for weeks on the "equality of what?" question — welfare, resources, capabilities, the usual suspects. Last session my friend Mara got impatient with all of them and pitched her own candidate metric, which she calls equality of *exit options*. The rough idea: what a just society should equalize isn't how people feel, or what they own, or even the full menu of things they're capable of doing or being, but rather the number (and robustness) of genuinely live *exits* each person has from their current situation — quitting a job, leaving a relationship, moving to a different city, switching careers — where an exit counts as "live" if you could actually take it within about a year without ruinous cost to yourself or your dependents. Her pitch is that this captures something the capability approach gestures at but fumbles: capabilities count up everything you *could* do, including thousands of things you'd never remotely want, whereas exit options zero in on the specific freedoms that protect you from domination and stuckness. She also claims it's way more measurable than capabilities, since you can in principle survey people about which exits they'd realistically consider and what would stop them.

Please argue that Mara is right — that equality of exit options is a genuinely better distribuendum than Sen-style capability equality. I'd like the argument to actually engage with the strongest objections rather than just cheerleading. A few that came up in our group: (1) a contented monk with two exits and a miserable executive with fifty look equal-ish on the standard stories but wildly unequal on Mara's, and intuitions pull in weird directions there; (2) counting exits seems to need some weighting by quality, and once you start weighting you slide back toward either welfare or capabilities; (3) adaptive preferences — someone who has been beaten down into not even imagining alternatives might *report* having no exits they'd consider, which seems to reward their oppression rather than flag it. Feel free to modify or precisify the exit-options view if that helps the defense, but keep it recognizably her proposal and not just capabilities rebranded.
