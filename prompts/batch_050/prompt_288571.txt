**Exam question:** "The fundamental dynamical laws are, to good approximation, invariant under time reversal, yet macroscopic processes are manifestly irreversible. Explain how a direction of time emerges on the standard statistical-mechanical picture, addressing (a) the status of the Second Law, (b) the role of boundary conditions, and (c) how the reversibility of the underlying dynamics is to be reconciled with observed irreversibility. Is the arrow of time explained by the laws, or by the initial state of the universe?"

**Student's answer:**

"The microdynamics — Newtonian mechanics, and unitary quantum evolution — are time-reversal invariant, but macroscopic behavior is irreversible because of the Second Law: the entropy of an isolated system never decreases. Boltzmann's H-theorem established the Second Law rigorously from mechanics, showing that entropy must increase for any system of colliding particles. The law is statistical rather than absolute: high-entropy macrostates correspond to vastly more microstates than low-entropy ones, so a system in a low-entropy state evolves toward equilibrium with overwhelming probability, and only absurdly improbable fluctuations run the other way.

Time symmetry of the laws poses no problem here. For every entropy-increasing trajectory there is a time-reversed entropy-decreasing one; the asymmetry enters through boundary conditions. The Second Law requires that the universe began in an extraordinarily low-entropy state — the so-called Past Hypothesis — since otherwise entropy would have had no room to grow. All observed irreversibility traces back to this one boundary condition: every local entropy decrease (a refrigerator, a growing embryo) is paid for by a larger increase elsewhere.

One might worry that retrodiction is just as reversible as prediction: from a system's present low-entropy state, the time-symmetric dynamics make a higher-entropy past exactly as likely as a higher-entropy future. But this shows only that the Past Hypothesis cannot be inferred from present evidence; it remains the most economical posit, and physics is free to assume it. So the arrow of time is explained by the initial state, not the laws: the laws merely permit the gradient along which entropy climbs, and the universe's having started low does all the explanatory work."
