I keep hearing "the brain is a computer" thrown around and I've realized I have no idea what's actually being claimed. Latest instance: a friend of mine (neuroscience PhD student, fwiw) used it to shut down a debate we were having about whether you could ever upload a mind. His move was basically: brains are computers, minds are what brains do, so running the right program on different hardware gets you the mind, end of story. But the more I think about it the more "the brain is a computer" seems to slide between a bunch of totally different claims.

So: can you lay out the distinct precise theses that sentence could be expressing? I can gesture at a few myself -- maybe just "the brain processes information" (seems trivially true?), or "cognition is literally Turing-style computation over representations," or something about input-output functions being replicable in principle, or maybe a stronger claim that whatever the brain does, some digital computer could do the same computations. There are probably others I'm missing. For each one: state it precisely enough that it could actually be false, say whether it's a platitude, genuinely contentious, or probably false, and what would even count as evidence for or against it (like, does the fact that neurons do analog-ish graded stuff matter? does it matter that the brain is wet and embodied and constantly rewiring itself?).

And then the payoff question: which of these readings does my friend actually need for his upload argument to go through, and does he get it? My suspicion is he's using the weakest most-platitudinous reading to seem obviously right, while his conclusion needs one of the strong contentious ones. But maybe I'm wrong and even the weak reading gets him most of the way there.

Also curious whether "the brain is a computer" is even the right comparison class -- like is the interesting claim "brains are computers" or "computers could be brains"? Those feel different but I can't articulate why.
