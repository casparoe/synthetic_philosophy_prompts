So here's the situation. I'm a retired entomologist -- spent about thirty years on social insects, mostly harvester ants and some Temnothorax work -- and since retiring I've gotten really into philosophy of mind. The local university has a philosophy reading group and they let me give a talk next month. The topic I picked is Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary, the conscious ant colony from Gödel, Escher, Bach, which half of them have apparently read.

My thesis, roughly: real ant colonies actually do a bunch of the things Hofstadter gave Aunt Hillary -- colony-level decisions with speed-accuracy tradeoffs during house-hunting, memory that outlives any individual ant, learning, distributed control with nobody in charge. That part isn't science fiction, it's in the literature (Deborah Gordon's work, the quorum-sensing stuff on nest choice). But, I want to argue, Hofstadter's setup still misleads, because ants aren't neurons. Each ant is a little agent doing its own thing, so colony "decisions" are more like committee votes than thoughts. Colonies have cognition without anything it's like to be the colony, and Aunt Hillary tricks you into conflating the two.

The audience: maybe ten philosophy grad students plus two faculty. They know functionalism cold, the Block-style China nation / absent qualia objections, symbol grounding, some of them are into 4E/enactivist stuff, and there's definately one IIT person. None of them know ant biology beyond pop-sci level, and a couple will assume I've gotten the philosophy wrong because I'm a bug guy.

Prep me for the Q&A. I want:

- The objections I'll actually get, ordered by how likely times how damaging, phrased the way these people would phrase them (not the way a biologist would)
- For each one: what I should concede, what I should hold firm on, and a reply I can give in about two minutes
- A note on where each reply of mine is soft
- The question I'm praying nobody asks (I have a suspicion it's the one about whether my years of pest-control-adjacent fieldwork make me a mass murderer on my own view, but tell me if you think it's something worse)
- Also flag any spot where the philosophy people will think my reading of the ant science is doing more work than it can carry

Keep in mind I can't cite papers from memory on the spot, so don't build replies that depend on me name-dropping specific studies.
