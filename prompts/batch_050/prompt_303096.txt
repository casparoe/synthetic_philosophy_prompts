I am a PhD student in metaphysics, and I keep returning to the problem of universals with a suspicion that the debate has quietly changed from a genuine contest into a competition of bookkeeping. Consider two freshly machined discs that are exactly alike in colour: the immanent realist says there is one universal, redness, wholly present in each; the trope theorist says each disc has its own numerically distinct redness-trope, with the tropes exactly resembling one another; the resemblance nominalist says the discs simply resemble, and that resemblance needs no further ontological backing; the austere nominalist says the truth that both are red needs no entity or primitive relation beyond the discs and their being red. Every side can redescribe the case without contradiction, every side claims the others have merely deferred the explanatory burden, and no observation or logical result appears to decide between them.

My working hypothesis is that the dispute is not really about how many entities exist but about what a putative explanation must explain: whether exact resemblance is a fact that requires a truthmaker, whether a primitive resemblance relation is an ontological cost or merely an ideological one, and whether positing a single repeatable entity explains commonality better than positing many exactly similar particulars. Is that diagnosis right, or is there a substantive disagreement here that survives once questions of ontology, ideology, and explanatory demand are separated? I want a diagnosis of why the debate has stalled, grounded in what the positions actually say rather than in a survey of them, and an account of what would count as progress: a formal impossibility result, a principled way of weighing ontological against ideological parsimony, a case one side genuinely cannot accommodate, or an argument that the question should be abandoned as ill-posed.
