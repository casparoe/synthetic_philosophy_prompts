So I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training, and I keep running into this thing while reading ethics stuff for fun and I can't tell if it's a real problem or just philosophers being weird.

The setup: some moral views say certain acts are just off-limits, period. Like, don't torture, don't kill an innocent person, even if doing it once would stop five other people from doing the exact same forbidden thing. So the rule against killing means... five people get killed instead of one, all in the way the rule was supposedly protecting against. That seems straight-up incoherent to me? Like if the point of the rule is that killing innocents is bad, why does the rule make you tolerate more of the bad thing. In engineering terms it feels like the rule optimizes for the wrong variable -- it's measuring "did *I* personally kill anyone" instead of "how many innocent people got killed."

I've seen this called the "paradox of deontology" and apparently serious people take it seriously, so please explain to me, intuitively not technically:

1. Why this is considered a genuine puzzle and not just an obvious bug in deontological views. What's the strongest version of the objection, stated as forcefully as possible?
2. Why the obvious fix -- "just act to minimize total violations of the rule" -- apparently doesn't count as deontology anymore. What exactly gets lost when you go that route?
3. What the actual responses are. I know there's something about the threshold idea (constraints hold up to some catastrophic point, then switch off) but that seems to just relocate the weirdness to wherever the threshold is. And I've vaguely heard of a reply where the point of the constraint isn't minimizing killings but something about what you do to the victim, like using them -- does that actually dissolve the paradox or just rename it?
4. At the end, tell me honestly: do most philosophers think this paradox can be answered, or is it more like deontologists just have to swallow it?

Bonus points if you can tie it back to something concrete, like whether a self-driving car should ever be programmed to deliberately swerve into one pedestrian to avoid plowing through a crowd, because that's the kind of case where I keep feeling the pull of "obviously minimize deaths" and "obviously don't program a machine to aim at people" at the same time and maybe the paradox is just that feeling, formalized.
