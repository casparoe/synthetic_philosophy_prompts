Ok, my opening. I'm a software engineer, and in my world, if your argument leans on a measurement, you live and die by that measurement. A big chunk of your case against the guillotine is empirical: it doesnt deter anyone, the statistics show no drop in murders, and the state hides executions precisely because the so-called example would disgust the public if they actually saw it. Fine. But imagine someone hands you airtight numbers showing that executing one murderer really does prevent three future murders. On your own reasoning you'd have to support it. So which is it: either your abolitionism is just a bet on deterrence that could lose, or the statistics were decoration and your real argument is something else -- something that would hold even if the numbers went the other way. And if its the second one, I want to hear that argument stated cleanly, without the statistics crutch.
