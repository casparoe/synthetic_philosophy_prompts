Question from a working novelist, forgive me if this is half baked. I'm on my third book, all of them first person, and there's a thing I keep running into that I don't have a word for. None of my writer friends do either, though everyone recognizes it the second you describe it.

When a first-person narrator is working well, readers stop experiencing the narrator as a person. Nobody forms a face for them, nobody really hears a voice; the events just sort of arrive, as if the reader were standing where the narrator stands. The 'I' does all the seeing and none of the being-seen. Then the narrator does some small thing -- mentions being handsome, feels sorry for himself two sentences too long, apologizes to the reader for a digression -- and suddenly there he is, this guy-shaped object standing between you and the story, and you have to peer around him. My editor calls it the narrator getting in the way, but that's a workshop complaint, not a concept.

I notice the same thing from the inside when drafting. The chapters where I forget my narrator has a body write fastest and read best. The ones where I keep remembering him are sludge. Though, annoyingly, some of the most beloved first-person narrators in fiction are constantly drawing attention to themselves and it works beautifully, so it's not a simple rule like keep the narrator invisible.

So: does this have an established name, in philosophy of mind or in literary theory or wherever? I've heard people say 'transparent prose' but I'm pretty sure that means unobtrusive style, which is about sentences, not about this. It's also not the unreliable narrator thing (my guy can be perfectly honest and still get in the way) and not breaking the fourth wall (he never addresses anyone). If there's a real concept somewhere I want to know what it takes the phenomenon to actually be and where it stops -- does it apply to third person, to film voiceover, to that thing where you forget you've been driving for twenty minutes beacuse the driving self just went quiet? And if there is no name, coin me one, but with edges: what counts, what doesn't, and where the borderline cases are. A bare label with no carving will just annoy me.
