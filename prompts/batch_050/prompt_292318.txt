Write a satirical op-ed, roughly 600 words, in the voice of a committed dialetheist — a philosopher who holds that the Liar sentence ("this sentence is false") is both true and false — complaining bitterly that the philosophical establishment refuses to take his view seriously. The humor must come from his being scrupulously internally consistent rather than from making him sloppy: he cannot flatly reject a claim without being prepared, in principle, to accept it as well; he must affirm that the law of non-contradiction has governed rational inquiry for millennia and that it is false; he must insist that his position does not commit him to accepting everything, and explain patiently that his paraconsistent logic blocks explosion; and when confronted with the revenge Liar ("this sentence is not true"), he must handle it with the same deadpan confidence rather than quietly smuggling classical bivalence back in. At some point he should accuse a critic's rebuttal of resting illicitly on disjunctive syllogism, and he should sincerely announce his intention to write a letter to the editor refuting this very op-ed, which he fully endorses. The irony should stay completely deadpan — no winking, no confession that the view is absurd — and the piece should be genuinely funny to a logician who will notice exactly which moves are being made, while remaining readable and amusing to a layperson who has never heard of truth-value gluts.
