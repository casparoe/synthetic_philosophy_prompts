The diagnostics I already have, so don’t just rename one: actors’ self-description, i.e. whether people at the time used the label or an equivalent for themselves; institutional discontinuity, i.e. whether universities, courts, guilds, churches, journals, tax systems etc. actually reorganised around the boundary; genealogical suspicion, i.e. checking when and why later historians coined or popularised the label; causal-bundle coherence, i.e. whether the label picks out a cluster of changes that tend to rise and fall together rather than one cherry-picked thread; and predictive/compressive value, i.e. whether the periodisation helps you guess new facts better than a neutral timeline or some rival carving. Is there a sixth diagnostic that is genuinely different from these — not a mixture, not a special case, and not merely “ask whether the sources survive unevenly”?
