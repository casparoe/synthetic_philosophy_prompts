I am designing a graduate seminar unit on weakness of will, and before we read anything I want a specification: the list of desiderata that a theory of akrasia must satisfy to count as adequate, given that our purposes are (a) explaining how clear-eyed action against one's own better judgment is so much as possible, and (b) guiding the design of commitment devices for ordinary people, not saints or addicts. Please write the requirements themselves, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, rather than a survey of existing theories.

The specification should at minimum engage with the following pressure points, and should add any I have missed. First, the theory must say what distinguishes a genuine case of akrasia from a mere last-minute change of mind or a revision of one's evaluative judgment, and it must handle the awkward consequence that the better the agent is at rationalizing, the fewer akratic actions there apparently are. Second, it must reckon with hyperbolic discounting: if preference reversals near the point of reward are just what time-inconsistent valuation predicts, decide whether acting on the near-term preference counts as akrasia at all, or whether the theory must locate the failure elsewhere, for instance in the agent's failure to see the pattern across occasions. Third, it must take a stand on whether akrasia is essentially synchronic, essentially diachronic, or both, and on what that implies for precommitment: is binding oneself a cure for akrasia, an avoidance of it, or itself a symptom? Fourth, it must accommodate two cases I want treated as data, not edge cases: a person who succumbs to temptation while fully narrating to herself that she shouldn't, and a person who acts against her better judgment with no felt temptation at all, out of something more like listlessness or inertia; a theory that only fits the first should say what it is a theory of instead. Fifth, the theory must say whether there can be second-order akrasia, failing to deploy the commitment devices one has judged one needs, and whether that is the same phenomenon iterated or a different one. Sixth, it must be consistent with the truism that sometimes acting against one's better judgment is a good thing, since our better judgments are sometimes wrong, without collapsing into the view that akrasia is never a failure.

For each requirement, state the motivating consideration, whether you take it to be non-negotiable for any theory serving purposes (a) and (b), or a preference of one camp, and say which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate accounts. If any subset of the desiderata turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, for instance if honoring the hyperbolic-discounting data makes the clear-eyed case inexplicable, flag that explicitly, because an inconsistent specification is itself the most interesting possible result. End by noting, in a sentence or two each, how the two most familiar families of approaches fare against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria.

Please write the response as continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points or numbered lists, no bold text, no tables, even though the subject is a list of requirements.
