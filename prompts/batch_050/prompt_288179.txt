I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of cognitive science deciding how to spend the final eighteen months of my dissertation, and I would like a defended agenda rather than a survey. The thread running through my work is sphexishness: the phenomenon, familiar from the digger wasp that mechanically re-runs its nest-inspection routine each time its paralyzed prey is moved back to the same spot, of behavior that looks purposive but turns out to be rigid. My dissertation so far argues that sphexishness is best understood not as the absence of flexibility but as flexibility of the wrong kind. I introduce a distinction between what I call output-plasticity, where an agent varies what it does, and index-plasticity, where an agent varies which features of the situation it treats as relevant in the first place. On my view a system can be lavishly output-plastic and still be sphexish, because its index of relevance never changes.

Against that backdrop, which open questions in this area deserve priority, and in what order? The candidates I see are these. First, whether the philosophical moral drawn from the wasp depends on the entomology at all, given that the standard anecdote has been questioned and the wasp may be less rigid than advertised; if the example collapses, does anything in the debate about mechanical rigidity actually change? Second, whether large generative models, which display enormous output-plasticity, are usefully diagnosed as index-rigid, and what a genuine test for that would even look like rather than a suggestive intuition pump. Third, whether compatibilist accounts of free will that define freedom as responsiveness to reasons covertly inherit the same distinction, so that an agent who responds to reasons but never revises what counts as a reason would be free on those accounts yet sphexish on mine. Fourth, whether index-plasticity can be made empirically tractable for comparative cognition, coming in degrees and admitting of measurement, or whether it is doomed to remain a thought-experiment label.

I care less about the final ranking than about its defense. State your criteria explicitly: how many other questions in the area are downstream of each candidate, how tractable each is within roughly eighteen months for a philosopher without a laboratory, and how much each answer would actually move the free-will debate. Where the criteria pull apart, explain the trade-off rather than papering over it, and say what finding over the next couple of years would move each item up or down your list.
