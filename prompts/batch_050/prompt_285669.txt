I’m making a workshop handout that sorts AI safety proposals by what they would still do if the model were already misaligned, not by whether people call them alignment or control. Build a decision tree starting from: assume the system may be strategically trying to get dangerous capability X while hiding that it wants X. Each branch should be a question someone could answer without safety jargon: would we detect trouble before or only after an irreversible act, does the defense depend on the model not realizing it is being tested, must humans understand the reason or merely block the effect, and does it degrade gracefully after being fooled once or twice. Use two coined diagnostics: verification asymmetry, meaning bad plans may be easier for the model to find than for us to certify, and sabotage radius, meaning how much damage one undetected defection can do. Put common approaches as leaves only by their answers: capability limits/boxing, monitoring/tripwires, evals/red-teaming, interpretability, scalable oversight/critique, training-time motivation shaping, honeypots/canary tasks, defense-in-depth. Mark empty answer-combos and say whether they look incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored; flag leaves the tree cannot separate, especially evals vs red-teaming and oversight vs critique. Keep it to 10-14 branch questions, then show a five-line classification of this new proposal: an untrusted model may only suggest code that a weaker verified model can refute if unsafe.
