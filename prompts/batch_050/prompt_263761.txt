So I TA'd an undergrad game theory course for two years and I just stumbled onto this old Scientific American stunt from the 80s that broke my brain a little. Some editor ran a lottery where readers could mail in as many entries as they wanted, and the prize was something like a million dollars divided by the total number of entries received. And there was a related thought experiment where 20 people each get to send one postcard, and if exactly one person sends one they win a prize, otherwise nobody gets anything. The guy behind it had this concept he called "superrationality" -- roughly, if everyone in the game is perfectly rational and symmetric, you should reason "whatever I conclude, they'll all conclude the same thing, so I should pick whatever I'd want everyone to pick." In the postcard case that leads you to flip a weighted coin and only send it with probability 1/20, so that exactly one person ends up sending in expectation.

Here's my problem. Every time I try to formalize this in the tools I actually know, it falls apart, and I can't tell whether that's because the idea is incoherent or because my tools are missing something. So: restate the superrationality prescription as carefully as you can in standard game theory vocabulary -- mixed strategy Nash equilibria, symmetric equilibria, correlated equilibria, rationalizability, expected utility, the whole toolbox. For each move in the reasoning ("I'm symmetric with them, so my decision is evidence about theirs, so I should choose as if I were choosing for everyone"), tell me what the honest translation is.

And then the part I actually care about, the residue report: which parts translate cleanly (my guess: the symmetric mixed equilibrium of the postcard game is just... the superrational answer, and the guy reinvented Nash without knowing it?), which parts you had to distort to fit (the "my choice controls theirs" step seems to violate the causal independence assumption baked into non-cooperative games -- is that just evidential decision theory sneaking in?), and which parts have no expression at all in equilibrium concepts. Is superrationality a real third thing beyond Nash and correlated equilibrium, or is it confused, or is it just a nice name for a symmetric equilibrium selection rule? Give me a verdict, not a both-sides shrug.
