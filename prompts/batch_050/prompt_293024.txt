I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about how skilled practitioners relate to their tools. I keep noticing a pattern I lack the vocabulary for. When a touch-typist writes, the keyboard is somehow absent from her awareness -- she thinks about the sentence, not the keys -- yet the moment a key sticks, the keyboard becomes vividly present as an object with parts, weight, and defects. Then, once she learns to strike the faulty key at an angle, it recedes from awareness again, defect and all. The same thing seems to happen with software, and even with the body: I never notice my glasses until they smudge. Is there an established concept for this oscillation -- a tool's disappearance in fluent use, its emergence on failure, and its re-disappearance once a workaround becomes habit? I've heard the smooth half of this loosely described as flow or muscle memory, but those don't seem to capture the failure or the re-absorption. I'd like a committed answer: name the single concept you judge the best fit, explain how it differs from the nearest rivals and whether it covers the re-disappearance phase, and if nothing established covers all three, say so plainly and coin a term with a definition and boundary cases.
