I teach an undergraduate course on historical sociology — about 24 students, one 75-minute session. Design an in-class game that lets participants generate, rather than hear about, the dynamics at the heart of Peter Turchin's structural-demographic theory: elite overproduction and popular immiseration jointly driving a society toward instability. The game should feature a fixed, small number of elite positions, a mechanism that produces aspiring elites faster than positions open up, and incentives that gradually push insiders from cooperation into factional competition with one another and with counter-elite challengers. Players should not be told in advance what the game is modeling; the crisis (rising conflict, declining cooperation, state insolvency) should emerge from their own choices. Include roles and rules, simple materials, a payoff scheme using chips or points rather than money, a round-by-round timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments of play onto Turchin's concepts — elite overproduction, intra-elite competition, state fiscal strain, disintegrative phases. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and be frank about which features of secular cycles the game cannot reproduce (e.g., the generational timescale, the role of actual violence) and must be supplied in discussion.
