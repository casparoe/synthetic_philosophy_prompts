At a party last weekend two friends of mine got into it over whether economics counts as a science, and it's been bugging me ever since because I genuinely couldn't tell who was winning.

Ana's opening shot was that econ fails the demarcation test: its core claim, that people respond to incentives, can't be falsified. Whatever people do, the economist invents some incentive that explains it after the fact -- someone gives to charity and it's a "warm glow," someone refuses a raise and it's about status or identity. She said Popper made exactly this complaint about Marxists and Freudians: a theory that fits everything predicts nothing.

Daniel pushed back that economics makes risky predictions all the time, like demand curves sloping downward. Find a good where people buy more when the price goes up and the theory's in trouble.

Ana said that's actually her favorite example, because when that kind of behavior did show up (people buying more of some staple foods as prices rose), economists didn't abandon the theory -- they carved out an exception about income effects overwhelming substitution effects. That's the immunizing move in a lab coat.

Daniel said hold on, astronomers did the same thing when Uranus's orbit didn't match Newton's predictions: instead of ditching the theory they posited an unseen planet, and then they found Neptune. Protecting a theory by adding an auxiliary hypothesis is sometimes exactly what good science looks like.

Ana said the difference is Neptune was independently checkable -- you could point a telescope at the predicted spot. The added incentive or reshaped utility function isn't checkable except through the very behavior it was invented to explain, so the two cases only look alike.

Then Daniel kind of shifted ground and said fine, maybe the real complaint isn't falsifiability but independent testability, and since plenty of respectable science patches theories with ad hoc fixes when the data misbehaves, maybe falsifiability was never the right line between science and pseudoscience in the first place.

Ana said ok, then what makes astrology worse than astronomy? And Daniel said those are actually different sins: astrology makes testable predictions that keep failing when anyone bothers to check, Freudian-style stuff dodges tests altogether, and economics is some third thing in between -- and that "unfalsifiable" is a lazy label that lumps all of it together. She said that was a dodge, he said it was a diagnosis, and then they both looked at me like I was going to settle it and I had nothing.

Can you go through the arguement and say who's right at each step? Like: is "people respond to incentives" really unfalsifiable in the way Ana says, does the Neptune analogy actually help Daniel or does Ana's telescope distinction defuse it (and is that distinction as clean as she makes it sound), did the Giffen good exchange go to her or was Daniel's original point still standing, and does his late move -- maybe falsifiability isn't the demarcation line after all -- undermine his own earlier defense or is it a seperate point? Also curious what you think both of them are missing.
