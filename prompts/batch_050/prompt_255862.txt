Within the debate over origin essentialism -- the thesis, associated with broadly Kripkean intuitions, that a person could not have originated from a different sperm and egg than the ones they in fact came from -- several questions remain genuinely contested. Among them: whether origins are essential in whole or only in part (could you have come from the same sperm and a slightly different egg, or from the same gametes fertilized a day later?); what metaphysically grounds the necessity, if anything does, beyond the bare intuition; how origin essentialism interacts with the non-identity problem in population ethics, where choices about who exists seem to undermine complaints about harm; whether the thesis should extend to non-biological entities such as artifacts, nations, or works of art; and what the epistemology of these modal judgments could be, given that we have no empirical access to the relevant counterfactuals.

Suppose you are planning a one-semester graduate seminar devoted to this debate, aimed at students who will go on to write dissertations in metaphysics or applied ethics, and you can devote serious attention to only three of these open questions (or others you judge more important). Which three should make the cut, in what order should the seminar take them up, and why? I am less interested in the list itself than in the reasoning: for each question you include, explain how many other questions in the debate depend on its resolution, how tractable it is with the standard tools of analytic metaphysics rather than requiring empirical input the field lacks, and what its downstream consequences would be outside the seminar room -- for instance, for reproductive ethics, donor conception policy, or theories of personal identity. Also say explicitly which of the listed questions you would exclude and defend each exclusion: is it overrated, already settled, a pseudo-question, or simply downstream of something else? Where two questions compete for a slot, state the tiebreaker you are using. Please write your answer in American English.
