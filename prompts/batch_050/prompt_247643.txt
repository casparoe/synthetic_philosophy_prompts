I TA an intro metaphysics seminar (about 18 undergrads, one 75-minute session) and we're up to the identity of indiscernibles. Rather than present Black's two-sphere universe as a thought experiment on slides, I want them to run into the problem physically. My rough idea: everyone gets one of two genuinely indistinguishable objects, a screen goes up, a swap either happens or doesnt, and then they have to bet -- with justification -- on whether what they ended up with is the same object they started with. Build this out into a full activity: exact rules and materials, what I should and shouldn't be able to see as facilitator, a second round where they get to secretly mark an object so they can feel the difference that makes, and a debrief that lands them on Black's thought experiment and the bundle-theory vs haecceity dispute without me naming anything up front. Also flag what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (students palming marks, someone insisting the objects differ microscopically) and which feature of Black's argument the game can't actually reproduce, since it's really about what we can track rather than what's metaphysically possible.
