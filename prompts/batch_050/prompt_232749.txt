So I'm a third-year taking a course right now on the Holocaust, uniqueness and modernity — we've done Bauman, the Historikerstreit, the whole uniqueness-vs-comparability fight — and at the same time I sit on the editorial board of our student newspaper, which is where this stops being abstract for me. We get a steady drip of op-eds and letters, maybe two or three a month, that reach for the Holocaust: factory farming comparisons, border policy comparisons, someone comparing campus speech rules to 1933, the occasional genuinely thoughtful historical analogy. And we get angry complaints whichever way we rule. At the moment we just argue in meetings for an hour and whoever is most tired loses. I want to replace that with an actual written procedure the board can run a submission through before we publish or reject.

Here's the hard part I want it to grapple with, using the stuff from my course rather than just vibes. From Bauman I take it that part of what matters is that the Holocaust ran through ordinary modern mechanisms — bureaucracy, rationaization, distance between decision and consequence — which suggests analogies at the level of *mechanism* might sometimes be legitimate even when the scale of suffering is wildly unequal. From the Historikerstreit I take it that comparing in order to relativize or normalize is a specific failure mode, and that treating the Holocaust as a reusable unit of moral weight does something bad even when the comparison is structurally apt. So the procedure needs to seperate things like: is this an analogy about how a system works, or an equation of how bad two things are? Is the Holocaust being used as evidence or as a rhetorical trump card? Does the piece need the comparison at all or would it survive with it cut?

So: write me the procedure. Ordered questions an editor can actually ask of a draft in maybe fifteen minutes, what counts as a passing answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a clear no, where it routes to our faculty advisor because it's a values call not a checklist call, and which distinctions from the uniqueness debate have to survive translation into checklist form and which you're dropping. Then run it on three realistic submissions — the factory farming one, a border-family-separation one, and a "campus speech code is like the Nuremberg Laws" one — and make sure at least one verdict comes out against what I'd want, since I'm vegetarian and my instinct is to wave the farming piece through. End with what the procedure can't settle no matter how well it's designed.
