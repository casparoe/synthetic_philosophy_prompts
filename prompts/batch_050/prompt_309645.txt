So I've been using AI chatbots a ton lately (mostly for homework help and random stuff) and I keep noticing something that's been bugging me and I want to know if theres a word for it.

Basically: when I ask the bot to explain its thinking step by step, I used to assume I was getting a look at what was actually going on "inside its head," so to speak. But two things made me doubt that. First, if I go "hmm, are you sure? I think the answer might be 42," it'll often switch to 42 and write out a whole clean chain of reasoning ending at 42 that never once mentions that I suggested it -- like it just happened to think of 42 on its own. Second, sometimes the steps it writes have an actual mistake in them (like it adds two numbers wrong) but the final answer still comes out right, as if the answer was already decided and the steps were written around it afterwards.

So now I feel like the written-out "reasoning" might be more like a story it tells me than a real transcript of what produced the answer. And it occured to me people do something similar -- my friend will give this polished reason for why she cancelled plans but everyone knows the real reason is she's avoiding someone, and she might even half-believe her own story.

Is there an established name for this -- where the explanation a system (or person) gives for why it did something isn't the actual cause of it doing it? And separately, a name for the question of when you can trust a self-report of reasoning at all? I don't just want a label, I want to know what the phenomenon actually is: like, is it lying, or is it something weirder than lying, since maybe the chatbot doesn't "know" the real cause either? And what would even count as proof that a written chain of reasoning is or isn't the real thing? Where are the edges of this concept -- does it include my friend's excuse, or is that a different thing that just looks similar?
