Here's my half-formed idea I'd like turned into a proper proposal. The standard takeaway from Thomson's lamp (and Benacerraf's reply) is that the infinite sequence of switchings fully determines the lamp's state at every time before the limit, but simply doesn't settle what happens AT the limit, so there's no paradox, just an incomplete description. Fine. But then I notice that other supertasks don't work like this: the bouncing ball that completes infinitely many bounces has its position fully fixed at the limit by continuity, and the urn paradoxes where you add and remove balls seem to give conflicting answers depending on whether you track cardinality or the individual balls. Basically: is Benacerraf's "incomplete description" response itself incomplete, because it lumps together cases that are importantly different?
