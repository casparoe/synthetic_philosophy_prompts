So I've been voluntold into giving one session of the med school's ethics module next term. Third-year medical students, ninety minutes, and the topic the course director assigned me is moral testimony. These are smart students but they have zero philosophy background, and they will arrive assuming that of course you can just ask someone whether something is wrong — they literally have an ethics consult service they'll be paging in a year.

I don't want the lecture written out, I want the plan for it. Specifically:

The order the pieces have to come in. My instinct is that before the puzzle can even be felt as a puzzle, they need to be fully comfortable with how much ordinary knowledge is just taken on trust (geography, whether their taxi driver knows the route), so the weirdness shows up as an asymmetry rather than as general skepticism. But tell me what else has to be in place, and what depends on what. In particular, I think there are two puzzles people constantly run together — why testimony seems not to settle a moral question, and why deferring seems like a defect in the agent even if it did — and I want the arc to keep them seperate without ever using jargon like 'moral understanding' until late.

The one example that carries the session. I was going to use a student who asks a thoughtful friend whether eating meat is wrong and just accepts the answer, but I'm worried it drags the room into the vegetarianism debate itself. Tell me what the carrying example must have and must lack, and whether mine survives that.

The misconceptions, in the order this audience will generate them, and where in the sequence each gets headed off. I'm expecting at least: "this is just about whether moral experts exist," "the claim is that testimony gives you no evidence whatsoever," and — this is the med-student special — "so you're saying we should shut down ethics consults." The last one I want to absorb, not deflect.

Finally: one end-of-session question that shows whether they got the distinction between the two puzzles and not just the vibe, and what I should openly punt on at this level.
