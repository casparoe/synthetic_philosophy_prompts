Proposition: by 2075, someone will have produced a derivation of the Born rule from the bare Everettian formalism -- unitary dynamics plus decoherence, with no independent probability postulate smuggled in -- that most non-Everettian specialists in quantum foundations accept as valid. Note the bar: not merely that Everettians themselves are convinced (they mostly claim this already), but that people with no prior commitment to many-worlds concede the derivation goes through. My colleague reckons this is near-certain, something like 0.85, on the grounds that the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic programme has basically done the job and the remaining resistance is sociological. I'm much lower.

Give me a number, but decompose it first. A probability with one line of justification for each, then how they combine into your final figure. Then tell me which sub-question your number is most sensitive to, and what would shift it most -- a particular theorem, a consensus survey, a clean refutation of envariance approaches, whatever.

If you think my decomposition loads the dice against my colleague, say so and re-cut it. I'm more interested in where the disagreement actually lives than in winning.
