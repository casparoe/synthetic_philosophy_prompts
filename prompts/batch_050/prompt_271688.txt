For a graduate seminar I have been asked to circulate a short statement of my views on moral testimony so the group can pressure-test it before we meet. Here is the statement in full:

(i) Testimony is a generative source of knowledge in most domains: when a reliable speaker sincerely tells me that p, and I have no defeaters, I can come to know that p without independently verifying the speaker's grounds. This is how I know most of what I know about history, geography, and science.

(ii) Moral deference — coming to believe a moral claim purely on someone's say-so — cannot yield moral knowledge, no matter how reliable the speaker is. Something about the moral case is different, though I have not yet worked out exactly what.

(iii) Moral expertise exists: some people (professional ethicists, for instance) systematically reason better about moral questions and arrive at true moral beliefs more reliably than laypeople.

(iv) I myself know various moral truths — for example, that torturing animals for amusement is wrong — and I hold these beliefs largely because of how I was raised and what the people around me say, not because I have worked out the justifying reasons for myself.

(v) What matters for moral credit is understanding rather than mere belief: an agent who does the right thing because she grasps why it is right deserves more praise than one who does the same act solely because someone trustworthy told her to, even if their behavior is identical.

(vi) If two agents both truly believe that some act is required, the one who can explain why it is required has something epistemically better, but both count as knowing that the act is required.

Please audit this set of commitments rather than give me a survey of the debate — I do not need references to the literature, just an analysis of the positions as stated. In particular: (a) go through the commitments and identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which pairs only appear to be in tension but in fact cohere once the distinctions I am already drawing are applied consistently; (b) for each genuine tension, grade its severity and describe the cheapest revision that would resolve it, including what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set; (c) note any case where two apparent tensions turn out to share a single root, so that one revision handles both; and (d) conclude by telling me which single commitment, if dropped or weakened, would restore the most overall coherence. I am not signaling which commitment I would rather give up, so please do not assume the repair has to spare any particular one.
