I'm prepping for my phil bio reading group (we've been doing the units of selection stuff and now moved on to fitness) and I got asked to write down my "position statement" on the propensity interpretation so everyone can attack it. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I'd like to know if the package is actually coherent before I get shredded:

(1) Fitness is a propensity, not an actual outcome -- it's something like an organism's objective chance of leaving a certain number of offspring, so two genetically identical beetles have the same fitness even if one gets squished by a falling branch on day one and the other leaves fifty descendants. (2) Fitness differences causally explain evolutionary change: when a trait goes to fixation, part of the explanation is often that its bearers were fitter, and this is a genuinely causal-explanatory claim, not a redescription. (3) The principle of natural selection -- roughly, that fitter types increase in frequency -- is a substantive empirical generalization about how populations actually behave, not something true by definition. (4) Drift is real, and in small populations it can swamp selection, so fitter types sometimes decrease in frequency and even go extinct. (5) Fitness is not an intrinsic property of the organism but a relation between organism and environment; the same genotype can be fitter in one environment and less fit in another. (6) Since propensities aren't directly observable, in practice the only way we ever measure or estimate fitness is by counting actual reproductive success in actual populations.

I can already feel some friction here but I can't quite pin down where the real problems are. Like, does (6) drag me back into the tautology objection that (1) was supposed to save me from? And is there a conflict between (2)/(3) and (4), or is that one fine? Please go through the set and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me what I'd have to give up and what that would cost. If there's one revision that cleans up the most damage, flag that explicitly -- but don't just tell me to abandon the propensity interpretation wholesale, that's the thing I'm trying to defend.
