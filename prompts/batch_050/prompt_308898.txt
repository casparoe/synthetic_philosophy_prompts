In my game theory elective we went over the strategy-stealing argument: in a game like Hex, the second player cannot have a winning strategy, because the first player could open with an arbitrary move and then simply adopt the second player's supposed strategy as their own, treating the extra mark on the board as irrelevant. Our teacher summarized this by saying it proves the first player always wins in Hex. Two things bother me about this. First, if the proof really establishes that the first player wins, why has no one ever written down what the winning moves actually are, even on moderately sized boards? Shouldn't a proof that something wins come with, or at least lead to, the way of winning? Second, chess also has a first player, so it seems like White could use the same trick against any winning strategy Black might have. Does strategy-stealing therefore show that chess is a win for White, and that high-level chess is just a matter of whether White executes correctly? If not, what exactly is different about chess, and what precisely did the Hex argument prove in the first place?
