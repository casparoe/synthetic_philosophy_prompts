I am a PhD student in decision theory preparing a seminar problem on imprecise probability, and I want a case that cannot be solved by reciting the usual Ellsberg moral. Act as an expert in formal epistemology and robust decision-making. Think carefully, but do not show private chain-of-thought; instead give a brief design rationale, the scenario, and then a verification.

Construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. The setting must be realistic and current: a hospital antimicrobial stewardship committee choosing one policy for a narrow patient cohort, not a casino, urn, trolley, demon, or perfect predictor. The committee’s uncertainty is represented by a credal set over two unknowns: probability that a new protocol prevents resistant infection, and probability that it triggers a rare interaction. Use a small explicit credal set, for example four to six vertex distributions, so a reader can compute without specialized software. Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph of at most 180 words, with all numbers needed inside that paragraph.

Engineer the case so three clearly defined rules deliver three different verdicts from the same credal state, without merely stipulating the verdicts. Rule one: security-first maximin, maximizing worst-case expected clinical utility over the whole credal set, must choose the conservative standard protocol. Rule two: E-admissibility must exclude that same conservative protocol as inadmissible, while leaving at least two options. Rule three: define a centroid-regret rule as follows: compute the centroid of the credal set, compute expected utility relative to the centroid, compute worst-case regret over the credal set relative to that centroid benchmark, and choose the option with the lowest positive regret only if its centroid-expected utility is within a stated tolerance of the best centroid-expected utility; otherwise choose the centroid-best option. This rule must choose the new protocol. The specifications are jointly satisfiable only if the geometry of the credal set does real work: maximin must be driven by a bad vertex, inadmissibility by a tradeoff across vertices, and centroid-regret by the set being asymmetric around its own barycenter.

After the scenario, provide: a compact table of acts by vertex and relevant aggregates; exactly which inequalities imply each rule’s verdict; the single load-bearing feature of the credal set; what verdicts follow if that feature is removed while keeping expected utilities fixed; and the strongest objection from someone who thinks the committee’s credal set is illegitimate because it was produced by merging expert models rather than by evidence. Avoid citations, bibliographies, and quotations; paraphrase any standard idea in your own terms. End by stating whether the case shows genuine rule sensitivity or only an artifact of an ill-posed credal set, and defend that verdict in five sentences or fewer.
