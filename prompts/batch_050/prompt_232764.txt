For the past six years I have co-directed a small family foundation with my sister; we inherited an endowment from our grandfather with the vague instruction to "do good with it," and we make all grant decisions jointly. The problem is that we disagree deeply about what doing good means. I am convinced that the money does the most good sent abroad, where a few thousand dollars can fund malaria treatment or vitamin supplements that quite literally save lives. My sister believes we have a special duty to the town our family came from and wants the money going to its library, its youth programs, and the restoration of the old theater downtown. I don't think her view is stupid or selfish, and she doesn't think mine is — we just weigh things differently, and neither of us has talked the other out of it in six years of trying.

So far we have handled this the way most people handle it: every year we split the budget roughly in half, and every year both halves feel like a watered-down version of what either of us would actually choose. Last week she proposed something different. Instead of splitting every year's budget down the middle, we take turns: in odd years I direct the entire budget toward global health, in even years she directs it all toward the town. She pointed out that we'd each get twice as much of what we most want, half as often, and that the total good done — by each of our own lights — would be greater than under the compromise, since the big grants we each prefer do more than two small ones. When I ran the numbers she seemed to be right. And yet something about the proposal makes me uneasy in a way I can't articulate. It feels less like we resolved a disagreement about what goodness requires and more like we agreed to stop having one — as though my convictions about saving lives were just a preference to be traded against hers, like swapping shifts at a job.

So my question is this: is there actually anything wrong with her arrangement, or is my unease just squeamishness I should get over? If trading turns out to be better for both of us by our own standards, is the "meet in the middle every year" approach we use now really more principled, or is it just a habit that costs lives and lets the theater roof leak? And if you think the trade is permissible, I'd like to know where, if anywhere, you'd draw the line — would it still be fine if instead of alternating years we were simply auctioning off individual decisions to whichever of us wanted them more intensely? I'd rather not give you my own half-formed answers to these questions; I'd like to hear yours first.
