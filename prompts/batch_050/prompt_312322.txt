Next month I am presenting a paper at our department's jurisprudence and political theory colloquium, and I need a preparation sheet for the question period. The paper defends a thesis about constitutional precommitment, and it hangs on a reading of the Sirens episode in Book 12 of the Odyssey. Here is the passage I put on the first slide (Samuel Butler's 1900 prose translation, which I use because it is out of copyright), where Circe instructs Ulysses:

"Therefore pass these Sirens by, and stop your men's ears with wax that none of them may hear; but if you like you can listen yourself, for you may get the men to bind you as you stand upright on a cross piece half way up the mast, and they must lash the rope's ends to the mast itself, that you may have the pleasure of listening. If you beg and pray the men to unloose you, then they must bind you faster."

My thesis is that this passage, read carefully, contains the three design requirements of a legitimate constitutional precommitment, and that most actual entrenchment practices satisfy at most two of them. Specifically: (i) the bound party is not deafened — Ulysses keeps his hearing, so the binding protects him from acting on a temptation he is still permitted to experience and assess, which I argue is the right model for a people binding itself against panics and demagogic moments rather than against information; (ii) enforcement is delegated to agents who cannot hear the pleas — the crew's waxed ears do the real work, since Ulysses retains his voice and his begging is anticipated — and I argue this maps onto why judicial enforcement of entrenched norms requires institutional insulation from the political branches, not just textual supremacy; (iii) the response to revocation attempts is settled in advance ("then they must bind you faster"), which I use to argue that eternity clauses and heightened amendment thresholds are defensible only if paired with an analogue of the episode's built-in exit condition — the crew unbinds him once they are past the Sirens — such as sunset triggers or defined emergency termini, since Ulysses' contract is self-terminating and constitutions, notoriously, are not.

The audience: roughly twenty people. Two public-law scholars who favour strong judicial review but are on record against eternity clauses; a democratic theorist very much in Waldron's mould who will press the dead-hand and disagreement points; a philosopher of action who works on weakness of will and thinks the entire "second-order preference" literature on precommitment is confused because it treats persons as divisible time-slices; and one classicist who contributes nothing to the argument but will police my use of Homer and will know, for instance, that Circe's framing of the binding as optional ("if you like you can listen yourself") complicates my self-control reading. Nobody else will have read the paper.

Prepare me for the objections I will actually get from this room — not the generic objections to precommitment I could find in a textbook. I want the sheet in British English, organised under exactly these headings, in this order:

**The Objections, Ranked by Damage** (roughly 900 words): about eight objections, ordered from most to least dangerous to the thesis, each phrased the way the particular audience member would actually put it — the Waldronian's objection should sound like a Waldronian, the philosopher of action's should sound like someone who thinks akrasia talk about constitutions is a category error — and each followed by a two-minute reply and a candid note on where that reply is soft.

**What I Must Concede in the First Five Minutes** (roughly 200 words): the points I should grant before anyone raises them, including whatever the classicist is most likely to catch me on about the passage itself, so that I lose those points on my own terms.

**The Reply That Has to Land** (roughly 150 words): the single exchange on which the paper's survival depends, given this audience's composition.

**The Question I Am Hoping Nobody Asks** (roughly 150 words): identify it, explain why I cannot currently answer it, and give me the least damaging available holding response.
