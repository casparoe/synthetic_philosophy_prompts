ok so context: last week I finally finished the novel my sister's been nagging me to read for like two years, and one of the main characters dies near the end and I full-on cried on the train. told my roommate about it and he hit me with the whole "you know she's not real right, there's literally nothing to be sad about" line, which, rude, but also?? he's kind of got a point and it's been bugging me since.

I went down a bit of a rabbit hole and apparently philosophers have been arguing about this for decades (the "paradox of fiction" -- how can you feel real emotions for people you know don't exist, why don't we ever try to save them, why does it still wreck you on a re-read, etc.). Instead of just reading more about it I want to process this the only healthy way I know, which is by making it a bit.

Write me a deadpan parody FAQ page from a fictional government agency -- something like the "Office of Fictional Bereavement" -- that processes citizens' grief over fictional characters. Around 600-700 words. Format it as actual FAQ questions with official, bureaucratic answers, maybe with a form reference or two. The jokes should come from the agency quietly taking positions on the real philosophical puzzles and running into the real problems with those positions, e.g.:

- eligibility requirements (does my grief count if I only watched the adaptation? what if the character was based on a real person?)
- a claims assessor explaining why your tears are "quasi-tears" and therefore not covered
- why nobody at the agency ever intervenes in the fictional events themselves, with an increasingly flimsy justification
- repeat-viewing policy (is your third rewatch a new loss or a pre-existing condition?)
- the adjudication of whether the applicant's sadness was "rational" in the first place

It should stay completely straight-faced the whole time, and someone who's read Walton or Radford should recognize what's being skewered without the piece ever name-dropping them. Readable and funny for everyone else too.

Just start with the FAQ itself -- title and all -- no intro, no "here you go", no commentary after.
