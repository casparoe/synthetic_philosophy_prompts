So a bit of context: I write music on the side (little ambient/electronic stuff, nothing serious) and last month I hit the worst creative block of my life. A friend who's way too into Douglas Hofstadter told me my problem is that I think creativity is about summoning something from nothing, when actually it's knob-twiddling: you take a theme and figure out which of its properties are "slippable" — the knobs — and then you twiddle them. Change the key, change the meter, swap which hand has the melody, play it backwards, stretch it, shrink it, and at some point one of the variations is genuinely new and good. His claim, which I've sort of talked myself into over the past few weeks, is that this isn't just a useful trick for when you're stuck — it's basically ALL creativity is, even the stuff that feels like genius. Einstein was twiddling knobs on Newton. The Beatles were twiddling knobs on Chuck Berry. When something feels like a bolt from the blue, it's just that the person can't see which knobs they were turning, maybe because the twiddling happened unconciously or across analogies so distant you can't trace them.

Now here's what I want: what would count AGAINST this view? Because it has a suspiciously convenient quality — any example of creativity I bring up, my friend just redescribes as knob-twiddling, and if I can't find the knobs he says they're hidden, and that starts to feel like the view wins no matter what. So: what would a case of genuine non-variational creativity even look like, such that a knob-twiddling partisan would have to concede it? Are there specific kinds of creative acts — inventing a new genre rather than a new song, say, or mathematical proofs, or jokes — where the model strains? Which defeaters would a committed defender of the view themselves accept as real threats versus wave away? I'd like them sorted from fatal to merely awkward. And be honest about the falsifiability thing: if "the knobs are just hidden" can absorb literally any counterexample, does that make the view empty, or is it more like a research program where the burden is to actually FIND the knobs in each case (the way Copycat-style projects tried to do for analogy)? Also, separately: is there a difference between knob-twiddling as a description of how creative output is generated and as a description of how we RECOGNIZE something as creative — because it strikes me the view might be much stronger as a theory of the second than of the first, and conflating them might be where the unfalsifiability sneaks in.
