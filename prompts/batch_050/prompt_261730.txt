So I was reading about the sphex wasp thing -- the one where it keeps dragging the cricket to the burrow entrance, goes in to check, and if you move the cricket it just starts the whole routine over, forever. The point people take from it is that what looks like clever goal-directed behavior can actually be a totally fixed mechanical loop, no real "deciding" going on.

But here's what I'm stuck on. Human behavior is also just neurons following fixed physical laws, same as the wasp's. Our routines are longer and more complicated, sure, but if the laws are fixed either way, then isn't our so-called flexibility just a more elaborate fixed routine? So it seems like the sphex argument actually shows there's no real difference between the wasp and us -- determinism means we're all sphexish deep down, and talk of free will is just us not seeing our own program clearly.

Is that right? And if not, what experiment would tell the difference between a genuinely flexible agent and a very complicated sphex?
