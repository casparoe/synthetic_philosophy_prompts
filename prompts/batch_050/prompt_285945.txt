Yesterday evening, a friend and I spent three hours arguing about whether a committed conspiracy theorist can ever be argued out of their position, and we ended up reading passages of Wittgenstein's On Certainty aloud to each other without settling anything. To prepare for the rematch, I would like a taxonomy of the main positions in contemporary hinge epistemology, built as a decision tree rather than a list.

Please construct the tree so that it begins at a shared starting commitment — Moorean gestures such as "here is a hand" do not, by themselves, answer the skeptic — and proceeds through branch nodes phrased as questions a reader could answer without knowing which position they lead to. Good candidate questions concern whether hinges are propositions at all; whether they can be known, justified, or doubted; whether the certainty attaching to them is epistemic or merely enacted in action; whether hinges can change, and whether such change counts as rational revision or as something closer to conversion; and whether parties relying on different hinge sets can ever settle their disagreement by argument. The leaves should include, at minimum: the non-propositional animal-certainty reading associated with Moyal-Sharrock; Wright's view of unearned rational entitlement; Pritchard's quasi-fideism; Coliva's extended rationality; and the naturalist reading descending from Strawson. For each leaf, add one sentence stating what the position commits its holder to say about my original dispute — that is, whether reason-giving can ever reach across a deep disagreement. Note any combination of answers that yields an unoccupied position, and say briefly whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate.
