I'm a journalist covering science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm working up a long feature on whether consciousness research has actually explained consciousness or has instead, under that heading, explained something easier -- attention, report, information availability. Global workspace theory will be my main exhibit, with its critics (the "overflow" camp, higher-order theorists) in the wings. Before I write a word, I need the yardstick.

Write me the specification: the desiderata a theory of consciousness must meet before we are entitled to say it explains phenomenal experience rather than cataloguing its functional accompaniments. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it -- blindsight, Sperling-style partial-report experiments and the debate over whether experience outruns what we can access, inattentional blindness, dreams, the methodological awkwardness that nearly all our evidence about experience is gathered through report. Where relevant, say what distinction the requirement forbids collapsing (for instance, between a state's being poised for global broadcast and its being experienced at all).

Then do three things with the list. First, mark which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable and which are the house rules of one research program -- I'm thinking of demands like "the theory must be mechanistically implementable" or "it must not posit phenomenality that no experiment could detect" -- and explain what each camp gains by treating its house rule as mandatory. Second, tell me whether some plausible subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: my worry is that requiring a theory to be testable only through subject report sits badly with requiring it to explain experiences that, if the overflow argument is right, outrun report. If that tension is real, say which requirement has to give and what conceding it costs the science. Third, identify the single desideratum that excludes the most current contenders.

You may briefly note where global workspace theory stands against the finished list, but keep that short -- the criteria, and the cases behind them, are what the piece hangs on. The purpose to fix the standard against: a theory adequate to tell my readers whether headlines announcing that science has explained consciousness are warranted.
