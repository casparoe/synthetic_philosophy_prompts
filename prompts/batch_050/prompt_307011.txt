In my department's animal ethics reading group, a colleague defending the moral considerability of insects leaned on an analogy I keep hearing elsewhere: consciousness is like a light on a dimmer switch, not a light on an ordinary on/off switch. Her argument ran as follows. Just as a lamp's brightness varies continuously from fully dark to fully bright, sentience varies continuously from humans down through mammals, birds, fish, insects, and perhaps beyond; there is no threshold below which the light simply goes out. Therefore moral status is also a matter of degree, and a bee's suffering counts for a small but nonzero amount, roughly in proportion to how bright its light burns -- so insect welfare deserves real, if modest, weight in our calculations, and creatures like thermostats, being at effectively zero brightness, deserve none. An opponent replied that dimmer switches are the wrong picture entirely: what matters morally is whether the light is on at all, and the dimmer metaphor papers over the only question that counts.

Please audit this analogy rather than referee the wider debate. Structure your response in the following sections, in this order:

1. **The correspondence table** (roughly 200 words): a table mapping each element of the source domain -- the bulb, the electricity, the brightness level, the slider, the room being lit, the point at the bottom of the slider's travel where the switch clicks off -- onto its intended counterpart in the target domain of minded creatures, noting wherever my colleague's use leaves a mapping implicit or unresolved.

2. **Where the analogy holds** (roughly 150 words): the respects in which phenomenal consciousness plausibly does vary along something like an intensity dimension, and what work this does for the argument.

3. **Where it breaks** (roughly 350 words): the disanalogies, with the load-bearing ones distinguished from the incidental. Consider at least: whether a single global dial can represent a mind with many independent channels (pain, hunger, vividness of perception, self-awareness) that may not dim together; whether "degrees of consciousness" conflates wakefulness, richness of content, and intensity of valenced experience, which may come apart across species; and the awkward fact that a real dimmer switch clicks discretely off at the bottom -- whether the analogy thereby quietly concedes the threshold it was deployed to deny, and where on the slider insects and thermostats would even sit.

4. **What the analogy smuggles in** (roughly 150 words): features of the source domain the argument imports without defense -- for instance, that brightness is a scalar measurable on a single shared scale across all lamps, and that moral weight scales linearly with it.

5. **Verdict** (roughly 100 words): whether the proportional-moral-weight conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it; if the former, state the strongest defensible version of the conclusion, and if a better analogy exists for what my colleague actually wants to say, offer it in a sentence or two.
