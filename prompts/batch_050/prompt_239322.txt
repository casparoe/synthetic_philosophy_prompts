About four years ago I moved into a cohousing community with 25 other households. The founding deal was simple: everyone bought in for the same amount, everyone got an equal-sized unit, and we split shared costs equally. One of our neighbors, Tomas, is a fantastic carpenter. Since then, basically every household has at some point hired him — bookshelves, a new staircase, kitchen renos — always at prices both sides agreed on, and honestly everyone was happy, he's that good. He's now saved up way more than the rest of us, maybe three or four times what a typical household here has.

Last month the majority put forward a proposal: the big upcoming projects (solar panels on the shared hall, fixing the roof) shouldn't be split equally anymore but funded "according to means" — in practice that means Tomas would cover something like a third of it on his own. He's furious. His point, as I understood it at the meeting, was that every cent of the gap between him and us came from deals we all entered freely, at prices we thought were fair, and none of us would take back. If we can just vote afterwards to even things back out, he says, then those deals never really meant anything, and "equal starting shares" plus "everyone deals freely" was always a contradiction in disguise. The majority's answer is basically: come on, he can afford it, it's for everyone's benefit, nobody's taking his house away, and the community is not a marketplace anyway.

The vote is in two weeks and I keep flip-flopping. A few things complicate it for me: Tomas has used the communal workshop rent-free for years, and he originally learned carpentry at evening classes that the community budget partly paid for — does that weaken his complaint, or is it beside the point? And would it change anything if, instead of a one-off levy, we announced in advance a standing rule like "from now on, savings above X get taxed 20% for shared projects"? Is that different in kind, or just the same thing done slower? I don't want a both-sides summary, I want to know how to vote. Tell me whether Tomas is right, which of the complicating details actually matter philosophically and why, and — if you think he should still pay up — what you'd actually say to him at the meeting when he makes his "the deals meant nothing" speech.
