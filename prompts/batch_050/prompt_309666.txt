Last night I got into a long, unresolved argument with a friend about actual infinity, and I'd like the dispute replayed in a vocabulary where I can actually tell who was making contact with reality.

My position was broadly Cantorian: infinite sets are legitimate completed objects, Hilbert's hotel is not a paradox but a straightforward illustration that a set can be equinumerous with a proper subset of itself, and the diagonal argument shows that some infinities are strictly larger than others. My friend holds a finitist or at least potentialist position: infinity is only ever a process that can be continued, never a finished totality; the hotel is incoherent because it smuggles in a completed infinity under the guise of an ordinary physical object; and talk of different sizes of infinity is formal symbol-pushing that we mistake for discovery.

Here's the catch: I'm a computer scientist, not a mathematician or philosopher, and I suspect half of our disagreement was about words neither of us could cash out. So: restate the entire debate in the vocabulary of my discipline — types and their inhabitants, lazy versus strict evaluation, streams and infinite data structures, enumerability, computable versus non-computable functions, diagonalization as it appears in the halting problem and in proofs that no program enumerates the reals-as-streams. Reconstruct my friend's potentialism as precisely as possible in those terms (presumably something like: only lazily generated, indefinitely extensible structures exist; the "whole stream" is never a value). Reconstruct my position in the same terms (presumably something like: we can still quantify over and reason about totalities we can never fully evaluate, and Cantor's argument is a real theorem about them).

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the classical debate translate cleanly into the CS framework? Which parts survive only with distortion — for instance, does the hotel thought experiment, with its physical rooms and arriving guests, do any work that a bijection between ℕ and ℕ\{0} doesn't, and does that work evaporate in translation? Most importantly: is there anything in my friend's insistence that a completed infinity is metaphysically, not just computationally, suspect that simply has no counterpart in the vocabulary of types and streams? Where there is residue, say whether you take it to be a limitation of the CS framework — a real distinction it is too coarse to express — or a sign that the untranslatable claim was less clear than it sounded. I want to know whether my friend was pointing at something my conceptual toolkit can't see, or gesturing at nothing.
