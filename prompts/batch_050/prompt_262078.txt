My partner and I co-manage a small charitable fund, about 40k a year, and we've hit the classic wall: I mostly care about near-term global poverty, she mostly cares about far-future stuff like AI risk, and we both think the other's cause is roughly fine but way less important than our own. Someone suggested we "just compromise" and it made me realize nobody can tell me what that actually means here. Half the fund each? A joint portfolio we both rate as okay? A coin flip each year for the whole pot, which we both weirdly seem to prefer by our own lights to most 50/50 splits, since I'd rather have a 50% shot at 40k of malaria nets than a guaranteed 20k I think is badly spent?

So: what would an actual theory of compromise between value systems have to get right? I want the requirements list, not a recommendation. Give me the desiderata any adequate account of when moral compromise is rational (and when it's genuinely better than fighting it out or randomizing) must meet -- the cases it has to handle, like ones where the mushy middle option is worse for *both* parties than a lottery over the pure options, the distinctions it can't collapse (trading across decisions vs. splitting one decision, compromising between values vs. between people who hold them, compromise vs. just capitulating to whoever's more stubborn), and the results from elsewhere it can't contradict (whatever the decent results in bargaining theory and social choice are). For each requirement give me the motivating case, not just the label.

Two more things. First, tell me which requirements are actually load-bearing and which are just one camp's prejudices dressed up as criteria -- I suspect "the compromise has to be a single coherent plan" is doing some sneaky work somewhere. Second, if some subset of the obvious desiderata turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, say so and show it, because that's the kind of result I'm actually hoping for. Optionally score the main naive proposals (split the pot, maximize sum of normalized utilities, bargaining-solution style approaches like Nash's) against the list in a short table, but keep that brief -- the criteria are the deliverable. Purpose of the theory, if it matters: good enough for two stubborn people with real money on the line, not a pub debate.
