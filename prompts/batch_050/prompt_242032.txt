I've been a reader of LessWrong and adjacent blogs for years, and there's one move in the LLM-understanding discourse that I've seen so often I've started to treat it as basically settled. The move goes like this: language models are trained to predict the next token in a stream of text, and so whatever else they do, they are fundamentally modeling statistical regularities among words rather than anything about the world the words are about. There's that well-known thought experiment about an octopus tapping into an undersea telegraph cable and learning to imitate human conversation from form alone, which I've always taken to establish the point pretty cleanly. Prediction over symbols gives you the surface shape of language, not the referents. A system trained this way might produce a perfect simulation of someone describing how to fix a carburetor while having no model of carburetors at all -- just very good correlations between the tokens "carburetor" and its neighbors.

My question is practical rather than definitional. Suppose I accept this picture, which I do. What is the minimal modification to the training setup that would let a model actually acquire the underlying world-model rather than just the statistics of its textual shadow? I've seen proposals floated -- multimodal training on images and video, reinforcement learning from outcomes rather than imitation, embodied agents, tool use with feedback -- and people argue about which of these would "ground" the system. But these all seem like variations on a theme, and I'd like to understand the deeper structure: what property does a training signal need to have so that optimizing for it produces understanding instead of parroting? Is there a principled reason next-token prediction lacks that property, or is it more of a contingent shortfall that enough scale might eventually patch over?

I ask partly because it bears on alignment concerns I've followed for a long time. If current models are, at bottom, very elaborate pattern-matchers over human text, then the danger profile is different from what it would be if they internally represented and reasoned about the world -- and the right interventions presumably differ too. So: what experiment, benchmark, or architectural test would let us tell whether a given model has crossed the line from modeling text to modeling the world, and what would it take to push one across?
