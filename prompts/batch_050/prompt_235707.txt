I've been thinking on my own about the idea that some categories are "socially constructed," and I've written down where I've landed. I'd like to know whether these views actually fit together.

1. Money is completely real even though it's nothing but collective agreement. A dollar has value only because we all treat it as having value, and that's enough to make it real -- you can pay rent with it.
2. Race is socially constructed, and that's exactly why it isn't real. There are no races; there's only the persistent illusion of races.
3. Since race isn't real, the long-term goal should be to stop categorizing people by race altogether. Once we stop sorting people into races, racism will no longer have a target.
4. Gender is partly socially constructed, but a person's own sense of their gender is fully real and valid no matter what anyone else believes about it.
5. Constructed categories can do enormous real damage, so calling something constructed doesn't mean calling it harmless.

Some of these feel like they might be in tension with each other, but I can't tell which ones, or how seriously. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and for the real conflicts, what's the least costly revision to make -- and what does it cost? Please order the problems by how severe they are rather than treating them all as equal.
