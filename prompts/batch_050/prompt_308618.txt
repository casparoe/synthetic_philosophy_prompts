I've been reading LW since like 2012 and I keep meaning to write down my decision theory positions properly, so here goes. Tell me if these actually hang together:

(a) CDT is dead. One-box on Newcomb, and more generally your "decision" is just the output of an algorithm that might be instantiated or predicted in lots of places, so you should pick the output that's best for the algorithm, not for the local physical act.

(b) Updatelessness is the right way to cash that out: act on the policy you'd have wanted to commit to ex ante, before any observations. So yes, I'd pay in counterfactual mugging even though I get nothing causally.

(c) On Sleeping Beauty I'm a halfer. The thirder arguments and the betting-odds arguments have never moved me; self-locating uncertainty just seems different from ordinary uncertainty.

(d) I'm a strict Bayesian about credences: conditionalization, full stop.

(e) Logical truths are necessary, so assigning them probability less than 1 is incoherent. "Logical uncertainty" is a confused label for ordinary empirical uncertainty about whether some proof exists.

Which of these genuinely conflict vs. only look like they do? Rank the tensions by severity and for each tell me the cheapest revision and what it costs. Don't flag everything just to be thorough.
