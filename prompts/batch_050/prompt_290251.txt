Here are my instincts on licensing parents, and I want the principle behind them. A couple with no childcare training should be allowed to have a baby, even if training would help. Someone convicted of seriously abusing a child should not automatically get custody of another child, but should be able to earn back that chance through strong evidence of rehabilitation. It seems unfair to screen adoptive parents while imposing nothing on biological parents merely because they are biological. Poverty, disability, political extremism, or an unpopular religion should not count against someone. But parents who deliberately keep a child isolated, medically neglected, or unable to imagine a life outside their community seem different. I also think the state may license control over a child more readily than it may license conception itself. My tentative princple is that intervention is justified only when parenting predictably inflicts serious harm or closes the child’s future options, not when it merely lowers expected child welfare. Does that rule capture my cases, or do some need revising? Please lead with your bottom-line principle in the first sentence, then give the reasoning without preamble or restating the question. Use American English.
