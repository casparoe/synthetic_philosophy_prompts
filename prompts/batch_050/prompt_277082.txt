Can you write a detailed spec for what any theory of the line between manipulation and legitimate persuasion must deliver? Make it a numbered list of desiderata, with the case or conceptual pressure behind each one, and say whether each requirement is genuinely non-negotiable or mostly the prejudice of a particular camp. The theory should at least handle: emotionally powerful protest art; a friend using guilt to stop someone driving drunk; a charity timing appeals for right after a disaster; a store using music and layout to encourage browsing; a campaign microtargeting people’s private insecurities; an openly disclosed casino feature designed to encourage loss-chasing; a skilled debater who gives only true arguments but frames things so the audience never considers the strongest alternative; and an AI fitness coach that has learned exactly which notifications make a user feel ashamed.

Please make the criteria cut finer than influence is bad when it is irrational. I want requirements covering things like bypassing versus engaging agency, deception versus mere nondisclosure, pressure versus inducement, transparency, consent, vulnerability, power asymmetry, the influencer’s intentions, the target’s welfare, and whether someone would resent the method after learning how it worked. Flag any desiderata that seem jointly unsatisfiable -- for example, respecting actual choices while allowing autonomy-based paternalism, or making intention essential while still covering recommender systems that have no settled intention. Finally, briefly test four broad approaches against the list: rational-capacity bypass accounts, covertness accounts, authenticity accounts, and bad-motive or harm-based accounts. Don’t just rank them; I mainly want to know which single requirement does the most work in ruling contenders out, because thats what I’ll organize the essay around.
