I am preparing a seventy-five-minute seminar for graduate students in public policy on Nick Bostrom’s singleton hypothesis. The students understand collective-action problems but have no background in philosophy of agency or longtermism. Design the explanation I should give, rather than giving the explanation itself.

The session should turn on this passage from Bostrom’s “What is a Singleton?”:

“Yet another way in which a singleton could form is through convergent evolution, e.g. if it turns out that all sufficiently advanced individuals or cultures come to accept fundamentally the same values or goals. These common values in combination with all the individuals and cultures that embrace them would then be an ‘agency’ in the broad sense intended here, and it would constitute a singleton.”

Plan the sequence in which the relevant ideas must be introduced. In particular, determine what must already be clear before the quoted passage will seem coherent rather than metaphorical: the relevant level of decision-making, the difference between an agency and an agent, Bostrom’s two capacities of preventing challenges and controlling major features of a domain, and the distinction between the concept of a singleton and the stronger hypothesis that humanity will or should eventually produce one.

Choose one carrying example for the seminar. It should let students test whether convergent institutions can count as a single highest-level agency without treating every instance of widespread agreement as a singleton. Explain why that example is better than beginning with either a world government or an omnipotent artificial intelligence, since those examples encourage opposite distortions.

Map the misconceptions I should expect, including at least: that a singleton must have a single mind, leader, or decision procedure; that it must micromanage everything within its domain; that moral convergence is automatically sufficient for singletonhood; that “highest level” means national rather than terrestrial or cosmological; and that singletons are by definition authoritarian, stable, desirable, or permanent. For each misconception, identify where in the sequence it will arise, how I should head it off, and whether the quoted passage helps cause or correct it.

Include two borderline cases for students to classify—one involving decentralized enforcement of a shared norm and one involving several mutually constrained institutions—and specify what each case should reveal. End with one diagnostic question that would show whether the class has understood the breadth of Bostrom’s category without collapsing it into ordinary agreement or ordinary government. Also state what technical material I should explicitly leave out at this level.
