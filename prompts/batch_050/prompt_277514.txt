I’m making a quiz for a philosophy reading group that just finished a couple of sessions on Mīmāṃsā. People kept running three different claims together: that a cognition is presumptively valid simply because it arises, that testimony is an independent source of knowledge rather than a disguised inference from a speaker’s reliability, and that a text can carry authority without having an author. I want a 9-question quiz called Which theory of testimony do you actually hold?, but the questions should sort people by their answers to concrete cases, not by whether they like the label Mīmāṃsā.

Use invented cases rather than direct questions about doctrine. For example: directions from a stranger, a voice produced by a machine with no discoverable designer, a ritual instruction preserved for centuries, a report contradicted by what you can see, a statement that only becomes trustworthy after a community has used it successfully, and a text whose author is unknown but whose transmission history is unusually stable. The answer options should describe an epistemic policy in plain language: accept it unless defeated, check the source first, reduce it to perception and inference, trust the tradition as a whole, suspend judgment, and so on. No option should say intrinsic validity, extrinsic validity, reductionism, or apauruṣeyatva; the diagnostic work should happen underneath the vocabulary.

Build the scoring key as a small logical space, not just as a list of famous positions. Score three axes seperately: whether validity is intrinsic or earned; whether testimony is independent or reducible; and whether authorlessness changes a text’s epistemic status. Then locate at least six leaves in that space, including the full classical Mīmāṃsā package, a view that accepts intrinsic validity for perception but not testimony, a testimony-independent reliabilist view, an authorless-text foundationalist view that rejects intrinsic validity, a skeptical view giving testimony no default authority, and any coherent hybrid your tree naturally generates. For every leaf, give a name, a one-sentence explanation, and its most awkward commitment. If two positions cannot really be separated by the quiz, say so rather than inventing a spurious distinction.

End with a short note on which combinations are empty and why: incoherent, merely unattractive, or historically possible but not part of the classical Mīmāṃsā debate. Please don’t turn this into a bibliography or require Sanskrit philology; the point is to make the epistemology of authorless testimony visible through ordinary and slightly weird cases.
