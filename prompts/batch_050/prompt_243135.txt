A thought experiment, then some homework. Imagine a country — call it Veridia — that scraps its parliment and hands all legislation over to an AI system, Lex. Some facts about how Lex runs things:

- Lex maintains a single unified legal code, roughly 40 million pages and growing. No human has ever read more than a tiny sliver of it.
- Everything in the code is genuinely general: no names, no singling out individuals, just rules of the form "if conditions X obtain, consequence Y follows."
- The code is internally consistent — Lex prunes contradictions nightly — and it never demands anything a citizen couldn't actually do.
- It changes constantly. Lex revises rules every few hours in response to new data. Nothing is retroactive in the strict sense (past acts keep whatever legal status they had when performed), except that Lex sometimes "harmonizes" old contracts with current law, adjusting which of their terms are still enforceable.
- Nobody reads the code. Instead there's a free public API: you describe what you plan to do, and it tells you the legal consequences, as of right now, with perfect accuracy. Enforcement is fully automated and matches the API's output 100% of the time.

For reference, Fuller's eight desiderata are, roughly: generality; publicity/promulgation; prospectivity; clarity; consistency between laws; not requiring the impossible; stability over time; and congruence between official action and the declared rules.

Here's what I want from you:

1. Score Veridia on each of the eight desiderata, 0–10, with a few sentences defending each number. Some of these are genuinely hard — is the API "promulgation"? Is a code no one can read "clear"? Is nightly revision with accurate prediction "stable"? Commit to real numbers; no hedging with 5s across the board.
2. Then the bigger question. Raz thinks the rule of law is basically about law being capable of guiding conduct, and that it's a negative virtue that says nothing about justice. Waldron thinks it crucially involves respecting people as agents who can understand, contest, and argue about the rules binding them — courts, hearings, reasons. Does Veridia have the rule of law or not? Citizens there are arguably guided *better* than in any real country (perfect prediction, perfect congruence), but they relate to the law the way they relate to the weather: something you check and adapt to, never something you understand or challenge. Reach a verdict: is Veridia a triumph of the rule of law, a technically-compliant gutting of it, or evidence that Fuller, Raz and Waldron were all quietly assuming something (guidance through internalized rules) that isn't actually essential to the ideal?
3. Finally, put your money where your mouth is: if you had to choose between living under Lex and living under a mediocre human legal system that scores 4-6 on all eight desiderata, which do you pick — and does that choice match your verdict in part 2? If there's a gap between the two answers, explain what's doing the work in it.
