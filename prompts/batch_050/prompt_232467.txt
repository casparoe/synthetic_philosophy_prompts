I host a small philosophy podcast and my next guest is a philosopher who's built a career defending Taurek's anti-aggregation view from "Should the Numbers Count?" -- roughly, that if you can save one stranger or five others but not all six, you aren't required to save the five, and flipping a coin between the groups is a fair way to decide. Draft eight to ten interview questions that make him do more than restate the position. I want at least one question on the complaint that the coin flip doesn't actually give the five equal respect, since each of them can object that their claim to be saved was never weighed at all; one on his distinction between the best outcome and what anyone is owed, and whether it survives cases where the chooser has a role -- a ship's captain or a triage doctor -- that seems precisely to require counting; one on whether the view really refuses tradeoffs all the way down (one death versus a million headaches -- no aggregation there either?); and one on whether, given that he concedes he'd save his own friend over five strangers, the position quietly becomes a license to save whoever you happen to like. For each question, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give, and end the set with a question about what hypothetical case, if any, would make him abandon the view.
