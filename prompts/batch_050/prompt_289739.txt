For my dissertation chapter on the pessimistic meta-induction, I want the specification in hand before I survey the literature, so that I can assess realist replies against a fixed standard rather than letting each author define adequacy for themselves. Write the desiderata an adequate realist response to the PMI must meet: the historical cases it must be able to accommodate rather than explain away (Fresnel's ether is the one I care about most, since his novelly successful predictions issued from a posited entity we now reject), the distinctions it must draw with some precision (which theoretical components carried the predictive success versus which were idle, and how we identify that without hindsight), the results it must explain rather than merely permit (novel predictive success above all, but also why later theories so often retain the mathematics of their predecessors), and the commitments it cannot take on (a criterion of selection that only works retrospectively would disqualify it, since the point of realism is to license confidence in our current theories now). For each desideratum, give the consideration that makes it a genuine requirement rather than a prejudice of one camp, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — for instance, whether explaining Fresnel-type cases is compatible with a principled, non-ad-hoc restriction of belief to retained structure. Tell me which single desideratum does the most work in eliminating extant replies. Then, briefly, score the main contenders — entity realism, structural realism, and deployment-style selective realism — against the list. Throughout, keep separate what is settled (for example, that the bare appeal to theory succession establishes nothing either way) from what remains genuinely contested, including the historiography of Laudan's cases, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
