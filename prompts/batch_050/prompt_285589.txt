Write a villanelle whose form enacts the central worry about iterated distillation and amplification: that an instruction faithfully executed by a slow, trusted process can drift out of alignment as that process is copied, compressed into a faster approximation, and then retrained on its own outputs, round after round. Someone familiar with this research agenda should be able to recognize the mechanism from the poem's structure alone; someone who isn't should still read it as a strong poem about transmission and loss. Please use American English.

Specifically:

- Keep the standard skeleton: five tercets and a closing quatrain, built on two refrains. Refrain one is the overseer's original question or charge; refrain two is the system's first answer to it.
- Villanelle convention demands that refrains return verbatim. Break that rule deliberately and minimally: each recurrence may change at most a word or two, so that no single repetition looks wrong, yet the refrain that arrives in the final stanza is no longer the line that opened the poem. This slow corruption should carry the idea of distillation loss compounding across iterations.
- Build the non-refrain lines of each tercet largely from fragments of earlier lines, recombined and repurposed, so that the middle of the poem visibly decomposes and reassembles its own material — sub-answers produced separately and stitched into a whole. At least one recombination should subtly fail: a fragment that fit its first context but not its second.
- In the quatrain, the two drifted refrains must appear side by side for the first time, and the poem must turn on whether the answer, as it now stands, still counts as an answer to the question, as it now stands. Let the poem take a position — resigned, hopeful, or undecidable — rather than trailing off.
- Do not mention machine learning, training, models, or anything technical. Choose a concrete surface subject in which compression across generations is natural — a song passed down by ear, a recipe copied from memory, a story retold by grandchildren — and let the form do the philosophical work unassisted.

Aim for roughly the canonical nineteen lines; if a line needs to break meter to register a failed recombination, that break should feel earned rather than sloppy.
