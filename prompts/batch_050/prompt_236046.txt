So I'm working on this campus novel and there's a scene I keep rewriting and it keeps coming out flat. Two of my characters, Mira and Dev, are grad students sharing an office, and at like 1am during a grading session they get into it about Pascal's mugging. In the book, a guy actually approaches Dev on the street earlier that day and says he's a traveler from a future with a population in the trillions, and unless Dev hands over the 40 bucks in his wallet he'll go back and arrange things so that a billion of those future people get tortured, and Dev just walks past him. Mira thinks Dev did the obviously right thing. Dev is rattled because he's the kind of person who defends expected value reasoning to the death and he can't fully justify to himself why walking away was fine.

Write the dialogue between them for me, maybe 500-800 words. Here's the thing though: I want both of them to actually be smart. Mira shouldn't be some hand-wavy normie going "that's absurd lol" — have her press the real pressure points, like whether the probability of the mugger's story can really stay fixed as the numbers he throws around get bigger, and whether the sheer size of the threat is itself evidence the guy is lying. And Dev shouldn't be a robot who immediately tries to Venmo the mugger — have him point out that Mira's move seems to prove too much, because we do sometimes act on tiny probabilities of huge stakes (asteroid detection, pandemic prep) and he wants to know what her principled line is that blocks the mugger but doesn't block those. Somewhere in the middle I want Dev to introduce a wrinkle from my novel's plot: the mugger offered to prove his credibility with one small verifiable demonstration first, and Dev is genuinely unsure whether that changes the math or whether it's still just noise.

The dialogue should feel like two tired people at 1am, interrupting each other, occasionally conceding a point and then clawing it back. I'd rather they end somewhere interesting and unresolved than have one of them "win" — though if one side naturally gets the last word, make it earned, and maybe have the other one privately land a jab that never gets answered. Oh and please don't have them cite papers by name, they're talking off the top of their heads.
