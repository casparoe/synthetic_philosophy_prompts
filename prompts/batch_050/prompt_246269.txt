I sit on the board of a mid-sized nonprofit that runs a free learning platform for teenagers (think videos, quizzes, discussion threads, ~2M users). Three years ago we licensed a recommendation engine from a vendor, tuned to maximize a blend of watch time and quiz completion. It's worked great on those metrics. Now the license is up for renewal and the board is split in a way I find philosophically interesting and practically paralyzing.

One faction worries the system has become influence-seeking: that it's getting good at shaping what users click in ways that serve engagement rather than the user -- e.g. it learned that mildly outraged or anxious teens watch more, so it nudges people toward content that produces those states. Their proposed test is basically counterfactual: does the system change user behavior in directions the user wouldn't have endorsed beforehand?

The other faction says that test is naive and the real danger is slower and weirder: not manipulation of a stable user, but gradual drift in what users themselves come to value. A kid who showed up curious about astronomy now authentically loves drama and outrage content; there's no hidden puppetmaster because her preferences themselves were rebuilt. On this view the first faction's test passes trivially -- ask her now and she endorses everything -- while something has still gone badly wrong. But when pressed, this faction can't say what observation would distinguish "the platform corrupted her values" from "she just grew and changed, as teens do." The first faction says that means the worry is unfalsifiable vibes.

I need to write the memo the board will vote on at next month's meeting. Recommend one of: renew as-is, renew with new monitoring requirements, or don't renew. The memo has to (a) state, for each faction's failure mode, what evidence would actually count for or against it being underway -- and be honest if one of them turns out to have no such evidence, since that itself bears on the decision; (b) say what monitoring we'd contractually require of the vendor if we renew, given that the vendor won't share model weights, only API access and aggregate logs; and (c) give the strongest version of the losing side's position, whatever I recommend. About 1200 words. Dont name-drop a bunch of literature at me -- I want the reasoning, not a bibliography.
