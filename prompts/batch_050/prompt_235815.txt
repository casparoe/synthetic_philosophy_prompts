I've been chewing on Molyneux's problem for a while now — the one where someone blind from birth, who can tell a cube from a sphere by touch, suddenly gets their sight, and the question is whether they could tell which is which just by looking, before touching anything. I know it goes back to some question a fella called Molyneux put to Locke centuries ago, and I gather people have actually tried to test it since with folks who've had sight restored, but I've mostly just been thinking about it on my own.

Anyway, my position is yes: the person would get it right straight away, or at least very fast. My rough reasoning: the shape you feel and the shape you see are the same shape. Touch and sight are like two different doors into the same room — once the visual system works at all, the geometry underneath has to be shared. Blind people clearly have a solid grip on space, corners, roundness etc., which suggests to me the concepts aren't locked to one sense.

So what I want to know is: what would count against me? Not a reading list or a survey of the literature, I have no access to journals and honestly wouldn't wade through them. I mean: what would the world have to be like for me to be wrong? Which findings would sink the view versus just dent it? E.g., if newly sighted people can't match shapes across senses for the first few weeks but then pick it up quickly, does that refute me, or can I absorb it as "the system needs calibrating"? If they never learn it, that's clearly fatal, right? What about messy in-between cases — people who had partial blurry vision as kids — do those count as tests at all, or is the original question set up so cleanly that no real-world case ever quite matches it? And be honest about my "two doors, one room" idea: what's the thing about how brains actually wire the senses together that would show it's just a pretty metaphor?

Rank the possible defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones I'd have to concede versus which I could legitimately shrug off, and tell me straight if I've built in so many escape hatches ("he'd get it quickly", "needs calibrating") that nothing could ever count against the view — because if so it's definately not as clever as it feels. Please answer in British English.
