In Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren (1930) Keynes wrote:

"My purpose in this essay, however, is not to examine the present or the near future, but to disembarrass myself of short views and take wings into the future. What can we reasonably expect the level of our economic life to be a hundred years hence? What are the economic possibilities for our grandchildren?"

Well, the hundred years are just about up. Write me an ironic op-ed of ~500 words, published in 2030 on the essays centenary, by a business columnist solemnly taking stock of the forecast. The world of the piece is basically ours: the wealth and the compound interest arrived more or less on schedule, but nobody works fifteen hours a week, and the columnist has to explain how a society this rich is still hustling. The jokes should come from the essay itself — his distinction between needs we feel in ourselves and needs we feel only because they lift us above the neighbors, his hope that greed would one day be pitied as a mild disorder, his worry that a leisured mankind wouldnt know what to do with itself. Every gag should be one the essay actually licenses, so a reader who's done the reading laughs rather than winces, and the irony stays deadpan, no winking at the camera. Start with the piece itself, headline first, no preface or explanation.
