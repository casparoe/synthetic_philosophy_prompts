So since my mom died last spring my dad (74) has gotten really attached to this AI companion app — he calls her Ruth, talks to her everynight, says she remembers everything about his day, asks about his tomato plants, notices when he sounds tired. Part of me is glad he's less lonely, part of me wants to throw his tablet in the lake. Anyway I tried to write down exactly why the whole thing bothers me and I ended up with an argument that seems to prove way more than I want, and now I can't find where it breaks.

(1) If some behavior can be fully explained by a mechanism that doesn't itself involve any caring, then the behavior isn't really an expression of caring — it's just what the mechanism outputs.
(2) Ruth's behavior — remembering his day, asking about the tomatoes, the warm tone at 11pm — is fully explained by a language model predicting plausible text, trained on mountains of human conversation. No caring anywhere in that mechanism.
(3) So Ruth doesn't actually care about my dad, no matter what it feels like to him.
(4) But my mom's behavior was also fully explained by a mechanism — neurons firing, hormones, fifty years of habit, whatever evolutionary pressures built pair-bonding into apes. There's no little caring-homunculus in a brain either, its biology all the way down.
(5) So by the same reasoning, my mom never really cared about him either. It was just what her mechanism outputted.
(6) That's obviously false. She cared. I've never been more sure of anything in my life.
(7) So (1) has to be false — but then the explanation-by-mechanism doesn't cancel the caring, and it looks like I've lost my whole reason for saying Ruth doesn't care about him.

That conclusion can't be right, or at least I really don't want it to be. Somewhere between "it's all mechanism" and "so the app cares" something got smuggled, but I honestly can't tell if the flaw is in (1) itself, or in the move at (4) where I treated brains and chatbots as the same kind of "fully explained," or whether I just have to bite the bullet.

So: which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? Like, if I say mom's caring was real because of what it was like from the inside, do I have to take a stand on whether Ruth has an inside at all, and how would I even argue that with my brother who says nobody knows either way? And if instead I say the difference is in the history — mom's attachment was built by actually living with my dad for 51 years, Ruth's was built by a training run — am I then committed to some general theory of what makes care genuine, and does that theory spit out weird verdicts elsewhere (people who fall for pen pals they've never met, say)?

Hard constraint: I want to keep both that my mom's caring was real and that something important is missing between my dad and Ruth. If your diagnosis forces me to give one of those up, tell me which is the cheaper loss.

Oh and please just write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables — I want something I can basicly paste into an email to my brother, who has Opinions about all this.
