So I retired from the physics department two years ago (condensed matter, nothing to do with consciousness) and like a cliche I've been filling my days with philosophy books. Lately I've gotten stuck on panpsychism, or pan-sentience, or whatever the right label is -- the idea that experience goes all the way down to the fundamental stuff. My old office mate thinks the whole topic is unfalsifiable nonsense and told me so over beers, and maybe he's right, but it occured to me that the real problem is I don't even know what I'd be asking of a theory here. Everyone seems to argue past each other because nobody states the entrance requirements.

So that's what I want from you: the desiderata. What would a theory of sentience at the level of fundamental physics have to deliver before we could even start asking the ethical question -- i.e., whether there is suffering down there and whether it matters? Spell out which puzzles it has to dissolve or at least address (the combination problem seems like one, causal closure seems like another -- physics sure looks causally complete, so what is experience *doing*?), which distinctions it must respect (I'm pretty sure "has experience" and "suffers" are not the same thing, and a theory that conflates them is dead on arrival, but tell me if I'm wrong), and which mundane empirical facts it has to stay consistent with -- anesthesia, brain lesions, the fact that hitting my thumb with a hammer hurts in a way that presumably my toaster's operations don't. I also want it to say *something* principled about where the morally relevant boundaries fall: if I split a rock, have I doubled the suffering in the world? If the quantum field has proto-experiences, does the vacuum itself have a welfare? A theory that shrugs at those questions strikes me as useless for ethics, however elegant it is metaphysically. For each desideratum, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which ones you'd consider genuinely non-negotiable versus which are just the hobby horses of one camp.

The thing I'd most value: flag any pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. I have a hunch that "take suffering seriously wherever it appears" collides with "don't let the inanimate world swamp the moral calculus," and that "experience must be causally efficacious" collides with closure, but I want someone to actually work this out rather than gesture at it. Then, briefly -- this should be the short part -- run the main contenders against the list: panpsychism proper, Russellian monism, something like integrated information theory, and the boring emergentist view that sentience only shows up in organized nervous systems. And since adequacy is relative to a purpose, let me fix mine upfront: I don't need the true metaphysics of mind, I need a theory good enough to tell me whether the ethics of matter is a real subject or a category error.
