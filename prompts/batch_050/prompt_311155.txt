Okay so I'm a novelist, about 70k words into a draft, and my critique partner keeps writing "this makes no sense, nobody acts like this" in the margins of my protagonist's big decision scenes. The annoying thing is the whole plot depends on a secondary character making a series of individually reasonable-seeming trades that leave her catastrophically worse off, and my partner says that's also unbelievable. This sent me down a rabbit hole about money pump arguments and intransitive preferences, and now I'm genuinely confused about the difference between a character whose preferences are *irrational* (cyclic, exploitable, someone could pump them for money) versus a character whose values just *change over time*, which humans do all the time and which seems like a weird thing to call irrational. Some of what I read suggests the money-pumpability is what does the diagnostic work, but then there are cases where you'd refuse a sequence of trades precisely because you can see where it leads, and I can't tell if that's the same phenomenon or a different one.

So here's what I want. Act as a decision theorist who also moonlights as a fiction editor, someone who gets both the formal stuff and what makes characters legible to readers. Think carefully about this, brainstorm the key distinctions first before you settle on a final answer, and then give me a reusable procedure -- basically a checklist or flowchart in words -- that I can run on any character's arc of choices in about fifteen minutes to answer: is this sequence (a) genuinely irrational in the money-pump sense, (b) rational but involving a change of values across time, or (c) conflicted at a single moment but not irrational. The steps should be in order, each step should say what evidence in the text settles it, and I need it to handle the diachronic wrinkle properly -- like, a step that asks whether the choices happen at one moment or spread out over months, whether the character could foresee the whole sequence, and whether new information arrived between choices, since I gather those matter a lot for whether it's a "pump" or just growing up. Flag which steps are judgment calls where the checklist can't do the work for me.

Tell me which of us the procedure sides with, and if it's my partner, say so.
