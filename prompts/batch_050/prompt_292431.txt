Last night I spent three hours arguing with my freind Jonah about Boethius on God's foreknowledge. Jonah's line was that eternity solves the problem: God doesn't know tomorrow before it happens, because for God there is no before; he timelessly sees all of history in a single present. My line was that this sounds neat but just relocates the fixity. If God timelessly and infallibly knows that I will write this email, then there's no possible outcome in which I don't, even if God's knowledge doesn't cause me to write it. Jonah accused me of confusing temporal priority with causal compulsion; I accused him of treating a change in God's location as a change in the modal facts. Around 2am we agreed that Boethius's distinction between simple and conditional necessity should settle it, and then realized neither of us could say exactly how.

I'm doing a student podcast episode where a classicist will play Boethius. Write me 10-12 interview questions, addressed to Boethius himself, that would help us figure out who was missing what. The questions should be natural to say aloud, but don't make them generic. For each one, include the reply I should expect from a competent Boethius, plus a follow-up that presses that reply instead of just moving to the next topic.

Please make the sequence cover at least these pressure points: ordinary prediction versus timeless seeing; whether the spectator analogy really fits when the divine spectator is also the creator; simple versus conditional necessity; whether my future action can genuinely be otherwise if God already knows it; whether freedom needs alternate possibilities or only the absence of compulsion; what happens if we say God would have known differently had I chosen differently; and why deliberation, advice, blame, or prayer still make sense. End with a question about what, if anything, could make Boethius admit that the eternity move does not actually solve the problem.

Don't let him answer everything by saying that time is our mode of knowing, not God's. Build the later questions so that this move gets harder to reuse. And please don't invent Latin phrases or quotations.
