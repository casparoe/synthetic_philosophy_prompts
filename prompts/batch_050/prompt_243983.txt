I direct an upper-level undergraduate seminar in the history and philosophy of economics ("Hume and Smith on Commercial Society"), and I am drafting this year's final examination. I would like you to write one essay question on Hume's monetary thought, together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

The question must turn on the following passage from David Hume's essay "Of Money" (Part II, Essay III of the Essays, Moral, Political, and Literary, first published in the Political Discourses of 1752). The passage may be quoted verbatim in the question itself and does not count toward any length limit:

"There seems to be a happy concurrence of causes in human affairs, which checks the growth of trade and riches, and hinders them from being confined entirely to one people; as might naturally at first be dreaded from the advantages of an established commerce. Where one nation has gotten the start of another in trade, it is very difficult for the latter to regain the ground it has lost; because of the superior industry and skill of the former, and the greater stocks, of which its merchants are possessed, and which enable them to trade on so much smaller profits. But these advantages are compensated, in some measure, by the low price of labour in every nation which has not an extensive commerce, and does not much abound in gold and silver. Manufactures, therefore gradually shift their places, leaving those countries and provinces which they have already enriched, and flying to others, whither they are allured by the cheapness of provisions and labour; till they have enriched these also, and are again banished by the same causes. And, in general, we may observe, that the dearness of every thing, from plenty of money, is a disadvantage, which attends an established commerce, and sets bounds to it in every country, by enabling the poorer states to undersel the richer in all foreign markets."

My requirements are as follows.

First, the essay question should be answerable in roughly 75 minutes (about 750 words) by a strong student in the seminar, and it must not be answerable by reciting the quantity theory of money or the price-specie-flow mechanism in the abstract. The students have read "Of Money" and "Of the Balance of Trade" in full. The question should force them to confront an apparent tension: elsewhere in "Of Money," Hume argues that a *rising* stock of money stimulates labour and industry during the interval before prices fully adjust, yet here he argues that the *dearness* produced by a plentiful stock of money is precisely what checks a rich nation's commerce. A good question will require them to explain whether Hume's position is coherent -- that is, whether the level of money and the rate of change of money can play the opposed roles he assigns them -- and it should push them to say something about whether the mechanism in this passage is the same as the self-correcting specie-flow mechanism of "Of the Balance of Trade" or a distinct argument about international competitiveness between rich and poor states. You may, if you find it sharpens the question, ask students to apply Hume's reasoning to a hypothetical: a low-wage, low-money kingdom today attempting to displace an established commercial power in foreign markets. What would Hume predict, and would the prediction vindicate him as a theorist of catch-up growth or expose the limits of a purely monetary explanation?

Second, provide a model answer of about 600 words. It should take a definite position on the coherence question rather than merely survey both sides.

Third, provide a marking scheme out of 20 marks, built around five must-hit moves, with the marks for each, that separates an A answer from a B answer by name: state explicitly which specific move an A answer must contain that a B answer will typically lack. For each must-hit point, add a one-line note on the characteristic way a weak or merely memorized answer goes wrong on it -- for instance, treating Hume as a straightforward quantity theorist who believes money is neutral in all respects, or conflating a high domestic price level with a balance-of-payments deficit without explaining the connection Hume actually draws. The scheme should also say how to credit an answer that defends the minority reading (that the two claims are in genuine tension and that Hume simply did not notice), provided it is argued rather than asserted.

Please present the deliverable in three clearly labelled sections: the question as it would appear on the exam paper, the model answer, and the marking scheme.
