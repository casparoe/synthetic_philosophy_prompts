I own a small coffee shop on a street with three other coffee shops. A couple years ago one of them started opening at 5:30am to catch the early commuters, so the rest of us matched it. Then someone launched a loyalty app, then someone slashed drip coffee prices, and now we're all open 16 hours a day, all running the same promos, and from talking to the other owners I'm pretty sure all of us are making less than before any of this started. Nobody's actually won customers either -- total foot traffic on the street is basically unchanged, we've just raised our costs fighting over the same pie.

Here's what I keep going back and forth on. I could just stop: go back to 8am-6pm, kill the promos. But when I game it out, that seems to hand the morning crowd to the others while saving me less than I'd lose. Or I could propose we all agree to scale back together, but there's no way to enforce it, and the moment anyones revenue dipped they'd have every incentive to quietly defect. Plus one of the shops is a franchise whose corporate parent can eat losses the rest of us can't, so I'm not even sure he'd want out.

So: why does it feel like we're all doing something individually reasonable that leaves everyone worse off? Is there any way out that doesn't rest on everyone just trusting each other? And is this actually the same kind of situation as, say, countries stuck in arms races or athletes doping, or does something change when it's money and survival on the line instead of prestige?
