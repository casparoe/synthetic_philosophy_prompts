You are a philosopher of mind and value theory with experience consulting for hospitals and health-technology designers. Before writing your analysis, spend a few sentences brainstorming the rival accounts of what unifies pleasure and pain, and how each would interpret a measurement practice; then write the analysis itself (roughly 700–900 words), followed by the structured summary requested at the end.

The situation is this. Clinical medicine runs on a single number. A post-surgical patient is asked to rate her pain from 0 to 10; a chronic-pain clinic tracks the same score over months; analgesics are dosed, studies are powered, and treatments are judged successes or failures on the movement of that one score. Wellbeing policy mirrors the practice from the positive side: national statistical offices ask citizens to rate their life satisfaction from 0 to 10, experience-sampling apps ping people to record how happy they feel right now, and the resulting averages are treated as a quantity that rises and falls like temperature. In both directions, the practice treats the badness of a migraine, the grief of a bereavement, the itch of a rash, and the frustration of a delayed train — and likewise the pleasures of a warm bath, a solved proof, vindictive triumph, and nostalgic reverie — as differing essentially in *degree* along one dimension, a dimension a person can report with an integer.

This is philosophically loaded, because the heterogeneity problem asks what, if anything, all pleasures have in common, and likewise all sufferings. The candidate answers are familiar: a shared phenomenal character (a hedonic tone that tinges otherwise different experiences), a shared functional role (pleasure is whatever motivates approach and repetition), a shared attitude (pleasure is whatever the subject, in some sense, wants to continue), or nothing at all beyond a family resemblance that our practical interest groups under one word. Each answer has a different implication for what the 0-to-10 scale could possibly be measuring.

Now the twist. The hospital network is considering replacing the single pain dial with a three-part instrument: (a) a sensory-intensity rating, (b) a rating of how strongly the patient wants the experience to stop, and (c) a rating of how much the experience interferes with activities the patient values. The designers insist the redesign is a neutral measurement improvement, not a philosophical statement.

Your tasks:

1. Reconstruct the implicit theory of the traditional single-number practice. Which of the candidate answers to the heterogeneity problem does it presuppose, and which does it merely sit well with? Be precise about the difference between what the practice strictly needs in order to make sense and what it is merely compatible with.
2. Say which views the single-number practice quietly rules out — for instance, would an attitudinal account, or a family-resemblance account, predict that the scale is systematically misleading rather than merely crude?
3. Assess the three-part redesign. Does it take sides on the heterogeneity problem — for example, by separating the felt intensity of pain from the desire that it cease, does it commit to those being distinct things that can come apart? Or can a proponent of each candidate theory accept the redesign as a purely practical refinement? Identify where the redesign, too, is philosophically underdetermined.
4. State what would have to change in clinical or policy practice if we fully adopted the view that there is no common hedonic currency — that the heterogeneity of pleasure and suffering is irreducible. Would measurement have to be abandoned, replaced by narrative reports, or reorganized around some other unit (capabilities, preference satisfaction, functional impairment)?

After the prose analysis, provide a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "implicit_theory_of_single_scale" (a string naming and briefly describing the presupposed view), "strictly_presupposed" (a list of claims the single-number practice cannot make sense without), "merely_compatible" (a list of views that would license the practice without being required by it), "ruled_out_or_embarrassed" (a list of views the practice sits badly with, each with a one-sentence reason), "redesign_takes_sides" (a boolean), "redesign_commitments" (a string explaining your boolean answer), and "if_no_common_currency" (a string describing the most defensible replacement practice).
