I'm a second-year PhD student in neuroscience (minoring in phil of science) and I'm trying to get a handle on the "new mechanist" literature — Machamer, Darden, and Craver and the stuff that's grown out of it. My advisor handed me a chapter from a science-studies book that sums up the MDC account like this:

"According to Machamer, Darden, and Craver, a mechanism is just a organized collection of parts whose causal interactions produce some phenomenon. Their headline claim is that there are no laws in biology, so biological explanation has to bottom out in describing these little machines — which is why mechanistic explanation is basically reductionist: to explain something like long-term potentiation, you have to break it down to the molecular level. On their view, once you've described the mechanism you've explained the phenomenon; the description just is the explanation. Critics have pointed out that 'mechanism' is never precisely defined, so the framework is circular, and that pretty much settles it."

I have a strong hunch this is mostly-right-but-off in a few spots, but I can't pin down exactly where. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me what someone working in this literature would accept, what they'd push back on, and what's just wrong? A few things I'm specifically unsure about:

- The "just parts and their causal interactions" bit — I vaguely remember the account making a big deal of "activities" as a separate category alongside entities, but I don't really get why that matters or what work it's supposed to do. Is dropping it a real distortion or a harmless simplification?
- The laws claim. Do MDC actually deny that biology has laws, or is it something weaker — like laws just not doing the explanatory work the covering-law model said they do? Those seem really different.
- The reductionism line. I thought Craver's later stuff on levels and constitutive relevance was supposed to be anti-reductionist in some sense, or at least friendly to multilevel explanation. Am I misremembering?
- The circularity objection at the end. "Mechanism is never precisely defined" seems odd given that MDC literally offer a definition. Even if the objection has some force, does "that pretty much settles it" follow from anything, or do the mechanists have a standard reply?

Also, bigger picture: the chapter's overall conclusion is that new mechanism is "just old reductionism in new clothes." If the summary gets fixed up, does anything of that conclusion survive, or does it depend entirely on the misrepresentations? Please answer in American English.
