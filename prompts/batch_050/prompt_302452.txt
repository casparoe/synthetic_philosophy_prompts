So I'm prepping a unit for my aesthetics seminar on photography, and I want to build a session around Antonioni's Blow-Up. The basic setup for those who haven't seen it: a fashion photographer in 60s London takes some shots of a couple in a park, the woman freaks out and demands the negatives, and when he develops and keeps enlarging the pictures he becomes convinced he's photographed a murder -- a hand with a gun in the bushes, a body under a tree. Then the prints get stolen, the body he later finds (or thinks he finds) disappears, and the film famously ends with him watching a mimed tennis game and kind of accepting the imaginary ball.

Read the film through the philosophy of photography debate about transparency -- roughly, Kendall Walton's claim that photographs are transparent, that we literally see the photographed object through the picture the way we see through a telescope or mirror, versus the Roger Scruton-style response that photographs are representations shaped by intention and convention, so we see a picture of things rather than the things themselves. Here's the thing I want the essay to dig into: Blow-Up seems at first like a drama FOR transparency. Thomas treats the enlargements as a window into what really happened in the park, and the whole plot depends on the photos being causally hooked up to a real event rather than being something he made. But the more he blows them up, the more the image degrades into grain, and the less clear it becomes whether he's seeing a corpse or projecting one -- so is the film actually an argument that transparency breaks down at the limit, that seeing-through-a-photo is continuous with interpreting it, even constructing it? Where does the film support each side, and where does it resist being co-opted by either? And what about the ending -- does the mimed tennis game suggest Thomas has defected from realism about what his camera showed him, or something subtler about how much of what we call seeing was always partly confabulation?

I'd like a reading that's fair to both the film and the actual philosophical positions (don't strawman Walton -- his view is about a causal/mechanical relation, not about photos being uninterpretable), names specific scenes, and ends by saying what Blow-Up contributes to the debate that the journal articles don't. Also, one worry I have: is it cheating to lean on the grain/degradation stuff, since that's arguably about the limits of resolution rather than about transparency per se? Tell me if you think that move is illegitimate. Answer in American English.
