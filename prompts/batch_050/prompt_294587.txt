Ok here's my attempt to figure out what I actually think about sweatshops, going by gut on cases. I keep saying "mutually beneficial" like that settles things and it clearly doesn't, so tell me what principle I'm working with, if any.

Cases:

1. A factory in a poor country pays $2/day. The next best option for the workers is subsistence farming that nets them less, and they line up for these jobs when openings appear. The factory owner could pay $3/day and still make money, just less of it. Feels: fine, or at least not the sort of thing anyone should be punished for doing.

2. Same factory, same wage, but the owner lobbied the local government to shut down a foreign aid clinic that was raising workers' outside options, specifically so he could keep wages at $2. Feels: clearly wrong, even though the wage and the workers' choices are identical to case 1.

3. A guy lost in the desert stumbles on a hiker with a water bottle. Hiker sells him the bottle for $500, which is everything in his wallet. The lost guy agrees eagerly. Feels: exploitation, wrong, even though the deal saved his life and he'd pay it again.

4. Same desert, but the hiker charges $10 for the bottle, which is a bit above what it cost at the gas station but not gouging. Feels: completely fine. But I can't tell you where between $10 and $500 it flips.

5. A pharmaceutical company tests a new drug in a poor country, pays participants well by local standards, follows real safety protocols, and the drug will be priced too high for any of them to ever afford it once approved. Feels: bad, but I have trouble saying why, since the participants consented and benefited and nobody's worse off.

6. Same as 5, except the drug is for a disease that only exists in that country, and the company promises to sell it there at cost for ten years. Feels: fine, maybe even good. But structurally it seems like the same deal as 5, just with a discount attached.

7. A wealthy tourist country, a beach vendor charges me $6 for a Coke that costs 50 cents. I pay it without blinking. Feels: totally fine, even though it's a bigger markup than case 3 in percentage terms and the vendor knows I have no other options on this stretch of beach.

So: 2 feels worse than 1 despite identical outcomes for workers, 3 feels way worse than 7 despite both being "take it or leave it" deals with a captive buyer, and 5 bugs me in a way I can't articulate. Is there one principle that captures most of this, or am I actually tracking two or three different things (how the situation came about, how much of the surplus each side gets, whether the weaker party had alternatives the stronger party created or destroyed)? And if the principle can't save all my verdicts, tell me which one I should drop -- I suspect it's either 1 or 7, but I want to see the argument.
