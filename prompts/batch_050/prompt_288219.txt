here's my opening move. i'll grant that false assumptions can predict fine. but consider what i'm going to call assumption laundering: a central-bank model assumes perfectly rational agents -- false, everybody knows it's false -- but the model gets calibrated on twenty years of data, and the calibration quietly soaks the agents' actual irrationality into the estimated parameters. the model predicts beautifully, right up until the calibration regime breaks (say, a pandemic) and it fails along exactly the dimension where the assumption was false. so the "irrelevant" assumption was doing hidden work the whole time: it marked where the model would break, and treating it as a harmless as-if fiction is precisely why nobody saw the break coming. your methodology says judge by predictions alone -- but by the time the predictions fail, its too late, and the failure was legible in the assumptions all along. doesn't the F-twist make you systematically blind to exactly this kind of blowup?
