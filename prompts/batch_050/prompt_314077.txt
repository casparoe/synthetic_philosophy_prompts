So I keep getting into arguments about whether taste is "all just subjective," and I've realized my own verdicts are kind of a mess. I read Hume's Of the Standard of Taste ages ago, so if my intuitions turn out to be secretly Humean, or to contradict him, tell me. Anyway, here are my verdicts, and I want to know what principle (if any) I'm actually committed to.

1. A friend says there's no fact of the matter about whether his favorite dumb comedy is a better film than Citizen Kane, it's all preference. I think he's just wrong. Some taste is better than other taste.
2. At 19 I thought jazz was tedious noodling. Now I love it, and I don't think of this as my taste merely changing -- I think 19-year-old me was failing to hear things that were actually there. Present me gets to correct past me.
3. My sommelier friend detects things in wine I genuinely cannot detect. When we disagree about a bottle, I defer to her judgment about quality -- but I don't think my enjoyment is somehow fake or counts for less than hers.
4. A guy who has never seriously listened to hip-hop tells me it's all garbage. I give that verdict zero weight. But a lifelong fan's rankings within the genre carry real weight with me, even though I barely listen to the stuff myself.
5. Cross-genre: if someone asked whether Beyoncé or Beethoven is greater, full stop, I don't think that's a nonsense question. I think a sufficiently equipped judge could in principle answer it -- traditions don't wall quality off completely.
6. Triumph of the Will: I think it can be genuinely great as film despite being Nazi propaganda. The evil of the message doesn't touch the aesthetic verdict.
7. But a stand-up bit whose punchlines lean on racist stereotypes -- I think the racism makes it worse as comedy. It's a flaw in the work, not just in the comedian.

So: what single account captures these? Something like Hume's ideal of true judges (delicate, practiced, unprejudiced, etc.)? Something else? I want the candidate principle stated precisely, with which case motivates each clause, and an honest answer about which of my verdicts it can't accomodate -- I'm pretty sure 6 and 7 are a problem for each other, and maybe 4 and 5 are too. If the cleanest theory forces me to drop a verdict, tell me which one and why it should be that one. Also give me its prediction for a case I haven't listed, like two fully qualified critics deadlocked over the same painting, or AI-generated music.
