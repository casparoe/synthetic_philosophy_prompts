So I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and lately my beat has bled into politics, and I keep hitting the same wall: roughly every couple of weeks I have to decide whether some story gets the word "backsliding" in it or not. Hungary's media regulator yanking licenses, Poland's fight over the constitutional court (and now the new government doing its own arguably sketchy stuff to undo the old government's sketchy stuff), Turkey jailing opposition mayors, whatever the latest US thing is. My editor pushes back half the time -- not because she disagrees politically but because she thinks the word has become a vibe, something you apply to governments you dislike. Honestly I can't fully defend myself when she says that, wich bugs me.

So here's what I want: turn the concept of democratic backsliding into an actual procedure I can run on a story before I file. Like, an ordered checklist or decision tree in words. The questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer to move to the next one, and where the procedure just stops and says "no, you may not use the word here." I want it to at minimum handle these distinctions, because they're the ones my editor throws at me: (a) backsliding proper vs. a country that was never really consolidated democratic in the first place, (b) erosion-by-a-thousand-legal-cuts vs. the sudden rupture kind, and whether the procedure should treat them the same, (c) genuinely anti-democratic moves vs. ordinary hardball politics that my side happens to hate, and (d) a real downward slide vs. a crisis the system's own antibodies are visibly fighting off.

Two hard requirements. First, the procedure has to be symmetric: there should be at least one step that's basically "run the same test on a government you sympathize with," and tell me how to do that without it becoming fake both-sidesism. Second, mark the points where no checklist will save me and I just have to exercise judgment, and say why judgment can't be eliminated there.

Then run the finished procedure on three cases, out loud, showing each step: Hungary since roughly 2010, Poland after the 2023 change of government (I specifically want to know whether your procedure can even output "backsliding reversed" or "backsliding by the other side," or whether it silently assumes slides go one direction), and a US case of your choosing. One of the three has to be a case where the procedure tells me not to use the word even though my instincts say use it -- and don't rig that, pick one where the answer genuinely stings a little.

Finish with what the procedure can't do: what would have to be true for my editor to be right that the whole concept is just a partisan label with extra steps, and whether running the procedure faithfully would even detect that or whether it presupposes the concept is fine.

Constraints on my end: I usually have a day, sometimes two, of reporting time; I can't call five country experts for every story; whatever the procedure says, my editor has to sign off, so each step needs to be explainable to a smart skeptic in one sentence.
