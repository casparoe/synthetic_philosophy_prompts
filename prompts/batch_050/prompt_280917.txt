I'm a 2L and my roommate is a philosophy PhD student, and we've been arguing for weeks about whether some future advanced AI system should be granted legal personhood, with the analogy to corporate personhood doing most of the work on both sides. We want to stage the debate as a dialogue for our law school's AI policy journal club, and neither of us trusts ourselves to write the other side fairly, so I'm outsourcing it.

Write a dialogue between two characters, Dana and Priya. Dana argues that corporate personhood shows AI personhood is perfectly coherent and maybe inevitable: legal personhood was never about having a soul or a mind, it's a bundle of conveniences (suing and being sued, holding property, entering contracts) that the law extends to whatever it's useful to extend it to, and a future AI agent that manages money and makes deals would slot right into the same slot the corporation did. Priya argues the analogy actually cuts the other way: the whole point of corporate personhood is that there are natural persons behind the corporation whose interests the fiction serves (shareholders, employees, creditors), so corporate rights are really human rights wearing a costume, whereas an autonomous AI has no human beneficiaries behind it -- and on top of that, the standard tools for disciplining a corporate person (fines, liability, reputational pressure) only bite because humans behind the entity feel them, which wouldn't apply.

A few requirements, since I've read bad versions of this debate: both characters should be sharp and neither should win; they need to actually engage with each other's specific arguments rather than reciting prepared statements; and somewhere in the middle Dana should raise the wrinkle that an AI could simply be made the sole member of a limited liability company, so that the "no humans behind it" objection collapses into a question about one weird shareholder rather than about personhood itself -- and Priya should have a real answer to that, not a shrug. End the dialogue with them locating an actual crux they disagree about (some fact or principle where, if it went one way, one of them would switch sides), not with them agreeing to disagree. Aim for something we could perform in about ten minutes.
