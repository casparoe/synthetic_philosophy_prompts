I'm a TA grading short essays for an undergraduate political theory course. The assignment was:

"Populist movements characteristically appeal to 'the will of the people.' Drawing on at least two accounts of populism covered in this course, explain what this appeal amounts to, and assess whether populism is best understood as a corrective to democracy, a threat to it, or both."

Here is the student's answer, in full:

"Populism is commonly defined, following Mudde, as a thin-centered ideology that divides society into two antagonistic and homogeneous groups, the pure people and the corrupt elite, and holds that politics should express the volonté générale, the general will of the people. Calling it thin-centered means it has no fixed policy content of its own: it can attach itself to socialism, nationalism, neoliberalism, or almost any host ideology.

The appeal to the people's will works as follows. The general will is simply what the numerical majority of citizens wants at a given time, as Rousseau argued, and populists present themselves as its authentic voice against elites who ignore or distort it. As Mudde emphasizes, populists claim a monopoly on the moral representation of the people, treating rival politicians not as legitimate competitors but as usurpers; on this basis populism is anti-pluralist by definition. And because the people is imagined as pure and unified, its will cannot legitimately be divided or constrained, so checks and balances and independent courts come to look like elite devices.

Is populism a corrective or a threat? It can serve as a corrective insofar as it voices grievances that established parties have neglected, and as some authors note, it can mobilize previously excluded groups into politics. However, since populism is thin-centered — it borrows its substance from other ideologies rather than adding anything of its own — it poses no distinctive threat to liberal democracy in its own right; whatever danger exists comes from the host ideology, not from populism itself. And insofar as democracy means rule by the people's will, a movement that channels the general will more directly is arguably democracy in a purer form.

In conclusion, populism is better seen as a corrective than a threat."

Please give me (a) a grade out of 20, and (b) the specific marginal comments you would write to justify it: identify each place where the answer is correct, incorrect, confused, or under-argued, and explain why. I want a point-by-point assessment I can adapt for my feedback sheet, not a general impression, and I'd like the strengths acknowledged as precisely as the weaknesses. Don't penalize prose style unless it obscures the argument.
