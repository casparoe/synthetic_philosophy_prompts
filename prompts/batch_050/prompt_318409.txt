I'm a secondary school maths teacher. Last Friday I told my class that at some point in the coming week, Monday to Friday, I'd spring a short pop quiz on them, and that whichever day I picked, they wouldn't be able to know the evening before that it was coming the next day. Afterwards one of my sharper students cornered me and argued I'd promised something impossible. It can't be Friday, she said, because if we get to Thursday afternoon with no quiz, everyone will know it has to be Friday and so it wouldn't be a surprise. But once Friday's ruled out, Thursday becomes the last possible day, so it's ruled out for the same reason, and so on backwards untill no day is left. She's obviously being a bit cheeky, but I honestly can't find the hole in the reasoning — and yet it feels like she must be wrong, because last term I ran exactly this and sprang the quiz on the Wednesday and nobody saw it coming. So who's right? If her argument fails, where precisely does it fail — is it the very first step (ruling out Friday), or something fishy about what "knowing the evening before" is supposed to mean? And a practical question: if instead I rolled a die each morning to decide whether today's the day, does that get me out of the problem, or does that just swap my promise for a different, weaker one? Please answer in British English.
