I have no academic training in logic, but I have been trying to take seriously the claim that some contradictions might be true rather than merely believed, printed, or legally enacted. Write the specification a theory of dialetheism or paraconsistent logic must meet before I should regard it as an adequate account rather than wordplay: give exactly five numbered requirements, each in one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, where each requirement names the case or phenomenon that motivates it (self-reference such as the liar or Curry, vague boundaries, inconsistent statutes or databases, and ordinary mathematical and empirical reasoning) and states whether it is non-negotiable or mainly the preference of one camp. For each requirement, mark what is settled among people who know the field (for example, that classical logic validates explosion, or that formal paraconsistent systems exist) from what is genuinely contested (for example, whether any contradictions are true, or whether logic itself can be revised), and do not present a contested view as consensus. Make at least one requirement test whether the theory avoids both triviality and evasive relabeling of “true” or “contradiction,” and make the final requirement say which two demands are most likely to be jointly unsatisfiable and which single requirement would knock out the most popular contenders; do not provide citations, reading lists, or a scorecard of named logicians.
