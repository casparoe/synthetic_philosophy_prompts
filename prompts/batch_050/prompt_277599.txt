Hobbyist question, no formal background. Say I take an ordinary brick wall and watch it for an hour. Its molecules are in some new configuration at every moment. Now, after the fact, I invent a labeling scheme: configuration at noon counts as "register holds 0", the next one counts as an instruction being executed, and so on, and I pick the labels so that the wall ends up "running" any program I like -- a chess engine, a word processor, whatever. Nothing about the wall changed; only my labels did. And it seems like the same trick works for a bucket of water or the solar system, as long as I'm allowed to choose the mapping after watching what states actually occured. But if that trick always works, then saying the brain "computes" doesn't seem to say anything more than what we could say about the wall, which makes computationalism about the mind feel empty. I've seen the word pancomputationalism thrown around but that seems to be the *view* that everything computes, not this specific problem that "computes" stops meaning anything once mappings are free. Does this problem have an established name of its own? And I don't just want the label -- I want to know where its edges are. Does it bite equally against a giant lookup-table machine, or only against cases where the mapping is cooked up retroactively? And what do people take the fix to be, i.e. what extra ingredient makes a real laptop compute something while the wall doesn't?
