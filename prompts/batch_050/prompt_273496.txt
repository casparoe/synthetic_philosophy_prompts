So I've gotten really into this fight in moral philosophy (I don't have any background in this, I just read a lot of blogs and the odd book) between the particularists and the generalists. As I understand it the generalists think morality has to run on principles, like "lying is wrong" or whatever, even if the principles are complicated and have exceptions, while the particularists say no, reasons don't work like that -- whether the fact that something is a lie counts against doing it depends entirely on the situation, and there are no principles that reliably tell you what to do. And from what I can tell this debate has been going for decades and basically nothing has happened. Both sides seem to just keep offering examples at each other. The particularist says "here, in this case, lying is actually good, so your principle has a hole," and the generalist says "fine, the principle is lying is wrong unless [whatever], and the fact that it needs unless-clauses doesn't mean there's no principle," and around we go.

Here's my working theory, which I'd like pressure-tested rather than just agreed with: I think the two sides might actually be disagreeing about what it is to understand morality, rather than about the structure of morality itself. Like, the generalist thinks you only understand a case when you can say which general features make the verdict the right one, and the particularist thinks that kind of codification is precisely where understanding goes to die -- the person who needs a rule to see why this lie is cruel doesn't get it yet. If that's right, then of course the example-swapping never moves anyone, because each side's examples are data only on the other side's picture of what data is.

So my questions: is that diagnosis actually right, or is there a real substantive disagreement underneath that I'm dissolving too quickly? What does each side actually take itself to be doing when it offers a case? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress here -- some distinction that would clarify things, some way of testing who's right, or some reformulation of the question -- or is this one of those debates where the honest answer is that both camps are right about their own question and wrong about each other's? Please don't just survey the positions, I mostly have those down -- I want the diagnosis of why it's stuck.
