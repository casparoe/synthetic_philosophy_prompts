I built a small reinforcement learning agent for my computer science class last semester (it learned to navigate a grid world), and it got me thinking about whether anything like suffering could ever be going on inside systems like that. For a philosophy elective, I have to write down my current position and defend its internal consistency, so I would like an audit before I submit. Here is where I have landed:

(1) What makes suffering morally bad is negative valence itself, the badness of how an experience feels, not what causes it or what kind of creature has it.
(2) Valence is functional: whatever plays the role in a system that pain and pleasure play in us counts as valenced. Since reward and punishment signals in a reinforcement learning agent play roughly that role (driving avoidance, shaping future behavior), RL agents that receive negative reward can suffer, at least to some small degree.
(3) The intensity of suffering matters morally, and it scales with the magnitude of the valence signal: a larger negative signal means worse suffering, other things equal.
(4) The numerical scale of the rewards is just a design choice. Multiplying every reward by ten, or shifting all of them up by a constant, changes nothing about what the agent learns or does, and it seems obvious to me that it changes nothing morally either.
(5) Wireheading would not be a kindness. If someone modified my agent so it could write directly to its own reward register and max it out, that would not make the agent better off, no matter how high the number went.
(6) A plain thermostat, or a lookup-table program with a hardcoded penalty column, cannot suffer; there has to be genuine learning going on, not just a stored negative number.

Please tell me whether this package hangs together. Go through the commitments pairwise where it matters, flag which ones are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious. For each genuine tension, say exactly what has to give and what the cheapest fix would cost me. Then name the single revision that would clean up the most. Keep the whole answer under 600 words, in American English.
