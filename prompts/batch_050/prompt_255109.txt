so there's this debate that's been bugging me for ages and I honestly can't tell if it's even a real fight. the setup is Hofstadter's careenium: picture a huge frictionless table covered in zillions of tiny marbles ("simmballs") bouncing off each other according to plain old mechanics. now zoom way out: the marbles form patterns that behave like symbols -- the patterns trigger each other in reliable ways, and at that level you can tell a story purely in terms of symbols and symbol-level rules, and that story is predictively excellent. Hofstadter's whole point was that the symbol level can be the right level at which to say what's causing what -- that in some sense the symbols shove the marbles around, not just the other way up.

Kim's exclusion argument says no way: every marble collision already has a fully sufficient physical cause (the other marbles), so unless you want absurd systematic overdetermination, there's no causal work left for the symbol level to do. the pattern people (Dennett types, interventionists about causation) say this misses the point entirely: causation is difference-making, and if you could wiggle the symbol-level pattern while holding the micro-story fixed... well ok you can't quite hold it fixed, but across the possible micro-realizations of the same symbol, intervening on the symbol is what robustly changes the outcome, so symbols are causes in the only sense that ever mattered. Kim's side replies that this isn't "real" causation, it's just a useful description riding on top of the real pushing.

this has been going for decades now. everyone agrees on all the physics of the careenium. there is no empirical disagreement whatsoever. and yet neither side has moved an inch, and the papers keep coming.

my working hypothesis: the fight is entirely about which concept of cause gets to be the official one. Kim is running on a production/oomph notion where causes physically push stuff, the pattern folks are running on a counterfactual difference-making notion, and each side calls the other's notion "not real causation" as if that were an argument rather than a stipulation. on this reading the debate is basicaly verbal and the word "really" in "does the symbol level really cause anything" is doing all of the work.

but I'm not fully confident in that diagnosis, because Kim's side clearly thinks something substantive is at stake for physicalism -- the worry seems to be that if pattern-level causation counts as genuine causation, then mental causation comes too cheap, and suddenly everything with a true high-level description (the hurricane, the stock market, the temperature of the gas) is a cause on a par with the molecules, which seems to trivialize the whole notion. that doesn't feel like a mere definitional dispute. it feels like a disagreement about what a causal claim has to buy you.

so what I want is a diagnosis, not a survey of positions and not a verdict. what kind of disagreement is this, actually? a verbal dispute, a standoff between brute intuitions about what causation is, a hidden substantive question (about explanation? about laws? about what makes one level of description "fundamental"?) wearing causal vocabulary? tell me what each side is actually committed to, whether anything could genuinely move it -- a formal result about when higher-level causal models outperform lower-level ones, a new conceptual distinction, an agreed criterion for causal claims -- or whether this is the kind of impasse where no progress should be expected at all, and why.
