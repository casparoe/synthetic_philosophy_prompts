I'm a junior in high school and I serve on the committee that rewrote our student council constitution this year. During the process I proposed a clause saying that a certain rule -- the one requiring a two-thirds vote to remove an officer -- could never itself be changed, and our faculty advisor vetoed it on the grounds that such a clause "can't actually do what it pretends to do." Later, in AP U.S. History, I found out the framers tried something very similar. Article V of the U.S. Constitution ends with the proviso "that no state, without its consent, shall be deprived of its equal suffrage in the Senate." So there is apparently a long tradition of rule-writers trying to build a lock that no future key can open.

Here is the pattern I keep noticing and don't have a word for. A rule system contains a procedure for changing rules. Someone uses that procedure, or writes into the document from the start, a rule whose only job is to put some rule -- possibly itself -- permanently out of the procedure's reach. But the protection seems to collapse the moment you look at it: if the protective clause can be reached by the amendment procedure, then future members amend the protection away first and the protected rule second, and the "permanent" rule lasted exactly one extra step; and if the protective clause genuinely cannot be reached, then it was never really inside the system it claims to belong to, and it's unclear what gives it authority. Our constitution committee ran into the same knot from the other direction when someone proposed a game for our club's game night where players vote to modify the rules, and one player immediately moved to make the voting rule itself unmodifiable. My history teacher said this is all just a legal version of the question whether an all-powerful being could create a stone it cannot lift, but I'm not convinced that's the same problem, since here the power and the limit are supposed to live in the same document.

Does this configuration -- a rule that tries to seal itself, or another rule, off from the very process that sustains it -- have an established name? I'm not looking for a bare label. I'd like to know what the nearest named concepts are, how the case where a rule protects itself differs from the case where it protects different content, whether the two-step maneuver I described shows that such protection is impossible or merely evadable, whether the analogy to the omnipotence puzzle is exact or superficial, and, if nothing established fits precisely, what you would call it and where its boundary cases lie. Please keep your answer to 350 words at most.
