Hey, I TA a second-year political economy course (env policy block) and I'm writing the final exam. I need one essay question plus a model answer and a marking scheme I can hand to the other graders. Here's the setup I want the question built around, which I've made up so students can't just recycle an op-ed they've read:

A mid-sized industrialised country passes a textbook carbon tax: starts low, rises on a pre-announced schedule, ALL revenue returned as equal per-capita rebates so it's progressive on net, border carbon adjustment to stop leakage. Economists love it. Emissions fall 18% over five years. Then a change of government repeals it after a campaign dominated by pump prices. The replacement is a messier package -- fuel-economy standards, a coal phase-out mandate, heat-pump subsidies -- which delivers smaller cuts per year but survives three election cycles, partly because nobody can point to its cost on a receipt. Meanwhile the country's degrowth-aligned green party argues the whole episode shows pricing is a dead end and only planned reductions in material throughput will cut emissions fast enough.

The question should ask students to (1) explain the divergence -- why the efficient instrument died and the clunky one lived -- using at least two *distinct* mechanisms from political economy (I'm thinking salience/visibility of costs, concentrated-vs-diffuse interests, that kind of thing, but they have to pick and apply them, not just name them), and (2) evaluate whether the degrowth conclusion actually follows from the case. Constraints: 45 minutes, roughly 700-900 words, 30 marks.

For the model answer and marking scheme, I want it granular enough that a grader who hasn't studied this stuff can apply it. Specifically:

- Name the 3-4 moves a top-band answer MUST make. One of them has to be noticing that the regulations also raise prices, just invisibly -- the cost is hidden in the price of cars and heating, which is precisely why the package survives -- and a top answer has to use that observation rather than treat regulation as costless.
- Name the specific mistakes that should cost marks, with how many marks each costs. E.g., treating the repeal as evidence the tax didn't work on emissions; conflating relative and absolute decoupling when assessing the degrowth claim; strawmanning degrowth as "just wanting a recession"; or "the public is irrational" with no mechanism -- that's a non-answer and I want it penalised as such.
- Tell me where partial credit should go for an answer that does (1) well but whiffs (2).

One more thing: if you think the question as I've set it up has a confused premise, or is unfair/unanswerable in 45 minutes, or the marks don't map sensibly onto the parts, say so plainly and tell me how you'd fix it rather than just politely complying. I'd rather hear that now than after 200 students have sat it.
