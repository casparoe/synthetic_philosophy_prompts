Two friends of mine got into a surprisingly heated argument last week and I want a referee. Some background: Milo is convinced that the overwhelming moral priority is making sure the far future goes well, because there could be astronomically many future people, so even tiny probabilities of affecting that future dominate everything else. Jess thinks the simulation argument messes this up. Round by round it went:

(1) Jess: if there's a decent chance we're living in a simulation, the astronomical-stakes reasoning is badly weakened -- simulated civilizations plausibly get switched off, or at least don't get to colonize actual galaxies, so the real future we can influence is far smaller than the argument assumes. Tiny probabilities of gigantic payoffs should no longer run the show.

(2) Milo: simulated experiences are still real experiences, suffering counts wherever it happens, and if the basement universe can run zillions of sims then the total number of minds is bigger, not smaller -- so the stakes go up. Also nothing stops a simulated civilization from having a long future *inside* the sim.

(3) Jess: but from a decision-making point of view the probability mass shifts toward scenarios where our influence is capped. Sims are plausibly run for a purpose and terminated once they stop being interesting -- say, once they start running their own simulations -- which truncates exactly the long, galaxy-spanning futures the whole argument needs. So it's specifically the fanatical part, letting minuscule chances of vast payoffs swamp everything, that gets dampened.

(4) Milo: that's self-defeating. The simulation hypothesis is itself a speculative, lowish-probability, weird-payoff scenario -- if you're using it to dismiss far-future reasoning, you're doing the same kind of fanatical reasoning you claim to be rejecting. And a truncated-but-huge future is still huge, so even granting all your premises the conclusion doesn't change, only the numbers do.

Neither of them budged and honestly I can see it both ways. Can you go through the four moves and say who's right about what at each step -- and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
