Me and my friend Sam got into a genuinely stupid three-hour argument last weekend about the Fermi paradox and I want a way to prove we were actually disagreeing about different things the whole time. His line was basically: the galaxy should be crawling with civilizations, so the silence means they're *choosing* to be quiet. My line was: nah, we're probably just early, or life is rare, and "they're hiding" is unfalsifiable cope. We kept going in circles because every time I attacked one version of his view he'd slide to a slightly different one.

So: write me a decision tree for Fermi paradox positions. It starts from the single observation "we see and hear nothing," each branch node is a question, and each leaf is a named hypothesis. Requirements:

- Build it from the questions, not from a list of names. I want roughly 7-9 questions whose answers carve up the logical space. Things like "is the silence a fact about them (rare, short-lived, far away) or a fact about us (we can't detect them, we're too early)" might be one cut, but don't just use my examples — find the cuts that actually separate the views. Questions about timescales, detectability across light-years, whether quietness is a decision or a side effect of physics, whether expansion has definately started somewhere else.
- Leaves must include at least: rare earth / life is just very uncommon, great filter behind us, great filter ahead of us, and zoo/planetarium-style deliberate non-interference. Plus these two less standard ones Sam pulled from stuff he read, which I want on the tree too:
  - "aestivation": advanced civilizations go deliberately dormant for billions of years, waiting for the universe to cool down because computation gets more energy-efficient as the background temperature drops. So they're not hiding from us, they're asleep.
  - "grabby aliens": expansionist civilizations spread outward at a decent fraction of light speed, and we'd eventually meet their frontier. The reason we don't see any yet is that we're weirdly early — and we had to be, because any civilization arising later would already be inside someone else's settled zone.
- Phrase every node so someone can answer it without knowing which leaf it routes to. No "do you believe in the zoo hypothesis?" style nodes.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate. I'm suspiscious that "great filter ahead of us" and "civilizations reliably self-destruct" are the same leaf wearing two hats, and that "too far away" and "signals too weak or too brief" collapse into each other too. Tell me if I'm wrong about either.
- If some combination of answers lands nowhere — no named position occupies it — say so, with a one-line diagnosis: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- End by pointing at the earliest question where Sam's answers and mine part ways, so we can go have *that* argument instead of the mushy one.

One tree, plain text, indent or number the branches however works. And be honest if two of the leaves I demanded can't be told apart by any sane question.
