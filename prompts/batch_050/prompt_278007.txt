I'm putting together a handout for a second-year aesthetics seminar on what, if anything, purely instrumental music is *about*. The anchor for the session is §52 of The World as Will and Idea (Haldane–Kemp translation, Vol. 1 — it's public domain, on Project Gutenberg as ebook 38427). In that section Schopenhauer claims that music is not a copy of the phenomena the way the other arts are, but a copy of the will itself: it doesn't depict this or that particular grief or triumph, it presents grief and triumph in the abstract, which is why we can find a piece heartbreakingly sad without being able to say what it's sad about. Please write a decision tree that sorts the main positions on the meaning of absolute music, with Schopenhauer's §52 claim as the root question — i.e. the first split is whether you accept that music can present an emotion itself, detached from any object, person, or story. Each branch node should be a concrete question a student can answer without knowing where it leads (use cases where handy: a funeral march played at double speed, whether you need to imagine a persona 'inside' the music to hear it as angry, whether a symphony could survive being revealed to be a computer-generated pastiche of a style). Leaves should include Schopenhauer's own view, formalism, the arousal theory, persona-based expression accounts, and the sui generis view that music means itself, plus any others the space demands — six to eight leaves total. Give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment, note any unoccupied combinations of answers and why nobody sits there, and flag any pair of positions your tree can't really seperate.
