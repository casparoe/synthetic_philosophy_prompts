I’m a high-school student in a philosophy club, and we’ve been arguing about origin essentialism: could I have existed at all if a different sperm or egg had been involved? I want to test whether my friend Mara has a stable view or is just going case by case. Please predict how she would judge a new case, but only from the pattern in her earlier verdicts.

Her verdicts so far are these. If my parents had conceived a child from a different sperm and a different egg, even on the same night and raised by the same family, she says that child would not be me. If the very same sperm and egg had formed me but the embryo had been implanted in a different surrogate, frozen for thirty years, born in another country, or given a completely different name and upbringing, she says it would still be me. If the same sperm and egg had formed the embryo and doctors had replaced its mitochondria right after fertilization with donor mitochondria, leaving the nuclear DNA alone, she says still me. If the same sperm and egg had been used but the early embryo’s epigenetic marks were heavily reset in a lab before implantation, she still says me, as long as the nuclear genome and the continuous embryo remain. But she denies that a later clone made from one of my skin cells and an enucleated donor egg would be me, even if the nuclear genome matched mine, because that would be a later copy with a different starting event.

Now give her the new case. In a nearby possible world, before I am conceived, the actual sperm and egg that would have made me are destroyed. Scientists instead take skin cells from my mother and father, reprogram them into an egg and a sperm, and make those gametes carry chromosome sets that are exact copies of the chromosome sets in the destroyed gametes. They fertilize the lab-made egg with the lab-made sperm, implant the embryo in my mother at the same time I would otherwise have been implanted, and the child is born into my family with the same nuclear genome, same womb environment, same name, and same life from there. Would Mara say that child is me?

Please do three things. First, state at least three precise rules that fit all of Mara’s earlier verdicts, such as same-token-gamete essentialism, same-nuclear-blueprint essentialism, same-continuous-zygote essentialism, or a sufficiently-close-origin rule, and say exactly what each rule predicts about the new case. Second, give your best prediction of Mara’s verdict, explain which rule you think she is implicitly running, and admit it if the track record genuinely underdetermines the answer. Third, describe one further thought experiment that would best discriminate among the leading rules, and say what each possible verdict there would show.

If you think this request rests on a confused premise, for example because being me is not the sort of thing that verdicts about origins can settle, or because my description hides an impossible biological assumption, please say so plainly rather than forcing a tidy answer.
