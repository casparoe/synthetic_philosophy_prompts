Is there a name for this? I tutor kids and one asked me whether Sherlock Holmes is real, and I said no, he's made up, and then maybe ten minutes later in another conversation I said Holmes is probably the most famous detective in history and nobody batted an eye. So in one breath "Holmes doesn't exist" is true and in the next "Holmes is the most famous detective ever" is also true, and both feel completly fine until you put them side by side. And it gets weirder. "Holmes played the violin" is true, "Holmes played the tuba" is false, fine, that's just what the stories say. But what about the stuff the stories never mention -- "Holmes had two kidneys" feels true, "Holmes was secretly a woman" feels false, and "Holmes had blood type AB" feels like it has no answer at all, and I can't figure out what rule is doing the sorting, because it obviously isn't just "whatever the text says," since the kidneys one isn't in the text. So my question: is there an established concept for what's going on here, either for the way a name seems to flip between referring and not referring depending on the conversational setting, or for whatever principle sorts the unstated cases into true / false / no-answer? I've seen people gesture at "pretense" or "make-believe" but I'm not sure whether that's this exact thing or something adjacent to it. If nothing established fits cleanly, I'd rather get a careful carving of the phenomenon than a bare label. Also please say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the one point you're least sure of.
