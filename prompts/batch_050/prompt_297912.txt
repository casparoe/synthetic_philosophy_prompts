Say I've ended up in charge of a small fund — enough for maybe four or five researchers for two years — for conceptual work on Darwinian theories of culture: memetics, cultural evolution, gene-culture coevolution, that whole cluster. Not empirical work, just people thinking.

Here's the thing that motivated the fund in the first place. It seems to me that recommendation algorithms (the TikTok feed, YouTube's up-next, etc.) constitute a genuinely new kind of selection environment for cultural variants. For most of history, memes spread because they were good at getting human brains to retain and retransmit them — selection for psychological stickiness, roughly, with some side-constraints from being useful or harmful to hosts. Now there's an intermediary that pre-filters what anyone even sees, and it's optimizing for engagement metrics rather than for anything a human would endorse. So the variants that win are the ones that please the filter, which is only loosely correlated with pleasing or benefiting humans. Call it a two-stage selection process if you want, I don't care about the label.

What I want from you: which open questions in the Darwinian theory of culture are most worth settling if the ultimate goal is understanding — and eventually maybe steering — what algorithmically-mediated selection does to the culture it acts on? Give me a defended agenda, not a survey. For each question: why it's upstream of other things we'd want to know, whether it's tractable for conceptual people at all or secretly needs data we don't have, and what would move it up or down the ranking over the next couple of years.

Some questions I'm wondering about, to give you a sense of the level I'm thinking at — feel free to ignore or demolish any of them:

- Whether the two-stage thing (variants must first satisfy the algorithm's criteria, then human attention) actually changes the evolutionary dynamics in kind, or just changes which traits get selected for within the same old dynamics
- Whether the right unit of selection here is still the individual meme, or whether it's really bundles/formats/whole creator-personas, and how much that even matters for predicting outcomes
- What the analog of host fitness is when the "host" is partly a corporation's metric — whether culture can now be parasitic on the metric while being neutral for humans, and what that predicts

Also tell me which famous open questions in this area you'd deliberately NOT fund. Like, my gut says the endless definitional fight over whether memes are really replicators or whether the whole thing is just a loose analogy is mostly a tar pit that has eaten thirty years of smart people, but if you think that question is actually load-bearing for the algorithmic stuff, argue me out of it.
