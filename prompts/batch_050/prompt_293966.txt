I assembled the following argument as a defense of sweatshop labor for a workshop paper, and it now appears to prove considerably more than I intended. I would like to know where it goes wrong, if it does.

(1) If one party makes another an offer that the second is free to decline, and the second accepts, then the transaction leaves the acceptor better off than he would have been had no offer been made; had it left him worse off, he would simply have declined.
(2) Leaving a person better off than he would otherwise have been cannot wrong that person.
(3) Therefore no accepted offer can wrong the person who accepts it.
(4) Sweatshop employment at wages that workers line up to accept, payday lending at triple-digit interest, the sale of bottled water at ten times its ordinary price in the days after a hurricane, and the bargain in which a passerby agrees to throw a rope to a drowning man only in exchange for the man's entire fortune are all, in the relevant sense, accepted offers.
(5) Hence none of these transactions wrongs anyone, and the notion of mutually beneficial exploitation is simply confused: where there is mutual benefit, there is nothing left to complain of.
(6) Yet the rope bargain strikes me as a paradigm of wrongful exploitation — we condemn the passerby precisely for the terms he set, and courts would refuse to enforce the agreement — and I am only marginally less confident about the hurricane case.
(7) So something above has to give.

I built the argument to protect (3), but the conclusion in (5) is more than I can swallow, and I am not prepared to give up (6). My suspicion is that the trouble lies in (1) or in the move from (1) and (2) to (3), though each step looked innocent when I wrote it. Which premise or inference do you reject, why did it seem plausible, and what does rejecting it cost — in particular, is there a way to keep a defense of sweatshop wages without thereby licensing the rope bargain, and does your diagnosis treat the two cases differently or explain why they were never really alike?
