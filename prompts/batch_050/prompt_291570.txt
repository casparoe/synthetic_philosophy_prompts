I’m a novelist stuck on a strand of my book: near-future Britain, a cash-strapped mental-health trust rolls out a new category, “Late-Onset Narrative Agency Disorder” (LONAD), for people whose main symptom is organising their life too tightly around whichever diagnosis they’ve just been given. The dark joke is that the criteria keep being revised because patients keep performing, resisting, faking or recovering from the label in ways the committee then counts as evidence. Please write, in British English, a deadpan parody of an official clinical guideline/consultation document for LONAD, around 700 words: numbered sections, glossary, “service user involvement” bit, safeguarding note, outcomes metrics. Satirise the practice, not patients or any real person. Let the humour come from teh loop itself: assessment changing the assessed, prevalence figures behaving like plot twists, humility clauses that secretly claim more authority, a biomarker panel that tests only for response to being tested. Dont state the moral; make the form do it. Keep it legible to a general reader but with enough accurate texture about classification, reliability/validity, feedback and incentives that a philosopher of psychiatry would recognise the target and maybe wince approvingly. Avoid citations or a reading list; invent any committee names.
