I’ve been a rationalist-blog lurker for years, and the line I keep hearing is that the liar is basically a self-reference problem, while Yablo’s list is meant to show you can get the same disease with no sentence pointing back at itself. That never fully sat right with me, because Curry’s paradox seems to poison an entirely different part of the machinery: not naming or looping, but the way we let a conditional detach its consequent and then reuse the assumption more than once. So here is the distinction I want tested. Call a paradox referentially circular if some sentence literally talks about itself, and call it justification-loopy if there is no such self-talk but every finite attempt to warrant the sentences still has to pass through the very warrants it is trying to establish; I’ll call the second kind engineered paradox, which is definately not standard terminology, just a label for the thing.

Please argue, in British English, for this position: Yablo’s construction, and a Curry-style variant where each conditional in an endless relay says only that if some later conditional in the relay is true then an arbitrary doom follows, are engineered paradoxes rather than referentially circular ones, and therefore the right moral is that the fault lies in the combination of a transparent truth predicate with unrestricted structural rules like contraction, not in self-reference as such. Make the argument positive rather than just a survey: spell out the relay carefully enough that a smart reader could reconstruct it, show exactly where explosion enters, and explain why banning self-reference would leave the engine intact. Then steelman the two best objections I can think of — that the sequence still smuggles in circularity through a fixed point or diagonal construction, and that Curry shows the real problem is only the conditional, so lumping it with Yablo is a category error — and say what would have to be true for your diagnosis to be wrong. No citations or reading lists, and please seperate the load-bearing logical moves from the merely suggestive metaphors.
