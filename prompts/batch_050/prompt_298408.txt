So I've been arguing with a couple of friends about this for weeks and I want to stress-test my own position before I dig in any deeper. My view: the right way to define life is in terms of self-maintenance against entropy — a living thing is a system that uses energy and matter from its environment to continuously rebuild and repair itself, keeping itself far from thermodynamic equilibrium, with reproduction as basically an extension of that same trick at the population level. Something in the ballpark of autopoiesis, though I don't want to commit to any particular theorist's machinery. I like this definition because it seems to get the right answer on the hard cases: fire grows, metabolizes, and reproduces but doesn't count as alive because it doesn't maintain an organized boundary or repair itself; viruses count as not-quite-alive or alive-only-on-borrowed-machinery; a dormant bacterial spore is alive because the repair apparatus is intact even when idle.

What I want to know is: what would count against this? Not just "some people define life differently," but concrete stuff. Which borderline cases does my definition classify in a way that should genuinely embarrass me rather than just being counterintuitive? Are there systems that clearly meet the self-maintenance criterion but that we'd be crazy to call alive (I'm worried about things like candle flames with a wick, hurricanes, certain self-replicating chemical networks, maybe even sufficiently elaborate software agents or a country's economy)? And going the other direction, is there something obviously alive that fails the criterion? I'd also like to know what the strongest rival criteria are and whether they handle the cases mine fumbles.

One more thing that nags me: is a definition of life even the kind of thing that can be defeated? If someone shows me a counterexample I can always just say "then my definition was tracking a different concept than yours," and we talk past each other forever. Does my position actually risk anything, or have I built myself an unfalsifiable little fortress? If definitional disputes like this can be won or lost at all, tell me what losing would look like — like what observation, experiment (say, from origins-of-life chemistry or synthetic biology), or argument should actually make me abandon the thermodynamic self-maintenance picture, ranked by how decisive they'd be. Be honest about whether my view is well-insulated and whether thats a feature or a bug.
