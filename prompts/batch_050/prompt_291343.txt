At a city council budget meeting I recently attended, a speaker closed her remarks by saying, "defund the police," and the discussion that followed made it obvious that everyone in the room had heard something different. One councilor took her to be proposing that the department be dissolved outright; another heard a modest request to move a few line items toward mental-health crisis responders; a third dismissed the phrase as pure negotiating theater. I would like a careful disambiguation. Lay out, as a list, the distinct precise claims that "defund the police" could be making, ordered from the most modest reading (reduce the rate of budget growth, trim overtime, cut specific equipment programs) through intermediate readings (shrink the department's headcount, unbundle its functions so that traffic enforcement, homelessness outreach, and crisis response go to civilian agencies) up to the most radical (abolish policing as an institution, and with it the state's claim that a professional armed force is the legitimate responder to social disorder). For each reading, say whether it is a coherent policy position or collapses into another reading on inspection, what empirical or moral considerations would bear on it, and whether it is plausibly what the people who popularized the slogan actually meant. Then tell me which readings are compatible with one another and could be held together, which are rhetorically effective but substantively empty or deliberately ambiguous, and which reading the speaker would have needed for her remark to count as an answer to the question actually before the council, namely whether to approve next year's budget increase for the department.
