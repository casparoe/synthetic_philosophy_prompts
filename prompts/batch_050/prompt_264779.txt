I'm designing a one-day seminar on the epistemic regress problem for graduate students who know the terminology but, I suspect, haven't worked out where they actually stand. Please write a decision tree that takes a participant from the starting commitment "at least some of my beliefs are justified" to a named response to the regress. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of positions: each branch node should be a genuine fork (e.g., whether justification must be inferential all the way down; whether a circular chain of reasons can ever justify; whether an infinite chain is something a finite mind could possess; whether non-inferentially justified beliefs need a further mark such as certainty, reliability, or apparent self-evidence; whether the whole demand for justification is internalist in a way one might reject). Phrase every node as a question answerable without the technical vocabulary, ideally via a concrete case of someone pressed to defend a belief under repeated challenges of the form "but what reason do you have for that?" Leaves should include at least: classical infallibilist foundationalism, modest or fallibilist foundationalism, holistic coherentism, infinitism, skepticism that embraces the regress as a reductio, and an externalist dissolution of the problem. For any combination of answers your tree leaves unoccupied, note it and say briefly whether the gap reflects incoherence, mere unpopularity, or unexplored territory; and flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, explaining what further question would be needed.
