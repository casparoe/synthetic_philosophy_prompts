I manage compliance documentation at a mid-sized firm, and although I have no academic training, I've been preoccupied for over a year with a question that started at work: when a colleague pastes an AI chatbot's confident but invented statistic into a client report without checking it, what exactly has gone wrong? I've read enough popular philosophy to know the standard three-way scheme from Harry Frankfurt: a lie requires knowing the truth and concealing it, honest error aims at truth and misses, and bullshit is speech produced with indifference to whether it is true at all. The chatbot can't lie (it knows nothing), and strictly speaking it can't bullshit either, since having no attitudes at all means it can't be indifferent to anything. So on the standard scheme its fabrications are mere errors. But that verdict seems to misplace the problem: the trouble isn't located in the machine, it's located in what my colleague did with it.

My half-formed idea is that the standard categories all classify speech by the mental state of the one who produces it, but AI splits the producer-role into pieces: the model generates, the company tunes it for plausibility, and the user decides what gets passed along. I want to develop a notion I keep calling "delegated bullshit": a speaker can be indifferent to truth not by failing to care, but by deliberately routing their speech through a process they know to be truth-indifferent, so that indifference is achieved by outsourcing rather than by attitude. Whether that is a coherent new category, or collapses back into ordinary recklessness or negligence, is what I want to find out.
