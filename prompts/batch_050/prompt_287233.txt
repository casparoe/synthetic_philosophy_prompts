So I'm putting together the syllabus for an undergrad seminar on "peoples and their traits" and I keep circling back to the question of whether national character is even a coherent notion, let alone whether it's ever okay to generalize about a whole people. One text on the syllabus is Hume's essay Of National Characters, where he pushes back on the old climate theory and argues that nations get their distinctive characters mostly from what he calls moral causes -- their form of government, their laws, their economic situation, how long they've been one political body -- rather than from air and soil. He even notes that peoples living on the borders between nations tend to blend the two characters, which he takes as evidence against the climate story. I actually find his account more subtle than its reputation, so I want objections that would put real pressure on it, not just on some cartoon version.

Objections I already have on the syllabus and don't need repeated:

1. The variance objection: differences within any nation dwarf the average differences between nations, so the generalizations are near-vacuous even when statistically real.
2. The outgroup homogeneity objection: we perceive other peoples as uniform because we know few of them, so "national character" is partly an artifact of who is looking from where.
3. The fast-reversal objection: stereotypes of the same people have flipped within a couple of generations (industrious vs. lazy Germans being the classic example), which suggests they track institutions and circumstances, not character -- though I grant Hume could just say "exactly, moral causes."
4. The circularity objection: the trait is inferred from observed behavior and then invoked to explain that same behavior.
5. The who-gets-to-say objection: these generalizations are historically made by dominant groups about subordinate or foreign ones, and self-descriptions of a people rarely match what outsiders say about them.
6. The unit objection: there's no principled reason the level of aggregation should be the nation rather than the region, the class, the city, or the language group, and the choice of "nation" looks politically loaded.

What I want from you: one more objection, not on this list and not a rewording, special case, or mash-up of anything on it. (E.g. "within-group variance is even bigger for personality than for income" is just #1 with extra steps -- don't do that.) Bonus points if it specifically bites Hume's moral-causes version rather than the climate version he was attacking, since that's the version I'd actually defend on a good day. Tell me how it relates to the closest item on my list so I can see it's genuinely new, and then tell me honestly whether you think it's any good or just cute -- I'd rather hear "this one is weak, here's why" than get flattered.

Also, if you think the whole exercise is set up wrong -- say I'm conflating the coherence question with the permissibility question, or the Hume paraphrase above misleads you, or "objections" isn't the right frame -- push back on that plainly instead of politely working within it.
