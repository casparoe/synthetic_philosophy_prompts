Ok so I've been a lurker on LW and the adjoining blogs for like a decade, and somehow this led to me signing up to give a talk at my local philosophy department's grad colloquium. My thesis: in Parfit's hitchhiker case, the disposition to pay the driver even after you're safely in town isn't a puzzle case where rationality and self-interest come apart -- it's just what rationality looks like for agents who are transparent to each other, and the only reason it feels paradoxical is that we inherited an evidentialist picture of rationality that treats your own future choices as things you predict rather than things you make. I'll also gesture at a hobby-horse of mine: that this is the same mistake people make when they say an AI can't be trustworthy unless it has a deontological rule bolted on, when actually what you want is an agent whose payoff-relevant dispositions are just... visible and stable.
