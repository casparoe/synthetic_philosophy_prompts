A student in my seminar summarized Warren Quinn's self-torturer puzzle for the class like this: "Quinn imagines someone fitted with a device with settings 0 to 1000. Each click up increases the pain by an amount too small to notice, and pays you a nice cash bonus. Click by click it seems rational to keep going, but eventually you're in agony for money that can't be worth it. Quinn's point is that this proves transitivity of preference is a requirement of rationality: the torturer prefers each setting to the one before but prefers setting 0 to setting 1000, so his preferences are intransitive, and that's exactly the mistake -- anyone whose preferences were transitive would have a principled place to stop. So the puzzle is basically a money-pump argument against intransitive preferences."

I told her this was mostly right but that I thought she'd flattened the interesting part, without having time to explain. Can you do the accuracy audit for me? What's right here, what's a subtle misrepresentation -- especially in how she moves from imperceptible differences to intransitive preferences, and in calling it a money pump -- and what was Quinn actually trying to get out of the case? Does her conclusion that transitivity alone solves the puzzle survive once you state the setup more carefully?
