Write a short research proposal for a one-semester philosophy paper of roughly 6,000 words on “reflexive Jeffrey conditionalization”: cases in which an uncertain sensory signal changes both one’s credence in a target proposition and one’s credence about the signal’s own evidential reliability. For example, after a dim glimpse of a marble, I become uncertain both about whether it is red and about whether the lighting is distorting its appearance; the latter uncertainty changes how strongly the glimpse supports the former. The proposed question is whether Jeffrey conditionalization should treat the signal’s evidential weight as fixed during the update, revise that weight after the evidential probabilities change, or instead seek a fixed point at which the revised evidence and posterior are mutually stable. State the question so that it has a determinate answer, explain why standard accounts of Jeffrey updating and the obvious alternatives leave it open, identify the formal constraints and test cases the paper would use, consider the strongest objection to each possible answer, and specify what would count as a successful result or as failure. Keep the project realistically scoped, and describe relevant positions without citations or a bibliography; the author will add references later. Begin with the proposal itself, with no preface or commentary.
