Two friends of mine, Maya and Ben, have been going in circles about Malthus and I want a referee. Round one: Maya said Malthus was just flat-out falsified -- world population has multiplied like sevenfold since he wrote, living standards went up not down, the predicted misery never showed up, case closed. Round two: Ben said no, the theory was conditional -- population presses against subsistence unless checks or technology shift, and pointing to two lucky centuries of tech outrunning mouths doesn't touch the mechanism. He even claimed the demographic transition vindicates Malthus, since people changed their fertility behavior once they got richer, which is basically one of his preventive checks actually working. Round three: Maya came back with, that's exactly the problem -- if every failed prediction gets redescribed as "the conditions didn't hold yet," nothing could ever count against the theory, and she pointed out the population bomb crowd in the 60s and 70s pulled the same move: famines didn't arrive on schedule, so the answer was always wait longer. Round four: Ben tried to split the difference and said a long-run theory earns the right to say "right but early" only if it specifies its mechanism plus the observable conditions under which it would concede defeat, and Malthus's heirs never did that but could have. Round five: Maya said if you have to rewrite the theory to make it defensible, then the rewrite is a new theory and the original one stays falsified -- "right but early" is just unfalsifiability with good PR. They both seem half right to me and I can't tell where. Go through it move by move: who's winning each exchange, does Ben's concession condition actually work or does it smuggle in what it was supposed to rule out, and is there anything both of them are missing about what it would even take to falsify a theory whose whole point is that it plays out over centuries?
