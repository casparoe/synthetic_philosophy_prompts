I sit on a three-person hiring panel at my company, and our last decision left me genuinely puzzled.

Our policy is that we hire every candidate who is both technically strong and a good fit for the team, and only such candidates. Each interviewer votes on the two criteria separately and then on the bottom line. Here is how we voted on our most recent candidate:

- Interviewer 1: technically strong — yes; good fit — yes; make an offer — yes.
- Interviewer 2: technically strong — yes; good fit — no; make an offer — no.
- Interviewer 3: technically strong — no; good fit — yes; make an offer — no.

So a majority of the panel thinks she is technically strong, and a majority thinks she is a good fit — yet a majority voted against hiring her. One colleague insists we must make the offer: count the criteria, and the panel has found her qualified on both. A second says the bottom-line vote is the only one that should count, so we must reject her. The third points out that each of us was perfectly consistent and that the contradiction appears only when our votes are pooled; perhaps, he says, the panel as such simply has no view.

Which way of counting is right, and why? Does the panel believe anything over and above what its members believe? And if the candidate asks why she was turned down, what can I honestly tell her without making the panel look irrational?
