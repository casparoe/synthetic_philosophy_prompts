I keep going back and forth on whether interpretability research on neural nets is actually discovering real structure in there or just projecting tidy human stories onto a big pile of linear algebra. To make it concrete, say "feature realism" is the view that models genuinely compute with the features our tools attribute to them (sparse autoencoder features, probe directions, circuits found by patching, whatever), and "instrumentalism" is the view that these features are just compression schemes that work for us, the way center of mass works for physicists -- useful, predictively fine, but not a joint the system is actually carved at.

Here's my situation: I'm advising a small AI safety lab, about a dozen researchers and maybe two years of runway, trying to decide how much of their overall safety case to rest on interpretability as opposed to just behavioral evals, control measures, and boring empirical testing. So my question is: which open questions in the realism-vs-instrumentalism debate matter most for *that* decision, and which are tempting distractions? Give me a ranked list of the top three or four questions, defend the ordering with explicit criteria (how much the lab's strategy depends on the answer, how tractable the question is in two years, how much other work downstream depends on it), and be honest about which questions are philosophically juicy but don't actually change what the lab should do.

Two things to fold in. First, suppose next year someone publishes results showing that sparse autoencoder features which look cleanly monosemantic on natural text shatter into mush on adversarial or strongly out-of-distribution inputs. Would that flip your ranking, or would a realist have a principled response that keeps interpretability viable anyway? Second, a lot of people seem to treat "the features predict model behavior really well" as settling the realism question -- I'd like you to say whether prediction success is even the right kind of evidence here, or whether realists and instrumentalists can both claim it.

I want a defended agenda for the lab, not a survey of the literature, and definitly not a reading list.
