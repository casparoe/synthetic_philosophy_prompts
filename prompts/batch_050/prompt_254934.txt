I'm a novelist working on the comic parts of my next book and I keep going in circels about what I actually think humour is, so I'd love a consistency check. Roughly where I've landed: (1) I basically buy benign violation theory — something's funny when it's a violation you can simultaneously see as benign. (2) That's why fiction is such a gift for comedy: I can do awful things to my characters and it plays as funny because none of it's real; the unreality is what makes it benign. (3) Jokes that punch down just aren't funny, full stop — only punching up works. (4) Superiority theory is dead; the idea that laughter is about feeling better than someone is a relic. (5) But my most reliable comic engine is dramatic irony, where the reader knows what the character doesn't. (6) Surprise is essential to humour — if the reader sees the punchline coming it dies on the page. (7) And yet my best material is running gags; the ninth callback lands harder than the first. Which of these are genuinely in tension and which only look like it? Rank the real ones by how bad they are, tell me what would have to give in each case, and what's the single cheapest revision. Please don't just flag everything as equally problematic.
