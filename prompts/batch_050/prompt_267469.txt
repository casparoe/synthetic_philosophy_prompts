So I've been circling the wild animal suffering question for years (was lurking on Felicifia back in the day, if anyone remembers that) and I want to stop hand-waving and get an actual number on the habitat destruction version of it.

The claim I keep seeing treated as almost obvious in my corner of the internet: most wild animals live short, brutal lives with net-negative welfare, so destroying habitat -- or declining to restore it -- is good, actually, because it means fewer animals get born into those lives. Other people whose judgment I respect think this is a galaxy-brained mistake, but I've never seen either side actually decompose the thing.

So: give me your credence that paving over, say, a thousand hectares of species-rich grassland and keeping it paved permanently reduces total wild animal suffering, compared to leaving it alone. I want an actual number, but mostly I want the decomposition. The terms that occur to me:

- P that the average small wild animal in that grassland (insects included, weight them however you think is right) has net-negative lifetime welfare
- P that habitat destruction actually reduces wild animal populations in the long run, rather than just shuffling who's alive where, or selecting for boom-and-bust species whose members suffer even more
- P that the comparison is even well-defined given how lost we are on insect sentience and how to weight tiny minds
- whatever other terms you think belong -- maybe something about the predators and scavengers downstream, I dunno

Give each a number and a one-line justification, combine them however you think they combine (tell me if they don't combine cleanly), and give me the bottom line. Then tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence would move it most.

One more thing: a friend of mine says the whole decomposition is pointless because the question dissolves once you reject total-view aggregation -- he thinks comparing "total suffering in ecosystem A vs. empty lot B" is a category error unless you've already bought the population ethics that makes those sums meaningful. Assign a probability to him being right about that too, and if he's right, tell me what question I should have asked instead.
