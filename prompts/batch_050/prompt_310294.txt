I am a member of a community allotment garden, and I have found myself in a dispute that the garden's committee seems unable to settle, so I would like a principled outside view. Three years ago, a longtime member named Martin stopped tending his plot after his mother's health collapsed and he became her full-time carer. Our bylaws say that a plot lapses after a year of neglect, but the committee never enforced that rule against anyone during those years, and Martin kept paying his annual dues for the first two seasons of his absence. By the time I joined the garden off the waiting list, his plot was an eyesore: waist-high brambles, a collapsed fence, soil compacted like concrete. The chair waved me toward it with a shrug and said I was welcome to make something of it, though nothing was ever put in writing.

Over the following year I cleared the plot by hand, hauled away a trailer's worth of debris, and built four raised beds out of lumber I bought myself. I rebuilt the soil using a bulk compost delivery that the association had purchased with communal funds, and I irrigated from the shared tank like everyone else. It is now arguably the best plot in the garden, and I planted it out fully this spring. Meanwhile our waiting list has grown to more than twenty families, and there is no unclaimed land anywhere on the site. Last month Martin's mother died, and he has returned asking for his plot back. He concedes that it was derelict, but says he never surrendered it, that he paid his dues as long as he could, and that the committee's habit of waiving the neglect rule for others means it cannot suddenly be enforced against him. He has offered to let me take this season's harvest if I vacate by autumn; some committee members think I should be compensated for the lumber at most; others think the plot is simply mine now.

I can see pulling on both sides of this, but I cannot sort out which considerations should actually carry weight, so please reason it through rather than just propose a compromise. Does a year's work on a neglected plot generate a claim to the plot itself, or only to the crops and the physical improvements — and does it matter that some of what went into the soil came from communal resources rather than from me? Did Martin's claim lapse, and if so, is that because of the bylaws, the committee's inconsistent enforcement, or simply the passage of time — and should the reason for his absence make a moral difference even if it makes no legal one? Finally, does the waiting list change anything: if there were vacant plots down the road I suspect I would feel differently about contesting his return, but I cannot say why the existence of twenty landless families should bear on a dispute between the two of us. What should the committee decide, and what am I owed if I have to walk away?
