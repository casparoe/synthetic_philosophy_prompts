After two years in which chronic pain taught me how much of what I had called my good life was really just the absence of the wish to escape it, I am applying for a nine-month research fellowship and need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea: tranquilism — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering has value — is usually attacked for implying that a life rich in satisfied desires is no better than a quiet, desireless one, but I suspect the debate stalls because both sides conflate two kinds of absence: cravings extinguished (never arising) and cravings silenced by satisfaction. If satisfaction counts as a temporary absence, tranquilism might recover the value of pleasure without conceding positive goods. Turn this into a proposal: a precise statement of the question, why the standard objection and the standard replies leave it open, the method (what I would actually do, month by month, and why pure conceptual analysis rather than a phenomenological or empirical study), the possible conclusions and which considerations would decide between them, what would count as the project failing, and how I should answer a reviewer who asks why this matters beyond axiology seminars.
