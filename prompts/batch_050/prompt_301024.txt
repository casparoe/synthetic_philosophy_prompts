I host a philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is a metaphysician who defends the constitution view of material objects: the statue and the lump of clay are numerically distinct things that spatially coincide, differing in their persistence conditions and modal properties. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question each on the following: the grounding problem — given that the statue and the lump share all their parts and occupy exactly the same region of space, what makes them two rather than one; the counting worry — whether it is genuinely profligate, or merely surprising, to say that two objects sit on the pedestal; the squashing case — whether anything of value is lost when the statue is flattened, given that every atom remains; and the extension of the view to persons, where constitution threatens a too-many-thinkers problem: if my body constitutes me, does my body also think my thoughts? For each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give — for example, if she answers the grounding problem by appealing to different sortals and persistence conditions, press on whether sortals can bear that much metaphysical weight without the view collapsing into a claim about our concepts rather than about the world. Add one closing question about what would make her abandon constitution in favor of a rival such as perdurantism or the one-object view. After the list, say how confident you are that each anticipated reply is the one an actual defender of the view would give, and single out the question you are least sure would put genuine pressure on her position.
