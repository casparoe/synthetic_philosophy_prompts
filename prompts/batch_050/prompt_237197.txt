My evolutionary biology proffessor has this running thing where, any time someone brings up a trait in seminar, she rules on whether it's an adaptation, a byproduct, or something in between. Her verdicts so far:

- male nipples in humans: byproduct, obviously not selected for
- the human chin: byproduct of how the growth fields of the jaw interact, not an adaptation (she said this was a famous example from some paper, Gould I think?)
- the redness of blood: byproduct of hemoglobin chemistry, nothing needed blood to be red
- feathers: originally for insulation and only later co-opted for flight, so as flight structures they're "exaptations," not straight adaptations
- reading: a byproduct of brain machinery that was built for other things
- viper heat-sensing pits: adaptation, clear case
- disgust at feces and rotting meat: adaptation, pathogen avoidance

Last week someone asked her about the human appendix. Apparently it's not the useless vestige everyone assumed -- comparative work suggests something appendix-like evolved independently many times across mammals, and there's evidence it acts as a kind of safe house for good gut bacteria, repopulating the gut after an infection flushes everything out. She said she'd rule on it next session and I have a small bet going with a classmate, so: predict her verdict.

More than the verdict itself I want to know what rule she's actually running, because I can see at least two that fit her track record. One: a trait is an adaptation only if selection shaped it for the job it currently does, and convergent evolution across lineages is the gold-standard evidence of selection -- in which case the appendix is looking like an adaptation. Two: a Gould-style default where, if a trait can be explained as a leftover or a developmental byproduct, you don't get to invoke selection for it -- in which case the appendix is at most an exaptation, a vestige that found a second career. Say which rule you think she's on and why, and tell me a case that would settle it for real.

Also, since I'm literally betting on this: say how confident you are in the prediction, and single out the one claim in your answer you'd least want to put money on.
