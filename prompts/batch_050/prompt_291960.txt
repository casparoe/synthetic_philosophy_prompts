I've been co-running a war & peace seminar where the same fight breaks out every year between the game-theory people and the critics. So: draft the joint statement that a committed Schelling-style deterrence theorist and a committed member of the "threat to retaliate is irrational" camp could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. The first camp thinks nuclear deterrence is stable and can be made rational (via brinkmanship, threats that leave something to chance, reputation, precommitment); the second thinks that once the missiles are incoming there's no reason left to retaliate, so the threat can't be credible to anyone who sees this, and deterrence rests on luck or self-deception.

I want the claims both sides can actually sign (e.g., whatever they agree perfect rationality plus perfect information would do to deterrent threats, and whether the paradox is specific to nukes), then the precise residual disagreements in wording each camp would accept for its own side, then the cruxes, and for each crux the argument or case both agree bears on it with each side's prediction recorded -- my guess is one crux is whether an agent can rationally bind its future self against its own future reasons, and another is whether past non-execution of threats has or hasn't undermined credibility. If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements and cruxes. Lead with the joint statement itself, no preface or framing remarks.
