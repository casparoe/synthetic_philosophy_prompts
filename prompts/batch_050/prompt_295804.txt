I've never studied philosophy or biology formally, but I've been reading about living things on my own for years, and I keep circling back to one question: what makes it true that a body part has a function at all? As best I can tell from my own thinking, there are two main answers, which I'll describe in my own words so we're on the same page. The history-first answer says a trait's function comes from its past: the heart's job is to pump blood because pumping blood is what hearts did that kept heart-havers alive and reproducing, so a trait only has a function if it was kept around (or copied) because of what it did. The contribution answer says a trait's function is whatever it currently does that helps the whole system work: the heart's function is pumping because pumping is how the heart contributes to keeping the organism going, full stop, regardless of history.

I've been keeping a notebook of test cases where these two answers pull apart, to see which one survives. Here's what I have so far:

1. An animal assembled by sheer freak accident (a bolt of lightning in a swamp, say) that ends up molecule-for-molecule identical to a normal lion. The contribution view says its heart has the function of pumping, since it does everything a lion's heart does. The history-first view says nothing in it has any function, because none of its parts have the right kind of past.
2. A deformed heart that works anyway: an animal born with a heart that came out wrong by pure developmental luck, yet happens to pump blood just fine, and this quirk is never passed on. Contribution says it has the function of pumping; history-first seems forced to deny it.
3. A brand-new beneficial mutation: the very first animal ever to have some helpful trait, so the trait has no track record yet. History-first says it can't have a function yet (nothing has been retained because of it); contribution says it plainly does.
4. A leftover trait that no longer does anything useful, like the wings of a bird that can no longer fly or the human appendix. Contribution struggles to say these still have a function, since they contribute nothing now; history-first can say they once had one, or that their function has lapsed.
5. The heart's thumping noise versus its pumping. Both views want to say pumping is the function and the noise is a mere side effect, but they explain the contrast differently: history-first says pumping is what hearts were kept around for and noise wasn't; contribution has to argue that pumping, but not noise, is what explains the organism's working.

What I want is one more test case, and I want it to be genuinely different from these five. Not another freak-origin case (no more accidental copies or lucky deformations), not another case about new traits lacking a track record, not another case about traits that stopped contributing, and not another function-versus-side-effect contrast. It should have a structure of its own, so that someone who agreed with how cases 1 through 5 come out could still be surprised by yours. Ideally it should be a case where the two views genuinely diverge, not one where both can comfortably agree.

Please give me the case, then: (a) say what the contribution view has to say about it and what the history-first view has to say about it, (b) tell me honestly which view it puts more pressure on and whether that pressure is serious or just awkward, and (c) flag whether you think the case is truly new in structure or whether it's secretly a disguised version of one of my five — I'd rather you catch that yourself than leave it to me.

Please write your answer in American English, and keep technical jargon to a minimum, since I'm a layperson.
