Quick definitions so we're calibrated. SSA: reason as if you're a random sample from all the observers in your reference class across the whole of history. SIA: reason as if you're a random sample from all observers, period — so theories that predict more observers get a likelihood boost from the sheer fact that you exist, proportional to how many they predict. The notorious consequence is the presumptuous philosopher problem: take two cosmological theories with equal priors, one saying ours is the only intelligent civilization anywhere, the other saying there are a trillion of them; SIA says you should be nearly certain of the crowded one before anyone so much as looks through a telescope.

Here's my opening, and don't pivot away from it. Fine — I'll grant you the trillion-to-one update, existence is evidence, bullet bitten. But now take any live scientific dispute where the rival theories predict different observer counts: inflationary multiverse vs. a one-shot universe, say. On your view the rational procedure is: estimate the population ratio, note that it crushes any plausible prior odds, and go home. The decisive experiment was already performed the moment you noticed you exist, and telescope time is just decor. So either you think cosmologists can rationally settle observer-count-sensitive questions from the armchair, or you owe me a principled line between your update and that one. Which is it? And no hiding behind 'it depends on the reference class' — pick observers-with-my-evidence and the arguement still runs. If your Sleeping Beauty commitments are load-bearing here, go ahead and use them, but only if they actually are.
