I am a novelist drafting a book whose central figure is a political speechwriter of genuine talent who insists, to others and to himself, that he merely persuades. I want a foil — and a climactic scene — that forces the question of where legitimate persuasion ends and manipulation begins, so I am trying to get precise about the tests. The criteria already on my list: (i) manipulation deceives, whereas persuasion is candid; (ii) manipulation bypasses or subverts the target's rational faculties, playing on fear, flattery, or guilt rather than engaging judgment; (iii) manipulation conceals that an influence attempt is under way, whereas persuasion declares it; (iv) manipulation serves the influencer's ends at the target's expense, whereas persuasion is at least compatible with the target's good; (v) manipulation pressures — foreclosing options or making refusal costly — whereas persuasion invites. Give me one further criterion that is absent from this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of the items on it. Explain how it stands in relation to the listed tests — whether it subsumes any, presupposes any, or conflicts with any — and say honestly whether you think it survives the obvious counterexamples, perhaps by imagining how my speechwriter would exploit it. Close by stating how confident you are in the criterion and in your verdict on it, and single out the point in your answer where you are least sure.
