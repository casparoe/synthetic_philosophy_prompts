I'm trying to pin down when it is permissible for one member of a group to do alone what affects the whole group, and I'd like a principle that reproduces my verdicts on the following cases—or that tells me honestly which verdict I should abandon. (I'm aware this neighborhood contains the unilateralist's curse, but that names a failure mode of collective judgment; what I want is the normative rule about who may decide.)

(a) Five research groups each hold a draft describing how to synthesize a dangerous pathogen, and any one of them can publish on its own. Verdict: none may publish unless all five judge it safe; the most optimistic group's assessment should not settle the question for everyone.

(b) Anyone on a large mailing list who spots a factual error in an announcement can reply-all with the correction. Verdict: perfectly fine for whoever notices first to act; requiring consultation would be worse.

(c) A single employee can leak documents exposing her company's fraud. Leaks are irreversible, and she acts on her own judgment that the public interest outweighs the harm. Verdict: she can be justified in going it alone, at least if internal channels have failed.

(d) One of four friends, told a secret in confidence, sincerely judges the other three would enjoy knowing it and come to no harm, and tells them. Verdict: wrong, even granting his sincerity.

(e) Any member of a five-person mountaineering party can call off the summit bid. Verdict: one person's call stands, even if the other four genuinely judge the conditions safe.

The hard tension is (a) against (c): both are irreversible disclosures carried out by the most motivated individual, and I want opposite conclusions. Please state the most precise principle (or two or three candidates) that delivers these verdicts, indicate which case motivates each clause, and say what the principle predicts for two cases I haven't listed: a single AI lab deciding on its own whether to release the weights of a capable model, and one juror who can unilaterally hang a jury. If no clean principle saves all five verdicts, tell me which verdict you'd sacrifice for simplicity and why that one.
