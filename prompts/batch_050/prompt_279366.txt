So I'm a novelist, and for years I've "seen" my scenes before I write them -- faces, light, the layout of a room -- and I always assumed readers did the same thing when they read. Then I found out a bunch of people (including some readers who love my stuff) report experiencing nothing visual at all, and meanwhile I got sucked into the old fight about whether mental imagery is real picture-ish representations or just propositions/descriptions that feel pictorial from the inside (Kosslyn vs. Pylyshyn, roughly). Fifty years of scanning brains and timing mental rotation and it feels like no one has moved.

Here's my question: what kind of disagreement is this actually? My suspicion is that the two sides aren't answering the same question -- one side is asking what the experience is like, the other is asking what format the brain stores information in, and both keep winning arguments the other side wasn't having. Is that right, or is there a genuine empirical disagreement in there that brain scans or behavior experiments could have settled but haven't? Diagnose it using what each side actually claims, tell me what (if anything) would count as real progress, and bonus: does the existence of people with no imagery at all break the stalemate, or just give both sides more ammo?
