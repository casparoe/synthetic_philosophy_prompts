In his tower of generate-and-test, Dennett distinguishes four kinds of minds by the way candidates for action are produced and selected. I have the standard taxonomy in front of me and do not need it repeated: Darwinian creatures, whose behavioural repertoire is fixed and hard-wired, so that selection operates only across organisms over generations; Skinnerian creatures, which can generate variable behaviours within a lifetime and have them reinforced or extinguished by the environment; Popperian creatures, which can represent candidate actions in an inner model of the world, so that hypotheses can be tested and eliminated in the head rather than in the field; and Gregorian creatures, which import designed mind-tools from their culture -- words, numerals, diagrams, habits of thought -- so that the inner environment in which hypotheses are tested is itself partly a cultural artefact.

I would like you to propose a fifth floor for the tower: a further kind of creature, defined by a mechanism of generating and selecting candidate thoughts or behaviours that is not on this list. It must not be a mere subtype, hybrid, or re-description of one of the four (in particular, it should not just be a Popperian creature with fancier models, or a Gregorian creature with fancier tools), nor a regression to something below the Darwinian floor, such as a purely hard-wired automaton. For your proposed creature, please: (a) state precisely what does the generating and what does the selecting, and on what timescale; (b) explain what selective advantage it would confer that the four existing kinds do not already provide; (c) describe what empirical evidence -- in animals, humans, or artificial systems -- would show that the mechanism is actually instantiated rather than merely possible; and (d) give an honest verdict on whether your proposal is a genuinely distinct floor of the tower or whether it collapses, on closer inspection, into one of the existing four.

Write the answer as a connected essay in British English.
