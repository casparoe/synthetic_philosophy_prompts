My pitch to her is that this matters a lot for reporting, because we quote people explaining their own choices constantly (voters, whistleblowers, CEOs, athletes, whoever) and mostly treat those quotes as straightforward evidence about why they did the thing. I want the session to change how they use quotes like that, without turning them into people who think every self-report is worthless, which is its own failure mode.

Help me plan the explanatory arc rather than just giving me the content. Specifically I want: the order in which the ideas have to be introduced and why -- i.e. which distinction has to be in place before the rest makes sense. My instinct is that the difference between knowing what you're currently experiencing and knowing why you did something has to come early, because the findings land very differently on those two, but tell me if I'm wrong about that. Then the one example to build the whole session around -- I'm leaning toward those experiments where people explain a choice they didn't actually make and don't notice anything odd -- but tell me what a carrying example here must have and must lack, because a bad version of it lets people shrug it off as a lab trick.
