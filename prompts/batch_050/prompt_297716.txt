Write a parody of a wellness-magazine advice column, maybe 550-650 words, in which the guru has fully converted to tranquilism -- the view that the only thing that matters for wellbeing is the absence of craving and suffering, and that pleasure has no value in itself, only as a way of relieving some itch or want (the way scratching is only good because the itch was bad). Everything the guru recommends follows from this, consistently and deadpan: a 30-day program for deleting desires instead of fulfilling them, praise for the enlightened life strategy of never developing a taste for anything expensive, an explanation of why the ideal evening is the one where you notice nothing is bothering you, and a rankings sidebar where a very calm houseplant scores well above a thrilled lottery winner. Include at least two reader letters (someone asking whether they should still bother with a birthday party for their kid, someone asking if falling in love is a good idea) and have the guru answer them in a way that's perfectly internally consistent and quietly horrifying, without the guru ever noticing. The jokes should all come from actually applying the view -- the asymmetry between relieving a craving and creating a new one, the problem that a good meal first requires hunger, the awkward fact that the best day of your life is indistinguishable from a nap -- so someone who knows the position laughs at recognition rather than at a strawman, and someone who doesn't still gets why the column is funny. Don't name any real philosophers, invent the guru.
