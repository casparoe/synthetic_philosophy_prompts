So I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and I've run into a thing in my current draft that I keep needing a word for. Quick setup: in one chapter a sculptor shapes a lump of clay into a bust of the narrator's dead sister. The bust is just... the clay, sitting there, same atoms, same shelf. But later in the book someone flattens the bust back into a ball, and the way I've written it, the clay is still around -- it's the same lump it's always been -- while the bust is gone for good, and the narrator grieves it like a second death. So during the years on the shelf there were apparently two things in the exact same spot, one of which can survive being squashed and one of which can't.

My writing group is split. Half say there's obviously just one object and "the bust" is a way of talking about the lump during a phase. The other half insists you can destroy one and not the other, so they can't be identical. I feel like this has to be a known problem, but everything I stumble on is about ships being rebuilt plank by plank, which feels like a different issue -- that's about change over time, mine is about two things at one time. Does my situation have a name, is it actually the same problem as the ship thing or genuinely its own one, and if nobody's named it, what would you call it and where exactly does it stop applying?
