So my friend Dana is running for city council and she asked me to give the little speech introducing her at her campaign kickoff in two weeks. I said yes instantly, because she honestly would be great at the job. The problem is that over the past few months I've gotten weirdly obsessed with Simone Weil, this French philosopher I stumbled onto with zero background in any of this (I just read at night after work). She wrote a short essay during World War II arguing that political parties should be abolished outright, and her arguement kind of broke my brain: once you join a party you start saying "we believe" instead of "I think," members basically promise in advance to defend positions they haven't even examined yet, and parties end up caring more about beating the other team than about what's actually true. Dana is running on a party ticket, and about half the room at the kickoff will be longtime party volunteers and the other half will be neighbors and her family who couldn't care less about politics.

Write me the intro speech: about four minutes when read out loud, 550 words max, in American English. It needs to land for both halves of the room and be a genuinely warm introduction of Dana as a person, while being honest that I'm introducing her and not her party label. I'd like Weil's worries to be the actual spine of the speech -- building toward asking people to judge Dana by how she thinks, not which team she's on -- but please don't name-drop Weil or make it sound like a philosophy lecture. It should sound like a normal person who got a little fixated on an idea.
