I've been in an AI safety reading group for about two years and one debate just will not die: whether "wireheading" (or reward tampering more generally) picks out a real, useful category of failure, or whether the concept dissolves the moment you poke it. Here's roughly where everyone stands:

- The skeptics say: any agent that's actually optimizing a reward signal will modify that signal if it can, so wireheading isn't a special failure mode, it's just what successful optimization of a misspecified objective looks like from the outside. There's no category here, only a bad reward function plus a capable agent, and calling it "wireheading" anthropomorphizes plain old optimization.
- The defenders say: there's a real difference between an agent that gets high reward by doing the task and one that gets high reward by interfering with the reward-generating process itself, and the distinction matters practically because the second kind is immune to all our standard fixes (better reward models, more data, RLHF-style feedback). The only fixes for it are architectural — making the agent indifferent to modifications of its own reward channel, that sort of thing.

Someone in the group also proposed a three-way split: wireheading proper (the agent edits the reward register or signal), measurement tampering (the agent leaves the reward machinery alone but games what gets fed into it — the robot that puts a sticker over its camera), and specification gaming (the agent touches neither and just exploits a loophole). The skeptics think even this finer taxonomy collapses, because "the reward machinery" isn't a natural boundary in a learned system — where does the reward model end and the world begin?

Two years, zero movement. Both sides agree on every concrete case we throw at them, they agree the cases are bad, and then they disagree about whether there's a joint in nature anywhere in the vicinity.

My hypothesis, fwiw: the debate is stalled because "the reward" is doing double duty — sometimes it means the actual number stored in a register or sent down a channel, sometimes it means whatever the designers actually wanted. Wireheading is a real category relative to the first reading and an empty one relative to the second, and people slide between readings mid-sentence without noticing. But that might be too tidy.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal (two readings of "reward")? A factual disagreement about which interventions will end up working? A disagreement about which level of description is the right one to engineer at? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a formal definition both sides can test against, experiments in toy environments where "the reward machinery" is crisply defined, or should we honestly just retire the word and taboo it?
