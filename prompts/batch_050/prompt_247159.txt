I serve on the deployment review board at a mid-sized AI lab, and a decision has landed on my desk that I can no longer treat as routine. Three months ago our automated interpretability pipeline flagged an anomaly in our flagship model: it occasionally produced confident but wrong answers to calendar-arithmetic questions. Our interpretability team traced this to a specific internal configuration -- a small set of attention heads and sparse-autoencoder features whose causal role they confirmed by activation patching -- and the board classified it as a documented mechanism, accepted a workaround, and moved on. Last week the pipeline flagged a new anomaly: the model garbles regnal years and dynasty successions in long historical answers. The overlap is partial. The top-activated SAE features are largely the same ones from the calendar case, but the activation-patching results do not reproduce the earlier circuit's causal structure, and the failure shows up on different prompt distributions.

This classification matters more than it might seem. Under our standing policy, a recurrence of a documented mechanism triggers monitoring only; a novel mechanism triggers a full investigation and possible deployment rollback. My team ships next month, and the engineers are split down the middle on whether this is the same mechanism manifesting in a new context or a distinct one that merely shares components.

Write a memo to the board recommending a decision rule the lab should use for judging when two anomalous behaviors count as the same mechanism, and apply that rule to the case above. The memo should weigh the plausible individuation criteria on their merits -- surface behavioral similarity, overlap in activated features, identity of causal structure under interventions, and sameness of functional role in the model's computation -- and should say explicitly what each criterion treats as decisive and where it breaks down (for instance, whether two behaviors sharing a low-level component but differing in downstream causal routing should count as one mechanism or two, and whether a single mechanism misapplied to a new domain is still one mechanism). Give a fair statement of the strongest argument for the classification you recommend against, since half my team holds it. End with a clear, actionable bottom line the board can vote on.

After the memo, include a comparison table of the candidate criteria with exactly these columns: Criterion (the proposed basis for individuation); Individuates By (what it treats as decisive for sameness); Handles Well (a case type it classifies correctly); Handles Badly (a case type where it gives an implausible verdict); Verdict on Our Case (how it classifies the regnal-years anomaly relative to the calendar one); and Overall Assessment (adopt, adopt with amendments, or reject, in one sentence).
