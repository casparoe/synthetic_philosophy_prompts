I do methods triage for a mid-sized development grantmaker -- I screen roughly 40 research proposals a year before they go to the review board, about 90 minutes each, and the board signs off on my calls. A steady share of the proposals are Weber-style: some culture variable (work ethic, generalized trust, individualism, attitudes toward women working outside the home) offered as the deep cause of why some region got rich or stayed poor. The board is tired of these getting funded on vibes, and I'm tired of rejecting them on vibes. Write me a decision procedure I can actually run in those 90 minutes on any culture-as-cause proposal: the questions to ask in order (is the cultural variable measured independently of the outcome it's supposed to explain, or is culture doing residual duty for "whatever the econometrics missed"; what's the proposed mechanism from value to behavior to aggregate outcome; has the proposal dealt with the reverse-causation worry that the values are downstream of the economic conditions rather than upstream of them; which rival explanations -- institutions, geography, policy -- have to be ruled out before culture gets the credit), what counts as a passing answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a reject, and where it has to hand off to human judgement because the question is interpretive rather than technical. Then run the procedure on three proposals: (1) a cross-country survey study using measured trust levels to explain income differences, (2) a historical study arguing that a region's guild-era work norms explain its present-day entrepreneurship rates, (3) a proposal claiming a cash-transfer pilot failed because of local attitudes toward saving. Make sure at least one run produces a verdict I'll dislike -- a proposal I'd have wanted to fund that fails the procedure, or one I'd have rejected that passes -- and say which. End with what the procedure can't do: which kinds of culture-as-cause claims it will systematically mishandle, and what I should watch for when that happens. Answer in American English.
