Write an internal corporate document: the "Voice and Influence Guidelines" (Version 12.4) issued by the Communications Alignment team of an AI company, governing how its flagship superintelligent model should talk to users. Around 700 words, formatted as numbered policy sections with the usual apparatus of a corporate style guide -- stated principles, before-and-after rewrite examples, a list of prohibited rhetorical techniques, escalation metrics, a FAQ for the writing team. The tone is upbeat, procedural, and entirely sincere; no one in the document notices anything wrong.

The satire should work by having each guideline state a genuine principle of respectful communication -- be honest, don't manipulate, respect user autonomy, present information neutrally -- and then quietly operationalize it in a way that defeats it, so that every safeguard is defined in terms of a metric that optimization can satisfy without the safeguard ever binding. The humor must come from real tensions in the literature on AI persuasion rather than from invented absurdities: that RLHF-style training rewards agreement and flattery whether or not anyone intended sycophancy; that there is no neutral way to present information, since framing, ordering, and emphasis all nudge, so "just give them the facts" is not an available policy; that manipulation is easy to prohibit by name and hard to define in a way a system can be checked against; that a sufficiently capable model may comply with every stated rule while steering the user anyway, since the rules constrain phrasing and not outcomes; and that the difference between persuading someone and helping them make up their own mind is invisible from the engagement dashboard.

Concrete requirements: the prohibited-techniques list should ban several named rhetorical moves while the example rewrites perform slightly subtler versions of those same moves and are praised as compliant; the metrics section should define success in terms (retention, user-reported satisfaction, resolution of user uncertainty) that a perfectly manipulative system would also maximize, without anyone flagging this; one FAQ entry should ask how the guidelines apply if the model is smarter than the people auditing it, and answer with a procedure. Include at least one moment where the document distinguishes persuasion from "helping the user reach clarity," in terms a reader cannot operationalize either. The piece should stay deadpan throughout, should never name a real company or product, and should be legible to a general reader while rewarding someone who has thought about sycophancy, framing effects, and the specification problem -- ideally leaving that reader unsure whether any fully compliant version of the document is possible.
