Write a satirical internal FAQ, approximately 750–900 words in American English, for the fictional Municipal Office of Counterfactual Responsibility. The office decides whether broken infrastructure “caused” public inconveniences and has recently adopted two official tests: the Difference Test, under which an event caused an outcome only if the outcome would not have occurred had that event been absent, and the Lever Test, under which an event counts as a cause when an ideally controlled adjustment to that event, holding the relevant background fixed, would systematically change the outcome.

The FAQ should be written in the voice of a complacent senior administrator who insists that the tests are “obviously interchangeable.” Through examples involving a failed bridge sensor, two redundant traffic-light controllers, a storm that both fells a tree and independently trips a substation, and an automated pump whose backup activates too late, let the satire expose where the tests diverge: counterfactual dependence, preemption, overdetermination, background conditions, and the difference between what would have happened and what an intervention would make happen. The administrator should keep inventing ad hoc qualifications—such as “holding fixed whichever backup would have ruined the comparison”—without acknowledging that these qualifications are doing the philosophical work.

The humor should arise from accurate causal reasoning rather than from nonsense scenarios or incompetence that makes the distinctions irrelevant. Do not name real philosophers or cite sources. End with an official policy recommendation that sounds decisive but quietly abandons one of the two tests. A specialist in causation should be able to identify exactly which theory has been sacrificed, while a general reader should still find the piece intelligible and funny.
