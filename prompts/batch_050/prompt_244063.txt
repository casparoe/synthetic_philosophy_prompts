You're a comedy writer who also happens to have a PhD in phenomenology, and you've been asked to write a parody Amazon-style product review, around 450 words, for a product called The Lived Body (Model: Mine). The reviewer is steeped in Merleau-Ponty and rates the product accordingly. The humor has to come from real features of the account in Phenomenology of Perception, not made-up ones: motor intentionality, i.e. the body already reaching for the doorknob or catching the falling glass before the owner authorizes anything, with the body's practical I-can running ahead of any reflective I-think; habit as the body silently downloading the width of the car or the layout of a keyboard without ever notifying the user; the phantom limb as a subscription that keeps auto-renewing after the hardware is gone; the way the body constantly tunes itself toward the best available grip on whatever it's engaged with. A specialist should laugh because the review gets the phenomenology right; someone who's never heard of Merleau-Ponty should still enjoy it as a review of being stuck inside a body. Give it a star rating the reviewer visibly agonizes over (too embodied to give zero, too mortal to give five, that kind of thing). Then, after the review, step out of character briefly and flag where you leaned on a contested reading of Merleau-Ponty -- e.g. how thoroughly non-representational motor intentionality really is, or what the phantom limb discussion is actually meant to show -- and name one alternative reading under which a specific joke in your review would fall flat.
