So I'm TAing an intro logic course this semester and we just got to the part where students ask why they have to accept modus ponens at all, like what justifies the rule itself rather than the premises. I mentioned Carroll's tortoise piece to them -- the one where the tortoise keeps granting every premise but refuses to take the inferential step, so Achilles keeps adding the step as another premise, and it just goes forever -- and half the class found it more upsetting than any skeptical argument we'd covered. I want to close the last session with something that captures why it's unsettling instead of just summarizing it, so: write me a villanelle about the regress.

The two refrains should do the actual philosophical work. One of them should keep reasserting the tortoise's move -- that every time you write the inference rule down as a premise, a fresh gap opens between the new longer premise list and the conclusion, so the demand for one more premise regenerates no matter how many you add. The other refrain should track the moral, or a moral: that a rule of inference isn't a premise among premises but something you do, and that treating it as one more thing to be asserted is exactly the category mistake that feeds the regress. The whole point of the villanelle form is that the same lines come back in new contexts and mean slightly different things each time, which is literally the structure of the tortoise's trap, so lean into that -- by the final quatrain the refrains should land differently than they did at the start, ideally so the reader feels the difference between seeing the rule stated and actually inferring.

Keep it accurate enough that someone who's taught this paper would nod rather than wince: the tortoise never denies a premise, only withholds the step; adding "if these premises then the conclusion" as premise n+1 doesn't help; and the regress isn't solved by any further proposition but only by, well, going ahead and concluding. Don't name-drop Achilles and the tortoise constantly, maybe once. Roughly standard villanelle form (nineteen lines, five tercets and a quatrain, the aba rhyme), though I'll forgive a slant rhyme or two. Start with the poem itself, no preface or explanation.
