So I've been going back and forth with a guy from my philosophy reading group about longtermism and whether the simulation argument should change how much we care about the far future. His line is that if a Bostrom-style argument is right and we might be sims, then the far future isn't really ours to affect, so the usual fanatical reasoning (tiny probabilities, astronomically huge stakes) loses its grip -- the far future is someone else's, or a fake one. I think that's way too quick: if sims can influence their simulators, or if the base-reality people running us face versions of the same decisions, the stakes might multiply instead of shrinking. The problem is neither of us can say what a sensible view here would even need to DO, and every take we find just picks one case and runs with it.

So: what would a theory of how much weight to give the far future under simulation uncertainty actually have to get right? I want the full spec first, before any verdicts.
