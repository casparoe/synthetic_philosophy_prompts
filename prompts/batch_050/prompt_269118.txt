I'm a phd student in cog sci / phil mind starting a dissertation chapter on tangled hierarchies, and before I read any more I want a spec to judge theories against. The setup as I understand it: in the Escher lithograph Drawing Hands, two hands each draw the other, and Hofstadter's point is that Escher himself sits on an inviolate level outside the tangle, untouched by it. He seems to think real systems (brains, formal systems) likewise have a level the self-reference loop can never reach. So: what would a theory of tangled hierarchies have to deliver to count as adequate? Give me the desiderata, with the case or consideration behind each. Some cases I think any contender has to handle:

- the picture itself: what makes Escher's level inviolate, and does that generalize to real systems or is it an artifact of a static image
- formal systems: PA can talk about itself via gödel numbering but can't rewrite its own axioms from inside -- is that the same kind of inviolateness as Escher's or a different one, and the theory has to say which
- hardware/software: software supposedly can't touch the physics of the chip it runs on, except rowhammer-style attacks flip bits in DRAM purely from software. A serious theory has to say what happens to the inviolate level there -- does it dissolve, or just get relocated a level down
- self-amending constitutions: legal systems revise themselves, but some clauses are entrenched (germany's eternity clause). Is entrenchment genuine inviolateness or just a convention that holds until a revolution ignores it?

Also: which of these requirements are negotiable and which aren't, whether some pair is jointly unsatisfiable (my hunch is that "every tangle needs an untangled base level" and "no level is ever truly inviolate" can't both survive, which would make the list itself a result), and which single desideratum knocks out the most positions. Then briefly -- and I mean briefly, the list is the point -- score the obvious contenders against it: strict hierarchy (tangles are always illusions), Hofstadter-style strange loops, and full relativity (inviolateness is always relative to an observer or attacker).
