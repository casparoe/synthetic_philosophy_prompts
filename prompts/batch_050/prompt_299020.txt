ok so every year the wittgenstein seminar i TA ends with a weird-poetry night (long story, the prof started it and now its tradition) and this year i want to contribute something better than the usual limerick about private languages. my idea: a sestina about family resemblance that doesn't just describe the idea but actually *is* an instance of it. like, the form itself should make the point.

so the specs: standard sestina, six stanzas of six lines plus the three-line envoi, with the usual rotation of end-words. pick six end-words that are each capable of genuinely different uses -- im thinking words along the lines of "game," "play," "rule," "name," "measure," "move," but swap in better ones if you find them. the hard constraint: each time an end-word comes around again it has to be doing a different job, a different sense, a different little language-game. "game" as chess in one stanza, as what hunters hunt in another, as in "i'm game" somewhere else, a game leg, child's games, and so on. no single meaning should run through all the uses of any one word, but neighboring uses should overlap with each other the way his examples of games do -- so that someone tracing a single word down through the stanzas gets that thread-with-no-one-fiber-running-the-whole-length effect from the investigations. aim for at least four genuinely distinct uses per word across the poem; if the rotation forces a repeat somewhere, fine, as long as it doesnt collapse into one dictionary definition holding the whole thing together.

the envoi is where im most stuck, since it has to jam all six words into three lines and envois usually sound forced. id love it if here the cramming felt like the point landing instead -- like the poem, and the concept, only hangs together the way it does because of all the overlapping uses, not despite them. audience is a dozen grad students who will absolutely audit whether each use is distinct and will heckle me if two stanzas use a word the same way, so be careful. and dont name-drop anything in the lines themselves, no "language-games" or wittgenstein references in the verse, the structure should carry it. if you want to tack on a short paragraph after the poem explaining how the rotation enacts the idea, go ahead, i can steal from it for my intro remarks.
