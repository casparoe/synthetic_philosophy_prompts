Write a sestina about Douglas Hofstadter's claim, developed in I Am a Strange Loop, that the human 'I' is not an entity residing in the brain but a self-referential symbol: a pattern a brain builds to model itself, which then looms so large in the model that it comes to seem like the thing doing the perceiving. Choose your six end-words so that three of them belong to the lower level of description (neurons, chemistry, signalling) and three to the upper level (self, meaning, point of view), and let the sestina's machinery do philosophical work: because the form forces the same words to return in ever-altered positions, the reader should feel the two levels re-entering and re-describing one another, which is precisely the loop the poem is about. The envoi, which compresses all six words into three lines, should enact the strangest part of the view — that the 'I' is a small pattern which appears, from the inside, to contain the whole system that contains it. Render the position accurately enough that someone who knows the book would recognise its actual commitments (the Gödelian inspiration, the apparent causal grip of the symbolic level on the physical) rather than a vague trope about mirrors, while remaining a good poem for someone who has never heard of a strange loop.
