For a seminar on the evolution of cognition, I need a dialogue (roughly 900–1200 words) between two philosophers applying Dennett's taxonomy of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures — to large language models. One argues that LLMs are essentially Skinnerian creatures: their capacities were shaped by reinforcement-like selection on outputs, and nothing inside a single forward pass deserves to be called hypothesis-testing. The other argues that they are Popperian creatures at inference time: generating, comparing, and discarding candidate continuations lets hypotheses die in the model's stead. Have the dialogue turn on a distinction neither speaker initially makes explicit — whether Popperian status depends on where the selection process happens (inside versus outside the system) or on who bears the cost of failed hypotheses — and on the further wrinkle of whether an LLM used as a thinking-tool by a Gregorian creature thereby acquires Gregorian status itself, or remains the tool rather than the thinker. Both speakers should be equally competent, respond to each other's actual arguments rather than restate prepared positions, and end by identifying what empirical or conceptual evidence would genuinely settle their disagreement.
