I coordinate a hospital's tumor review board. Roughly twenty times a month, a panel of seven clinicians must reach a binary call on borderline cases — biopsy vs. watch-and-wait — and we currently just deliberate and take a show of hands. I've been told the Condorcet jury theorem says majority voting amplifies individual competence, but also that it fails badly when voters are correlated or worse-than-chance, which seems like exactly the situation a deliberating panel of colleagues is in. Write me a decision procedure for how the board should structure its judgment: what to establish about individual competence and independence before any vote is taken, how the voting and discussion steps should be ordered, when majority rule should be replaced by something else (weighted votes, a threshold other than simple majority, deferral to the single best diagnostician), and where the procedure must stop and hand the call to clinical judgment because the theorem's assumptions simply cannot be checked. Apply the procedure to two cases from my practice: one where all seven members saw the same ambiguous scan in a shared meeting beforehand, and one where three junior members are known to defer to a senior colleague. Include at least one outcome the board will dislike. Mark every step where judgment cannot be eliminated, and state what the procedure cannot do. Maximum 700 words.
