Okay so I'm a novelist, mostly sci-fi and a bit of literary stuff, and I keep hitting this weird wall in my writing that apparently philosophers have an actual name for: readers will swallow literally any impossible thing I invent (dragons, backwards time, a planet where lying is physically impossible) but the second I write a world where, say, cruelty is just straightforwardly good, people bounce off it. They can't or won't go along. I was complaining about this to a friend and she said philosophers call it imaginative resistance and have like five different theories about what's going on.

So here's what I want: a decision tree / sorting quiz that takes someone from "huh yeah, that IS weird" to one of the main named positions on imaginative resistance. I'm thinking maybe 5 to 7 branching questions. The catches:

- Each question has to be answerable by someone who has never heard the labels. So don't ask "do you think the problem is alethic or phenomenological?" -- ask something like "could you write a version where the reader does go along with it, if you were a good enough writer, or is that impossible in principle?"
- I want the leaves to include at least: the view that it's just impossible to imagine moral falsehoods as true, full stop; the view that we could but refuse because imagining it feels like endorsing it in real life; the view that it's a genre/convention problem (we read the narrator as making claims about our world unless signaled otherwise); and a deflationary leaf where the puzzle basically dissolves. Add others if the tree needs them.
- Use cases I'd actually run into as a writer: an unreliable narrator, a comedy where the cruelty is the joke and it works, alien cultures with genuinely different values, a story told from a villain's POV where you're clearly meant to disagree.
- Flag any two leaves your tree can't actually tell apart, and note if there's a combo of answers nobody defends and whether that's because it's incoherent or just unexplored.

I'm not looking for a verdict on which theory is TRUE exactly, but at the end give me your best guess about which position would be most useful for me as a working novelist (not the same thing!), and tell me what would change your mind about that -- like, what observation about how readers actually respond would make you pick a different leaf. Also flag where your tree is most likely to be oversimplifying the actual debate.

American English please, and keep it readable -- I'm a fiction writer, not a grad student lol.
