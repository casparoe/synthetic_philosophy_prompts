Okay so I help run a small discord for amateur poker players and sports bettors and we keep having the same argument: when you have a real edge, what fraction of your bankroll do you put on the line? Everybody name-drops the Kelly criterion but it turns out people mean totally different things when they say why its the right answer, and the fights go nowhere because nobody realizes they're disagreeing about the justification, not the formula.

So: build me a decision tree for a handout. It should take someone from the shared starting point — "a bettor with a genuine edge faces a long, repeated sequence of similar wagers and must pick a fixed staking fraction" — to a named position on what makes a staking rule rational. Every branch node has to be a question someone can answer without already knowing the labels (so nothing like "do you accept the ergodic hypothesis?"). Each leaf gets the position's name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

The tree has to be able to reach all of these leaves, roughly 6-7 total:

1. Full Kelly justified by almost-sure long-run dominance — it beats any other fraction with probability 1 as the number of bets grows, so utility functions never enter the picture.
2. Kelly as plain expected-utility maximization with log utility — nothing deep about time or growth, log just happens to be your utility function.
3. The deflationary position that Kelly has no special claim at all, since log is just one utility function among many and full Kelly is irrational for anyone whose utility isn't log (Samuelson-style).
4. The view that maximizing the time-average growth rate of wealth is the foundation of rational choice itself, not merely a betting rule — arithmetic/ensemble averages are the wrong tool for anything multiplicative.
5. Fractional Kelly (say half-Kelly) as the genuinely rational answer once you admit your edge estimate is noisy — the shrinkage isn't squeamishness, it's the correct response to parameter uncertainty.
6. A behavioral rejection: real people don't and shouldn't maximize growth, preferences are reference-dependent, and log's comfort with long drawdowns isn't a feature.

Questions I want somewhere on the tree, though you can reorder or rephrase: whether ruin/absorbing-zero changes what "average" even means for a multiplicative process (the geometric-vs-arithmetic issue), whether the justification leans on the sequence being stationary/i.i.d. and what happens when it isn't, and whether shrinking below full Kelly out of risk aversion is rational or just an admission you belong in leaf 2 or 3 with a different utility function.

Also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate (I suspect 1 and 4 collapse into each other — if so, say what, if anything, distinguishes them), and note any combination of answers that's unoccupied and diagnose why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Write it in American English, and keep the branch questions short enough that a guy in the discord with a stats 101 background can answer them.
