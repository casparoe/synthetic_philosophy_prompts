Exam question: State the paradox of the stone. Does it show that omnipotence is logically impossible? Discuss at least two distinct responses to the paradox.

Student's answer:

The paradox of the stone asks whether an omnipotent God can create a stone so heavy that He cannot lift it. If He can create it, there is something He cannot do, namely lift it; if He cannot create it, there is likewise something He cannot do. Either way omnipotence fails, so the paradox demonstrates that the concept of omnipotence is internally contradictory and that no omnipotent being could possibly exist.

Aquinas responded that God is bound by the laws of logic, which stand above Him as external constraints, so His inability to do logically impossible things is not a genuine limitation on His power. Mavrodes answers differently: since the phrase "a stone an omnipotent being cannot lift" describes something contradictory, creating such a stone is a pseudo-task, and being unable to perform a pseudo-task does not count against omnipotence. A third response, associated with Harry Frankfurt, grants that God can create the stone but maintains He can lift it anyway, thereby accepting that God can do the logically impossible.

The paradox is therefore not decisive against every conception of omnipotence, but it does prove that classical theism is false.
