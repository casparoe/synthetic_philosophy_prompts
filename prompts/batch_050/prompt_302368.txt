So I've been chewing on something and I can't figure out if there's already a term for it. Here's the pattern I keep running into. Say someone is trying to decide whether to have a kid, and they know you can't really know what it's like beforehand, so they do the responsible thing: they babysit a lot, they hang out with their friends' toddlers, they read a bunch of parent forums, maybe they even do a trial run caring for a niece for a summer. But here's the weird part -- by the end of all that "research," they're not the same person who started asking the question. The nannying made them like kids more, or exhausted them into resenting the whole idea, or the forums rewired what they think a good life even is. So the preference they were trying to measure got pushed around by the measuring. And it's not that they discovered what they "really" wanted all along -- a different research process would plausibly have produced a different person with different wants.

I see this everywhere once I look. Someone test-drives a career by interning and the internship itself teaches them to value status or autonomy or whatever the job rewards. Someone agonizes over converting to a religion, spends two years attending services "to figure out if they believe," and surprise, the attending is doing a lot of the work. Even small stuff: you can't "just try" meditating to see if you'd value stillness without the trying already bending what you value.

I know L.A. Paul's transformative experience stuff is in the neighborhood, but as I understand it her point is mainly that you can't know in advance what the experience will be like, which is about epistemic access. My thing is different, I think: here the problem isn't just that you can't peek at the outcome, it's that every method of peeking rewrites the preferences you're peeking on behalf of. And it's not quite adaptive preferences / sour grapes either, since that's supposed to be an unconscious accommodation to what you can't have, not something your deliberation procedure actively causes. Does this specific thing have a name? If it does, tell me how it differs from Paul's setup and from adaptive preference formation, and where its edges are (does it apply when the value-change is purely informational? does it require irreversibility?). And if it doesn't have a name, don't just say "no" -- propose one, define it properly, and give me some boundary cases where it stops applying.
