I am a machine learning engineer with a strong background in evaluation methodology and no formal training in historical-critical scholarship beyond a university survey course. I have been reading about the criteria of authenticity used in the quest for the historical Jesus, and I keep involuntarily translating them into concepts from my own field. I would like you to perform that translation systematically and then tell me what it fails to carry.

Take each of the standard criteria in turn — multiple independent attestation, double dissimilarity, embarrassment, coherence, and linguistic or environmental plausibility — and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually work in: independent and dependent data sources, selection effects and censoring, distributional shift, held-out validation, ensembling, nearest-neighbor consistency checks, null models, one-sided versus two-sided tests. For each criterion, sort the result into three categories: what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion (and state the distortion), and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. My own guesses, for calibration: independent attestation looks like ensemble agreement but with unknown correlation structure between the sources; embarrassment looks like data that survived an adverse selection process and is therefore informative in a way curated data is not; coherence looks like bootstrapping from an already-accepted seed set, which worries me as circular. Tell me where these guesses hold up and where they flatten the original idea.

Then give me the residue report at the level of the whole enterprise, not just criterion by criterion. Two things strike me as possibly untranslatable. First, the criteria operate without any ground truth: there is no test set of sayings known to be authentic against which the criteria's precision and recall were ever measured, so their validation is entirely internal. Second, dissimilarity presupposes well-specified background models of both Second Temple Judaism and the early church's agenda, and those models are themselves contested inferences from the same corpus — it is as if both the null distribution and the alternative distribution were estimated from the evaluation data. If the criteria resist translation at exactly these points, I want your judgment on which side is at fault: is the residue a defect of the statistical vocabulary, which assumes repeatable sampling and identifiable data-generating processes that a single unrepeatable historical process cannot supply, or is it evidence that the criteria are judgment heuristics dressed in methodological clothing, doing less discriminating work than their name suggests? Conclude with a verdict on that either/or, or a principled refusal to accept its framing. And state what would change your assessment: which argument or consideration would move you toward the opposite verdict, and at which step in the translation your reasoning is most likely to have gone wrong.
