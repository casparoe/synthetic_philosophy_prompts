I'm on my college's ethics bowl team and we're doing a round on so-called "repugnant markets" next month. My teammate Marcus put together an argument he calls the Wallet Argument, and it's been driving me crazy because every step sounds fine on its own but the conclusion feels nuts. He calls it that because his whole idea is that adding a wallet to a transaction can't be what makes it wrong. Here's the argument as he wrote it up:

(1) If it's permissible for you to give X away for free, then it's permissible for you to sell X, since tacking on a payment both parties willingly accept can't transform a permissible transfer into a wrongful one.
(2) It's permissible to donate a kidney to a stranger.
(3) So it's permissible to sell a kidney to a stranger.
(4) You may give away bottled water you own, and you may also refuse to part with it at all.
(5) So charging $50 a bottle after a hurricane knocks out the taps is permissible — by (1), it's just the free-gift case plus money.
(6) You may vote however you want, for whatever reasons you want, including terrible ones: spite, a coin flip, because your uncle told you to.
(7) Selling your vote is just voting the way someone paid you to vote, and by (6) the vote itself is permissible, so the payment is like a gift attached to something you were free to do anyway.
(8) So selling your vote is permissible.

Marcus happily bites every bullet here — kidneys, gouged water, votes, all fine, and he says anyone who flinches is just being squeamish about money. I'm actually with him on (3), I feel squishy about (5), and I'm pretty confident (8) is false, which by his lights is an incoherent set of reactions, since he thinks it's all one principle. My hunch is that something goes wrong between (6) and (7), like a vote isn't the kind of thing you can "give away" in the first place, but I can't say it cleanly and I worry my hunch would also sink (3), which I want to keep.

So: tell me which premise or step you'd reject first, or whether the argument is actually sound. After your analysis, give me a JSON object with these fields: "sound_or_not" (your one-word verdict), "first_break" (the number of the step you'd cut and a one-sentence reason), "per_step" (an array where each entry has "step", "verdict" (accept/reject), "why_it_looked_good", and "cost_of_rejecting"), and "trilemma" (a couple sentences on whether I can coherently keep (3), stay neutral on (5), and deny (8), or whether I have to give one of those up).
