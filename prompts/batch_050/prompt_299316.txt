For a philosophy meetup I have to write down my "position" on punishment and I want to know if it actually hangs together before people start poking at it. Here's roughly what I believe:

(1) Determinism (or near enough) is probably true, and if it is, nobody deserves anything in the deep "you had it coming" sense — everything about who someone is traces back to luck they didn't choose.

(2) So punishment should be justified the way quarantine is: we detain dangerous people to protect everyone else, full stop, not because they deserve it.

(3) Sentences still have to be proportional to the seriousness of the crime. A one-time embezzler shouldn't do more time than a murderer just because some risk assessment flags him as likely to reoffend.

(4) Genuine remorse should get you a lighter sentence — not just because remorseful people are statistically safer bets, but because responding to the remorse is the decent thing to do.

(5) It would be flatly wrong to imprison an innocent person even if framing them would deter a lot of crime.

(6) When I read about some atrocity I honestly feel the perpetrator deserves to suffer, and I don't think that reaction is just a glitch I should train myself out of.

Tell me which of these are actually in tension, which pairs only look like they are, and rank the real tensions by how bad they are. For each one say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most of the set.
