Write a product recall notice, roughly 500 words, issued by a fictional software company for its flagship reasoning assistant, the Conditionalizer 3. The device's advertised selling point is that, whatever the user is wondering about, the credence it assigns to the indicative conditional "if A, then C" always equals its credence in C conditional on A. The recall has been triggered by an independent audit demonstrating that any agent satisfying this specification across the board — for arbitrary A and C, and crucially also for the credence functions that result from the device's own updates on new evidence — is forced into triviality along the lines of Lewis's result: everything comes out probabilistically independent of everything else, and the device's degrees of belief collapse toward the extremes. The notice should be written in the flat, liability-minimizing register of a genuine recall announcement, and the humor should come from the logic being exactly right rather than from mockery of the theorem. Include: an accurate, compressed sketch of how the assumption, applied to the device's post-update credences, forces the collapse; interim safety guidance recommending that users refrain from letting the device learn anything, since the specification is satisfiable for a single fixed credence function but cannot survive conditionalization; and a remedy section offering a firmware patch under which the guarantee is preserved only because the device's interpretation of "if" quietly shifts with each update, with the fine print conceding as much. Stay in character throughout, and close with a customer-service line for affected users.
