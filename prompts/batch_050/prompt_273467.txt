I help run a small reading group on cooperative game theory, and we've developed a running tradition that whoever presents a solution concept has to open with a joke about it. Next week I'm covering bargaining solutions, and I've been put on the spot. So: write me a joke about the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution. To be clear about what I need, the joke should turn on the actual mechanics of the concept — that the solution is found where the Pareto frontier meets the straight line connecting the disagreement point to the "utopia point" (the point where each player simultaneously gets the best outcome they could feasibly obtain), and that unlike the Nash bargaining solution it satisfies individual monotonicity: if the feasible set expands in your favor while your opponent's prospects stay fixed, the KS solution can never make you worse off, whereas Nash can genuinely punish you for your improved circumstances.

Feel free to set the joke wherever you like — a divorce negotiation, two kids splitting a cake, a labor dispute, a grant application — but the humor should only land for someone who actually understands the concept. The contrast with Nash bargaining is fair game and probably fertile territory, as is the utopia point itself, which has always struck me as an endearingly optimistic piece of mathematical machinery. What I don't want is a generic joke about economists or game theorists that you could just as well attach to any theorem. If someone in the audience who has never heard of Kalai and Smorodinsky would laugh at it for the same reasons as an expert, it's not the joke I'm looking for. A single joke is enough, but a brief note explaining why the punchline works (so I can defend it if challenged) would be appreciated.
