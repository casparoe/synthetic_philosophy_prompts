The exam question was:

"Zombie arguments against physicalism infer the metaphysical possibility of a physically identical but phenomenally empty world from its conceivability. (a) State the strongest Kripke-style objection: why do a posteriori necessities like 'water is H2O' seem to show that conceivability is a fallible guide to metaphysical possibility? (b) Explain how distinguishing prima facie from ideal conceivability (or primary from secondary intensions) is meant to answer the objection, and why the answer is supposed to go through for zombies but not for water. (c) Briefly: can we ever know that a scenario is ideally conceivable, rather than merely apparently so?"

The student's answer, verbatim:

"A zombie is a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a human that lacks phenomenal consciousness. The argument runs: zombies are conceivable; if conceivable then metaphysically possible; if possible, then the physical facts don't necessitate the phenomenal facts, so physicalism is false.

Kripke showed that identities like water = H2O, though discovered empirically, are necessary. It seems conceivable that water could exist without H2O, yet this is metaphysically impossible -- if water is H2O, it is H2O in every possible world. So the objection is that conceivability only ever establishes epistemic possibility and never metaphysical possibility; the two simply come apart.

The defender replies by distinguishing prima facie conceivability, where a scenario merely seems coherent to us given our ignorance, from ideal conceivability. Water existing without H2O is only prima facie conceivable: the terms 'water' and 'H2O' have different secondary intensions but the same primary intension, so on ideal reflection the scenario collapses. The zombie scenario, by contrast, survives ideal reflection -- no contradiction can be derived from the hypothesis of a physical duplicate without consciousness.

On the last part: ideal conceivability is defined in terms of ideal rational reflection. Since no one has derived a contradiction from the zombie hypothesis despite much scrutiny, zombies are ideally conceivable, and the argument succeeds."

Grade it -- and be especally careful with part (b), students love to sound precise there while getting it backwards.
