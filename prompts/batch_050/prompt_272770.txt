But then my coworker pushed back and said that's way too quick — his point was that sometimes the subgroup variable is itself downstream of the treatment, and if you condition on something the treatment caused, you're the one introducing the distortion, and the pooled number is actually the one to trust. Now I genuinely don't know when to believe which table.

Please give me an intuitive, gut-level explanation of when Simpson's paradox means "trust the disaggregated numbers" versus when it means "trust the pooled number." Use a fresh concrete example if you can, ideally not kidney stones or Berkeley admissions, since I've read those a dozen times and they always get told with the same moral. And please address the harder version of the question: can two datasets have literally identical numbers in every table, pooled and sliced, and yet call for opposite decisions?
