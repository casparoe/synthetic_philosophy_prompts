Some background first: I spent about 35 years as an evolutionary geneticist (mostly Drosophila population genetics) and since retiring I've gotten into philosophy of biology and, lately, the cultural evolution literature — Dawkins on memes, Dennett, and some of the dual-inheritance stuff. At our local philosophy cafe I got into an argument with a retired English professor who thinks the whole memes project is pseudoscience, and I realized I should definately check whether my own views are even coherent before I go defending them at next month's meeting.

Here's roughly what I believe. (1) Wherever you find design-like fit you should reach for natural selection, and since cultural artifacts and practices show plenty of design-like fit, cultural change has to be explained selectionist-style too, in a broadly analogous way to how genes work. (2) The sensible units of cultural selection are memes: discrete, high-fidelity copied items like tunes, recipes, turns of phrase — not fuzzy wholes like entire cultures or worldviews. (3) Unlike genes, memes spread because people actively pick them up — they're catchy, useful, high-status — so human psychology and choice do the real explanatory work, and "meme" is basically just a handy label for whatever gets chosen. (4) The meme's-eye view is genuinely illuminating rather than a cute metaphor: asking what features help an idea get itself copied explains things that asking what people want does not. (5) Cultural transmission is too horizontal and too blended, with constant recombination and no clean lineages, so the phylogenetic tree methods that linguists and anthropologists borrow from biology are misguided.

So: does this set hang together? Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only look like they do, and for each real conflict what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and tell me which single change would clean up the most.
