Ok so I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I got into it with a coworker over lunch about Zeno's paradox. I figured I had it solved: the dichotomy thing is just a convergent series, 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... sums to 1, so the runner covers a finite distance in finite time, done. He came back the next day with this argument and I can't find the hole, but the conclusion seems insane:

(1) To reach the finish line, the runner has to actually complete each of the infinitely many halfway stages: first half, then three quarters, then seven eighths, and so on. There's no skipping them.
(2) My series argument only shows that the *total distance* is finite. It says nothing about whether infinitely many distinct stages can each be completed, one after another, in a finite amount of time. Those are different claims.
(3) But completing infinitely many sequential tasks in finite time is impossible. Like, imagine a lamp that gets toggled on and off at every stage -- at the end it has to be either on or off, but the process doesn't settle which, which shows the whole setup is incoherent.
(4) So either motion is impossible, or there aren't really infinitely many stages -- meaning space and time are discrete at some smallest unit, like pixels or CPU clock ticks.
(5) Motion obviously happens.
(6) Therefore spacetime must be discrete at the bottom.

Getting "the universe is quantized" out of an ancient paradox feels like proving way too much. But every time I push on it I slip. Which step should I actually reject, why does that step *look* fine, and what do I lose by rejecting it? If I have to choose I'd rather give up (2) than accept pixelated spacetime, but I want to know the price. Also: does the standard calculus answer I gave originally even address (2), or is it just talking past it?
