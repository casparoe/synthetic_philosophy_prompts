So I moved from Chicago to Lisbon eight months ago for my girlfriend's job, and we keep having the same fight. I'll say something like "the Portuguese are just bad at queuing" and she'll say that's a crappy thing to say, and then two days later she'll say "Americans can't do subtlety" and somehow that's fine. I've been keeping a running list of my actual gut verdicts on these, and I want to know if there's any principle underneath them or if I'm just a hypocrite with vibes.

1. Me telling a visiting friend "heads up, people here run late, don't take it personally": fine.
2. A travel guidebook writing "the French are brusque with tourists": not fine, even though it's basically the same kind of claim as #1, aimed at the same audience.
3. My girlfriend saying "we Portuguese are conflict-averse" about her own family: fine.
4. A Dutch colleague saying the exact same sentence about Portuguese people: not fine. Same words!
5. An anthropology paper with actual survey data concluding that some culture scores higher on collectivism: fine.
6. A hiring manager reading that same paper and then passing on a candidate from that culture because "they don't push back on managers": obviously not fine.
7. Me saying "Nigerians are the warmest people I've ever met": positive, but it still makes me a little queasy and I don't know why.
8. A stand-up comedian doing a bit built entirely on national stereotypes: mostly fine, depending on the bit, which is a useless verdict, I know.
9. My girlfriend's grandma saying emigrants who come back for the summer "act too German now": I find this charming and would be horrified if I said it.

Is there one principle that gets all nine, or even seven of nine? If your candidate principle has to overturn one of my verdicts, tell me which one you'd sacrifice and why that one. And if the best you can do is two or three mini-principles stitched together (one about who's speaking, one about what's done with the claim, one about how the claim was established, or whatever), I'd rather hear that than a forced unified theory. Also: test whatever you land on against the case I'm currently stuck on, which is my girlfriend insisting her brother flaked on our dinner plans because "he's Portuguese, what did you expect" -- about her own brother, who is also Portuguese, and who is also, to be fair, chronically late. Is that the same as #3 or something else entirely?
