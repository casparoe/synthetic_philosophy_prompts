Next month I'm giving a short talk at my local philosophy meetup defending an idea I came up with after reading a bunch about panpsychism. My claim: if experience really is fundamental and everywhere, then suffering isn't special to brains, and any physical process that does lots of irreversible, high-friction work (erosion, combustion, stars burning out) could be generating vast amounts of low-grade suffering, so panpsychism implies physics itself might be a moral catastrophe and we should maybe care about reducing entropy production, not just factory farming.

The audience: mostly scientifically literate amateurs, a couple of them pretty committed physicalists who think consciousness is just what brains do, one guy who's a big panpsychism fan but hates moral realism, and a few utilitarians who'll take the suffering part seriously but not the physics part.

Prepare me for the Q&A. Give me the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, roughly in the order they'll come up, phrased how these people would actually phrase them (casual, a bit combative). For each one: what I should concede, what I should push back on, and a reply I can give in under two minutes. Also tell me which objection is the most dangerous to my whole argument and which one I'm secretly hoping nobody asks.
