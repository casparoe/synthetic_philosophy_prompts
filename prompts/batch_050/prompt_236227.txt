I've been putting together a handout for my animal minds seminar on cases where an apparently brilliant bit of animal behavior got deflated by a lower-level explanation -- the kind of thing Morgan's canon is supposed to license. (I know, I know, the canon gets misread all the time as simple parsimony about animals, when Morgan himself allowed that sometimes you do have to posit the higher faculty; but the deflationary use is still the interesting one for my students.) So far my list is: (1) Clever Hans, the horse who seemed to do arithmetic but was reading his owner's involuntary posture shifts; (2) Morgan's own terrier Tony, who looked like he'd figured out the gate latch by reasoning but had in fact built it up by slow trial and error, which is basically where the canon came from; (3) Thorndike's puzzle-box cats, whose escapes look insightful until you plot the learning curves and see gradual improvement instead of an aha moment; and (4) Povinelli's work suggesting chimps who follow gaze and beg appropriately are reading behavior rather than understanding that others see things. My worry is that all four of these work by one of two moves: either the animal is cueing off a human, or the behavior was shaped by trial-and-error rather than insight. That makes the handout a bit repetitive, and it gives students the false impression that deflation always looks like this.

So: give me one more case -- a real, reasonably well-known one -- where an impressive animal behavior got a lower-level explanation by some other route, not cueing off people and not simple trial-and-error shaping. It could be an associationist or perceptual-mechanism explanation, or even a case where the deflation itself later got deflated, if you think that fits better. Tell me roughly what the original impressive claim was, what the lower-level explanation is, and then honestly assess it: does the case actually support the canon's deflationary reading, or does it show the canon cutting the other way (i.e., warning us that we'd be under-attributing)? Please don't just restate one of my four in new clothes -- a border collie that cues off its owner is still Clever Hans, seperated only by species.
