I'm helping run a reading group on democratic theory, and our next session is built around Simone Weil's essay On the Abolition of All Political Parties, which she wrote in 1943 and which was published posthumously. Her argument, as I understand it, runs roughly like this: a political party is an organization whose internal logic pushes it to generate collective passion and collective thought in its members, to subordinate the individual conscience to party discipline, and to treat its own growth and victory as ends in themselves rather than as means to any public good; and since these defects belong to the very form of a party rather than to any particular corrupt party, the remedy is not reform of parties but their abolition. Our group found the essay bracing but were split on where exactly it goes wrong, if it does. To structure the discussion, I'd like you to write a decision tree -- seven to nine branch nodes -- that takes a participant from the shared starting point ("elected legislatures and competitive elections are worth keeping") to a named position on political parties, with each node phrased as a question answerable by someone who has not read the literature.

The leaves should include, at minimum: Weil's own abolitionism; the broadly Schattschneider-style view that mass democracy is unworkable without parties, so their vices are costs to be managed rather than eliminated; the Madisonian pluralist position that organized factions are inevitable and best countered by multiplying them; a position that accepts Weil's diagnosis of party pathology but draws the line at abolition, proposing internal reforms (open primaries, anti-whip rules, funding changes) instead; and a Rousseau-adjacent position that dislikes parties specifically because they are partial associations mediating between citizen and general will. The branch questions should be concrete where possible -- e.g., whether a citizen who finds her own judgment reliably outvoted by party-line reasoning has lost anything of value, or whether coordinating a million voters without an organization that disciplines them is even coherent -- rather than asking directly "do parties corrupt thought?"
