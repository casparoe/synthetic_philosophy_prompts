I'm a software engineer, no formal philosophy background, and I've been reading up on epistemic logic and the so-called "logical omniscience problem." As far as I can tell, the standard models (Kripke-style possible worlds semantics) make agents know every tautology and every logical consequence of what they believe, and the reason this is treated as a bug is that figuring out what follows from your beliefs is computationally brutal -- SAT is NP-complete, provability in general is undecidable -- so no real agent could ever live up to the model. So here's my question: now that modern SAT solvers chew through instances with millions of variables, isn't the logical omniscience problem basicly obsolete? Is this just a case of philosophers not keeping up with solver benchmarks, or is there anything left of the problem once compute is cheap?
