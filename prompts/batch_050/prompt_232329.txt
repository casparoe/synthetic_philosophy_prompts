So I just spent like three hours arguing with my friend Marcus about advance directives and neither of us gave an inch, and I want to see the fight done properly. Here's the case we kept circling: a guy is about to get deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's, which apparently can sometimes shift people's personality and what they want out of life. Before surgery he writes a directive saying: if I come out of this seeming happy but with totally different values -- say I quit my job, leave my partner, start gambling, whatever -- don't treat those new preferences as really mine. My pre-surgery self stays in charge. Marcus says the directive should bind even if post-surgery guy insists he's never been happier and begs everyone to ignore it. My position is that's making a living, content person into a puppet of a ghost, and an earlier self has no more authority over who he is now than one friend has over another.

Write a dialogue between two characters taking our two sides. Rules: both of them have to be genuinely sharp, no strawmen, and they need to actually respond to what the other one just said instead of just re-stating their opening. Make them grapple with at least these two pressure points: (a) if the post-surgery guy were *miserable* instead of happy, everyone would defer to the directive, so is "he seems happy" doing all the real work in my view? and (b) we follow Ulysses contracts for addiction all the time, so why is this any different? Don't let either side clearly win -- I want it to end still live. Max 450 words, and definately at least six turns each so it doesn't feel truncated.
