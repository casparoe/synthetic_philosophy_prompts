Is there a name for this? I took a political theory survey last semester where we did the whole freedom thing — freedom from interference, freedom as realizing your true self or whatever, and then this third one about how you're unfree if someone has power over you even if they never actually use it, like the servant with a nice master. And I keep noticing something that doesn't cleanly fit any of the three and it's bugging me.

Here's the pattern. My friend writes a newsletter and she told me she stopped writing about certain topics entirely — not because anyone threatened her, not because anyone ever did anything to her, and there's no specific person whose permission she needs or whose mood she has to track. It's more like there's this diffuse potential audience out there and if she says the wrong thing some unpredictable subset of strangers might pile on, screenshot her, email her employer, whatever. Nobody in particular holds power over her. There's no master, nice or otherwise. She'd say she's perfectly free — nothing is stopping her — and yet she's clearly arranging her whole writing life around a kind of threat that never has to materialize. Second instance: a guy at my old job worked remotely for a company with one of those keystroke-logging setups. As far as he knew nobody ever actually looked at the data. But he said he worked differently under it — took fewer breaks, never vented in chat, kept his tone bland — and the weird part is there was no manager whose favor he was currying, just a system recording things into a void that maybe nobody would ever review.

So it's like the unfreedom-by-unexercised-power thing, except the "power" doesn't belong to any agent — it's scattered across a crowd or baked into a monitoring infrastructure, there's no one you could negotiate with or appease or overthrow, and half the time the people doing the self-policing can't even say who exactly they're afraid of. Is this covered by the third concept of freedom once you loosen it up, or is it something else — I've seen people gesture at things like "chilling effects" and "panopticon" stuff but those feel more like descriptions of the mechanism than the condition of the person living under it. If there's an established term, tell me which of the candidates fits best and where the fit strains; if there isn't one, coin something and give me the definition with a couple boundary cases — like, does it count if the crowd is genuinely powerless but the person wrongly believes it isn't? Does it count if the self-censorship actually makes her writing better? Don't just hand me a label, I want the carving.
