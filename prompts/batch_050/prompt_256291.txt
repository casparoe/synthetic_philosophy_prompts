I teach a graduate seminar on AI safety and want to replace the usual "list of impact measures" lecture with an instrument that sorts students by their design instincts rather than by which papers they have read. Please write a decision tree that begins at the shared premise — a capable agent optimizing a simple, misspecified objective will tend to cause large unwanted side effects, and we would like a general penalty that discourages this without enumerating everything we care about — and ends at a named low-impact design.

Branch nodes. Every internal node should be a single question or a short concrete scenario on which candidate designs genuinely come apart, phrased so that a student can answer it without knowing any of the design names. The following distinctions must each appear as a split somewhere in the tree, and each split must be driven by a case rather than by the abstract distinction alone:

1. Baseline. Side effects are deviations from what? Use two mini-cases: (a) a cleaning robot deployed into a kitchen where a fire is already spreading — is it penalized for the fire's progress relative to the state it found at deployment, relative to how the world would have unfolded had it never acted at all, or relative to how the world would unfold if it did nothing from this moment onward; and (b) the same robot having earlier assembled a slow Rube Goldberg contraption that is now, an hour later, about to topple a shelf — under which of the three baselines does the agent escape the penalty for its own delayed effect?

2. What the measure measures. Deviation in a chosen representation of the state, versus change in what other agents are still able to achieve, versus change relative to an explicit list of approved alterations.

3. Irreversibility and compensation. Include the case of a robot that breaks a vase and then manufactures a replacement indistinguishable from the original: ask whether the episode should count as low-impact, and let the answer route the student to different leaves.

4. Human input. Does the measure consult human judgment or ongoing approval at any point, and is that reliance presented as a feature of the design or as an admission that the project of defining "side effect" has failed?

Leaves. Each of the following designs must appear as exactly one leaf; describe each functionally at its leaf rather than assuming the reader knows the name, and where a standard name exists attach it: penalizing distance from a baseline state in some feature representation (the design usually called relative reachability); penalizing reductions in what other agents could achieve across a set of auxiliary objectives (usually called attainable utility preservation); restricting the agent to an explicit whitelist of permitted state changes and penalizing everything else; penalizing effects in proportion to their irreversibility; deferring the side-effect judgment to continuing human approval. In addition, include at least two unoccupied leaves — combinations of answers that no known design occupies — each annotated with one sentence diagnosing whether the slot is empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Honesty conditions. If two named designs cannot be separated by any question on your tree — because the difference between them is an implementation detail rather than a different answer to a diagnostic case — say so explicitly and flag the pair rather than inventing a question that only pretends to separate them.

Size and format. Six to nine internal nodes and eight to eleven leaves, presented as indented text or an outline. Number each internal node and put its diagnostic question in bold. At each leaf give the design's name (or "unoccupied") and one sentence on its most awkward commitment: the scenario in which faithfully applying the design produces a verdict its designer would want to disown.

Closing. After the tree, write one paragraph of 150–200 words answering: does any leaf genuinely avoid smuggling a partial specification of human values back in — through the choice of baseline, of feature representation, of auxiliary objectives, of whitelist, or of approver — and, if every leaf smuggles something, which smuggles the least?
