For my stats class I'm doing a project on the algorithm my school district uses to flag students who might fail a class, and I have to write a page about what I think a fair version of it would look like. Here's where I've landed, but I want to know if it actually hangs together before I hand it in:

1. The scores should be calibrated for every group: if the algorithm says a group of students has a 70% chance of failing, about 70% of them should actually fail, no matter which demographic group they're from.
2. The false positive rate should be the same across groups -- it shouldn't flag, say, Black students who would've passed more often than white students who would've passed.
3. Same for false negatives: it shouldn't miss struggling students at different rates depending on their group.
4. The underlying failure rates really do differ between groups (because of stuff like funding and tutoring access, not anything about the students themselves), and the algorithm is just reading that off the data.
5. The algorithm itself shouldn't take race or gender as an input -- it should be blind to that stuff.

Are all five of these jointly possible, or am I asking for something math won't let me have? Don't just lay out the options -- tell me straight up which one(s) have to go, which pairs only look like they're in conflict but aren't, and rank the problems from worst to least bad. I'd rather hand in something with a definite position than a mushy "on the one hand, on the other hand" thing.
