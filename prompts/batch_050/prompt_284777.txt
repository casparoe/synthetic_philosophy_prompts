A friend of mine who fixes cars cornered me with this over the weekend and it's been bugging me since. It's about trusting instruments, and I can't figure out where it breaks, but the conclusion seems to prove way too much. Here it is, his numbering:

1. I glance at the fuel gauge in my truck, it reads half-full, and on that basis I come to know the tank is half-full. (This is just ordinary instrument-based knowledge, the kind everyone relies on constantly.)
2. So on each occasion, I know two things together: the gauge reads a certain way, AND the tank actually is that way. In other words, I know the gauge was accurate on that occasion.
3. I do this every day for a month, under varied conditions, and by straightforward induction I come to know the gauge was accurate on all thirty occasions.
4. A gauge that's been accurate across thirty varied occasions is a reliable gauge. So I now know my gauge is reliable.
5. Here's the kicker: at no point did I ever check the tank's level by any means other than reading that very gauge. No dipstick, no filling it up and doing the math, nothing independent.
6. But you obviously can't come to know a source is reliable using nothing but that source's own deliverances. That's just circular reasoning, and if it worked I could "establish" the reliability of a psychic hotline the same way.

He says this means either premise 1 is false (and ordinary instrument knowledge is impossible, which seems nuts), or circular reasoning can genuinely produce knowledge (which also seems nuts, since it would vindicate the psychic hotline method). I'm pretty sure something goes wrong between 2 and 4, but every time I try to put my finger on it, he asks what exactly I'd be giving up, and I don't have a good answer.

So: which step do you actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I want the price stated honestly -- like, if you reject 2, what else do I lose that I probably wanted to keep? And if your answer is that the argument is fine and we should just swallow one of his two conclusions, tell me which one and why it's less bad than it looks. Also, if it matters, I'd really like to hang on to the idea that I can know stuff from my car's gauges without crawling under the truck with a dipstick every time, but I get that wanting that doesn't make it so.
