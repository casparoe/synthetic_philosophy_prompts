I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I recently went down a rabbit hole on Maxwell's demon and why it can't actually violate the second law of thermodynamics. I find the standard resolution oddly moving: the demon's sorting works fine, measurement can in principle be done for free, and the whole scheme collapses only because the demon must eventually erase its record of each measurement -- and erasing one bit of information dissipates a minimum amount of heat, exactly canceling the entropy the sorting won. A creature defeated not by its task but by its own bookkeeping feels like it deserves a poem.

Write a villanelle about Maxwell's demon in which the form does the physics. The two refrains should correspond to the two halves of the argument: one refrain states the demon's apparent triumph (sorting the fast molecules from the slow, building a temperature difference from nothing, work extracted from a single heat bath), and the other states the resolution (every observation leaves a record, every record must be erased, and erasure pays heat back into the bath). The refrains' alternating returns across the tercets should enact the way the gain keeps being immediately canceled -- the sorting line always answered by the erasure line -- and when the two refrains finally stand side by side in the closing quatrain, the reader should be able to see the ledger balance: the entropy won by sorting is precisely the entropy paid by forgetting.

Two constraints on accuracy. First, don't have the demon fail at the measuring step -- someone who knows the Szilard engine analysis should recognize that the trap is memory, not perception. Second, the closing quatrain should land on the ledger balancing, not on some vaguer moral about order and chaos. Someone familiar with the physics should be able to tell which refrain is which and find the science right; someone who isn't should still get a good poem about a clever creature undone by the cost of forgetting.
