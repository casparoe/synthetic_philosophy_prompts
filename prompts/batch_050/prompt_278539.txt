A friend of mine is building a meeting-transcription tool that tracks the "common ground" of a conversation, and his pitch to me leans hard on the baseball-scoreboard analogy from the presupposition literature: a conversation keeps score like a game, where what's presupposed, how precise "flat" has to be, what's under discussion, etc. are all entries on a scoreboard, and just like in baseball, if you say something that only makes sense given a score you haven't established yet, everyone just lets the score update to accommodate you -- play moves on, no whistle blown. He uses this to argue that accommodation is basically frictionless and automatic, so his tool can freely backfill presuppositions into its record of the meeting without flagging them to anyone. Audit the analogy for him. What maps onto what -- what's the score, what are the rules, who's the umpire, what counts as a play? Where does it break -- things like hearers who push back, power asymmetries over who gets to move the score, the fact that accommodation can be strategically exploited, records that persist after the "game" ends? And does his frictionless-backfill conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it?
