The example I keep coming back to is the split between pharmaceuticals and ordinary consumer technology. A new drug generally has to pass demanding trials before it can be sold: the manufacturer bears the burden of showing that it works and that its risks are acceptable. By contrast, a company can usually release a social app, home robot, wearable device, or AI feature without first proving that it is safe; regulators mostly wait for evidence of harm, then issue warnings, fines, or recalls after the fact. Both systems claim to be protecting the public, but they seem to run the burden of proof in opposite directions.

What philosophical commitments does this split presuppose?
