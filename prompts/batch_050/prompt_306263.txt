A colleague and I are drafting a paper on how slurs mean what they do, and we want to fix the standard of adequacy before arguing over which account meets it. Write the list of desiderata a theory of slur meaning must satisfy, each paired with the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which are non-negotiable (a theory that fails them is simply mistaken) and which are the preferences of a particular camp. At minimum, your list must settle how an adequate theory handles: derogation surviving embedding under negation, questions, and conditionals; the asymmetry between a slur and its neutral correlate, and what exactly the slur adds on top; reclamation by in-group members, and why the reclaimed use remains unavailable to outsiders; slurs whose targets do not exist, as when a novel coins a derogatory term for a fictional people and readers still experience it as slurring; whether mere mention of a slur — in a linguistics seminar, a court transcript, a slur's own dictionary entry — offends in the same way as use; and variation in intensity among slurs aimed at the same group. Where two desiderata pull against each other (for instance, fully projective derogation versus the tolerability of quotation, or a uniform content story versus the flexibility of reclamation), say so explicitly and say which you would trade against which. Do not score the existing accounts — semantic-content views, prohibitionist views, expressivist views, perspectival views — against the list; the specification is the deliverable. Finish by identifying the single requirement that excludes the largest share of those accounts, stated as \answer{...}.
