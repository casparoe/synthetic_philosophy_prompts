Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a causal decision theorist and an "FDT-style" functional decision theorist about Parfit's hitchhiker. Quick recap so we're on the same page: you're stranded in the desert, about to die. A driver pulls up and offers you a ride to the city for $200, payable only after you arrive -- but she's an uncannily good judge of character and will only take you if she can tell you genuinely intend to pay once you're safe. If she detects that you'd stiff her, she drives off and you die. CDT says: once you're safe in the city, the ride is already in the bag, nothing you do then can cause it to have happened, so the rational act is to not pay. FDT says: your decision procedure is what's being read in the desert, so be the kind of agent who pays. Yet both camps seem to agree you'd better be someone who pays, or you die -- they just disagree about what that fact shows.

Your statement should have: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can actually sign -- e.g. about what's causally fixed at the moment of payment, about why the disposition to pay is instrumentally valuable to *have* even if paying is irrational to *do*, about whether this case is just Newcomb wearing a trenchcoat or a genuinely different structure; (2) the precise points of disagreement, worded so each side would endorse the description of its own position -- especially the disagreement about whether "rational act" and "rational disposition" can come apart, and whether the driver's mind-reading introduces a subjunctive dependence that CDT is obliged to respect or can legitimately ignore; (3) the cruxes: what sub-question, if settled, would actually move one of the sides? And (4) whether there's a nearby variant of the case (say, the driver reads a brain scan instead of your face, or payment happens before the ride) on which the two theories give the *same* verdict, which might reveal how much of the fight is really about the case vs. about the theories. If part of the disagreement turns out to be verbal -- e.g. both sides agree on what to do but call different things "rational" -- say so and point at exactly which part.
