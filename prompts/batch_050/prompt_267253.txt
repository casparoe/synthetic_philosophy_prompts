Six months ago I began changing my diet in order to reduce animal suffering, and I have since learned that the ranking I assumed — chicken worst per meal, red meat in the middle, plants best — may not survive contact with two complications I had never considered: the animals killed incidentally in cultivating crops, and the suffering of wild animals on the land that food production either occupies or spares. I am tired of reading tours of the considerations and want a defensible number instead.

So: please give me your credence in the following proposition. For a typical consumer in an industrialized country, replacing all chicken consumption with an equivalent number of calories from pasture-raised beef reduces total expected animal suffering, counting both the farmed animals directly involved and the downstream effects on wild animals through land use.

I want the reasoning decomposed rather than just the figure. The sub-questions I can see include: the relative welfare capacities and typical living conditions of chickens versus cattle; how many animal lives, and how much suffering per life, each product embodies per calorie; whether crop cultivation harms enough field animals to shift the comparison; whether grazing land, relative to the cropland needed for feed or to the wild habitat it replaces, increases or decreases wild-animal suffering on net; and how much weight the wild-animal term should carry given how little we know about whether typical wild lives are even worth living. Assign a rough probability or range to each sub-question, show how they combine into your final credence, and identify the single term to which the answer is most sensitive — the one where, if I could hand you better evidence, your number would move most.

If you think the proposition is mis-specified — for instance, if the pasture-raised versus feedlot distinction swamps everything else, or if a plant-based option dominates both and makes the comparison moot — say so, restate the question as you think it should be asked, and give me your credence for the better version instead. I ask because I genuinely have to decide what goes in the shopping cart next week, and I would rather act on a number with its weak points marked than on a slogan.

Lead with your bottom-line credence in the first sentence, then give the decomposition, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
