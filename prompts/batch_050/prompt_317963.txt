I recently came across the Four–Seven debate in Korean Neo-Confucianism, and I can't stop turning it over. The question is whether our moral emotions are fundamentally the same kind of thing as our everyday feelings. On one side: the "Four Beginnings," such as the spontaneous alarm and pity anyone feels on seeing a child about to fall into a well, spring from principle (i) itself and so are pure; the "Seven Feelings" of joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate, and desire arise from material force (gi) and so can be misdirected. On the other side: the Four are just the Seven at their clearest, both carried by the very same energy, with no separate source.

Please write a short story, set at a Joseon-era academy in the tradition of the famous exchange between Yi Hwang and Ki Daeseung, that dramatizes this disagreement without settling it. Give me a senior teacher and a younger scholar who argue the two positions through one concrete incident — say, one of them acting, or failing to act, on a sudden flash of compassion — with each interpreting the same inner moment in opposite ways. By the end I want to feel the pull of both readings and remain honestly unsure what was really happening inside that person.
