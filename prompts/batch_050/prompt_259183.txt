So I've been reading about Pascal's mugging and I want to nail down my position before a discussion group. Here's where I think I stand:

(1) Expected utility maximization is the right basic framework for decision-making under uncertainty.
(2) I don't buy bounded utility functions as the fix here. Bounding utility just to dodge the mugging feels ad hoc, and I don't think there's some amount of good such that twice that amount isn't actually twice as good.
(3) Still, I say you refuse the mugger. Someone promises me 10^80 happy lives tomorrow if I hand over my wallet, I walk away, and I don't think that's irrational no matter what number they name.
(4) I'm also not willing to say there's some probability threshold below which you're allowed to just ignore an outcome -- no de minimis cutoff. If there were, nobody could criticize you for skipping a cheap fix on a one-in-a-billion safety risk.
(5) On the St. Petersburg paradox I basically shrug: real casinos have finite bankrolls, so the infinite expectation never comes up in practice, and I don't think it forces any deep revision of the framework.

Does this hang together? My gut says yes but a friend insists at least one of these has to go and won't tell me which. If there are tensions, rank them by how bad they are and tell me what the cheapest fix is for each -- and if some of these only *look* like they conflict, tell me that too rather than flagging everything.
