I'm in a metaphysics seminar this term and we just did supertasks and Thomson's lamp, and I keep flip-flopping on which setups I think are even coherent. Here are my gut verdicts, and I want to know if there's one principle behind them.

Zeno's runner: totally fine. The runner crosses the room, the "infinite tasks" are just a weird way of describing one continuous motion. No paradox.

Thomson's lamp: I also think this is basically fine. A switch flipped at 1/2 min, 3/4, 7/8, etc. -- at 1 minute the lamp is just on or off, and the description of the supertask doesn't settle which. Weird but not contradictory.

Ross's urn (put in balls 1-10, remove ball 1; then balls 11-20, remove ball 2; etc.): at the end the urn is empty. My gut says that's right, even though it feels like a scam, because you can't name a ball that's still in there.

The Grim Reaper setup: infinitely many reapers, each set to check at a time (1 min, 1/2 min, 1/4 min...) and kill Fred if he's still alive. THIS one I say is impossible -- not just undetermined, but straight-up incoherent, because Fred has to die at some specific reaper's hand and there is no first reaper.

Here's the problem: I can't see why the reaper case is worse than the lamp. In both cases you have infinitely many discrete acts compressed into a finite interval, and in both cases the final state seems to be "left open" by the description. If "no answer is forced, but some answer obtains" is fine for the lamp, why isn't it fine for Fred? Conversely if the reaper case is incoherent because there's no first reaper, why isn't the lamp incoherent because there's no last flip? My TA just said "think about it" which was super helpful.

Also: my verdict on Zeno vs. the lamp might be doing hidden work. I said Zeno is fine because the infinity is "just a description," but the runner does traverse infinitely many sub-intervals, so why does that not count as performing infinitely many acts?

So: is there a single principle that sorts "coherent supertask" from "incoherent supertask" and matches my verdicts, or do I have to give one up?
