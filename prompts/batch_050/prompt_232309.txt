I am a doctoral student in history, and my advisor and I have a running exercise. I describe a stylized case of perpetrator participation in mass atrocity, and she tells me which single factor does the most explanatory work. She has read the Browning–Goldhagen debate closely but refuses to sign up for either side wholesale. Her verdicts so far:

1. Conscripted, middle-aged reservists with unremarkable political records are ordered to shoot civilians at close range; their commander explicitly offers, and honors, the chance to step aside. About one man in ten steps aside. Her verdict: the unit explains it — standing in the line matters more than what the men believed.
2. Young, ideologically vetted volunteers begin killing on their own initiative before any order arrives. Her verdict: conviction explains it — this is what the belief looks like when it meets opportunity.
3. Clerks who spend the war scheduling transports and never see a victim comply almost without exception. Her verdict: structure explains it — fragment the task and distance the victim, and ordinary careerism suffices.
4. Men whose cruelty escalates over months of continuous duty, from reluctant participants to inventive brutality. Her verdict: practice explains it — the disposition is the residue of the acts, not their source.
5. A unit in which the keenest participants turn out to be those who volunteered for the posting, though their measured beliefs are indistinguishable from those who declined it. Her verdict: selection explains it — ask who walked toward the posting, not what they said they believed.

The new case, loosely patterned on the auxiliaries recruited from prisoner-of-war camps to serve as camp guards and shooters: men with no prior formation in the regime's eliminationist ideology, recruited by an offer they could refuse only at the price of remaining in a starvation camp; foreigners with weak bonds to one another and to their commanders; put to killing work quickly, with alcohol and routine, at which most became proficient and some became enthusiastic. Which factor will my advisor say does the most explanatory work here, and will she expect high participation? Please give me your best prediction; state the rule her five verdicts are implicitly running; give at least two alternative rules that fit those five verdicts equally well but diverge on this case; and name the one further stylized case — not yet in her record — that would best discriminate among the rules.
