I am preparing a two-page research proposal for an eighteen-month postdoctoral grant in phenomenology and the philosophy of technology, and I need help turning a rough conviction into a defensible plan. The conviction is this: in Heidegger's tool analysis, equipment is transparent while it works and becomes conspicuous only through malfunction — the broken hammer suddenly reveals the workshop as a network of assignments and purposes. But the technologies that interest me are engineered precisely to prevent that moment. Redundant systems reroute around failures before users notice, autosave absorbs crashes, and AI assistants respond to their own errors with fluent confabulation rather than interruption. My working hypothesis is that such artefacts fail without breaking down: the malfunction never becomes conspicuous, so the juncture at which Heidegger says the worldhood of the world announces itself simply never arrives. I want to know what phenomenological category describes this condition, and what, if anything, now performs the disclosive work Heidegger assigned to breakdown.

Please develop this into a proposal containing: (1) a precise statement of the research question, including a working name for the phenomenon and a principled criterion for distinguishing it both from ordinary smooth functioning and from conspicuous breakdown; (2) an account of why existing discussions of the tool analysis and its standard applications to digital technology leave this question open — in particular, why classifying silent failure as merely successful functioning, or as breakdown the user happens not to notice, each beg the central question in opposite directions; (3) a realistic work plan for the eighteen months, combining close engagement with the primary texts and their reception with first-person phenomenological case studies I would conduct myself — I am imagining cases involving silent route recalculation in navigation software, confident error in AI assistants, and automatic failover in cloud infrastructure; (4) a sketch of what a positive result would look like, whether that is a fourth mode of equipmental being alongside conspicuousness, obtrusiveness, and obstinacy, or instead an argument that the existing three suffice once properly applied; and (5) explicit conditions under which I would conclude the project has failed, so evaluators can see the hypothesis is falsifiable in practice and not only in principle.

The proposal should also explain why this requires phenomenological method rather than empirical human-computer interaction research or reliability engineering, since the committee will press on exactly that point. Do not include a bibliography or any citations to secondary literature; refer to positions and interpretations by description, and I will supply the references myself.
