I recently took over responsibility for our company's retirement-plan enrollment process, and in preparing to redesign it I've been forced to write down what I actually believe about nudging, since every design decision now lands on real colleagues. I'd like a consistency audit of the following five commitments before I defend them to our benefits committee:

(a) There is no neutral choice architecture: some default has to be set, and declining to nudge is itself a design choice with foreseeable effects, so "we'll just present the options" is not a way of staying out of people's decisions.

(b) A nudge is legitimate only if the person being nudged could, with modest effort, become aware of the influence and of the intention behind it.

(c) Autonomy has purely instrumental value: what ultimately matters is people's welfare, and agency is worth protecting insofar as protecting it tends to serve welfare.

(d) Dark patterns in commercial interface design are wrong even in cases where they leave the people subjected to them better off, because they bypass the person's agency rather than merely harming their interests.

(e) Automatic enrollment is justified even for employees who will never notice the default, never read the materials, and never understand that a choice was made on their behalf, provided it foreseeably leaves them better off.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, identify exactly which commitments are involved, what each would have to give up to restore consistency, and how costly that revision would be. Rank the tensions from most to least severe. For each merely apparent tension, explain briefly why it dissolves. I haven't signaled which commitment I'd abandon, so don't assume one is sacrosanct; and where a tension rests on how a commitment is motivated, say what the competing motivations are.

After the analysis, give me your findings as a JSON object with this schema: a field "tensions", a list of objects each with fields "pair" (the letters of the two commitments), "severity" (an integer from 1, mild, to 5, severe), "diagnosis" (one or two sentences), "cheapest_fix" (which commitment to revise and how), and "cost" (what the revision sacrifices); a field "apparent_tensions", a list of objects each with fields "pair" and "why_it_dissolves"; and a field "verdict", one sentence stating whether the set as a whole is jointly tenable as written.
