For my IB Theory of Knowledge class I've been asked to submit a written "position profile" on how history works, and I want to check whether my views are actually consistent before I hand it in. Here's where I've landed: (a) I accept the core of historical materialism — the way a society organizes material production explains most large-scale historical change, including much of its politics, law, and dominant ideas; (b) I think moral ideas can be independent historical forces in their own right, so that, for example, the abolition of slavery should be credited partly to moral persuasion rather than reduced entirely to shifts in the economic base; (c) I think individual leaders genuinely matter, such that history could have gone differently had certain people never lived; (d) I think we can legitimately condemn past practices as unjust even though the people involved had their moral outlook shaped by their material conditions; and (e) I want my view of history to be a falsifiable, evidence-sensitive one rather than a framework that can absorb any outcome.

Which of these commitments are in real tension, which only appear to be, and where the tension is genuine, what is the least costly revision? Please rank the problems by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and in particular tell me whether the standard move for reconciling the base with the independence of ideas — saying the superstructure is "relatively autonomous" — actually saves my position or just trades one inconsistency for another elsewhere in the list.
