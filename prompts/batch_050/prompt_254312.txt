Write a deadpan satirical annual report, about 600–700 words, issued by an invented body called the Institute for Comparative Atrocity Metrics, announcing this year's updated league table of history's greatest evils. The report should apply, in full bureaucratic earnestness, the idioms of sports analytics and actuarial science to events such as the Holocaust, the Atlantic slave trade, the Mongol conquests, the Holodomor, and the Cambodian genocide: promotion and relegation between divisions as new scholarship revises death tolls, disputed casualty figures sent to a review panel, tiebreak procedures, a 'contested season' for events whose classification as genocide remains under appeal, and a preseason outlook noting emerging contenders. The target of the satire is the practice of ranking and commensurating atrocities — the 'worst atrocity in history' debates, the casualty-count arms race, the conversion of suffering into a single metric — not the events or their victims, who must never be the butt of a joke. The horror should arise from how naturally the administrative language fits, and the irony should stay legible: a reader must be able to tell that it is the Institute's cheerful assumption of commensurability that is being indicted. Include at least two moments where the apparatus visibly strains — for instance, the committee deadlocking over whether famine deaths count as intentional for seeding purposes, or a footnote conceding that the metric cannot represent certain categories of loss at all, before proceeding anyway. Close with a 'Note on Methodology' that is technically scrupulous and morally tone-deaf in exactly equal measure.
