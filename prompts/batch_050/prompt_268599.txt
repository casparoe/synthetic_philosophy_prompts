Last week down the pub my mate Dan (physics degree, now builds trading algorithms) told me that the mind is 'just software running on the hardware of the brain', and that this is why he reckons mind uploading must be possible in principle, even if the tech is a century off. My girlfriend, who's doing a masters in cognitive neuroscience, rolled her eyes and said the slogan was seventies dogma that the field has moved well past. Neither of them, when pressed, could actually state what the slogan claims beyond the general vibe, which is what got me annoyed enough to write this.

So take the one-liner 'the mind is to the brain as software is to hardware' (or the snappier 'the mind is the software of the brain'). I've since run into it again in some Hofstadter I'm reading, where it gets dressed up as this picture of tiny balls ('simmballs') knocking around in the brain whose collisions only matter insofar as they instantiate big-picture symbols ('simms') bumping into each other, with the strong suggestion that the symbol level is where the real shoving happens. Pull the slogan apart into the distinct precise theses it could be making: that mental states are multiply realisable; that what matters about a brain is its functional organisation rather than the squishy stuff it's made of; that the symbol level is genuinely causally efficacious in its own right rather than just a handy redescription of neurons firing; maybe even that computation is substrate-independent all the way down. For each reading, say whether it's basically a platitude, a substantive but defensible claim, or probably false -- and crucially, which of these theses Dan would actually need for the uploading argument, because I suspect the slogan lends his conclusion more support than the weak readings and less than he quietly assumes.

One thing nagging at me as a follow-up: on the Hofstadter-flavoured reading, if all the causal work happens up at the symbol level, is anything still pushing the simmballs around at the bottom, or have we just renamed the mystery and called it a level of description? Please answer in British English.
