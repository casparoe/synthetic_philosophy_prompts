Since retiring from the lab I've ended up running a little philosophy circle at our community centre, about 12 adults, one 90-minute evening a month. Next session is Newton's bucket and the whole absolute-vs-relational space fight, and I don't want to lecture at them — I want them to bump into the problem in their own bodies before anyone names it. Design an activity for the evening with at least two parts: one where people genuinely feel rotation with their eyes closed (swivel chair, bucket of water on a rope, whatever's safe with a group this age), and one using that old fairground trick where a spinning striped surround makes a stationary person feel like they're the one turning. The point is that the group discovers, in their own stomachs, that the same "I can just feel I'm moving" evidence seems to prove absolute space in part one and then gets undercut in part two — which is roughly Mach's line, but they shouldn't hear that untill the debrief. Give me materials, timing, safety notes, and a debrief mapping each moment onto substantivalism vs relationism. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be upfront about what no tabletop demo can settle: I take it nothing in a church hall can tell us whether the water would still climb the walls if the rest of the universe weren't there, so the activity should make that gap visible rather than paper over it.
