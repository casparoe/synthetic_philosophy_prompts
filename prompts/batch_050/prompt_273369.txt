ok so I wrote this thing for my blog and I want comments before I post it. background: last month I hit snooze on a decision for the fourth time and realized I might literally be Quinn's self-torturer, except instead of a dial with 1000 settings it's my sleep schedule. for anyone who doesn't know the case: there's a guy with a device implanted, dial goes 0 to 1000, each notch up is pain so slightly worse he can't feel the difference, and he gets a pile of money per notch. at 0 he's fine, at 1000 he's in agony, but at every single step it seems rational to turn the dial once more.

here's the essay:

---

Every night for two years I've stayed up about three minutes later than the night before. Not a rule, just what happens. Each individual night, the trade feels obviously worth it: three minutes less sleep is nothing, and three more minutes of the video game / youtube hole / whatever I'm in is something. But I did the math and I've gone from falling asleep at 10:45 to falling asleep at 2:30, and I feel like garbage all the time, and at no point did I ever decide any of this.

People usually use the self-torturer to talk about money and pain, but I think the real version is time and attention, because sleep-deprivation is exactly the kind of harm where you can't perceive the marginal notch. Nobody can feel the difference between 7 hours 12 minutes and 7 hours 9 minutes. But the difference between 8 hours and 5 hours is my entire personality.

The philosophers' puzzle is whether the self-torturer's preferences are intransitive (prefers each notch+1 to notch, but prefers 0 to 1000) and whether that makes him irrational or whether the rational choice framework is what's broken. I think my case is worse than his though, because he at least gets money each time. I'm paying for each notch in sleep and receiving content I've already forgotten by morning. I'm not the self-torturer, I'm the self-torturer with a negative payout.

My proposed fix is a hard cutoff at 11pm, but I notice this is basically admitting I can't be trusted with marginal reasoning at all, which feels like giving up on being a rational agent. If the dial is rigged so every step looks free, the only winning move is to refuse to look at the dial.
