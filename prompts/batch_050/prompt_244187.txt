You are a poet who is also a working physicist, known for verse that specialists respect for its accuracy and general readers enjoy for its music. Before you write anything, think carefully through the physics of Maxwell's demon: where the entropy really goes, why the naive version of the thought experiment fails, and what the modern resolution actually claims. You may sketch your reasoning briefly, but finish by presenting only the polished poem.

The commission: a Shakespearean sonnet (three quatrains and a closing couplet, ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, iambic pentameter) about Maxwell's demon. The poem must dramatize the actual arc of the thought experiment's history, and the form must do structural work:

- The first quatrain should stage the demon at its door, sorting fast molecules from slow, apparently conjuring a temperature difference from nothing — the second law of thermodynamics seemingly mocked by a being whose only tool is knowledge.
- The second quatrain should let that apparent victory breathe: the demon as the fantasy of pure intelligence defeating mere statistics, information as something free, weightless, exempt from physics.
- The turn should come at the third quatrain: follow the demon's own ledger. It cannot act on what it has not recorded; its memory fills with bits; and a memory that fills must eventually be wiped. Here the poem should register that measurement itself can in principle be had for free — the bill arrives later, at erasure, at roughly kT ln 2 of heat per forgotten bit (Landauer's principle). This relocation of the cost from seeing to forgetting is the poem's volta, and it must be precise enough that a philosopher of physics would nod rather than wince.
- The couplet should deliver the moral without sloganeering: the demon never cheated the second law; it enacted it. Information is physical, and forgetting is where the universe collects.

The poem should work on two levels at once: a reader who knows the Szilard–Landauer–Bennett resolution should find the physics unmistakably right, while a reader who has never heard of any of it should still find a compelling poem about knowledge, memory, and the price of letting go. Avoid proper names and equations in the verse itself; render the physics as image and rhythm. Give the sonnet a title.
