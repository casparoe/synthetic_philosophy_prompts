As a longtime reader of LessWrong and adjacent forums, I've absorbed the usual diet of decision theory posts — Newcomb problems, timeless decision theory, Hofstadter's superrationality, the occasional acausal trade speculation — but I've recently gotten stuck on the more general idea sometimes discussed under the heading of "evidential cooperation in large worlds." As I understand it, the rough thought is this: in a sufficiently large universe (or multiverse), enormous numbers of agents are running decision procedures similar enough to mine that my actual choice constitutes evidence about what all of them choose; and since their choices matter to me even though I can never causally interact with most of them, a single decision of mine can be "worth" vastly more than its local causal footprint suggests. My friend and I have been arguing about whether this is a deep insight or a dressed-up confusion, and I'd like a careful conceptual explanation that helps me see which of our disagreements are substantive.

Specifically, I'd like you to address the following points of confusion. First, how does this differ from ordinary superrationality? In Hofstadter's Lotingen setup there's a known, symmetric game with known counterparts; the large-worlds version seems to ask me to cooperate with agents I've never identified, on the strength of a similarity I can't measure. Does that move from "symmetry with known copies" to "statistical similarity with unknown others" introduce a gap, or is it smooth? Second, what actually determines the size of my "cluster" — is it everyone running EDT-like reasoning, everyone with a human-ish cognitive architecture, everyone who would be moved by this very argument? It seems like the answer does enormous work in the theory but is rarely pinned down. Third, does the argument effectively turn mundane, low-stakes choices — voting, eating meat, being considerate in traffic — into leveraged interventions, and if so, does EDT in a large world quietly converge on something like rule consequentialism? Fourth, the flip side that worries my friend: if my decisions correlate with a vast population, doesn't that license strange "policy coupling," like reasoning my way into cooperating with hypothetical aliens, or feeling evidentially pressured by what choices would be common among agents like me — is that a feature or a reductio?

Finally, I'd like a sense of how much of this survives a change of framing. The whole line of thought is usually stated evidentially, but people in these communities often prefer functional or updateless framings that replace evidential correlation with logical or subjunctive dependence. Does the large-worlds conclusion change, shrink, or dissolve when you translate it out of the evidential idiom? Please give me a gut-level explanation rather than a formal treatment — I want to walk away able to tell, in a concrete case, when evidential-cooperation reasoning is genuinely doing work and when it's just moral intuition wearing a decision-theoretic costume.
