I am preparing a graduate seminar on Bernard Williams's critique of impartial moral theory, and the standard example -- the man who rescues his wife rather than a stranger, and whose deliberation contains what Williams calls one thought too many -- has been discussed to death in my students' source readings. I need a replacement thought experiment built to the following specifications. First, the scenario must be realistic and contemporary: no trolleys, no drowning swimmers, no transplants, no science fiction. Second, the agent's situation must be one in which impartial moral theory and the agent's personal commitments recommend the very same action, so that what is at issue is not the verdict but the deliberative route by which the agent reaches it. Third, and this is the constraint that makes the case hard to construct, the superfluous thought must concern the agent's own ground project or vocation -- a career, a life's work, a creative undertaking -- rather than a personal relationship, so that the case exercises the integrity limb of Williams's objection rather than the familiar spousal-attachment limb. Fourth, the deliberation must be specified explicitly enough that one can point to the exact premise Williams would fault, and it must be plausible that a decent, non-monstrous person would actually deliberate that way; a case that only works by making the agent comically self-effacing stipulates rather than generates the objection. Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario itself, in two to four sentences; (2) the agent's deliberation, stated as the agent would think it; (3) an explanation of which premise Williams would call one thought too many, and why the same objection does not collapse into the claim that the agent should simply abandon the project; (4) the strongest reply available to a defender of the impartial theory, redescribing the deliberation so that the offending premise becomes permissible; (5) a diagnosis of which feature of the case is doing the load-bearing work, including what happens to the objection if that feature is removed.
