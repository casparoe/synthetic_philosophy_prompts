An article in a popular science magazine summarises recent philosophical work on degrees of sentience and moral status as follows:

'Philosophers have established that sentience is not an on–off switch but a dial, like temperature. Insects, shrimp and fish sit low on the dial, dogs and pigs higher, adult humans at the top; a thermostat, which merely registers temperature, has none at all. Because moral status tracks the dial, harming a shrimp is wrong in the same way that harming a dog is wrong, only less so — roughly in proportion to how sentient each creature is. And since we cannot prove that any other being is sentient, the only safe policy is to extend equal moral consideration to every organism, just in case.'

I'm inclined to think the dial metaphor captures something real, but the final sentence doesn't seem to follow from it, and I'm not sure the thermostat point is as settled as the article implies. Audit the summary sentence by sentence: for each, say what a philosopher working on graded moral status would accept, what they would want to qualify (for instance, whether 'sentience' is one dimension or several, and whether moral status is usually taken to scale continuously and proportionally with it), and what is simply wrong or a non sequitur. Then tell me whether the closing policy recommendation survives your corrections.
