ok so quick background, no philosophy degree or anything, i fix boilers for a living and read old books on my lunch breaks, and for like three months now me and my friend Tomasz (civil engineer, extremely confident) have been arguing about Thomas Hobbes and geometry. His position: Hobbes was a math crank. The man announced he'd squared the circle, got taken apart in print by John Wallis, kept doubling down for years anyway, and his whole "philosophy" of geometry was a cover story for not being able to do the actual work. My position: Hobbes's idea of geometry -- that it's about bodies in motion, that a definition of a figure is really a recipe for how to make it, so we understand figures because we construct them -- is genuinely interesting even if his proofs were garbage, and being wrong at math doesn't make you wrong about what math is.

The thing we keep circling is the opening of Leviathan, the Introduction, where he says:

"Nature (the art whereby God hath made and governes the world) is by the art of man, as in many other things, so in this also imitated, that it can make an Artificial Animal. For seeing life is but a motion of Limbs, the begining whereof is in some principall part within; why may we not say, that all Automata (Engines that move themselves by springs and wheeles as doth a watch) have an artificiall life?"

Tomasz reads this as the smoking gun: Hobbes thought that making something and knowing something are the same thing, and that conflation is exactly why he could never see his geometry was broken -- if you built it, it must be right. I read it almost the opposite way, like he's saying geometry is special because it's the one place where making and knowing really do line up, and the watch stuff is him extending that insight, not the mistake itself. We are clearly going nowhere yelling about it so we agreed to try one of those adversarial collaboration things and i want you to draft the joint statement we're supposed to end up with.
