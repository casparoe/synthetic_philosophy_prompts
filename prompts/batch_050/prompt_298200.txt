Ok so I'm in this ethics unit at school and we got into this debate about whether morality is made of actual rules or not. I decided to just go by my gut on a bunch of cases and write down my verdicts, and now I can't find any rule that fits all of them. Here's what I got:

1. My friend lent me his bike and I promised I'd give it back saturday. Saturday comes and he texts me that he wants it right now so he can chase down his little brother who took his phone, and he's clearly furious and looking for a fight. Giving it back right then feels wrong, even though I promised.

2. I promised my cousin I'd come to her band concert, but my grandma ended up in the hospital that same night so I skipped it. Totally fine, nobody would even question it.

3. But: I once promised a friend I'd watch his dog and ditched to go to a party instead. Obviously not fine.

4. A kid in my class copied off my test. Telling the teacher feels right, even though everyone would call me a snitch.

5. But my best friend copied my homework once and I covered for him, and honestly that felt fine? Maybe because nobody got hurt.

6. My little brother asked me if his drawing was good. It was terrible. I said yes. Completely fine.

7. A friend asked me if she should try out for the soccer team. She's genuinely bad and she already barely keeps up with school, and I said go for it anyway to be nice. That felt NOT fine, like I set her up to fail.

So 2 and 3 fit something like "keep your promises unless something more important comes up," and 4 vs 5 could maybe be explained by whether anyone gets hurt. But 6 and 7 wreck everything because they're both about sparing someone's feelings with a little dishonesty and I gave opposite verdicts. And 1 shows even something as basic as "give back what you borrowed" can flip.

Our teacher mentioned this idea (I think it comes from a guy named Dancy?) that maybe there just is no rule — that the same fact, like "you promised," can be a reason to do something in one situation and count for nothing, or even count against it, in another. Like how "she's shy" might be a reason to invite someone to a party in one case and a reason not to in another.

So my questions: (a) Is there actually a principle that captures all 7 of my verdicts, even a complicated ugly one with alot of clauses? Give me your best attempt, stated as precisely as you can. (b) If it can't be done, which verdict should I give up? I feel most shaky about 5 and 7. (c) And if no clean principle works, does that actually mean the no-rules view wins? Or could the rules people just say the principles are super long and context-sensitive and we simply haven't found them yet — and if so, is there even a real disagreement there, or is it the same position wearing two outfits?

Don't need sources or anything, just your reasoning.
