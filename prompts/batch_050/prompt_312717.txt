Suppose a contradiction were discovered in Peano Arithmetic tomorrow — a widely checked, formal proof of 0=1 that no one can fault. How should mathematics respond? The fallback positions I already know, and don't need repeated:

1. Adopt a paraconsistent logic so that the contradiction no longer entails everything, and keep working.
2. Go dialetheist: accept that some contradictions, including this one, are simply true.
3. Retreat to a weaker, trusted fragment (say, primitive recursive arithmetic) and rebuild as much mathematics as survives on that base, treating the rest as merely heuristically reliable.
4. Blame infinity: become an ultrafinitist and hold that the fault lies in quantifying over all natural numbers or in unrestricted induction.
5. Quarantine and patch: excise the axioms or proof steps implicated in the contradiction and carry on with the theorems, as set theory did after Russell showed Frege's system inconsistent.

Give me a genuinely different response — not a rewording, special case, or combination of the five above. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good. Format your reply as a numbered list of exactly three items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion: first, the new response; second, how it differs from everything on my list; third, your verdict on its merits.
