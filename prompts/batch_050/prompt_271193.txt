My half-formed idea: in the second Untimely Meditation, Nietzsche holds that monumental, antiquarian, and critical history each serve life in the right measure and damage it in excess, and commentators generally stop there — but I think the text contains, or can be made to yield, a criterion for what the right measure is, centered on his notion of a culture's plastic power, and that without such a criterion his prescription is empty. Turn this into a prospectus: state the question so it has a determinate answer (is the balancing of the three modes governed by any principle beyond case-by-case judgment, and if so, what is it and whose standpoint does it require?); explain why the obvious readings — that the right measure is whatever serves survival, or whatever serves the production of great individuals — leave the question open or collapse into each other; describe what I would actually do, month by month (close reading, reconstruction of the strongest candidate criterion, testing it against cases where the three modes conflict, such as a community deciding whether to preserve or repudiate a compromised founding figure); say what a positive and a negative result would each look like, and what would count as the chapter failing. Be honest about scope risks, especially the temptation to drift into a general interpretation of the early Nietzsche. I'll supply the citations myself.
