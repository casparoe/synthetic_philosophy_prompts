I TA for a graduate seminar on semantics-pragmatics interface, and next term I'm designing the unit on presupposition. Rather than assign yet another survey of dynamic semantics, trivalent logics, DRT, and the various pragmatic-reductionist accounts, I want students to first work from a specification: what does a theory of presupposition — and in particular of presupposition projection — actually have to accomplish to count as adequate? I'd like you to write that specification for me, as a requirements list with the consideration or data point motivating each requirement. My rough sense of what belongs on it includes: explaining projection behavior under the standard embeddings (negation, conditionals, modals, questions, attitude reports), including the filtering asymmetries between, say, antecedent-triggered and consequent-triggered cases; handling the binding problem, where a trigger's content seems partly entailed by the local context and the theory must say what residue gets projected; accommodating the proviso problem, where the projected presupposition of a conditional often looks stronger than what speakers actually take on board; modeling accommodation itself — when hearers can silently adjust the common ground, what makes an accommodated presupposition too implausible or too controversial to slip in, and what distinguishes local from global accommodation; respecting the differences among trigger classes (definites, factives, aspectual verbs, expressives, additive particles) rather than treating presupposition as one uniform phenomenon; and staying consistent with an independently plausible compositional semantics rather than bolting presupposition on as an ad hoc device. Please add whatever I've missed and correct me where I've mischaracterized something.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark it as non-negotiable (any theory failing it is refuted, not merely unfashionable) or as a camp-specific preference (something one tradition treats as a datum and another treats as negotiable or as explained away pragmatically). What I most want to see is whether some subset of the natural desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, whether full compositionality, uniform treatment of triggers, the observed filtering pattern, and a single mechanism for accommodation can all be had at once, or whether the standard theories each buy two or three of these by surrendering another. If the requirements list itself rules out every existing framework in at least one respect, say so plainly; that's a finding, not a failure of the list. Also identify which single requirement does the most eliminative work across the contemporary contenders.

Two framing constraints. First, fix the purpose as: adequate for a working semanticist building a compositional fragment that could interface with pragmatics research, not for a psycholinguist modeling processing or a historian of the field — if the purpose changes the requirements, note where. Second, end with a candid note on the soft spots: which of your classifications of requirements as non-negotiable versus camp-specific is most contestable, and what new data, argument, or formal result would lead you to revise the list or move an item between columns. No bibliography needed — I know the literature well enough to attach the readings myself.
