Build me a thought experiment I can use in a seminar on meritocracy and desert. I want a hiring or admissions case where the candidate who most *deserves* the spot is not the candidate the meritocratic institution should actually pick -- and here's the kicker, choosing the more deserving candidate has to be the *anti-meritocratic* move, not some compassionate exception to an otherwise merit-respecting process.

Constraints, and please respect all of them:

1. Realistic setting -- a hospital residency, a fire department, a grad program, something like that. No sci-fi, no perfect information machines, no demons.
2. Nobody cheats, lies, bribes, or is biased. The divergence has to fall out of the situation itself, not out of anyone's bad behavior.
3. Don't pull the standard Rawls move where the deserving person's edge turns out to be a product of lucky family background or whatever. Assume both candidates had equally fortunate or unfortunate upbringings; the case has to work even after that variable is held fixed.
4. The scenario itself should be one paragraph. I want to be able to read it out loud without losing the room.

After the scenario: walk through why the desert verdict and the meritocratic-selection verdict come apart, and point at the *exact* feature of the case doing the prying. Then tell me what happens if I remove that feature -- does the case collapse, or does it survive in weakened form? Finally, steelman the person who thinks desert and merit-tracking selection can never really diverge in a well-designed institution: what would they say is structurally wrong with my case, what design tweak would they propose to make the two verdicts converge again, and does that tweak survive contact with the scenario as I've asked you to build it?

Please write the whole thing in American English.
