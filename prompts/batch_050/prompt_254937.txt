After a career in experimental science, I have recently been reading philosophy and have provisionally accepted the following interpretation of Wittgenstein’s private language argument: the argument does not show that sensations are unreal, that introspection is impossible, or that every claim about one’s inner life requires public verification. Its target is narrower. A wholly private ostensive definition cannot, by itself, establish a genuine rule for applying a sensation term, because it supplies no independent distinction between applying the term correctly and merely seeming to oneself to apply it correctly. On this reading, public practices matter not because outsiders must be able to inspect the sensation, but because usable concepts require standards of correction that cannot be constituted solely by whatever feels right to the speaker at the time.

What would count against this interpretation? Please distinguish several kinds of possible defeaters: textual considerations concerning Wittgenstein’s likely target; conceptual counterexamples in which a private rule appears genuinely normative rather than merely habitual; empirical findings about memory, sensory discrimination, locked-in communication, animal cognition, or isolated speakers that would bear on the argument; and reasons for thinking that public language faces the same bootstrapping problem allegedly fatal to private language. I am especially interested in cases that fall between full publicity and absolute privacy—for example, a person who invents a stable code for recurring experiences that no one else can identify, or a community whose members can correct one another’s reports without sharing any directly comparable inner episodes.

Please rank the defeaters from potentially fatal to merely inconvenient, and separate what defenders of my interpretation would themselves have to concede from what they could plausibly absorb by clarifying notions such as rule, criterion, correction, and publicity. Also assess whether the interpretation has been made so flexible that any apparent private regularity can be redescribed as unsupported by a genuine standard. If so, explain whether that unfalsifiability is a defect in the interpretation or simply reflects the fact that it is a conceptual claim rather than an empirical hypothesis.
