So I keep hearing the standard pitch for functionalism as "the mind is software, the brain is hardware" -- my cog-sci textbook leans on it hard, and my friends use it to argue that uploading is possible in principle and that there's no mystery about a computer being conscious, since consciousness would be a matter of running the right program, and programs don't care what they run on. I want an audit of how far the analogy actually holds, because I suspect it's doing more work in those conclusions than it deserves.

Give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the program, the hardware, the compiler, the input/output, the running instance -- and where multiple realizability fits in (the same software on different chips = the same mental state in different substrates?). Then the disanalogies, and be ruthless about which ones matter: e.g. software is cleanly separable from hardware in a way a brain's activity maybe isn't from the wet stuff doing it; a program was written by someone with a spec, a brain evolved; hardware is passive and interchangeable, neurons are living cells doing a million things besides "computing"; you can copy a program bit-for-bit but what would copying a mind even copy; programs run on a clock with discrete states, brains are squishy continuous analog messes. Tell me which of these (or others) actually threaten the multiple-realizability thesis itself, and which are just aesthetic differences that don't touch it.

Then the payoff question: once the disanalogies are on the table, does the analogy still support the conclusions my friends draw (uploading in principle, no special problem for machine consciousness), or was the software/hardware picture quietly smuggling in the separability and copyability that those conclusions need? If the analogy breaks at exactly the load-bearing points, what's the honest version of functionalism that survives -- and is there a better everyday analogy for it, or does ditching the computer metaphor make functionalism suddenly look way less obvious?
