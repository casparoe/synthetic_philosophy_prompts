I'm drafting a short statement of my views for an animal ethics reading group, and before I circulate it I want an audit of whether the package is actually coherent, because I have a nagging feeling something in it doesn't fit but I can't locate it. Here is roughly where I've landed. (1) I'm a graded sentientist: moral status comes in degrees, tracks the capacity for and intensity of valenced experience, and nothing else — not species membership, not rationality, not relational ties to humans — contributes fundamental status on top of that. (2) I think insects have some status, at least bees and probably fruit flies: swatting a fly for idle amusement is pro tanto wrong, though far less bad than killing a chicken. (3) I think a thermostat has exactly zero moral status — there is nothing it is like to be one, and this wouldn't change if the feedback loop were made arbitrarily sophisticated, say an adaptive PID controller that learns. (4) I reject species discounting: a unit of suffering is a unit of suffering whoever bears it, so if a fly could somehow suffer as intensely as a person, its suffering would count for exactly as much. (5) I'm a substrate independence theorist: a sufficiently faithful whole-brain emulation of a bee would have the same moral status as the bee. (6) I think nature contains no sharp thresholds here — moral status does not jump discontinuously from zero to nonzero at some precise level of circuit complexity. And (7) I think it's plainly permissible to drive on the highway on a summer evening, accepting the mass insect deaths this foreseeably causes, because the human interests at stake are weighty enough.

Please treat this as a consistency audit rather than a survey of the literature. Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs or triples are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each tension is. For each genuine tension, identify precisely which commitment has to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me — I'd rather amend one clause than rebuild the whole view. In particular, if some of my commitments jointly force me toward a conclusion I'd find embarrassing (about thermostats, about emulated bees, or about my windshield), spell out the derivation step by step rather than just gesturing at it. And for any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, show why, since the reading group will certainly raise it. End by ranking the problems from most to least serious and telling me which single revision would buy the most coherence.
