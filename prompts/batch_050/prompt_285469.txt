So I've been going back and forth with a friend who defends negative utilitarianism (the suffering-focused kind, not the meme version) and I want to stress-test it properly. Here are the objections I already have on my list, so don't just hand me one of these back:

- the world-exploder problem: if only suffering counts, painlessly ending all sentient life looks like the best available outcome
- the pinprick problem: a paradise containing exactly one brief mild headache ranks below a completely empty world, which seems absurd
- it can't rank painless outcomes against each other at all, so it's indifferent between things we clearly aren't indifferent between (eternal unconscious dreamless void vs. a flourishing but suffering-free civilization)
- it licenses ugly instrumental tradeoffs, like painlessly killing one person to prevent a huge number of very mild pains spread across strangers
- the asymmetry at its foundation (suffering is bad in a way happiness isn't correspondingly good) is doing all the work and may just be a contested intuition dressed up as an axiom
- in practice it converges with classical utilitarianism on almost every real-world policy question, so it's less a rival theory than a change of emphasis

That's everything standard as far as I can tell, so I'm asking for something new. Give me one objection that is not on this list and not a rewording, special case, or combination of anything on it -- in particular not just another gruesome conclusion it commits you to, since the list already covers that shape of complaint.

And I want you to actually commit on whether it's any good: pick a side, say whether you'd rank it above or below the strongest objection already on my list, and why. If the best you can come up with is weak, say that plainly rather than overselling it.
