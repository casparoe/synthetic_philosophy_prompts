I am a novelist researching a story about people who mistake dependence for freedom, and I would like a philosophically careful reading of E. M. Forster’s The Machine Stops through Marx’s account of technology. Begin with this passage from Chapter 15 of Capital, Volume I, where Marx writes:

“Like every other increase in the productiveness of labour, machinery is intended to cheapen commodities, and, by shortening that portion of the working-day, in which the labourer works for himself, to lengthen the other portion that he gives, without an equivalent, to the capitalist. In short, it is a means for producing surplus-value.”

Forster seems to imagine the case that Marx’s sentence appears to exclude: a comprehensive Machine that supplies every want while leaving almost no visible working day to lengthen. Does this mean that domination has ceased to take the form of extracting surplus-value and has instead become dependence, administration, and the inability to live without the apparatus? Or does the Machine still embody a capitalist logic even after the capitalist and the labourer have become difficult to locate?

Please distinguish carefully between machinery as a productive force, machinery as capitalists deploy it, and machinery as it appears when the social relations behind it have been forgotten. In doing so, discuss the button-serviced cells, the culture of remote lectures and mediated contact, the authority of the Book of the Machine, Vashti’s reverence, Kuno’s desire for direct bodily experience, and the final collapse. Does Kuno identify the true source of unfreedom, or does he mistake spiritual and sensory impoverishment for the whole problem? And what should we make of the ending: does it recover genuine human relations, or only offer a tragic humanism after political agency has disappeared?

I especially want one place where Forster’s story resists the Marxist reading rather than merely illustrating it. Please do not quote Forster or supply a bibliography; I am interested in the strongest interpretation, including where Marx illuminates the story and where the story exposes a limitation in Marx’s formulation.
