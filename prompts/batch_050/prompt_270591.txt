You are a philosopher of mind specializing in the scientific study of consciousness. Think carefully about what exactly the question below presupposes before answering.

I recently read about the color phi phenomenon: a red dot briefly flashes at one spot on a screen, and a fraction of a second later a green dot flashes at a nearby spot. Observers don't see two separate flashes — they see a single dot that moves from the first position to the second and changes from red to green about halfway along its path. What puzzles me is this. The color change is seen at the midpoint of the trajectory, yet the information that the second dot is green only becomes available when the second flash occurs. So the brain must hold back the experience until it knows what happened next, and only then deliver the finished movie to consciousness. Conscious perception, in other words, must systematically lag behind reality by at least the interval between the two flashes. My questions: how large is this perceptual lag in general, and given that people can catch balls and swat insects, how is it possible to react to events faster than the lag permits? Can training shrink the delay?
