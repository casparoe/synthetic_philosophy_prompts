Here is the student's answer:

"A 'why P rather than Q' question is well posed when P and Q form a legitimate contrast class: the two cases must share enough background structure that a single relevance relation can pick out the difference-makers between them. Asking why the Industrial Revolution happened in Britain rather than China satisfies this condition, since by the eighteenth century both were advanced agrarian-commercial economies.

Consider Pomeranz's explanation: Britain escaped the ecological constraints pressing on its core region because it had conveniently located coal deposits and access to land-intensive goods from its New World colonies, whereas the Yangzi Delta's hinterland could not provide equivalent relief. This is a good contrastive explanation because it identifies factors present in Britain and absent in China while holding the rest of the comparison fixed. Needham asked essentially the same question — why China failed to industrialize despite its early technological lead — and his answer, that Chinese bureaucracy suppressed merchant enterprise, is another contrastive account of the same phenomenon.

The question does face the objection that Britain and China may be too dissimilar to form a proper contrast class: comparing a small island kingdom to a vast continental agrarian empire risks smuggling the answer into the framing. However, this objection can be met by narrowing the comparison to England and the Yangzi Delta, which are much more comparable units.

Applying the reversibility test: the question passes, because 'why China rather than Britain' is obviously the same question asked from the other side, so both directions are equally answerable. That said, the question is not fully well posed in one respect: since we cannot run history twice, the counterfactual claims it relies on are untestable, and an untestable question cannot count as fully well posed."
