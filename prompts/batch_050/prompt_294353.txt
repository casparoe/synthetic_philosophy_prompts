I coordinate a small current-affairs discussion group that meets at a local café, and ever since our last national election -- in which, as usual, the seat where I live was decided long before anyone cast a ballot -- I have been circling the question of electoral reform. What strikes me is that when my friends argue about "proportional representation" versus "first-past-the-post," they are rarely disagreeing about the mechanics. They are disagreeing about what an election is for in the first place: whether it should manufacture a government that someone can be clearly blamed for, or assemble a legislature that mirrors the electorate, and whether a "wasted vote" is a defect of the system or simply the price of decisiveness. I have no training in political science, so I want to exploit that: I would like a sorting quiz I can hand to the group that places each person at a position on electoral system design based purely on their instincts as voters, without them knowing in advance which camp they are headed for.

Please build the quiz as a decision tree whose branches are drawn from the logical space of the debate rather than from the vocabulary of it. Each diagnostic question should be phrased as a concrete trade-off a voter could weigh in their gut -- for instance, choosing between a parliament where a party with a tenth of the votes holds a tenth of the seats but no one can form a stable government, and a parliament with a clear winner where your own ballot never influences anything; or between a representative you personally chose who belongs to a party you oppose, and a party you support whose candidates you had no hand in picking. No question may mention the name of any system, and no question should require knowing what terms like "proportionality" or "accountability" mean to a specialist. The tree should have exactly five terminal positions: a defender of first-past-the-post majoritarianism, a supporter of a mixed compensatory system, a supporter of pure party-list proportionality, a supporter of candidate-centered ranked voting in multi-member districts, and a person whose answers reject the framing altogether -- someone who concludes that the trouble lies with political parties themselves rather than with the formula for counting votes, and so ends up sympathetic to something like selection by lot.

Format the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, one per terminal position, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should be a sentence or two that names the position in plain language, states the sequence of quiz answers that lands a person there, and identifies the commitment that position's defenders are most reluctant to say out loud. If any two of the five positions cannot actually be separated by honest instinct-level questions, do not paper over it: say so inside the relevant items and explain which further distinction a longer quiz would need.
