I’m a software engineer building a taxonomy service used by conservation teams. Each week we receive proposals to merge, split, or relabel populations as species, but our team has no specialist taxonomist and decisions can affect which populations receive legal protection. Write a reusable decision procedure for evaluating these proposals in light of the species problem: which forms of evidence to request and in what order; how to weigh reproductive isolation, ongoing gene flow, shared ancestry, ecological specialization, and morphological discontinuity when they conflict; what counts as sufficient evidence; and when the system should mark a case as undecidable or refer it to human judgment because the answer depends on the purpose of classification. Distinguish changing our model of a population from changing the rank or unit we choose to track, and make the procedure explicit about what it cannot settle. Apply it to three cases: two populations that interbreed freely at a contact zone but remain distinct elsewhere, an asexual lineage with a stable ecological niche, and a population that conservation managers want protected despite incomplete evidence of evolutionary independence. Include at least one outcome that would be practically inconvenient but follows from the procedure.
