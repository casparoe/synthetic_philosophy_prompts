I've been reading Overcoming Bias and LessWrong since the late 2000s, and I'm organizing a small screening-and-discussion night for friends from that milieu: we're watching two Star Trek: The Next Generation episodes back to back -- "Realm of Fear" (Barclay's transporter phobia, where he stays conscious mid-transport and sees creatures in the matter stream) and "Second Chances" (the transporter accident that produces two Rikers, each apparently continuous with the pre-beaming original). I'd like you to write the discussion guide.

Read these two episodes through the debate about teletransportation versus gradual replacement. The contrast I want at the center: on one side, the intuition (familiar from Parfit-style teletransportation cases and from a lot of the transporter-as-death writing on the blogs) that destructive scanning plus reconstruction yields a copy, not a survivor; on the other side, the intuition pumped by Chalmers-style gradual replacement thought experiments, where a brain is swapped neuron by neuron for functional duplicates and it seems absurd to name the neuron at which the person dies and the impostor wakes up. "Realm of Fear" seems written as if the first horn is simply false -- Barclay's experience is continuous through the beam -- while "Second Chances" dramatizes the branching problem that the gradual-replacement intuition has trouble with: if continuity of function and memory is what matters, which Riker is the original, and does the question even have an answer?

For each episode, tell me where it illuminates the philosophy, where it smuggles in a premise a careful person should refuse (does "Realm of Fear" just stipulate away the hard question by giving Barclay a point of view inside the stream?), and where the fiction complicates something the thought experiments leave out -- I'm thinking of how the two Rikers' diverging lives and resentments make the identity question feel different in kind from Parfit's branch-line case on paper. End with the half-dozen questions you'd actually put to the room to get a good fight going.

One request: if you think my framing is confused -- for instance, if you think gradual replacement and teletransportation aren't really two horns of one problem but answer different questions, or that I'm misreading what an episode commits to -- say so plainly rather than politely building the guide on a bad foundation. I'd rather have the premise challenged than get a polished guide to a muddle.
