Ok so at lunch last week two people on my team got into an arguement about the color phi thing, where you flash a red dot on the left side of a screen and then a green dot on the right, and people report seeing a single dot move across and flip from red to green around the midpoint — even though at the moment you supposedly "saw" the midpoint, the green dot hadn't flashed yet, so how did your brain know?

Maya said the visual system buffers a few hundred milliseconds and renders the conscious version late, using info from after — she compared it to B-frames in video codecs, where a frame gets reconstructed using future reference frames before anything hits the screen. So the experience was never wrong, it was assembled with the green dot already known. Dan said no, you really experience the dot as red all the way across in real time, and then memory gets rewritten a split second later, like overwriting a log entry, so "I saw it change mid-flight" is a false memory of something that never consciously happened.

Maya said his view forces you to posit a conscious experience that leaves zero trace and gets misreported one second later — a moment of consciousness nobody, including you, can ever know about — which she said is basically incoherent. Dan said her view needs some kind of render deadline where the finished movie gets shown, and shown to who, plus the buffer would somehow have to know in advance how long to wait. Then Maya said fine, but both stories predict identical verbal reports, reaction times, and brain scans, so maybe the difference between them is empty. Dan said identical predictions just means we can't measure the difference, not that there isn't one.

I looked it up after and apparently philosophers call these the Orwellian and Stalinesque versions (rewriting history after vs staging the show trial in advance). Anyway — go move by move and tell me who's right about what, and whether they're both missing something, like whether "when exactly did you experience the color change" is even a well-posed question.
