I am a novelist, and my working vocabulary is that of the craft: narrator reliability, point of view, what a character intends versus what they deceive themselves about, and the difference between a speaker who is tracking the truth, one who is deliberately inverting it, and one who simply has no stake in it. Restate, as faithfully as you can in those terms, Frankfurt's distinction between the liar and the bullshitter — the liar's design on our belief about what is true, the bullshitter's indifference to truth in service of some other effect — and then translate into the same vocabulary the current dispute over what LLM hallucination is: lying, bullshit, or plain error. For each claim, tell me whether it translates cleanly, only with distortion, or not at all. I suspect the hardest part will be finding, in a novelist's terms, the notion of a speaker with no intention whatsoever: a narrator is at least pretending to be one, and unreliability in fiction is always someone's achievement. Tell me what a generation of text with no authorial stance behind it corresponds to in my framework — a found document, a narrator so unreliable the category collapses, something else — and whether the residue points to something the novelist's vocabulary cannot say or to something the philosophical debate is getting wrong, for instance by smuggling agency into a place it does not belong. Finally, say what argument or consideration would most likely change your verdict, and where in the translation you think the reasoning is weakest.
