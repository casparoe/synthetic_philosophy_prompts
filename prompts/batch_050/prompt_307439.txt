So in my philosophy elective we've been doing a unit on sleep and consciousness, and my teacher mentioned this old debate about whether dreams are even real experiences you have while asleep, or whether you just wake up with a memory of a dream that never actually "played" during the night. At first I thought that was the dumbest thing I'd ever heard, but then I tried to reconstruct the argument for it and now it's stuck in my head because I can't find where it breaks. Here's my version: (1) For any claim that something happened, there has to be some way, at least in principle, to tell the difference between it actually happening and it only seeming afterward like it happened. (2) The only evidence anyone ever has for their dreams is the impression they wake up with. (3) There's no possible check -- not brain scans, not anything -- that could settle whether you were actually experiencing the dream at 3am versus just waking up at 7am with the feeling of having dreamt, since the brain scan stuff only shows activity, not experiences. (4) So the claim "I had a dream experience last night" can't ever be distinguished from "I woke up with a false memory of one." (5) But a claim that nothing could ever confirm or disconfirm isn't really saying anything about the world. (6) So there are no experiences during sleep -- dreams are just waking memories, and it's literally meaningless to ask when during the night the dream "happened."

Here's why this is bugging me: I can't accept (6), because it seems insane to say that when I woke up terrified from a nightmare last week, nothing frightening was going on at all while I was asleep. But also, the argument feels like it proves way too much. My friend pointed out that you could run the exact same steps for the headache I had alone in my room yesterday -- the only evidence now is my memory of it, no one can check it, so by (5) did my headache never happen either? That can't be right, but the headache case and the dream case seem exactly parallel to me, so I feel like whatever saves the headache should also save the dream, and yet step (1) seems totally reasonable in both cases?

So: which step do I reject? Ideally I'd like to keep the idea that dream reports really are based on memory, because that seems obviously true, and I sort of like the skeptical vibe of the argument. But I want to know which premise is the cheapest one to drop, why that premise looked so convincing in the first place, and what it costs me to drop it. Also if you could say whether my friend's headache objection actually works or whether there's a real disanalogy between dreams and past pains that I'm missing, that would help a lot becuase that's the part we kept going in circles on.
