A colleague of mine studies political ideology and has spent years sorting movements into left, right, or genuinely neither, and I have never managed to reconstruct her rule. Her verdicts so far: Nordic social democracy — left; Thatcherism — right; Catholic distributism (anti-capitalist and anti-socialist, aiming at widely dispersed small property under traditional family and church authority) — right; Maoist communism — left; legitimist monarchism — right; kibbutz-style utopian socialism — left; and, notably, early-twentieth-century progressive eugenics of the Fabian kind — left, "however much today's left wishes otherwise."

I want to predict how she will sort two further movements. The first would abolish markets and wage labor and redistribute all land equally, but vests authority in hereditary religious elders, prescribes strict traditional family roles, and treats social hierarchy as divinely ordained. The second is anarcho-capitalism: it rejects the state, inherited status, and tradition alike, while accepting unlimited market-generated inequality.

Please answer as a political theorist who specializes in the conceptual structure of the left–right distinction. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming at least three rules that each fit all seven of her verdicts (candidates might include equality versus hierarchy, collective provision versus markets, transformation versus tradition, universalism versus particularism). Note which rule her eugenics verdict puts under the most pressure, and why. Then predict her classification of each new movement, saying which rule you take her to be running and which earlier verdict is the closest analog for each case. Finally, if her record genuinely underdetermines the answer, describe the single further test case — a movement, real or constructed — whose classification would best discriminate between the two rules you take most seriously, and say what her verdict on it would settle.
