Construct a thought experiment designed to embarrass the causal exclusion argument by turning its own machinery against an intuitively paradigmatic case of everyday, non-mental causation. The scenario must satisfy all of the following constraints: (1) it involves no minds, beliefs, or intentional states whatsoever — the cast should be something like a grenade, a sledgehammer, a gene, or a boiling kettle; (2) it features two candidate causes of a single effect, one specified at a relatively coarse or higher level of description and one at a more fine-grained or lower level, where the relation between them is one of genuine determination or realization rather than accidental correlation; (3) there is no true causal overdetermination — the two candidates are not independent sufficient causes in the way two assassins firing simultaneously would be; (4) the physics may be treated as deterministic, with no appeal to quantum indeterminacy to carve out causal room; (5) the higher-level candidate must pass a plausible counterfactual or explanatory test for causal relevance — intuitively, citing it should explain the effect better than citing only its lower-level realizer; and (6) the whole scenario fits in one paragraph and is realistic rather than science-fictional.

After presenting the scenario: (a) state the exclusion reasoning in its standard form (closure of the physical, the ban on systematic overdetermination, and the exclusion principle) and apply it step by step to your case; (b) show that it delivers a verdict nobody can accept — that the higher-level candidate is causally idle; (c) identify exactly which feature of your case is doing the work, and say what happens to the verdict if the determination relation is replaced by mere correlation or by identity; (d) explain how a defender of the exclusion argument would try to escape your case (for instance, by identifying the higher-level cause with its realizer, or by restricting the exclusion principle), and — this is the point of the exercise — assess whether that escape route remains available to a non-reductive physicalist who wants mental properties to be both distinct from and efficacious through their physical realizers, or whether the escape generalizes in a way that rescues mental causation as a byproduct.
