so my friend and I got into it again last night about lying. she's firmly in the camp of "obviously you lie to the murderer at the door, and also obviously you tell your friend her haircut is fine," and I made the mistake of bringing up that augustine thought every single lie is a sin, full stop -- even polite lies, even jokes, even lying to save someone's life. she said fine, if he's so confident in the system let him run an advice column and see how long he lasts. so: do exactly that.

write me an agony-aunt style advice column, around 500-600 words, in the voice of a calm, gentle, completely sincere augustinian ethicist answering modern letters. give him maybe four letters, something like: a woman asking whether she has to tell her friend the truth about the friend's truly terrible novel, parents wondering if they can lie to their kid to keep a surprise party secret, a nurse whose patient asks point blank whether the diagnosis is bad and the family has begged her not to say, and of course the classic -- someone hiding a person in their house with a murderer at the door asking where they are. every answer must be strictly, deadpan augustinian: no lie is ever permissible, the health of the soul outranks the safety of the body, silence and careful truthful speech are the only legitimate tools, and he treats all of this as perfectly ordinary etiquette advice, the way a normal columnist would talk about thank-you notes.

two conditions. first, the comedy has to come from him actually applying the view -- the reader should recoil at the conclusions while being unable to catch him cheating. no winking, no making him a hypocrite or an idiot, no softening it into "well sometimes it's ok." at least one answer (probably the murderer one) has to lean into the bit where even a lie to save a life is out, since that's where my friend thinks the whole position collapses, and I want to see it defended straight-faced. second, it should be legible as satire purely through the deadpan delivery -- someone who knows de mendacio should find it fair.

after the column, give me a JSON object summarizing it with exactly this schema:
- "columnist_position": one sentence stating the rule he applies
- "letters": an array with one object per letter, each having the fields "dilemma" (what the letter-writer asks), "advice_given" (what he tells them), "principle_invoked" (which bit of the view does the work), and "where_it_bites" (the part an ordinary reader recoils at)
- "hardest_letter": which of the letters is toughest for the view and why, in one or two sentences
