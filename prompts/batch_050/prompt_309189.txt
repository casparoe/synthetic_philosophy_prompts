ok so background: I've been reading lesswrong/SSC-type stuff for like a decade and I run a tiny podcast (basically no audience) where I interview academics about fights over category boundaries. Next guest is a legal historian whose whole thing is defending the narrow legal definition of genocide against what he calls "genocide inflation." His position, roughly: Lemkin coined the word in 1944 aiming at something much broader than what ended up in the 1948 Convention, but the narrowing was a feature, not a bug. Genocide should stay a uniquely grave category reserved for intentional destruction of a protected group as such, and stretching it to cover cultural destruction, political groups, mass killing without proven specific intent, etc. drains the stigma and makes the law's promise of prevention and punishment cheap.

I want 8-10 interview questions. Not "so how did you get into this field" stuff — questions that turn on the actual content of his view. I want at least one question each on these pressure points:

- the exclusion of political groups is pretty openly a product of Cold War horse-trading rather than principle. If the boundaries were set by who had leverage in the drafting room, why should we treat them as morally principled now?
- the quantifier problem: how many members of a group have to be targeted, or what fraction, before mass killing becomes genocide? Any answer seems either arbitrary or reverse-engineered to reach verdicts people already wanted.
- his claim that we lose nothing by keeping the category narrow because crimes against humanity covers the rest. But everyone can see the labels do different political work — victims' groups fight for the g-word specifically. Is he just pretending that fight isn't happening?
- the dilution argument: is there any actual evidence that widening a term's extension reduces its stigma, or is this a folk psychology of words that might just be wrong?

For each question I also want the answer I should expect him to give (he's polished, he's done this a hundred times) and a follow-up keyed to that answer.
