I've been chewing on the puzzle behind his Letter Spirit project, the one about fonts and the letter a. Quick version of the puzzle as I understand it, in case it's fuzzy: there's no shape common to all a's. A Times serif a, a Futura a, my mom's handwriting, a blackletter gothic a — they share essentially nothing geometric, yet they're all a, and we can tell, even for a's we've never seen in a style we've never seen. And the deeper point is that an a's identity isn't just about the a itself. A font is a consistent style applied across the whole alphabet, so whether a shape counts as an a depends partly on its role in a system — it has to be the a that coheres with that hand's b, q, r, g. The style is like a spirit spanning the letters, and each letter is an instance of a category (a-ness) and a style (this font) at once.
