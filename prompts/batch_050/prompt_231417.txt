So I've gone down this rabbit hole lately about the historical Jesus, and I should say upfront I'm not religious and have zero theology background, I just watched a documentary and then started reading whatever blogs and library books I could find. The thing that really hooked me is this "criterion of dissimilarity" that historians apparently use: if a saying attributed to Jesus doesn't fit the Judaism of his time AND doesn't serve the later church's interests, then it must genuinely be from him, because nobody along the chain of transmission would've had a motive to invent it. Which honestly sounds pretty clever, like a lie detector for ancient texts.

Here's where I get stuck, though. If this criterion works the way it's presented, then it seems like scholars could just run every saying and episode in the gospels through it, keep whatever passes, and end up with a definitive list of the real Jesus's actual words and deeds. From there, figuring out who he was should be more or less mechanical, like assembling a person from verified parts. But instead, scholars who all claim to use these criteria somehow end up with wildly different Jesuses -- apocalyptic prophet, wandering cynic-style sage, social reformer, messiah who knew exactly what he was doing. So my question is: given that the dissimilarity criterion proves which material is authentic, why hasn't it settled once and for all which portrait of Jesus is correct? Is the problem just that individual scholars are biased, or that nobody has applied the method rigorously enough yet, or what?
