Ok, I just got home from a stupidly long argument with my friend Lena about Judy Benjamin / updating on conditionals, and I want to know if my position is secretly a mess. We were using the usual setup: you’re dropped somewhere in a region split into Red and Blue, Red is split into HQ and not-HQ, your prior is flat over the four little cells, then you get only a conditional constraint like if Red then probably HQ, say credence HQ given Red should become 3/4, without being told Red and without being told any ordinary categorical proposition.

Here’s roughly where I landed, please audit it rather than just explain the literature at me:

1. Rational updating is accuracy-first: if a rule is the unique thing that minimizes expected inaccuracy under a strictly proper scoring rule given the evidence, that’s a strong reason to call it rational. So ordinary conditionalization is right when you become certain of E, and Jeffrey is basically conditionalization generalized to uncertain evidence.

2. In the Judy case, the marginal P(Red) should stay at 1/2 after hearing the conditional constraint. Any update that moves Red just because the conditional mentions Red is confusing if C then probably A with the material conditional, or double-counting the antecedent as evidence.

3. The right general rule for pure conditional information is minimum cross-entropy subject to the new conditional constraint. Not because entropy is metaphysically deep, but because it changes only what the constraint forces and leaves everything else maximally alone.

4. I’m broadly Adams/Stalnaker about indicative conditionals: no categorical truth conditions for plain English ifs, assertability tracks conditional probability, and anyway the radio message in Judy is not a proposition you can just condition on. But conditional constraints can still be real evidence; denying truth-values to ifs doesn’t make the message epistemically inert.

5. The evidential import of a message is exhausted by its explicit content once you’ve corrected for reliability. So if Lena adds a selection effect to the example — imagine the operator is more likely to send that conditional message when she can rule out Red, and stays silent more often when Red is true — I still want to say: hearing the message gives you the same conditional constraint, maybe with a reliability discount, but it should not by itself change P(Red), because nothing in the content says Red or not-Red.

6. Dynamic consistency matters: if the same total information arrives in two orders, the final credences should agree; and forgetting which order it arrived in shouldn’t create a new update. I think this is basically guaranteed by 1 and 3, but Lena kept saying I was smuggling in a memory of priors.

Lena’s claim was that 2 and 5 cannot both survive once 1 and 3 are on the table, because minimum cross-entropy plus scoring-rule accuracy will notice asymmetric likelihoods from the selection effect and move Red even when the semantic content is purely conditional. I said that only shows she’s changed the evidence from a conditional constraint to the categorical proposition that a message was sent under a biased protocol, so of course Bayes moves then; that’s not a counterexample to Judy as I mean it.
