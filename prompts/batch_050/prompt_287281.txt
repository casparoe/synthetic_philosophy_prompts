I'm a TA for an undergrad philosophy of biology course and the professor wants me to prep a handout on the species problem before we read any actual species concepts. Her idea is that students keep picking a favorite concept (usually the biological species concept, because it's the one they met in high school) and then just... not noticing everything it fails on. So instead of starting with the contenders, I want to start with the job description.

Write me the desiderata: what would a theory (or concept, or account, whatever) of species have to get right to count as adequate? I want the full spec — the kinds of organisms and cases it has to deliver a verdict on (I'm thinking asexual lineages, ring species, hybridizing populations, microbes swapping genes sideways, paleontologists working from morphology alone, but push back on or add to that list as needed), the distinctions it can't collapse (I've seen people insist that confusing "species" as a category with particular species taxa ruins half the literature, but I only half-understand the point), and the empirical facts from biology it simply can't contradict (e.g. that lineages evolve gradually, that reproductive isolation comes in degrees). For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which ones are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are really just preferences of one research tradition (I suspect "must be operational for field biologists" is doing more ideological work than it admits).

Then the two things I care about most: first, which single desideratum knocks out the most standard contenders at once — my money is on the asexuality requirement, but tell me if I'm wrong; second, whether some pair of desiderata on the list is jointly unsatisfiable, e.g. whether demanding cohesion-through-gene-flow and demanding that species be monophyletic historical lineages can both be satisfied in cases of ongoing hybridization, or whether you always have to give one up. If the list itself turns out to be inconsistent, that's the headline of the handout, so don't bury it. Finish with a quick pass of the biological, phylogenetic, and ecological species concepts against the list — just a sentence or two each, the scoring is not the point. A table for the desiderata would actually work great for the handout format.
