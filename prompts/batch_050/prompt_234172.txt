I have no academic background — I repair watches for a living — but I have been turning over a puzzle for years and cannot find out whether it is a recognized thing. In my twenties I drank the same inexpensive coffee every morning and loved it. I buy the same brand today, and it tastes thin and faintly sour. The obvious explanation is that my palate changed. But when I try to say what that means, I get stuck. Is the raw taste I get now a different raw taste from the one I got then, or is the raw taste the same while everything around it — my expectations, my comparisons, whatever judges the taste — has shifted? I kept detailed notes as a young man, and they are useless, because they describe the coffee in terms of other experiences (bitter like grapefruit pith, warm like toast) that are just as open to the same question.

A second instance: a song that once gave me chills now sounds ordinary, and I cannot tell whether the chills left the sound or left me.

Here is what really puzzles me. There seems to be no possible test, even in principle. Even if I could somehow replay my twenty-year-old experience, it would be my present self listening to the replay. Does this phenomenon have an established name? How does it differ from an ordinary change of preference, and from ordinary forgetting? And if the impossibility of settling the question is itself the interesting feature, is that a recognized position, or does it just show my two options were badly drawn? If nothing established fits, please coin a term, define it, and mark its edges — I would like to know whether it covers color and pain too, and a bare name without the carving will not satisfy me.
