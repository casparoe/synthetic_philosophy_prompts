Now that I've retired from the lab (thirty-odd years in protein chemistry, for my sins) I've somehow ended up on the ethics and governance committee of our regional NHS trust -- I think they wanted a 'scientific layperson' and I was daft enough to say yes. Anyway, we've been landed with a genuinely thorny one. The trust is considering buying an AI system that would help allocate scarce resources -- things like scheduling elective surgery backlogs, assigning district nurses, that sort of thing -- and the vendor keeps saying the system can be 'aligned to our values', as if that's a switch you flip. At the last meeting it became painfully clear we don't agree on what that even means. One surgeon wants it tuned to maximise health outcomes measured in QALYs full stop; a community rep says it should reflect what local people actually want, which means running surveys or citizen panels, though nobody can say whose survey or which panel; our chair, who's read a bit of political philosophy, keeps insisting the trust should stay 'neutral between conceptions of the good' and just follow whatever the legal framework says -- but the legal framework leaves nearly everything up for grabs, and frankly I suspect 'neutral' just means smuggling in whoever got to write the defaults.

Could you draft the written opinion I'm going to circulate before next month's meeting, recommending a concrete decision the committee could actually vote on? I want it to engage seriously with a problem that's been nagging at me: when the people affected disagree about values -- some patients would trade life expectancy for quality of life, others the reverse, and the staff disagree with both -- is there any coherent sense in which the system can serve 'our' values at all, or is that a category error? The opinion should weigh up the main options on the table (optimise for a measurable outcome chosen by clinicians, aggregate surveyed preferences, or some deliberative arrangement where affected groups set the trade-offs), and come down on one of them, or on a workable hybrid, with a clear recommendation. And please give the strongest argument for the option you're rejecting a proper run-out rather than a strawman -- our chair will tear the thing apart otherwise, and honestly I'd deserve it.
