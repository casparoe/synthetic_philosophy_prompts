ok so I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, but I've gotten sucked into the Frankfurt cases literature and I'm keeping a little list of counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP) -- the idea that you're only morally responsible for what you did if you could have done otherwise. The ones I've got so far:

1. The original setup where Black has a device implanted in Jones's brain that will force Jones to choose to kill Smith if Jones shows any sign of deciding not to, but Jones decides to kill Smith on his own and the device never fires. No alternative, still responsible, or so the intuition goes.

2. Locke's old case (way before Frankfurt) of the guy who stays in a room because he genuinely wants to, not knowing the door is locked -- he'd have stayed anyway, so the locked door is irrelevant to his choice but removes the alternative.

3. The blocked-alternative version: someone tries to shoot the target and hits it, but (unknown to them) a malfunction or a barrier would have stopped them from missing on purpose -- the mechanism removes the "could have done otherwise" without ever operating, like a dead-man's switch that never trips.

4. The prior-sign cases, where the intervener doesn't wait for the choice itself but watches for some early indicator (a tell, a preliminary neural pattern) that reliably predicts the choice, and only intervenes if that sign shows the person going the "wrong" way.

What I notice is that every single one of these has basically the same engineering: there's a failsafe that never activates, and the whole argument rides on the claim that an inactive failsafe doesn't touch responsibility even though it kills the alternative. So here's my question. Is there a counterexample to PAP that does NOT work this way -- no counterfactual intervener, no locked door, no backup mechanism waiting in the wings at all? Like, a case where the person couldn't have done otherwise for some structurally different reason, but still seems responsible. It can be a known one I just haven't run across, or you can try to construct one. And then tell me honestly whether you think it actually survives the usual dodge (i.e. people saying "well then they weren't really responsible" or "actually there IS a hidden alternative, like refraining from trying"), because that move seems available for pretty much every case in this genre.
