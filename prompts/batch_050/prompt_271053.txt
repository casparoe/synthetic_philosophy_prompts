I chair the appeals panel of a professional licensing board for financial advisors. The case before us: a member, on his own initiative and after days of quiet deliberation, diverted client funds into a personal account; the transfer was flagged and reversed within hours, and no client lost money. One fact is not in dispute and complicates everything. His line manager, who had suspected him for months, had covertly arranged with IT that if he hesitated or backed out at the final authorization step, the transfer would be executed under his credentials anyway. He never hesitated; the override was never triggered. On appeal, he argues that because he could not actually have done otherwise, he cannot be as culpable as someone who had a genuine alternative, and he asks that the sanction be reduced from license revocation to a two-year suspension. Please draft the written opinion I will circulate to the panel before we vote. It should recommend a concrete outcome, set out the reasoning in terms a mixed panel of practitioners and lay members can follow, take a position on whether the absence of alternatives bears on culpability itself or only on the fairness of the process that judged him, and give the strongest version of the appellant's argument a fair hearing before answering it.
