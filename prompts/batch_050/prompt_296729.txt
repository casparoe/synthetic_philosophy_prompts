I am writing a paper for my philosophy class on whether mathematical beauty is genuine evidence of truth in science. So far, my draft discusses five examples: Dirac trusting his equation partly for its elegance and thereby predicting antimatter before it was observed; Einstein standing by general relativity on aesthetic grounds while its empirical support was still thin; Weyl's gauge theory, initially rejected on physical grounds and later revived in a form that its beauty had arguably kept alive; and, on the other side, Kepler's model of the planetary orbits built from nested Platonic solids, and the failure of supersymmetric particles to appear at the Large Hadron Collider despite many physicists finding the theory beautiful.

I need one more example, but it must differ from these in structure, not merely in subject matter. Each item on my list is a historical episode in which a beautiful theory either succeeded or failed, so a sixth episode of that same pattern would not count. I am looking for a case with a different logical bearing on the question — for instance, one in which two rival theories were comparably elegant yet only one survived, or one in which the mathematically ugly theory turned out to be right. Please give me one such example, explain how its structure differs from the items above, and say briefly whether you think it genuinely supports or undermines the claim that elegance is evidence — or whether it mostly shows that elegance is too vague a standard to settle anything.
