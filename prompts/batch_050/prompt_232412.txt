I'm in a phil of biology seminar this term and we just finished three weeks on the function debate — etiological/selected-effects accounts vs causal-role (Cummins-style) accounts. I get both views fine on paper. What I don't get is why the debate is still going after like fifty years when both sides seem to agree on all the facts.

Everyone agrees the heart pumps blood and that's why we have hearts. Everyone agrees a brand-new mutation that happens to help an organism but hasn't been selected for yet is a hard case for the etiological view. Everyone agrees the heart's thumping noise is a causal contribution to the system but not a function. Then the etiological people say: without evolutionary history you can't make sense of malfunction, and biology definately needs malfunction talk — a diseased heart is failing to do something, it's not just doing something else. And the causal-role people say: actual biologists don't dig through evolutionary history when they ascribe functions, they look at what a part contributes to a capacity of the whole system, and selection-history stuff is backward-looking metaphysics bolted onto real science. And then they just keep saying these things at each other, forever.

My hypothesis, which might be totally wrong, is that they're not disagreeing about the correct analysis of one concept called function at all. They're disagreeing about which job the word is supposed to do — one side wants it to explain why a trait exists and to underwrite normative talk (working properly vs broken), the other wants it to describe how a system works right now, and those are just different jobs one word got stuck doing. Like two people arguing about what a hammer is for when one of them is using it to pull nails.

So: is that diagnosis right, or is there a genuine substantive disagreement buried underneath — like a real question about what biologists actually do when they attribute functions, or about whether normativity is essential to the concept? Is this a verbal dispute, a dispute about standards of explanation, or two sides answering different questions under one name? Could it be settled empirically (e.g. by studying how function talk is actually used in journals) or is it not that kind of dispute? And what would progress even look like — splitting the concept in two and calling it a truce, or does something get lost that way? I'm thinking of how the etiological view gets used in philosophy of mind to ground content, which seems to need exactly the normativity the causal-role side wants to drop.

Also if my hammer analogy is bad, tell me why — my TA was very noncommital about it and it's been bugging me.
