I'm a psychiatry resident who keeps ending up in arguments with a philosophy PhD friend about what delusions even are, and we realized we're stuck because neither of us can say what a theory of delusion would have to accomplish before we'd count it as correct. The literature I know (two-factor accounts, the Maher-style "it's just normal reasoning about weird experience" line, the doxasticists vs the people who say delusions aren't really beliefs) all seem to be optimizing for different things and talking past each other. So: what are the desiderata? Give me the spec sheet for an adequate theory of delusion -- the clinical phenomena it has to explain (why delusions are usually monothematic and circumscribed rather than global, why Capgras patients don't act on the "impostor" the way you'd expect, why some right-hemisphere lesions produce delusions while seemingly similar ones produce denial or neglect instead, why delusions can be so circumscribed that the patient is otherwise rational), the conceptual distinctions it has to respect or explicitly dissolve (belief vs. acceptance vs. some in-between attitude, explanation of content vs. explanation of maintenance), and the empirical findings it can't contradict.

For each requirement, give me the case or observation motivating it -- I'd rather have "a theory that can't explain X fails, because X is the datum that generated the whole debate" than a bare bullet list. Then mark which requirements you think are genuinely non-negotiable for any theory and which are really just the priors of one camp (e.g., is "delusions must come out as beliefs" a real constraint or a philosophical prejudice? is "must preserve the patient's rationality" a desideratum or a romantic wish?). And the part I care most about: are any of the desiderata in tension with each other -- e.g., does explaining the bizarre content in a naturalistic way tend to make the failure-to-update on evidence look worse, and vice versa? If some subset of my requirements list is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly, because that would actually explain why my friend and I keep going in circles. A brief note on which single requirement knocks out the most existing contenders would be great, but skip the detailed scorecard of every theory.
