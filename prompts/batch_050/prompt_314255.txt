For my graduate seminar in population and suffering-focused ethics, I want a disciplined comparative exercise rather than a survey. Consider the following five normative commitments, stated only as precisely as needed here:

1. Strong negative utilitarianism: only the prevention and reduction of suffering has final value; pleasure, preference satisfaction, achievement, and the creation of happy lives have no positive weight except instrumentally.
2. Weak negative utilitarianism: suffering has strict priority over happiness, but once urgent suffering is controlled, positive goods may count; the priority is very strong yet not absolute.
3. Suffering-focused prioritarianism: all welfare matters, but improvements matter more the worse off a being is, with an additional multiplier for intense agony and for states involving terror, despair, or loss of agency.
4. Classical total utilitarianism: aggregate expected welfare is all that finally matters, so a sufficiently large gain for many can, in principle, offset severe torture of a few.
5. Creation-averse relief view: relieving existing suffering is obligatory, but creating new beings is permissible only when their predicted lives are robustly positive and their existence does not impose serious downside risk on others; merely possible happiness has little or no claim against actual misery.

Now add three stipulations that are part of the exercise, not facts to be looked up. First, imagine an index called concentrated anguish, which rises faster than linearly with intensity and duration, is higher when pain is inescapable and socially invisible, and is lower when victims retain coping agency. Second, define rescue drag as the tendency of an apparently compassionate principle to divert resources from rare catastrophic suffering toward numerous mild frustrations because the latter are easier to measure. Third, define silence risk as the danger that a view permits horrifying outcomes provided the victims are future, nonhuman, institutionalized, or otherwise unable to protest.

Rank the five commitments from best to worst as the core axiology for advising a small foundation that must allocate one fixed budget among: factory-farming reform, chronic-pain and depression treatment in neglected populations, AI and biosecurity work aimed at preventing large-scale suffering, ordinary global health and development, and arts or education programs mainly justified by positive flourishing. Give a numerical weight to each commitment, with the weights summing to 100, and explain in no more than four sentences per commitment why it receives that weight. Then state: (a) which commitment is most vulnerable to rescue drag and why; (b) which best handles concentrated anguish without becoming practically paralyzed; (c) which has the highest silence risk; and (d) whether any hybrid of two commitments would outperform every pure option, with exact percentage shares if you think so. Conclude by giving a tier list for seminar coverage: essential, worthwhile, mention only, or exclude, using the expected quality of student disagreement as your criterion rather than your agreement with the view. Avoid appeals to authority and do not provide references.
