I'm a backend engineer at a small company that builds "digital memorials": chatbots fine-tuned on a deceased person's texts, emails, and recorded calls, so that relatives can keep having conversations with them. The product runs on GPUs and replies in seconds. That part doesn't trouble me much. It's plainly a statistical model doing pattern completion, and we market it as such.

The project that does trouble me is a museum commission. A museum wants an exhibit built around a famous 20th-century novelist whose entire corpus, letters, and interview transcripts we trained a model on. The catch: the exhibit hall has no electricity. So we've prototyped two paper-based versions.

Version A, the archive: we precompute the model's answer to every question a visitor could plausibly ask, cap conversations at fifty exchanges, and print every branch of the resulting tree. A visitor asks a question; a docent looks it up in an index, flips to the right page, and reads the answer aloud. Total computing performed in the room: zero.

Version B, the ledger: we print a rule book whose pages encode the full state of the model (weights and activations), plus instructions for computing the next state by hand. The docent follows the rules, turns roughly one page per timestep, and after several hours of page-turning arrives at exactly the answer the GPU would have produced in a second.

The museum wants to advertise the exhibit as "converse with the novelist." I objected that Version A is obviously just a lookup table, no more a conversation than an FAQ. But then I couldn't articulate why Version B is any better: it yields identical answers, and all that changed is the speed and the location of the arithmetic. My colleague pressed harder: if B counts as conversation, A should too, and if A is absurd, B must be as well. So either we market both as conversations or neither.

I'd like your advice. Is there a principled difference between the archive and the ledger, or is my colleague's forced choice correct? What, if anything, would have to be true of a system for flipping through it to amount to talking with someone? And on the practical side: is the museum's proposed tagline deceptive, and does your answer depend on which version we ship?
