I run the small graphics team at a natural history museum -- we do the wall text, the maps, the little charts comparing bone sizes, that kind of thing. Our director has gotten really into the idea that every visual should be graspable by a tired parent holding a toddler, and she keeps pointing me at Otto Neurath and the ISOTYPE picture-language stuff as the model to imitate. I've read a bit about it and I get the general vibe (standardized silhouettes, show amounts by repeating little figures instead of stretching one figure bigger, no decorative perspective, consistency across the whole exhibition), but "get the vibe" isn't something I can hand to four designers and a revolving cast of interns and say "follow this."

So what I actually want: turn Neurath's approach into a review procedure we could run on any proposed graphic before it goes to print. The checks to run, in order, and for each one what counts as passing vs. failing -- e.g. how do we decide when a quantity is small enough that repeating unit-symbols works versus when we have to give up and use a bar (a chart showing 4 million annual visitors is not getting 4 million beetle icons, so where's the line?). Be explicit about which Neurath-style rules are hard constraints and which are trade-offs where a normal data-viz consideration (precision, showing uncertainty, comparing ratios) legitimately wins. And flag the spots where judgment can't be proceduralized -- I'd rather know where those are than get a fake-complete checklist.

Then run the procedure on three real-ish cases from my queue: (1) a timeline graphic of visitor numbers since 1950, (2) a wall display comparing the body masses of twelve dinosaurs to a human, and (3) -- the one I dread -- a gorgeous full-color 3D-rendered infographic of ocean depth zones that one of my designers spent a month on and that I suspect the procedure is going to reject, so tell me straight if it does. Also, be honest about your epistemic footing: say how confident you are in the overall picture of Neurath's views, and single out the specific claim or reconstruction in your answer you're least sure of -- I don't want to present something to my director as "Neurath's rule" if it's really just your extrapolation.
