I'm finalizing the fairness policy for a risk-scoring tool we plan to deploy in consumer lending, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency check on the package of commitments I have written into it. Here is where I have landed:

(1) The scores must be calibrated within demographic groups: among applicants assigned a given predicted default probability, the realized default rate should be the same regardless of group membership, so that a score of, say, 0.2 means the same thing for everyone.

(2) The false positive rate must be equalized across groups: applicants who would not in fact have defaulted must be classified as high-risk at the same rate in every group.

(3) Observed default rates genuinely differ across groups, but this disparity is substantially a legacy of unjust background conditions rather than a difference in underlying creditworthiness; a fair system must not treat the observed disparity as a neutral fact about the world.

(4) The tradeoffs people mention between these criteria are engineering problems rather than mathematical ones: with enough data, better features, and careful preprocessing, all the standard fairness criteria can be satisfied at once.

(5) I want equalized odds rather than merely equalized opportunity: false positives and false negatives both impose real harms, so both error types should be balanced across groups, not just the treatment of those who would have repaid.

(6) The system should additionally be individually fair: any two applicants who are similar in the relevant respects should receive similar scores.

My question is whether this package hangs together. Please identify which commitments genuinely conflict, which apparent conflicts dissolve on inspection, and, for each genuine tension, state exactly which commitment has to be revised or dropped and what that revision costs. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and keep the entire response to at most 500 words.
