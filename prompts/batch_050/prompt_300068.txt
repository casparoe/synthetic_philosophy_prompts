I work on the product team at a mid-size software company, and last week we got into a suprisingly heated argument about an update. Option A adds about two seconds of load time for all ~50 million users. Option B keeps things fast but badly breaks a workflow for a few thousand legacy customers, like genuinely ruining their week. Half the team said obviously A, and half said you can't trade off a few people getting screwed against strangers losing two seconds each. So I went googling and ended up on this old 2007 blog post by Eliezer Yudkowsky, "Torture vs. Dust Specks". The part that stuck with me:

"What's the least bad, bad thing that can happen? Well, suppose a dust speck floated into your eye and irritated it just a little, for a fraction of a second, barely enough to make you notice before you blink and wipe away the dust speck."

He then sets up an enormous number of people getting specks versus one person tortured for fifty years, and ends with "I think the answer is obvious. How about you?"

I sent it to a friend who reads a lot of philosophy and asked him to explain what the post is actually arguing. He said: 'It's a thought experiment showing what aggregative ethics commits you to. A dust speck in the eye is a harm so tiny it's basically zero, below the threshold of anything that morally matters -- but multiply it by a big enough number of people and it sums to something worse than torturing one person for fifty years. Yudkowsky says the answer is obvious, i.e. you're supposed to pick the torture, and his point is that any view that adds up harms across people is stuck endorsing torture once the numbers get big enough. The insane number is there so you can't dodge that.'

Is that fair? Two things bug me. First, the passage I quoted seems to go out of its way to make the speck a real, noticed harm, not a zero one, and I suspect that matters. Second, I've now read the post twice and I can't actually find where he says torture is the right answer -- "the answer is obvious" doesn't say which answer. Which parts of his summary are accurate, which would the author push back on, and does his takeaway (that adding up tiny harms forces you to endorse torture) still follow once you fix the unfair parts? And practically: does whatever the post actually shows settle anything about our A vs B fight, or are we conflating it with a different question?
