I'm trying to pin down what I actually think about compulsory military service by going on gut verdicts, and I want the principle behind them if there is one. Cases:

1. Finland-style: small country next to a big hostile neighbor, every man does 6-12 months, women can volunteer, genuine conscientious objectors get civilian service. Fine, maybe even admirable.
2. Same setup, but there's also an exemption tax -- pay a few percent extra income tax every year and skip service (Switzerland roughly does this). Honestly, also fine.
3. A draft in the middle of an actual invasion, no buy-outs, medical exemptions only. Fine, maybe even obligatory.
4. A lottery draft where you can pay a substitute to go in your place, American Civil War style. Disgusting.
5. A rich, safe country with no conceivable enemy keeping conscription anyway because it mixes the classes and builds national character. Not fine.
6. A professional volunteer army that ends up recruiting mostly poor kids from towns with no jobs, lured by signing bonuses. Feels bad but not wrong the way 4 is.
7. Hiring foreigners to do your fighting (Foreign Legion, Gurkhas). Somehow worse than 6 and I can't say why.

The pair I keep tripping over is 2 and 4 -- both let the well-off buy their way out, and I want one fine and the other disgusting. And 1 vs 5 seems to hang entirely on whether the threat is real, but "it's okay if you actually need it" feels like it proves too much.

"Verdicts I have to drop" -- be honest about which of mine don't survive (~100 words). "Cases I didn't ask about" -- what the principle says about e.g. drafting nurses in a pandemic, drafting people to defend an ally abroad, and an all-volunteer force that's better paid and genuinely competitive (~150 words). "My weakest verdict" -- the single verdict you think I should give up first (~50 words).
