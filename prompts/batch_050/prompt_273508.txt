A few years ago a friend talked me into taking evidential decision theory seriously, and I have since found myself persuaded by a stronger and stranger thesis that seems to follow from it: that in a sufficiently large universe, evidential cooperation with distant counterparts is rational, and that this does real moral work. The thesis, as I hold it, has three parts. First, the world is very plausibly large enough — via cosmological inflation, an infinite universe, or merely an enormous number of independent civilizations — that there exist many agents facing decision problems structurally similar to mine, including near-copies of me at various degrees of similarity. Second, where another agent's deliberation shares enough of the relevant machinery with mine, my choice is genuine evidence about theirs, so cooperating in one-shot prisoner's-dilemma-like interactions with them can be rational for me even granting that I causally affect nothing beyond my own light cone. Third, this scales: since the correlated agents vastly outnumber the agents I can causally reach, evidential considerations can in principle swamp direct ones, turning a purely self-concerned evidential agent into something that behaves, at the limit, like an impartial altruist.

What would count against this? I want a map of the position's exposure rather than a list of rehearsed objections, so please do the following. Identify the observations, formal results, and arguments that would tell against it: for instance, cosmological findings that would undermine the size premise, or the observation that we seem to be alone — how much should that update me?; results about how evidential correlation decays with dissimilarity, and whether the similarity required for substantial correlation is so fine-grained that the relevant measure of counterparts is negligible; the possibility that the whole framework inherits the measure problem and is not even well-defined; and the standing objections to evidential decision theory itself, including the charge that the evidential agent merely manages the news rather than achieving anything. Also consider defeaters of a different kind: consequences the view is committed to that I should find unacceptable on reflection — that it extends cooperation to malicious counterparts, say, or that it values agents in proportion to their similarity to me, which looks like a prejudice wearing formal dress — and the possibility that, once suitably qualified, the position collapses into a version of causal decision theory with updatelessness and loses whatever made it distinctive. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each, say whether a committed defender would concede it counts against them or would dismiss it, and on what grounds.

Two further requests. First, tell me honestly whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability: given that its defenders can always retreat to the claim that there is some correlation, with some counterparts, somewhere in the cosmos, is there any observation or result left that would touch it, and if not, how serious a problem is that for a view that rests partly on an empirical hypothesis? I should say that I am most moved by empirical considerations and formal results and least by bare intuition pumps. Second, indicate how confident you are in the main claims of your assessment, and single out the one point in it you are least sure of.
