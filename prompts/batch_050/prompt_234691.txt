I am a PhD student in early modern philosophy preparing a seminar handout on Locke’s memory criterion and Reid’s brave-officer objection. Write a 22-line poem in terza rima, in the voice of Reid’s aged general, that makes the objection intelligible through the form rather than merely naming it.

The poem should move through three stages: the boy punished for a childhood misdeed, the young officer who remembers that punishment while performing a courageous act in battle, and the old general who remembers the officer’s act but has lost all memory of the punishment. Treat consciousness or retrospective memory as Locke’s proposed criterion of personal identity: the present person is identical with a past person only when the former can remember the latter’s experience from within.

Use the interlocking rhyme scheme to enact the problem. Each tercet should pass its middle rhyme into the next, so that boy and officer, and then officer and general, are locally linked; but the opening rhyme must disappear well before the final lines, so that the first and last stages are not directly joined. A reader should thus see formally why a relation can connect every adjacent pair without connecting the endpoints.

Include the recurring image of a regimental ledger recording the same body, name, promotions, and acts across all three stages. The poem should contrast this continuous third-person record with the broken chain of first-person memory, without confusing the ledger itself for Lockean consciousness. The final detached line should identify the precise difficulty for the memory criterion: personal identity is transitive, while overlapping chains of remembered experience need not be. Do not add notes, commentary, or an explanation of the poem.
