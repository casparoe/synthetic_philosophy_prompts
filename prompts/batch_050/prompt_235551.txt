I am a novelist planning a linked story cycle, and I need one story written that I can use as a touchstone for the rest. Write a short story that dramatizes the distinction between equipment that is transparent in use — the hammer you hammer with rather than look at — and equipment that suddenly becomes a mere object of inspection, with this constraint: the "equipment" in the story must be a long marriage that has run so smoothly for decades that it is entirely invisible to the husband. Structure the plot around three distinct breakdowns: first, the marriage malfunctions at its task (a small failure that makes it conspicuous); second, some constituent part of it goes missing, so that everything around the absence becomes glaringly obtrusive; third, some element of it refuses to go away and stands obstinately in the path of the life he is trying to carry on. The marriage should end neither repaired nor dissolved but permanently re-seen — an object he now inspects and can never again simply use. Keep the prose formal and literary, and do not let any character lecture about the underlying philosophy; the ideas should live in the scenes. Begin with the story itself, with no preface or commentary.
