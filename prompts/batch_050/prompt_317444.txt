I help run a small grantmaking fund that finances research on catastrophic risks from AI, and over the past year we've started getting more and more proposals framed as targeting s-risks — risks of astronomical suffering, futures where vast numbers of sentient beings (human, animal, possibly digital) suffer intensely, rather than futures where everyone just dies. We have a decent review process for extinction-risk proposals, but nothing for these, and each reviewer is now seeing maybe five to ten of them per quarter with about an hour to spend on each.

Act as an expert on existential risk and grant evaluation. Think carefully and brainstorm a bit before you write the final answer. I want a decision procedure my team can actually run on any s-risk proposal: the questions to ask, in order, what counts as an adequate answer to each, and where the procedure stops and hands things to a person because it's a values question rather than a factual one. It has to handle at least these cases: (1) telling whether a proposal genuinely reduces expected suffering vs. just sounding darkly important; (2) proposals where reducing suffering risk and reducing extinction risk pull apart — e.g. work that makes a survivable-but-bad future more likely, or work aimed only at making bad futures less bad; (3) how much evidential weight to give highly speculative scenarios involving digital minds given how uncertain the science is; (4) disagreements among our own reviewers about how suffering weighs against other goods. Then run the procedure on two hypothetical proposals you invent, including one where it gives an answer we probly won't like, and be honest about what the procedure can't settle and where it will misfire.
