So I got into Dennett through some youtube lectures and then read a bit of his stuff on what he calls a strange inversion of reasoning — Darwin showing you can get design without a designer, and Turing showing you can get computation without anyone actually understanding what's being computed. His slogan is "competence without comprehension," nature and machines can both be fantastically competent while comprehending nothing.

Here's my question though. If Turing basically showed that computers only ever shuffle symbols around by blind rules with zero comprehension of what the symbols mean, doesn't that settle the whole AI debate? Like, no matter how impressive chatbots get, there's no understanding in there, it's all competence and no comprehension, so its not real intelligence and never will be. Which would mean all the panic about AI right now is kind of embarassing — people getting scared of autocomplete. Dennett was writing about this decades ago and nobody in tech seems to have noticed. Am I missing something, or is this just a case of philosophers having solved a problem that engineers haven't caught up on yet?
