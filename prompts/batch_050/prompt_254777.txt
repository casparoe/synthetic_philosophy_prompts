a friend of mine and i have been arguing for weeks about dennett's bit with the two coffee tasters, chase and sanborn, and i want to celebrate the stalemate by giving him a poem about it for his birthday.

quick recap, and please get this right in the poem: both of them were professional coffee tasters for years, and both now say they no longer enjoy the coffee they used to love. chase says the taste itself has changed for him -- the coffee tastes different now, worse. sanborn says the opposite: the coffee tastes exactly the same as it always did, it's his appreciation that's changed, he's gotten jaded and cant take pleasure in the old taste anymore. dennett's whole point is that neither of them can actually know which situation hes in, because checking would mean comparing the current taste against the remembered old taste, and the memory might be the very thing that drifted. so the difference between "my qualia changed" and "my reactions to my qualia changed" turns out to be one that nobody, not even the person having the experience, can ever detect.

here's the poem i want: two voices, one chase, one sanborn, in conversation with each other, but with NO speaker labels, and the constraint that does the philosophical work -- every single line has to make equal sense coming from either taster. i shouldnt be able to go through with a pencil and figure out wich lines are chase's and wich are sanborn's, because that's the point: their two situations are indistinguishable from the inside. roughly 20-30 lines, no fixed form beyond that, but id love it if the two voices converge by the end on a single line they both say together, since dennett thinks there's no fact of the matter seperating them. it should be accurate enough that someone whos read the chapter nods along rather than winces, and a good enough poem that my friend (who has read it) wants to frame it.
