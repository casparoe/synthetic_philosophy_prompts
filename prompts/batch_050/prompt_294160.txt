my girlfriend's in med school and her favorite move is the body-as-machine line: health is every part working to spec, disease is a part failing or running out of tolerance, and medicine is basically very advanced mechanics. she wheels it out to settle arguments -- whether something counts as a disease is a purely factual, scientific question, no values anywhere, same as "your timing belt is shot" is purely factual. according to her, depression, hypertension, ADHD, chronic fatigue, whatever: you just check the spec sheet, and if a system isn't doing its job it's broken, end of story.

i want you to actually audit this analogy instead of just nodding along. give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the blueprint/spec sheet, the broken part, the factory tolerances, the mechanic, the intended use of the machine. then where it breaks. a few pressure points i especially want covered: (1) machines get their specs from a designer with intentions, so "working properly" is unambiguous given the manual -- but organisms have no manual, and evolution is a blind tinkerer that never intended anything, so where does the body's spec sheet come from, and is that source as objective as she thinks? (2) two cars off the same line are near-identical, two humans aren't -- so "to spec" for a body is a statistical range, and somebody has to choose the cutoffs (is hypertension 130 or 140? who decided, and on what grounds?). does that choice sneak values back in even if all the underlying measurements are hard facts?
