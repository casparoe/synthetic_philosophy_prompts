Last week I had a long and genuinely unresolved argument with a close friend about utilitarianism. She is a convinced aggregative consequentialist; I kept pressing the separateness of persons objection — the complaint that summing costs and benefits across distinct lives, and licensing burdens on one person for the sake of gains to others, fails to take seriously the distinction between persons the way prudential reasoning within a single life does not. We ended up stuck not on whether the objection is rhetorically powerful (we agree it is) but on what, exactly, it still amounts to after fifty years of replies. I have roughly a year of part-time research time and want to write one strong paper in this neighborhood, and I need help deciding where the live philosophical work actually is rather than re-litigating settled ground.

The candidate questions we circled: (1) whether the objection targets aggregation as such or only the total-welfare version of it — i.e., whether prioritarian and sufficientist views inherit the problem or genuinely answer it; (2) whether intra-personal aggregation across a life stands or falls with inter-personal aggregation, so that rejecting the latter threatens ordinary prudence (or, conversely, whether a reductionist view of personal identity blunts the objection by making lives less sealed-off than it assumes); (3) whether contractualist alternatives, with their individualist restriction on complaints, actually avoid the objection or merely reproduce its structure under another name; (4) how much of the objection's force survives translation into population ethics, where refusing to aggregate seems to generate its own paradoxes; and (5) whether the objection is really doing normative work or is instead a disguised dispute about the metaphysics of persons.

Give me a defended prioritization of these (or of a better list if mine misdraws the map): which question is upstream of the others in the sense that its resolution would settle or dissolve several of them; which is most tractable for a single paper by someone with training in normative ethics but not in population axiology; which are already overcrowded; and what considerations would move an item up or down your ranking. I want an agenda, not a survey of the debate.
