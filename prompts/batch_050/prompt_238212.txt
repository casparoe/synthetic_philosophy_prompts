I am a novelist who also serves, one semester a year, as a visiting lecturer in an undergraduate political philosophy seminar. I am grading short-answer exams and would like a second opinion on one response before I finalize the grade.

The exam question was:

State Mill's harm principle as it appears in On Liberty, and explain what follows from it for the freedom of expression. Does the principle, by itself, settle which harmful or offensive speech a society may restrict? Why or why not?

The student's answer, in full:

Mill's harm principle holds that preventing harm to other people is the only legitimate reason for coercing a competent adult against that person's will; conduct that concerns the agent alone lies beyond the rightful reach of either the state or public opinion. Mill is careful to note that the principle covers only adults in the maturity of their faculties, not children. True to his utilitarianism, he defends the principle not as a natural right but on grounds of utility, understood in terms of the enduring interests of human beings as progressive creatures.

For speech, the upshot is that expression may be restricted whenever it harms others. Mill's own example of the corn dealers shows this: the opinion that corn dealers starve the poor, he says, is protected when printed in the press but may be punished when shouted to an angry crowd gathered outside a corn dealer's house — in other words, Mill permits suppressing speech on the basis of its inflammatory content when circumstances make it dangerous. Mill also argues that suppressing an opinion wrongs everyone, since if the opinion is true we lose the truth, and if it is false we lose the clearer grasp of truth that comes from its collision with error. Since mere offense is not harm, offensive speech is protected, and since harmful speech may be banned, the harm principle by itself draws the line between protected and punishable expression.

Please assign this answer a letter grade (A through F) and supply the specific comments that justify it. Identify, point by point, where the answer is accurate, where it misreads or overstates Mill's position, and where its reasoning fails to establish its conclusion. Please respond in American English.
