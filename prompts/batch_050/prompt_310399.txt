So here's something I've been chewing on. Everyone knows the Wilt Chamberlain bit from Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia: start from whatever patterned distribution you like (D1), then a whole bunch of people voluntarily each pay a small amount to watch Wilt play, he ends up rich (D2), and Nozick asks on what grounds the pattern-lover can object, since every step was consented to. The standard reading is that the consenting transfers are what do the argumentative work.

But I'm not actually sure that's right, and I want a thought experiment to test it. Build me a case that isolates this variable: a patterned distribution gets upset by holdings-changes that are unambiguously legitimate by entitlement theory's own lights (just initial acquisition, valid transfer, no force, no fraud, no theft) but where the warm glowy "everyone knowingly agreed to each little transaction" element is gone. So maybe the transferors don't grasp the cumulative effect, or they're paying for reasons totally orthogonal to anyone's holdings, or the person being enriched doesn't even know it's happening. Your choice, but engineer it deliberately.

Then, for the case you built, do the full check:

1. What does Nozick's entitlement theory have to say about the resulting distribution, and does the answer even change relative to the original Wilt Chamberlain setup?
2. Does the patterned theorist (say, a strict egalitarian) have a *better* reply available here than in the original case, or a worse one? Spell out the actual reply they'd give, not just "they'd object."
3. The verdict: does stripping out the consent-flavored element weaken Nozick's argument, or does it reveal that consent was never really the load-bearing part? Defend your answer.

Constraints: keep it realistic, no sci-fi or weird stipulations, and keep the scale small — a village or a workplace, not a million basketball fans, since part of the point is seeing whether the million-people-each-paying-a-quarter framing was smuggling anything in. And dont cheat by just asserting which theory wins; the verdicts have to fall out of the theories themselves.
