I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and my dissertation defends a position I call the single-subject, split-access view of commissurotomy: split-brain patients remain one subject of experience throughout, and the apparent duality in the classic experiments reflects a division of access, agency, and report within one consciousness rather than two coexisting streams. My version adds two commitments I take to be load-bearing. First, what keeps the subject unified is subcortical and bodily integration -- shared attentional orienting, shared affect, a single sensorimotor frame anchored in one embodied agent -- so the corpus callosum is a channel of access, not the tie that binds the subject. Second, the view predicts that no experiment could ever reveal one patient simultaneously acting on two experiences whose contents each side would endorse as its own; apparent cases of this are sequential, not simultaneous.

I want to know what would count against this position, and I want you to be committed rather than even-handed: tell me which potential defeater you actually judge strongest, and rank the rest from fatal to merely awkward. Please cover at least: (a) empirical findings, actual or clearly describable, from the commissurotomy literature or from adjacent cases such as callosal agenesis, hemispherectomy, and alien hand, that my view cannot accommodate without ad hoc repair; (b) theoretical results about what the unity of consciousness consists in that would undercut my claim that access is the wrong level at which to locate disunity; (c) consequences of my view that a fair-minded defender would have to concede are costs -- for instance, what it must say about the moral status of a right hemisphere that cannot speak. Finally, assess honestly whether my view is insulated by the privacy of consciousness -- we cannot simply ask both sides -- and whether that insulation is a methodological necessity or a vice. If you conclude the position is effectively unfalsifiable, say so plainly and say whether that should make me abandon it.
