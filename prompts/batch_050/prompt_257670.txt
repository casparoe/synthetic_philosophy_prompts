I'm writing an essay on The Wire and I want to read it through Robin Hanson's futarchy idea -- the proposal where we vote on values but let prediction markets decide policies, with bets on some agreed welfare measure settling which policy gets adopted. Everyone reads The Wire as a show about institutions failing individuals, but I think it's specifically a show about metrics, and that makes it a direct stress test of futarchy. The whole series is full of number games: the department juking stats to keep felony rates down, the school system teaching to the test, Carcetti's compstat scene, the dockworkers with their tonnage reports that say nothing about whether anyone's actually working. And then Hamsterdam: Colvin's drug zones genuinely work by any humane standard -- fewer bodies, fewer arrests, the public health people love it -- and it's illegible to every official measurement, so it has to be destroyed.

Here's what I want, in this order with roughly these lengths:

1. The basic fit (~150 words): set up futarchy accurately enough that a reader who doesn't know Hanson gets it, and say why The Wire's obsession with stats makes it the right test case.

2. Where the show cuts deepest (~250 words): the strongest objection the show mounts. I think it's that futarchy needs a welfare measure everyone agrees to bet on, and the show dramatizes that any such measure gets gamed by the people whose careers ride on it -- Goodhart as a way of life. Use the stats scenes and Hamsterdam.

3. What a futarchist would say back (~200 words): steelman the reply. Markets aggregate dispersed information, and the show is also full of information that *does* exist but never enters the numbers -- the street knows things the precinct doesn't. Would a market actually price Hamsterdam correctly? Be honest about whether that helps Hanson or hurts him.

4. Scenes that resist my reading (~150 words): where the show doesn't fit the frame -- e.g. is McNulty's whole arc about something metrics can't capture at all, and is that a problem for my essay or for Hanson?

5. Verdict (~100 words): does The Wire refute futarchy, refine it, or accidentally argue for it?

Don't reduce the show to a debate prop -- if a scene complicates the philosophy, say so instead of flattening it. And no citations or quotes, I'm going from memory too.
