So I work on the backend of a civic-reporting app (think 311-ish: people flag stuff like downed power lines, water main breaks, a gas smell on a corner, an aggressive dog loose in a park) and a small team of moderators decides which reports get pushed to "confirmed" status and dispatched, vs. which sit in the queue waiting for a second report or a photo. There's no real procedure right now, it's vibes, and the vibes are inconsistent between moderators. A friend who's into philosophy told me this is basically the old debate about testimony, and pointed me at Hume's Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, the "Of Miracles" section, where he writes:

"It will be sufficient to observe, that our assurance in any argument of this kind is derived from no other principle than our observation of the veracity of human testimony, and of the usual conformity of facts to the reports of witnesses."

Okay, but then there's apparently this other camp (I think associated with Thomas Reid?) that treats testimony as a basic source of knowledge in its own right -- you're entitled to take someone's word by default, and you only need reasons to *doubt*, not reasons to believe. Those two views sound like they'd give my moderators completely different workflows, and I honestly can't tell which one our current mess is closer to.

So here's what I want: turn this Hume-vs-Reid disagreement into an actual procedure my team can run per report, in order, in under two minutes, with what counts as a sufficient answer at each step and where the procedure just stops and hands it to a human. Then run it on three cases: (1) a first-time anonymous user reports a gas smell at 3am, no photo, no corroboration, high stakes if real; (2) a user with a long history of accurate pothole reports reports a downed line after a storm; (3) three brand-new accounts created within an hour all report the same aggressive dog at the same park. Make at least one of them come out somewhere I won't like (I suspect the gas leak one does, on a strict Humean reading -- the reporter has zero track record, but you're obviously not going to sit on it). Also: be honest about which parts of the philosophers' views survive being turned into a checklist and which parts quietly get dropped, and where judgement calls are hiding inside steps that look mechanical. And go back to the Hume quote at the end -- what exactly does his "observation of the veracity of human testimony" demand for a reporter we've never seen before? Does his view mean you can rationally never trust anyone new, and if so, is that a bug in his view or a bug in my app? Definately don't give me a reading list, I just want the procedure and the reasoning.
