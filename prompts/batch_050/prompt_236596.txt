I've just been handed the job of redesigning the intro world-history curriculum for our community college, and the previous version leans hard on "Middle Ages," "Renaissance," and "Scientific Revolution" as the spine of everything, which half my department thinks is fine and half thinks is borderline malpractice. I need something more defensible than vibes but I don't have time to read the entire historiography debate.

Act like a professional historian who specializes in periodization and how these labels got invented. Think carefully and brainstorm a bit before settling on a final answer. What I want is a usable decision procedure I can apply unit by unit when writing course materials: given some stretch of time and place, how do I decide whether to teach it under a period name, a neutral date range, or some hybrid? The procedure should be a short ordered list of questions or tests (e.g., did contemporaries use the label, does it distort what was happening for most people, does it smuggle in a story of progress, does dropping it cost students anything in terms of connecting to other courses), with a clear rule for what to do at each step, and it should flag the points where it's genuinely a judgment call and no procedure can settle it. Be honest about that part rather than pretending its mechanical.

Then actually run the procedure on three cases so I can see it work: (1) roughly 1300–1600 in Italy and northern Europe, where I'm personally attached to the word Renaissance and suspect the procedure will tell me something I don't want to hear; (2) roughly 1300–1800 in China, where the European labels obviously don't fit but I still need students to be able to compare timelines; and (3) the printing press and Copernicus through Newton, where "Scientific Revolution" feels earned but I've heard historians argue there was no single coherent thing happening. End by telling me the two or three failure modes where following this procedure would actually make my course worse, so I know when to override it.
