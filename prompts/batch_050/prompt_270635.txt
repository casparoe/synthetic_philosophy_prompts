I am writing the specification chapter for a philosophy-of-cognitive-science syllabus on realism about interpretability, and I want the standard of evaluation settled before any particular view gets discussed. Please write the desiderata that a theory of representation in artificial neural networks must satisfy in order to license claims of the form "the network represents that p" or "the network uses a representation of F in computing its output." The purpose is fixed: the theory should be good enough to adjudicate interpretability claims that are supposed to bear on what a model is doing, including safety-relevant ones -- not merely good enough for talks.

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether you take it to be non-negotiable for any adequate theory or instead a commitment of a particular camp. Among the requirements I expect to see defended, rejected, or refined:

- Something to handle the passenger-information problem: a linear probe can often decode from a network's activations information (say, properties of the training distribution, or the identity of the previous token) that no downstream computation ever reads off. If decodability sufficed for representation, networks would represent vastly more than they plausibly use. Say what further condition does the work -- downstream causal role, and if so, specified how -- without making the condition so demanding that it rules out representations that are computed but only conditionally used.
- Something to handle the probe-sensitivity problem: for the same activation space, a linear probe fails to find some content while a nonlinear probe succeeds (or vice versa). Does the network represent the content or not? A theory must say whether representational status is relative to a decoding method, and if it is, what keeps the notion from collapsing into "anything extractable is represented."
- Something to handle the granularity problem: sparse decomposition methods can often split one seemingly unitary direction into several, or merge several into one, depending on hyperparameters. If the individuation of features is method-relative, is realism about individual features still available, or only realism about coarser structure?
- Consistency with what is known about how these systems actually work -- e.g., that a single direction can participate in many behaviors and a single behavior can depend on many directions (polysemanticity and superposition), that interventions on interpretable directions sometimes change behavior and sometimes merely shift it off-distribution in ways that tell us little -- so that the theory cannot be refuted by an ordinary experiment.
- A verdict on the distinction between a representation's being present in the network and its being readable by us: whether interpretability is epistemic (about our access) or partly constitutive (a direction counts as a feature only if some procedure could isolate it), and what hangs on the difference.
- A statement of what the theory takes representation-talk to explain that a purely behavioral description of the network would not -- since a theory that explains nothing extra is an eliminativist theory wearing realist clothes, and the list should force that admission either way.

Then do three things. First, identify any subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied -- for instance, if the natural fix for the passenger-information problem and the natural fix for the probe-sensitivity problem pull toward incompatible conditions, say so explicitly; the list generating its own impossibility result is the part I care most about. Second, name the single requirement that knocks out the most positions currently implicit in the interpretability literature. Third, briefly -- no more than a short paragraph each -- score the three main contender frameworks against the list: an instrumentalist or eliminativist view on which "features" are just useful fictions for prediction and control; a structural or similarity-based realism on which representation consists in the right correspondence between activation geometry and the represented domain; and a causal-role realism on which something is represented only if it plays the right role in the network's internal computations. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring; I will run the scorecard exercise myself in the seminar.
