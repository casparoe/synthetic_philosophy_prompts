"The extended mind thesis, from philosophers Andy Clark and David Chalmers, says that your smartphone is literally part of your mind in the same way your hippocampus is. Their famous case is Otto, an Alzheimer's patient who keeps directions in a notebook: since Otto uses the notebook the same way a healthy person, Inga, uses her biological memory, the notebook counts as one of Otto's beliefs, and there's no principled line to draw once you accept that — your phone, your laptop, maybe the whole internet are in the mind too. This follows pretty directly from the now-standard idea of embodied cognition, that thinking isn't just brain-bound. Critics have pointed out that if everything you causally interact with becomes part of your mind, the concept of mind gets so bloated it stops being useful for science, which is a good reason to think the thesis is really just a vivid metaphor for how dependent we are on tools, not a literal claim about where the mind ends."
