Analyse the Facebook–Cambridge Analytica affair as a stress test for theories of valid consent. Take the rough version everyone knows: a relatively small number of people installed a personality app and clicked through whatever permissions it asked for; the app gathered information about them and also about their Facebook friends; that material later ended up with a political-data firm and was used for profiling. Don’t get bogged down in the exact chronology, the legal fallout, or whether it actually changed any election results. I’m interested in the consent problem.

Be disciplined about the mapping. Whose consent was even in question -- the app users, their friends, the people later targeted with political messages, Facebook itself? What did each group plausibly understand they were agreeing to, and what did they agree to only in the lawyerly fine-print sense? Separate straightforward deception from omission, manipulation, and users simply failing to read. Likewise, don’t call the platform’s social importance coercive without argument: does depending on Facebook to keep in touch with people mean there was no reasonable alternative, or is that a different objection from coercion? And is the main competence problem that users lack technical knowledge, that no ordinary person can trace the downstream uses of their data, or that bounded rationality makes individual notice-and-choice a bad joke?

The trickiest bit is consent by proxy: can my decision to install an app authorise access to information about you merely because Facebook’s architecture treats our friendship as a permission? Then run the analysis in the other direction. Does the episode show that the usual trio of information, voluntariness and competence is sufficient if applied strictly, or that individual consent is the wrong tool for networked data altogether? If the latter, what should supplement or replace it -- collective authorisation, purpose limits, fiduciary duties, treating some uses as impermissible regardless of consent? Say where the consent framework goes quiet instead of forcing it to pronounce on every wrong involved.
