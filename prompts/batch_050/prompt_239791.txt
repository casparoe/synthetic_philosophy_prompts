Over the past month I have been agonising over whether to leave my stable academic post for a riskier position in industry, and in the course of thinking it through I came across risk-weighted expected utility theory. As I understand it, the theory evaluates a choice by taking the probabilities of the possible outcomes and transforming them with a 'risk function': a risk-averse agent gives extra weight to the worse outcomes, as though treating them as more likely than they actually are, and correspondingly discounts the better ones.

This strikes me as deeply troubling, and I would like help resolving the worry. If the risk function systematically distorts the probabilities, then acting on risk-weighted expected utility seems to amount to acting on false beliefs about how the world is — turning down the industry job, say, because I have implicitly come to regard failure as more probable than the evidence warrants. Shouldn't rationality require that I first form accurate, undistorted probability judgements and only afterwards factor in how I happen to feel about risk? And if I were to adopt the theory, how would I quarantine the distortion so that it does not contaminate the beliefs I rely upon in all my other decisions?
