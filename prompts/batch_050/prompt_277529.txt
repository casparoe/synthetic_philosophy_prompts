I'm a novelist, and I've run into something in the way people experience stories that I keep wanting a word for, and I figure philosophers of mind or psychologists might have one. Here's the setup. When I write a twist — say, in chapter twelve you find out the kindly neighbor has been sabotaging the protagonist all along — my beta readers report something odd: scenes from chapter three, the neighbor bringing soup, whatever, now "feel" sinister to them. And I don't just mean they interpret the events differently now, I mean they misremember the feeling itself. They say things like "oh, I knew something was off with that guy," when some of them plainly did not know, I watched them not suspect a thing over months of feedback. The eerie part is they can't tell, and honestly neither can I, wether the ominousness was sitting there in their original experience the whole time, just unlabeled and unnoticed, or wether the twist traveled backward and repainted an experience that was genuinely innocuous at the moment it happened.

Here's what makes the fiction version interesting to me, as opposed to ordinary cases of misremembering how you felt about something: the text doesn't change. If you reread chapter three, the words are the exact same words, so there's a fixed external record of what the "evidence" actually was — in real life you never get that. And yet rereading doesn't settle anything, because the reread feels ominous now too. It's as if the twist contaminates even the fresh perception, not only the memory of the old one. So the book gives you the closest thing to a controlled experiment on this, and the result is somehow still ambiguous.

So: does this have a name? I've been calling it "the twist rewrite," which is terrible. I know it's somewhere in the neighborhood of hindsight bias, and I dimly remember there's an old debate about wether experience gets revised after the fact or wether it was never fully determinate in the first place — something involving a dot that changes color mid-motion, which I read about years ago and can't reconstruct properly. But I don't just want a label. I want to know which carving is best for the fiction case: "the experience was ambiguous all along and the twist merely labels it," versus "the twist falsifies the memory of the experience," versus "the twist changes present experience while memory stays intact" — and wether those three are even distinguishable in principle when the reader has a fixed text to consult, or whether they collapse into the same thing dressed differently. And if what I'm describing doesn't actually match anything established, say so and tell me what you'd call it, with where the edges of the phenomenon are.
