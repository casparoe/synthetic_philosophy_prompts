I'm a graduate student in economics, and I've been persuaded by the argument of Milton Friedman's 1953 essay on the methodology of positive economics: a theory is to be judged by the accuracy of its predictions, not by the realism of its assumptions, and the more significant a theory is, the more unrealistic its assumptions will tend to be. Given this, I'm puzzled by how much effort researchers in economics and biology put into relaxing strong assumptions and making their models more realistic. On Friedman's view, isn't examining the assumptions themselves beside the point — a confusion between testing a theory by its implications and testing it by its premises? Should I conclude that this realism-seeking work is largely wasted, and stop worrying about whether the assumptions in my own models hold, provided the models predict well?
