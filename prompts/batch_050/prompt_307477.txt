I recently took a position with a regional development agency, and my first assignment is helping to design a small local digital currency intended to keep spending circulating within a district whose economy has been depressed for decades. A colleague with a background in the history of economics suggested I look at George Berkeley's *The Querist* (first published in 1735), a pamphlet he wrote in response to the economic conditions of Ireland, which he later described as an attempt "to feed the hungry and clothe the naked, by promoting an honest industry." The entire work consists of questions, and I am finding a run of them about money both suggestive for my project and genuinely hard to parse. The relevant queries (numbered 5, 21, 22, 25, and 26 in the collected edition) read as follows:

"Whether money be not only so far useful, as it stirreth up industry, enabling men mutually to participate the fruits of each other's labour?"

"Whether other things being given, as climate, soil, etc., the wealth be not proportioned to the industry, and this to the circulation of credit, be the credit circulated or transferred by what marks or tokens soever?"

"Whether, therefore, less money swiftly circulating, be not, in effect, equivalent to more money slowly circulating? Or, whether, if the circulation be reciprocally as the quantity of coin, the nation can be a loser?"

"Whether the terms crown, livre, pound sterling, etc., are not to be considered as exponents or denominations of such proportion? And whether gold, silver, and paper are not tickets or counters for reckoning, recording, and transferring thereof?"

"Whether the denominations being retained, although the bullion were gone, things might not nevertheless be rated, bought, and sold, industry promoted, and a circulation of commerce maintained?"

Here is where I need help. First, can you reconstruct the argument connecting these queries into a single theory of what money is? Query 5 seems to define money entirely by its effects on industry; query 25 seems to reduce gold, silver, and paper alike to instruments of reckoning, recording, and transferring. Am I right to read Berkeley as denying that money needs intrinsic value at all — as holding what would now be called a nominalist or chartalist position, against the view that money must be valuable metal — or is something more subtle going on when he considers money as a standard, a measure, or a pledge?

Second, query 22 confuses me. What exactly is the conditional about circulation being reciprocally as the quantity of coin? Is Berkeley here anticipating something like the later idea of the velocity of money, and if so, is his version of the claim coherent as stated, or does it smuggle in an assumption?

Third, query 26 strikes me as the most radical: the bullion disappears, yet trade continues under the retained denominations. What has to be true of a society for that to work — trust in law, custom, the state — and does Berkeley anywhere supply that ground, or does he simply leave it as a question? I gather that the more famous Query 23 presses a similar point about the true idea of money being that of a ticket or counter; how do these less-discussed queries relate to that better-known one?

Fourth, the form itself: every query is a "Whether...?" question, and nothing in the work is straightforwardly asserted. Is this interrogative format a sincere expression of uncertainty, a way of hedging politically sensitive proposals, or a dialectical device for leading the reader? Should it change how confidently we attribute the radical thesis of query 26 to Berkeley himself?

Finally, a selfish application: if this reconstruction is right, would Berkeley's view license a project like ours — a token with no commodity backing, designed purely to speed local circulation — and where would it caution us? In particular, our currency would have no physical tokens at all, only ledger entries. Does his talk of tickets and counters depend on there being something tangible passed hand to hand, or does the argument go through untouched for purely digital records? Where the text is genuinely underdetermined, please say so rather than forcing an answer.
