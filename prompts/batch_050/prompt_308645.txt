Here is my current position on the Church–Turing thesis; please audit whether it hangs together. (1) Logical CT is not a theorem but a constraint on what may count as an effective procedure: finite, surveyable, rule-governed operations by an idealised agent. (2) Oracle machines and other hypercomputational models are mathematically respectable but do not bear on CT, because their extra power is simply assumed in the oracle. (3) Quantum computers threaten at most the strong, efficiency-sensitive version of CT, not CT itself. (4) Physical CT — nothing in nature can compute a non-recursive function — is empirical and could in principle be refuted by a reliable device emitting, say, successive bits of a halting probability. (5) No finite experiment could establish such refutation, since unlimited precision, unbounded running time and noise cannot be distinguished from genuinely non-recursive output. (6) I endorse a calibration norm: an alleged black box refutes physical CT only if its internal mechanism is finite and surveyable, not merely if its input–output behaviour looks anomalous. Identify the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, separate them from merely apparent ones, and state for each what must be abandoned and at what cost; conclude with the single cheapest revision. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
