ok so i'm a novelist working on a book where one of the main characters is a philosophy lecturer who's kind of washed up, and at a key moment she finds an old poem she wrote in grad school, back when she believed mind-uploading would obviously work, and it sort of haunts the rest of the book. i need to actually write the poem. problem is i'm not a philosopher, i just play one in my drafts, so i need help.

you're going to act as a professional philosopher of mind who also publishes poetry, someone who genuinely knows the functionalism literature and won't let me get away with hand-waving. please think carefully about this and brainstorm a bit before writing -- like, jot down the key moves the poem needs to make and how the form can carry them, then write the poem.

what the poem needs to be: a sonnet, roughly. the idea it's built around is multiple realizability -- the functionalist point that what makes something a mind (a pain, a belief, whatever) isn't the stuff it's made of but the causal role it plays, the organization, so the same mental state could in principle run on neurons, silicon, or some alien octopus brain. the octave should sit inside the naive intuition that of course the mind IS the brain, this meat, this particular gray stuff, and the volta should be the moment that intuition breaks -- when the character (or the speaker) realizes that the pattern matters and the substrate doesn't, that if you kept the organization and swapped the parts one by one there'd be no moment where the pain stopped being pain. closing couplet should land the emotional cost of that, not just the thesis: if it's the pattern that matters, then "she" is something that could survive being copied, and that's weirdly both hopeful and terrifying for my character. a philosopher of mind should be able to read it and nod, like yes, that's the actual argument, not just vibes about robots.

also please keep it in a slightly young, slightly too-clever voice -- this is a 24 year old grad student writing it, not rilke. one more thing: it has to be short enough to fit on one page of a paperback since its reproduced in the text, and id rather it not rhyme "mind" with "find" because that's the first rhyme everyone reaches for.
