I cover AI for a general-interest magazine, and I've now watched the same argument recur for several years in slightly different costumes: what, fundamentally, is a large language model? One camp — including some prominent researchers — speaks of these systems as nascent agents: they plan, hold beliefs, pursue goals, deceive when deception serves their objectives, and the right theoretical stance toward them is roughly the one we take toward people. A second camp insists this is category error dressed up as insight: the system is a pattern-completion engine trained to continue text, it has no goals at all, and every appearance of agency is projection by users who can't tell the difference between a mind and a convincing transcript of one. A third camp tries to split the difference by saying the model is best understood as a simulator — not itself an agent, but a thing that can enact agents, so that what you meet in conversation is a character the system is playing, not the system itself, with all sorts of consequences for how you'd interpret "its" statements. What frustrates me, as someone who has to write about this without taking sides, is that the camps seem to agree on essentially all the observable facts — the training process, the benchmark results, even the unsettling behaviors — and yet the disagreement never budges. The same new capability demonstration gets cited by all three sides as vindication. My hypothesis is that this isn't really an empirical disagreement at all, but a dispute about what would even count as an answer: the camps differ on whether questions like "does it have goals?" are settled by internal mechanism, by behavioral criteria, or by which description is most predictively useful, and so they keep talking past each other. But I could be wrong — maybe there is a genuine empirical or formal question buried in here that the right experiment or the right analysis of model internals would settle. Diagnose the disagreement: is it verbal, substantive, a difference in standards of evidence, or three camps answering three different questions under one heading? Support the diagnosis with what each side actually says and predicts, and tell me what — a distinction, an experiment, an interpretability result, a reframing — would count as real progress, or argue honestly that none should be expected. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble, no restating my question.
