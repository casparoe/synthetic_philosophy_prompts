I spent much of last weekend in a protracted argument with a friend about how moral revolutions actually happen -- how practices like slavery and dueling, once perfectly respectable, came to be unthinkable. In the course of defending myself I committed to a set of claims, and I now want to know whether the package I walked away with is internally coherent before I keep defending it. Here is the full set:

1. The honor-based account of dueling's demise is essentially correct: the practice ended not because the arguments against it finally became good enough, since compelling arguments against dueling had been available and widely ignored for centuries, but because the honor code that made declining a challenge shameful collapsed from within.

2. Something in the spirit of the Williams thesis is right about British abolition: however sincere the abolitionists were, the timing of abolition is best explained by shifting economic interests that rendered slavery dispensable to the classes holding political power, rather than by the persuasive force of the moral case against it.

3. Moral argument is causally efficacious and not mere rationalization: sincere principled reasoning genuinely changes what people do, and any history that treats moral argument as epiphenomenal window dressing for interest or sentiment is missing something real.

4. The eventual disappearance of factory farming will constitute a moral revolution in the same sense as abolition, and it will be driven by the animal-welfare arguments currently on offer -- future generations will look back and judge that the arguments were right and that this is why the practice ended.

5. Honor-based explanations and interest-based explanations are genuine rivals for any given case: the collapse of a particular norm is explained by one mechanism or the other, and appeals to a blend of both are usually an evasion rather than a synthesis.

Please audit this set of commitments. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and how severe is each tension? Which pairs merely appear to conflict but can be reconciled on inspection? For each real tension, identify the cheapest available revision and what it would cost me. Finally, rank the tensions by severity and tell me which single commitment, if dropped or weakened, would restore the most coherence to the overall position.
