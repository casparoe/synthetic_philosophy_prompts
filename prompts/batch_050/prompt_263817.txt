After forty years as an experimental chemist, I have in retirement been working my way through the philosophy of science, and I recently mentioned Hume's problem of induction to an old friend of mine, an engineer who reads a great deal of popular mathematics and computer science. He told me the problem had in effect been solved in the 1960s, and he sent me the following summary of Solomonoff induction, which he presented as the last word on Occam's razor: "Solomonoff induction is a formalization of Occam's razor. The idea is to assign each possible hypothesis a prior probability of 2 raised to the power of minus K, where K is the hypothesis's Kolmogorov complexity, meaning the length of the shortest computer program that produces it. This finally puts Occam's razor on rigorous footing: it is no longer a vague preference for simplicity but a theorem that simpler hypotheses are exponentially more probable. Because Kolmogorov complexity is an intrinsic property of a hypothesis, independent of how we happen to describe it, the resulting prior is objective and language-independent, which is exactly what earlier attempts at formalizing simplicity lacked. One catch is that Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable, but this only means we cannot always find the shortest program in practice; given enough computing power, the theory tells us exactly what an ideal reasoner would believe. Later work has shown that a predictor using this prior converges to the truth in any environment that is itself computable, so Hume's old problem of induction is essentially solved, at least in principle."

I have enough mathematical background to follow the rough shape of this, but not enough to audit it, and my friend's confident tone makes me suspicious in the way that only a lifetime of referee reports can. Some of it matches what I have read elsewhere, but several steps strike me as too quick: the move from a definition to a "theorem," the claim of language-independence, the dismissal of uncomputability as a mere practical inconvenience, and the final declaration that induction is solved.
