For my A-level extended project I am writing on testimony in Indian epistemology, and I want a case built to order. The contrast I have in mind is between the Mīmāṃsā doctrine of intrinsic validity (a cognition is presumed valid as it arises and is only overturned by a defeater, which is how they can treat the authorless Veda as beyond suspicion) and the Nyāya view that testimony yields knowledge only when extrinsic conditions, above all the speaker's trustworthiness and competence, are independently secured. Construct a thought experiment in which the two schools give different verdicts on whether a hearer knows something: the hearer's belief is true, the speaker is in fact unreliable, yet the hearer has no inkling of this and no defeater is present, so Mīmāṃsā must count the cognition as valid while Nyāya must deny knowledge. Constraints: an everyday setting (a stranger giving directions, say — no scripture, no gods), the unreliability undetectable by the hearer, and the verdicts must follow from the two theories rather than be stipulated. After the scenario, give each school's reasoning, identify which feature of the case is doing the work, say whether removing that feature collapses the disagreement, and set out how a Naiyāyika would attack the case.
