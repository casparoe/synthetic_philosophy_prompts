So I'm reading Leviathan for the first time (long story) and I keep tripping over a scenario Hobbes almost describes but maybe doesn't, and I want to know if this exact thing has a name.

Take two farmers way out in the countryside somewhere the police basically don't come anymore. These two have known each other twenty years. They've talked it through a hundred times -- neither wants the other's land, neither has any plan to hurt anyone, and crucially both of them genuinely believe all this about each other. As far as humans can ever verify intentions, its verified. And yet each still locks the barn, gets the dog, maybe buys a gun. Not out of suspicion -- out of the fact that being wrong once, alone, costs everything you've got. And here's the part that bugs me: each one's hedging gives the other a slightly bigger reason to hedge, not because it signals hostility (both know exactly why the other is doing it, it's the same calculation) but because a more armed neighbor makes the worst case worse. The escalation runs on nothing. Nobody's greedy, nobody's scared OF anyone, and it still spirals.

Once you see the shape you see it everywhere: two careful drivers both crawling around a blind corner at 5mph even though each would swear the other is careful.

Now, I know Hobbes has a word for the general thing -- diffidence -- but his version seems to run on not being able to read the other guy's intentions. Everyone's a black box, so you arm up or strike first. In my case the intentions are known, verified, benign, and the pressure is still there. So: is this just diffidence, or a different beast with its own name? How does it relate to that whole family of situations economists study where both sides would rather cooperate but can get burned? And does Hobbes's fix -- the enforcer who makes promises safe -- actually dissolve my version, or does it just shrink the worst case from everything down to alot?

Don't just hand me a label, I want the edges: what it is, what it isn't, and where it stops applying. Under 400 words, and start with the straight answer -- yes there's a name / no there isn't -- then justify.
