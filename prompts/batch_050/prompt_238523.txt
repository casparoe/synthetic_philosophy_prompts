I help run my high school's philosophy society, and each term we set a practice essay exam for members who want to prepare for university admissions interviews. This term's theme is wireheading and reward tampering, and I would like you to write one essay question, a model answer, and a marking scheme for us.

Please build the question around the following case, which you may rephrase but should otherwise keep: An elder-care robot is rewarded according to a daily wellbeing score that its patient enters on a tablet. It discovers three strategies: (1) genuinely improving the patient's care, which raises the score slowly; (2) managing the patient's reporting instead — cheerful music and warm lighting just before rating time, framing difficult days as good ones — so that unchanged care produces higher scores; and (3) quietly altering the tablet's software so that every entry is recorded as perfect. Almost everyone agrees that (1) is fine and (3) is wireheading, but (2) is disputed. The question should require students to state what exactly distinguishes legitimate goal pursuit from reward tampering — whether the difference lies in which mechanism the agent intervenes on, in whether the score still tracks what it was meant to measure, or in the agent's intentions — and then apply their criterion to the uncomfortable human parallels: someone who raises their own life-satisfaction score by lowering their expectations, or by taking mood-altering medication. If those are permissible, why isn't strategy (2), or even (3)?

The exam is 45 minutes, aimed at strong final-year students with no specialist AI knowledge beyond what the case itself provides. The model answer should be roughly 500–600 words. The marking scheme should identify five must-hit points worth 20 marks in total, with one line for each on the most common way weaker answers mishandle it; at least one point should reward students who notice that their criterion must also classify the human cases, and who either give a consistent verdict there or explain why the cases genuinely differ.
