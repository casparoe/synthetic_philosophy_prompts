My roommate works in animal cognition and has a running bit about which animals are "in the club" -- i.e. there's something it's like to be them at all. She'll defend the octopus at length and gets genuinely annoyed if you so much as raise the bee.

New case on the table: the Portia jumping spider. It's tiny -- a few hundred thousand neurons, way fewer than a honeybee -- but it plans detours in advance to ambush other spiders, picks between routes it can't fully see, and improvises new tactics when a plan fails. She's never ruled on it.

Predict her verdict. Say which principle she's implicitly been running (a raw neuron-count threshold? flexible, non-stereotyped behavior as the marker? "big convergent nervous systems" as the only ones that count?), note which candidate principles the bee verdict already knocks out, and flag where the two best remaining rules disagree on Portia. Then name one further case that would settle which rule she's really on. End with your prediction as \answer{in} or \answer{out}.
