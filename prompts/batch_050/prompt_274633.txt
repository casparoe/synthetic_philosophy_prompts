I'm a retired physicist (condensed matter, forty years of stat mech) and since retiring I've gone down a philosophy rabbit hole, the way you do. Right now I'm stuck on Nietzsche's eternal recurrence. As far as I can tell there are two readings people take seriously: the half-baked cosmological hypothesis from the notebooks (finite number of configurations of stuff, infinite time, so everything has to come back around), and the existential version where a demon shows up and tells you your life will repeat exactly as it was, forever, and the real question is whether you could stand that or even affirm it. Don't worry about the exact wording of any passage, paraphrase is fine.

Here's what I want: translate the doctrine, both readings, into the vocabulary of statistical mechanics. Phase space, measure-preserving dynamics, ergodicity, Poincaré recurrence, entropy, the arrow of time, fluctuation theorems, recurrence times, heat death. Go piece by piece, and for each piece say whether it translates cleanly, translates only with distortion, or has no expression in that vocabulary at all. And please be honest about the translation -- don't quietly import a philosophical assumption and then present it as a bit of physics.

Some specific things I want checked along the way:

- Nietzsche's notebook argument looks to me like a folk version of Poincaré recurrence. Is it? And does an actual universe satisfy the theorem's assumptions -- bounded phase space, finite measure, measure-preserving flow -- given cosmic expansion, an apparently infinite universe, and quantum mechanics? Wich assumptions fail first?
- Poincaré recurrence gives you arbitrarily close returns, not identical ones. Does the demon's test need exact repetition, or would a return that's indistinguishable for all practical purposes do the same psychological work?
- Run the numbers, at least in orders of magnitude. Recurrence times for even small systems are beyond absurd. Does the claim that you will live this life again survive contact with the actual magnitudes, given that whatever recurs that far out isn't obviously you in any sense that matters?
- If the whole universe recurs, the low-entropy past has to recur too. Does the doctrine need that, and does the thermodynamic arrow of time make the whole picture more coherent or less?
- Now the part I suspect is the real problem: amor fati, the idea that you should live so that you could will the repetition. Does any of that survive translation into stat mech language, or is the entire normative weight of the doctrine precisely the residue that won't carry over?

End with a verdict on the residue: is it a defect of the physics vocabulary (it just has no words for first-personal normative claims), or does it show the cosmological reading was a category error all along and the doctrine was never really a physics claim, even when Nietzsche dressed it up as one?
