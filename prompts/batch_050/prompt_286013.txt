I'm a third-year PhD student in public policy working on a dissertation chapter about what smaller European countries should actually take away from the Germany-versus-France nuclear contrast. Germany shut its last three reactors (Isar 2, Emsland, Neckarwestheim 2) in April 2023, right after an energy crisis caused partly by losing Russian gas, while France keeps running its big legacy fleet and is planning new EPR2 units even after the Flamanville EPR came in many years late and several times over budget, and after 2022 exposed how fragile the fleet can be when corrosion problems knocked a huge share of reactors offline at once. My committee keeps telling me the chapter reads like a survey of both sides instead of an argument, and honestly they're right.

So here's what I'd like: imagine you're advising the government of a mid-sized European country (decent but not world-class nuclear engineering base, some existing reactors nearing end of life, strong wind resources, an anxious public, and neighbors who would be affected by either a build-out or a shutdown). Which considerations should actually dominate the decision about whether to extend the existing fleet and/or build new units, and in what order? Candidate considerations include levelized cost versus the system-cost comparisons people make when they say renewables are cheaper, the value of firm low-carbon capacity in a grid with lots of intermittent generation (Dunkelflaute-style risks), construction risk and cost overruns, energy security and geopolitical exposure, waste and decommissioning liabilities, and whether a nuclear program can survive politically across multiple election cycles. Rank them and defend the ranking -- I want the criteria you used (tons of CO2 avoided per euro? robustness to worst cases? reversibility?) and how they trade off against each other, not just a list. Also tell me which of these considerations are overrated or mostly rhetorical, and what new evidence over the next five or ten years would move something up or down your ranking. Please write in American English.
