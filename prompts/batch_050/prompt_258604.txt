For a bioethics seminar I'm designing a diagnostic instrument called "Where do you draw the therapy–enhancement line — and do you draw it at all?" I want a decision tree that sorts respondents into the live positions on the therapy/enhancement distinction by what they say about cases, not by which label they find attractive.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a roster of names. Start from the branching questions on which the positions genuinely come apart — for instance: whether the distinction carries any moral weight at all or is merely an administrative convenience; whether "normal functioning" is fixed by species-typical biology, by statistical norms of a population, or by the demands of a particular social environment; whether an intervention's status depends on where the recipient starts (below vs. above the baseline) or only on where it takes them; whether treating a condition imposed by an unjust environment counts as therapy or as complicity; whether the distinction survives in reproductive contexts where there is no patient yet. Then locate the actual positions in the resulting space.

Requirements:

1. Each node must be a concrete case on which positions diverge, phrased so someone can answer without knowing the literature. Use cases of this kind: stimulant medication for a healthy student facing competitive exams; growth hormone for a child of normal but short stature; memory-dampening drugs after trauma; cochlear implants where the Deaf community objects; gene edits conferring disease resistance in embryos; a cognitive intervention that would lift everyone, not just the impaired. Each branch label should state the claim that splits it, not the name of a view.

2. The leaves must include, at minimum: the position that the distinction tracks species-typical normal functioning and that treatment restores it while enhancement exceeds it; the welfarist position that only outcomes matter and the line is morally inert; the position that the line is socially constructed and shifts with whatever a society happens to insure; and at least one position that keeps the distinction but relocates it (e.g., to capabilities or to the demands of justice rather than to biology). Six to eight leaves total.

3. For each leaf, give the position's name (or "unnamed" if it is a coherent but undefended region of the space) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment — the case it mishandles or the bullet it must bite.

4. Note any unoccupied combinations of answers and diagnose briefly why they are empty: incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

5. Flag explicitly any pair of named positions that your tree cannot separate with these cases, and say what further question would be needed to split them.

The deliverable is a handout, so present the tree in indented outline form, keep each diagnostic case to two or three sentences, and end with a short paragraph advising me which single branch point is doing the most philosophical work and which leaf is most likely to surprise undergraduates.
