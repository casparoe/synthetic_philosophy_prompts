After forty years as an experimental chemist, I have in retirement been reading my way into philosophy of psychology, and I keep returning to a literature whose trajectory puzzles me as an outsider: the debate among prototype, exemplar, and theory-theory accounts of concepts. As I understand the terrain, prototype theory rested on typicality gradients and family-resemblance structure; exemplar models then showed that much of the same data falls out of stored instances plus a similarity metric; and the theory theory drew support from phenomena neither of its predecessors handled naturally -- children's sensitivity to causal and functional structure, category-based induction, essentialist judgments about kind membership. What strikes me is that this succession looks nothing like the succession of theories I knew in the laboratory. There, the old account eventually could not be stretched to cover the new result, and its partisans conceded or converted. Here, so far as I can tell, nobody has conceded anything in decades. Prototype theorists absorb the counterexamples as context-sensitivity; exemplar theorists fit almost any categorization curve given enough free parameters; theory theorists are charged with vagueness and answer that the mind is vague. Meanwhile the experiments continue and the camps persist.

My question is diagnostic rather than adjudicative. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Three rival empirical theories that happen to be underdetermined by current methods, in which case the remedy is better experiments of a specifiable kind -- which kind? Or theories pitched at different levels of explanation, in Marr's sense, which have been mistakenly treated as competitors? Or a disagreement about what a theory of concepts is for -- predicting response times, explaining development, underwriting a compositional semantics -- being conducted as though it were one question? I would also like your assessment of the eliminativist suspicion, associated with Machery, that the debate stalls because "concept" does not pick out a natural kind, so that each side is right about the phenomena its favored construct tracks.

Given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a decisive experiment, a formal result about model equivalence, a stipulation that splits the field, or an argument that no progress is to be expected. Finally, please state what would change your own verdict -- which argument or finding would lead you to a different diagnosis, and where your reasoning here is most likely to fail.
