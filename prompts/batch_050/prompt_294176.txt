I’m a volunteer with a little neighbourhood history project that started because my nan kept every birthday card and bus ticket, and somehow I’ve ended up agreeing to speak at our borough’s culture committee next month. My pitch is that the council archive should stop pretending it just receives history neutrally: I want them to fund what I keep calling a silence audit, where we map whose lives are thick in the boxes and whose are basically absent, then spend part of the collecting budget on oral histories, WhatsApp voice notes, photos of shopfronts, tenant meeting minutes, that sort of thing, even if it’s messy and not very parchment-and-seal. I’m also arguing that catalogue descriptions should admit when a record was produced by police, landlords, employers or the council itself, instead of treating the file like it fell from the sky. I’m not an academic, I’ve just read bits and bobs and noticed that the people with power seem to leave paper trails whilst everyone else gets remembered as rumour.

Can you prep me for the Q&A like a proper sceptical rehearsal? The room will be: two councillors worried about money, elections and anything that sounds political; a professional archivist who cares a lot about provenance, original order and not contaminating the record; one local historian who thinks volunteers are well-meaning vandals; a data-protection person who will definitely mention GDPR; and two community activists who may attack from the other side and say the archive has harmed people and shouldn’t be trusted with more stories. Give me the objections in the order they’re likely to come, phrased how each person would actually say it, not in seminar language. For each one, tell me how dangerous it is, what I should concede straight away so I don’t sound defensive, what I must not give up, and a reply I can deliver in under ninety seconds. Include the sneaky technical objection the archivist might raise about appraisal and description, the activist objection that consent can be coerced when the council holds the keys to housing and benefits, and the councillor’s version of isn’t this just dredging up division. Also tell me the one sentence that has to land or the whole idea dies, the weakest bit of my own plan that I should shore up rather than hope nobody notices, and the question I’m praying nobody asks.
