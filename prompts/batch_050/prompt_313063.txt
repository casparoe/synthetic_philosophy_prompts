Two of my coworkers have been going back and forth about this for months and I want to settle it by making them cosign something. One of them is basically a Moloch-first theorist: she thinks most of what's bad about modern institutions -- clickbait media, academic publishing rat races, doping in sports, 80-hour work weeks in finance and law, enshittified platforms -- comes from multipolar traps where every individual actor is behaving sensibly and everyone hates the outcome. The other is what I'd call a conflict theorist: he thinks the trap framing is mostly a comforting story, that in most of these cases somebody is winning on purpose and likes the outcome fine, and that talking about faceless coordination failures lets actual beneficiaries off the hook. They both cite the same examples at each other constantly, which is how you know its a real disagreement.

Draft the joint statement they should be able to produce after a serious adversarial collaboration. Don't adjudicate it for them, and don't soften either position -- I want wording each of them would actually sign for their own side, not mushy compromises. Where their disagreement turns out to be about words (I suspect "trapped" is doing a lot of work -- is a fishery that keeps overfishing because each fisher profits trapped in the same sense as one where everyone's losing?), say so explicitly.

Structure it like this, in this order:

- Agreed claims: numbered list, ~200 words total. Things both can sign -- presumably that genuine traps exist somewhere, that some bad outcomes really are just winners winning, that you can in principle check which situation you're in.
- The exact disagreements: a short table, one row per disagreement, columns for the neutral formulation, her position, his position. Each position stated so its own side would accept it.
- Cruxes: three or four, ~120 words each. For each one: a concrete case or observation both agree bears on it, plus what each of them predicts about that case in advance. For example, I bet they disagree about whether the people winning a race to the bottom would support enforceable regulation if it were free -- that's checkable in principle.
- What's actually verbal: ~100 words, which parts of their fight dissolve once terms are pinned down, if any do.
