So a reading group I'm in does this thing where you have to circulate your "position statement" on some topic before the meeting and everyone tries to break it. My assigned topic is the special composition question -- under what circumstances do some things compose a further thing. Here's where I've landed:

1. Ordinary objects exist. Tables, cats, planets, bicycles. Any view that says there are no tables is a reductio of itself, I dont care how clever the arguments are.
2. Mereological universalism is false. There is no object consisting of my left shoe plus the moon plus the number 7 (ok the number one is cheating, but you get it -- no arbitrary fusions). Composition is restricted.
3. Borderline cases are genuinely possible. You can have a series of cases shading continuously from "these things definitely compose something" to "these definitely don't" -- imagine two planks being slowly nailed together, or a table gradually losing molecules into the air -- and somewhere in the middle it's just vague whether composition has happened. Sharp cutoffs are absurd.
4. Vague existence is incoherent. I'm convinced by the Evans-style line that there can't be indeterminate identity, and I think the same goes for existence: it can't be indeterminate whether something exists or how many things there are. Either the simples arranged tablewise compose something or they don't.
5. Whatever the answer to the SCQ is, it has to be principled. "Composition just brutally happens sometimes" is not an answer, and I want the criterion to be something like physical bonding or fastening rather than anything mind-dependent -- conventions about what counts as an "object" are just us talking, they don't make things.
6. I'm a normal set theorist -- for any things, there's a set of them, however scattered or heterogeneous. Sets of arbitrary things are fine.
7. My considered case verdicts: two strangers shaking hands do not compose an object; a heap of sand does not compose an object; a watch does; a galaxy does.

Tell me whether this hangs together. Which pairs of commitments are in real tension, and how bad is each one -- rank them by severity rather than flagging everything. For each genuine tension, tell me what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me, and be concrete about which commitment has to go. Also tell me which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, because I suspect at least one of these only looks like a problem. If one single revision cleans up the most, say which it is.
