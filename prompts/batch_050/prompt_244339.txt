Okay so I'm a novelist (crime fiction mostly, two unpublished literary misfires in a drawer) and I've realized my moral instincts about deception are all tangled up with my craft instincts, and I want someone to audit them before I embarrass myself in an interview or something. Here's where I think I stand:

1. Lying is morally worse than misleading, full stop. When you mislead someone you leave them an epistemic path to the truth -- they could have drawn a better inference. When you lie you slam that door. So the categorical line tracks real moral weight.

2. The reason lying is wrong is that asserting something works like an implicit guarantee: by saying it, you stake your credibility that you believe it. The liar cashes in on that guarantee while secretly voiding it.

3. In fiction, deliberate misdirection -- red herrings, withholding the crucial scene, letting the reader assume the wrong suspect -- is not a moral matter at all, because the reader consented to the conventions of the genre the moment they picked up the book. BUT: an omniscient narrator flat-out stating something false (not a character talking, the narrator) is a cheat, basically the author lying to the reader.

4. Lying to the murderer at the door asking where your friend is hiding is permissable, maybe even required.

5. A politician who "palters" -- says only technically true things carefully engineered to make you believe a falsehood -- deserves nearly as much condemnation as an outright liar.

6. Bluffing in poker, faking left in football, all fine. Nobody's wronged. Conventions again.

So: do these hang together? Where do they pull against each other -- not just surface-level friction, I mean where the thing that motivates one commitment actively undermines another? And which apparent tensions would dissolve if I thought about them for five more minutes? Rank whatever real problems you find by severity, and for each one tell me what I'd actually have to give up to fix it. If there's a single revision that cleans up the most mess, name it. And please don't just tell me it's all coherent because you want to be nice -- I genuinely can't tell if 5 and 1 fit together, or if 3 and 6 do, and it's bugging me.
