So I'm in this philosophy of mind seminar and we just did the extended mind stuff -- the whole Otto and his notebook case, where Otto has memory loss and writes everything down, and the claim is that the notebook entries count as his beliefs the same way Inga's biological memories count as hers. I was complaining about it over dinner and my roommate (who's an engineer and loves being annoying about this) walked me through this argument and I genuinely can't find the exit, but the conclusion can't be right.

His argument, roughly: (1) The whole point of the parity principle is that if some bit of the world drives your reasoning and behavior the way internal cognitive machinery would, it's part of your mind -- what matters is the functional role, not where the stuff is located. (2) Otto's notebook plays that role, so its entries are his beliefs. Fine so far, that's just the standard move. (3) But now: my roommate doesn't actually remember his car's license plate or his mom's birthday -- his girlfriend does, and whenever it comes up he just asks her, gets the answer instantly, and acts on it without double-checking anything. Her stored knowledge is driving his behavior in exactly the functional-role sense. (4) So by parity, chunks of her memory are literally part of his mind. (5) And the same goes for the librarian who always knows where his books are, the coworker who remembers the office wifi password, the shared drive at work, etc. (6) So it turns out minds routinely contain other people and institutions, and the boundary between persons basically dissolves -- you're part of my mind, I'm part of yours, who knows where anyone ends. (7) That's obviously nuts, so something in the extended mind setup has to go.

I suspect the slip is somewhere between (2) and (4), like asking-your-girlfriend isn't the same *kind* of access as flipping your own notebook, but when I said that he just said "so Otto flips pages slower than Inga recalls, and that matters why, exactly?" and honestly that stung. Which step do you think I should reject, what does rejecting it actually cost me (especially if I want to keep Otto!), and is there a version of the access/fluency/automaticity criteria that blocks the girlfriend case without looking like it was reverse-engineered just to get the "right" answer?
