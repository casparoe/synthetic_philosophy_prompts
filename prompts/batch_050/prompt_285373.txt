before i read any more about the paradox of tragedy i want the spec first, not another tour of the theories. you know the puzzle: why do people seek out, pay for, and often love art that makes them sad, scared, or gutted, when they avoid those same feelings everywhere else in life. write the desiderata -- what any adequate account of the enjoyment of sad art has to deliver, with the case or observation behind each requirement.

the purpose, so you know what adequate means here: i want a theory good enough to settle practical and critical questions. whether its rational for someone dreading the last 100 pages of a devastating novel to push through, whether a downbeat ending is a defect in a film or a feature, whether reccomending a grief memoir to a recently bereaved friend is kind or cruel. so a theory that just relabels the phenomenon ('catharsis!') and moves on fails on arrival.

cases i think the theory has to get right, tell me if i'm wrong about any of them:
1. the rewatch asymmetry -- people replay a sad song two hundred times but almost never rewatch the film that wrecked them. if the enjoyment worked the same way in both, that shouldn't happen.
2. the news aversion -- the same person who queues up a tragedy on friday night switches off the sad documentary and avoids the sad headlines. so 'it's fiction, we're at a safe distance' can't be the whole story, because the documentary is at a safe distance too.
3. grief memoirs -- people knowingly read first-person accounts of real loss and call the experience valuable, so fictionality isn't doing the work either.
4. the skip-the-scene effect -- on a rewatch people skip the exact scene they'd name as the best in the film.
5. spoilers sometimes help -- knowing the sad ending in advance doesn't always spoil it and sometimes increases enjoyment. a theory built on suspense gets this backwards.
6. abandonment patterns -- people quit sad books halfway way more often than they walk out of sad films. is that sunk time, control over pacing, or something a good theory should actually predict?

requirements on distinctions too: the theory can't treat 'enjoying' as one undifferentiated thing (enjoying the film vs enjoying the sadness itself are different claims and it has to say which is going on); it can't just deny the datum and say we don't really enjoy it, because then it owes an explanation of why people pay money and come back for more; and it can't contradict the psych findings, like people in sad moods actively choosing sad music, which the simple mood-repair story gets exactly backwards.

then the part i care about most. which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just the house style of one camp -- the hedonic-reversal people, the meaning-and-connection people, the arousal-and-control people. and is there a pair of desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied? my suspect: respecting first-person reports that the sadness itself is valued AND locating the real payoff somewhere else (relief, insight, solidarity). if the list itself forces a fork, that's the result i want. finally, which single requirement knocks out the most standard contenders, and then briefly -- prose not tables, and no bibliography -- score catharsis, conversion theory, safe-distance, and the sadness-as-route-to-meaning view against the list.
