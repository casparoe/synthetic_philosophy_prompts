Fitch's paradox of knowability appears to end a long-standing metaphysical dispute with a page of modal logic. As I understand the derivation: assume that every truth is knowable in principle, and suppose some truth p is in fact unknown. Then the conjunctive truth that p is true and unknown would itself have to be knowable; yet knowing that conjunction would require knowing p while simultaneously knowing that no one knows p, which is impossible. The knowability principle therefore collapses into the claim that every truth is actually known — an obvious absurdity. Since semantic anti-realism is defined by its commitment to the knowability of all truths, hasn't Fitch refuted anti-realism outright? Why do its defenders persist with restriction strategies and revisions to the underlying logic instead of conceding that the derivation has settled the matter?
