I'm preparing a graduate seminar session on what exactly the cogito establishes, and I want a decision tree that sorts participants by their reading of the argument rather than by which famous name they sympathize with.

Start the tree from the minimal datum every reading accepts: that in the very act of doubting, some thinking is undeniably occurring right now. Each branch node should be a question a participant could answer without knowing the labels it leads to — for instance, whether the move from thinking occurring to there being an I who thinks is an inference at all, or something else entirely; whether the I, if secured, refers to a persisting substance, a merely momentary subject, or nothing; and whether the result licenses any claim about what I am beyond the present instant. As leaves, locate at least: the Cartesian substantial self; a reading on which the cogito secures a subject only for the duration of the thought and nothing about persistence or identity; Lichtenberg's impersonal reading, on which thinking happens the way raining happens, with no thinker behind it; Nietzsche's charge that the I is smuggled in by grammar, so the argument establishes nothing; and a performative reading on which my existence is certain because doubting it enacts it rather than because it follows from a premise.

Where your tree terminates in a combination of answers nobody defends, note the empty slot and say briefly whether it looks incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored. Flag any leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate — for example, where Lichtenberg ends and Nietzsche begins, or whether the performative reading is a genuine rival to the substantial and impersonal readings or a cross-cutting claim about the argument's epistemic status that any of them could absorb. Finally, for each occupied leaf, add one sentence stating what the cogito actually secures on that reading, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
