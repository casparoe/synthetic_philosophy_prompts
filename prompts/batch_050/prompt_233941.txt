My buddy Dave and I have been having basically the same arguement for like two years now and I want to settle it properly. I heard about this thing called an adversarial collaboration, where two people who disagree have to sit down and write one joint statement they can both sign, and I want you to draft ours.

The fight: is history basically locked in by accidents, or would the big stuff have happened no matter what? Dave is team "locked in." His whole thing is that small flukes get frozen in and then you're stuck with them forever. His examples: the QWERTY keyboard everyone uses even though other layouts are supposedly better, VHS beating Betamax, railroads having different gauges in different countries so trains can't just roll across the border, whole countries driving on the left because of decisions made centuries ago that nobody can undo now. Once enough people have sunk costs into a standard, he says, it stays even if it's worse, so history is just a chain of accidents we can't escape.

I'm team "it was coming anyway." Calculus got invented independently by two guys who then spent decades fighting over priority. Natural selection occurred to Darwin and Wallace separately. Edison gets the credit for the lightbulb but loads of people were closing in on it at the same time. And if Einstein had never been born, somebody else gets relativity within a decade or so -- the pieces were already lying around. To me the deep conditions (population, trade, incentives, geography) push everything toward the same outcomes, and the flukes just decide whose name ends up in the textbook.

Here's what the document needs to contain:

1. A numbered list of claims we can BOTH sign. Neutral wording, nothing either of us would want to quibble with.
2. The exact points where we still disagree, each one phrased so the person holding that view would accept it as a fair statement of his own position. If part of our fight is really just us using a word like "inevitable" in two different senses, say which part and show it.
3. The cruxes -- the questions such that settling them would actually move one of us. My guesses, which you can use or push back on: (a) whether lock-in and convergence might both be true at different scales, so the whole fight is really about which box a given event belongs in; (b) whether "it would have happened anyway" concedes too much, since timing and form still matter -- a world where computing shows up 40 years later is a totally different world even if it "shows up anyway"; (c) whether any of this is even testable, given you can't rerun history and see.
4. For each crux, some case or observation we'd both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. One case I keep coming back to: if you replayed the last 600 years with small random changes, does the printing press still show up within a century of when it actually did? Use that one plus whatever else fits.

Last thing: I don't want a "both sides make good points" mush. Commit to what the actual cruxes are, give your reasons for the choices you made in the drafting, and then after the joint statement (since that part has to stay neutral) tell me straight out which side you think has the better of the argument and why. I'd honestly rather you be wrong than vague.
