I am drafting the requirements chapter of a dissertation on rational choice, and before I survey any proposed solutions to the puzzle of the self-torturer, I want the specification fixed in advance. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of rational preference over a long series of options differing by imperceptible increments must deliver, with the case or consideration motivating each requirement.

For reference, the puzzle as I am using it, following Quinn's well-known formulation: a person has a device implanted with a dial of a thousand settings. Each step up the dial increases the pain she will suffer by an amount too small for her to discriminate from the previous setting, and pays her a fixed sum, say ten thousand dollars. At setting 0 she feels nothing and has no money; at setting 1000 she is in constant agony and has ten million dollars. At every setting it seems rational to advance one notch, since the pain difference is indiscriminable and the money is real, yet it seems clearly irrational to end at the top, and there is no notch at which stopping appears rationally mandated rather than arbitrary.

The desiderata should include, at a minimum, requirements along the following dimensions, though you should not treat my list as exhaustive and should reorganize it if you think the joints lie elsewhere: how the theory must accommodate the apparent rationality of each individual step without licensing the endpoint; what it must say about the transitivity of preference and of indifference, and whether these stand or fall together; whether it must yield a determinate answer to where the agent should stop, or whether refusing to do so is a feature rather than a failure; how it must handle the agent's foresight, given that she can survey the entire trajectory before taking the first step; what it must imply about susceptibility to money pumps and related forms of exploitation; and how it must generalize to less contrived gradients, such as incrementally worsening working conditions, small cumulative policy harms, and piecemeal health tradeoffs. For each requirement, give the motivating consideration and state whether you take it to be non-negotiable for any adequate theory or instead a commitment of a particular camp.

The part I care most about is this: identify any subset of your desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, and demonstrate the conflict informally. I suspect that some combination of the stepwise-permissibility claim, a composition principle for rationally permissible sequences of choices, and the irrationality of the top setting forms an inconsistent triad, and I want to know whether the inconsistency survives careful statement or dissolves once the principles are made precise. Finally, briefly, one short paragraph each, indicate how the main families of response fare against the list: views that permit rationally intransitive preferences, views that declare some stopping point arbitrary yet rationally mandatory, sorites-style treatments that deny there is a fact of the matter about each step, and views that locate the irrationality in the agent's diachronic planning rather than in any single choice. Keep the scoring subordinate to the criteria, since the criteria are the deliverable. Please write your answer in American English.
