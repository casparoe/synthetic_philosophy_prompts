I recently finished a popular science book on the embodied mind — the sort of thing aimed at a general audience — and it devotes a chapter to Merleau-Ponty. Its summary of his account of motor intentionality runs roughly as follows. According to the author, Merleau-Ponty's central claim is that the body possesses its own intelligence, entirely independent of thought: when a skilled typist's fingers find the keys, or a footballer adjusts to a pass, there is no inner representation, no plan, and nothing mental going on at all — only trained reflexes stored in the muscles rather than the brain. The author adds that Merleau-Ponty took this to show that cognition is built from the bottom up out of such bodily routines, that perception itself is just a readiness to act, and that reflective, deliberative thought is a thin and dispensable veneer over this machinery. On this basis the author concludes that Merleau-Ponty had essentially refuted Descartes decades before cognitive science caught up, and that modern work on embodied cognition simply vindicates him.

Something about this strikes me as a caricature, though I can't locate the problem precisely. From what little I've read elsewhere, Merleau-Ponty seems to have thought bodily skill involved a genuine kind of directedness — the famous 'I can' — which sounds like more than reflex, and I had the impression he engaged seriously with the neurology of his day rather than dismissing the brain in favour of the muscles. But the book is by a reputable science writer, and I may simply be projecting a more respectable view onto him.

Could you audit the summary for me? Which parts would a careful reader of Merleau-Ponty accept as accurate, which are defensible simplifications, and which are misrepresentations — in particular, does he really deny that skilled bodily activity involves anything representation-like or normative, and does he treat reflective thought as dispensable? And once the summary is corrected, does the author's conclusion — that Descartes was refuted and embodied cognition merely catches up — still follow, or does it depend on the distorted parts?
