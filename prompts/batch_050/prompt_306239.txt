I'm a journalist working on a long feature about the new pronatalism -- the Musks and Simons of the world saying we need way more babies, the effective altruists saying future people count for a lot, the environmentalists saying fewer people would be better. Everyone in my interviews just asserts that a bigger (or smaller) population is obviously good and I have no way to compare their claims. So before I write anything I want the spec: what would a theory of optimal population size actually have to deliver to be taken seriously? Give me the requirements list -- the cases it has to get right (e.g. does it have to avoid the repugnant conclusion, or is biting that bullet allowed?), the distinctions it cant collapse (average vs total wellbeing, adding happy people vs making people happier, people whose existence depends on the policy), the empirical stuff it has to stay consistent with -- with the reason each one belongs on the list. Then flag any pair of requirements you think cant be jointly satisfied, since I suspect that's the real story here, and say which single requirement knocks out the most positions my sources hold. A table is fine. And since I'll be translating this for readers: say how confident you are in each of your claims about what's negotiable vs non-negotiable, and single out the one point in your answer you're least sure of.
