On one side: a defender of non-empirical theory assessment, who holds that string theory's standing is legitimately strengthened by considerations such as the apparent absence of alternatives, its unexpected explanatory interconnections with established physics and mathematics, and the track record of theories once pursued on similar grounds (a position associated with Richard Dawid). On the other side: a falsificationist critic, who holds that a theory generating no testable consequences has not yet earned confidence, whatever its virtues, and that the non-empirical arguments mostly measure the sociology of a research community rather than its proximity to truth (a position associated with Lee Smolin and with George Ellis and Joe Silk). The statement should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, which must be substantive rather than vacuous — including what, exactly, the no-alternatives argument establishes even on the critic's own terms, and whether the string-theory multiverse counts as a prediction or as an excuse; (2) a table of residual disagreements, each row phrased so that the side whose view it states would accept the wording as accurate and unslanted; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp — and for each crux, the argument, hypothetical discovery, or future development (e.g., a specific pattern of null results, a found alternative UV completion, or a post-hoc sociological study of theory adoption) that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Flag any disagreement you conclude is merely verbal and dissolve it in the agreed-claims section.
