Here's a distinction I made up: call epistemic dependence on an AI system *opaque* when you couldn't tell, from the inside, whether the system's outputs are good reasons or confabulations — you trust it the way you trust a calculator you can't check. Call it *translucent* when you could in principle follow the system's reasoning but in practice you stop doing so because it's faster not to. Standard worries about AI and the erosion of understanding tend to target opaque dependence. Please argue that *translucent* dependence is actually the bigger threat to understanding, even though (or precisely because) it looks epistemically respectable — e.g. the doctor who could check the diagnostic system's reasoning but never does, the grad student who could prove the lemma but asks the model instead. Your argument should engage the obvious objection that translucent dependence is just ordinary division of cognitive labor, no worse than relying on a colleague, and should say something about what understanding actually consists in such that it can erode without the believer ever being wrong about anything.
