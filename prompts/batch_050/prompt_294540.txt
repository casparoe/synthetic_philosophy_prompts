1. You can only deserve blame or praise for things that were under your control.
2. Whether a drunk driver actually hits someone isn't under her control — it depends on whether a pedestrian happened to be there, which is pure luck.
3. So the driver who kills someone and the driver who gets home safe are equally blameworthy.
4. But the same holds for everything about what our actions end up causing, since once you've acted, how things turn out is always at least partly luck. So we can only be judged for what we intended or tried to do.
5. Which means someone who tries to murder a person and fails deserves exactly the same blame — and the same punishment — as someone who succeeds.
6. And it gets worse: your character and even your intentions come from your genes and how you were raised, which you didn't control either, so by step 1 you can't be judged for those either. So strictly speaking nobody is blameworthy for anything, ever.
7. That's obviously absurd, so step 1 has to be false and we CAN be morally judged for things outside our control.
