Alright folks, quick creative exercise for you. Write an internal HR memo, maybe 500-600 words, from the Ministry of Ethical Health of a modern state that has decided to run its foreign policy strictly on Hegelian principles -- specifically Hegel's claim in the Philosophy of Right that war has an "ethical moment," that it keeps peoples from sinking into the stagnation and self-absorption of civil society, the way (as he puts it elsewhere in that text) winds keep the sea from going foul.

The memo should announce the state's new quarterly War Wellness Initiative, complete with sign-up sheets, participation metrics for "ethical health," and an FAQ section. Format it as a numbered memo with an FAQ at the end.

The humor should come from getting Hegel right, not from making him a strawman: the memo writers genuinely believe that sacrifice of life and property in war actualizes the state's substantial unity, that a people devoted purely to bourgeois comfort and private interest has lost something real, and that perpetual peace is not obviously a good thing. So the jokes land when these sincere views collide with HR-speak: KPIs for self-sacrifice, complaints about insufficiently edifying border skirmishes, a note from Legal clarifying that conscripts are expressing their ethical substance, not experiencing an "externality." Have at least one exasperated footnote responding to a Kantian employee who keeps bringing up perpetual peace in the suggestion box.

Keep the tone deadpan corporate the whole way through -- no winking at the reader. A reader who knows the Philosophy of Right should recognize that the satire is fair to Hegel's actual position, and a reader who doesn't should still find it funny and come away wondering whether Hegel really meant all this.

Please dont have any character break the fourth wall or explain the joke.
