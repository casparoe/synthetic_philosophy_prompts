So I get that the second law of thermodynamics is supposed to explain the arrow of time — entropy increases, which is why eggs break but never unbreak, and why we remember the past but not the future. But my freezer turns water into ice, which lowers the entropy of the water. Does that mean time is running backwards inside my freezer, just on too small a scale for me to notice? And if not, what exactly is the difference — because if the answer is "total entropy still goes up because of the heat the freezer dumps out the back," then it seems like the arrow of time isn't really about any particular process at all, it's just about whatever the biggest net number is doing. And in that case, if we built a big enough closed system that steadily decreased its own entropy by exporting it elsewhere, could we get a whole room where time flows in reverse relative to us, with people inside remembering our future?
