I'm in a small reading group on free will and moral responsibility, and we've just spent two sessions on Frankfurt cases. Quick recap so we're on the same page: Black wants Jones to perform some action, and has a device that will force him to do it if Jones shows any sign of deciding otherwise; as it happens Jones decides to do it entirely on his own and the device never activates, and Frankfurt's claim is that Jones is still morally responsible even though he couldn't have done otherwise. The pushback we kept circling was the standard dilemma defence: either the device has to read some prior sign of Jones's decision, which seems to presuppose determinism and so begs the question against the libertarian, or it doesn't, in which case Jones retains a flicker of freedom (he could at least have shown the sign) and the principle of alternative possibilities survives in a weakened form.

Then one of us floated a variant none of us had come across before, and I want to defend it at the next meeting, so I'd like you to argue the case for me. In the variant, Black's device doesn't monitor Jones in advance at all. Instead it watches the decision as it happens, and if Jones begins to choose against Black's wishes, the device rewinds time by a few seconds and lets the decision unfold again, and again, until Jones finally chooses as Black wants. As it happens Jones chooses the way Black prefers on the first pass and nothing is ever rewound. Please argue that this rewind case yields a genuine Frankfurt-style counterexample to the principle of alternative possibilities that escapes the dilemma on both horns: it needs no prior sign, so no question-begging determinism is built into the setup, and it leaves Jones with no flicker of freedom either, since beginning to choose otherwise never issues in actually doing otherwise.

There are two objections the arguement needs to meet head-on rather than wave away. First, if Jones really could have begun to choose otherwise, and each replay is a fresh indeterministic decision, couldn't the loop in principle repeat forever, so that nothing in the setup actually guarantees Black's preferred outcome? And if Black instead rigs the replays so that Jones must eventually comply, hasn't the old determinism worry just crept back in through the side door? Second, does being able to start choosing otherwise but never being able to finish count as an alternative possibility worth the name, or is a stifled beginning no alternative at all? I definately want the argument to take a clear stand on both points rather than hedge.
