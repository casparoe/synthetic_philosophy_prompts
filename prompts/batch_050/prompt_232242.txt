I've been reading Douglas Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop and thinking about the idea that a self is not a thing but a pattern: a symbol a system gradually builds for itself out of its own activity, with selves coming in degrees rather than being all-or-nothing. A friend of mine has strong views on this, and over the past year I've been tallying her verdicts on whether various systems have an "I" in this sense, even a faint one:

- a thermostat: no
- a video camera pointed at the monitor that displays its own feed, producing infinite regress: no
- a self-driving car that maintains a detailed real-time internal model of its own position, damage state, and route: no
- a dog: yes, modestly
- a newborn human: yes, barely
- an ant colony taken as a whole: yes, faintly

Now we've hit a case we genuinely disagree about, or at least can't settle: a large language model given a persistent memory of its past conversations and a running written self-description that it can read and revise, where what it says about itself feeds back into how it subsequently behaves. I'd like you to predict her verdict, but I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the reasoning: name two or three different rules that would each reproduce all six of her past verdicts, state what each rule says about this language model, tell me which rule she is most likely running and why (I'd note the ant colony verdict seems telling to me, but I can't articulate why), and propose one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish her rule from its strongest competitor.
