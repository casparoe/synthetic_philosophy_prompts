Is there a name for this? When I think about the billions of years before I was born, during which I didn't exist, it honestly doesn't bother me at all. Zero dread. But the billions of years after I die feel awful to contemplate. At first I assumed this is just the standard thing where death is bad because it robs you of future goods, while pre-birth nonexistence robs you of nothing. But then I noticed the asymmetry survives cases where nothing is taken away in either direction: if a doctor told me I'd spend the next 40 years in a coma and then wake up and live normally, that prospect horrifies me, whereas being told I actually spent 1930-1970 in some coma-like state before "really" being born doesn't bother me -- even though in both cases it's 40 unexperienced years I could otherwise have had. So the badness seems glued to the direction in time, not to deprivation per se. My question: is this just an irrational time-bias, and if so does it have a name in the literature? Or is there a serious position that treats the asymmetry as legitimate and builds it into why death is bad -- something distinct both from the usual "death deprives you of goods" line and from the Epicurean "you won't be around to mind, so it can't harm you" line? If it does turn out to be a bias, does that actually rescue the Epicurean symmetry argument, or is that a further step?
