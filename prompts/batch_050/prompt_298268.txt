I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on agency and AI alignment, and I've been asked to contribute a short piece to our department's graduate workshop on "designing artificial agents." Rather than write another position paper, I want to stage the central dispute of my dissertation chapter as a dialogue. The dispute is between two design philosophies for advanced AI systems. On one side, approval-directed design: the system is trained to take whichever action an informed human overseer would rate most highly, where the overseer may inspect not only the action but the reasoning and intermediate steps behind it. The system's objective is, in effect, "do what the overseer would approve of upon reflection." On the other side, goal-directed design: the system is given an objective defined over states of the world (say, maximize verified scientific output, or keep a factory's inventory balanced) and is rewarded for outcomes, not for how its behavior looks to anyone watching.

Write a dialogue between two equally competent advocates of these designs -- call them whatever you like -- who are both engineers-philosophers at the same lab, sitting down to decide which architecture to use for a forthcoming autonomous research assistant that will plan experiments, order equipment, and write up results with minimal supervision. I want genuine back-and-forth, not two alternating monologues: each character must engage with the other's actual last point before raising a new one. Concretely, I want each side to (a) present the strongest failure mode of the opposing design as a specific, concrete scenario involving the research assistant -- for the goal-directed side, think about what a sufficiently clever approval-seeker does when the overseer's approval can be influenced by the system itself; for the approval-directed side, think about what a capable goal-pursuer does when the specified objective diverges from what anyone actually wanted -- and (b) respond seriously to the failure scenario raised against their own view, whether by biting a bullet, offering a patch, or showing the patch merely relocates the problem. Also have them engage at least once with the deeper question of whether an approval-directed agent is even an "agent" in the same sense a goal-directed one is, or whether it is better understood as an extension of the overseer.

Neither character should be a strawman: no lazy dismissals, no obviously bad arguments planted for easy refutation, and each should concede at least one real point to the other over the course of the exchange. The dialogue should end with honest, well-diagnosed disagreement -- ideally with both characters agreeing on what the crux of their disagreement is, even though they still disagree. Aim for roughly twelve to eighteen exchanges. Please write the whole thing in American English.
