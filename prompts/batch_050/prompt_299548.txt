I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've lucked into a one-semester visiting fellowship at a philosophy department. I have to hand in a proposal for a term paper (about 6000 words) and I want to write about something that's bugged me ever since I covered an AI research conference. Solomonoff induction gets sold constantly -- by researchers, by rationalist-adjacent blogs, by everyone -- as the result that finally vindicates Occam's razor: prefer the shortest program that outputs your data, and you can prove you'll home in on the truth. But as I understand it, the whole construction depends on fixing a universal Turing machine to define "shortest," and the invariance theorem only says that two machines' complexity measures differ by a constant. That constant is no comfort at all with finite data, since it can swamp your entire dataset. So my question: does Solomonoff actually justify preferring simple hypotheses, or does it just relocate the simplicity bias into the choice of reference machine and call it objective?

Write me the proposal. State the question so it has a determinate answer (not "is Occam's razor good?" but something checkable), say briefly why the usual replies -- "the constant washes out asymptoticaly" and "just pick a natural machine" -- leave it open, describe the approach you'd take and why that one rather than the obvious alternatives, sketch what a conclusion would look like in either direction and what would count as the project failing, and give an honest read on whether this fits in one semester.
