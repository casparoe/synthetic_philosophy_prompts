I'm a chemical engineer who reads philosophy of science on the side, and I keep running into a pattern I can't find the right word for. Two instances first, then the puzzle.

Instance one: near their critical points, fluids with wildly different molecular makeups -- water, carbon dioxide, liquid helium mixtures -- behave identically in certain respects, obeying the same numerical relationships in how properties like density and correlation length scale as the critical point is approached. My textbooks say the molecular details somehow wash out at these points, and there is even a well-developed mathematical story explaining why they wash out and why very different systems end up grouped together.

Instance two: the same control scheme -- an error signal, a gain, a feedback loop -- shows up in a housefly's flight stabilization, in the thermostat on my wall, and in autopilot software I once helped maintain. The useful explanations are written entirely at the level of the loop: they say what the system will do given a disturbance, and they kept being true when components were swapped for physically quite different ones.

What strikes me is not that the low-level details are complicated or unknown. It's that the high-level story keeps working while the low level is replaced wholesale -- and, here is the part that really puzzles me, the low-level theory itself can often be used to prove that the replacement won't matter. So the independence of the high-level explanation is not a case of our being unable to trace things downward. The downward tracing is exactly what establishes the independence.

People tell me the word for this neighborhood is emergence, but every definition of emergence I can find is built around what cannot be derived from or predicted by the lower level. My cases seem to be the opposite: everything about the high-level behavior is derivable in each particular instance, and yet the explanation plainly lives at the high level. Nobody explains the fly's recovery from a gust by simulating its neurons, and it seems to me that doing so would explain less, not more, because it would bury what the fly shares with the thermostat.

So, three questions. First, does this specific configuration -- explanatory independence that is demonstrated from below rather than arising from undeducibility -- have an established name of its own, distinct from emergence on one side and from the mere fact that one functional kind can be built out of many materials on the other? Please don't just hand me a label; tell me how the nearest candidate concepts differ from what I described and which fits best. Second, if nothing established fits exactly, coin something, with a definition and boundary cases: does it cover thermodynamics, where the high-level laws are derivable only under heavy idealization, and does it cover cases where the derivation merely hasn't been done yet rather than having been done? Third, once the low-level theory has explained why the details don't matter, is there still a substantive sense in which the high-level science is autonomous -- or has the derivation quietly reclaimed all the territory, leaving the high level as nothing but a convenient summary?
