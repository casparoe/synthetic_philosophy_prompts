The AI control literature claims we can stay safe even if a model is actively misaligned: untrusted outputs get reviewed by trusted monitors, suspicious actions are audited or resampled, and overall safety comes from the scaffolding rather than from the model's intentions. If that's right, though, alignment looks redundant — a scheming model inside a well-designed control protocol should be exactly as safe as an honest one, since in neither case do we rely on what the model wants. When safety researchers respond that control will break down once models get more capable, that seems to concede that control is a temporary patch rather than a principled answer, and that current control evaluations are only informative because today's models happen to sit below the capability level where monitoring fails. So is there actually any in-principle reason to keep pursuing alignment once control is in place, or is the entire case for alignment just the empirical bet that we can't verify which side of that capability threshold a given model is on?
