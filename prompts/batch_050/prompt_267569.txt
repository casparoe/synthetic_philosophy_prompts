my roommate is doing an ethics minor and last night he cornered me with an argument he says proves numbers never matter in rescue cases — not just the taurek thing where flipping a coin is allowed, but that saving the greater number isn't even better. i've numbered it so we can fight about it precisely:

(1) In a rescue dilemma, every person at risk has a claim on you to be saved, and the strength of each claim depends only on what that person stands to lose (their life), not on anything about the other people around.

(2) Claims don't merge: five claims, each grounded in one person's own prospective loss, don't add up into a single claim five times as strong, because there is no extra person — "the group" — who would suffer a loss five times as bad as one death.

(3) So when you can save either one stranger or five strangers, all at equal risk and with equal stakes, neither side has a stronger claim on you than the other.

(4) When neither side has a stronger claim, neither outcome is better than the other as far as your reasons go, since in these cases your reasons just are the claims.

(5) So saving the five is in no way better than saving the one, and you have literally no reason to prefer it.

(6) Nothing in (1)–(4) mentions how many people are on either side, so the same goes for any numbers: one against a thousand, one against the entire rest of humanity.

(7) It also goes for cases where the same person is on both sides. Say you can save Ann alone, or save Ann plus four others. The four others' claims don't merge into anything bigger (by 2), and Ann's claim is satisfied either way, so saving Ann-plus-four is no better than saving Ann alone. Flipping a coin between the two boats is perfectly fine.

(8) But (7) is completely insane. Obviously you save the boat with Ann plus the four. So something above is wrong, except every step felt fine to me at 1am.

tell me where it breaks. i'd really like to keep (2) — the point that there's no sixth person who suffers the fivefold loss honestly seems right — and (1) seems close to undeniable. so if the problem is in (3)–(7), name the exact step, explain why it looked valid but isn't (or if it is valid, which premise is cheapest to drop and what dropping it costs). then tell me what the repaired view should say about the original case, one stranger against five with no overlap: is the coin flip still permissible there, or does fixing (7) force me to say you must save the five every time?
