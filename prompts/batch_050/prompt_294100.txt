Okay so I need a wedding toast. I'm the father of the bride. Here's the situation: my new son-in-law, Daniel, is a behavioral economist, and when he proposed he did it with a plain silver band and a little speech about how diamond engagement rings are a socially wasteful arms race and he'd rather put the money in an index fund. My daughter said yes, which honestly still surprises some of the relatives. Half the wedding guests are his econ colleagues who think this was the most romantic thing ever. The other half are my side of the family, several of whom pulled me aside tonight to ask, in varying tones, whether Daniel is cheap.

Write the toast (4-5 minutes out loud). I want to tease him a bit -- that's the job -- but I also want to actually engage with the idea rather than just do the standard "my daughter could've done better" bit. Because here's the thing I've been turning over: the classic defense of the expensive ring is precisely that it's costly and hard to fake, that the waste is the point, it's how you prove you're serious. So Daniel's move could be read as refusing to signal commitment at all. But I don't think that's right -- I want the toast to find the real way in which what he did was costly (a guy whose whole professional identity is built on this argument can't quietly upgrade later without humiliating himself, for one). Work the costly-signaling logic through properly, for both halves of the room, without turning it into a lecture or name-dropping any academics. Land it as warm. And end on the index fund joke, I've been saving it.
