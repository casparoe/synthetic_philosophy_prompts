I'm in eleventh grade and part of my school's economics club, and this week we've been working through the topic of information cascades and rational herding, using the classic setup where people choose sequentially between two restaurants and later arrivals rationally follow the crowd even against their own private signal. I'm now stuck on what seems to me like a flat contradiction in how the material is presented, and I'd like it resolved before I have to present on it next week.

Here's my reasoning. In the cascade model, every agent is a perfect Bayesian: each one weighs their own signal against the observed history of choices and picks the option with the highest posterior probability of being right. So every single choice in the chain is, by construction, the individually rational choice. But rational choice just means maximizing expected accuracy given all available information. So if everyone in the sequence is doing exactly that, the group as a whole must be aggregating information at least as well as independent voters would, since each person sees strictly more information than they would on their own. It seems to follow that a cascade should be at least as reliable as the wisdom of crowds, and presumably more so.

Yet every explanation I've read insists that cascades are fragile, that they can lock onto the wrong option almost immediately, and that they're a kind of informational market failure. I don't see how all three claims can be true at once. If each follower is rationally incorporating the choices they observe as evidence, then more participants should mean more information in the system, not less, and errors should wash out as the sequence grows long. So my question is: isn't the standard claim that cascades are informationally wasteful actually incoherent, given that cascades are defined as situations where everyone responds optimally to the information available? If the textbook conclusion is right, where exactly does the information get lost, and why doesn't the rationality of each individual step rule that out?
