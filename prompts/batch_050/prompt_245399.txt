I'm helping design a governance framework for a proposed national agency that would license the training of very large AI systems, much as nuclear facilities are licensed. To defend the framework, my team keeps reaching for three stock historical analogies: nuclear weapons, the printing press, and the Industrial Revolution. I suspect these analogies differ a lot in how much argumentative work they can actually do, and I'd like you to test that.

Please rate each analogy from 0 to 10 on each of the following two criteria, so six scores in total:

1. Mechanism match: how closely the causal structure of the historical case (what made it dangerous or transformative, how the risk propagated, who could control it) resembles that of transformative AI, as opposed to superficial resemblances like "it changed everything."
2. Preemption track record: how much the historical case actually teaches us about regulating a technology before its harms are fully realized, including whether early restrictive measures worked, backfired, or were simply never tried.

Then, on the basis of those scores, tell me which single analogy my team should build its public case around and which one we should retire entirely, even if it is rhetorically the most striking. If you find that a criterion rewards an analogy you nonetheless think is misleading, say so and explain where the scoring breaks down.
