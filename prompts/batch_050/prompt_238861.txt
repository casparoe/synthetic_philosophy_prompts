A friend and I just spent an evening arguing about standing to blame, and we got stuck on a case neither standard position seems built for. Consider someone who, years ago, committed exactly the wrong she now condemns in another — but she was never caught, never punished, never even confronted, and she has since genuinely repented and changed her ways. My friend insists she has full standing to blame: her repentance shows the condemnation is sincere rather than self-serving, and blame from someone who knows the wrong from the inside is arguably better informed. I insist her standing is at least diminished: she escaped all the costs — punishment, apology, restitution — that her blame now implicitly demands the other person bear. Write an essay adjudicating this dispute. In particular, examine whether the usual arguments that hypocrites lack standing (that blaming involves claiming a kind of moral authority, or issuing a demand one is not oneself willing to honor) actually extend to the reformed-but-unpunished case, or whether they only condemn the currently guilty hypocrite; and consider whether requiring the reformed blamer to first confess or make amends restores her standing or instead confuses the conditions of blaming with the conditions of being blameless. Take a definite position — full standing, diminished standing, or no standing — and defend it against the strongest objection from the losing side.
