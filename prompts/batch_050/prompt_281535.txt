For my undergraduate seminar on ethics under uncertainty, I need a satirical piece that would work as a handout the week we discuss consequentialist cluelessness. Write a deadpan parody in the form of an official memo plus FAQ from an invented campus body, the Office of Total Consequences, announcing that every teaching decision must henceforth be assessed for its effects on all causal descendants until the heat death of the universe. About 850–1000 words, in a polished administrative voice that never winks, formatted as numbered sections followed by questions and answers.

Invent and define three bureaucratic instruments inside the piece: a Ripple Budget limiting how much unknowable downstream influence any one lecturer may spend; a Shadow of Unknowability coefficient assigned to actions whose expected value changes sign depending on unsettled empirical and normative facts; and a Pre-Commitment Amnesty for policies whose justification depends on claiming to know that future people will endorse them. Make the target a practice rather than a named person: the use of cluelessness sometimes as an all-purpose solvent for responsibility, and sometimes as a license for pseudo-precise long-range managerial confidence. The humor should come from getting the philosophy right: distinguish ordinary risk from deeper cluelessness, let the symmetry or canceling-out worry appear in procedural form, show why more data does not automatically wash the problem away, include the fanaticism pressure created by enormous stakes, and make at least one joke depend on the performative tension of an office issuing rules whose own long-run value it cannot know.

The satire must be committed: by the end a reader should be able to state the definite verdict it is pushing, roughly that cluelessness neither licenses doing whatever feels intuitively good nor justifies paralysis dressed up as rigor. Keep it legible to classmates who have not read the specialist literature; explain any technical term you use within the piece, and do not include quotations, citations, or a bibliography. After the piece, add a 120-word accuracy note saying where it exaggerates fairly, where it simplifies for comic effect, and which contested interpretation of cluelessness it leans on.
