A hobbyist question about infographics. I've noticed that charts showing amounts of stuff come in two styles. Style A: one icon of the thing, drawn bigger or smaller -- a huge cow for a country with lots of cows, a tiny cow for a country with few. Style B: the icon always stays the same size and the chart just shows more or fewer of them, ten little cows versus three little cows, usually with a rule like one cow = one million cows. In style B you can literally count the symbols, whereas in style A you have to judge how much bigger one shape is then another, which I'm pretty sure people are bad at (an icon that "looks twice as big" usually isn't, if you measure it). Then there's a third thing I see everywhere, a grid of identical icons with some filled in and some greyed out, like 7 out of 10 people -- no idea if that counts as style B or is its own thing. So: does the A vs B distinction have an established name, and is there an actual theory or argument for why one is better, or is it just a matter of taste? The "never stretch the symbol, repeat it" rule feels too specific to be an accident, like somebody at some point had real reasons for it. I'd also like to know where the greyed-out grid fits -- same idea, or does it solve a different problem? Not looking for a textbook glossary entry, I want to understand what the principle actually is and where it breaks down.
