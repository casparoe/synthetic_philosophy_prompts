I sit on the oversight board of a regional ambulance service, and we must certify one of two reasoning modes for a new AI dispatch system. In rule mode, the system executes a fixed priority code: calls are ranked by triage category, the nearest available unit is assigned, and a unit once dispatched is never diverted. In optimization mode, the system continuously re-solves the entire allocation problem: it can divert an ambulance already en route to a lower-priority patient when a more urgent call arrives, and it pre-positions crews in areas where it predicts calls are likely to occur. Simulations indicate that optimization mode would save meaningfully more lives per year, but it does so partly by occasionally abandoning patients the rules would have protected, its pre-positioning systematically lengthens response times in low-call-volume neighborhoods, and some of its decisions can be explained to affected families only by appeal to aggregate statistics. Draft the written opinion I will circulate before the board votes: it should recommend one mode, give reasons a dissenting member could actually engage with, and address directly whether it is permissible for a machine to make trade-offs we would never permit a human dispatcher to make — including what we should say to a family whose parent died because their ambulance was reassigned mid-route.
