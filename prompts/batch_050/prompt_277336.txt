I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, building a small command-line flowchart for a reading group that just finished the property chapter of Locke's Second Treatise. I need the decision tree that powers it.

The tree should take a user who accepts only that some resources are initially unowned and that people can come to own things, and route them to a named position on the legitimacy of original acquisition. Please build the nodes from the logical space rather than from a list of labels: first work out which questions genuinely separate the positions, then locate the positions among the possible answer combinations.

Constraints:

- Every branch node must be a single question a user can answer without knowing any terminology — no asking whether they endorse the Lockean proviso by name. My hunch is that the real fault lines are (a) whether mixing one's labour with a thing generates any moral claim at all, or whether title is purely a creature of convention and positive law; (b) whether appropriation requires others' consent or can be unilateral; (c) whether the requirement to leave enough and as good for others is a test of welfare effects or of opportunities to appropriate; and (d) whether violating that requirement voids the title or merely triggers a duty of compensation. If you think I've misidentified the fault lines, say so and explain.
- The leaves must include: the weak-proviso historical-entitlement view associated with Nozick; an egalitarian or left-libertarian reading on which the proviso demands an equal share or ongoing compensation; a strong reading on which acquisition that leaves others worse off is simply void; the conventionalist view that pre-legal acquisition generates nothing; and any position that finds the labour-mixing idea itself incoherent, as dramatized by the objection that pouring a can of tomato juice into the ocean would not make the ocean one's property.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate, and note any answer-combination that is unoccupied, with one sentence on why (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored).
- Finally, test each leaf against one concrete case of your choosing (for instance, enclosing the last reliable water source in a region) and report in a sentence whether the leaf yields a determinate verdict on it.
