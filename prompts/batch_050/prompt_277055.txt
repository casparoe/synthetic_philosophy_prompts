I run the grants program for a small community arts foundation (budget ~$400k/yr, about 30 proposals a cycle, decisions made by me plus a rotating panel of five volunteers who are mostly not art people). Every cycle we get the same fight. Half the panel thinks art with an explicit political message is automatically better because it "does something"; the other half quotes "art for art's sake" at me and wants to fund whatever is technically best regardless of content. Meanwhile the actual proposals are a mess of cases neither slogan handles.

Someone suggested I read W.E.B. Du Bois's 1926 essay "Criteria of Negro Art" (published in The Crisis, originally his speech at the Spingarn Medal ceremony for Carter G. Woodson), and this passage stuck with me:

"You and I have been breasting hills; we have been climbing upward; there has been progress and we can see it day by day looking back along blood-filled paths. But as you go through the valleys and over the foothills, so long as you are climbing, the direction -- north, south, east or west -- is of less importance. But when gradually the vista widens and you begin to see the world at your feet and the far horizon, then it is time to know more precisely whither you are going and what you really want."

And later in the same essay he describes the goal as a world built by "the Seeing Eye, the Cunning Hand, the Feeling Heart."

Here's what I want: turn this essay into an actual decision procedure my panel can run on any proposal in about 20 minutes of discussion. The questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure just stops with a fund/don't-fund, and where it has to hand things back to a human judgment call because the question is about values rather than anything checkable. Some things I specifically need it to handle:

- Du Bois seems to say all art is propaganda anyway, so "this piece is political" can't by itself be a reason for or against. But then what IS the relevant test? Operationalize it -- what do we actually look at in a proposal to answer it?
- The passage above suggests the right answer depends on where the community is in the climb, not just on the artwork. How do we assess "where we are" without it becoming an excuse for panel members to just assert their priors? Give me something concrete here, even if imperfect.
- The Seeing Eye / Cunning Hand / Feeling Heart bit -- can each of those become a scorable criterion a non-expert panelist can apply? If one of them can't survive translation into a checklist item, say so and say why rather than forcing it.

Then run the finished procedure on three hypothetical proposals: (1) a technically stunning realist portrait series with zero political content, by a young artist who just wants to master the craft; (2) a blunt protest mural with mediocre technique that half the panel will love and half will find preachy; (3) a well-made documentary aimed at winning over suburban white audiences that soft-pedals its subject to stay palatable. I suspect the procedure will tell me to fund (1) and give (3) a harder time than my board will want to hear -- if so, don't sand that down, show me the working.

Also tell me honestly where this procedure will misfire, and where Du Bois's 1926 context (a fighting civil rights organization deciding what its artists owe the movement) stops mapping onto a 2020s grant panel, because I don't want to lean on his authority for decisions he'd probably recieve as none of his business.
