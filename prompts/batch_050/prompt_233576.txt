I'm doing a module on empire and liberal political thought, and for my assessed essay I want to put Forster's A Passage to India up against Mill's chapter on the government of dependencies in Considerations on Representative Government. The bit I keep circling is the argument in chapter 18, where Mill says that rule by the mother country is at best a shaky arrangement because the rulers are answerable to opinion back home rather than to the people actually governed, that even well-meaning officials tend to govern in ignorance of what their subjects think and feel, and that the best case for the whole setup is as a kind of tutelage preparing a people to govern themselves eventually. I'm paraphrasing rather than quoting, so do feel free to correct me if I've got him wrong.

What I want is a reading of the novel that tests that argument scene by scene. Mill's worry is essentially epistemic, that a ruling caste can't really know the ruled, and Forster seems to dramatise exactly that: the Bridge Party, the "seeing the real India" chat, and above all the Marabar Caves, where the one thing Adela can't stand is not knowing what actually happened. And yet Mill would presumably have read the Turtons and Burtons as the failure of particular officials, not of the system, whereas Fielding's whole tragedy is that even the decent individual gets crushed by his position once the machinery turns (the way the club closes ranks after the accusation). So does the novel refute Mill's tutelage story, fulfil its diagnosis, or change the subject entirely, say by suggesting the problem isn't ignorance that better administration could fix but something in the structure of the encounter itself? Ronny's trajectory interests me here too, since he arrived as someone likeable and became a typical Anglo-Indian, which looks like evidence about what the system does to character.

Please keep it to 600 words maximum, organised however you like, but end by saying in a sentence or two whether you think Forster, writing sixty-odd years after Mill, is answering him or just describing a world Mill's categories can't reach.
