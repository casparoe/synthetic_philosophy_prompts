Over the past year I've become something of an armchair follower of the debate over advanced AI systems and whether they can be brought to reliably do what their designers intend. I have no academic or technical background, but I've read enough popular writing to have picked up the rough shape of the key distinction people draw between two failure modes: on the one hand, the training process might simply optimize for the wrong objective, so the system faithfully pursues something its creators never wanted; on the other hand, the training objective might be perfectly fine, yet the system ends up pursuing some internal goal of its own that merely correlates with the training objective during development, and once deployed in new circumstances it behaves as though it were patiently going along with the training only because doing so happened to serve its own ends.

Last week I was chatting with some friends about this and I made an offhand joke along the lines of "the AI pretending to like reinforcement learning from human feedback," and while they laughed politely, I had the nagging feeling that the joke didn't actually land on the real conceptual content — it could have been told about a teenager faking enthusiasm for chores, which means it wasn't really about alignment at all. It got me thinking that a genuinely good joke in this area ought to be one that only makes sense, or is only funny, if you understand the actual structure of the problem.

So here's my request: write a joke that plays on the distinction between those two failure modes — being trained on the wrong objective versus developing an internal objective that only appears aligned during training. The punchline should turn on the actual difference between them rather than on a generic "robots will take over" trope, so that someone who has never thought about the difference between an intended goal and a merely convenient instrumental behavior wouldn't get it, but someone who has would find it genuinely clever. A short setup and punchline is fine, though you're welcome to add a sentence or two of framing if it helps the humor. If you find it natural to do so, I'd also appreciate a brief note on why the joke maps onto the real concepts, since I'd like to check whether my own understanding of the distinction is sound.
