Is there an established name for a pattern I keep running into? While helping catalogue a county record office's holdings, I noticed that the better documented a powerless group is, the less the records are really about them: labourers appear in wage books and assize depositions, servants in their employers' correspondence, colonised subjects in district officers' reports — always slotted into the categories of whoever needed to count, tax, marry, or prosecute them. The strange part is the asymmetry. Whenever such people acted in ways the record-keepers had no form for, nothing survives at all, so the archive is fullest precisely where their lives were most thoroughly managed, and emptiest where they were most their own. I know historians speak of reading against the grain, but that names a remedy rather than the condition. Does the condition itself have a name? How does it differ from ordinary archival silence, where records were simply never kept or were lost? And if nothing in the literature quite fits, what would you call it, how would you define it, and where would you draw its edges?
