So I was a physical chemist for fourty years and have only got into philosophy since retiring, which is my excuse for what is maybe a naive question. My son made me watch Tenet and I can't stop turning it over. The film has people and objects 'inverted' so that their entropy runs backwards and they travel through time in reverse, and the plot leans hard on that premise. But the philosophy of time stuff I've been reading since (Boltzmann, the Past Hypothesis, the point that we have records and memories of the past but not the future) suggests the direction of time just is the direction of entropy increase, nothing more. So I'd like a reading of the film that takes both the thermodynamics and the philosophy seriously. Where does the film's picture of inversion match what a Boltzmann-style account would actually imply -- would a genuinely inverted person remember the future rather than the past, and is that anything like what the film shows? Where does it cheat, and does it know it's cheating? Relatedly, the ending seems to commit to a block universe where everything is already fixed; does that sit comfortably with an entropy-gradient account of the arrow, or does the film quietly need time's direction to be something extra over the pattern of events? And one thing that really bugs me: if the arrow is just a gradient, what would it even mean to invert a person as opposed to a whole region? No citations needed, just a careful reading that says where the film resists the philosophy as well as where it invites it.
