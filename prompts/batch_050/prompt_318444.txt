I'm teaching a seminar on Korean Neo-Confucianism and want a handout that gets my students past the textbook summary, so: write me a dialogue between Yi Hwang (Toegye) and Gi Daeseung (Gobong), imagined as the two of them finally hashing it out face to face after their long letter exchange on the Four-Seven question.

The core dispute has to be the real one. Toegye holds that Mencius's four sprouts (compassion, shame and dislike, deference, the sense of right and wrong) issue from principle/li with material force/ki merely following, whereas the seven feelings (joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hatred, desire) issue from ki with li just riding along on top. Gobong thinks this is one dualism too many: every feeling is ki issuing and li riding, full stop, and the Four are simply the morally salient slice of the Seven that Mencius singled out for teaching purposes, not a separate metaphysical pipeline.

Requirements:

- Neither of them is a dummy. Each has to respond to what the other actually just said, no parallel monologues.
- Toegye should press Gobong on why the Four are unfailingly good if they bubble up from the same morally neutral ki-process as greed or rage, and on why Mencius would bother drawing the distinction at all if it marks nothing real.
- Gobong should press Toegye on whether saying li *issues* anything smuggles activity into principle, which is supposed to be still and without doing, and on whether we now need a second Four-Seven split inside the Four themselves (is the compassion of a well-fed person the same issueing as the compassion of someone exhausted?).
- Partway through, have them test their views against two cases I've been using to annoy my students: (a) indignation felt on behalf of a total stranger wronged on the other side of the world -- it seems as morally pure as any sprout but isn't on Mencius's list, so is it a fifth sprout, one of the Seven (which one?), or a problem for both of them; (b) a flash of envy that, on reflection, turns into sincere admiration for the same person -- did the feeling's metaphysical source change halfway through, or just its object? Each thinker should actually commit to a verdict on both cases, even awkward ones.
- No neat resolution. End with each of them landing one blow the other visibly can't fully parry, and a short closing beat where they acknowledge what's still at stake (cultivation practice is a good place for this -- if the Four and Seven have different sources, do you cultivate them differently?).

Aim for something a student with one lecture of background could follow, but that doesn't sand off the genuine weirdness of the disagreement.
