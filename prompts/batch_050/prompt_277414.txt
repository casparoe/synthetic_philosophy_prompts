ok so i just got home from a like 3 hour argument with my friend Tom about Berkeley's master argument and im still annoyed. his whole thing was: try to picture a tree existing while nobody perceives it. you cant, because the very act of picturing it means you're conceiving it, so it is conceived, checkmate, matter is dead. i kept saying that's just sliding between "conceived by me right now" and "conceived by anyone ever" and he kept saying i was begging the question, and eventually the bartender asked us to wrap it up.

so here's what i want: write the master argument back at him, but as satire. specifically, a late-night infomercial script or a shady online product listing (pick one, or blend them) for a product called The Master Argument™ — the one weird trick that dissolves the entire material world, yours today while stocks last. around 600 words.

the jokes have to come from the actual argument, not a strawman of it, so take its real features and just exaggerate them:

- the argument only works if you yourself perform the conceiving, so the product requires active customer participation, and results may vary the second you stop concentrating
- it slides from "you can't conceive of something without conceiving of it" to "nothing can exist unconceived," and the ad copy should make that slide with total confidence, like the copywriter genuinely cannot see the join
- the continuity problem: stuff keeps existing when you leave the room, so the product needs an upsell — a premium subscription to a Divine Perceiver plan that keeps all your unperceived furniture and houseplants ticking over while you're out
- to be is to be perceived as the corporate slogan, trademarked, appearing on merchandise
- somewhere in the testimonials, a satisfied customer who says he used to kick stones to refute idealism but now simply kicks ideas

a philosophy undergrad who's actually read the Berkeley should laugh rather than wince, and Tom should ideally feel personally attacked but be unable to say exactly where the parody misrepresents the argument — because that's the part that stings. keep it deadpan, no winking at the camera, and whatever you do don't have the infomercial openly admit it's a scam. the humor is that it's a perfectly sincere, professionally produced ad for a product with a design flaw that the marketing department has failed to notice.
