Write an essay on the following controversy in the philosophy of biology, reaching a defended verdict rather than a survey of positions. In his intellectual autobiography, Karl Popper argued that Darwinism — the theory of evolution by natural selection — is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme: a fertile framework that generates testable theories without itself being open to falsification. Some years later, in a lecture on natural selection, he announced that he had changed his mind about the theory's testability and logical status, presenting this as a recantation of his earlier view. Interpreters disagree about what actually changed. On one reading, the recantation was genuine: Popper came to accept that natural selection is falsifiable after all. On another, the concession was largely rhetorical, since his original reasons — the apparent near-circularity of defining fitness in terms of survival, and the absence of any specifiable observation that would refute the theory as a whole rather than some particular application of it — were never directly answered, and the lecture's real claim is only that specific Darwinian hypotheses about particular adaptations, mechanisms, and phylogenies are testable. Your essay should (1) reconstruct the strongest version of Popper's original worry about testability; (2) assess whether that worry survives once fitness is decoupled from actual survival — for instance, by defining it in terms of expected reproductive success, or by treating adaptation claims as engineering-style hypotheses about design; (3) say what a satisfactory recantation would have had to establish; and (4) conclude whether the later lecture amounts to a genuine retraction, a mere clarification, or a quiet abandonment of the interesting claim. If you judge the recantation genuine, explain what becomes of Popper's science–metaphysics boundary in evolutionary theory; if you judge it hollow, explain whether the metaphysical-research-programme diagnosis remains defensible today, given how population genetics and adaptationist hypothesis-testing have developed since he wrote.
