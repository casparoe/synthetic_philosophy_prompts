I'm a PhD student in philosophy of biology and my advisor wants me to write up my "position" on adaptationism for our lab group, so people can attack it. Before I hand it around I'd like to know whether it even hangs together. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a methodological adaptationist: whatever the truth about how common adaptation is, the most productive way to actually study a trait is to start by asking what it might be for. Reverse engineering gets you hypotheses you wouldn't otherwise have.

(2) I think Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique basically won: you cannot infer that something is an adaptation from the fact that it looks well designed or does something useful. Apparent fit is compatible with the trait being a byproduct, a consequence of developmental constraint, or drift, so adaptation claims need independent evidence.

(3) I think drift is the right null hypothesis, and not just at the molecular level -- ideally for phenotypic traits too. No adaptation claim should be accepted unless drift has been ruled out.

(4) I think optimality modelling is the best available test of adaptation hypotheses: work out what the optimal trait value would be given the relevant tradeoffs, then check whether the observed trait matches it.

(5) I think most complex organs -- eyes, wings, echolocation, the vertebrate immune system -- are adaptations, full stop, and the "just-so story" worry barely applies to them.

(6) I hold a selected-effects account of function: a trait's function is whatever it was selected for, which is why I think spandrels genuinely have no function rather than a different one.

My sense is that some of these are in real tension and some only look like they are -- e.g. people keep telling me (1) and (2) conflict, but I'm not convinced. Go through the set, tell me which pairs actually pull against each other and which dissolve on inspection, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be. If one single revision would clean up most of the problems, tell me which one it is, but don't assume I want to give up adaptationism wholesale.
