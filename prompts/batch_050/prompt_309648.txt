I sit on the anomaly review board at a mid-size cell therapy company, and I need a decision memo I can circulate before friday's meeting.

Background. We grow therapeutic cells in batches, and any batch that fails QC gets flagged as a "deviation." Our SOP has a rule with teeth: if three deviations are judged to be instances of the same underlying mechanism, the production line shuts down for a full root-cause overhaul. Deviations with different mechanisms just get seperate tickets and we move on. So the question "is this the same mechanism as that one?" is, for us, a question with a six-figure price tag, and nobody has ever written down what "same mechanism" actually means.

The live case. Three failures last quarter:

1. Batch 118: a contaminated media reagent drove up expression of gene REX1, which blocked the cells from maturing. Batch failed.
2. Batch 131: a temperature excursion in the incubator *also* drove up REX1, same maturation block, identical failure signature on every downstream test we run.
3. Batch 140: the same contaminated reagent as batch 118, but this time it wiped out a subpopulation of support cells, and the batch failed with a totally different downstream profile.

Now do the math on the three-strike rule. If sameness tracks the upstream cause, 118 and 140 are a pair and 131 is its own thing — no shutdown. If sameness tracks the downstream pathway, 118 and 131 are a pair and 140 is seperate — no shutdown. If we require the whole causal chain to match end to end, none of them match anything, the rule can never fire, and we might as well delete it from the SOP. And if someone argues that 118+140 sharing a reagent plus 131 being "functionally equivalent" gets us to three anyway, I want to know whether that's principled or just reaching for the shutdown button.

Write the board's decision memo. It should:

- recommend a concrete criterion for "same mechanism" that reviewers can actually apply to these three batches, and reach a verdict on the shutdown question,
- engage seriously with the strongest version of the losing criterion — e.g., if you count by downstream pathway, take seriously the reply that this lets the same contaminated reagent keep poisoning batches forever under different ticket numbers; if you count by upstream cause, take seriously the reply that two totally different causes producing the identical failure mode is exactly the pattern a root-cause rule exists to catch,
- say something useful about the grain problem: any two events are the same mechanism at a coarse enough description ("perturbation disrupted culture") and different at a fine enough one ("this molecule bumped that molecule at 3:14 pm on a tuesday"), so the criterion has to pick a descriptive level and justify the pick rather than pretend there's a natural one,
- and address whether the criterion should be purely biological (whatever the science says the mechanism is) or partly pragmatic (whatever makes the three-strike rule do its regulatory job) — or whether that distinction even survives scrutiny, given that "the science" doesn't come pre-chunked into mechanisms.

Don't just gesture at "it depends on context." I need a bottom line the board can vote on. Skip the citations and lit review, I'd rather have the actual reasoning.
