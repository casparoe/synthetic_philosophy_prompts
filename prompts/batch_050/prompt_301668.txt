My friends keep saying dignity culture is obviously the grown-up option, honour culture is for feuds and group chats, and victimhood culture is just sympathy-farming with paperwork. I think that is too tidy. Please argue the less fashionable position that in large, anonymous, high-mobility societies a disciplined victimhood culture can be morally superior to both, because it turns harms that would otherwise stay private into testable public claims and creates precedents strangers can use. Treat the three as enforcement technologies rather than vibes: honour depends on audiences and retaliation, dignity on self-command plus reliable courts, victimhood on appealing to third parties when gossip and exit no longer discipline people. The hard bit is to make the case without ignoring moral inflation, pile-ons, jurisdiction-shopping and the incentive to perform woundedness; tell me which brakes would have to exist for your argument to survive, definately including what counts as costly testimony rather than a mere accusation.
