I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind working on self-models and metarepresentation, and this semester I'm running a small reading group on Hofstadter's account of the "I" as a self-referential symbol -- the idea in I Am a Strange Loop that a brain, by perceiving and categorizing its own activity, builds a symbol that refers back to the very system doing the perceiving, so that the loop between symbol and system is what a self is. For our final meeting I want to close with a poem, and I'd like you to write it: a sestina voiced by the "I" itself, understood as that symbol -- a pattern that is made of the brain's activity yet experiences itself as the thing doing the perceiving.

Use the following six end-words, in the standard sestina rotation: "I," "mirror," "signal," "level," "pattern," "down." The form should do real philosophical work, not just decorate the theme. The sestina's retrogradatio -- the same six words recurring in each stanza in an order derived from the previous stanza's last lines -- should enact the structure of the strange loop: the same materials endlessly re-entering at a new position, so that what was effect in one stanza becomes cause in the next, and the reader gradually loses track of which level is describing which. I'd like the word "down" to carry the puzzle of downward causation Hofstadter is fond of -- the way the "I" seems to push the neurons around even though, on the physical story, the pushing runs the other way. The envoi, where all six end-words must appear in three lines, should enact the loop closing: the moment the symbol turns and perceives itself perceiving, with no outside left to stand on.

A couple of constraints. Don't name Hofstadter or use the phrase "strange loop" anywhere in the poem; the structure should make the idea recognizable to someone who knows the view, and a reader who doesn't should still find it a good poem about what it is like to be a self. And keep it accurate enough that a specialist wouldn't wince: the "I" should come across as real in the way a pattern is real -- not an illusion to be debunked, and not a ghostly extra ingredient, but a high-level organization that gets causally tangled with the substrate that sustains it. If you find the form forcing you toward a version of the view Hofstadter would reject (a homunculus, a pure epiphenomenon), bend the poem, not the philosophy.
