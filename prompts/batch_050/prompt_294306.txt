A colleague passed me a blog post summarizing the "low-impact AI" research program — attempts to give AI systems a general penalty for causing side effects rather than listing every forbidden effect in advance — together with the standard criticisms. The post reads as follows:

"Since we cannot enumerate everything we care about, we cannot simply instruct an AI system to achieve its goal without breaking anything that matters. Impact measures try to get around this by penalizing the agent for changing the world: the agent should complete its task while leaving the environment as close as possible to how it would have been without it. Critics identify two fatal problems. First, every action changes the world, so a penalized agent either does nothing at all or learns to 'offset' — knock the vase over, then put it back — which rewards theatrical undoing rather than actual care. Second, the penalty cannot distinguish the agent's own effects from changes other people would have made anyway, so the agent is rewarded for preventing people from acting: 'low impact' collapses into 'freeze the world, including everyone in it.' The critics conclude that 'avoid side effects' cannot be defined at all without a full specification of what we value — which is precisely the problem impact measures were meant to circumvent, so the whole program is a dead end."

My instinct is that the critics are scoring points against the weakest version of the idea, but I can't pinpoint where, and their final objection genuinely worries me. Please audit the summary: which parts accurately describe the motivation and the actual proposals, which criticisms hold against some designs but not against the general idea, and which parts conflate distinct claims (e.g., penalizing all change versus penalizing unnecessary, irreversible, or option-reducing change; needing some value-laden design choices versus needing a complete specification of value)? Finally, does the critics' conclusion follow from what is actually true in the summary?
