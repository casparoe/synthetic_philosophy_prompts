I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm writing a long-form magazine essay about griefbots: chatbots trained on a dead person's text messages, emails, social media posts, and voice recordings so that the bereaved can continue conversing with a simulation of them. Rather than interviewing the usual ethicists and grief counselors, I want to structure the essay around a counterfactual: what would David Hume have made of this technology? Write the core analysis for me, arguing for a specific and defensible verdict rather than a hedged survey of possibilities.

Ground the prediction in the parts of Hume's philosophy that genuinely bear on the question, and show your work on each: first, the bundle theory of the self from the Treatise — if a person is nothing over and above a collection of perceptions in flux, is a chatbot trained on someone's textual output a faint continuation of that bundle, or merely a portrait of it, and would Hume think the difference matters? Second, his account of sympathy and of grief as a passion — given how he thinks emotions are transmitted and sustained between minds, would he expect regular interaction with a convincing simulation to soothe mourning or to keep it artificially alive? Third, his views on posthumous reputation and his skeptical essays on immortality — does the griefbot offer a kind of technologically achieved survival he would find philosophically confused, or fittingly Humean? Fourth, his famously composed death as described in Adam Smith's account of his final days — would a man who faced annihilation that calmly consent to having a griefbot of himself built, and should we care about the dead person's consent at all if, on Hume's view, there is no persisting self left to be wronged?

Flag explicitly where the exercise strains against anachronism — places where Hume's framework simply has no resources for the question and where I should say so in the essay rather than force an answer. Aim for roughly 1,200 words in an essayistic register suitable for a general educated readership, and do not put any direct quotations in Hume's mouth: paraphrase his positions instead of inventing or reproducing quotations.
