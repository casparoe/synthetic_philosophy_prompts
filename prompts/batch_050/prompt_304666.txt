The question (20 pts total): Consider two-player bargaining problems (S, d), where S is a compact convex comprehensive subset of R² and d is the disagreement point. (a) State Nash's four axioms and define the Nash bargaining solution. (5 pts) (b) Explain the objection to independence of irrelevant alternatives that motivates Kalai and Smorodinsky, and state the axiom they put in its place. (5 pts) (c) Define the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution. (5 pts) (d) Let S = {(u1, u2) ≥ (0,0) : u2 ≤ 4 − u1²} and d = (0,0). Compute both solutions for this problem and say briefly why they come out different or the same. (5 pts)

The student's answer:

(a) Nash's axioms are: Pareto optimality (no feasible point can make both players strictly better off than the solution), symmetry (in a symmetric problem both players get the same), invariance under positive affine transformations of either player's utility, and independence of irrelevant alternatives (if the solution of a problem remains feasible after shrinking the set, it stays the solution). The Nash solution is the point in S maximizing (u1 − d1)(u2 − d2) over points weakly above d.

(b) Kalai and Smorodinsky object that IIA has a perverse consequence: if the feasible set expands in a way that gives one player more available outcomes, IIA can force that player to end up worse off than before, since the solution can be pinned down by alternatives that haven't changed. They replace IIA with individual monotonicity: if the set expands so that every other player's best achievable outcome is unchanged, the player whose options improved should not end up worse off. Note that the KS solution still satisfies IIA on top of this, since removing alternatives is just a special case of the set shrinking, which monotonicity already covers.

(c) The Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is the maximal point of S where both players make equal utility gains relative to the disagreement point, i.e. the point where the Pareto frontier meets the 45-degree line through d.

(d) For the Nash solution, maximize u1·u2 on the frontier u2 = 4 − u1²: the objective is 4u1 − u1³, derivative 4 − 3u1² = 0 gives u1 = 2/√3 ≈ 1.155 and u2 = 8/3 ≈ 2.667. For KS, set u1 = u2 = t on the frontier: t = 4 − t², so t² + t − 4 = 0 and t = (√17 − 1)/2 ≈ 1.56, giving roughly (1.56, 1.56). The two solutions differ here because the frontier is curved; on symmetric problems they'd coincide.
