I work with archaeological material a lot and I keep noticing I have strong gut reactions about when we're allowed to infer what people *believed* or how they were *organized* from the stuff they left behind, and I can't tell if my reactions hang together. Here they are:

- A midden full of broken pots and animal bones: this tells us what they ate and used, nothing more. Fine, easy.
- Bodies buried carefully with tools, food, and ornaments: people often say this shows belief in an afterlife, but I don't buy it -- the goods might be for the mourners, a display of family standing. Verdict: doesn't license the afterlife inference.
- A huge stone circle that would take thousands of coordinated labor-hours: this *does* show some capacity for organizing lots of people, though I'd resist inferring kings or a coercive elite from it. Cooperation can happen without that.
- Swords deliberately bent and thrown into rivers, repeatedly, over centuries: here I actually do want to say something ritual is going on, since the objects are destroyed and unrecoverable. Though a sceptic could say it's just disposal.
- An infant buried with gold ornaments while most adults get nothing: this one feels rock solid for inherited, ascribed status -- a baby can't have earned anything, so wealth must attach to birth.
- The same distinctive pot style turning up across a huge region: trade or contact, sure, but I resist any inference to shared ideology or a unified culture.
- Hundreds of clay figurines in one house: people leap to "household cult" here and I really don't think you can -- they could be toys, decorations, anything.

The pair I can't reconcile myself is the baby grave versus the adult graves: in both cases I'm looking at deposition of valuables, yet I accept the inference to social structure in one and refuse the inference to belief in the other. And the stone circle vs. the pot distribution bothers me similarly. Is there a principled line here -- maybe something about which alternative explanations have to be ruled out before an inference is legit, or about functional explanations having priority over symbolic ones? State the best candidate principle precisely, say which clause each of my cases motivates, flag which verdicts it forces me to give up, and tell me what it would say about a case I haven't considered, like deliberately deformed skulls found across a whole valley.
