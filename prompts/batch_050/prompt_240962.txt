Our small coastal town has a climate assembly that votes on things like floodwalls, evacuation plans, and how to spend adaptation money. It isn’t a formal election, so we get to decide who counts as part of the community for these decisions. I’m on the admissions subcommittee, and I’m worried our past calls don’t add up to one clear rule.

So far we’ve decided:

- A renter who has lived here for 22 years gets a vote, even though she owns no property.
- An absentee landlord who owns two houses here but visits only a few weekends a year does not get one.
- A grocer who lives in the next town but has run a shop in the flood zone for 15 years does get one.
- An undocumented family living here gets votes; we said immigration status was irrelevant.
- University students who live here about eight months a year do not get votes, since most see this as temporary and keep their main homes elsewhere.
- A former resident who was displaced by the last hurricane, is renting inland for now, and is on the waiting list for rebuilt public housing does get one because she intends to return.

The new case is a nurse at our hospital. Her legal address is ninety minutes away, she has no plan to move here permanently, but she works four night shifts a week, rents a small room here so she can sleep between shifts, buys most of her groceries locally, and her kid attends school here. She says the evacuation routes and flood defenses affect her almost as much as they affect residents. She also admits she would probably leave if the hospital ended her contract.

Should we admit her if we want to stay consistent with our own precedents? Please don’t just give a yes or no. Identify two or three plausible rules that could explain all the earlier decisions, say what each rule implies about the nurse, and pick the decision you think coheres best. Also say which earlier case is the closest analog, which cases pull in the opposite direction, and whether the precedents genuinely underdetermine this. If they do, be honest about what broader priciple we’d be adopting by voting yes versus no. Finally, give me one hypothetical future case that would best reveal which rule we had really chosen.
