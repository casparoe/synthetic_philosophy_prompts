Two people in my orbit -- a moral philosopher and a forensic psychologist -- have spent a year arguing about whether psychopaths know that what they do is wrong. Write the joint statement they'd produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration between the two camps: the "no genuine moral knowledge" side (psychopaths can say the words "that's wrong" but lack whatever knowing-that-wrong actually consists in) and the "they know, they just don't care" side. I want: a numbered list of claims both can sign, about what psychopaths in fact do on moral/conventional distinction tasks, what their crime patterns and their language use show, and what none of that settles; then the exact residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording -- I suspect one is whether moral knowledge requires some motivational or emotional grip, and another is whether passing the moral/conventional task demonstrates knowledge or just fluent rule-tracking; then for each disagreement a crux, plus a concrete case or study design both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Have the statement also rule on what I'd call the parrot problem: does confident wrongness-talk from someone completely unmoved by it count as moral knowledge, and if the camps split on this, make them specify what observation or experiment would seperate them. And if it turns out part of the fight is just two different things being called "moral knowledge," the statement should say so and split the question accordingly rather than papering over it.
