I've spent enough years in the metaethics corners of LessWrong and adjacent forums to have the standard Frege-Geach presentation essentially memorized: the expressivist renders "stealing is wrong" as the expression of an attitude rather than a truth-apt proposition, and then owes us an account of what the conditional "if stealing is wrong, then getting your brother to steal is wrong" expresses, and of why the resulting modus ponens is a valid inference rather than an equivocation on "wrong." I also know the standard repairs — Blackburn's commitment-tied-to-the-inference approach, Schroeder's being-for construction and higher-order attitudes, Gibbard's consistency constraints on hyperplan-like states, and the minimalist-truth gambit of deflating the problem away. What I genuinely lack is a feel for how portable the problem is beyond ethics.

Give me an example of a non-moral domain in which an expressivist treatment would face a structurally analogous embedding problem. Candidate territories: epistemic normativity ("you should update on this evidence"), credence and probability talk, causal claims, epistemic modals, or conditionals themselves. For whichever domain you pick:

1. Write down the toy inference explicitly — the analogue of the "lying is wrong" modus ponens — indicating which premise is the conditional, what the expressivist says each occurrence of the target vocabulary expresses, and where the threat of equivocation arises.

2. Locate exactly where the problem bites: negation, embedding under conditionals, explaining validity, or someplace else. Does it bite harder or softer in your domain than in the moral case, and why?

3. Assess whether the standard repairs transfer. Does the domain's own machinery — formal semantics for modals, probabilistic coherence norms, the logic of conditionals — dissolve the problem, make it worse, or simply relocate it? One thing I'd particularly like addressed: whether expressivism about epistemic normativity faces a special self-application worry, since the expressivist's own theory is put forward as something we epistemically ought to accept.

I'm not asking for a literature survey or a final verdict on expressivism. I want one worked example, precise enough that I can judge whether Frege-Geach is really a problem about moral non-cognitivism in particular or about expressivist treatments of normative discourse as such.
