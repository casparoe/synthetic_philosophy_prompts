I'm a novelist working on a book set in first-century Palestine, and my friend Dave, who has read way too many historical Jesus books, keeps ruling on which bits of the gospels really happened. His verdicts so far: crucifixion under Pilate, definately happened; the guard posted at the tomb, invented; the saying about rendering to Caesar what is Caesar's, authentic; the Bethlehem birth, invented to fit prophecy; the teaching about loving your enemies, authentic; and the weird little miracle where Jesus withers a fig tree for not having fruit out of season, authentic, because why would anyone make that up.

New case: the words over the bread and wine at the Last Supper. Paul has a version decades before the gospels and Mark has a different-looking version, so it's early and independent twice over -- but it's also exactly what the young church needed, since they were already doing the ritual.

What does Dave say about this one? More than that: what rule is he actually running, which earlier verdict is the closest analog, and if his track record underdetermines this case, what principle is he committing to with each possible answer -- and what further gospel passage would tell the two rules apart? And honestly, I'm starting to wonder whether any rule like this can tell me who Jesus actually was, or only what the church found useful or awkward. Am I wrong about that?
