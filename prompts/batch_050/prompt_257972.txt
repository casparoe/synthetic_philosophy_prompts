Predict the mistakes these students will make in the order they'll make them: my guesses include "the probabilities aren't really 50/50," "you can't switch twice because you'd be using information from opening the envelope" (when I haven't said the envelope is opened), and "this is just the St. Petersburg paradox, so it's about infinite expectations." Structure your answer as a numbered sequence of teaching moves with the misconception interjections marked at the points where they belong, and lead with your bottom line in the first sentence -- the single most important sequencing decision and why -- before laying out the plan, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
