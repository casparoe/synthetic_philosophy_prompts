I'm a longtime reader of LessWrong and its surrounding blogs, and the Sequences are the closest thing I have to a philosophical formation. I think in the vocabulary of reductionism-as-done: gears-level models, causal diagrams, interventions, making beliefs pay rent, the map-territory distinction, and the general conviction that explaining a phenomenon means exhibiting the smaller parts whose interactions produce it.

I've recently encountered the "New Mechanism" literature in philosophy of biology (Machamer, Darden, Craver, and that whole tradition), and I want it translated into my vocabulary. As I understand it, the mechanists hold that explanation in the life sciences is not fundamentally about subsuming phenomena under laws (as the old covering-law model claimed) but about describing mechanisms: entities and activities organized such that they produce the phenomenon. They also seem to make a set of further claims that are harder for me to parse: that mechanisms form genuine levels; that constitutive relevance is established by a "mutual manipulability" criterion linking interlevel interventions and within-level interventions; that activities are not reducible to entities plus their causal properties (Machamer et al. apparently insist activities are a distinct ontological category); and that there is an important distinction between "how-possibly" and "how-actually" models.

Please do the following. First, restate this whole cluster of positions as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: causal Bayes nets and interventions, gears-level versus black-box models, reductionism, prediction-paying-rent, and (if it helps) the notion of abstraction as throwing away information. Second, give me a residue report: which mechanist claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no obvious counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I'm particularly suspicious of two candidate residues: the insistence that activities are ontologically basic rather than just the dynamics of entities, and the mutual-manipulability criterion for what counts as a genuine level of mechanism. On each, I want your verdict on whether the residue points to a real gap in the rationalist vocabulary (something the gears-plus-interventions picture genuinely cannot express) or whether it marks a place where the mechanists are being less clear than they think -- for instance, whether "activities" and "levels" are doing metaphysical work that dissolves once you ask what predictions they screen off. Keep the translation honest: don't smuggle in my framework's assumptions and then declare the translation a success.
