Write a nineteen-line villanelle about predictive processing whose two refrains are exactly “The world corrects the model” and “The model corrects the world.” Let the first refrain represent perceptual inference, in which sensory prediction error revises an internal generative model, and the second active inference, in which action changes the body or environment so that incoming sensations better fit the model. Use the refrains’ repeated returns to show how the same imperative to minimize mismatch can produce either flexible learning or a self-sealing refusal to revise. Include at least one passage in which precision weighting determines whether an unexpected sensation overturns a confident prediction or is dismissed as noise. By the final quatrain, the two refrains should meet not as rival doctrines but as complementary ways of resolving prediction error. Keep the concepts distinct: do not collapse surprise, uncertainty, prediction error, and free energy into one another. The poem should be formally musical and intelligible to an untrained reader, while knowledgeable readers should recognize why its refrains had to end side by side.
