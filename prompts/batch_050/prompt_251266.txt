Before I commit to an account of verisimilitude in my thesis, I want the specification written out first, since it seems to me most proposals in this literature fail for predictable reasons and I'd rather see the failure conditions up front. Write the desiderata any adequate theory of truthlikeness must meet, each with the case or result that motivates it. The list must at least cover: (1) ranking the familiar historical sequence of strictly false theories — Ptolemaic, Copernican, Keplerian, Newtonian astronomy — as increasing in closeness to the truth, without leaning on the later ones being true; (2) escaping the Tichý–Miller result that, on Popper's content-based definition, no false theory can be closer to the truth than any other; (3) not letting the ranking flip under an equivalent choice of vocabulary, which is what Miller's weather-style arguments show content definitions do; (4) handling numerical approximation — the temperature is exactly 12 degrees versus near 12 — and not just propositional error; and (5) coming apart from probability, since a tautology is maximally probable but no answer at all. Then tell me which of these desiderata you think are genuinely non-negotiable and which are the prejudices of one camp, whether any subset looks jointly unsatisfiable (and what would have to give), which single requirement knocks out the most existing contenders, and finally, briefly, how the content-based and likeness/similarity families fare against the whole list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than the scorecard.
