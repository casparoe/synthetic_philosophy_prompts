So I've been reading up on the endurantism/perdurantism debate and I keep running into a move I find suspicious, and I'd like you to defend my suspicion with a proper argument.

Here's the suspicion. The perdurantist's big selling point is that she solves the problem of intrinsic change without contradiction: the candle is straight at t1 and bent at t2, and there's no puzzle, because really it's one temporal part that's straight and a different temporal part that's bent, and nobody thinks one thing having different parts with incompatible properties is a contradiction (my left hand is cold, my right hand is warm, big deal). But I want to argue that this "solution" mostly just relocates the puzzle instead of dissolving it. The hard question was never really "how can incompatible properties show up in the career of one object" but "what makes it ONE object across the change," and the perdurantist still owes an answer to that: what glues the straight-stage to the bent-stage into a single worm? And whatever answer gets given (counterpart relations, continuity, immanent-causal connections, whatever), I suspect the endurantist can help herself to a structurally similar answer and get persistence just as cheaply, so the perdurantist's alleged advantage evaporates.

Please argue for this position as strongly as you can: that perdurantism's treatment of change doesn't earn it a real advantage over endurantism, because the explanatory work just gets shifted to the unity-of-stages question, where endurantists have parallel resources. Feel free to formalize bits of it if that helps.

A couple of ground rules for the answer. First, steelman the perdurantist before knocking her down -- I don't want to win against a strawman, and I know Lewis and Sider aren't idiots, so if the obvious version of my argument fails against the sophisticated version of their view, say so and tell me whether a repaired version of the argument survives. Second, please be upfront about what's actually settled in this literature versus what's still genuinely contested. If most working metaphysicians think some premise of mine is wrong, or think the whole framing of "who has the advantage" is misguided, I want to know that clearly, not have my view presented as the consensus. If the debate is just at a stalemate on some point, say its a stalemate.
