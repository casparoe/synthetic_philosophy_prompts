I'm a theist but I came out of a pretty devout family and watched my older brother lose his faith after years of genuinely seeking, so this topic is not abstract for me. Before I circulate mine, I want someone to check whether my positions actually fit together, because I have a nagging feeling they don't but I can't locate the problem. Here's what I currently hold:

(a) Classical theism: God is omnipotent, omniscient, and perfectly loving, and I think the right model for that love is the love of an ideal parent -- not a distant monarch or a cosmic accountant.
(b) Nonresistant nonbelief is real and not rare: there are sincere seekers and reluctant apostates (my brother) who find nothing, and it's not their fault.
(c) The intuition that makes hiddenness seem like a problem is basically correct: a loving parent doesn't stay hidden from a child who is calling out for them, and so much the worse for any theology that says otherwise.
(d) God's evidence is ambiguous on purpose, because a free, uncoerced response of love requires epistemic distance -- if God's existence were obvious, faith and love wouldn't be genuinely ours.
(e) The "dark night" stuff in the mystical tradition -- God withdrawing the felt sense of his presence even from devout believers, sometimes for years, for the sake of their growth -- is real and fully compatible with perfect love.
(f) Explicit belief isn't required for a right relationship with God; a decent atheist of good will can be related to God without knowing it.
