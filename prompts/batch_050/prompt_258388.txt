Okay so I run a small writing group out of my flat, five or six of us, mostly people messing around with alternate history and historical fiction, and it's become painfully obvious that none of us (me included, if I'm honest) actually knows how to think about counterfactuals. One guy's stories have a single tiny thing go differently and then literally nothing in twentieth century Europe happens remotely the same way, pure chaos, which makes his plots feel weightless. Another one writes "what if the assassination in Sarajevo failed" and then the war just happens anyway a couple years later, because momentum or whatever, like history is a train that climbs back onto its tracks no matter what you do to the driver. Both feel wrong and I can't quite say why, wich is the problem.

So next meeting I'm taking about 40 minuites to teach them how historians actually argue about whether an event was inevitable or contingent, because I want their plotting to have some discipline. Don't teach me the material, plan the teaching. Specifically: what's the first distinction that has to land before "was X inevitable" even parses as a question (my instinct is it's something like separating "could the past have gone differently at all" from "given the forces actually in play, was this outcome overdetermined," but tell me if that's the wrong place to start); the order the pieces have to come in and what each one depends on; the single historical case that should carry the whole evening, since I'm leaning toward the July Crisis 1914 and I want to know either why that's the right carrier or what beats it and why mine would mislead them; the wrong moves my lot will make in roughly the order they'll make them (butterfly-chaos nihilism, the "history is about facts, counterfactuals are party games" objection, hindsight making everything look fated in retrospect, the great man / Cleopatra's nose thing) and the exact point in the sequence where I should head each one off rather than let it fester; some teachable discipline for how much you're allowed to change in one counterfactual before it stops being a question and turns into fan fiction; one question to end on that shows me whether the idea actually landed; and what I should openly refuse to get into with this crowd at this level at all.
