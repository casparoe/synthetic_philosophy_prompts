I recently read Samuel Scheffler's Death and the Afterlife and ended up in a long argument with a friend about his "afterlife conjecture" — roughly, the claim that our confidence in humanity's survival after our own deaths matters much more to the value and point of our current activities than we ordinarily notice. Consider two precise versions of the claim, stated so as to be assessable. Version A (doomsday): if most people became convinced that a natural catastrophe would extinguish humanity thirty days after their own natural death, then a wide range of their projects — scientific research, art, political reform, even characteristically self-interested long-term pursuits — would lose much of their perceived point, beyond what the mere anticipation of others' deaths explains. Version B (infertility): if most people became convinced that no further humans would ever be born, while everyone now alive lived out a normal lifespan (as in P. D. James's scenario in The Children of Men), the same draining of significance would occur, roughly as severely.

Please give me your credence in Version A and in Version B, taken as empirical-psychological claims about how most people would in fact respond and feel, decomposed into the sub-questions your numbers rest on — for example: the probability that the effect survives reflection rather than being a first-glance intuition; the probability that it attaches to the continuation of humanity generally rather than to one's descendants, culture, or projects specifically; the probability that what it reveals is descriptive of actual valuing rather than a normative fact about what people should value; and any other terms you think belong. Give a number and one line of justification for each term, state how the terms combine, and report the two final credences. Then tell me which term each final number is most sensitive to, which of the two versions you think Scheffler's argument actually supports better if they come apart, and what evidence — psychological, cross-cultural, or thought-experimental — would move each credence most. Please keep the entire answer under 450 words.
