I administer a shared workshop and materials library run as a commons by a few dozen members, and disputes keep arising when someone claims private ownership of a common resource on the grounds that they "improved" it -- a member who repaired a broken lathe now calls it hers; another who sorted the scrap-metal pile wants to reserve the good pieces; a third annotated a shared dataset and now demands exclusive use of her version. We have no agreed rule for when such claims are legitimate. Please turn Locke's labour theory of acquisition, including the proviso that enough and as good be left for others and the condition against spoilage, into a decision procedure our committee can actually run on any such claim: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a denial, and where it must hand the decision back to the membership because the question is one of values rather than of what Locke's theory says. Be explicit about which of Locke's distinctions must survive translation into a checklist and which can be dropped, and about how the proviso should be assessed differently for rival goods like the lathe versus non-rival goods like the dataset. Then apply the procedure to the three cases above, including at least one where it yields a verdict the claimant will not like, and state plainly what kinds of claims it cannot settle.
