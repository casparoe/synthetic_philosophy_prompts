ok so my friend and i got into this argument in precalc like two weeks ago and neither of us has been able to end it, and its actually starting to bug me. we learned that you can build the natural numbers out of sets, and he turned it into this whole argument that i cant find the hole in. it goes:

(1) If the numbers are real objects, then any question about how they relate to each other has a definite answer, even if nobody knows it. Either 2 really is an element of 4 or it really isn't, same as either there's life on mars or there isn't.

(2) But mathematicians build the numbers in different ways. On the standard way (von Neumann I think its called), 4 is the set {0, 1, 2, 3}, so 2 literally is an element of 4. On the older way of doing it, 2 is not an element of 4. Both constructions prove the exact same arithmetic -- everything about addition, multiplication, primes, all of it comes out identical.

(3) If two ways of building the numbers prove all the same math, then nothing mathematical could ever settle which one is the "real" one. No proof, no experiment, nothing.

(4) So there is no fact of the matter about whether 2 is an element of 4. (from 2 and 3)

(5) So the numbers are not real objects. (from 1 and 4)

(6) So statements like "2 + 2 = 4" aren't really true the way "water boils at 100 degrees" is true. They're just conventions we made up and agreed to play along with, like the rules of chess or facts about harry potter.

The part i refuse to accept is (6). Physics works, bridges stay up, and it seems insane to me that if humans had never existed then 2 + 2 wouldn't have equaled 4. but every step before that looks fine to me when i stare at it. which step do i reject, and what does rejecting it actually cost me? like if i say numbers ARE objects, do i have to believe there's a secret true answer to whether 2 is in 4 that nobody can ever find out? that also sounds nuts.
