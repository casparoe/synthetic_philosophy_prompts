I got into an argument with my brother-in-law over Christmas about whether everyday conventions are actual moral obligations or just habits, and I realised I have strong gut verdicts on a bunch of cases but no idea what principle, if any, is behind them. (2) Not tipping in Japan, where tipping can come off as insulting: I feel equally obligated *not* to tip, and "but I'm just being generous by my own lights" doesn't move me at all. (3) Jaywalking: in my hometown everyone crosses on red when it's clearly safe and I do too, zero guilt. But when I visited Germany I felt like I should stand and wait at the empty intersection at 1am, even though the rule struck me as pointless. So convention-relative, apparently. (4) But queueing is different: cutting in line seems wrong *everywhere*, even in places where queuing norms are famously looser. I can't shake the feeling that the difference between (3) and (4) is real and not just me being inconsistent, but I can't articulate it.
