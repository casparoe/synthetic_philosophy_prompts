ok so I'm putting together my position statement for a grad seminar on moral particularism vs generalism and before I present it I want to know if the thing is even internally consistent. Here's where I've landed:

1. Reasons holism. I think Dancy is basically right that any feature of a situation can switch valence depending on context -- the fact that something is fun can count for doing it in one case and against it in another (fun of the lynch mob, etc). Nothing has its moral polarity built in.

2. But I also think there are a few bedrock reasons. Like, the fact that an action would cause serious avoidable suffering to an innocent person is always, in every possible context, at least a reason against doing it. I can't imagine a case where that's morally neutral or counts in favor.

3. My main argument for particularism (which I plan to lead with) is that every candidate moral principle anyone has ever proposed has counterexamples -- every "always wrong to X" gets some case where X is clearly fine -- so principles are at best rough rules of thumb and the idea of a true exceptionless moral principle is a myth.

4. I'm committed to moral supervenience: two situations that are identical in every non-moral respect can't differ in what's right in them. This seems non-negotiable to me, it's just part of what morality is.

5. Epistemically I'm with the particularists: what a morally mature person has is a kind of trained sensitivity to the salient features of the situation in front of them, not a stock of generalizations they apply. If someone needed to consult a principle to figure out that torturing a kid for fun is wrong, the problem isn't that they're missing a premise, something has gone wrong with them morally.

So: does this hang together? Go through the commitments pairwise-ish and tell me where the real tensions are and which ones only look like tensions. If something has to give, tell me which single revision would clean up the most and what it would cost me -- I don't want to hear "it depends," rank the problems by how severe they are. For context I'd rather keep 4 and 5 than the others but don't let that stop you from telling me if one of them is the actual problem.
