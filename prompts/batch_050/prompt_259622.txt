I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and our department is holding its annual "lightning round" social event next week, where grad students are expected to deliver short humorous bits about their research areas. My dissertation concerns the causal exclusion argument, so I need a joke that turns on its actual machinery rather than a generic gag about philosophers of mind staring at their own brains. For context: the worry (most associated with Jaegwon Kim) is roughly this — if the physical domain is causally closed, then every physical effect, such as my hand reaching for aspirin, has a sufficient physical cause; but if my headache is a genuinely distinct, non-reducible mental cause of that same reaching, it seems either causally otiose or the reaching is systematically overdetermined, which looks just as bad. Non-reductive physicalists try to keep mental causation without paying either price, and that squeeze is where I want the humor to live.

The joke should be intelligible and ideally funny to people who actually know this literature, meaning the punchline should exploit something like the exclusion principle itself, the closure of the physical, the specter of rampant overdetermination, or the awkwardness of claiming the mental is causally efficacious while "realizing" the physical without competing with it. A joke that merely signals that I've heard the word "supervenience" would be a failure; a joke where the punchline is itself a miniature exclusion argument, or where a mental event suffers the fate the argument assigns it, would be a success. Please avoid anything requiring obscure knowledge beyond what a first-year graduate student in the field would have covered.

Format-wise, I'd like the joke delivered as a numbered list of exactly three items: (1) the setup, (2) the escalation or complication, and (3) the punchline. Each item should be one or two sentences, and please give me no introduction, commentary, or explanation of why the joke is funny — if it needs explaining, it has failed.
