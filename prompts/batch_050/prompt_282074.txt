I'm a philosophy prof and next month I'm presenting in our joint philosophy-and-engineering colloqium series, defending a view I'm calling frozen politics: artifacts never have politics in the present tense. Politics happens at design time, when actual people fight over the specs, and then closure happens and the artifact just dumbly enforces whatever won. On my view the low parkway bridges and the speed bumps and the hostile benches aren't political objects, they're enforcement lag -- fossils of a politics that's already over, and confusing the fossil for the animal is the mistake both sides of the artifacts-have-politics debate keep making. The audience is about half STS-sympathetic philosophers (they've all read Winner and Latour and some will know the Moses bridge case has been empirically challenged) and half working engineers who think calling a bridge political is just a category error. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the seven or so objections I'll actually get from this particular crowd, phrased the way each camp would phrase them -- engineer objections in engineer vocabulary, STS objections in theirs, not generic objections to social constructionism -- ordered by how much damage they'd do to the frozen politics thesis, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
