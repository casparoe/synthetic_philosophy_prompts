I am writing a chapter arguing that the familiar contrast between Mencius and Xunzi — Mencius the optimist about human nature and hence the gentler political theorist, Xunzi the pessimist and hence the authoritarian — conflates a claim about moral nature with a claim about the proper scope of political intervention. To make the argument I need one worked example, built to order.

Give me a scenario set in a single modern institution — a school, a court diversion program, a public housing estate, a hospital ward; your choice, but only one — in which a policymaker reasoning from Mencius's account of human nature ends up prescribing substantially more intrusion into people's lives than a policymaker reasoning from Xunzi's. The reversal must be earned rather than asserted:

1. The Mencian prescription has to follow from Mencius's own machinery: the sprouts of virtue, the claim that wrongdoing reflects circumstances that starved or distorted those sprouts, and his insistence that material security precedes moral reliability. No straw version of him.
2. The Xunzian prescription has to follow from Xunzi's own machinery: goodness as artifice, ritual and clear convention as the press that straightens warped wood, habituation that makes compliance second nature. No uncharacteristic leniency smuggled in.
3. Measure intrusion by the depth of what the institution claims jurisdiction over: inner states, motives, and the remaking of circumstances, versus outward conduct and the maintenance of channels. The Xunzian must come out lighter on this measure even if his rules are, on their face, the stricter ones.

After the scenario, reconstruct the inference from each thinker's premises to his prescription, step by step, so the verdicts are generated rather than stipulated. Then identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and tell me what happens if it is removed: does the example collapse back into the standard alignment, and if so, does that show my chapter's thesis holds only at the margins?

Finally, give the strongest objection available to a defender of the textbook contrast — for instance, that a genuinely Xunzian order requires credible punishment and a morally authoritative ruler to keep the channels in place, so my light-touch Xunzian is unstable; or that a genuine Mencian merely removes obstacles to growth rather than engineering hearts, so my intrusive Mencian is a misreading — and say whether the scenario survives it.
