I'm taking a course on the history and philosophy of science, and we just finished a unit on the Vienna Circle. The figure who most caught my interest was Otto Neurath, partly because his project feels strangely contemporary. As I understand it, he developed Isotype, a picture language for communicating social and economic statistics to ordinary people: rows of repeated pictograms rather than abstract axes, so that a worker with little schooling could grasp, say, how housing conditions or unemployment compared across countries. He seemed to think pictures could do an end run around the biases and vagueness of everyday language, that visual representation could be made politically neutral in a way words cannot, and that this democratization of information was itself part of the socialist project. He also insisted on standardization: one consistent visual vocabulary, usable everywhere, so no one has to relearn the conventions for each new chart.

Here's what I've been arguing about with a classmate, and where I'd like your help. Where would Neurath land on the visual-information landscape we actually ended up with? Take three things: (1) modern data journalism, with its interactive dashboards, animated charts, and things like the COVID case curves everyone refreshed daily; (2) the near-universal pictogram systems that did get standardized, like airport signage, road signs, and weather icons; and (3) emoji, which are standardized pictograms but used expressively and ambiguously rather than to convey statistics. My classmate thinks Neurath would be delighted by all of it, since pictures finally won. I suspect he'd be sharply divided: thrilled by some of it and horrified by the rest, because most modern visualization serves experts, advertisers, or engagement metrics rather than public enlightenment, and emoji are arguably the opposite of what he wanted -- pictograms that increase rather than reduce ambiguity. Please reconstruct what his actual commitments imply about each of the three cases, flag where the historical record genuinely underdetermines the answer, and say whether there's a tension in his own position (can a picture language really be neutral?) that the modern examples expose. Indicate how confident you are in each part of your reconstruction, and single out the specific claim you're least sure of.
