In my philosophy of mind reading group, the causal exclusion arguement has completely stalled. We keep returning to this toy case: Maya’s C-fiber firing causes her to withdraw her hand; Maya is also in pain; the pain is realized by that firing; physics is closed, and there is no magical double causation.

One side says the physical cause excludes the mental one: if the firing is sufficient for the withdrawal, then either pain just is the firing or pain does no causal work. The other side says the “mental cause” and “physical cause” are not competitors, because they pick out the same event at different levels. They also argue that changing whether Maya is in pain would change whether she withdraws, so pain plainly makes a difference. The first side replies that level-talk smuggles in distinctness, while the second says exclusion wrongly treats every cause as if it were a separate little mechanism.

Please diagnose why the debate is stuck rather than simply declaring a winner. Is this a substantive disagreement about causal structure, a verbal disagreement over terms like event and cause, a clash between interventionist and metaphysical standards, or two different questions being run together—what produces the bodily motion versus which property best explains it? Say whether identifying pain with C-fiber firing solves the problem or merely relocates it, and what would count as real progress: a formal criterion, a better toy model, or some empirical distinction from neuroscience. If one of my premises is confused or the impasse rests on a false choice, push back plainly instead of politely rescuing it.
