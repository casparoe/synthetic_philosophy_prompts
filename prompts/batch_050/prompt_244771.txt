I'm a novelist working on a near-future thriller and I need a thought experiment I can basically lift into a chapter. You know Dennett's Where Am I? puzzle — brain in a vat, body wandering around on a radio link, and the creepy question of where the person actually is. I want something in that spirit but engineered so that three natural answers to "where is the person?" come apart cleanly: (1) the person is where their brain is, (2) the person is where their body and sensory point of view is, and (3) the person is wherever their stream of consciousness is currently running. So build me a setup with three characters/bodies/locations such that each criterion picks out a different one as the original character — a detective who's gone into an operation, say, and afterwards three different answers to "where did the detective go" all have a real claim. Constraints: no teleportation and no copying or uploading minds (duplication feels cheap and done to death), the tech should be the sort of thing you could almost read about in a Wired article, and the scenario itself should be a paragraph at most. After the scenario, show me the work: which criterion lands on which character and why, which single feature of the setup forces the three-way split, and what happens to the split if I cut that feature out of the draft. Also give me the strongest objection from a reader who thinks one of the three criteria is just obviously right, and whether the setup survives it. Finish with a one-sentence summary as your last line.
