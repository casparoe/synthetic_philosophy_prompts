I'm in a political theory reading group and we've just done Berlin's Two Concepts of Liberty, and next week we start on the republican stuff, Pettit and so on. I want to get my objection into shape before the meeting because right now I feel like the republicans are pulling a fast one. As I understand Berlin, positive liberty is the dangerous one: it says freedom is self-mastery, being governed by your rational or true self rather than your passing appetites, and once you buy that, the state can coerce you in the name of what you really want, so you can end up being forced to be free. Negative liberty is just the absence of actual interference, which is humbler and doesn't license any of that.

Now Pettit says republican freedom is neither of these: you're unfree when someone has the power to interfere with you arbitrarily, even if they never actually do anything — his case being the slave with a kind master who interferes with nothing but could interfere with everything at any time. Ok. But here's what I don't get. Securing non-domination seems to require the state to actively build and maintain institutions and laws that protect people against domination, and it defines freedom in terms of whether interference would track your real interests as a citizen rather than whether interference actually happens. That sounds to me exactly like the positive-liberty move Berlin warned about: some idealized picture of what you would will gets substituted for what you actually choose, and coercion in its name gets rebranded as compatible with your freedom. So isn't republican liberty just positive liberty in a trenchcoat, with all the same risks? Alternatively, if the republicans insist it's really about power rather than acts, then it sounds like negative liberty with extra steps, and Berlin's two-way split still covers the whole terrain. Either way I don't see the conceptual space for a third concept — so what am I missing, or is the answer that there genuinely isn't any space and the republican literature is just relabeling?
