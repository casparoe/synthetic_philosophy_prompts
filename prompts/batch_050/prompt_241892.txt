So I've been reading LessWrong and the EA Forum for years, and one idea I keep chewing over is safe Pareto improvements -- deals where everyone party to the deal is made better off, but which might still be bad moves for the world as a whole, because they burn option value, shift future bargaining power, or feed Moloch in some subtler way. I got into a long argument about this with friends at a coworking night last week and realised my intuitions are a mess. Here are my verdicts on cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, is generating them.

Two neighbours swap chores, one hates mowing and one hates cooking, both end up better off, nobody else is affected: obviously fine, paradigm case of a safe improvement. Two rival AI labs agree to share their safety research with each other but stop publishing it openly: both labs judge themselves better off, and I have a strong gut feeling this is NOT safe, even though I can't point at a specific person who's harmed. A factory owner automates, profits go up, and the laid-off workers are bought off with a package generous enough that they genuinely prefer it: fine, I think. A tabloid pays a whistleblower handsomely to drop a story that would have embarrassed a politician: both sides happier, clearly not fine. Two superpowers sign an arms-reduction treaty with imperfect verification, both judge the deal worth the risk of the other cheating: fine, even though it structurally resembles the lab case in that both parties gain a private informational edge nobody outside can audit. And the one I keep flip-flopping on: a trade deal that makes both countries richer in expectation but leaves both supply chains more brittle, so everyone is better off ex ante and more exposed to tail risk.

My tentative principle, which I'm pretty sure doesn't work: a Pareto improvement is safe iff it doesn't reduce the option value or bargaining position of anyone not party to the deal. That seems to kill the tabloid case (the public loses the option of knowing) and save the chore swap, but it also seems to kill the factory automation (the workers' union, the town, future job applicants all lose bargaining position) and I'm not willing to give that verdict up, and it says nothing clean about the brittle supply chains case since the parties themselves are the ones taking the tail risk. So: repair my principle or replace it. State the repaired version precisely, clause by clause, and say which case motivates each clause. Tell me which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and why I should feel okay dropping it (or why the principle needs another clause instead). Then give me its verdicts on three cases I haven't listed: carbon offsets bought voluntarily, an employer paying extra salary in exchange for a non-compete clause, and someone selling their governance tokens in a DAO to an unknown buyer. Please write your answer in British English.
