Here is my opening move. Your position strikes me as overstated. Any rational agent who understands the curse can correct for it directly: just as a savvy bidder in a common-value auction shades her bid knowing that winning is evidence she overestimated, a potential initiator can discount her own estimate conditional on being the most optimistic member of the group. Once everyone does this, the selection effect is neutralised and the curse dissolves. So the curse is a diagnosis of insufficient Bayesian sophistication, not a structural problem — 'be a better Bayesian' is the entire remedy. Why is that wrong?
