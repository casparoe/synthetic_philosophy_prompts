I’m putting together a handout for my philosophy of science seminar on whether science carves nature at its joints. Build a decision-tree taxonomy of natural-kind positions, not a glossary: each branch should be labeled with the question seperating it from its siblings, starting from commitments like whether boundaries exist independently of our interests, whether members need a hidden essence, whether fuzzy or disjunctive boundaries count, whether different sciences must converge on the same kinds, and whether predictive usefulness is evidence of a real joint or just usefulness.

Then locate traditional essentialism, homeostatic-property-cluster realism, promiscuous or pluralist realism, interest-relative conventionalism, and eliminativism in that space, plus any viable positions the usual labels miss. Use six concrete test cases—chemical elements and biological species, plus four invented ones such as a psychiatric category produced partly by diagnostic practices or a machine-learning disease cluster that predicts treatment response. Note any unoccupied answer combinations and whether they’re incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Aim for 8–12 leaves for advanced undergrads, and flag any views the tree can’t distinguish. Dont make the answers depend on recognizing named theorists.
