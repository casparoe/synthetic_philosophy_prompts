I’m a retired chemist who has lately got into philosophy, and I definately overthink seminars. For a mixed undergrad group of 16, 80 minutes, design a classroom game that makes intuitionistic logic feel sane before anyone hears the words Brouwer or Heyting. The hook should be that truth means having a method: an existence claim only scores if the player can produce a witness or a recipe for one; an or-claim only scores if they commit to a side and back it up; a not-claim has to be a reusable refutation procedure, not just a vibe. Give concrete materials, roles, a scoring scheme with tokens not money, one round where excluded middle seems obviously fine, then a nasty finite-search round where double negation and actually finding the thing come apart, and a final round where the class must decide whether a proved impossible search license them to assert every positive alternative. Include a tight timeline, a debrief that maps the meltdowns onto constructive proof, negation as implication into absurdity, and why finite domains can mislead, plus what will predictably go wrong the first time and which part of real constructivism the game cannot reproduce.
