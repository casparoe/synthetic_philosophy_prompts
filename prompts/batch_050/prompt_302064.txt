So next month I'm presenting at my department's decision theory seminar (I'm a grad student, but the audience is faculty + other grads) and I'm defending a take on the absent-minded driver problem that I've basically absorbed from years of reading rationalist blogs. My thesis: once the driver is actually sitting at the junction, he should just follow the ex ante optimal mixed strategy (in the standard payoff version where exiting at the first junction gets him nothing, exiting at the second gets him the big prize, and blowing through both gets him a small consolation, so the optimal plan is to exit with probability 1/3), and the whole "recompute what to do given that you're at the junction right now" move that seems to recommend deviating is a mistake. My argument is the updateless-ish one: choosing at the junction is, given his amnesia, effectively choosing a policy for every copy of himself that faces this situation, so the at-junction deliberation that treats "which junction am I at" as evidence to update on is where the reasoning goes off the rails.
