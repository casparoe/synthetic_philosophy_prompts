I TA an intro philosophy class thats mostly international students, and last week I ran a Gettier-style case (the stopped clock one) as a warm-up, expecting everyone to say "not knowledge," and maybe half the room said it obviously WAS knowledge, split pretty roughly along where-people-grew-up lines. My co-TA walked out of that session with an argument he now wheels out at every pub night, and I cant shake the feeling it proves way too much. His version: (1) When an instrument gives systematically different readings to different groups of people looking at the same thing, the readings tell you about the instruments, not the thing — a scale that reads differently depending who's standing on it is measuring the person, not their weight. (2) Case intuitions are the philosopher's instrument, and they give systematically different readings across cultures, class backgrounds, order the cases get presented in, framing, you name it. (3) So case intuitions tell us about the people having them, not about knowledge or causation or the good. (4) So the whole method of cases is basically anthropology of philosophers. (5) But (1) isn't specific to thought experiments: it covers any judgment made without an external check, including judgments about whether an inference is valid, whether a premise is plausible, whether a study was well designed. (6) So the same reasoning debunks logic, math, and the empirical studies cited in (2), since all of that bottoms out in unchecked judgments somewhere. (7) So either (1) is wrong or basically no reasoning about anything survives. He says he's fine biting that bullet — "maybe nothing survives, at least its honest." I'm not. I'd like to keep (2) and the thought that the split in my classroom means something real, and I'd really like to avoid the conclusion that my job is anthropology. So: which step do I attack, why did it look fine when he said it, and what do the obvious escape routes (restrict (1) to some class of judgments, say disagreement is compatible with a shared target plus noise, say intuitions were never really evidence in the first place) each end up costing?
