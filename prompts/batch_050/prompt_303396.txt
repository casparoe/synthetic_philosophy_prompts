I inherited about thirty acres of overgrown farmland, and a neighbor who knows ecology has offered to help me turn part of it into a wetland to bring back frogs, ducks, and other wildlife. I was excited until I started thinking about what life in the wild is actually like: most of the animals born there will die young, and many of the ones that survive will starve, freeze, sicken, or be eaten. If the average wild life is short and ends badly, then creating habitat doesn't just create happy animals -- it creates more births into lives that may contain more suffering than anything else. So now I'm stuck: is restoring habitat genuinely good for animals, good for species but not for the individual creatures, or possibly bad for both? My neighbor says suffering in nature is just how things are and not my responsibility, but it feels like that answer dodges the question of whether I'm helping or harming anyone. Please give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, that would help me think through this clearly, with no introduction or conclusion.
