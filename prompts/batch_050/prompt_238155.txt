I work in health economics, mostly burden-of-disease stuff -- DALYs, QALYs, disability weights, cost-effectiveness thresholds, that whole apparatus. I've been reading some suffering-focused ethics (negative utilitarianism and its cousins) and I keep having the feeling that these people are reinventing things my field already has machinery for, or maybe that my field accidentally committed to a philosophical position without noticing. So here's what I want.

Take the main positions in that cluster -- classical negative utilitarianism, the versions that give suffering lexical priority over happiness, the "asymmetry" views where creating suffering is bad in a way that failing to create happiness isn't, and the more recent tranquilist-flavored idea that what's bad is needing or craving rather than pain per se. Restate each one in the vocabulary I actually use at work: DALYs as years of healthy life lost, QALYs as the flip side, disability weights from 0 to 1, tradeoffs priced against a cost-effectiveness threshold, population-level aggregation. As precisely as you can, not just vibes.

Then the part I actually care about: a residue report. For each position, what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge, and what just has no expression in QALY-land at all. Some things I suspect are untranslatable: the QALY framework treats death at birth as a fixed loss and treats "one more year at full health" as a fixed gain, which seems to presuppose the symmetry that half these views deny. Also disability weights cap out at 1 -- is there anywhere in my machinery for suffering so bad it's lexically worse than any amount of mild inconvenience, or does the framework structurally forbid that? And can "never existing" even be represented, given that DALYs are always losses relative to a counterfactual life lived?

End with your honest take on whether the leftover residue is (a) a real blind spot in health economics, (b) stuff the philosophers are confused about that my framework is right to be unable to say, or (c) mixed, and which bits go which way. Where you fudge something to make it fit, flag it -- don't let my framework's assumptions sneak in disguised as translation. Also, please be upfront about what's actually settled vs. genuinely contested: both about what these philosophical positions claim (people seem to disagree about what "negative utilitarianism" even commits you to) and about whether the QALY framework has the symmetry commitments I think it does. Don't present a contested reading as the consensus one.
