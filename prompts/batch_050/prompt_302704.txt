ok so me and my friend Sam have this running argument about what counts as actually creative versus just tweaking something. Our english teacher mentioned this idea (I think its from Douglas Hofstadter?) that creativity is basically taking an existing thing and twiddling its "knobs" — like the setting, the mood, the characters, the style, whatever — and the interesting part is figuring out which knob-twiddles count as real creativity and which are just lazy variations.

Sam's verdicts so far:

- someone swapped new characters into a meme template, same joke — "not creative, thats just filling in blanks"
- a kid in our class rewrote a fairy tale but set it on a space station with robots instead of animals — "ok thats genuinely creative"
- someone translated a pun-based joke into French and had to build a completely different pun so it still landed — "creative, definitely"
- a band covered a happy pop song in a minor key with literally nothing else changed — "creative, it becomes a different song"
- someone recolored a drawing, just shuffled the colors around, and it honestly looked pretty much the same — "not creative"

Now the new case: an artist takes a goofy, cheerful comic strip and recolors the whole thing in a dark washed-out palette, so the exact same panels and dialogue suddenly read as creepy and ominous. Nothing else about it is changed.

What I want from you: (1) at least two different rules that both fit all five of Sam's earlier verdicts, (2) what each rule predicts he'll say about the comic strip, since I think they disagree on it, (3) your best guess at which rule he's actually running and why, and (4) one more example that would settle it between those two rules for sure.
