For my grad seminar on dispositions next week: before we read any of the literature I want the class to agree on what an analysis of dispositional properties (fragility, solubility, conductivity and the like) actually has to deliver. Write the desiderata list -- the cases it must get right, the distinctions it can't collapse (e.g. lacking a disposition vs. having it but never showing it) -- with a sentence on the case motivating each. At minimum it should cover finks, masks, and mimics, plus this case I'm adding to the problem set: a "restless fink." A wire is hooked to a device that removes its conductivity the instant current starts flowing, but the device also switches off -- restoring the conductivity -- the instant the current stops. So under a standing stimulus the wire flickers between conductive and non-conductive. Say whether an adequate theory has to give a verdict on whether the wire is conductive during all this, or whether it's allowed to call the case indeterminate, and what either choice costs. Then mark which desiderata you take to be non-negotiable and which are really just the price of admission for one camp (dispositional essentialists vs. people who want a categorical basis for every power), and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. A brief scorecard of the simple conditional analysis against the list at the end is fine, but keep it short -- the list is the deliverable.
