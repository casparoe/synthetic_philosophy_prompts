So I'm giving a talk in two weeks at our phil bio reading group and I need to be ready for the q&a because last time I presented I got absolutely shredded. The talk defends the propensity interpretation of fitness against the old tautology / supervenience objections, but with my own spin on it: I argue that fitness propensities are genuine dispositions, but they're dispositions of trait-population-environment triples rather than of individual organisms, and that this is actually a feature not a bug -- it explains why fitness differences are causally explanatory without having to say that some single organism's fitness caused its own reproductive success (which is where the whole "certain death, lucky survival" style counterexamples get their grip). I also argue that the dispositions are model-relative in the same way propensities for radioactive decay are relative to a half-life model, so the charge that they're not "real" single-case propensities misses the mark.
