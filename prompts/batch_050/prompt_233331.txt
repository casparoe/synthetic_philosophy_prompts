Can you explain the wrong kind of reasons problem for fitting-attitude analyses of value? I get the rough shape of the standard demon case -- some powerful demon will torture everyone unless you admire him, so it seems like you have reason to admire him, but he clearly isn't admirable or valuable in any way, which is awkward for anyone who wants to define value in terms of there being reason to have pro-attitudes toward a thing. But I'm fuzzier on the details than I'd like, so a few sub-questions:

1. Walk me through the object-given vs state-given reasons distinction and how people have used it to rescue fitting-attitude analyses. Is the idea basically that the demon only gives you a reason to *want* to admire him (or to get yourself to admire him), not a reason that bears on whether he's actually admirable? And does that move assume anything controversial about which attitudes can and can't be had "for a reason"?

2. Does the fix generalize? I've seen the claim that the object/state split is easy to state for admiration but harder for other fitting attitudes -- e.g. with fitting belief there's no obvious analog of "merely wanting to believe," since any reason you have to bring it about that you believe p seems to float free of what makes believing p fitting. Is that a real asymmetry or does it dissolve on inspection?

3. Suppose the split does save the buck-passing analysis. Does it do so at the cost of making the analysis circular or uninformative -- i.e. the "right kind of reasons" can only be picked out as the ones that bear on the object's value, which smuggles value back in through the back door? How bad is that worry, and what are the main moves for answering it?

One more request: please keep clear throughout what's more or less settled in this literature (e.g. that the demon case is a genuine puzzle that needs *some* answer) versus what's still genuinely contested (like whether the object/state distinction is the right answer, or whether it even makes sense for all attitudes). Don't present one side's fix as the consensus if there isnt one.
