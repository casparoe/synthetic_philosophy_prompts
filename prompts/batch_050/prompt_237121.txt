Is there a name for something I keep seeing in meetings? Someone will ask a question like "has anyone followed up with the vendor about the delays" — and the thing is, nobody in the room had heard there were any delays. But rather than ask "wait, what delays?", people just start answering, because asking makes you look out of the loop. By the end of the meeting the delays are basically an established fact, and a week later it turns out there were no delays, the person misremembered something from a different project.

Here's the part I actually want a name for. The original question-asker gets blamed, sure. But half the room repeated the "delays" thing to other teams, and somehow none of them feel like they said anything false, even though without them the claim would've died in that meeting. It's not the smuggling move itself I'm curious about — I gather philosophers have written plenty about sneaking assumptions into questions. It's the second stage: the people who just went along with it become the main vector, and responsibility for the assumption sort of dissolves into the group. Same thing happens in families: my aunt asks my cousin "are you going to apologize to your brother this time," which quietly establishes he did something worth apologizing for, and everyone who earnestly debates the apology question ends up spreading the guilty-verdict without ever having stated it.

So: is there an established concept for that specific configuration — going along with an implied claim in a way that makes you a co-owner of it without ever asserting it — and if not, what would you call it? I don't just want a label, I want the edges: cases it covers, cases it doesn't (e.g. how is it different from people merely failing to fact-check each other?), and what it implies about who deserves the blame when the smuggled claim turns out false.
