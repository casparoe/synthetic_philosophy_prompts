For a graduate seminar on conceptual engineering in political science, write the specification that a concept of democratic backsliding must meet in order to do real analytical work rather than function as a partisan label. I want the list of requirements, the consideration behind each, and your judgment about which are non-negotiable and which are optional or camp-specific.

The specification should address at least the following pressure points, and for each one state the requirement and what motivates it:

- Classification: the concept must deliver determinate verdicts on the standard hard cases -- Hungary under Orbán, Poland under PiS (and after), Turkey under Erdoğan, and the United States across the last decade -- and it must do so in a way that does not simply track which government the classifier dislikes. What has to be built into the concept for its verdicts to be checkable by someone who does not share the analyst's politics?
- Boundary-drawing: it must distinguish backsliding from (i) ordinary democratic politics, including polarizing or norm-breaking but legal governance; (ii) outright authoritarianism (is backsliding a process, a regime category, or both?); (iii) democratic breakdown, such as a coup, where there is nothing gradual left to describe; and (iv) mere failure to consolidate, where a country was never democratic enough to slide back from. Say which of these distinctions you think the concept genuinely needs and which are terminological housekeeping.
- The baseline problem: backsliding is decline relative to something. State what the reference point should be -- the country's own past, a regional standard, an ideal type -- and what goes wrong with each option.
- Agency and intent: must backsliding be deliberately engineered by incumbents, or can it be the unintended aggregate effect of independent decisions? Whichever you require, note the cost.
- Who decides: the concept presumably needs operationalization -- expert-coded indices, measurable institutional criteria, or something else. State what kind of evidence a verdict must be answerable to, and whether deference to democracy indices (V-Dem, Freedom House, and similar) is a requirement or merely a convenience.

Two further asks. First, if some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable -- for instance, if full partisan neutrality is incompatible with any substantive standard rich enough to catch gradual legalistic erosion, since incumbents will contest the standard itself -- say so explicitly and identify the smallest set that cannot be jointly met. That is the part I most want to see. Second, close by identifying which single requirement does the most exclusionary work, i.e., which one rules out the most candidate definitions currently in circulation.

Do not score existing scholarly definitions against the list; I will do that in the seminar. The deliverable is the standard itself, not a literature review.
