Write an in-universe issue of a weekly briefing — call it something like The Structural-Demographic Outlook — from a world where cliodynamics has matured into a boringly established forecasting industry, somewhere between the weather channel and an economics bulletin. Assume I know the basics of secular cycles and structural-demographic theory (elite overproduction, immiseration, state fiscal strain, the usual). Deadpan throughout: this week's elite-competition index with a tidy little graph described in prose, the five-year outlook for state fiscal health, a severe-instability warning for some republic ("conditions favorable for intra-elite conflict; residents are advised to secure their patronage networks"), and a sports-desk-style sidebar comparing two historical polities' disintegrative trajectories. The humor has to come from real features of the research program — spurious precision over centuries-long cycles, confident backtests on a handful of cases, forecasts that predict a crisis but never its trigger — not from strawmen, and the obvious objections (can you really put a number on this? would the model ever be allowed to fail in public?) should land without ever being stated. Around 600-700 words. Commit to the conceit and pick your targets rather than spreading the satire thin over everything; stay in character the whole way. Then after the piece, a short note on where you took liberties with the ideas or leaned on a contested reading of the field.
