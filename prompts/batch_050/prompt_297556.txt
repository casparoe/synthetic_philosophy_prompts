So I've been following the alignment discourse for years (mostly lurking, occasionally posting on LW) and I keep hitting a snag on terminology. I found this explainer post someone linked in a Discord, and it summarizes the basic structure of the alignment problem like this: 'Alignment research divides into two projects. Value alignment is the big one: getting an AI to internalize actual human values, so that it wants what we want. Intent alignment is the easier, narrower version where the AI just has to follow its operator's instructions -- it does what you tell it to do, no more, no less. Since intent alignment is about obedience, a perfectly intent-aligned system is by definition safe: it would never do anything its operator didn't order it to do, so any catastrophe would have to be the operator's explicit command. That's why most serious researchers treat intent alignment as basically solved in principle and focus on value alignment instead.'

I have a bad feeling about this summary but I can't fully articulate what's wrong. The intent alignment = obedience equation seems off to me -- my recollection is that the concept was deliberately defined to be about what the operator *wants* rather than what they literally say, precisely because literal instruction-following goes wrong. But then I'm not sure the post is even wrong in a way that matters, or whether I'm just nitpicking phrasing. And the "by definition safe" bit sounds like it's smuggling something in, but I can't tell if it's a fair gloss of the narrow-problem framing or a total distortion of it.

Can you audit this? Go through the claims one at a time: which parts match how people who coined or use these terms actually mean them, which are subtly wrong, and does the final conclusion -- that intent alignment is solved in principle and value alignment is where the real work is -- survive once you fix whatever's wrong? Also, if there's a real distinction the summary is flattening (something like trying-to-do-what-I-want vs doing-what-I-say vs doing-what-I-would-endors-on-reflection), I'd like it spelled out, because I suspect that's where the sleight of hand is happening.
