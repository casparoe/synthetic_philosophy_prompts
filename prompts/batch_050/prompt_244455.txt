I've been in a political theory reading group for about two years now and we've circled back to the same fight maybe five times: the old equality-of-what argument. The welfarists say what matters is how well people's lives go for them, the resourcists say you can't distribute welfare directly and anyway it's unfair to compensate someone for their expensive tastes, and then the capability people say both of those miss the point because the same bundle of resources does different things for a disabled person and an able-bodied one. Everyone in the room can run all three scripts perfectly. Nobody has ever changed sides. And then there's a fourth person who keeps saying the whole question is malformed -- that egalitarianism is about not having hierarchy and oppression between people, not about finding the right currency to equalise -- and the rest of us just sort of nod and go back to arguing about expensive tastes.

Here's my hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than accept: I suspect the debate stalled because the sides aren't actually disagreeing about the answer to one question. The currency debate (welfare vs resources vs capabilities) is mostly an argument about how to handle responsibility and luck within a distributive picture -- everyone there agrees the state should equalise *something* and the fight is over which metric respects the difference between choices and circumstances. The relational crowd is rejecting the picture itself. So maybe it's two debates stapled together, and the stalled feeling comes from the stapling. Or maybe I'm wrong and there's a real shared question underneath, like "what should an egalitarian state minimise," and one side is just losing it badly.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Work through what each position actually says -- the expensive tastes objection, the disability argument, the responsibility-cut move, the relational critique -- and diagnose where the genuine forks are: is it a factual disagreement about what institutions can measure and distribute, a conceptual one about what egalitarianism is *for*, or just competing intuitions about cases like the person with champagne tastes that nobody can argue anyone out of?
