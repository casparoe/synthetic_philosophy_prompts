Write a sestina about multiple realizability where the six end-words are six different physical substrates — things like neurons, silicon, clockwork, hydraulic pipes, ant colonies, whatever six you like — and the poem's speaker is a single mind that keeps waking up in a new one each stanza. The point is that the sestina form does half the philosophical work for you: the same six words keep coming back in different positions every stanza, so the structure itself is already an argument that the pattern survives being rearranged and re-homed. Make sure the reader can feel the speaker staying the same person even as the hardware changes out from under them.

The poem should take a side, not just admire the scenery: I want it committed to the claim that multiple realizability really does sink the simple type-identity theory (the "pain = c-fibers firing" style view), with the envoi landing that verdict rather than hedging it. If you think that's wrong and the identity theorist survives, commit to that instead and make the envoi land that — but pick one and give the poem a spine.

After the poem, add a short note (prose, a few sentences) saying where you took liberties with the actual philosophy — e.g. whether you've flattened the difference between functionalism and mere substrate-independence, or leaned on a contested reading of what the identity theorist was ever claiming. Don't play both sides in the note either; tell me which reading you went with and why.
