I co-host a small philosophy-of-biology podcast, and in two weeks we're interviewing a philosopher known for defending the etiological (selected-effects) account of biological function against causal-role rivals in the tradition of Cummins. She is a careful and practiced interlocutor, and on past recordings I've noticed she tends to fall back on two moves: first, dismissing counterexamples involving artifacts or hypothetical creatures as trading on the difference between natural and derived functions; and second, shifting to a "modern history" variant of the view whenever ancient selection pressures seem irrelevant. I'd like a set of eight to ten interview questions that engage the actual machinery of her position rather than inviting her to restate it.

The questions should collectively cover the following pressure points, roughly in an order that builds: the malfunction problem (how a token heart can have the function of pumping blood while being structurally incapable of it, and why an appeal to what its ancestors were selected for doesn't merely relocate the difficulty); the "first instance" problem for novel traits (the very first organism bearing an advantageous mutation has no selection history for that trait, so on her view the trait apparently has no function in its first bearer, which seems to get the biology of adaptation backwards); exaptation (structures co-opted for a role different from the one that explains their historical selection, such as feathers used for flight after an earlier thermoregulatory role, and where the function switch is supposed to happen on the timeline); and a case I want to press from synthetic biology: an engineered organism containing a gene circuit designed from scratch by researchers, never selected by anything, which nonetheless seems obviously to have a function — I want to see whether she classifies this as derived function and whether that classification can be motivated without smuggling in causal-role considerations.

Please also include at least one question on the charge that selected-effects attributions are unfalsifiable historical speculation — that any current utility can be retrofitted with a just-so selection story — and one on whether her dispute with causal-role theorists might be partly terminological, given that the two accounts seem to partition the explanatory work differently across physiology and evolutionary biology. For every question, give me the follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, including follow-ups that anticipate her two habitual moves described above; where the modern-history variant is invoked, I'd like the follow-up to probe whether it can still explain functions of traits whose environments have changed too recently for selection to have operated.
