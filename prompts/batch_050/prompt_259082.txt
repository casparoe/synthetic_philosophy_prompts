Ok here's something I've been chewing on. Nietzsche was weirdly obsessed with diet and health — the man spends a solid chunk of Ecce Homo on what to eat and drink, rants against alcohol and German cooking, and more or less blames Germany's intellectual sluggishness on bad digestion. At the same time his philosophy drips with contempt for comfort-seeking, for people who make a virtue out of weakness, for the last man content in his cozy self-preservation. Those two things pull in opposite directions when you look at modern wellness culture, and I can't figure out where he'd land.

So: drop Nietzsche into 2025 and tell me how he'd react to each of these, with actual arguments not just vague "he hated weakness" gestures:

1. Gym culture, protein tracking, hypertrophy influencers
2. Bryan Johnson–style longevity biohacking: measure everything, optimize biomarkers, try not to die, ever
3. The quantified-self crowd: sleep scores, HRV, glucose monitors on people who aren't even diabetic
4. SSRIs, therapy culture, and framing mental health struggles as conditions to be accomodated rather than overcome
5. The vegan vs carnivore diet tribal wars
6. Ozempic and friends as a shortcut past willpower

For each one, predict whether he'd be sympathetic, scathing, or torn, and ground it in his actual positions: his own lifelong sickness and what he claimed to have learned from being ill, the line between the ascetic ideal he savaged in the Genealogy and the self-discipline he clearly practiced and praised, his argument that life aims at discharging and growing power rather than at self-preservation (which seems bad news for the longevity people), and his suspicion that pity and medicalizing suffering can turn it into something demeaning instead of something you transform.

The part I really want you to grapple with: modern wellness culture looks from one angle like exactly the regimen-obsessed self-mastery he performed his whole life, and from another angle like herd morality plus the ascetic ideal wearing a lab coat. Take a stance on which items fall on which side, and flag anywhere his views seem genuinely inconsistent — e.g. was his own health obsession self-overcoming or just the ascetic priest in denial? And the nasty one: given his migraines, his collapse, the whole mess, would he have taken the SSRIs?
