I'm a novelist (literary thriller, small town, 1990s, no sci-fi whatsoever) and I'm stuck engineering a scenario for chapter 9. What I need is basically an acausal threat that works between two ordinary humans, no computers, no prediction machines, no lab-coated anything.

The setup I want to dramatize: a blackmailer threatens a victim, and the threat works even though the blackmailer will never find out whether the victim complied. So the victim isn't buying anything real by complying -- no reputation, no future leniency, nothing flows back to her. The blackmailer just reasons about what kind of person she is and what she'll do, sets the punishment scheme accordingly, and she knows all of this and complies anyway, and it has to be at least defensibly rational that she does. Basically the structure of those weird decision theory cases but with two people who could exist.

Constraints, because my editor will kill me otherwise:

- Compliance must be causally inert wrt everything the victim cares about, except via the blackmailer's model of her. The trigger has already been set or is otherwise out of her reach.
- The blackmailer is fallible. Shrewd judge of character at best. She knows he's fallible and complies anyway.
- No precommitment gadget she could wriggle out of, no hypnosis, no drugs, no identical twins.
- Her compliance has to run through a surrogate goal: at the moment of decision she can't aim at the actual outcome (that's causally out of reach), so she aims at being the kind of person who complies, or at following a policy, or something in that family. Make the surrogate goal explicit in the scene because I want to write her internal monologue around it.

Then do the work for me:

1. Write the scenario, two paragraphs max, plain enough that I can adapt it. For flavor: he's an ex-priest, she's a pharmacist, but feel free to ignore that if something else works better.
2. Show it meets each spec: why does the threat bind her, why is complying rational or at least defensible given his fallible model of her, what exactly is the surrogate goal and why can't she just aim at the real one?
3. Tell me which single feature of the setup is doing all the work, and what happens if I cut it -- does the scene collapse into ordinary blackmail, or into her just shrugging at him?
4. Steelman the attack I most fear: that I've smuggled in a perfect predictor wearing a trenchcoat, i.e. the scene only works because the blackmailer is secretly infallible. If a critic said that, would they be right, and can I patch it?

I keep going back and forth on whether this is even possible without cheating, so if the answer is "it's impossible under your constraints, heres the nearest thing," tell me that instead.
