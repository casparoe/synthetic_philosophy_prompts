So I'm a science and ideas journalist and I'm messing around with fiction for a side project, and I want a short story to use as a springboard. Here's the setup I'm picturing. The story should be about consciousness as a "user illusion" -- the idea that what we experience as our inner life is less like direct access to the mind and more like a desktop interface on a computer: simplified icons, drag-and-drop metaphors, all the actual processing hidden, and the simplification is *useful*, not a bug. Set the story maybe thirty years from now. A mid-level engineer at a neurotech company gets access to an internal prototype that doesn't give you the usual polished "wellness dashboard" of your own brain states -- it strips away the interface entirely and lets you perceive, raw, the actual machinery underneath: the predictive processing, the confabulation happening in real time, the way your "decisions" get narrated to you a beat after they're made. She's not supposed to have this access; it's flagged as psychologically hazardous, which is part of why she's curious.

Here's the angle I want the story to actually *explore* rather than just decorate: the interface theory would predict that seeing the raw machinery isn't enlightening the way she expects, it's useless or even corrosive -- like trying to do your taxes by watching voltages flip in the CPU. So build the plot around her discovering that the "illusion" is load-bearing. Maybe she starts making objectively better predictions about her own behavior while becoming functionally worse at being a person -- she can no longer tell whether she's angry or just modeling anger, she catches the little lies her narrator-self feeds her before she can benefit from them, that kind of thing. And then give her a real choice at the end with actual stakes: go back to the interface (and know it's an interface, which maybe ruins it anyway?) or stay on the raw feed and become something that works differently.

Don't make it a lecture with a plot stapled on -- I want the philosophy to come out through what happens to her, her marriage or her work, small concrete scenes. Somewhere in the 1500-2500 word range is fine. Oh and please no twist where it turns out she's an AI or a simulation, that's been done to death.
