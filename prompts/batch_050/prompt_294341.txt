I've been reading rationalist blogs for years, and one topic keeps coming up in the comment sections without ever being properly understood, mine included: whether there are rational priors. I've volunteered to give a ninety-minute session at my local rationality meetup on objective Bayesianism and the maximum entropy principle. The audience is a dozen people who know Bayes' theorem cold, are comfortable with probability as degree of belief, and have absorbed the folk position that priors are subjective and that's the end of it -- but who have no training in information theory or formal epistemology.

Don't give me the lecture; give me the plan for it. Specifically: the order in which the pieces must be introduced so that maxent arrives as an answer to a problem they've already felt rather than as a mysterious formula -- I'm guessing the principle of indifference has to come first and then be shown to break, but tell me what actually has to be in place before its failure means anything to them; the one example that should carry the session (I need something where indifference gives different answers depending on how you parameterize the problem, and I'd like to know why I should or shouldn't build it around the classic cube-factory case versus something of my own construction); and the misconceptions I should expect in the order I'll encounter them. My predicted list: that maxent is just a fancy name for uniform priors, that it's importing something from thermodynamics, that it claims to recover objective truth rather than minimize assumptions, and that choosing a parameterization is itself smuggling in a prior. Tell me where each one will naturally arise in the sequence, whether my list is complete, and exactly where to defuse each so it doesn't derail the next hour.

Also: what should I openly refuse to cover at this level -- the measure-theoretic machinery, group invariance arguments, the connection to statistical mechanics? And give me one question I can pose at the end that would show they've grasped the actual claim of objective Bayesianism, rather than just having learned to say maximum entropy in a knowing tone.
