Something's been bugging me and I want to know if philosophers have a name for it. Say I'm running a raffle with a thousand tickets and one winner. Of ticket #1 I can sensibly say: it'll lose. Same for #2, and so on all the way down, and each individual call is basically certain. But obviously I can't turn around and say every ticket will lose, since one of them has to win. So somehow I'm rational to believe each thing on its own and irrational to believe them all together.

Then I notice what looks like the same shape somewere else: I've just finished checking the manuscript of a book I wrote, I'm confident at least one error still slipped through, wich feels like saying "at least one of my thousand careful judgements is wrong" even though I'd stand behind each of them individually.

So: is this one phenomenon or two that merely rhyme? If there are established names, don't just hand me the labels, I want the carving. What exactly is the pattern, do the raffle and the manuscript differ in a way that matters (something about pure chance vs. my own fallibility?), and where are the edges, e.g. is a weather forecaster who's sure it'll rain on at least one day next month but won't commit to any particular day caught in the same thing, or is that actually fine and different? If nobody has named the general pattern, coin a term with a definition and a boundary case or two.

Please answer in British English.
