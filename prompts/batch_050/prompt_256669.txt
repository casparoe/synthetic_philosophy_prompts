Need a consistency audit of my moral views before my ethics reading group thursday. We watched a documentary about that old shipwreck case where the starving sailors ended up eating the cabin boy, and afterwards we argued for like two hours about what they should've done instead, and I realized I kept contradicting myself depending on which case we were discussing. So I wrote down where I think I actually stand, and I want to know whether it hangs together as a package or whether I'm just collecting intuitions that feel good one at a time.

(1) When not everyone can survive, a fair lottery is the right way to pick who dies, and whoever loses is genuinely obligated to go along with it — the others are within their rights to enforce the outcome if the loser refuses to cooperate.
(2) Numbers count. All else equal, five people dying is worse than one person dying, and if you can only save one of two groups you should save the bigger one.
(3) It's flat-out wrong to kill one healthy innocent person to use their body to save others. So no, the surgeon may not carve up the healthy patient to harvest organs for five transplant patients, no matter how the math comes out.
(4) In a genuinely overloaded lifeboat — everyone drowns unless someone goes over the side — it's permissible to put one person overboard to save the rest, and if there's no time to organize anything, you don't first need to hold the lottery from (1).
(5) In disaster triage you treat whoever has the best chance of surviving first, not whoever arrived first, and definately not women-and-children-first.
(6) Nobody's life is worth less because they're old, or single, or have fewer people depending on them. There are no second-class passengers.

My own suspicion is that (5) and (6) are where the problem is, since triage seems to rank people and (6) says don't rank people, so tell me if I'm right about that or if I'm worried about the wrong pair. Which of these are actually in tension and which only look that way? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what I'd have to give up to fix it.
