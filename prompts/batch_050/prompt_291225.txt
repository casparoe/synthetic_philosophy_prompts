Two of my friends have been having the same fight for months and I'm sick of mediating. One is a hard "man of his time" defender: blaming Jefferson or Churchill for views that were mainstream in their day is unfair, because you can't hold people to standards that weren't really live options for them. The other thinks that's a cop-out: owning people was wrong in 1800 too, the people being owned certainly knew it, and saying so now isn't anachronism, it's just honesty. Draft the joint statement the two of them could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration.

What I want: first a numbered list of claims they'd both actually accept (I'm guessing stuff like "moral progress happens and part of how it happens is people being judged by standards beyond their own time" and "what someone could reasonably have known matters for blame" -- but push past these if you find better ones, and make sure none of them are just the two sides rephrased in vaguer words). Then a disagreements table where each residual disagreement is stated precisely, in wording each side would accept as a fair statement of its own position. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where an answer would actually move one of them. I suspect one crux is whether the right baseline for judging someone is the average view of their time or the best view that was genuinely available to them -- i.e., whether it matters that the correct position wasn't just floating around in the abstract but was pressed on the figure by contemporaries they personally encountered. Another might be whether "couldn't have done better without paying an enormous cost" works as an excuse, and who bears the burden of estimating that cost. For each crux, give a concrete historical-style case both sides agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side would predict or concede about that case. Keep the whole thing the kind of document both people would actually sign, not a summary with a winner baked in.
