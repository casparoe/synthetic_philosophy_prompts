At a dinner party last week two friends of mine got into it about immigration and I'm still not sure who won. Marta said a country is basically like a house: it belongs to its citizens collectively, so they can keep outsiders out for good reasons, bad reasons, or no reason at all, the same way I don't have to justify not letting strangers sleep in my guest room. Dev said the house analogy is rigged: my right to my house doesn't let me control who walks down the public street, and a national territory is way more like the street than the house -- excluding people from a huge chunk of the earth's surface is the kind of thing that needs an actual justification. Marta said fine, drop the house, think of a club or a marriage: freedom of association means groups get to pick their own members even for bad reasons, and citizens are just an ongoing association deciding who joins. Dev replied that clubs and marriages only get that latitude because the excluded have other clubs to join and other people to marry, whereas there's exactly one habitable planet and where you're born basically determines your whole life -- associations may exclude only when exclusion doesn't trap the excluded. Marta's last move was that self-government requires control over membership: if anyone can show up and vote, a people's decisions about its own future are meaningless, so the right to exclude is just part of the right to self-determination. Dev said that proves way too much, since the same argument would justify banning emigration, blocking internal migration between regions, or excluding co-nationals for their religion. They both looked at me and I punted. Can you go through their exchange point by point, say who's right about what, and tell me who's more right overall?
