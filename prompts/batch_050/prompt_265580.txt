So I volunteer with a small animal advocacy group and we've somehow ended up in charge of allocating about $40k and a bunch of volunteer hours toward "the insect issue," which mostly means pest control and insecticides -- what gets sprayed on crops, what exterminators do in homes, mosquito control programs, that kind of thing. The group is split. Half of them think this is obviously the biggest deal in animal ethics because of the sheer numbers (trillions of insects killed, vs billions of farmed mammals and birds), and the other half think it's a waste of money until we have better evidence insects can even suffer, and meanwhile we're the ones who have to pick.

What I'd like is a prioritized agenda, not a survey. Of the open questions in this area -- does the evidence for pain/suffering in insects support acting now or waiting, does it matter morally whether a death is by poison vs. being crushed vs. starving after habitat loss, does the math on "expected suffering per dollar" actually survive how uncertain the sentience question is, do lethal methods vs. deterrent/exclusion methods vs. reducing the conditions that attract pests differ enough to matter, the weird question of whether pest control even reduces total insect suffering or just shifts which populations exist where -- which two or three should actually drive where our money and hours go? Rank them and defend the ranking. I want the criteria explicit: how much hinges on the answer, how tractable it is for a small group with no lab and no scientists on staff, how neglected it is relative to what big animal orgs are already doing, and what finding would flip your ordering.

Two things I specifically don't want: first, don't just say "more research is needed on sentience" -- tell me whether *we* can meaningfully contribute to that or whether we should treat it as someone else's job and act under uncertainty instead. Second, give me your actual recommendation for the split of the $40k at the end, committed, with your reasons. I'd rather have a definite position I can argue with at the next meeting than a menu where every option comes out looking reasonable.
