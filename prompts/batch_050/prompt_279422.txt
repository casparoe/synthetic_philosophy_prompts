The thing is, back in college I read about how Elizabeth Anscombe fought against Oxford giving Truman an honorary degree in the 50s, on the grounds that dropping the atomic bombs was murder — killing civilians as a means of pressuring a government into surrender is just murder, and you can't wriggle out of it by saying you merely foresaw the deaths while only intending to end the war. It's been living rent-free in my head ever since I took this job.

So: if Anscombe were alive and looking at today's targeted killing programs — drones hitting individuals identified through metadata and "pattern of life" analysis, strikes on people whose names nobody actually knows, the occasional hit on a funeral or on rescuers showing up after the first strike, all of it wrapped in lawyer-approved proportionality math — what would she actually think? Does precision change anything in her framework, or is this just Truman with better optics?
