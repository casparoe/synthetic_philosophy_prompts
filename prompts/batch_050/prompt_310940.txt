For a piece I'm writing on whether anything without a mind behind it can really be about something, draft the joint statement from an adversarial collaboration between a Searlean (only actual conscious brains have original intentionality; everything else -- thermostats, computers, books -- has at most the derived kind we lend it) and a Dennettian (the intentional stance: meaning is about functional role, and derived intentionality is the only kind there is). Anchor it in Dennett's two-bitser: a vending machine built to detect US quarters by size and weight, later shipped to Panama, where the same machinery sorts quarter-balboas. First get both sides to sign off on what the machine does and doesn't do -- that's the warm-up. Then: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, including one about what the Chinese Room argument does and doesn't establish and one about whether derived intentionality is somehow second-class; (2) a disagreements table where each position is phrased so that side would actualy accept the wording of its own view; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling one would genuinely move at least one camp, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Don't adjudicate, and if part of the dispute strikes you as verbal, say which part. Close with a short note on how confident you are that each party would really sign your formulations, and single out the one claim you're least sure of.
