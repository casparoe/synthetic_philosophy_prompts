In an ethics anthology I am working through, the editor's headnote to Judith Jarvis Thomson's 1971 paper on abortion reads as follows:

"Thomson's strategy is to concede, for the sake of argument, that the fetus is a person with a full right to life, and then to show that abortion is nonetheless permissible in all or nearly all cases. Her famous thought experiment -- you wake up in a hospital bed, your kidneys hooked up to an unconscious violinist who needs your blood type to survive for nine months -- is meant to establish that the right to life never includes a right to use another person's body. Since pregnancy just is a case of one person being hooked up to another, whatever is true of the violinist case is true of pregnancy generally, so abortion is permissible for essentially the same reason you may unplug yourself. It follows that the entire debate about when personhood begins is beside the point, and Thomson herself took the argument to settle the question: she concluded that abortion cannot be seriously immoral in any case, though she allowed that unplugging the violinist might sometimes be unkind or indecent in a purely aesthetic sense."

Something about this strikes me as overstated, but I cannot pinpoint where. I would like a careful accuracy audit. Which parts of the summary would a philosopher who knows the paper accept as faithful? Which parts involve subtle shifts -- for instance, does Thomson present the right to life as never including a right to use another's body, or is her claim more qualified? Does she treat the violinist case as analogous to pregnancy in general, or only to some routes into pregnancy? Does she conclude that abortion is never seriously immoral, or does she carve out exceptions, and if so, what kind and for what reasons? Finally, the headnote draws a downstream conclusion -- that the personhood debate is wholly beside the point -- that the editor attributes to Thomson herself. Does that conclusion follow from the corrected version of her argument, or does it survive only if the summary's errors are left in place? Please be specific about what Thomson actually needs for her argument to work, and flag anything in the summary that is a defensible simplification versus an outright misrepresentation.
