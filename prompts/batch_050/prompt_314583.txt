I'm tired of arguements that wave Gödel's incompleteness theorems at minds, AI, or human knowledge generally and then conclude something dramatic (minds can't be machines, LLMs will never "truly" understand themselves, science can never be completed, whatever). Before I sit through another one, I want a checklist. Write the desiderata: what any argument that legitimately carries incompleteness out of arithmetic and into some other domain has to establish. For example, which of the theorem's actual hypotheses (consistency? effective axiomatizability? containing a certain amount of arithmetic?) have to be verified for the new target and how, what the self-reference has to accomplish in the argument, whether the Rosser strengthening matters or is just a technicality. Give the motivation behind each desideratum, say which are negotiable and which aren't, and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied in the minds/AI setting. Then, briefly, score two things against the list: Lucas's original minds-aren't-machines argument, and a friend's claim that an LLM can never fully understand its own weights because of Gödel. Also please keep straight what's actually settled about the theorems versus what's genuinely contested among logicians and philosophers of mind -- don't present a Penrose-style reading as the consensus view. 600 words max.
