I am compiling objections to Kant's Formula of Universal Law — roughly, the requirement that one act only on maxims one can will as universal laws of nature — for a graduate seminar, and I believe my list already covers the standard repertoire:

(1) The emptiness charge, associated with Hegel: the test is purely formal and generates no determinate duties unless substantive ends are smuggled in; universalizability as such cannot rule out maxims of indifference or self-interest.

(2) The false-positive problem: maxims we take to be immoral pass the test because they are too specific to generate a contradiction when universalized — for instance, a maxim indexed to a particular person, time, or circumstance, willed by someone so situated that no universal practice of the relevant kind could touch him.

(3) The false-negative problem: maxims we take to be permissible fail it — a maxim of voluntary childlessness (universalized, humanity dies out, so the maxim arguably cannot even be conceived as a universal law), or a maxim of always arriving first in order to get a seat on the train.

(4) The rigorism objection: the test yields absolutist conclusions, such as a prohibition on lying even to the murderer at the door, and any defensible moral theory should let circumstances or consequences in somewhere.

(5) The description-relativity or maxim-tailoring problem: since any action falls under indefinitely many descriptions, a resourceful agent can always reformulate his maxim until it passes; the test is therefore either vacuous or in need of an independent theory of which maxim an action is really performed on, which Kant does not supply.

(6) The consistency-is-not-morality objection: the test only excludes maxims the agent cannot rationally will to be universal; a sufficiently committed fanatic — one prepared to accept the relevant treatment should he turn out to fall within his own target class — can universalize even persecution, so passing the test cannot be sufficient for permissibility.

Is there another objection I am missing? It should be genuinely different from these six — not a restatement, special case, or mere combination of items already listed. For whatever you propose, please also say two things. First, whether it survives a sophisticated Kantian response: a defender will insist that the Formula is not a mechanical decision procedure for testing pre-given maxims but a constraint on deliberation, and that the relevant maxims are the agent's actual operative principles, so that much of (2), (3), and (5) trades on a caricature. Second, how your objection relates to the ones above — whether it presupposes one of them, undercuts one of them, or operates at a different level entirely (for example, as a meta-ethical or psychological objection rather than a counterexample to the test's verdicts).
