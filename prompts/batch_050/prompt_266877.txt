Write an essay in response to the following question, structured as a credence estimate with its reasoning made explicit.

The standard machinery of Goodman's new riddle of induction is familiar: define an object as grue just in case it is first examined before some fixed future time t and is green, or is not first examined before t and is blue. Every emerald examined so far then confirms both "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue," and the riddle is to say why only the first of these hypotheses is projectible — why past instances license one generalization and not the other, given that the evidential support appears symmetrical.

Now consider a parallel construction aimed not at emeralds but at proposed solutions to the riddle itself. Call a proposed solution sound-star if and only if either (a) it was first defended in print before 2040 and comes to be widely accepted by philosophers, or (b) it is first defended in 2040 or later and is ultimately rejected. Every solution defended so far — entrenchment-based accounts, appeals to natural kinds, Bayesian treatments of hypothesis complexity, deflationary views on which the riddle is merely verbal — was defended before 2040, so each of them counts as sound-star only under the assumption that it will eventually be widely accepted. The track record of defended solutions thus appears to confirm "the correct solution is sound" and "the correct solution is sound-star" equally well, and yet these two generalizations diverge sharply about any solution first defended after 2040.

The question: what is your credence that the new riddle of induction admits a solution that would genuinely satisfy a careful skeptic — one that explains why green is projectible and grue is not without merely encoding our antecedent preference for green — and that this solution will be widely recognized as such by 2100? Give a number, but derive it. Decompose at least the following sub-questions, assigning a rough probability to each: (i) that projectibility is grounded in something mind-independent, such as natural kinds or objective similarity structure; (ii) that, conditional on such a grounding existing, it can be stated without circularity — without invoking the very inductive practices it is meant to vindicate; (iii) that the sound-star construction above is a genuinely new problem for any proposed solution rather than a superficial relabeling that differs from the original grue construction in some crucial respect (consider, for instance, that acceptance by philosophers is a sociological fact about the very community doing the projecting, whereas an emerald's color is not); and (iv) any further sub-question you take to be load-bearing. Show how the numbers combine into your final credence, identify which sub-question the final figure is most sensitive to, and state what argument or development would shift it most.
