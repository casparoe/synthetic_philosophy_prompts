I've been going around in circles on how to rank worlds with infinitely many people in them, so let me just dump my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and you tell me what principle (if any) I'm actually committed to. Imagine people living in houses along a street that's endless in one direction, house 1, house 2, house 3, forever, one person per house.

Case 1: World A, everyone at welfare 5. World B, same street, everyone at welfare 10. Verdict: B is clearly better. Not even hard.

Case 2: Take World A and add one more person at welfare 5 (say a house 0 gets built). Still infinitely many people, still mostly 5s. Verdict: strictly better than A. One more good life, how could it not be.

Case 3: Same street as A, but now everyone alternates, house 1 at welfare 1, house 2 at 2, house 3 at 1, house 4 at 2, and so on. Now flip it so house 1 gets 2, house 2 gets 1, etc. Verdict: equally good. Same welfares, just shuffled around, and I don't see how which house you're in could matter morally.

Case 4: Start from the alternating world in Case 3, and raise only the person in house 1 from 1 to 2, everyone else untouched. Verdict: strictly better, someone gains and nobody loses. But here's what bugs me: the resulting world still just has infinitely many people at 1 and infinitely many at 2, so by my Case 3 verdict it should be equally good as the original. I can't see how to keep both.

Case 5: World C, person in house n has welfare n (1, 2, 3, 4, ... growing without bound). World D, everyone at a flat one million. Verdict: D is better, most people in C are worse off than anyone in D no matter how far out you go... though this is the verdict I'm least confident about, part of me wants to say they're not even comparable.

Case 6: A world with a trillion people at welfare 10 vs a world with infinitely many people at welfare 1. Verdict: the infinite one is better. Finite is finite.

So: what principle or principles capture most of this? If it takes a couple of candidate principles rather than one, fine, but state them precisely enough that I can tell what they say about cases I haven't listed (e.g. infinitely many at welfare 2 vs infinitely many alternating 1 and 3; or adding infinitely many people at welfare 0). Tell me which of my verdicts each part of the principle is there to save, and be upfront about which verdicts have to go if I want something consistent, since I'm pretty sure Cases 2-4 can't all survive.

One more thing: I have a vague sense that there are actual impossibility results floating around in this area. Please seperate what's genuinely settled on that front from what's still contested among people who work on infinite ethics, and don't present some contested fix (dropping one of my intuitions, whatever technical criterion you'd use to compare infinite sequences, etc.) as if it were the agreed-upon answer.
