okay so i'm a phd student in epistemology writing a chapter on the bootstrapping problem (Vogel's gas gauge / Roxanne-style cases, where you infer a source is reliable purely from its own outputs) and easy knowledge. I've got what I think is a fairly complete taxonomy of responses, and my supervisor keeps saying "is that really all of them?" so -- here are the positions I've already catalogued, please don't repeat any of these:

1. The reliabilist bullet-bite: track-record reasoning is genuinely fine, circularity only kills *dialectical* effectiveness, not epistemic justification (the Alston/Bergmann/Sosa-flavored line). Epistemic circularity is not vicious.
2. Cohen's basic knowledge view: we're entitled to treat our basic faculties as reliable antecedently, so the bootstrapping inference transmits justification it's just... easy. The "problem" is actually a mark in favor of the view.
3. Wright's entitlement strategy: we have unearned, non-evidential warrant to accept cornerstone propositions (like "my faculties are reliable"), so bootstrapping isn't needed to get reliability in the first place.
4. Transmission-failure diagnoses: the bootstrapping argument is valid and closure holds, but warrant doesn't transmit across the inference because the conclusion's warrant was already doing work in the premises (the Davies/Wright style move, distinct from 3 in that it blocks the inference rather than front-loading the warrant).
5. Restricting closure itself for multi-premise or reliability-involving inferences, so the whole bootstrapping derivation never goes through.
6. The holist/explanationist line: no single self-confirming loop justifies, but a wide enough web of cross-checks between faculties does (I see the gauge agrees with what I see, etc.), so individual bootstrapping is bad but collective coherence is fine.

What I want: one more response that is genuinely a *different kind of move*, not a rewording, combination, or special case of these six. In particular it shouldn't just be "circularity is fine" with extra steps, and it shouldn't be another version of pre-loaded entitlement. It can be a response that dissolves the problem by challenging the setup of the case rather than the inference, or a move from outside standard internalism/externalism debates -- whatever, as long as it's structurally distinct. Then tell me: (a) which of my six it comes closest to and why it's still not that one, and (b) honestly whether it survives the obvious counterattack (e.g., does it just relocate the circularity somewhere else, or does it have to give up something epistemologists generally won't give up). I'd rather hear "this move exists but it's bad" than get a fake confident answer.
