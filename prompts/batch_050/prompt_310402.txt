My background is in analytic metaphysics, and I'm fluent in the standard taxonomy of the philosophy of time: A-theory versus B-theory, presentism, eternalism, the growing block, temporal passage, the moving spotlight. What I want is a systematic translation of Dōgen's position in Uji into that vocabulary, term by term where possible, followed by a residue report. Is Dōgen best read as a presentist with unusual commitments about which times are "contained" in the present? As denying the A/B distinction itself? As a B-theorist about the structure of time who insists on something like passage at the level of experience? Or does the translation break down at a more fundamental point?
