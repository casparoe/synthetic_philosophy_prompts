ok so last week i glanced at my phone in traffic and missed a cyclist by maybe half a second. nothing happened, no harm, but i keep feeling like i “owe” something anyway, while my friend who actually clipped someone years ago says the guilt only matters because of the outcome. make me a “what’s actually doing the work in moral luck?” sorting quiz for people who’ve just had a near miss.

rules: 9 questions, each a concrete case (same reckless choice with/without bad outcome, same outcome from careful vs careless act, being born hotheaded, bad weather turning a small mistake fatal, discovering years later that your prank almost killed someone, etc). Each question gets 2-4 answer options phrased as gut reactions, not theory names. No option may say “Kant,” “Nagel,” “consequentialist,” etc.

Include a scoring key that maps patterns onto at least: resultant luck matters, circumstantial luck matters, constitutive luck matters, causal/epistemic luck only, plus these two made-up hybrids you should define inside the quiz: “near-miss absolutism” = the almost-harm itself creates a real moral debt even with zero damage, and “shadow-debt view” = no debt unless the bad outcome was genuinely probable in that moment, not just possible in hindsight.

Build it from cases first, then labels. If two views can’t be separated by any answer pattern, flag them instead of pretending. End each question with what the answer would make awkward for the respondent. keep it usable in a group chat, not a seminar, and don’t ask for sources.
