so I've been circling around suffering-focused ethics for a while and I want to write up where I've landed for a seminar, but first I want to know if my position is actually coherent or if I'm papering over cracks. Roughly what I believe:

1. Suffering is what matters morally. Happiness is nice but it doesn't *cancel out* suffering -- an hour of agony isn't made even slightly better by adding a year of bliss on top, and a world containing both torture and paradise is straightforwardly worse than an empty world.

2. Despite that, I don't think creating new people is bad. Having kids who'll probably live mostly happy lives seems fine, even good, even though every actual life contains some real suffering.

3. No number of mild bads can add up to one severe bad. A trillion pinpricks is still not as bad as one instance of torture, and if I had to choose I should always prevent the torture first, no matter the numbers on the other side.

4. When donating I should just do the math: expected suffering reduced per dollar, even across species. If shrimp sentience is, say, 10% likely and the intervention is cheap enough, that's the right call even over charities helping humans with near-certainty.

5. Painlessly killing someone who wants to keep living is seriously wrong -- not just wrong because of side effects or because it scares everyone else, but wrong in itself.

Which of these are actually in tension and which only look like it? My sense is that 1 and 2 get flagged a lot but I think there's a way out, and I'm more worried about stuff I haven't noticed between 3, 4 and 5. Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs me. If the whole package can be saved with one revision, say which.
