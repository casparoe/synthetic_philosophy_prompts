I've settled on a position about Pascal's mugging, and I want its weak points mapped before I commit to it in print. The position: the mugging poses no threat to expected utility maximization as such. The flaw, I hold, lies entirely in the agent's epistemic situation — the probability that the mugger can and will deliver astronomical payoffs collapses as the offer is scaled up, or the promised hypothesis fails to cohere — so once credences are assigned correctly, declining the deal is simply what expected utility recommends. No revision of decision theory is needed, and proposals that cap utility, neglect tiny probabilities, or impose lexicographic thresholds are overreactions that sacrifice good cases to kill a merely apparent problem.

Tell me what would count against this. Don't survey the standard responses; give me a map of the position's exposure. Which intuitions, formal results, or possible cases does it stand or fall with? For example: if someone exhibits a principled, non-ad-hoc probability assignment under which paying the mugger genuinely maximizes expected utility, is my position dead or merely wounded? Would a mugging variant with a trustworthy mechanism rather than a stranger's promise refute it, or would I have to deny such cases are coherently describable — and is that denial costly? Does my view predict anything about the structure of rational credence that utility-capping or probability-neglect views don't, such that some case could separate them? And where would a sympathetic defender concede defeat — say, if every method of shrinking the mugging probability also wrecks ordinary long-shot reasoning we accept — versus dismiss a defeater as changing the subject?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, note which are live rather than merely logically possible, and give an honest verdict on whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability and whether that would even be a defect in a view of this kind. Close with a final line of the form \answer{...} naming the single defeater that should worry me most.
