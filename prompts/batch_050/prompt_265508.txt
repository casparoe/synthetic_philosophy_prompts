Act as someone who genuinely knows both ends of this topic — the Indian religious and philosophical traditions where karma is a technical doctrine, and Western analytic ethics where people argue about moral desert — and think carefully before you write. Here's the situation. I have a hospice nurse who, near the end of the book, says something like "karma will handle him" about an abusive man who will never see a courtroom — he got away with everything and he dies comfortable. She says it flat, like reporting the weather, and the scene ends there. My critique partner flagged that the line only lands if I, the author, know what precise claim she's making, because each possible claim implies a different woman. So take the slogan — "karma," as in "what goes around comes around," as people actually throw it around — and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, as a menu. To start you off, here are the ones I've half-absorbed from culture and I want to know if they're even coherent:

2. A desert claim without the cosmology: it's not that the universe *will* punish him, it's that he *deserves* punishment, and saying "karma" is just pointing at that desert.
3. A psychological prediction: cruel people rot from the inside — guilt, paranoia, inability to love — so the punishment is built into the deed.
4. A social/functional reading: saying "karma will handle him" is really a move to stop the listener from seeking revenge or to console someone powerless, not a metaphysical claim at all.
5. Something about character: what you do shapes what you become, so his comfort is fake because of who he had to turn into to get it.

For each reading (including any I missed): is it trivial, substantive-and-plausible, or probably false? Does it need the full technical doctrine (rebirth, cosmic causation) to make sense, or does it survive as secular common sense? And — this is the actual point — which readings would let my nurse say the line *flat, as fact, about a man who visibly suffers nothing*, without her being either deluded or secretly talking about something else? I suspect that constraint kills most of the menu and only leaves one or two readings, and the surviving one changes who she is. Tell me if I'm wrong about that.
