I'm a junior in high school, and for my philosophy elective's end-of-term project I have to submit a "position statement" on education policy that will be workshopped by the class, where the other students try to find internal inconsistencies. I'd like to find them first. Here are the five things I currently believe about school choice:

(1) Parents have a strong presumptive right to direct their children's education. The state may regulate private schools and homeschooling only to enforce basic safety and a minimal core curriculum (literacy, numeracy, civics); beyond that, families should be left alone.

(2) Education is largely a positional good: a big part of its value to a student lies in how it compares to what other students receive, not just in the absolute level of knowledge and skill it produces. Two students can both become better educated while one of them gains and the other loses in relative standing.

(3) Choice and competition make the system better. Pressure from charters, vouchers, and open enrollment pushes underperforming public schools to improve, because schools that lose students lose funding and have to respond.

(4) Where a public school is oversubscribed, seats should be assigned by lottery rather than by entrance exams or interviews. Selecting on "merit" mostly rewards children for things they didn't earn — innate ability and home environment — so it's not actually fair.

(5) It is unjust when a child's life prospects depend heavily on their parents' wealth, and a school system should be judged partly by how far it breaks that dependence.

My request: audit this set for joint tenability. Tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which pairs only appear to. For each real tension, rank it by severity and say exactly what would have to give — not just "revise (4)" but what the revision would cost the rest of the set. I'd also like you to check one thing in particular: whether the reasoning I gave for any one commitment, if I applied it consistently, undermines a different commitment I stated. Finally, name the single cheapest revision that resolves the most serious tension, and say what I'd lose by making it. Please don't just flag everything as equally problematic — the whole point is to find out which parts of my position are load-bearing and which are decorative.
