Having retired from a career in experimental science, I now sit on the advisory board of a research institute that must regularly arrive at collective verdicts -- for instance, whether a proposed line of research is methodologically sound, whether it addresses an important question, and consequently whether it should be funded. In trying to formalise how the board should vote, I stumbled onto the judgment aggregation literature and the discursive dilemma, and I have been unsettled to learn that apparently reasonable voting rules can deliver collectively inconsistent verdicts even when every individual member is perfectly consistent. Before I read any more proposals for particular aggregation procedures, I would like the specification first.

Please write the desiderata that an adequate procedure for aggregating the judgments of a group into a collective judgment must meet. For each requirement, state it precisely and give the consideration or case that motivates it -- for example, the demand that collective judgments be consistent and complete, that the procedure treat all voters equally and all issues impartially, that the collective verdict on each proposition depend only on the individual verdicts on that proposition, that no single voter be decisive on every question, and so on through whatever further requirements you judge genuinely load-bearing. For each, I would like to know: which failures it rules out, and whether it is a non-negotiable condition of adequacy or merely the preference of one camp (for instance, I gather that some authors relax proposition-wise independence while others treat it as sacrosanct -- I would like to understand what is actually at stake in that dispute rather than be told which side to join).

Most importantly: if some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable -- as the impossibility results in this area seem to suggest -- please say so explicitly and identify the minimal conflicting set, since that, to me, is the real deliverable. I would also like to know which single requirement excludes the most intuitively attractive procedures, and whether the familiar escape routes (aggregating only the premises and letting the conclusion follow; voting only on conclusions; weighting judgments by distance from the individual profile; relaxing completeness) amount to satisfying the desiderata or to quietly abandoning one of them.

A brief scorecard of the main contender procedures against the list is welcome but secondary; the criteria and their justifications are what matter to me. Finally, please indicate how confident you are in the characterisation of each desideratum and its motivation, and single out the point in your answer where you are least certain you have represented the state of the debate fairly.
