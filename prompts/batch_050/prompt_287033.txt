So I'm drafting a little position statement for a political philosophy reading group and want to check it holds together before people start poking at it. Roughly where I've landed, all within a broadly Nozickian framework:

(1) Taxing income from labor is morally on a par with forced labor — the state is claiming partial ownership of your working hours.
(2) The Wilt Chamberlain argument shows that voluntary exchanges will mess up any distributive pattern, so no patterned or end-state principle of justice can be right; justice is about how holdings came about, not the shape of the final distribution.
(3) A flat per-person head tax, where everyone owes the same fixed amount each year to fund courts and national defense, is totally unproblematic.
(4) Taxes on carbon emissions and pollution are fine, since polluters are imposing harms on others without their consent.
(5) Since we can never realistically trace the full history of acquisitions and transfers, in practice we should treat currently held property as legitimate and just enforce voluntary exchange from here on out.

Does this package hang together? Tell me which pairs are actually in tension (and how badly), which only look like they conflict but don't, and if something has to give, what the cheapest revision would be. I'd rather you rank the problems by severity than just list everything as equally bad.
