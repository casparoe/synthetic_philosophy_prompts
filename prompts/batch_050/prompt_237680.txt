So I got properly buttonholed about this at the pub after an EA meetup last week and it's been chewing at me since, because I think I hold a bunch of things that might not cohere and I can't locate the faultline myself. Context: I've been reading SSC/LessWrong for a decade, I'm a scope-sensitivity person deep down, I give ~10% and use GiveWell-style reasoning to do it. But I also did history at uni and spent a lot of time in the literature on twentieth-century atrocities, and that part of me bristles at some of what the EA part of me says. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Scope sensitivity is a basic requirement of moral rationality, not an optional extra. If you can avert 1,000 deaths or 100 comparable deaths at the same cost, you're obliged to pick the 1,000, and someone who genuinely doesn't see why the bigger number matters is making a moral error, full stop. This is the whole foundation of how I donate and I think it's basically unassailable. (2) Those pub-style arguments about which was worse, the Holocaust or the Holodomor or the Great Leap Forward, are obscene. Not just tactless or politically loaded — I think the entire project of putting atrocities on a single ranked scale is a kind of category error that degrades the dead, even when the underlying numbers are accurate. (3) A death is a death: how bad a person's death is doesn't depend on whether it was intended, or who the victim was, or whether anyone survives to remember them. That Stalin-adjacent line about one death versus a million gets something right even if it's usually quoted approvingly by monsters. (4) Genocide is worse than an equal number of deaths from famine or gross negligence, because the intent to destroy a whole people is an additional wrong over and above the deaths — that's precisely why we needed the concept and didn't just file it under mass killing. (5) When my strong case-intuitions conflict with my favoured theory, I hedge rather than forcing the intuition into the theory's mould; moral uncertainty is real and I try to act on it.

Does this hang together? I genuinely can't tell whether I've got one clean consistent position with some rough edges, or whether I'm smuggling in something in one of these that another one rules out. I'd like an audit rather than a lecture: the actual tensions, ordered by how serious they are, with what would have to give in each case and what that costs me — including, if there are any, the pairs that look tense but actually dissolve on inspection, because I suspect at least one of my worries here is illusory. And be specific about which commitment is cheapest to drop or amend, because at the moment I can't tell whether the problem is with (2), with (4), or with something in how I'm holding (1).
