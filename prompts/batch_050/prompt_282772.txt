I sit on a city council committee that advises on the regulation of autonomous delivery services. One operator has proposed that the AI system which routes and supervises its fleet be registered as a distinct "electronic entity" with its own insurance fund: contracts would be made in its name, and anyone harmed by a delivery vehicle would claim against that fund rather than against the company itself. A colleague on the committee argues there is nothing novel here — commercial firms are themselves legal devices we invented to hold property, enter contracts, and bear liability, so extending a similar status to the AI is merely the next step in an old practice. I have an inarticulate sense that the analogy conceals an important difference, something to do with who is left holding responsibility when things go wrong, and whether such an entity can meaningfully be punished, compensated, or wound up in the way a firm can — but I cannot yet state the objection cleanly. Before our next session, set out where the corporate analogy genuinely holds, where it breaks down, and which questions I should put to the company's representatives to test whether the proposal allocates responsibility well or merely shelters it. Please answer in British English.
