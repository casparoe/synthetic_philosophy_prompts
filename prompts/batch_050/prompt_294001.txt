I sit on the conduct committee of a professional institute, and next month we must decide a pair of linked cases. Two members, independently, resolved to sell confidential client files to a rival firm; both took every step within their power to do so. In one case the transfer went through and clients were harmed; in the other the exchange failed because the buyer's payment was reversed at the last moment, and no client ever learned of it. Our tariff ties the sanction to the harm caused, which would mean expelling the first and merely cautioning the second. Last night I had a long and unresolved argument with a friend about this. Her view, which she traced to Abelard, is that wrongdoing consists entirely in consenting to what one knows one ought not to do, so the two members are equally culpable and the difference in outcome is mere luck that a conduct panel has no business pricing. Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting: it should recommend a concrete sanction for each member with reasons the committee can vote on, state whether our tariff should continue to track outcomes, and take my friend's position seriously enough to identify the strongest point in its favour — and what it would cost us, institutionally, to accept it.
