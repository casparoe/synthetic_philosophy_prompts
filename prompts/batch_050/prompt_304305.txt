I've been reading popular accounts of moral foundations theory on my own — the finding that self-described liberals base their moral judgments mainly on care/harm and fairness/cheating, while conservatives spread theirs across those plus loyalty, authority, and sanctity. What interests me is the argument over what this shows. One side says conservatives are more morally complete, drawing on the full palette; the other says loyalty, authority, and sanctity aren't genuinely moral concerns at all — that calling them 'foundations' launders tribalism, deference, and disgust into moral reasons. The two sides seem to have traded versions of this for years without moving. I want a diagnosis, not a survey. Is this really an empirical disagreement (about whether conservatives actually reason from those foundations rather than using them as rationalizations after the fact), a conceptual one (about what must be true of a concern for it to count as moral), or a normative disagreement in disguise — one about which foundations reliably track moral truth — that no amount of psychological description could settle? Tell me which it is, with reasons keyed to what each side actually claims, and then take a definite position, with justification, on whether the psychology of left and right tells us anything at all about who is right.
