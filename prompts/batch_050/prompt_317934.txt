I've been arguing about this with my roommate all week and we've hit a wall, so I want outside help. The question sounds stupid but I think it's hard: what do pleasures actually have in common? My roommate says pleasure is just a feeling, like a flavor, and anything without that flavor isn't pleasure. So I've been polling my own gut on cases, and here's where I land. The pleasure of a hot shower when you've come in freezing from the rain: obviously pleasure, obviously good. The pleasure of finally seeing how a tricky proof works after days of being stuck: definitely want to count it as pleasure and as genuinely good, but honestly it feels *nothing* like the shower, there's no warm glow, it's more like a click. Scratching a mosquito bite: intense, undeniable, but somehow worthless, maybe even slightly bad, like the pleasure is part of the trap. Sitting through a devastating sad film and being glad I'm watching it: I'd say I'm enjoying it, but "pleasure" feels like the wrong word entirely. The moment a bad headache finally lifts: counts as pleasure I think, but it's weird that it needs the pain first, like it's borrowed rather than real. Feeling a little glow when a smug coworker's presentation goes badly: that's a genuinely pleasant feeling, I won't lie, but I really want to say it's not good at all, maybe even bad. And three hours of rock climbing where I'm totally absorbed: during it I don't notice any pleasant feeling at all, but afterwards I'd rank it among the best experiences of my year.

Here's why I'm stuck. The schadenfreude case feels pleasant but I deny it's good; the climbing case I insist is good but I can't find the pleasant feeling in it. So neither "feels good" nor "is good" seems to pin down what pleasure is, which kills my roommate's flavor theory but also kills the fix I tried first, which was that pleasure just means whatever state you'd rather continue than stop while it's happening (scratching passes that test and I don't want it to; the sad film fails it at some moments and I still count it). Someone in my ethics reading group suggested pleasure is whatever we'd choose for its own sake, but that seems to smuggle in the goodness question and anyway I might choose the sad film for its own sake.

So: is there one principle that captures my verdicts, or at least most of them, about both (a) which states count as pleasures at all and (b) which pleasures are worth anything? And if you think my two cases that feel nothing alike (the shower and the proof) actually do share something introspectible that I'm just bad at noticing, argue for that instead.
