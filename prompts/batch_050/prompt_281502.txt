I am a historian recently appointed to chair my city's monuments and street-naming commission, and we review about a dozen proposals a year to rename, reinterpret, or retain public honors for historical figures. Our deliberations keep collapsing into the same unproductive move: someone invokes the "man of his time" defense and no one can say what it would take for it to succeed or fail. Please convert that defense into a decision procedure my nine-member commission — most of whom are not historians — can actually run on a case: the questions to ask in order (e.g., whether the conduct was condemned by the figure's own contemporaries rather than only by us; whether the relevant moral knowledge was genuinely available to someone in the figure's position, or only available in principle; whether the offense is central or incidental to what the honor celebrates; whether honoring the figure now sends a message distinct from anything about the person), what kind of evidence settles each question, where the procedure terminates in a recommendation, and where it must hand the question back to the commissioners because it is a matter of present values rather than historical fact. Then run the procedure on three cases: Thomas Jefferson, Winston Churchill, and Christopher Columbus, including at least one where the verdict will be unwelcome to the figure's defenders, and state plainly what the procedure cannot decide. Present the procedure itself as a table with the columns Step, Question, Evidence Required, Pass/Fail Condition, and Judgment Call (what a commissioner must supply that no evidence can). Present the three case applications as a single JSON object keyed by figure name, with fields verdict, steps_where_procedure_determines_outcome, steps_requiring_commissioner_judgment, and likely_unwelcome_finding.
