I am preparing a handout for an undergraduate course on food ethics, and I would like a decision tree that sorts students into positions on the question: which animal products, if any, is it permissible to eat, once one takes seriously not only the suffering of farmed animals but also the wild animals killed and displaced by crop cultivation (ploughing, pesticides, harvest machinery, habitat conversion)?

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names. The branching questions should be concrete enough that a student can answer them without knowing any position labels, and they must include at least the following fault lines, phrased as cases or comparisons rather than jargon:

- whether moral weight scales with the number of individual animals harmed or with the total amount of suffering, given that a chicken yields far fewer calories than a cow and typically lives under far worse conditions, so that a calorie of chicken "costs" many more animals than a calorie of beef;
- whether wild animals killed as a foreseeable side effect of growing crops count against plant agriculture in the same ledger as the deliberate killing of farmed animals, or whether the intention/foresight distinction (or the absence of a relationship with the victims) breaks the symmetry;
- whether bringing a pasture-raised animal into a life worth living, and then killing it, harms it at all — the replaceability question;
- whether land use and greenhouse-gas emissions enter the comparison as moral reasons on a par with animal suffering or only as a separate prudential concern.

The leaves must include, at minimum, positions answering to these descriptions (name them if they are standard, otherwise mark them "unnamed"): abstention from all animal products; the view that one should eliminate chicken and eggs first because they dominate the per-calorie suffering budget, while beef is a comparatively lesser evil; the view that pasture-fed beef causes less total death than a crop-based diet, so the least-harm eater prefers beef and dairy to grains and soy; and a rights-based view for which the beef-versus-chicken arithmetic is beside the point.

Five to seven branching questions, no more than ten leaves. For every combination of answers the questions permit but no one seems to occupy, say briefly whether the gap exists because the position is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any two required leaves your tree cannot separate, and give each leaf one sentence stating its most awkward commitment.
