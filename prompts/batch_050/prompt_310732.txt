Yesterday evening, a friend and I ended a long argument about structural realism without convincing each other. I defend epistemic structural realism: mature theories can give us durable knowledge of relations and invariances, while their claims about unobservable entities remain vulnerable to replacement. My friend defends ontic structural realism: structure is not merely what we can know but what fundamentally exists, with objects at most derivative from relational structure.

Draft the joint statement we should have produced if the discussion had been a genuine adversarial collaboration rather than a contest. Do not decide which of us is right. Organize it as follows:

1. A numbered list of claims we could both sign. Separate claims about the historical and predictive success of mature science from philosophical interpretations of that success, and state explicitly what the no-miracles consideration and pessimistic induction do and do not establish.

2. A disagreements table with four columns: the issue, a formulation I would accept, a formulation my friend would accept, and the exact residual question. Address at least the survival of structure across theory change, the ontological status of objects, whether there can be relations without independently constituted relata, and the difference between mathematical, causal, nomic, and observational structure.

3. A section distinguishing substantive disagreements from apparent disagreements produced by using structure in different senses. If either position becomes true by definition once structure is interpreted broadly enough, say so and formulate the remaining disagreement more sharply.

4. A cruxes section. For each crux, identify a case or argument both sides would accept as relevant and record what each side would say about it. Include these cases:

- A successor theory recovers its predecessor’s central equations in a restricted domain but denies that the variables in those equations refer to independently existing objects.
- A successor theory preserves every confirmed relation among observable phenomena but replaces the predecessor’s mathematical structure with an inequivalent one.
- Two empirically equivalent theories describe the same network of relations, while one treats the relations as grounded in objects and the other treats objects as nodes constituted by the relations.
- A future theory eliminates continuous spacetime but preserves a stable pattern of correlations among events.

For each case, explain whether either of us could classify it as preserving structure, whether our answers differ, and what the answer commits us to say about realism.

Avoid two caricatures: epistemic structural realism is not general skepticism about unobservables, and ontic structural realism is not the view that every mathematical feature of a successful model directly corresponds to reality. End with a short list of discoveries, arguments, or distinctions that each of us would recognize as putting genuine pressure on our own position.
