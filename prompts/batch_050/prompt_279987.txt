Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a proponent of the argument from divine hiddenness (a Schellenberg-style reasoning: a perfectly loving God would ensure that no capable person remains in nonresistant nonbelief) and a defender of the hiddenness-friendly theist position (whether skeptical theist, soul-making, or free-will based -- pick whichever you think is the strongest representative, and say why you picked it). The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign -- about what nonresistant nonbelief actually is, about what the empirical facts on religious and irreligious experience do and don't show, and about what perfect love minimally requires; (2) the exact remaining disagreements, each worded neutrally enough that both parties would accept the phrasing of their own position; (3) the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether "resistance" can be ascribed to all sincere nonbelievers without circularity, and another is whether a love relationship with God could remain meaningfully free if God's presence were unmistakable -- and for each crux, a consideration, case, or conceivable finding that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance. Where part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, identify which part. Throughout, mark which premises are broadly settled among philosophers of religion who work on this problem and which remain genuinely contested; do not present a contested premise as the consensus.
