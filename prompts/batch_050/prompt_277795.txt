I run a small furniture workshop with my brother-in-law, and this spring we took on four apprentices, all young guys with basically no training. We keep butting heads about how to handle them. His view: the first three months should be deliberately brutal -- tear down everything they think they know, long hours, harsh criticism, constant correction of their grip and posture and habits, so we can rebuild them the right way. He says people come in as raw lumber and our job is to cut them into shape, and if we're soft on them early they'll never get there. I have a bad feeling about all this but hes way more experienced than me so Ive mostly gone along with it.

I mentioned it to a friend who studies Chinese philosophy, and she sent me this passage from Mencius (the Gaozi I chapter, Legge translation) and told me it proves my brother-in-law wrong:

"Can you, leaving untouched the nature of the willow, make with it cups and bowls? You must do violence and injury to the willow, before you can make cups and bowls with it. If you must do violence and injury to the willow in order to make cups and bowls with it, on your principles you must in the same way do violence and injury to humanity in order to fashion from it benevolence and righteousness! Your words, alas! would certainly lead all men on to reckon benevolence and righteousness to be calamities."

I'm not convinced it settles anything. For one thing Mencius is arguing against some rival philosopher here, and my friend admitted that Xunzi -- who disagreed with Mencius about human nature -- would more or less take my brother-in-law's side, with his own metaphors about steaming warped wood straight. So this starts to look less like an argument and more like two guys talking past each other with rival carpentry metaphors, and a metaphor about wood can't tell me how to treat actual apprentices. Or can it?

So: what is Mencius actually claiming here beyond the rhetoric, does the claim survive Xunzi's comeback, and what does any of it imply for whether I should push back on the brutal-first-three-months plan? Please answer as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion.
