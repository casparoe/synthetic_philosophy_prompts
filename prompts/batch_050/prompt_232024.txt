I'm taking an upper-level seminar in philosophy of cognitive science, and next week I'm leading the session on the mental imagery debate — the long-running dispute between depictive or pictorial accounts associated with Kosslyn and propositional or description-based accounts associated with Pylyshyn. The room is split between psychologists and philosophers, and in earlier sessions I noticed people tend to sort themselves by which label they find congenial rather than by what they actually think the experiments show. For my session I want to hand out a decision tree that leads a reader from the naive starting point — it certainly seems to me that I picture things in my head — to a named position, purely on the basis of how they answer diagnostic questions.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first state the questions, then the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space. The leaves must include Kosslyn-style depictivism, Pylyshyn-style propositionalism, an enactive or sensorimotor account, a hybrid or dual-code view, and an eliminativist or deflationary position that denies imagery involves representations at all; add unnamed leaves for coherent but unoccupied answer combinations, with one sentence diagnosing why nobody is there (incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored). Every branch node should be a question about a concrete phenomenon or a hypothetical experimental outcome that someone can answer without knowing which position it commits them to — for instance, whether the fact that scanning an imagined island takes longer for greater imagined distances really shows a spatial medium or could instead reflect tacit knowledge and experimenter demands; what mental rotation timing data would have to look like to force analog processing; what to say about people who report no imagery whatsoever yet perform fine on spatial tasks; and whether imagery does causal work in reasoning or is epiphenomenal decoration. Avoid nodes like 'do you think images are depictive?'

Two further requests. First, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate (quasi-pictorial versus fully depictive accounts come to mind) rather than papering over the gap. Second, for each named leaf, append one sentence identifying the finding or phenomenon that position has the hardest time accommodating. Aim for roughly eight to twelve branch nodes, ordered from the most theory-neutral questions to the most committal.
