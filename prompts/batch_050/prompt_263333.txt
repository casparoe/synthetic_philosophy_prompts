I'm on the technical advisory board of a mid-sized AI company (think a few hundred employees, one decent frontier-ish model). We need to decide our deployment strategy for the next model, and the board is split:

- Option A: ship it as a pure "tool" API -- stateless, answer-a-question-or-complete-a-task calls, no memory, no ability to act in the world on its own initiative. The safety team likes this because in theory a tool has no goals, so no goal misalignment, and each call is inspectable.
- Option B: ship our own agentic product -- the model gets long-horizon tasks, some tool access, persistent state, but with our own monitoring, sandboxing, and a human-approval layer we design ourselves.

The argument tearing the board apart: the pro-tool people say agents are where the danger lives, full stop. The pro-agent people say this is confused, because the market will just wrap our tool API in agentic scaffolding anyway -- LangChain-style loops, auto-retry, agents-calling-agents -- except then the agency lives in a thousand janky third-party wrappers we can't see or control, instead of in one system we designed. Basically they're claiming "tool vs agent" is a property of the whole system-in-use, not of the artifact we ship, so Option A doesn't actually buy what it pretends to buy. There's also a wrinkle the CFO keeps raising: the safety properties people attribute to tools (passivity, no mesa-optimization of the deployment, etc.) may not survive fine-tuning the model to be maximally *useful* as a tool, since a really good tool arguably needs to model and pursue the user's goals.

Draft the board's decision memo. Pick A, B, or some hybrid (e.g. tool API plus published/audited reference scaffolding), give a concrete recommendation the board can vote on, and include an honest steelman of whichever side loses -- in particular, if you recommend B, take seriously the worry that this is just rationalizing the more profitable option, and if you recommend A, take seriously the objection that we're exporting agency to actors less careful than us.
