I work in formal epistemology and a colleague cornered me with this argument against accuracy-first epistemology (my preferred framework). It feels like it proves way too much but I can't locate the exact step where it slips. You're an expert in epistemic utility theory -- think carefully, maybe brainstorm a couple of candidate diagnoses before commiting to one.

(1) The only purely epistemic value is accuracy: closeness of a credence to the truth. (That's just the founding commitment of the accuracy-first program.)
(2) For any proposition, credence 1 in a truth (or 0 in a falsehood) is strictly more accurate than any intermediate credence, on any reasonable scoring rule.
(3) So the epistemically best credence function, full stop, assigns 1 to every truth and 0 to every falsehood. Calibrated, evidence-tracking credences are epistemically worse than this function.
(4) Epistemic rationality is about doing as well as possible by the lights of epistemic value: if one credence function is epistemically better than another, the better one is the one rationality favors.
(5) So whenever an agent could adopt extreme credences instead of calibrated ones, rationality favors the extreme ones -- hedging with 0.6 or 0.7 is at best a compromise for the timid.
(6) In particular, the reckless guesser who goes all-in on heads at credence 1 and happens to be right is epistemically better than the careful agent who assigns 0.5 on the basis of knowing the coin is fair.
(7) But that's absurd. Epistemic appraisal has to be sensitive to the evidence the agent actually had, not just to how the world happened to turn out -- the lucky guesser is lucky, not rational.
(8) So (1) has to go: accuracy cannot be the whole of epistemic value, and the accuracy-first program rests on a mistake.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I suspect the trouble is somewhere between (3) and (5) but I can't articulate it. If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (1) and (7) and sacrifice whatever's in between -- but tell me honestly if that's the expensive option. Also say a word about whether the standard accuracy-first machinery (dominance arguments for probabilism, expected accuracy, immodesty) already contains the resources to block the argument, or whether the accuracy-firster has to add something new.
