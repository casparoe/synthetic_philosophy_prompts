I'm putting together a seminar unit on the demarcation problem and I'm collecting objections to Popper's falsificationism as a criterion for what counts as science. Here's what I've got so far, and I dont need any of these repeated:

1. The Duhem-Quine point: theories never yield predictions on their own, only together with auxiliary assumptions and initial conditions, so a failed prediction never logically pins the blame on the theory itself. You can always save it by adjusting an auxiliary.

2. Statistical theories: a probabilistic claim like "this coin is fair" is compatible with any finite run of outcomes, so strictly speaking nothing ever falsifies it, and yet it sure seems like good science. So falsificationism seems to demarcate away a big chunk of actual science.

3. Historical failure: real scientists don't abandon theories at the first anomaly, and often they're right not to. If 19th century astronomers had been good falsificationists they would have dumped Newtonian mechanics over Uranus instead of discovering Neptune.

4. Conventionalism about basic statements: Popper himself admits that the "basic statements" doing the falsifying are accepted by a kind of decision or convention, which threatens to make falsification a matter of social agreement rather than logic.

5. Self-application: the falsifiability criterion doesn't look falsifiable itself, so by its own lights it isn't science -- at best a methodological proposal, and then why should scientists adopt it over some other proposal?

So: give me one more objection that isn't on this list and isn't a rewording, special case, or combination of the five (e.g. "auxiliary adjustments can be ad hoc" is just #1 in a hat). I specifically want it aimed at falsificationism as a demarcation criterion, not at Popper's wider philosophy of science or his politics or whatever.

And be straight with me about the quality of what you come up with: say how confident you are that it genuinely lands as a distinct objection, and single out the part of your answer you're least sure of -- whether that's that it's really new, or that it actually works.
