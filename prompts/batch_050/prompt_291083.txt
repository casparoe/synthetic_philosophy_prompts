I run the secretariat for my department's hiring committee and I'm tired of our votes coming out incoherent, so I want you to turn what judgment-aggregation theory actually knows into a procedure I can enforce. Setup: 8 of us, about 30 candidates a year, two-hour meetings, and the dean can send a decision back if our reasoning doesn't hang together. Our rule for an offer is: strong research AND (strong teaching OR exceptional promise), AND no unresolved concerns about the candidate file. Last year we had a case where a majority said yes to research, a majority said yes to the teaching-or-promise clause, a majority said no unresolved concerns -- but only 3 of 8 would actually vote to make the offer, because the yeses on each condition came from different people. We made the offer anyway and it was a mess.

Write me a step-by-step procedure for running these votes that covers at least: how to decide, for a given decision, whether to vote condition-by-condition and compute the result versus voting directly on the offer; how to handle the fact that different members have seen different evidence (only 3 people read the writing sample, only 5 were at the job talk -- does condition-by-condition voting even make sense then?); how to detect when the order I put questions on the agenda is changing the outcome, and what to do about it; what to do with abstentions and with a condition that's itself a disjunction; and when a supermajority or unanimity rule is worth the cost of basically handing a veto to one crank. For each step say which distinctions from the theory had to survive the simplification and which you dropped. Include a small test suite of vote-profile tables I can run the procedure on to check it's behaving, plus three worked examples, one of which must be a case where the procedure forces an outcome I personally won't want (e.g. it blocks the candidate everyone in the room would individually hire, or it makes us hire someone the majority of us would reject). End with a section on what the procedure can't settle and where I just have to use judgment, because I know some of this isn't mechanizable. Don't give me a bibliography, just build the thing from the theory itself -- doctrinal paradox, discursive dilemma, premise- vs conclusion-based aggregation, all of that, but operationalized for someone who hasn't read any of it.
