"In Epistemic Injustice, Miranda Fricker distinguishes testimonial injustice from hermeneutical injustice. Explain the distinction. and here's the answer i'm supposed to grade:

"Fricker's basic idea is that people can be wronged specifically in their capacity as knowers. Testimonial injustice happens when a hearer gives a speaker less credibility than they deserve because of an identity prejudice -- the classic case is Tom Robinson in To Kill a Mockingbird, where the all-white jury simply will not believe a black man's testimony over a white woman's, even though the evidence is on his side. The injustice here is located in the individual hearers: each juror is guilty of letting racial prejudice distort their credibility judgement, and that's what makes it an injustice rather than just an honest mistake.

Hermeneutical injustice is the mirror image of this. It happens when a speaker's experience is so unfamiliar to their hearers that the hearers can't make sense of what the speaker is saying. Fricker's example is women who experienced sexual harassment before that concept existed -- when a woman in the 1960s tried to explain what her boss was doing, her coworkers' prejudices about what women are like made them unable to understand her, so her testimony failed to get through. So both kinds of injustice are done by individual hearers: in the testimonial case they downgrade the speaker's credibility, in the hermeneutical case they fail to comprehend her meaning. Either way the fix is the same -- train people to listen to marginalized speakers more openly.

On the last question: no. If nobody treated the person with prejudice, there would be no hearer doing anything wrong, and since epistemic injustice is always something a hearer does to a speaker, there'd be no injustice. The person might be badly off in some general sense, but calling it an injustice requires a perpetrator."
