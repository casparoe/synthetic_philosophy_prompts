So I've been a LW/SSC reader for years and I've absorbed the whole predictive processing thing -- the "it's Bayes all the way up" posts, Surfing Uncertainty summaries, all that. Recently I got into an argument with a friend who's doing a philosophy PhD about "cognitive penetration of perception" -- the question of whether your beliefs, desires, expectations etc. can literally change what you see, not just how you interpret or attend to what you see. There's apparently a big literature: Fodor-style modularity people saying perception is "informationally encapsulated," other people pointing at experiments like the one where holding a gun makes people more likely to judge ambiguous objects as guns, and a whole side-debate (Siegel's, I think) about whether penetration creates a nasty epistemic circularity -- if my belief that he's angry makes me see his face as angry, can that perception then justify the belief?

Here's my problem: every time my friend explains a position, I automatically translate it into predictive processing vocab in my head and it feels like it just dissolves. Perception is posterior inference in a hierarchical generative model, beliefs live in higher levels as priors, of course high-level hypotheses feed down and shape lower-level inference -- so is "cognitive penetration" just... precision-weighted top-down signals? And modularity/impenetrability would be some architectural claim about message-passing being blocked between certain levels? But then my friend says no, that's too cheap, the real question is about which kinds of influence are perceptual vs. post-perceptual, and whether the influence is the right kind to threaten perception's justificatory role, and at that point I lose the thread because none of that seems to have any analogue in the framework.

So that's the request. Restate the cognitive penetration debate as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary of hierarchical Bayesian predictive processing / generative models -- priors, posteriors, precision weighting, message passing between levels, direct vs. mediated influence (I also know some ML if that helps, feel free to use it). Which positions translate cleanly, which ones you have to fudge or distort, and then the residue report: what's in this debate that simply cannot be expressed in that vocabulary at all. I have a suspicion the epistemic-circularity worry is the main casualty, but I can't tell if that's because predictive processing is missing something real (a normative dimension? something about justification?) or because the philosophers are tracking a distinction that collapses once you realize everything downstream of a prior is "penetrated" by it. Don't just tell me what I want to hear on that last part -- if the translation is hiding real content, I want to know where.
