A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about an ordinance our city council is considering: it would make a bar owner criminally liable whenever anyone under twenty-one is served alcohol on the premises, regardless of whether the owner checked IDs, trained the staff, or was even in the country at the time. I insisted that punishing someone who took every reasonable precaution is straightforwardly unjust; she replied that if the penalty is a modest fine and the alternative is that owners stop caring who works their taps, the law is simply pricing a risk into a business model, and that calling it "punishment" is tendentious. We never resolved whether we disagreed about justice or only about words, and I want an instrument that would have forced the issue.

Write a decision tree that starts from the question "May the state impose criminal liability without fault?" and leads to a named position. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of views: each node should be a yes-or-no question phrased so that someone could answer it without already knowing which position it commits them to. Candidate splits might include whether a criminal conviction necessarily expresses blame, whether negligence counts as genuine fault, whether voluntarily entering a regulated activity transfers responsibility onto mistakes made within it, and whether deterrence gains could ever justify condemning the blameless — but use whichever questions actually carve the space. The leaves must include, at minimum: the view that punishment without culpability is categorically unjust; the view that strict liability is legitimate only for regulatory offenses carrying light penalties; the view that apparent strict-liability cases usually conceal negligence, so no real faultlessness is involved; and the view that strict liability functions like an enforcement lottery and is justified when people can cheaply avoid the sanctioned activity. Note any combination of answers that no one defends, and say briefly whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate.

Keep the entire tree under 600 words, with each node a single sentence and each leaf capped at the position's name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
