So I've been arguing with a friend about supertasks, and I've landed on a position I want stress-tested. My view: Thomson's lamp doesn't show that supertasks are impossible, it shows that the lamp story is just underdescribed. The switching process fully determines the lamp's state at every time strictly before the two-minute mark, and whether it's on or off *at* the mark is a separate fact that the description simply leaves open — so the "paradox" dissolves once you notice the question was never answered by the setup, not that it couldn't be. More generally, my position is that no supertask is ruled out by the mere fact that it involves infinitely many steps in finite time; a supertask only becomes genuinely impossible when its later stages depend on there being a well-defined outcome of the infinitely many earlier ones in a way that can't consistently be supplied (like setups that need a "last" step, or machines whose behavior at the endpoint is supposed to be causally read off a divergent sequence).

What would count against this? I'm not asking for a rundown of famous supertasks for their own sake — I want to know what would have to be true for my dissolution to fail. For instance: is there a way to argue that the endpoint state *isn't* a free parameter after all, that physical or logical continuity considerations force a definite answer the setup can't provide? Does my "impossible only when the endpoint must be read off a divergent sequence" criterion actually carve things at the right joints, or does it wrongly classify some case as possible/impossible — e.g., does it let through something obviously absurd, or rule out something like the staccato run or relativistic supertask proposals that should count as coherent? And what would a defender of the genuine-impossibility view themselves concede as the strongest challenge to *their* side, so I can tell whether I'm just winning against a strawman? Please sort the candidate defeaters from "would sink the view outright" to "would be annoying but survivable," and be honest if it turns out my position is insulated by its vagueness about what counts as 'reading off the endpoint' — if the criterion is unfalsifiable as stated, tell me, and how to sharpen it.
