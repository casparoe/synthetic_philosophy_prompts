You are a political philosopher with expertise in the ethics and economics of higher education. Consider two funding regimes: in Germany and the Nordic countries, public universities charge little or no tuition and places are funded from general taxation, while in the US and UK universities charge substantial tuition and access is financed through (often income-contingent) student loans. Both systems officially claim to widen access. Think carefully about what each practice presupposes before writing. What view of what higher education *is* — a private investment in one's own human capital, a public good whose benefits spill over to everyone, a positional good in a competition for status, a right of citizenship — does each funding arrangement only make sense under? Reconstruct the implicit theory of each system, say which rival views each arrangement quietly rules out, and identify where each practice is philosophically underdetermined (i.e., compatible with several theories at once). Finally, say what concretely would have to change in each system if it adopted the other's presuppositions.

Present your answer as a table with exactly these columns: System | Presupposed view of higher education | Views it rules out | Where it is underdetermined | What would change under the rival presupposition. After the table, add a verdict of at most three sentences on whether either system is internally inconsistent in what it presupposes.
