So my brother-in-law took an intro philosophy class last fall and it broke his brain in a very specific way. He now claims -- sincerely, as far as I can tell -- to doubt basically everything: that the outside world exists, that other people have minds, that the past happened. Fine, whatever, lots of people go through this phase. But here's the weird part: nothing else about him changed. He still sets his alarm for work, still locks his car, still gets genuinely furious when the neighbour's dog craps on his lawn. When I asked him why he bothers reminding me about his birthday if he doubts I exist, he said the doubt is "at the philosophical level" and the rest of life is seperate. And honestly I can't even point fingers, because I read a bunch of stuff about determinism last year and now I tell people I don't believe in free will -- but I still feel proud when I get praised at work and I still want to throttle the guy who cut me off in traffic yesterday. The belief just sits there at the top and never seems to flow down into anything.

I've been trying to figure out what this even is, and none of the obvious words fit. It's not lying, he means it. It's not hypocrisy in the normal sense, because he's not preaching some standard to others that he fails to meet. It's not quite self-deception either, since he's not avoiding some uncomfortable truth -- if anything he's showing the doubt off. A friend said it sounds like the mirror image of that alief thing (you know, where you believe the glass walkway is perfectly safe but your legs still shake) -- here the stated attitude and the whole bodily, practical attitude point in opposite directions, except the practical side is obviously the one driving the bus. Another friend mentioned that late Wittgenstein has something about how you can't doubt everything at once, doubt only makes sense against a background of stuff standing fast, and there's some image about a riverbed that can shift while guiding the water. But I think that's about a different thing? That's about what *can't* sensibly be doubted, and I'm asking about doubt that gets professed but never actually does any work.

So: is there a name for this -- a professed belief or doubt that doesn't ramify, where the words come unhooked from the whole system of action and you could swap the words out without anything else in the person's life moving? And I'm not just fishing for a label, I want the carving. Does a case like this show the professed doubt isn't a real belief at all (some "beliefs" are just noises?), or does it show beliefs come in layers and the top layer is cheap? Where are the edges -- what would count as a genuine instance versus, say, bad faith, or compartmentalization, or just not taking your own views seriously? And how does it relate to that Wittgenstein riverbed business, if at all?
