I’m helping design the funding policy for a fictional philanthropic foundation, and my coauthor and I keep talking past eachother. She thinks rational agents should maximize ordinary expected utility, with all apparently risk-averse behavior explained by diminishing marginal utility. I’m sympathetic to risk-weighted expected utility, where outcomes are ranked and the probabilities of achieving at least each outcome receive weights reflecting an agent’s attitudes toward risk. Please write the joint statement we might produce after a genuinely adversarial collaboration about which theory should guide the foundation.

Use these recurring test cases, and feel free to add variants: a safe portfolio that reliably helps 10,000 people versus a moonshot with a 1 percent chance of helping 2 million; several partially correlated grants versus one large grant whose outcome is more uncertain but potentially transformative; and a staged program in which the foundation must decide now how it will react to intermediate evidence later. Format the result as a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, followed by a disagreements table with columns for the issue, each side’s formulation in terms that side would accept, the underlying crux, a case or argument that bears on the crux, and what each side predicts that case shows. Make sure the statement distinguishes risk aversion from ordinary diminishing marginal utility, separability from mere caution, and disagreement about rational choice from disagreement about the foundation’s moral obligations. Don’t turn it into a verdict on who wins, don’t pad it with citations, and explicitly mark any disputes that seem verbal or any cruxes where neither side currently has a clean test. End with a short note on where the joint formulation is most likely to go wrong and what argument or case would make you redraw the boundary between the agreed claims and the genuine disagreements.
