Draft the joint statement that could emerge from an adversarial collaboration between two colleagues of mine: a particle physicist who holds that philosophy has contributed nothing of substance to physics in living memory — no accepted physical result of the last several decades, she claims, rests essentially on philosophical argument, and physicists who philosophize are at best doing physics informally — and a philosopher of physics who holds that several landmark developments (he cites Bell's theorem, the interpretive work around quantum measurement, and debates over spacetime symmetries) were philosophical in character and that physics' apparent autonomy is an artifact of how credit gets assigned after the fact. The document should contain: first, a numbered list of claims both can sign, including claims about what a scientific result is, about the historical record both accept, and about which activities each concedes the other is right about; second, a disagreements table stating their residual disputes in wording each side would endorse for its own position — I expect at least one dispute over what counts as a contribution to physics rather than commentary on it, and one over whether a contribution counts as philosophical when it was made by a working physicist; third, for each dispute, the crux that would move at least one party, together with a concrete historical case or counterfactual test both agree bears on it and each side's prediction about how that case should be read. If you conclude that part of their disagreement is merely verbal — say, over the extension of the word philosophy — identify which part, and draft the more precise claims they would then be left disagreeing about.
