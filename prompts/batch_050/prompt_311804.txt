Okay so I keep having this argument with my brother about what counts as manipulation vs just normal persuasion, and I realized my own takes are all over the place. Going by gut:

- My dentist told me "9 out of 10 patients who skip the crown end up back here within a year" instead of "10% of people who get the crown didn't actually need it" -- feels like manipulation even though both are true.
- A charity ad showing a specific crying kid with her name and story to get donations: fine, or at least I'm fine with it.
- A timeshare guy showing a specific crying kid to sell timeshares: obviously not fine. Same technique though.
- My friend spent an hour arguing me out of a job offer using every trick in the book, including stuff he knew would hit my specific anxieties: fine, because he genuinely thinks the job would be bad for me and he told me upfront that was his goal.
- My manager announcing layoffs as "an exciting opportunity for personal growth" for those affected: gross.
- My wife asking me "do you want to call your mom tonight or this weekend?" when she knows I'll just pick one instead of asking whether to call at all: I laughed when I noticed, so I guess fine? But also she does it ON me, deliberately.
- A waiter complimenting everyone's outfit: mildly annoying but fine.
- A pickup artist "negging": clearly over the line.
- A politician giving a genuinely scary but accurate statistic about crime, chosen specifically because he knows fear moves voters more than hope does: I honestly can't decide on this one.

Is there one principle here or am I just tracking like three different things (truth, intent, who's on the receiving end)? And what does it say about the politician case -- that's the one I keep flip-flopping on, since "he's right about the facts" and "he's playing you" both seem true.
