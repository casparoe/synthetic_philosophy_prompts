I spent most of last weekend in a drawn-out argument with an old friend, and neither of us budged. I maintain that the elegance of a mathematical or scientific theory -- its simplicity, unity, inevitability of structure -- is genuine evidence for its truth, over and above how well it fits the data. My friend maintains that elegance is at best a useful temperament and at worst a bias with good press: nature is under no obligation to be beautiful, and the celebrated cases where beautiful theories won are, on her view, survivorship decorated after the fact, with "simple" redefined to fit whichever theory happened to survive. We eventually agreed that what we should have done from the start was behave like an adversarial collaboration and write down a joint statement. I want you to draft the document we failed to produce.

Draft it as the statement both of us could sign. Begin with the claims we would both accept. These should include, at minimum: an agreed characterization of what each side means by elegance or simplicity, since I suspect half our fight was that I meant the structure of fundamental laws while she meant the tidiness of working models, and neither of us noticed; an agreed list of historical cases each side concedes is genuinely awkward for its own position (I will concede cases where the elegant option lost badly, she will concede cases where beauty seems to have led rather than followed the evidence); and an agreed statement that both positions must explain why physicists' aesthetic judgments so often correlate with one another rather than varying like taste in music.

Then state our residual disagreements, each formulated precisely enough, and neutrally enough, that the person holding the position would sign the wording of it. Do not let either formulation smuggle in the other's concession; my friend's version of my view must not be a parody of a mystic, and mine of hers must not be a parody of a philistine.

Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us. I have two candidates I want included. First, whether the historical record of beauty-guided conjectures shows a hit rate above what a blind guesser would achieve, once you correct for the fact that we only remember the winners -- call this the prospective-versus-retrospective crux. Second, whether elegance can be given a prospective definition at all: could a committee blind to the data rank candidate theories by beauty in advance, and would that ranking predict which theories later succeeded? For each crux, name the evidence, argument, or test both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance -- for instance, what I predict a beauty-blind registry of physicists' conjectures would show, and what she predicts it would show.

One formatting constraint: I want the whole thing in continuous prose, the way one would write a long letter. No headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables. Signal the transitions between the agreed claims, the disagreements, and the cruxes through the writing itself rather than through any visual apparatus.
