Build me a thought experiment where Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge delivers a clean closure failure: the subject knows p, knows full well that p entails q, and believes q on that basis, but doesn't know q. The failure has to fall out of the tracking conditions themselves, not out of the subject being careless or irrational about the deduction -- I want a case where doing everything "right" by Nozick's lights still loses you the knowledge. Constraints: one ordinary everyday setting (an office, a family dinner, a commute, that sort of thing), no brains in vats, no evil demons, no lotteries, and definetly no fake barns. The scenario itself should be one paragraph.

Then show me it works. (1) State p, q, and the method the subject uses to form each belief. (2) Run Nozick's conditions on both beliefs and pinpoint exactly which condition the belief in q fails while the belief in p passes, with the relevant nearby worlds spelled out. (3) Check what a safety theorist says about the same case: does safety also deny knowledge of q here, or does your scenario split safety and tracking apart? If it splits them, say which verdict you find more plausible. (4) The honest part: identify the single feature of your scenario doing all the work, tell me what happens to the case if you take that feature out, and give the best attack a defender of closure (someone who insists knowledge is closed under known entailment, full stop) could make against your construction -- not a strawman version, their strongest move. Finish with whether you think the case makes closure failure look like a genuine cost of tracking, or like a bullet Nozick was right to bite.
