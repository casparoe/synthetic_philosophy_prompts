So I need help with a speech and it's a weird one. I'm the harbormaster in a small fishing town in Maine, and every June we do the blessing of the fleet. This year is a mess because we have TWO boats both claiming to be the Nellie B, our famous old lifeboat from around 1912 that pulled something like forty people out of the water over the years.

Here's the situation. One boat has been tied up at the lifeboat station the whole time, but it's been repaired continuously for over a century -- planks, ribs, engine, all swapped out piece by piece as they rotted, so the guys reckon maybe nothing original is left. The other one is the project of a retired shipwright, Dale, who was on the repair crew for decades and quietly took home every original timber they cut out and kept them in his barn, and last year he finished reassembling them into a boat. So the continuously-working boat has no original wood, and Dale's boat is ALL original wood but spent fifty years as a pile of lumber in a barn. Both are going to be floating in the harbor during the ceremony, with Dale's grandkids on one and the lifeboat crew's families on the other, plus a lot of tourists who just want to see pretty boats.

It gets worse: even the Nellie B's early history is shaky. No records before about 1930, the famous rescues are mostly stories people tell, and honestly nobody knows if some of them happened. I keep thinking of the opening of Plutarch's Life of Theseus (the Clough translation), where he says about the far-off periods of his subjects: "Beyond this there is nothing but prodigies and fictions, the only inhabitants are the poets and inventors of fables; there is no credit, or certainty any farther." Which is of course the very Life where the ship-repair story comes from, which feels almost too on the nose.

Write me the speech, about 5 minutes. Constraints: it can't just declare a winner (I'd have a riot either way, and also I genuinely don't know), but it also can't do the mushy thing of saying they're both equally the real Nellie B without saying what that could even mean. It should use the Plutarch bit above to do actual work -- the Nellie B's history is exactly the kind of edge-of-the-map territory he's describing -- and it has to land for Dale's family and the crew's families without baffling the tourists. Oh and its fine to be funny in places, its a festival not a philosophy seminar, but no jokes at either family's expense.
