Okay so I've been stuck for weeks on whether "better than" actually has to be transitive, and I want to figure out what my own gut reactions commit me to, because I suspect they don't add up. Here are my verdicts:

1. One day of a really brutal migraine is worse than a month of mild headaches. And a month of mild headaches is worse than a year of barely-noticeable twinges. But I'm not willing to say the single migraine day is worse than a *lifetime* of twinges so faint you have to concentrate to feel them. That last one seems obviously better than the migraine.
2. One person being tortured for a year is worse than any number of people — a billion, a trillion — each getting one dust speck in the eye. Specks just don't add up to that. But I also think a scam that takes $5 from a million people is worse than one that takes $1000 from one person, which sure looks like small harms adding up.
3. If my coffee got imperceptibly worse every single morning, I'd never have a reason to switch cafés on any particular day. But two years in I'd be drinking sludge and clearly should have switched. So "no reason to switch today" can't be the whole story every day.
4. I'd take 200 really good years over 100 slightly better ones, and 400 slightly-less-good over the 200, and so on — every step feels right. But I won't accept the conclusion that zillions of lives of barely-okay days, just barely worth living, beat the original 100 great years.

Is there one principle about betterness that gets all of these, or close? If something has to go, tell me which verdict and why that one.
