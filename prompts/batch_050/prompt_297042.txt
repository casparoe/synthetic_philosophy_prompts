so a friend of mine has this argument that the nordic model is basically exportable to any reasonably functional state, and each step looks fine to me on its own but the conclusion seems way too strong, like it proves too much. here it is as best i can reconstruct it:

(1) The good nordic outcomes -- low poverty, high mobility, generous welfare without fiscal collapse -- are produced by a particular policy mix: high broad-based taxes, universal (not means-tested) benefits, centralized wage bargaining, active labor market policy, and so on.

(2) Nothing in that mix depends on things other countries can't have. Denmark, Sweden and Finland have no oil to speak of, so it isn't resources, and it obviously isn't geography or climate.

(3) A policy mix is just a set of decisions. Any government with the standard tax-and-spend levers can in principle make the same decisions the nordic governments made.

(4) The usual objection is that you also need high social trust and high tax compliance, which other countries lack. But trust and compliance are themselves largely *products* of the institutions: universal programs treat everyone the same so nobody feels cheated, visible return on taxes makes people willing to pay, etc. So trust follows the policies rather than the other way round.

(5) So the only real obstacle to exporting the model is political will, and any country that adopted the full mix would converge on roughly nordic outcomes within a generation or two.

The reason i think it proves too much: if (5) were right, it would make the nordic countries' success look like a fluke of having chosen the right levers first, and it would predict that places which have adopted big chunks of the mix should already be visibly converging, which doesnt obviously seem to be the case. But i cant pin down which step does the damage.

If i had to keep one thing it would be (2) -- i really don't want to say it's oil or homogeneity all the way down. But i want to know what rejecting the bad step actually commits me to. If the answer is "trust can't be manufactured by policy," i want to know whether that's a claim about causal order or about something else, and how much of the exportability hope survives it.
