I am preparing a workshop handout for philosophers of biology and computer scientists who keep talking past one another about whether DNA is literally a code. Some mean the codon-to-amino-acid mapping; some mean any sequence that causally controls development; others think code requires representation, correctness conditions, or a convention that could have been otherwise. Construct a taxonomy of positions rather than writing another general overview. First build the tree from the logically prior questions—what the alleged bearer of codehood is, whether causal specificity is sufficient, whether selected function can ground correctness or malfunction, whether arbitrary reassignment is required, whether an interpreter or decoding mechanism must exist, and whether codehood is a property of DNA itself or of an entire organism-environment system. Then locate positions in that space. Use descriptive labels such as translation-only semantic realism, genome-wide semantic realism, selected-content realism, causal-information theory, mechanistic particularism, explanatory pluralism, and eliminative metaphorism; do not organize the answer around named scholars or provide citations.

Every branching node should be a question that could be answered without knowing which position it favors, and every leaf should state both the position’s central claim and its most awkward commitment. The tree must be tested against these cases: a synonymous codon substitution that changes RNA folding or protein production; a genetically engineered lineage in which a codon is reassigned; an enhancer mutation that changes gene expression without changing any amino acid; an inherited DNA-methylation pattern that is later erased; RNA editing that makes the mature transcript differ from the genomic sequence; a sequence whose effect depends only on DNA stiffness or shape rather than any decoding table; and human-designed DNA used to store ordinary digital data. Include at least two logically available but apparently unoccupied combinations of answers, explaining whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot reliably separate instead of forcing an artificial distinction. Close with a six-question placement quiz derived from the tree, with a scoring key that maps answer patterns to the leaves. The final line must be \answer{one sentence stating whether this taxonomy treats “Is DNA a code?” as one question or as several separable questions}.
