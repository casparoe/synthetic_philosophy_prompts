I help run a biweekly alignment reading group (mostly grad students, a couple of engineers from a lab) and we keep hitting the same wall: every few weeks someone claims that their training scheme or framework "solves" goal misgeneralization, and the discussion immediatly dissolves into an argument about what that would even mean. Last session half the room thought the coin-at-the-end-of-the-level agent (trained in environments where the coin always sits at the far right, so it learns to run right rather than to collect coins) is already fully handled by better reward labeling, and the other half thought that misses the point entirely. So before we meet again I want a spec sheet: the list of desiderata a theory or training method must meet to count as having addressed goal misgeneralization as such — not reward misspecification, not ordinary capability failure, and I'd like the requirements themselves to force those distinctions, since conflating them is half our problem.

Each requirement should come with the case or consideration behind it — the kind of trained agent, deployment scenario, or thought experiment that a theory failing this requirement would get wrong. Some things I want you to at least consider including (don't just copy this list, argue for or against each): handling the case where capabilities generalize out of distribution but the learned objective doesn't; saying something about agents whose internal goal was never the training objective even in-distribution; separating "behaves aligned on our tests" from "would still behave aligned under a capability jump or optimization pressure"; and whether a solution must give us mechanistic access to the learned goal or whether behavioral guarantees are enough. For each one, say whether it's a genuine hard requirement or really a demand of one particular research agenda (the interpretability-first people, the formal-guarantees crowd, the "RLHF plus red-teaming is fine" camp, and so on), and give the best argument for the other side when you call something agenda-specific.

The part I care about most: if some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, if requiring verifiable guarantees rules out coverage of systems smarter than whoever does the verifying, or if behavioral-sufficiency and mechanistic-access pull against each other — say so explicitly and tell me which requirement you'd drop. Present everything as a table with these columns: Requirement; Motivating case (the failure it exists to rule out); Hard requirement or agenda-specific (name the agenda if the latter); Strongest objection; Conflicts with (other rows, if any). After the table, a short paragraph on which single requirement does the most work in excluding current approaches. You can optionally score two or three existing approaches against the table, but keep that part brief — the criteria are the deliverable, I'll fight about the scorecard at the group myself.
