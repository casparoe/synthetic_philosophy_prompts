Write a citizens' information leaflet, around 600 words, issued by the Ministry of Welfare of a state that has adopted lexical threshold negative utilitarianism as its official morality: suffering above a certain threshold of severity cannot be outweighed by any amount of pleasure, while suffering below the threshold may be traded off against pleasure in the ordinary aggregative way. The leaflet should be deadpan and scrupulously helpful in tone — announcing, perhaps, the defunding of the national pleasure festival, explaining the Suffering Exchange Bureau where citizens register minor pains for offsetting, and answering frequently asked questions about where the threshold was set and by whom. The satire must work by taking the view seriously rather than by caricaturing it: a reader who knows the position should recognise the genuine pressure points — the commensurability of pleasure and pain, the arbitrariness of any chosen threshold, whether the threshold governs trades between pains or only pain against pleasure, and the worry that the doctrine licences dreadful conclusions — without the leaflet ever breaking character to name them. Append a short note, outside the fiction, saying which features of the position you treated as settled doctrine, where you leaned on a contested reading of how a lexical threshold is meant to operate, and which objection you found hardest to dramatise fairly.
