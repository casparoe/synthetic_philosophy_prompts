So I got into it in a comment thread about the problem of divine hiddenness. Someone ran a version of the argument — if a perfectly loving God existed and wanted a relationship with every person, he'd make his existence at least findable for anyone open to it, so the sheer amount of honest, nonresistant nonbelief in the world is reason to think there's no such God. And the reply, delivered like a mic drop, was: "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence." Which felt way too fast, but I couldn't pin down exactly why.

So: take that slogan apart. What precise claims could it actually be making? Lay them out as a menu. Some candidates I can sort of see — a narrow logical point (lack of evidence doesn't *disprove*), a stronger probabilistic claim (finding no evidence leaves your credence where it was), a claim about certain special cases (when you wouldn't have expected evidence either way, absence tells you nothing), maybe a piece of debate etiquette rather than an epistemological thesis at all. But I'm sure there are readings I'm missing, and I want the whole menu, not just my guesses.

For each reading: is it a platitude, a live substantive claim, or actually false? I have a hunch the catchy version everyone quotes equivocates between a boring true reading and an interesting false one, but maybe that's unfair. Be especially careful about the difference between "we looked and found nothing" and "we haven't looked," and about cases where the *pattern* of non-findings is itself the finding — because I think the hiddenness argument is really making the second kind of point, and the slogan might be answering a claim nobody made.

Then tell me which reading the person in my thread needed for the slogan to do the work they used it for. Does the hiddenness argument actually depend on anything the true readings rule out? And if the honest answer is "absence is evidence of absence exactly when you'd expect the evidence to show up if the thing were real," then the whole dispute collapses into a fight about whether a loving God would make himself more evident — in which case say so, and tell me who's actually burdened with what in that follow-up fight.
