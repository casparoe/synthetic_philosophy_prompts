So I'm on a philosophy of physics reading group and we just hit the Newton's bucket unit. Our group has one die-hard substantivalist (absolute space is a real thing, rotation is motion relative to it) and one die-hard relationist (all motion is relative to other bodies, Mach-style), and they keep yelling past each other about the bucket. I want to force them to actually do an adversarial collaboration.

Here's the wrinkle I want the whole thing to turn on. Everyone fixates on the bucket, but later in the same Scholium to the Definitions in the Principia, Newton gives a second, weirder experiment that almost nobody talks about: imagine two globes connected by a cord, revolving around their common center of gravity, out in empty space with nothing else around. Newton says that even then, you could read the tension in the cord and thereby figure out the quantity of their circular motion -- i.e., tell whether they're truly rotating, with no external reference bodies at all. (That's a paraphrase of the passage, not a quote, but it's the bit of the Scholium I mean.) The relationist in our group claims this argument is actually worse for Newton than the bucket is. The substantivalist claims it's the cleaner case because there's no water surface to get distracted by. I'm honestly not sure who's right.

Write the joint statement these two should produce if they had to sign one document. Structure it exactly like this:

1. Agreed Claims -- a numbered list of about 6-8 claims both of them can sign, covering what the globes-and-cord setup shows at minimum, what's just kinematics, and what GR does and doesn't settle. (~150 words)
2. Genuine Disagreements -- a short table with one row per disagreement, each phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position, not a strawman. (~200 words)
3. Cruxes -- the 2 or 3 sub-questions where, if one got settled, at least one side would have to budge. For each crux give a concrete case, observation, or argument both agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts about it. (~200 words)
4. Verbal Residue -- one short paragraph saying which part of their fight, if any, is really just about words (e.g. what "rotation" even means in a universe containing only the two globes), and why. (~100 words)

Dont adjudicate -- I'm not asking you to pick a winner. But also don't make the agreement section so bland it's vacuous. If the globes thought experiment actually smuggles in a premise both of them missed, say so in the Agreed Claims section.
