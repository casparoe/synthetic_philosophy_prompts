So some background: I'm not an academic or anything, I work in insurance, but I've been reading Nietzsche's genealogy of morality book on my lunch breaks for like two months and I can't stop thinking about it. The basic idea as I understand it: the moral values we all treat as obviously good — humility, meekness, turning the other cheek, feeling guilty about our natural instincts — didn't come from God or from reason, they got invented historically by weak and powerless people as a kind of spiritual revenge against the strong people who ruled over them. Call the strong ones "evil," call yourself "good," and you've flipped the scoreboard without ever winning a single actual fight. And Nietzsche thinks this whole resentful mindset, he calls it ressentiment I think, is still baked into what we consider moral today. I find this disturbingly convincing and also kind of upsetting.

Here's my situation. I'm in a bible study group at my church (long story, my wife got me into it) and next month it's my turn to lead the session. Instead of doing a normal reading I want to present this genealogy idea and argue that at least some of what we call Christian morality has this resentful origin, and that once you've seen where a value came from you can't just take it at face value anymore. I want to be clear I am NOT trying to destroy anyone's faith, I genuinely want to stress-test the idea and see if it survives contact with people I respect.

The audience, roughly ten people: mostly kind retired folks who've been in the group for decades and take the sermon seriously. Our pastor, who's pretty open-minded but will definately defend the faith if pushed. One guy, Dennis, who took a philosophy class in 1974 and brings it up constantly. And my sister, a social worker who thinks everything is about power dynamics anyway, so she's my only ally in the room.

Prep me for the discussion. I want:

- the objections I'll actually get from THIS group, phrased the way they'd actually phrase them — not philosophy-seminar phrasing, but like how a retired nurse would put it (e.g. "so are you saying being kind is just a scam?")
