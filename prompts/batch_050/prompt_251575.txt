I'm a novelist working on a book where a bunch of the characters are tangled up in bad things in a way I don't have good vocabulary for, and I want to get the moral texture right, so help me out.

Here's the pattern I keep writing. A character does something small in the service of something awful, but the awful thing would have happened anyway without them, and everyone including the character knows it. Like: a hotel clerk who's pretty sure the guests on the third floor are doing something terrible, but if he refuses to check them in, the night manager just does it instead. Or a translator at some dodgy government office who tells herself her replacement would translate the same documents by Tuesday. None of these people cause the harm in any normal sense. Take them out of the story and the story ends the same. And yet, as a writer, I can feel that they're stained by it in a way that the random bystander isn't, and readers feel it too.

I've been calling it complicity in my notes but when I looked around that seems to mostly mean something closer to the legal thing, aiding and abetting, where your help actually makes a difference. My characters' whole thing is that their help makes no difference. It's also not quite the dirty hands thing, because they're not making some tragic choice for the greater good. And it's different from just benefiting from something bad, like inheriting money with a horrible history, because my people are actually participating, doing a little action, showing up every day.

So: does this specific configuration have a name in ethics? If it does, what's the nearest established concept, and how exactly does my version differ from it? If there are a few neighboring concepts, walk me through which one fits best and where the edges are -- like, is the clerk the same as the translator, and is either of them the same as the customer who just buys the product? I care less about getting a label and more about getting the carving right, because my scenes depend on the distinctions. If nothing fits, coin me a term with a definition I could steal for an epigraph.

Keep it under 300 words, I'm on a deadline.
