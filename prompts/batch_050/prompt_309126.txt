I'd like an audit of a set of commitments I've arrived at concerning the special composition question — the question of what it takes for some things jointly to compose a further thing. Here is where I've landed:

(1) Composition is restricted. Sometimes a plurality of things composes something further and sometimes it doesn't. I reject both compositional nihilism (there are no composite objects at all, only simples) and unrestricted composition (any objects whatsoever, however scattered or heterogeneous, compose a further object).

(2) Composition is never vague. For any candidate things, it is always fully determinate whether or not they compose something. More generally, I hold that vagueness is always a feature of our representations and never of the world itself: there is no such thing as indeterminate existence or indeterminate parthood.

(3) The correct answer should be conservative. It should deliver the ordinary objects of common sense — tables, trees, cats — while withholding the gerrymandered ones, such as the alleged object consisting of my left shoe and the moon. A view that secures (1) only at the price of denying that there are tables is, as far as I'm concerned, barely better than nihilism.

(4) The answer may not rest on contact or similar physical relations. Whether some things compose a further thing should not turn on how near one another they happen to be, or on how tightly they happen to be fastened together. Such relations seem to me metaphysically shallow — unfit to mark a boundary in being itself.

(5) Composite objects can survive turnover of their parts. A cat persists through metabolism; a table persists through the replacement of a leg. Whatever answers the composition question must be compatible with this.

My question is whether this package hangs together. I suspect that some pairs of these commitments strain against one another, but I can't tell which strains are genuine and which only appear so. Please audit the set. Identify the genuine tensions, in order of severity, and for each one state which commitment would have to be revised or abandoned and what that revision would cost me. Identify also any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, and explain why they are apparent only. I'm not asking for a survey of the standard answers to the special composition question; I want to know what my particular combination of commitments forces, and where the cheapest exit lies.
