okay so my flatmate is writing his dissertation on Heidegger and last week over dinner he tried to explain to me that a hammer only really shows itself to you when it breaks — while you're hammering, the hammer sort of disappears, your attention is on the nail and the shelf and the job, and it's only when the head flies off mid-swing that it suddenly becomes this clunky dumb object sitting in your hand. I think I got the gist, but honestly what I found funniest is the implication that product reviews are impossible in principle: you can only actually write about a tool at the exact moment it stops functioning as one.

So write me a product review, deadpan consumer-magazine style (think Wirecutter or Which?), around 600-700 words, from a hypothetical review site that has fully committed to this idea and refuses to review any tool until it has failed. The review is of a mid-range claw hammer. The jokes should come from getting the phenomenology right rather than from nonsense: the testing lab grumbling that during two weeks of successful hammering there was literally nothing to report; a star-rating system that sorts failure into three grades — the hammer you merely start noticing, the hammer that keeps forcing itself on your attention, and the hammer that flat-out refuses and strands the whole project (you don't have to use the German, but if you do, keep conspicuousness/obtrusiveness/obstinacy distinct); a 'how we test' section that keeps accidentally describing the entire workshop — nails, lumber, the shelf, the flat-pack it's for — instead of the hammer itself; and a contemptuous aside about a rival magazine that reviews hammers by weighing them and photographing them on a bench, which our reviewer treats as performing an autopsy. Include at least one letter from an angry reader whose hammer has never once broken and who is furious the site can't tell him anything about it. It should be funny to someone who's read Being and Time but still scan as a real review for someone who hasn't. Stay in genre the whole way through, no winking at the reader.
