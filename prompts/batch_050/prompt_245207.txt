In one of the dialogues Hofstadter imagines an enormous book that fully simulates Einstein's brain on paper: the pages plus a set of turning rules encode everything his neurons would do, so you can "talk" to Einstein by writing a question, flipping pages according to the rules for however many years it takes, and reading off the answer the book produces. The obvious question the dialogue pushes is whether there's anyone home in that process -- whether Einstein, or something, is being conscious in the flipping.

Here's the argument. My roommate says this book is exactly what a large language model is: a static lump of symbols that only "says" things when an outside process mechanically walks through it, so if you wouldn't call the book conscious (and he thinks obviously you wouldn't), you can't call the model conscious either -- it's the same trick, just fast enough to fool people. I pushed back that the analogy might actually cut the other way: if you follow Hofstadter and grant the book-in-motion could be conscious, the model inherits that, and the only difference is speed and the material of the pages. He said the flipping is done by a mindless rule-follower and so is the GPU, which he thinks helps him, but I genuinely can't tell who that point belongs to.
