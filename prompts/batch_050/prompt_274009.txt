For a philosophy workshop I've been asked to circulate a written statement of my views on killing, constraints, and numbers so that the group can stress-test them. Before I do, I'd like an audit. Here is the full set of commitments, stated as I actually hold them:

(a) In the transplant case, it is impermissible for the surgeon to kill the one healthy patient to distribute his organs to five who will otherwise die.

(b) In the standard switch case, it is permissible to divert the runaway trolley onto the side track, killing one worker to save five.

(c) In the loop variant — where the side track loops back to the main track, and the diverted trolley only stops short of the five because it collides with the body of the one worker on the loop — diverting remains permissible.

(d) The constraint against killing is grounded in the inviolability of persons: each person has a moral status such that she may not be treated as a mere means, and no aggregate of benefits to others can override that status.

(e) Consequences nonetheless matter morally: other things equal, five deaths are worse than one, and a world containing fewer killings is morally better than a world containing more.

(f) Constraints are not absolute. There is some threshold — killing one to prevent a million deaths, say — at which the prohibition lapses and killing becomes permissible, even if I cannot say precisely where the line falls.

(g) It is permissible to redirect an existing threat away from five and toward one, but impermissible to initiate a comparable harm to one in order to save five, even when the death toll would be identical.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs or triples genuinely cannot be jointly maintained, which only appear to conflict and dissolve once the relevant distinction is drawn, and for each real tension, what would have to give. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally urgent — I want to know which inconsistency would most embarrass me in discussion, not a list that flags everything. For the most serious tension, tell me the least costly revision available and what it costs me: which commitment I'd have to weaken, and what other plausible views I'd forfeit by doing so. Finally, if a single revision would resolve more than one tension at once, identify it. I have no prior sense of where the problem lies, so please don't assume I'm attached to any particular member of the set.
