Two housemates of mine had a long argument last week, and I'd like a neutral adjudication. Their student government can either block or allow an invited speaker known for claiming that the Holocaust's death toll has been wildly exaggerated.

Anna, an exchange student from Germany, said it should be blocked: denial of this kind is not a contribution to any search for truth but functions purely as an attack on the dignity and standing of Jewish members of the community, and a democracy need not extend its freedoms to those who use them to undermine the equal citizenship of others. Ben replied that no institution should be trusted to declare a position too false or too harmful to be heard; such a power will not stay in its box, bad speech is best answered with more speech, and suppression mainly hands the speaker a persecution narrative while driving his audience underground, where it festers unrefuted.

Anna answered that the more-speech remedy assumes an audience weighing arguments in good faith, whereas denial spreads through repetition and normalization, so every public rebuttal chiefly amplifies it; and she noted that European bans have existed for decades without the predicted slide into general censorship. Ben countered that the slope has already been slid: the same dignity rationale underlies European laws punishing insults to religious feelings or to the state, which he considers clear overreach. Anna's final move was that line-drawing of this sort is routine, since even American courts decide what is true in defamation, fraud, and perjury cases, so the claim that government cannot judge truth proves too much. Ben's final move was that defamation works precisely because it targets discrete, checkably false factual claims about identifiable persons, and no comparable machinery can be built for historical or political claims, so the exception proves his rule.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: say who is right about which specific point, identify anything both parties are missing, and give an overall verdict on who is more right. Please answer in American English.
