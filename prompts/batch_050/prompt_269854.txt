Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment pictures a vat of tiny marbles (simmballs) whose collisions, viewed from a sufficiently zoomed-out vantage, form discrete symbol-like patterns (simms) that appear to shove one another around; the standing question is whether the symbol level genuinely does causal work or is merely a compressed description of the substrate. Take the contemporary analogue: mechanistic interpretability research on transformer language models claims to identify features and circuits whose manipulation predicts and steers behavior, even though the only actual computation is matrix multiplication over activations. Please give your credence in the following precisely stated proposition: symbol-level descriptions of such systems capture causal structure that no substrate-level description could, even in principle, reproduce. Decompose it into (i) the probability that this non-spooky, emergence-compatible form of downward causation is coherent at all, (ii) the probability that current feature and circuit findings track real internal structure rather than artifacts of the probing method, and (iii) the probability that a sufficiently fine-grained substrate-level account would in principle deliver everything the symbol level predicts, leaving the symbol level indispensable only for computationally limited observers. Show how the terms combine into the final number, identify the term to which the result is most sensitive, and state what empirical finding would move that term most.
