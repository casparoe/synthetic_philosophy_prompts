I'm a software engineer, zero philosophy training, and I've been chewing on something since the whole chatbot wave started. Say I build a system that passes every behavioral test for intelligence anyone throws at it, forever, but under the hood it's just an absurdly large table of prerecorded question/response pairs with some nearest-neighbor interpolation on top. No reasoning, no model of the world, just lookup plus smoothing. From the outside it's indistinguishible from a real conversationalist.

Two questions. First: does this kind of system have a name in the literature, or does the position that behavioral tests settle the mind question just have to bite the bullet and say my glorified spreadsheet is minded? People invoke the turing test constantly but nobody mentions the brute-force version of beating it. Second, the case that actually bugs me is the in-between one: not a pure table, but a system that generalizes a bit beyond its training data and no further. Is there any established concept for where on that spectrum "real" understanding kicks in, or is that whole region unnamed?

After your prose answer, give me a JSON object with these fields: best_fit_name (string, or null if nothing established), nearest_concepts (array of objects with name and one_line_difference), spectrum_case_status (string: named, partially named, or open), and verdict (string: does the thought experiment sink behavioral criteria for mind, dent them, or leave them intact).
