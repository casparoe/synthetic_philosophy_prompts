Ok so I recently came across the Condorcet jury theorem — the result that if each voter is even slightly better than a coin flip at getting the right answer, then a big enough majority vote converges on the correct answer with probablity approaching 1. That got me thinking: why do we still rely on small expert bodies at all? Like, instead of a 12-person jury, a 9-justice Supreme Court, or some FDA advisory panel deciding whether a drug is safe, couldn't we just put the yes/no question to a few hundred thousand randomly picked citizens and go with the majority? The theorem says that once the group is big enough the crowd is basicly guaranteed to get it right, and it would be way more democratic to boot. So is the only reason we don't do this that experts are protecting their turf, or is there something about how the math cashes out in practice that I'm missing?
