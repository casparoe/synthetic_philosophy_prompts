I am a PhD student in philosophy (my background is in epistemology and value theory) currently drafting a dissertation chapter on whether recursive oversight schemes can deliver aligned artificial systems more capable than their overseers. I have spent the term reading the main proposals in this space -- iterated amplification and distillation, AI safety via debate, recursive reward modeling, and the various "humans consulting copies of themselves" constructions -- and I have become frustrated with how the discussion proceeds. Everyone argues about whether a given decomposition preserves what we care about by appeal to one illustrative task, and nobody, so far as I can tell, states up front what a scheme in this family would have to demonstrate in order to count as a solution. Before I write the chapter, I want that specification.

Please write the desiderata that a theory of alignment-preserving decomposition must meet: what has to be true, and what has to be shown, for an amplify-then-distill (or otherwise recursively decomposed) training scheme to count as transferring both competence and values intact to a system whose outputs the base overseer cannot directly verify. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it. The list should at least cover: the classes of tasks the theory must be able to handle (including tasks that resist clean decomposition -- long-range narrative or mathematical coherence, judgments requiring global gestalt, insights that are not the sum of their parts); the failure modes it must rule out (compounding error across iterations, strategically dishonest or adversarial sub-components, distributional shift between the oversight regime and deployment); and the epistemic situation of the overseer (mistaken, biased, or manipulable at the base level). It should also state which distinctions the theory may not collapse -- I have in mind, at minimum, competence versus alignment, the overseer's actual approval versus the approval they would give under ideal deliberation, and fidelity to the overseer's values versus correction of the overseer's errors, since I suspect these last two pull apart in a way most proposals silently finesse.

Two further requests, which matter more to me than the list itself. First, mark which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable and which are the characteristic demands of one research camp, and if some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable -- I have a hunch about the pair (a) perfect fidelity to the overseer's actual judgments and (b) correction of the overseer's mistakes, since the second seems to require a standard of correctness external to the overseer, which is exactly what the scheme was supposed to let us avoid; likewise for the trio of unrestricted capability amplification, base-level checkability of every sub-query, and superhuman performance -- then say so explicitly, because an inconsistent requirements list would itself be the chapter's main result. Second, identify the single desideratum that excludes the largest number of existing proposals, with the reasoning. If it helps, you may close by scoring iterated amplification, debate, and recursive reward modeling briefly against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than the scorecard. A numbered list with the motivating consideration stated under each item would be ideal.
