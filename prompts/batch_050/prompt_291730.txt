My friend Mara and I have this running argument that started at a pub quiz and has now spread to basically every news story we read. She's got very confident intuitions about which transactions count as "exploitative," and I suspect she's not even working from one rule but from vibes. Her verdicts so far: the pharma guy who jacked up a patented drug's price a few hundred percent? Exploitative, obviously. Payday lender charging a desperate renter triple-digit interest? Exploitative. Her wealthy neighbor who pays a local teen under the table at way below minimum wage to help out in his woodworking shop, when the teen is thrilled and mostly there to learn? Totally fine, she says. Unpaid internships at a glamorous magazine that students claw over each other to get? Exploitative, even though nobody's forced. A scalper reselling concert tickets at triple face value? Not exploitative, just annoying, because "nobody needs a concert."

So here's the case I want to spring on her, and I want your prediction first. A sneaker company opens a factory in a poor region and pays double the going local wage. Workers aren't destitute -- most had okay-ish options in farming or other workshops, and they line up for these jobs because the pay is genuinely better. But the wages are a tiny fraction of what the sneakers retail for, and a fraction of what the company would have to pay anyone back home. Is Mara going to call this exploitative or not? I can see it going either way: if her real rule is about whether the stronger party is leaning on someone's weak bargaining position, the workers here aren't that weak, so it comes out clean; if her rule is about whether the split of the gains is wildly lopsided, this looks terrible. I'm less interested in the "right" answer about sweatshops than in which rule she's actually been running.

Give me exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion: (1) your prediction for the sneaker case, (2) the rule you think best fits all five of her earlier verdicts, (3) the strongest rival rule that also fits them, (4) one new case that would tell those two rules apart, and (5) how confident you are and what in her track record makes this genuinely hard to call.
