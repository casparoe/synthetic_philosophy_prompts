ok, so some background: I'm a retired biochemist, spent about 35 years on protein folding and metabolic pathways, and in retirement I've been reading metaphysics, which my old colleagues think is a mid-life crisis arriving twenty years late. I've landed on a set of views about material objects and I'd like an audit before I embarrass myself at the philosophy society's reading group next month. Don't tell me which view to abandon in advance -- I genuinely don't know where the problem is, if there is one.

Here's where I've landed:

(1) There are no tables, chairs, or bicycles. There are only simples (fundamental particles, whatever physics settles on) arranged tablewise, chairwise, bicyclewise. When I say "pass the chair" that's loose talk that works fine in practice.

(2) But I am not in the same boat as the chair. I exist, as a single unified object, and so does my dog, and the oak tree in the garden. Living things are genuinely composite objects in a way artifacts aren't. I gather a philosopher called van Inwagen defends something in this neighborhood, which reassured me I'm not totally alone.

(3) Nothing is causally overdetermined. The particles do all the causal work there is to do -- my pushing the door open is, at bottom, particles moving other particles. No composite object adds causal oomph on top of what its parts are already doing. As a scientist this one feels non-negotiable.

(4) I persist through time despite constant turnover of my matter. I've been told most of my atoms get swapped out over the years, and I'm still me, so identity over time can't require keeping the same parts.

(5) There is no vagueness about what exists. Either a composite object is there or it isn't; "sort of existing" or borderline cases of composition strike me as incoherent. So no brute "it's just vague wether the heap is an object" business.

(6) The Ship of Theseus puzzle doesn't worry me: since (per (1)) there never was a ship, only simples arranged shipwise, there's nothing whose identity could go missing when the planks are swapped. Puzzle dissolved, not solved.

So: does this package hang together? Please go through it pair by pair where it matters, flag which commitments are in genuine tension and which only look like they are (I suspect some apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, and I'd rather be told which), rank whatever real tensions you find by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. If one single revision would clean up the most, tell me which and why. Be concrete -- "there's a tension somewhere in the vicinity" is not what I'm after.
