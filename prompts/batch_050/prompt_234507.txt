Exam question: Mira uses “Hesperus” for a celestial body visible in the evening and “Phosphorus” for one visible in the morning. She accepts “Hesperus is Hesperus” but initially rejects “Hesperus is Phosphorus,” later discovering that both names refer to Venus. Explain why this creates a problem for a purely referential account of proper names, how Frege’s distinction between sense and reference responds, and how Frege should explain the failure of substitution in the report “Mira believes that Hesperus is Phosphorus.” Mention one difficulty facing Frege’s account.

Student’s answer: On a purely referential account, a proper name contributes only its bearer to what a sentence says. Thus, if Hesperus is Phosphorus, replacing one name with the other should preserve what is said, making the identity seem trivial even though it can be a genuine astronomical discovery. Frege distinguishes a name’s reference, the object it designates, from its sense, an objective mode of presentation that determines the reference and is not merely a private mental image. “Hesperus is Hesperus” and “Hesperus is Phosphorus” therefore have the same truth-value but express different thoughts and differ in cognitive significance. Before Mira learns the astronomy, however, “Hesperus is Phosphorus” is false for her and becomes true only once she accepts it. In belief reports, Frege holds that names do not have their customary reference: in “Mira believes that Hesperus is Phosphorus,” they refer instead to their customary senses. Consequently, Mira can believe that Hesperus is Hesperus without believing that Hesperus is Phosphorus, because she associates the names with different senses. Substitution fails in such opaque contexts because the report tracks the believer’s mode of presentation rather than the object alone. One difficulty for Frege is explaining how communication remains possible if different speakers associate different senses with the same name, and what fixes a shared sense.
