Two of my coworkers got into a genuinely heated argument over lunch and I got voluntold as referee. Background: we run big fluid-dynamics sims for work. Marcus noticed that one of our sims, if you coarse-grain the grid in a specific way, is structurally identical to a small reinforcement-learning agent being punished -- the state transitions line up perfectly, including counterfactuals (if the sim's "environment" region had been different, the "agent" region would've responded differently). He says that means there's literally a suffering mind on our cluster, and rerunning the sim with harsher parameters would be wrong.

Dana says this is nuts. Her point: with a clever enough mapping you can find any computation inside any sufficiently complex system, even a rock, so mapping-based sentience proves nothing. Marcus fired back that the same is true of brains -- there's no reading of your neurons as "beliefs" that we didn't choose -- so either brains don't count either, or she owes a principled rule for when a mapping is legit. Dana's rule: a legit mapping has to be one the system was selected for. Our actual RL agents on the other cluster were trained to avoid simulated shocks, so the "avoiding pain" reading was baked in by a process; the fluid sim's reading was reverse-engineered afterward and does no work. Marcus: so if lightning randomly assembled a molecule-for-molecule copy of a person, it wouldn't suffer, since nothing selected it? That seems obviously false. Dana conceded that's the hard case but said there's still a difference: the fluid-sim "agent" only exists relative to a description that's more complicated than the sim itself, so we didn't find a mind, we built one -- in the description, not the hardware.

Then they both stared at me. Can you go through it and say who's right at each step? Does the "you can map anything onto anything" objection actually refute Marcus? Does the selection-history rule do what Dana wants, or does the lightning-copy case break it? And is there a distinction both are missing between a mind existing in a system vs in a description of it?
