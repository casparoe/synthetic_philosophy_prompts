For a philosophy of religion project, I need a dialogue between a Mu'tazilite theologian and an Ash'arite theologian arguing over the Islamic version of Euthyphro's question: is an act just because God commands it, or does God command it because it is just? To keep the debate concrete, have them argue about one specific test case: suppose God commanded the gratuitous torture of an innocent child — would that make it just? The Mu'tazilite should defend the view that human reason can know good and evil independently of revelation, so God, being necessarily just, could never issue such a command; the Ash'arite should defend the view that justice simply is whatever God commands, that nothing is obligatory for God, and that there is no genuine moral knowledge before revelation arrives. Both characters should be equally sharp: no strawmen, and each has to actually answer the other's objections rather than talk past them — for example, the Ash'arite pressing the Mu'tazilite on whether binding God to independent rational moral standards compromises divine omnipotence and transcendence, and the Mu'tazilite pressing back that if good just means whatever God happens to will, then calling God good says nothing and we lose any basis for trusting revelation in the first place. Don't end the dialogue with a both-sides-have-a-point truce: push the exchange until one side is forced into a genuine concession, and let the dialogue take a definite stand on which position survives.
