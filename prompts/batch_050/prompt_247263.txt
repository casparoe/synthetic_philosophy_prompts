For a presentation in my high school philosophy club, a classmate gave me her summary of the philosopher Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of kinds of minds, and I'd like to know how much of it to trust before I reuse it. She writes: 'Creatures come in four kinds. Darwinian creatures have all their behavior fixed by natural selection, so they cannot learn anything in their own lifetimes. Skinnerian creatures, like rats and pigeons, can learn by trial and error, but this is blind conditioning with nothing going on inside them. Popperian creatures can test hypotheses in an inner environment so that their bad ideas die instead of their bodies. Gregorian creatures, which means only humans, import cultural tools like language into their thinking and are therefore the only minds that are truly intelligent. The four kinds form an evolutionary ladder, so more advanced creatures leave the earlier mechanisms behind.' Some of this sounds right to me, but I'm suspicious of the parts about nothing going on inside animals and about humans leaving the earlier kinds behind. Which parts would Dennett accept, which are distortions or overstatements, and does her closing claim about a ladder follow from the accurate parts?
