So I got kinda obsessed reading about Thomas Hobbes's long war with the mathematicians of his day and I want a satirical piece about it. Write me the fake minutes of a meeting of the early Royal Society, maybe 600-700 words, at which Hobbes has shown up (uninvited, since they never made him a member) to present yet another proof that he has squared the circle, while John Wallis sits in the back composing a furious reply in Latin. Keep it deadpan -- the clerk recording the minutes is scrupulously neutral, maybe slightly too neutral, and the comedy comes through the minutes' formality rather than anyone winking.

The humor has to come from the actual substance of the dispute, not from making Hobbes a generic crank. So get the real stuff in there: Hobbes thinks geometry is the only genuine science because its objects are motions of bodies that we ourselves construct and can therefore fully know the causes of, which is also why he thinks he can do political philosophy the same way; he despises Wallis's arithmetic-algebraic methods as a scab of symbols with no genuine demonstration behind them; and he keeps producing these circle-squarings where each new version quietly abandons the claim of the last one without admitting anything was wrong. Wallis, meanwhile, wins every actual mathematical point and loses every exchange on style, since his replies are savage, pedantic, and enormous. The minutes should register things like the society voting to have Hobbes's diagram inspected, a member asking an innocent technical question that Hobbes answers with a definition of definition, etc. Feel free to invent a minor fellow or two.

One rule: don't put any actual quotations from Hobbes or Wallis in quotation marks. Paraphrase their positions in the clerk's flat voice instead. If a reader who knows the Squaring of the Circle controversy finishes it thinking "yes, that's exactly what they were like, only slightly worse," you've done it right.
