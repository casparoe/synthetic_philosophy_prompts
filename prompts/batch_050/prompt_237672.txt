I'm in a reading group that's been working through Dennett's stuff on cranes and skyhooks, and last week we hit an argument that none of us could settle. For anyone rusty on the terms: a crane is an explanation of design or complexity that builds it up slowly, by mindless local processes, with no cleverness smuggled in from above; a skyhook is an explanation that just posits the cleverness (a designer, a miracle, an unexplained capacity doing the heavy lifting). My friend insisted the meme's-eye view of culture -- ideas replicating, mutating, and being selected -- is a legitimate crane. I said it's a disguised skyhook, because the copying machinery doing all the work is the human mind, and human minds are exactly the kind of fancy designed-looking thing the crane was supposed to explain, so we've just moved the mystery rather than explained it.

In the course of arguing I coined something I now want stress-tested: call it "crane debt." Every crane, I claimed, borrows unexplained complexity from the level below it -- natural selection presupposes heredity and variation, which presuppose chemistry, and so on -- and an explanation only counts as a real crane if the debt chain bottoms out somewhere that genuinely doesn't need explaining (chemistry, physics), with no suspicious jumps along the way. My friend thinks this is ridiculous, that a crane is a crane wherever it happens to be bolted in, and that demanding the whole chain makes the distinction useless. She puts her credence that memetics is a genuine crane (in my crane-debt sense, i.e., the chain bottoms out cleanly) at about 0.8. I'm at 0.25.

So: give me your own credence for that proposition, and decompose it -- roughly, how much rides on whether cultural transmission actually has the fidelity/fecundity/heredity needed for selection to do real work, how much on whether the psychological machinery of imitation can itself be crane-explained without circularity, how much on whether my crane-debt criterion is even the right test (versus cranes being a level-relative notion), plus whatever other terms you think belong. A number and a line of justification for each, then the combination. Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what empirical finding or argument would move it the most -- and finally, whether you think my 0.25 or her 0.8 most likely reflects a disagreement about facts, or about what the crane/skyhook distinction is even for.
