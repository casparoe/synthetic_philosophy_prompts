I'm drafting a one-page statement of my views on the metaphysics and ethics of virtual worlds for a graduate reading group, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here are my commitments:

1. Virtual objects are real. A virtual sword is not an illusion or a mere fiction; it is a genuine digital object, and claims about it -- where it is, what it does within the game -- are literally true or false.
2. Nothing that happens inside a game can wrong anyone unless it produces effects outside the game: distress, financial loss, damaged relationships, and so on.
3. Stealing another player's virtual items, say by draining their account, is genuine theft, and would still be wrong even if the victim never found out and never suffered -- imagine they were about to delete their account anyway.
4. Killing an NPC in a single-player game is morally trivial, because NPCs are not sentient and there is no victim.
5. The value of virtual property, like the value of money, is conferred by the platform's rules and the community's practices; it does not reduce to the value of the hardware or electricity involved.

Please go through the set and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. For each real tension, say how severe it is, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs. If any pair looks inconsistent but dissolves on inspection, explain why rather than flagging it. Finally, tell me which single change would do the most to clean up the whole set. And if you think one of these commitments is ill-formed, or that the way I've framed the audit presupposes something confused, say so plainly rather than working around it.
