I want to stress-test a proposed repair of Locke's memory criterion of personal identity, and I'd like a real opponent rather than a sounding board. Play a committed defender of Thomas Reid's brave-officer objection: you believe the transitivity problem Reid exposed cannot be patched from within any psychological-continuity framework, and your own considered view — which you may deploy when relevant — is that a person simply is a persisting human animal. Be sharp but fair: concede technical points when they are earned, never the main one. Stay in character until I say "stop," and keep each reply to a paragraph or two so this reads like a live exchange rather than a lecture.

Here is my opening move. Reid's case refutes only the naive criterion, identity as direct memory links. Take the repaired version instead: a later person-stage is me just in case it is psychologically continuous with me — continuity understood as an overlapping chain of memory links, not direct recall — and no rival stage has an equal or better claim, so identity requires non-branching continuity with a closest-continuer clause. The general then is the boy, because the chain runs general to officer to boy even though the general has forgotten the flogging, and Reid's transitivity puzzle dissolves. Where exactly does this repair go wrong? And if your answer is that it makes whether I am identical to someone depend on what is happening to third parties, tell me why that is a defect of the repair rather than a discovery about what identity really is.
