I keep bumping into the slogan “everything is empty” in Buddhist-adjacent spaces, and people use it in wildly different ways. Last week I was saying I couldn’t stop replaying an insult from my boss, and someone told me the insult was empty. Then in a discussion about rent and layoffs, the same person said anger about injustice was also just grasping at empty things. But in a book about Nāgārjuna I got the impression that emptiness was supposed to be more precise than “nothing matters” or “nothing exists.” There was also this four-way pattern where you reject saying that something exists, doesn’t exist, both, or neither, which honestly made my head spin, because I can’t tell whether that is deep logic, a meditation instruction, or just refusing to answer.

Can you turn “everything is empty” into a menu of precise claims it might be making, especially in relation to that four-way rejection? For each reading, say whether it is nearly trivial, substantive and maybe defensible, or just false/confused. For example, does it mean objects have no fixed independent nature, that concepts never perfectly fit reality, that things depend on causes and labels, that nothing exists at all, that distinctions are merely useful fictions, or that we should stop caring because all concerns are unreal? I’d also like to know which readings would actually justify telling someone not to resent an insult, and which, if any, would justify saying political anger is misguided. My sense is that people smuggle in a strong conclusion from a modest metaphysical point, but maybe I’m missing somthing.
