Our department's visiting-speaker series is bringing in a metaphysician whose new monograph defends a thoroughly Lewis-style modal realism: infinitely many concrete possible worlds, each as real as this one, mutually isolated in space and time, with 'actual' functioning as an indexical and cross-world identity handled by counterparts rather than by any object's literally existing in more than one world. I am moderating the post-lecture interview. I do not want questions that let her simply restate the view -- she can define plenitude and counterpart theory in her sleep -- and I do not want the stale first-round questions she has obviously answered a hundred times ('so you really believe there's a world where donkeys talk?'). Draft eight to ten questions that move past all of that. Specifically, I want the set to include: one question on the epistemology of the view, pressing the apparent tension between the causal isolation of other worlds and our claimed knowledge of them, with a follow-up for the predictable reply that we know about worlds the way we know about unobservable theoretical entities in physics; one question on the arbitrariness worry about plenitude -- why should the space of worlds include every recombination but not, say, worlds governed by alien modalities -- with a follow-up for the reply that any restriction would be even more arbitrary; one question on the moral objection that realism makes our choices inconsequential, since the sum of worlds is fixed regardless of what we do, with a follow-up for the standard response that what matters is which world we actualize; one question on the Humphrey-style worry that counterpart theory misdescribes modal claims, since what happens to my counterpart does not seem to be about me; and one question on what could possibly count as evidence against the view, given that it seems designed so that no observation could bear on it. The remaining questions are yours to choose, but they should each target a genuine pressure point rather than an invitation to summarize. For every question, give me a one-sentence note on what a strong answer would have to accomplish, so I can tell in real time whether she has actually answered or just maneuvered.
