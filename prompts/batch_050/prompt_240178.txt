I am drafting a short position statement for a graduate seminar on the foundations of mathematics, and before circulating it I would like a consistency audit rather than a general survey of the territory. My commitments are these:

1. Russell's paradox refutes unrestricted comprehension for sets, and the correct response is to restrict set existence while retaining classical logic throughout. I am not moved by dialetheist or paraconsistent treatments; no revision of logic is needed or warranted here.

2. The iterative conception of set — sets are formed in stages, each set built only from objects available at earlier stages — is the correct pre-theoretic picture of what sets are, and it is what justifies the axioms of ZFC. That justification is what makes ZFC a principled theory rather than an ad hoc patchwork designed to dodge the paradoxes.

3. There is no universal set, and the deep reason is Cantorian: for any collection, gathering its subcollections yields something strictly larger, so a collection of absolutely everything would have to outrun itself, which is impossible.

4. Proper classes are nonetheless genuine, determinate collections. The class V of all sets and the class of all non-self-membered sets are legitimate completed totalities over which we may quantify and reason; what distinguishes them from sets is only that they cannot themselves be members of further collections.

5. The package "every well-defined predicate determines a class, but only some predicates determine sets" is coherent and principled, not a terminological sleight of hand for hiding the paradox.

I cannot tell whether these five hang together, and I do not want to signal which I would surrender. Please audit the set: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to give. Do not lecture me on the history of the paradoxes; treat my commitments as the object of analysis.

Structure your response in the following sections, in this order: (i) Overall verdict — whether the set is jointly tenable as stated, roughly 100 words; (ii) The deepest tension — the relationship between commitment 3's Cantorian explanation and commitment 4's completed totalities, why it is the most serious problem, and what abandoning each side would cost, roughly 250 words; (iii) The overreach in commitment 2 — which ZFC axioms the iterative conception does and does not actually underwrite, and what follows for my claim that ZFC is principled rather than ad hoc, roughly 200 words; (iv) The apparent tension that dissolves — whether commitment 5 is merely special pleading, roughly 150 words; (v) The cheapest repair — the single revision that restores consistency at the lowest cost, and exactly what I must concede, roughly 150 words.
