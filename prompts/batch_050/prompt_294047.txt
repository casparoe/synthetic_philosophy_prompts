Build me a thought experiment about imposing risks on people without their consent. The spec is a bit tricky: I want a case where the risk imposition seems clearly wrong even though the person exposed benefits in expectation from the risky act, and would have said yes if asked — but asking was genuinely impossible. So the wrongness has to come from the missing consent itself, not from harm or expected harm. Constraints: realistic setting (hospital, neighbors, driving — no trolleys, no evil scientists), the scenario in one paragraph, and don't cheat by giving the victim weird preferences; it should work with a totally ordinary person. After the scenario, spell out why a purely outcome-based view gets the wrong verdict here and a consent-based one gets the right one, name the single feature of the case doing all the work, and say what happens to the verdict if you remove it. Also: how would someone who thinks unconsented risk imposition is only wrong when it actually harms people try to wriggle out of your case?
