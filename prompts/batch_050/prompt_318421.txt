Okay so I write fantasy novels for a living and I've hit a wall with my beta readers that I honestly can't figure out, and it's starting to feel like it's not just a craft problem. My book has a city that floats because gravity works differently there, dragons, a guy who dies and comes back wrong — nobody blinks at any of it. But there's a culture in the book where, when your parents turn sixty, you kill them in a ceremony, and everyone in that culture genuinely sees it as the most loving, honorable thing a child can do. I wrote it straight: no hand-wringing, no sinister undertone, the characters really believe it and the narration treats it as good. And my beta readers bounce off it HARD. Not like "this is disturbing" — a couple of them read grimdark for fun — more like "this isn't believable, people don't work like that, the book broke here."

Which, okay. They'll accept floating cities but not different morals?? And it's not that they can't picture the ceremony — they picture it fine, they just refuse to take the part where it's *good*. One of them said she kept waiting for the reveal that the culture was secretly brainwashed or there was a parasite or something, like the book owed her that reveal. I didn't write it because it's not a dystopia story, the point is they're just different.

So my question is two things I guess. First, what's actually going on in their heads — is there a real difference between asking a reader to imagine impossible physics and asking them to imagine a different right and wrong, or are my readers just being squeamish? Is this a fact about how imagination works, a fact about my particular readers, or a fact about what a novel is allowed to ask of you? I was suprised how unanimous the reaction was, honestly.

And second, practical: I want to keep the storyline. What are my actual options? Can you ease a reader into an alien morality gradually, or is there a hard wall where no amount of good writing gets them across — and if there's a wall, do I just accept that some readers won't come with me, or does hitting the wall mean I've failed at something?
