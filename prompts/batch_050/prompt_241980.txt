I am a doctoral student in the history and philosophy of psychiatry, and I keep encountering a pattern I cannot find named in the literature on Hacking's looping effects. In the cases I have in mind — hysteria in the late nineteenth century, multiple personality disorder in the late twentieth, arguably some categories in circulation today — the sequence runs roughly as follows. A diagnostic label is introduced to pick out what its authors take to be a stable clinical kind. The label becomes publicly known, patients begin to understand and present themselves through it, and because clinicians learn the category largely from such patients, the operative criteria drift toward the performed presentation. Eventually the category loses the predictive grip it was introduced for — it no longer reliably tracks course, prognosis, or treatment response — and it is split, demoted, or abandoned, with the abandonment then retrospectively read as proof that the phenomenon never existed. Hacking's looping describes classifications altering the classified, and notions like concept creep and diagnostic inflation describe categories widening, but none of these seems to capture a loop that destabilizes and finally consumes the category itself. Does this configuration have an established name, and how exactly does it differ from the nearby concepts — including whether it is a special case of looping or something structurally distinct? If nothing established fits, I would like you to coin and define a term, giving its boundary cases: what would count as an instance, and what would merely look like one.
