So in our school philosophy club we've been arguing about this thought experiment where you have to choose between one person being horribly tortured for decades versus a stupendously huge number of people each getting a single dust speck in the eye, the kind you blink away in a second. The kid who brought it up defends the dust specks option with an analogy he clearly thinks settles it: harms add up like money. One penny is nothing, but enough pennies make you richer than anyone alive, so enough dust specks must eventually outweigh the torture. He says anyone who picks torturing the one person is basically failing at maths.

I'm not so sure, and I want the analogy properly audited rather than just vibes. Give me the actual correspondence table: what plays the role of the pennies, the bank account, the single fortune at the end. Then tell me where it breaks. My worry is that money pools in one place -- a million pennies really do become one big pile of cash that one person can hold -- but a million dust specks are spread across a million different eyes, and there's no "account" where all that tiny pain collects into one big pain that somebody actually suffers. Is that disanalogy doing real work, or is it just me being squeamish?

Also: does the money analogy sneak in anything the harm case lacks, like how money's value is roughly linear (a penny is always a penny) whereas maybe suffering doesn't work like that -- like is a dust speck even "a little bit of suffering" in the same currency as torture at all, or is it a different kind of thing entirely, more like the difference between a nudge and a wound? And does the analogy smuggle in the answer by assuming the adding-up is legitimate before it's been argued for?

Finally: if the money analogy can't carry the conclusion, is there a better one for the specks side (the club's other example was drops of water filling an ocean -- audit that one too if it's different in any important way), or does the whole argument actually depend on the analogy and fall apart once you drop it? I'd like to know what the argument can and can't survive without, since we're continuing this next week and I want to actually win rather than just call him weird.
