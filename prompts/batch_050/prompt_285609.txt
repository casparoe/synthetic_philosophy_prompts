My best friend Jeroen is Dutch (Rotterdam Dutch, so extra blunt) and next month he's marrying Yuki, who grew up in Kyoto and can communicate an entire paragraph of disapproval with one politely tilted head. I'm giving the toast. Room is half his family and rowing-club friends, half her family including her grandmother, who I'm told has never once said a directly negative word in her life and is somehow the most terrifying person at the wedding.

Here's the complicating thing. Early in their relationship they had a huge fight about a white lie -- he told her he "loved" the curry she made, she later found the untouched leftovers in the bin and was somehow MORE hurt by the compliment than by any criticism would have been. So they instituted what they now call the Curry Rule: no compliments you don't literally mean, not even small social ones, not even to be kind. It's become their whole bit. Guests know about it. Yuki loves it -- her take is that Japanese politeness was never about lying anyway, it's about choosing what's relevant, and she thinks a culture of fake praise is just as alien to Kyoto as it is to Rotterdam.

So write me a 4-5 minute toast that obeys the Curry Rule itself. Every compliment has to be literally true. No "she's the most amazing cook" -- she isn't. Here are your raw materials, all true: Yuki once spent an entire Sunday helping Jeroen's mum fix her garden without being asked; Jeroen learned enough Japanese to apologize to her grandmother properly, but still can't order coffee without accidentally insulting someone; Jeroen is genuinely the person you call at 3am and he always shows up; Yuki laughs at his terrible puns but only in private; the curry has genuinely improved and the last batch was honestly good.

Somewhere in the toast, say one real thing about whether tact is a form of dishonesty or a form of respect -- but it has to land as a wedding observation, not a seminar. And the roast-y bits need to survive both audiences: his family will think it's too soft, hers will think any direct criticism is a scandal, so find the narrow path. Don't mention his ex, don't mention the karaoke incident with her mother, definately don't mention the curry bin.
