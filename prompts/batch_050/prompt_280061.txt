I'm building a 'would you survive mind uploading?' flowchart for a podcast audience of smart non-philosophers. Write a decision tree that starts from the one case almost everyone already has a verdict on -- your brain replaced neuron by neuron with silicon, slowly, while you stay conscious the whole time -- and then sorts people by what they give up as the cases get harder: the instantaneous destructive scan-and-copy, the non-destructive copy (original still alive and drinking coffee next to the upload), fission (two uploads run at once), and a version of the gradual case where the replacement is spread over 40 years so the 'replacement rate' worry kicks in. Each branch node should be phrased as a question about a case, not as 'are you a functionalist?'. Leaves: at minimum the continuity functionalist (survives gradual and instant), the functionalist who only trusts the gradual procedure (put it on the tree even if you think it's incoherent, and say why), biological continuity/animalism, and a Parfit-style 'identity isn't what matters' exit for people who keep answering 'wrong question'. One specific thing I want tested somewhere in the tree: whether the person's verdict flips when the upload runs on their own preserved neurons rather than fresh silicon -- I suspect a lot of self-described functionalists are smuggling in a materiality constraint, and I want a node that catches that. Flag any pair of leaves these cases can't actually seperate, and note any combination of answers that seems unoccupied, with a guess about whether it's unoccupied because it's incoherent or just unpopular. Roughly 8-12 nodes total.
