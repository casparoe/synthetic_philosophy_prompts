write me a nearly strict villanelle, 19 lines, in the voice of a municipal archivist in an imaginary city that has passed through Polybius's six-stage constitutional sequence: monarchy, tyranny, aristocracy, oligarchy, democracy, mob rule. Don't name Polybius or any of the stages. The two refrains should differ by only one word: the first about citizens holding one another's fate in common, the second about each citizen holding only his own comfort. As they alternate, the concrete images should move through the sequence while also showing asabiyyah (group solidarity) being spent across roughly three generations. In the final quatrain, let the second refrain return altered rather than exact, so the poem doesn't simply reset: the city has changed in a way the cycle can't capture, more Ibn Khaldun or Spengler than eternal recurrence. It should work as a poem first, with no explanatory couplet or history lecture, but a specialist should be able to identify both the political cycle and the point where the poem turns linear.
