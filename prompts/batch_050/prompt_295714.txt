On a physics-adjacent discussion forum I repeatedly encounter the line "there are no tables, only particles arranged tablewise," and it gets put to very different work: against naive realism about ordinary objects, against the suggestion that macroscopic objects do any causal work of their own, and once against someone who claimed that a quantum state could literally be a state of a chair. Please lay out the distinct precise theses this one sentence could be expressing — for instance, a claim about what exists simpliciter, a claim about what exists fundamentally, a claim about whether facts about tables reduce without remainder to facts about particles, a claim about whether ordinary talk of tables is strictly false or merely loose, and a claim about causal exclusion — and for each reading say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible position, or straightforwardly confused. Then tell me which readings, if any, would actually support each of the three uses I encountered, and which reading the people repeating the line most plausibly have in mind.
