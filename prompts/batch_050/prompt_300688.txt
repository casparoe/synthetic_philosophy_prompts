I want to know whether the following configuration has an established name, and if it does, exactly where its boundaries lie. I keep noticing cases where the evidence for a generalisation seems to support, with equal force, a second generalisation that agrees with the first on every case examined so far but diverges on all the rest. Three instances. An inspector checks the first two hundred components off a production line and finds them all sound: this appears to confirm that every component in today's run is sound, but it appears equally to confirm that every component produced before the lunch break is sound and the rest flawed. A treatment has worked in every trial conducted to date: that supports the claim that it works, but apparently also the claim that it works in trials run before 2030 and not afterwards. My music app has learnt my taste almost entirely from what I play on weekday evenings, and its recommendations hold up only there. What strikes me is that the two hypotheses in each pair are not on a par: one is confirmed by the evidence while the other is merely consistent with it, yet nothing in the evidence itself seems to mark the difference. This is not the trivial point that infinitely many rules fit any finite set of observations, and I do not think it is the old problem of justifying induction outright either, since I am happy to grant that induction works; the puzzle is why it confirms one hypothesis of the pair and not the other. Give me a definite answer rather than a survey: either name the established concept and show that it covers precisely this, saying whether all three of my instances fall inside it or only the manufactured ones, or tell me that it falls between established concepts and argue for the single neighbouring notion it belongs closest to, with reasons for ruling the others out.
