So I run a little philosophy-ish meetup at my local pub, about 8 to 12 people depending on the weather, none of us with any formal training, we just like arguing about weird thought experiments over beers. Lately I got obsessed with this puzzle I came across (I think its called the self-torturer, by some philosopher named Quinn?) where a guy has a dial on himself with like 1000 settings, each notch is so slightly more painful than the last that he can't even feel the difference, but he gets paid a bunch of money for each notch he turns it up. Every single turn seems obviously worth it, and then he ends up in total agony wondering how that happened.

I want to design an actual game we can play at the meetup that makes people FEEL this, not just nod along to it. Like I want someone to look at their own choices at the end and go "wait, how did I end up here, I would never have agreed to this at the start." No fake money with no meaning behind it though, I want real small stakes, like we each bring a chocolate bar or a beer token or something, and the trades people make have to actually cost them something they care about, just in tiny increments.

Can you design the full game for me? Rules, how many rounds, what the payoff ladder looks like, how to make sure each single step is genuinely tempting but nobody can feel the difference between step n and step n+1. Ideally about 45 minutes of play plus discussion. A few specific things:

- I'd love it if the game has two phases or two versions: one where people can walk their choices back (to see if the same reasoning traps them again in reverse) and one where its a ratchet, no going back, so we can talk about whether those feel different
- A debrief guide for me as the host, mapping what happened in the game onto the actual puzzle, including the part about whether the guy's preferences are irrational or whether each choice is rational and its something else thats broken
- What will predictably go wrong the first time (someone will try to game it, someone will refuse to take any step, etc) and how to handle it
- And be honest with me about what the game cant capture. I suspect the real puzzle depends on the pain differences being literally imperceptible and I don't know how to reproduce that with beer tokens, so tell me what I have to just explain with words instead.
