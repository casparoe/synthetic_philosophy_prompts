I'm auditing a graduate philosophy of science seminar next term, and while preparing for the unit on theory change I've become stuck on the verisimilitude literature. Popper wanted a formal notion of one theory being closer to the truth than another — specifically, a theory with more true consequences and fewer false ones — so that the history of science could be described as progress even when every theory we produce is strictly false. Then in the mid-1970s Tichý and Miller showed, more or less independently, that the proposal cannot do the job: on the consequence-counting definition, no false theory can ever be closer to the truth than another, since each side of the comparison inherits falsehoods the ordering cannot sort. Fair enough. What puzzles me is what happened next: fifty years of repair attempts — content-based approaches, likeness or similarity approaches, measures built on distances between structures — and no consensus that any of them works, but also no consensus that the project is hopeless, and no agreement even on what the project was.

Here is my hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the parties are not disagreeing about one thing. Some are trying to explicate an ordinary-language notion of approximate truth for use in describing science historically; some are trying to give the realist an argument against the pessimistic induction; and some just want a usable metric for comparing false models, which might be a problem for statisticians rather than philosophers. If that's right, the literature keeps talking past itself, and objections aimed at one project get treated as refutations of another. But maybe I'm wrong and there is one genuine formal problem here with a specifiable success condition, and the impasse tracks a real theorem-shaped obstacle, the way Miller's argument has been taken to generalize into a problem about language-dependence.

Give me a diagnosis, not a survey of the proposed measures: what kind of disagreement this actually is, what each camp would have to concede to be moved, and what — a distinction, an impossibility result, a change of question — would count as genuine progress, or else an argument that nothing would. Keep it under 450 words.
