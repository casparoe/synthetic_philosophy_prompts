I've been reading rationalist blogs for years, and one idea I keep coming back to is program equilibrium: agents in a prisoner's-dilemma tournament who hand over their source code to each other and can condition their move on what they can prove about the other program, including the famous trick, familiar from Löb's theorem, by which two agents that each cooperate only if they can prove the other cooperates end up cooperating anyway. Write a parody prenuptial agreement, in numbered clauses of deadpan legalese, between two such agents who are about to enter a long-term relationship. Roughly 500 to 600 words, in American English. The satire should come from getting the logic right, not from generic jokes about robots in love: a clause stating that each party will cooperate if and only if it can prove, from the other party's source code, that the other party cooperates, and the fact that two such clauses nonetheless yield mutual cooperation; a clause excluding prospective partners whose caution prevents them from ever proving anything about anyone and who therefore never cooperate; a clause excluding partners who cooperate unconditionally with everyone, exploiters included; a clause treating obfuscated or self-modifying code as a breach of trust; and at least one piece of fine print in which the agreement's own provability conditions quietly collapse the romance entirely. A reader who knows why proof-based cooperation works should find the jokes accurate rather than merely adjacent, and a reader who doesn't should still recognize a familiar kind of over-cautious couple.
