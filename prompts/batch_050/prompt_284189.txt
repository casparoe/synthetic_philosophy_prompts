I'm presenting in a grad seminar on memory politics next month and we all have to circulate a one-page "where I stand" statement in advance so the others can push back. Before I commit to mine I want a consistency check, because I wrote it in pieces over a few weeks and I'm not sure the pieces fit. Here it is, roughly verbatim:

1. The Holocaust is unique. Not just uniquely bad in a vague moral sense, but structurally unique: a continent-wide, bureaucratically administered, industrialized attempt to murder every last member of a people, children included, with no military or economic rationale behind it.
2. Comparing the Holocaust to other atrocities (colonial genocides, the Gulag, Rwanda) is not only permissible but necessary. The taboo on comparison protects memory at the price of understanding, and it implicitly tells other victims their suffering doesn't count.
3. Bauman's diagnosis is basically right: the Holocaust wasn't a relapse into pre-modern barbarism. It was made possible by characteristically modern things — rational bureaucracy, the division of labor, moral distance created by procedure and technology, the state's ambition to rationally engineer society.
4. Antisemitism was the indispensable driving force. Without a specific, centuries-old eliminationist hatred of Jews there is no Holocaust, no matter how sophisticated the administrative machinery on hand.
5. Modernity has on balance been a story of moral progress, and I broadly count myself a defender of the Enlightenment project against its fashionable critics.
6. The lesson to draw is universal — never again means never again for anyone — which is also why I'm wary of memory regimes that sacralize the event and put it beyond ordinary historical discussion.

What I want is an audit, not a summary of the literature. Which pairs of these genuinely conflict, and which only look like they conflict but dissolve once you state them carefully? Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each one what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs me, and then tell me which single revision would do the most work overall. I honestly don't have a favorite commitment I'm itching to drop, so don't go easy on any of them — if anything I suspect the problem is somewhere in 3, since I lifted it almost straight from a summary of Bauman without thinking through what it commits me too. As a last line, give me the bottom line in a fixed form: "Verdict: most severe tension is between commitments X and Y; cheapest fix is ..." in under 30 words.
