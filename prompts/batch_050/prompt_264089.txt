And here's my friend's answer:

For Tolstoy, art is a human activity where one person, having lived through a feeling, deliberately hands it on to other people by means of external signs like words, sounds, colors, or shapes, so that the audience comes to experience that same feeling and is joined together with the artist and with each other. Art is therefore basically a kind of infection: the audience catches the emotion the artist felt. A work succeeds as art to the degree that it is infectious, and Tolstoy says infectiousness depends on three conditions: the individuality of the feeling being transmitted, the clarity of its expression, and above all the sincerity of the artist, meaning that the artist genuinely feels what they are transmitting. The more infectious a work is, that is, the more people it reaches and unites, the better the art is. Tolstoy also separates good art from bad art based on the content of the feelings. Good art transmits feelings that flow from the religious perception of the age, which for our time he thinks is the consciousness of the brotherhood of all people, while bad art transmits the feelings of a small privileged class, things like pride, sexual desire, and the boredom of a life without labor. Works that fail to infect an audience at all he classifies as bad art, and this is why he attacks so much celebrated art, like Shakespeare, Wagner, and the late Beethoven, as artificial, insincere, and produced for the amusement of the upper classes rather than for everyone. He even applied this standard to himself and condemned his own famous novels.
