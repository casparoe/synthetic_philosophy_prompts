I'm putting together a workshop on the holobiont debate and I want to hand out a document as if the two sides had actually done one of those adversarial collaborations instead of talking past each other in review papers for a decade. The two camps: on one side, the "the holobiont is a genuine biological individual" people (the hologenome crowd, roughly -- host plus microbiome is a unit of selection, sometimes even the unit at which evolution acts); on the other side, the skeptics who say holobionts are ecological communities, the microbes are mostly along for the ride, partner switching and horizontal transmission mean there's no coherent lineage, and calling it an organism is a category error or at best a metaphor.

Write the joint statement they should be able to produce. Specifically:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can actually sign. I want the unglamorous common ground: what we know about vertical vs horizontal transmission, what we know about how much of host phenotype microbes explain, what everyone concedes about well-studied cases like aphids and Buchnera versus messier cases like the mammalian gut, and what both sides agree is true about the concept of evolutionary individuality itself (e.g. both presumably accept that there's a distinction between physiological individuals and units of selection, and that cooperation isn't the same as being one organism). Don't pad this with claims so weak nobody ever denied them.

2. A disagreements table. Each row: the disagreement stated in wording both sides would accept as neutral, then each side's position in wording that side would sign. At least one row should be about what "the holobiont is a unit of selection" even commits you to -- I have a hunch half this fight is people using "unit of selection" to mean different things (replicator-ish fitness tracking vs just "selection happens at this level sometimes") and I'd like the table to make that visible rather than hide it.

3. Cruxes: the sub-questions where an answer would actually move one or both camps. For each crux, give the case, observation, or experiment both sides agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts or would concede if it went the other way. E.g., I suspect one crux is whether microbiome-associated fitness variation shows the kind of parent-offspring heritability that models of selection at that level need, and another might be about how much partner fidelity is enough -- is there a threshold, or is that the wrong way to think about it?

4. A closing section where they each say which part of the dispute they now think was verbal, if any. If it turns out a chunk of the holobiont fight dissolves once you fix terminology, say exactly which chunk and why.

Format it like a real joint statement, not an essay about one -- headers, the numbered agreed-claims section, then the table. Don't adjudicate who wins; if you have opinions, keep them out of it. And don't attribute specific positions to specific named researchers unless you're sure -- I'd rather have the two camps as idealized positions than a misquote of an actual scientist.
