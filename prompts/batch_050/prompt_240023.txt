So I'm a PhD student (political theory, but I keep ending up in the population ethics reading group) and I TA for a prof whose seminar on population axiology I've now sat through twice. She's never stated her view outright -- she just gives verdicts on cases and lets us triangulate, which is maddening and also kind of great. Here's her track record so far, welfare numbers on some normalized scale where 0 is a neutral life:

- World A: 2 people at 100 vs. World B: 100 people at 30. She says B, clearly better.
- World C: 1000 people at 10 vs. World D: a million people at 1. She says C, and gets visibly annoyed if you call D better.
- World E: 1000 at 10 vs. World F: those same 1000 at 10 plus an extra 1000 people at 6. She says F -- the mere addition is fine, "how could adding decent lives make things worse."
- World G: 1000 at 10 vs. World H: 2000 at 4. She says G.
- World I: 500 people at 20 vs. World J: 500 at 20 plus 5000 extra people at 1. She says I, no hesitation.

My officemate swears she's just a total utilitarian who's squeamish about the repugnant conclusion, but I don't think that's coherent given the B verdict. I have my own suspicions (something like a critical-level view, or maybe totalism where lives below some threshold get discounted or lexically dominated). The exam question she's hinted at: World X, 2000 people at 8, vs. World Y, 1000 people at 8 plus 4000 people at 3.

Predict her verdict on X vs. Y, identify the two or three principles consistent with her earlier verdicts and say what each predicts here, give your best guess at which she's running and why, and name one further case that would cleanly distinguish the top two candidates. Max 400 words, bottom line first.
