I'm a philosophy PhD student (mind/language area) and this semester I'm TAing an undergrad course on consciousness. I've got a 75-minute discussion section with about 24 students, and next week we're doing Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment: a bowl full of tiny marbles (he calls them simmballs) rattling around and colliding, where small collisions trigger bigger avalanches, and the big stable avalanche-patterns are supposed to be symbols interacting with each other — two symbols "meet," bounce off, merge, whatever. At the symbol level it looks like the symbols are doing things and even causing other symbol-events, but every actual collision is just marble-on-marble physics. The question I want the section to land on is whether the symbol level is doing any causal work at all — do the symbols push the substrate around, or is all the pushing at the marble level, with symbol-talk just a convenient redescription we use because tracking every simmball is intractable? My students keep just asserting "obviously it's all the marbles" or "obviously the pattern matters" without feeling why either answer is wierd, so I want them to run into the problem with their bodies before we name it.

Design me an activity for this. I'd like the students to physically enact something like the careenium — each student is a simmball with a few simple local rules on an index card (stuff like "if exactly two people tap your shoulder, tap the person to your left"), the room runs the system, and something at the "symbol" scale should visibly happen that no individual student can see from inside — maybe observers watching from the balcony of the room get to describe the run purely in symbol vocabulary while participants only ever experience local taps. I want at least one phase where the class tries to predict or control a run using only symbol-level descriptions and then discovers whether that works, and one twist or second run that forces the question of whether describing it at the symbol level changes anything causal, or just makes prediction easier. Give me everything I need to run it cold: materials, how to write the rule cards so the system doesn't deadlock or go chaotic, roles, a rough timeline, and a way to handle the fact that my students are conscious and will try to figure out the pattern and coordinate, which real simmballs can't do (or can they? — honestly I'm not sure whether that's a bug or the whole point).

Then the debrief: map specific moments in the activity onto the positions in the literature — epiphenomenalism about the higher level, downward causation, the "the symbol level is real because it's the only level at which the system is predictable" move, and the eliminativist shrug. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (rules misfiring, students breaking character, the symbol pattern being too obvious or invisible, the debrief collapsing into "it's all just physics bro") and how to recover. And be honest about which feature of the careenium the activity can't reproduce because the students are persons who know they're enacting symbols — i.e. what I have to supply in lecture rather than let them discover. At the very end, as the last line, give me a one-sentence summary of the single thing the activity is supposed to make them feel.
