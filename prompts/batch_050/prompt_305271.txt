For a working paper on the pragmatics of conversation, I have been asked by my co-authors to circulate a one-page statement of my theoretical commitments so that we can check we are building on shared foundations. I would like an audit of the statement before I send it. Here is what I currently hold:

1. Presupposition is a pragmatic phenomenon, not a semantic one: to say that an utterance presupposes that p is to say that its felicitous use requires the conversational common ground to already entail p. Nothing in the conventional meaning of the sentence itself carries the presupposition.

2. Cases where the common ground does not yet contain the presupposed content are handled by accommodation: hearers silently and costlessly adjust the common ground to include the missing content, provided that content is uncontroversial and they have no reason to object.

3. Conversational scorekeeping involves more than the stock of mutually accepted propositions. The score also tracks such parameters as prevailing standards of precision, what is salient, and which question is currently under discussion, and these parameters are adjusted by the same tacit, defeasible negotiation that governs accommodation.

4. The common ground itself can be modeled in the standard way as a context set: the set of possible worlds compatible with everything mutually accepted, so that both asserted and accommodated content enter it by intersecting away worlds.

5. The distinction between assertion and presupposition is genuine and theoretically important, and it is this distinction — the difference between content proposed for addition and content required to be already in place — that explains why presupposed material characteristically survives embedding under negation, questions, and the antecedents of conditionals.

6. Presuppositions are cancellable: a speaker can felicitously continue an utterance by explicitly denying the presupposed content (as in denying the existence of the relevant bearer after using a definite description), without contradicting herself.

My questions: Which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, please state precisely what the conflict is, rate its severity, and identify what would have to give on each side — in particular, whether resolving it requires merely adding a clarification or abandoning one of the commitments outright. For each apparent tension that dissolves, explain the distinction or disambiguation that dissolves it, since my co-authors will certainly raise these as objections. Finally, if more than one genuine tension exists, tell me which single revision would restore consistency at the lowest overall cost to the package, and what theoretical work that revision would prevent me from doing later.
