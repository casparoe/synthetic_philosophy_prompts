ok so the sequences basically turned me into a hardcore bayesian, and for years my whole operating picture was: updating = conditionalization, evidence is propositions you become certain of, done. then i actually tried to apply this to daily life and kept hitting cases where nothing ever becomes certain, and eventually i read up on jeffrey conditionalization (update by weighting P(H|E) and P(H|¬E) by your new credence in E, keeping the conditionals fixed). the problem is my gut verdicts on when this is the right move are all over the place, and i want to know what principle, if any, is behind them. verdicts:

1. the classic: i glimpse a friend's jacket across a dark parking lot, end up ~0.7 that it's red. jeffrey update on "it's red" at 0.7: completely fine, this is the motivating case and it feels exactly right.

2. my roommate tells me the restaurant is closed today. he's reliable-ish, i land at 0.8. jeffrey on "closed" at 0.8: fine. but it bugs me that this treats testimony identically to the jacket case — same number, same math — when "a person SAID X" feels like a different kind of evidence than a percept. i still say fine, but for what feel like different reasons, and i can't articulate why.

3. the judy benjamin thing: soldier parachuted into territory split red/blue, 50/50 prior, and the red side is itself split HQ vs non-HQ. she gets a radio message: "if you're in red, you're 3/4 likely in HQ." jeffrey on that message shifts her credence that she's in red at all, and that feels WRONG — the message was about where she'd be given red, not about which side she's on. verdict: not a legit update, or at least it outputs something i don't endorse.

4. memory decay: i'm 0.6 that i locked the door, so i just jeffrey on "i locked it" at 0.6, treating my current impression as the evidence. fine. and this one seems like something plain conditionalization can't even state, which i count in jeffrey's favor.

5. order dependence: apparently if i get two pieces of uncertain evidence and jeffrey-update in sequence, i can get different final credences depending on the order, unless the pieces are independent. my gut says that HAS to be a bug — the evidence is the evidence, the order i write it in my diary shouldn't change my beliefs — but i can't tell if i'm wrong and it's actually encoding something real.

so: is there a principle that captures all of these — ideally one that separates 1/2/4 from 3 and explains exactly what goes wrong in 3 — or do i have to drop a verdict? if the principle says judy benjamin is actually a fine update, i want to hear the defense. also what does it predict for a case i havent listed: you wake up unsure whether you're you-from-monday or you-from-tuesday (mad scientist, memory tampering, whatever). is that uncertain evidence in jeffrey's sense, or is it something the framework can't even get a grip on?
