I'm a science and tech journalist, and I help run the monthly meetup of a local science writers group -- around 12-18 people, mostly folks who cover AI but from the "I interview researchers" side, not the technical side. I get 75 minutes. Every month the same argument breaks out over drinks afterwards: are LLMs actually agents with goals, or are they just simulating characters, or is that whole framing wrong. It goes in circles because everyone reasons from vibes.

Design an activity for the group that makes people feel the difference between the agent picture and the simulator picture in their bones -- like, they should personally experience what it means that a system's "behavior" can flip completely based on framing while nothing "inside" changes, and then feel why that observation doesn't settle the question either. I was thinking something involving role-play where some participants actually have to be the model, completing transcripts they didn't write, but you probably have better ideas -- go with whatever works.

I need: the actual rules and setup, materials (we have index cards, a whiteboard, snacks I can withhold as stakes), timings, and then a debrief structure that maps specific moments in the game onto the agent-vs-simulator debate -- which moments correspond to which theoretical claim, where someone in the room will push back, and how to handle it. Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time (someone always games these things), and be honest about which feature of real LLMs an activity with humans playing the model fundamentally can't reproduce, so I can name that gap explicitly rather than let someone smugly point it out at the end.
