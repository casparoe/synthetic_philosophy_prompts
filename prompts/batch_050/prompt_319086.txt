I am a junior in high school taking an elective on moral philosophy, and after a unit on Taurek and whether the numbers should count, our teacher has refused to state her theory outright. She only gives rulings on rescue cases, and my group is trying to reverse-engineer her before the seminar debate. Her verdicts so far are these: if she can save five strangers from one rock or one stranger from another, with everyone otherwise drowning and no special ties, she saves the five; if the one is her younger brother against two strangers, she saves her brother, saying special responsibility can defeat a bare headcount but not unlimited partiality; if one whole dose of medicine will certainly save patient A, while splitting it would give each of five other patients a one-in-five chance, she gives the whole dose to A rather than chasing expected lives; if exactly one person can be saved no matter what and six people are equally exposed, she insists on a random draw rather than sending the single lift toward the larger group, because the larger group does not increase the number saved; and if the choice is certainly saving four people or a fifty-fifty gamble between saving all five and saving none, she takes the certain four. Please treat these as fixed data, not as targets for a bibliography.

The new case is a collapsed metro tunnel. In the only safe working hour, rescuers can either certainly free one identifiable child from a stable pocket, or spend the hour on an unstable chamber where there is a sixty percent chance of freeing five adults and a forty percent chance of freeing no one; there are no relatives involved, no one is at fault, and saving any of them has no later effects on other lives. Predict her verdict, identify the closest precedent or precedents, and state the most economical rule she seems to be running, such as maximize expected lives, count numbers only when they change how many are actually saved, prioritize certainty or the strongest present claim, give each person an equal chance when outcomes are fixed, or some hybrid. If the record underdetermines the case, say exactly where it does, what principle she would be adopting by saving the child, what principle she would be adopting by attempting the five, and what one further case would best distinguish those rules.
