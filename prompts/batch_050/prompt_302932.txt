Last night a friend and I had a long argument about the Hart--Devlin debate on whether the criminal law may enforce morality as such. My friend, who thinks the matter was settled decades ago, summed up Devlin's position in The Enforcement of Morals like this: "Devlin's view was that a society is held together by a shared moral code, so the majority is entitled to criminalize any conduct it strongly disapproves of, even if nobody is harmed. He predicted that decriminalizing homosexuality, as the Wolfenden Report proposed, would cause British society to disintegrate, and he grounded this in the Christian moral foundations of English law. Hart replied in Law, Liberty, and Morality that there is no evidence private vice dissolves societies, and philosophers have treated this as a demolition of legal moralism ever since."

I came away from the argument doubting my friend's confidence, but I could not say exactly where he goes wrong. Please audit his summary: which parts would a careful reader of the debate accept, which parts overstate or conflate things Devlin actually keeps apart (for instance, I vaguely recall Devlin saying something more qualified about majority feeling, and about the private sphere), and what is simply wrong? Finally, once the summary is corrected, does the verdict that Hart demolished Devlin still follow, or is the debate more open than my friend allows?
