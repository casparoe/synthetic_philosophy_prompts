I'm a third-year philosophy student in a seminar on rational choice, and we've just finished the unit on L. A. Paul's Transformative Experience (2014) — the vampire thought experiment, the decision to have a child, the idea of choosing for the sake of revelation, and the replies in the literature. For our final paper we each have to write a short "position statement" setting out our own commitments on the topic, which the seminar leader and a partner will then attack. Before I submit mine, I want to know whether it actually hangs together. Here is what I currently hold:

1. Paul's central argument is sound. Because the outcome of a genuinely transformative experience cannot be known in advance — you cannot know what it will be like, or how it will strike you, until you undergo it — standard expected-utility reasoning cannot ground decisions like whether to have a child.
2. Nonetheless, such decisions can still be rational. The agent can rationally choose for the sake of discovery: the value of finding out what the experience is like. I take this to be Paul's own resolution, and I endorse it.
3. The epistemic gap at the heart of the problem is irreducibly first-personal. Testimony, statistics, and imaginative projection cannot give you what undergoing the experience itself gives you.
4. Deciding whether to become a parent is epistemically better-positioned than deciding whether to become a vampire, precisely because prospective parents can draw on the abundant testimony of actual parents, whereas there is no testimony to consult in the vampire case.
5. I deny that big decisions change one's fundamental preferences. The person who emerges from the transformation evaluates outcomes with the same core values she had going in; she simply has new experiences under her belt.
6. In cases where decision theory genuinely cannot operate, rational agents should fall back on non-comparative resources — identity, values, reflection on what kind of person to be — rather than on any maximization procedure.

What I want is an audit, not an overview of the debate. Please go through these commitments and tell me: which pairs are in real tension and which only appear to be; for each genuine tension, what exactly has to give and what it would cost me to keep the rest; and what the single cheapest revision to the set would be. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and don't tell me which commitment I "should" abandon — I want to see the options laid out, not a verdict on my behalf.
