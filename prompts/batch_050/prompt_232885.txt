Something I keep bumping into and I dont have a word for it. My kid once shouted "I'm not talking to you!" at me, while, well, talking to me. A coworker emailed the whole office to say he never reads email. And then in a philosophy book I hit the famous argument where trying to doubt absolutely everything is supposed to collapse, because the doubting itself is something being done by someone. These all feel like the same trick: the sentence defeats itself not by being logically impossible (like "this sentence is false" tries to be) but because actually saying or thinking it does the very thing it denies. Is there a name for this family? I won't be happy with just "self-contradictory," since an ordinary contradiction fails whether or not anyone utters it, while these only fail when performed. Also, is the doubting case really the same species as my kid's? With my kid the sentence just comes out false; with the doubting it seems like the denial can't even get started, and the positive version ("I'm here, doubting") is somehow guaranteed by its own attempted denial. If one name doesn't cover both directions -- sentences that kill themselves when said vs. sentences that prove themselves when you try to kill them -- where exactly should the line be drawn, and does the doubting argument still work if it turns out to belong to the first kind rather than the second?
