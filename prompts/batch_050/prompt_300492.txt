Write a sestina about the rule-following problem associated with Kripkenstein. Use the six end-words usage, rule, answer, mistake, warrant, and others in the standard retrogradatio cruciata pattern, followed by a three-line envoi containing all six. The speaker has previously applied addition only to pairs of numbers below 57 and must now respond to 68 + 57. Introduce quus, an operation that agrees with addition below that threshold but yields 5 when either input reaches or exceeds 57.

The changing contexts of the repeated end-words should track the dialectic rather than merely repeating its vocabulary. Let the first stanza establish the temptation to think that past usage already fixes a uniquely correct continuation. Let the second introduce quus and the insufficiency of finite examples. In the third, consider the proposal that an inner formula or intention settles the matter, while showing that any such representation must itself be interpreted. In the fourth, consider a dispositional account and its difficulty explaining how an actual disposition, especially a finite or error-prone one, can make an answer correct rather than merely likely. The fifth stanza should articulate the normative force of meant to go on this way without turning that force into a mysterious inner command. The sixth may approach the skeptical solution by locating warranted assertion within shared linguistic practice, but it must not claim that communal agreement simply makes 125 the right answer or that communities cannot err. The envoi should preserve the uneasy balance between practical confidence and the absence of the desired foundational fact.
