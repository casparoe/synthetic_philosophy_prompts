I am a retired organic chemist who has recently taken up the philosophy of language, and I am drafting a blog post for fellow scientifically trained lay readers on how slurs manage to offend. I would like a decision tree that sorts the main accounts of slur derogation—semantic content views (including views on which slurs have empty extensions), expressivism, presupposition or implicature accounts, and prohibitionist or taboo-based views—with each internal node a concrete diagnostic question phrased without jargon, answerable by someone who has never heard the labels: for example, whether the derogation survives embedding under negation or in the antecedent of a conditional, whether it can be explicitly canceled without contradiction, whether merely mentioning the word inside quotation marks is itself offensive, and whether a speaker could utter the slur sincerely while honestly disavowing all contempt for the target group. Please construct the tree from the logical space first—that is, from the questions and the combinations of answers they permit—and only then locate the named views among the leaves, rather than carving the space to fit a pre-existing list. Flag any unoccupied combination of answers with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored, and note explicitly any pair of views that my diagnostic questions cannot separate, suggesting what further test might distinguish them. Aim for five to seven internal nodes, and please write in American English.
