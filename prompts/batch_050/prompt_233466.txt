I'm putting together a handout for a mixed undergrad/grad seminar on free will and I want a decision tree (not an essay) that sorts the main positions on when repetitive behavior counts as merely mechanical versus genuinely free or intelligent.

Quick setup so nobody has to be rusty on the lore: the sphex wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, leaves it at the entrance while she goes inside to check things, then comes out and pulls it in. If an experimenter nudges the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she drags it back to the threshold and goes in to check all over again, and you can repeat this dozens of times without her ever just grabbing it from the new spot. Dennett and Hofstadter made this case famous as a challenge to humans: how do you know you aren't sphexish about the very things you think you're free about?

Requirements:

- The tree starts from the wasp, and each branch should be labeled with a concrete diagnostic question someone could answer without knowing any position names — e.g. whether counterfactual flexibility counts if it's never exercised, whether the agent has to represent its own reasons or just respond to the right things, whether being sphexish in one domain rules out freedom elsewhere, whether being informed you're in a loop would even change anything.
- Leaves get the position's name plus one sentence on its most awkward consequence. Must include at least: a reasons-responsiveness compatibilist view, the hard-line view that humans are sphexish all the way down so no one is free, and a hierarchical reflective-endorsement view. Add more leaves only if they genuinely occupy different spots in the space of answers.
- Run at least three test cases through the finished tree to show where they land: the commuter who drives home on total autopilot and remembers none of it, a chess engine that always plays the same opening, and someone who keeps returning to a relationship they sincerely insist they'd leave if they ever really thought about it.
- Note any combination of answers nobody seems to hold and say whether that's because the view is incoherent or merely unexplored, and flag any two positions the tree cant actually tell apart rather than papering over it.
- Finally, be upfront about which of these positions are mainstream and which are genuinely contested or marginal among people who work on this stuff — dont present any one of them as the settled consensus.
