I am putting together the opening session of a graduate seminar on the semantic paradoxes, and I want to frame it around a question my students almost never see posed cleanly: what would it be for a proposal to count as a solution to Curry's paradox, rather than a relocation of it? My sense is that the field's stock of adequacy conditions was built for the Liar and transfers poorly. The Curry sentence needs no negation at all — only a conditional and whatever structural apparatus lets one pass from a self-conditional to its consequent — so any account that does its work by weakening the logic of negation (paracomplete or paraconsistent treatments, for instance) leaves the paradox fully intact. Yablo's construction sharpens the problem from the other direction: an infinite sequence in which each sentence says only that every later sentence is untrue contains no self-reference whatsoever, so diagnoses that locate the pathology in circularity or in failures of groundedness owe us a different story for that case. And both paradoxes generate the familiar revenge pressure, where any predicate a theory introduces to quarantine the offending sentences can be fed back into a strengthened version of the construction.

I would like the seminar to begin from a specification rather than from a survey of solutions. Please state exactly five desiderata that an adequate treatment of Curry's and Yablo's paradoxes must satisfy, given that the goal is a theory of truth usable in ordinary reasoning rather than merely a consistent formal artifact. Present them as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. For each desideratum, give the consideration or construction that motivates it, and indicate whether it is a genuine constraint any solution must meet or a preference distinctive of one camp (e.g., those unwilling to surrender classical logic versus those unwilling to restrict the T-schema). If some subset of your five looks jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly — that is the result I most want the students to see — and identify which single desideratum excludes the most existing proposals. Throughout, separate what is settled ground among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present any contested diagnosis (for instance, that Yablo's sequence is covertly circular, or that Curry is really a paradox about negation after all) as though it were the consensus.
