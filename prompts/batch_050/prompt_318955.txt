My School Is a Panopticon and So Is My Phone

Foucault talks about the panopticon, a prison design from Bentham where the guards sit in a tower in the middle and the prisoners can never tell if they're being watched, so they end up behaving all the time just in case. His point is that the best kind of control is the one you do to yourself. I think my school works exactly like this. There are some cameras, sure, but mostly nobody is watching, and yet everyone behaves anyway, because we've all internalized the feeling of being watched. The surveillance isn't even needed anymore, which is way more efficient.

It's not just about watching though. Foucault's idea of discipline is about producing a certain kind of person. The timetable chops the day into identical blocks and teaches you to manage your body and attention. Exams and grades rank everyone against everyone else and create a "normal" student that you're supposed to measure yourself against. Even stuff like sitting still and raising your hand trains you. So the school doesn't just stop us from doing bad things, it manufactures us into a specific type of person.

Here's my main original point: Foucault wrote before smartphones, but I think the panopticon got upgraded. I track my own grades on the school portal like five times a day. I track my steps, my sleep, my screen time. Nobody forces me. The discipline now runs on voluntary self-monitoring, and I think Foucault would say this is power winning completely, because we do its job for it and even enjoy it.

My conclusion is that power is basically something institutions do to us, and the way to resist is to stop participating, so like, delete the tracking apps and refuse to self-monitor, and then you're outside the system.
