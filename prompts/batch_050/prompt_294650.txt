I cover management and workplace culture for a science-and-ideas magazine, and my editor wants a humor piece for the winter issue. Write me a satirical employee handbook, 600-700 words, formatted as numbered sections, for a company whose CEO has gone all-in on Han Feizi's Legalism after deciding that "culture" and "trusting people's virtue" were costing him money. So: salaries tied entirely to measurable outputs, generous bounties for reporting coworkers, a strict rule that managers must never reveal what they actually want so employees cant just perform enthusiasm, promotions decided solely by matching what someone promised against what they delivered, a section explaining why benevolent bosses breed lazy workers. The comedy should come from taking the Legalism seriously -- the two handles of reward and punishment, law over sages, the ruler concealing his desires -- not from making the CEO a cartoon tyrant. Deadpan throughout, readable by someone who's never heard of Han Feizi but extra funny to someone who has, and by the end the reader should feel the real question of whether a system that assumes the worst of everyone ends up producing exactly the people it assumes.
