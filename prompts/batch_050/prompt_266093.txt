My reading group has spent the term on evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics, and the recurring failure mode is that our two strongest members -- one sympathetic to a Street-style Darwinian dilemma for moral realism, the other a robust realist who thinks normative truths need no more vindication than mathematical ones -- keep talking past each other. The debunker insists that once you accept that the content of our moral beliefs was shaped by selection pressures aimed at reproductive fitness rather than at any stance-independent normative truth, the realist owes an explanation of why those beliefs happen to line up with the truths; the realist replies that the demand for such an explanation is question-begging, that no causal-explanatory contact with abstract normative facts was ever required, and that the argument proves too much by sweeping up logical and epistemic norms along with moral ones. Their sessions end with each accusing the other of assuming precisely what's in dispute.

I'd like you to draft the joint statement these two would produce if they ran a proper adversarial collaboration instead of another seminar shouting match. Format the whole document as exactly five numbered sections, each containing a sentence or two of framing followed by whatever compact content it needs, with no preamble or closing remarks. Section one: the claims both parties can sign -- that evaluative belief-content is substantially explained by evolutionary history, that this alone doesn't refute realism on pain of the genetic fallacy, that the debate concerns what realism must add beyond this. Section three: the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would force at least one side to concede ground -- including whether the argument overgeneralizes to epistemic norms in a way that self-undermines, and whether the disagreement is ultimately about explanatory standards rather than about evolution at all.
