So a friend of mine from the local LessWrong meetup and I have been arguing about manipulation vs legitimate persuasion for weeks now, and I've started keeping a tally of her verdicts because I swear there's a consistent principle in there somewhere. Her calls so far: outright lying to someone, obviously manipulation. Presenting true statistics that you specifically picked because you know they'll trigger the availability heuristic and leave the listener with a skewed overall picture: manipulation. Giving a rigorous, steelmanned argument but delivering it with great rhetoric, pauses, charisma, the works: legitimate, she says the persuasiveness there is "doing its job through the argument". Subliminal messaging (like flashing an image too fast to consciously register): manipulation. And she says a charity volunteer being warm and likeable while making their actual case is fine, but a salesman who gives you a small gift first specifically to exploit reciprocity before asking for anything is manipulation. I've noticed she never asks whether the target consented, and she doesn't seem to care much about the persuader's motives either, which narrows it down a bit.

The case we actually disagree about, and where I want your prediction: a university cafeteria manager who rearranges the layout so the healthy options are at eye level and the chips are hard to reach, deliberately, after reading up on nudges and choice architecture. Nobody's told anything false, all the food is still available, and the manager is upfront about the policy if anyone asks, but the whole mechanism runs on System 1 rather than on anyone deliberating about their diet. My guess is she calls it legitimate, since nothing false is believed and the mechanism doesn't exploit a bias that misleads you about facts, but my co-meetup friend insists her subliminal-messaging verdict commits her to calling any deliberate bypass of deliberation manipulation. Which of us is right about her pattern, or are the earlier verdicts genuinely consistent with both rules? Tell me which earlier case is the closest analog to the cafeteria, what each candidate principle predicts, and what new hypothetical case would actually settle which rule she's running.
