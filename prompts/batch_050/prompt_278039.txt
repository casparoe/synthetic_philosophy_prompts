I am preparing the final examination for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in political philosophy and rational choice. Draft one 90-minute, 45-mark essay-and-problem question on safe Pareto improvements, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The question must be self-contained and must not require outside reading, named authorities, or citations.

Build the question around the following stipulative case, including the table in the examination paper:

Three equal-sized communities—Upstream, Factory, and Renter—are deciding whether to retain the existing flood policy or adopt a new levee system. The numbers below represent each community’s all-things-considered lifetime welfare on a common scale stipulated for the case. Under the existing policy, each community receives 6 in every relevant state. There are two equally likely weather states, Dry and Flood. The levee’s outcomes before compensation are:

| Weather state | Upstream | Factory | Renter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dry | 9 | 7 | 5.5 |
| Flood | 5.5 | 8 | 7 |

A proposed compensation rule would transfer 1 unit from Upstream to Renter in Dry, and 1 unit from Factory to Upstream in Flood. If the transfer is guaranteed, terminal outcomes would therefore be:

| Weather state | Upstream | Factory | Renter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dry | 8 | 7 | 6.5 |
| Flood | 6.5 | 7 | 7 |

However, the transfer is not constitutionally entrenched. It can be implemented only through a vote held after the weather state becomes known. At the time the levee must be approved, everyone knows that the transfer will survive that vote with probability 0.75 in either weather state. If it fails, the uncompensated outcomes obtain.

For the purposes of the examination, define an institutionally safe Pareto improvement as a transition whose entire implementation path, including any separately enacted compensation mechanism, cannot leave an affected person uncompensatedly worse off in any possible terminal state. This is a stipulative category for the question, not an established doctrine to be summarized.

The question should require candidates to do all of the following:

1. Classify the proposal under ex ante expected Pareto improvement, ex post Pareto improvement with guaranteed compensation, and ex post Pareto improvement given the actual 75-percent compensation rule.
2. Determine whether it is institutionally safe under the stipulated definition, distinguishing the announced policy package from the sequence of decisions that implements it.
3. Explain why a merely potential or politically contingent transfer is not equivalent to actual compensation, while considering whether devices such as automatic payments, escrow, insurance, or prior legal entitlement could restore safety.
4. Assess whether safety should be assessed only over terminal lifetime outcomes or also at each temporal stage of implementation.
5. Evaluate the objection that a strict no-one-worse-off standard gives arbitrary protection to the status quo, creates veto rents, and ignores the losses produced by inaction.
6. Defend a conclusion about whether the proposal should count as a safe Pareto improvement and, if not, what minimal institutional modification would make it one without abandoning the standard’s point.

The model answer should give the relevant expected-value calculations, but should not treat arithmetic as a substitute for the conceptual analysis. It should state a definite position while acknowledging the strongest contrary interpretation. It should also distinguish individual-level safety from aggregate welfare gains, actual compensation from hypothetical compensability, and risk accepted ex ante from loss imposed ex post.

Provide a marking scheme organized around six must-hit components, with marks totaling 45. For each component, state what a top answer must do and give a one-line description of the characteristic mistake that should lose marks. Reserve substantial credit for the analysis of institutional sequencing and political reversibility; answers that merely recite the ordinary definition of a Pareto improvement should not be able to earn a high mark.
