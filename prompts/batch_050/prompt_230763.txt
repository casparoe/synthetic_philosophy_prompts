Exam question: In Grice's account of speaker meaning, explain the distinction between what a speaker says and what a speaker conversationally implicates. Describe the role of the Cooperative Principle and the conversational maxims in generating conversational implicatures, and use an example to illustrate (i) how a conversational implicature is generated and (ii) how it can be cancelled.

Student's answer:

Grice distinguishes what is said from what is implicated. What is said corresponds closely to the conventional meaning of the sentence uttered, together with the resolution of ambiguities and the fixing of referents; what is implicated is further content the speaker gets across in the context of the utterance. Because implicated content is not literally said, Grice held that it falls outside what the speaker means: implicatures, on his view, are hints or suggestions that hearers happen to pick up on, rather than part of the intended message.

Conversational implicatures are generated against the background of the Cooperative Principle — roughly, that one should make one's conversational contribution such as is required by the purposes of the exchange — and the maxims of Quantity, Quality, Relation, and Manner. Implicatures typically arise when a speaker appears to flout a maxim and the hearer reasons that the speaker must nonetheless be cooperative. For instance, if I ask how a dinner party was and my friend replies, "The host certainly kept his glass full," she flouts the maxim of Relation, since her remark appears unresponsive to my question; I conclude that she means to convey that the host drank heavily, or something in that neighborhood.

A distinctive feature of conversational implicatures is their cancellability. My friend could add, "Not that I'm saying he drank too much," thereby withdrawing the implicature without contradicting herself. Grice's conventional implicatures behave similarly: a word like "but" or "therefore" contributes a suggested contrast or inference, and this suggestion, like a conversational implicature, can be cancelled or suspended when the context does not support it.
