My cog-sci textbook glosses Block's distinction and global workspace theory like this: 'Block split consciousness into access consciousness — information poised for reasoning, report, and control of action — and phenomenal consciousness, the subjective feel of experience. His main point was that phenomenal consciousness is just access-conscious content that hasn't been accessed yet, so the distinction is really a matter of timing. Global workspace theory cashes this out: a state becomes phenomenally conscious exactly when its content gets broadcast to the workspace, so the two theories are essentially one theory. The upshot is that once neuroscience pins down the mechanisms of broadcasting, the so-called hard problem will be dissolved.' A friend in phil mind says this misrepresents both Block and GWT, but she couldn't quite pin down how, and honestly it sounds plausible to me. Is the summary fair? Please seperate what's accurate, what's subtly wrong, and what defenders of each view would object to — and if the summary is wrong about the theories, does its conclusion about the hard problem still stand?
