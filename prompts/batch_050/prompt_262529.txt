Before I commit to a formal model of belief for a project, I want the spec first, not a tour of frameworks. Write the desiderata a theory of belief must meet in light of the problem of logical omniscience: the phenomena it has to get right (people miss obvious consequences of what they believe, believe outright contradictions without noticing, and yet genuinely learn things by deduction -- a good theory shouldn't explain the failures by making the successes impossible), the distinctions it can't collapse (explicit vs implicit belief, believing a proposition vs being committed to it, mathematical ignorance vs empirical ignorance), and any constraints from computation or psychology it can't contradict. Give the case or argument behind each requirement, and tell me which desiderata are in real tension with each other -- whether the full list is even jointly satisfiable is the part I care most about. Also flag which demands are just dogmas of the possible-worlds camp or the sententialist camp dressed up as neutral requirements. A sentence or two on how the main contendors fare against the list is fine, but keep the weight on the criteria.
