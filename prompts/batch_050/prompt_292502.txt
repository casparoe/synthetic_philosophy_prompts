I've just finished rewatching Breaking Bad and a mate and I got into an argument about whether it's morally good art or just exceptionally well-made titillation. Read the show through Tolstoy's theory in What Is Art? -- art as the sincere transmission of feeling from artist to audience, infectiousness as the real test, his contempt for the 'counterfeit art' of the upper classes that runs on technique and borrowed feeling, and his demand that good art unite people in shared feeling rather than flatter a coterie. Where does the show pass his tests (it plainly transmits something powerful to millions), where does it fail them (does inviting us to admire Walt's competence corrupt the very feelings being transmitted, and is prestige telly exactly the class-bound art he despised?), and where does it resist the framework altogether? Would Tolstoy just have to call it a brilliantly executed forgery, or does the final season's reckoning do the kind of moral work he valued in folk tales and his own late stories? I want a reading that takes both sides seriously rather than using the show as a peg for the theory -- including where Tolstoy's criteria come out looking too blunt for what long-form drama actually does.
