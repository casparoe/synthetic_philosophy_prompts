Fine. But then our TA framed the whole Nyāya–Mīmāṃsā dispute as: does a cognition's validity arise from the very conditions that produce the cognition (Mīmāṃsā), or only from some separate confirming condition, like successful action or corroboration (Nyāya)? And the more I think about it the more it smells like the two sides might just be talking past each other, because they don't even seem to agree on what "validity" is doing in the first place.

So here's my question, and it's a diagnosis question, not a "who wins" question. Is this debate actually substantive, or is it (a) a verbal dispute where both sides define prāmāṇya differently and then triumphantly show the other side's definition fails, (b) a disguised disagreement about something else entirely, like whether knowledge needs to be certain versus merely reliable, or whether the real issue is Mīmāṃsā's theological need for the authorless Veda to be self-certifying, or (c) a genuine disagreement about the epistemology of testimony that modern philosophers would still recognize as live?
