I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I need help resolving a running argument. Last month my employer announced a "cognitive wellness" benefit, and our team chat spent a week debating whether it should cover prescription stimulants for healthy employees. My coworker Priya and I kept disagreeing, and afterward I started logging her verdicts on hypothetical cases, trying to reverse-engineer the principle she is applying. Here is her track record so far:

1. Insulin for someone with type 1 diabetes: clearly acceptable.
2. LASIK to correct severe nearsightedness back to roughly normal vision: acceptable.
3. A LASIK package marketed as "better than 20/20," pitched to applicants for a competitive commercial pilot program who want sharper vision than rival candidates: not acceptable.
4. Growth hormone for a child with a diagnosed growth-hormone deficiency: acceptable.
5. Growth hormone for a healthy child of average height whose parents hope it will help him win a basketball scholarship: not acceptable.
6. Reconstructive nose surgery after a car accident: acceptable.
7. Elective nose surgery for a local news anchor who says her appearance is costing her airtime relative to her colleagues: not acceptable.
8. A daily statin for someone with elevated cholesterol: acceptable.

Now the new case, which came up over coffee last week. Our neighbor is a 68-year-old retired translator. Her cognition tests as typical for her age — no diagnosis, no impairment — but she wants a prescription memory-enhancing drug purely so she can keep translating poetry as a hobby. She has no clients, no deadlines, and no one she is competing with. Assume for the sake of argument that the drug is safe, legal, affordable, and that her choice is fully informed and uncoerced, so I don't want the analysis to turn on side effects, access, or pressure.

Here is what I would like from you:

- Predict Priya's verdict on the retired translator case, and state how confident you are.
- Articulate at least two distinct rules that are each consistent with all eight verdicts above. I have a hunch one is about restoring normal functioning versus going beyond it, and another is about whether the intervention confers an advantage in a competitive or positional setting — but state them precisely, and tell me if either fails on some case in ways I haven't noticed.
- Say which rule you think she is actually running, and what in the track record makes you think so — or, if the record genuinely underdetermines the new case, say so plainly and explain what principle she would be committing herself to with each possible verdict.
- Describe one additional hypothetical case, ideally one I could put to her directly, whose verdict would cleanly tell the candidate rules apart.

Please answer in American English, and keep the reasoning explicit — I want to be able to check each step the way I'd check a diff.
