I run a university's hiring and curriculum committees, and we keep producing verdicts nobody accepts as coherent: the members unanimously find a candidate strong on research, strong on teaching, and strong on service, yet the committee votes 4–5 to reject; a curriculum panel agrees a proposal meets every listed criterion but a majority then votes it down, and minutes written from the criteria contradict minutes written from the final vote. I want a written procedure my committee secretaries can follow before, during, and after any multi-criterion decision, grounded in what the judgment aggregation literature actually establishes about majority voting over connected propositions: when the discursive dilemma can arise, why premise-by-premise and conclusion-only aggregation can diverge, and what a chair can legitimately do about it in advance (fix the decision rule, decompose or refuse to decompose the question, screen the agenda for logical dependencies) rather than patching things up afterward. The procedure should specify the tests to run in order, which choices must be made and minuted before votes are cast, and which inconsistencies cannot be procedurally eliminated and must be flagged to the members as judgment calls. Then apply the procedure to two of my cases: a hiring committee where each member votes independently on three criteria and then on hire/no-hire, and a curriculum panel where one member votes strategically on a criterion to force an outcome.
