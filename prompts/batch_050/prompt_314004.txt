Okay so I got into this argument with my brother last week and I keep going back and forth on it. He was laying into our cousin for cheating on her taxes, and I pointed out that he's definitely under-reported cash tips before, so he's a total hypocrite and should maybe sit this one out. He got annoyed and said that doesn't change whether tax fraud is wrong, which, fine, but that's not really what I was saying.

Here's the thing I can't get past. It seems basically obvious that if you've done the same thing yourself, your blame doesn't count anymore. Like that's just what hypocrisy is — the words might be true but you're not entitled to say them, and the other person is fully within their rights to ignore you. But then I started thinking about how far this goes. Pretty much everyone has lied at some point. Pretty much everyone has been selfish, or cut a corner, or broken a small promise. So for all the ordinary everyday wrongs, there is essentially nobody left who is allowed to call them out. The pool of legitimate blamers for lying is, like, zero people.

And thats where it gets weird, because what's a moral rule even doing if nobody has the right to enforce it? It starts to seem like lying kind of stops being *wrong* in any meaningful sense once everyone has done it — not because lying became fine, but because the wrongness has no way to actually land on anyone. The rule is still on the books but there's no one with clean enough hands to apply it, so it just sits there doing nothing. Which would mean common vices are basically unpunishable-by-blame and rare vices are the only ones that stay properly wrong, since rare things still have innocent people around to object.

That conclusion feels clearly broken to me but I genuinely cannot find the step where the reasoning fails. Is it that losing the right to blame isn't the same as the blame becoming false? Or that hypocrisy doesn't actually cancel your standing the way I'm assuming? Or is the mistake somewhere earlier? Walk me through where this goes wrong, because right now my brother is winning this argument and I'd like that to stop.
