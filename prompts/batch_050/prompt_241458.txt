My roommate walked me through this argument about Malthus and I feel like it proves too much but I can't pin down where. (1) Malthus claimed population grows geometrically while food supply grows at best arithmetically, so recurring famine and misery are inevitable unless population is checked. (2) The global collapse he predicted hasn't happened in over 200 years, but the mechanisms he identified — exponential population pressure against diminishing returns on land — are still real and still operating. (3) A theory whose core mechanisms are correct but whose timing was off hasn't been falsified, only postponed. (4) So Malthus was right but early, and his prediction still stands. (5) But by that standard, any long-run forecast — peak oil, the population bomb, ecological collapse by whenever — can never fail either: just push the date back and it's "pending" forever. (6) A claim that no amount of elapsed time can count against isn't really a prediction. (7) So either Malthus was simply wrong, or "right but early" is a get-out-of-jail-free card that makes every doomsday forecast unfalsifiable. He insists the argument is airtight and Malthus comes out vindicated. I want to keep (1) and (2), and I'm genuinely sympathetic to (3), but (7) seems to follow and it bothers me. Which step should I actually reject, what did that step have going for it, and what does rejecting it cost? If the answer is that some predictions really can be right-but-early without being unfalsifiable, tell me what distinguishes those cases.
