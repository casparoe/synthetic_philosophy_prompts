So I got into this massive argument with my friend last weekend that went til like 2am. He was going on about how Gödel's incompleteness theorems prove that no computer could ever really think like a human, because any formal system powerful enough to do arithmetic has true sentences it can't prove, and a human mathematician can just "see" that those sentences are true, so the mind must be doing something no machine can. I pushed back hard -- I think this is a bad argument that leans on the theorems saying way more than they actually do, and that he kept quietly assuming the human mathematician is consistent and sound in a way no actual human is.

Anyway, now I've somehow agreed to present my side at a philosophy society thing next week. The audience will be mostly math and CS grad students (who know the actual theorems cold and will pounce on any sloppy statement of them), plus a couple of philosophers of mind and one very smug physics guy who loves Penrose. My friend will be there too, obviously.
