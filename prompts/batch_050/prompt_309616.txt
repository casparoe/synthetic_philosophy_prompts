Write an essay on this policy dispute: a coastal city must choose between building a massive flood barrier and managed retreat. Either plan will change migration, when people have children, and which children are conceived, so almost nobody living there in 2120 under one plan would exist under the other. A city economist proposes a same-people rule: count future costs and benefits only when they affect someone who would exist under both options, and treat benefits to identity-dependent future residents as morally optional. Does this rule neatly avoid the nonidentity problem, or does it just hard-code one controversial response to it? Discuss whether people can be wronged without being made worse off than they otherwise would have been, what weight the complaints of actual future people deserve, and whether the city can compare outcomes involving different populations. Reach a definite verdict on whether the rule should guide the city’s decision, not just a both-sides summary.
