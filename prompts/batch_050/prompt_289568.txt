Here is my situation. In three weeks I am presenting a seminar paper defending the following thesis: the relativity of simultaneity, properly understood, establishes the block universe (eternalism) and rules out both presentism and the growing-block view. My argument runs roughly as follows. Take two observers in relative motion. By the relativity of simultaneity, an event that lies in observer A's present -- and is therefore real, if we define reality by what is co-present with a here-now -- lies in observer B's past or future. Since neither frame is privileged, what is real cannot depend on the frame; hence the reality of distant events cannot be tied to any single simultaneity slice, and the only consistent verdict is that past, present, and future events are equally real. I then add a transitivity step: if event E is real relative to A's now, and a distant event F is co-present with E in some frame, then F is real relative to A's now as well; iterating this across frames spreads reality over all of spacetime.

My audience: a mixed philosophy department seminar.
