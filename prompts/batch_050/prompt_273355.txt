No superintelligence, no doomsday devices, no exotic physics.

The flip has to come from how you individuate or weight the contexts, not from someone sneaking a tiny-probability catastrophe into one branch.

Constraints: the scenario itself one page max, concrete enough that a smart undergrad could redo the arithmetic with a calculator, and the two ensembles have to both be things a reasonable safety engineer would actually write down — not one honest ensemble and one strawman.

After the scenario, do the check: show the expected-harm math under both ensembles, show why the worst-case verdict is invariant, and name the single feature of the case doing the work — plus what happens to the case if you remove it. Then finish with the strongest move an average-case partisan could make to dissolve it (probably: argue one ensemble is objectively correct), and whether you actually buy that move.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether such a case is constructible and what it shows — then give the scenario and the reasoning. No preamble, don't restate my question.
