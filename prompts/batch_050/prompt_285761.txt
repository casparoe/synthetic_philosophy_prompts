Write a parody research grant proposal (like an NSF or ERC application) from an ornithologist, Dr. someone you invent, who has had the brilliant insight that instead of trekking into the field to find ravens, you can confirm "all ravens are black" from indoors by cataloguing non-black objects and checking that none of them are ravens. The proposal should be deadly serious in tone and get the confirmation theory exactly right -- it should lean on the logical equivalence between a universal claim and its contrapositive ("all non-black things are non-ravens"), cite Nicod's criterion in spirit if not by name, and treat every red apple, green leaf, and white sneaker as a data point. Include: a 3-sentence abstract, the specific aims, a budget justification where the majority of funds go toward aquiring and archiving household objects (plus one lavish line item for a single raven "as a control"), and a "response to preliminary reviews" section where the PI patiently rebuts a referee who has raised the objection that this is absurd -- the PI's rebuttal should be technically correct and completely miss the point. Maybe also a small sub-aim about how a pink flamingo disconfirms "all ravens are white" and therefore the team has made progress on two hypotheses at once. The humor has to come from the actual logic of Hempel's raven paradox, so that someone who knows the confirmation literature finds it fair rather than a strawman, and it should never break character or explain the joke. About 600-700 words, formatted with section headers like a real grant.
