ok so I've been thinking about responsibility and alternatives and I keep landing on verdicts that I'm not sure hang together. Here they are:

- Classic Frankfurt setup: Black would force Jones to kill Smith if Jones showed any sign of deciding otherwise, but Jones decides to do it entirely on his own and Black never lifts a finger. Verdict: Jones is fully responsible, the blocked alternative is irrelevant.
- Same basic setup, but instead of Black watching, Jones has a neurological condition that made it causally impossible for him to decide otherwise the whole time, though in fact he deliberated normally and chose for his own reasons. Verdict: not responsible, or at least much less so. The impossibility being "inside" him feels different.
- Two assassins independently shoot the same victim at the same moment; either shot alone would've been fatal, neither knew about the other. Verdict: each is fully responsible for the death even though neither's act was necessary for it.
- Someone drives drunk and kills a pedestrian. They couldn't have avoided driving drunk at the time (they were too far gone to make the right call once drinking), but they chose to start drinking knowing they'd drive home. Verdict: fully responsible anyway.
- A hacker has installed a device that *would* have made me click a phishing link, but I click it on my own because I genuinely fall for the scam. Verdict: responsible for clicking, blameable for my gullibility, though something about it feels slightly different from case 1 and I can't say what.

So: is there one principle about responsibility and alternatives that gets me all five? My suspicion is no, and that 1 and 2 are doing all the damage, but I want to see it shown rather than asserted. If a simple principle (something like Frankfurt's own conclusion, or some tracing condition, or a "robust alternatives" view) requires dropping one of my verdicts, tell me which one and defend the amputation.
