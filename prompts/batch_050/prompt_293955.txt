I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics and I'm running our reading group's session on the hole argument next week. I want to hand out a decision tree that walks someone from "ok, GR is generally covariant" to a named position, but every taxonomy I find just lists isms without saying what actually divides them, so the discussion always collapses into people talking past each other.

Please write a decision tree, 4-6 branching questions, each one phrased as a concrete question someone could answer without already knowing the position names. Stuff like: if two models of GR agree everywhere except inside some matter-free region where their metric fields are related by a smooth reshuffling, was there a genuine open question about what happens inside that region, or only one physical situation described twice? Or: when we say "the same spacetime point" in two different models, does that mean anything over and above the field values and collision events located there? No question should presuppose the jargon.

Leaves I definitely want on the tree: manifold substantivalism that just bites the bullet on the indeterminism, sophisticated substantivalism (the Leibniz-equivalence / anti-haecceitist move), a relationalism where spacetime points drop out of the fundamental ontology entirely, and the deflationary response that the whole argument equivocates on what counts as "physically distinct" so it never gets going. If two of these leaves turn out to be unseparable by any question on the tree, flag that explicitly instead of forcing a distinction -- I have a feeling at least one pair is basically the same view in two dialects.

The thing I actually care about: my hunch is that the hole argument is two arguments stapled together, one about determinism and one about what spacetime points are, and the standard "responses" respond to different halves. So build the tree from the logical space rather than from the names: first lay out the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the positions in that space, and for any combination nobody defends, say in a sentence whether its empty because its incoherent or just unattractive. At each leaf, also add one sentence on whether that position treats the indeterminism in the hole as a real feature of the world or as an artefact of descriptive redundancy.

Audience is other grad students who've read the Earman & Norton stuff but haven't committed to a stance, so keep every node answerable from the physics plus naive metaphysics.
