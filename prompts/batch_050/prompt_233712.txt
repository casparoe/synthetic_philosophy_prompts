You are a philosopher specializing in aesthetics and the theory of artistic value, advising a graduate seminar that has just finished reading Tolstoy's treatise What Is Art?. Think carefully and work through the problem step by step before settling on any conclusions.

The central positive account of art in that treatise rests on an analogy that has since become common currency in criticism: art is a kind of infection. The artist, having lived a feeling, externalizes it in words, sounds, or forms so that the audience catches the same feeling, much as one person transmits a disease to another; a work succeeds as art to the degree that it is contagious, and its worth is fixed by the worth of the feeling spread. A member of the seminar leans heavily on this analogy to defend Tolstoy's full package of conclusions: that infectiousness is the measure of art as art; that the sincerity of the artist is the master condition of infectiousness; that art produced by professionals who manufacture effects they do not themselves feel is counterfeit in something like the way quack medicine is counterfeit; and that Tolstoy was therefore justified in striking most of the canon — Shakespeare, late Beethoven, Wagner, nearly all of his own fiction — from the rolls of genuine art, keeping only what unites its audience in universally shareable feeling.

Your task is to audit the analogy rather than to recount Tolstoy's theory. Please proceed as follows:

1. Begin with a brief brainstorming stage: list as many candidate correspondences and candidate disanalogies as you can before filtering them, so that nothing load-bearing is missed.

2. Give an explicit correspondence table: what plays the role of the pathogen, the original host, the vector, the susceptible population, immunity, latency, the symptom? Flag any slots the analogy leaves unfilled or fills awkwardly.

3. Separate the disanalogies that matter for the intended argumentative use from those that are merely striking. Consider at least: infection is involuntary and indifferent to the host's cooperation, whereas reception of art is active and interpretive; a pathogen is replicated in the new host, whereas a feeling may be transformed in transmission; a carrier typically suffers the disease, whereas Tolstoy's counterfeit artist feels nothing yet supposedly still transmits; and infectiousness is morally neutral as a mechanism, while the theory makes the value of the transmitted content do the normative work.

4. For each of the four conclusions the seminar member draws, state whether the analogy actually supports it, supports it only given independent premises the analogy quietly smuggles in, or fails to support it once the disanalogies are on the table.

5. If the infection picture can be repaired, say how; if some better analogy for what Tolstoy actually cared about is available (inoculation, translation, resonance, mood contagion in crowds), propose it and say what it preserves and what it sacrifices.

Conclude with a verdict: does the infection analogy carry Tolstoy's purge of the canon, or was the analogy merely lending rhetorical force to conclusions whose real weight rests on independent commitments about feeling, sincerity, and human brotherhood?
