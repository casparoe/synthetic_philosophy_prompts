ok so heres the deal. i got into this long argument with my roommate about whether the brain is "basically a computer" and it went in circles for like three hours. every time i said sure, neurons do something like information processing, hed come back with this thing about how if computation is just about finding the right abstract pattern, then a rock or a wall "computes" whatever you want it to, so saying the brain computes either means nothing or needs some extra ingredient nobody has nailed down. and every time i patched it up (its about causal structure! its about what the system is FOR!) hed find a way for the rock to sneak back in. it honestly felt like the same two lines coming back over and over.

which made me think: thats literally what a villanelle does. so write me a villanelle (the form with the two refrain lines that keep coming back, nineteen lines, like the dylan thomas one) about this exact debate. one refrain should be the "the brain computes" side and the other should be the "then so does everything" side, and the way the refrains alternate and get recontextualized by each new stanza should mirror how every attempted fix just reactivates the same objection. when the two refrains finally sit next to each other in the closing quatrain it should land the actual point of the debate: that everything hinges on whether computation is something in the physics or something in how we choose to describe it.

someone who actually knows this literature should be able to tell which refrain is which and nod along, but it should also just work as a poem for someone whos never heard of any of this. dont make the rock side a joke position either, its annoyingly hard to kill, thats the whole problem lol
