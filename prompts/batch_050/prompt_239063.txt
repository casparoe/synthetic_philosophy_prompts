I'm taking a comparative politics seminar this semester, and we just finished a unit on corruption that left me more confused than when I started. Our working definition, borrowed loosely from the literature, is that corruption is the misuse of entrusted power for private gain, but the professor keeps pressing us on whether that definition travels well across cultures and institutional settings, and I'm not satisfied with where our discussions have landed.

For the unit's final paper I'm assembling a catalogue of contested or borderline cases -- practices that some observers classify as corrupt and others defend as legitimate or at least understandable -- and I want to test the definition against as many distinct challenges as I can find. Here is what I already have: clientelism, where politicians direct public goods or jobs toward specific communities in exchange for electoral support; guanxi-style gift exchange, where gifts to officials are embedded in long-term reciprocal relationships rather than one-off bribes; the revolving door, where regulators later take lucrative jobs in the industries they oversaw; large campaign donations that buy access rather than explicit favors; nepotism and cronyism in public hiring; and small customary gifts or facilitation payments that local norms treat as expressions of respect rather than bribery. I believe these cover the standard examples from our syllabus and the assigned readings.

I'd like one more case that is not on this list and not a rewording, special case, or combination of anything on it. In particular, my listed cases all involve public officials and some kind of personal benefit, so ideally the new case should put pressure on a different component of the definition -- for instance, whether entrusted power is necessary, whether private gain is necessary, or whether the practice even counts as misuse if everyone involved accepts it. Describe the case concretely, explain which part of the definition it strains, and then tell me honestly whether you think it exposes a genuine flaw in the definition or merely reveals a bias in our intuitions about which practices count as corrupt.
