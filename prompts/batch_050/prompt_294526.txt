ok so backstory: i gave up meat about eight months ago, felt great about myself, and then made the mistake of reading a bunch of panpsychism stuff at 1am (hard problem, russellian monism, the usual intro pipeline) and now i'm genuinely stuck. if there's something it's like to be an electron, does anything at all follow about how i should act? my brother keeps texting me "did your salad suffer" and the anoying thing is i can't actually tell him why the answer is no. or yes.

so here's what i want: a decision tree that sorts people on the ethics of panpsychism. not "which philosopher said what" — a tree built from the actual logical space, questions first, positions second. something a smart friend could click through in a google doc without knowing any jargon, where each node is a question they can answer from the armchair and at the end they land on a named position (or on "nobody holds this, here's why").

the node questions i have in mind are things like (you don't have to use mine, but this is the vibe):
- could a fundamental particle's experience be *unpleasant* at all, or does valence only make sense once you have a certain amount of structure?
- if you grant micro-subjects, do they combine into bigger subjects with their own experiences? and which collections count — brains, rocks, my table, the internet?
- even if electrons can suffer, is there anything we can *do* that makes things go better or worse for them, or is micro-suffering just fixed by physics whatever we get up to?
- does the sheer number of particles swamp the moral calculus — if you take micro-suffering seriously, does it drown out all the cow-and-human suffering we used to worry about?

requirements:
1. 6-8 branching questions, each phrased so someone can answer it without knowing which position it leads to. no "do you accept subject-summing?" — use a concrete case instead.
2. each leaf gets a name. if it's a position people actually defend, give the standard-ish label and roughly whose view it is (names are fine, but no citations or reading list). if the combination of answers is unoccupied, say so and diagnose why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?
3. for each leaf, one or two sentences on its most awkward commitment — the bullet a defender has to bite.
4. flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate, rather than pretending it can.
5. at least one leaf must be the position that panpsychism changes basically nothing ethically — i suspect that's where i end up and i want to see exactly what i have to believe to land there.
6. at least one leaf must be the "everything suffers and the calculus explodes" position, because i want to see precisely which answer(s) force you into it.

keep it casual, no bibliography, no "see chalmers 1996". i want something i can actually make my friends take.
