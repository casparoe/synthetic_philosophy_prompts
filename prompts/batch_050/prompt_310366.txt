I'm grading term papers for a metaphysics seminar this semester and the origin essentialism unit is coming up. Every year I assign the chunk of Kripke's Naming and Necessity lectures where he argues (well, mostly just asserts) that a given person couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg -- his example is the Queen, and his point is roughly that someone conceived from an entirely different sperm and egg, even one who grew up to be qualitatively identical to Elizabeth II down to the last hair, would just be someone else, a duplicate, not her. Students either find this instantly obvious or find it completely bonkers, and the papers split accordingly, and I've realised I don't actually have a principled standard for grading them beyond "engaged with Kripke, didn't strawman the other side." So before this year's batch comes in, I want you to write the desiderata: what does a theory of origin essentialism (or a rejection of it) actually have to deliver to count as adequate? Which cases does it have to get right -- the sperm-and-egg case, obviously, but what about artifacts (could this table have been made from a slightly different hunk of wood? from a wholly different one?), what about organisms where conception is fuzzy or gradual, what about twin cases and duplicates? Which intuitions must it vindicate and which is it allowed to explain away, and by what standard do we tell the difference? What must it be consistent with -- a workable modal semantics, the distinction between essential and accidental properties in the first place, presumably something about not generating silly essences like being self-identical or being such that 2+2=4? Give me the case or consideration motivating each desideratum, not just the list.

Two things I specifically want, and here I'd like you to actually commit rather than survey. First, tell me which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable for any theory in this space and which ones are just prejudices of one camp (I have a suspicion that "match the folk intuition about the Queen" is load-bearing for Kripkeans in a way they'd never admit, but maybe I'm wrong -- tell me). Second, if some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly and say which requirement is doing the excluding, because honestly that would be the most useful thing I could hand my students. Then, briefly -- like a paragraph, no more -- score the main contenders against your list: Kripke-style necessity of origins, a counterpart-theoretic view where the whole question gets deflated, and a haecceitist or anti-essentialist "you could have been anyone" line. Keep the weight on the criteria themselves; the scorecard is just there to show the list has teeth. And don't hedge everything -- if you think one of the desiderata I implied above is actually bogus, or if you think the whole debate is running on an equivocation between epistemic and metaphysical possibility, plant your flag and argue it.
