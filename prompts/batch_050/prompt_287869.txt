Alright, here's my situation. I'm a retired chemist, spent forty years on reaction kinetics and never read a page of philosophy until about a year ago, and now I can't stop. Recently my granddaughter made me play Undertale, the whole thing, pacifist route first and then, at her insistence and against my better judgement, the genocide route. I'm still thinking about it weeks later, which I did not expect from a cartoon game. So I want an essay that reads Undertale through the question of whether video game characters can matter morally at all, and does it seriously, not just as an excuse to talk about a quirky indie game. The philosophy angle I keep circling around is this: the game clearly wants me to feel that killing Toriel or Papyrus is worse than killing enemies in the hundred other games I've been handed over the years, and it builds that feeling through things like characters remembering your resets, Sans's final judgement, and Flowey turning out to be a stand-in for the completionist player who treats everyone as content to be consumed. But is any of that actually an argument? In my other games nobody bats an eye, and the standard line seems to be that nothing is harmed because nobody is there to be harmed, the characters are just code and sprites. So: does Undertale smuggle in the assumption that its characters have some moral standing, and if so, on what grounds -- because they act like persons, because they're written to model suffering, because the player has to invest real attention and affect in them? Where does that leave the supposed inconsistency in how we treat different kinds of violence in games (I've seen this called the gamer's dillema somewhere)? And please also push back where the game deserves it: is it cheating that the characters remember things no real game character could, so that the guilt it manufactures depends on a fantasy feature? Does the game actually earn its moral weight or does it just manipulate sentiment very skillfully, and if it's the latter, is that even a criticism? One more request: write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables. I want something I can read like a letter, not a listicle.
