Longtime LW/ACX reader, first time actually posting something like this. There's a line that gets thrown around in basically every comment section argument about LLMs and I want it taken apart properly: "it's just autocomplete." (Sometimes dressed up as "just statistics" or "just predicting the next token.")

Here's where I keep hitting it. I'll say something like "the model seems to understand this German paragraph well enough to translate it," and somebody replies "it's just autocomplete, there's no understanding happening," and then the conversation dies because I can never figure out what work "just" is doing there. It feels like it's being used to settle a question that Hofstadter raised about machine translation years back -- he had those essays poking at Google Translate, arguing its fluent output hid the fact that nothing had been grasped -- but people deploy the slogan as if the slogan itself were the argument.

So, can you separate out the distinct precise claims that could be hiding in "it's just autocomplete"? Some candidates I can gesture at: the claim that predicting the next token is all that's *happening mechanically*; the claim that there's no internal model of what the text is about; the claim that whatever is happening doesn't *count* as understanding, for some stated criterion of understanding; the claim that the model only interpolates and never generalizes; the claim that human understanding is a totally different kind of thing. There are probably others I'm not seeing. For each one, tell me: is it a platitude, is it substantive and plausibly true, is it substantive but false, and does it actually follow from the fact that the training objective is next-token prediction? Because I suspect people slide from the mechanical claim (which is basically definitional) to the no-understanding claim without noticing the gap, and I'd like to know if there's a version of the slogan where that slide is actually legitimate.

Also: which reading would someone need for the slogan to actually refute Hofstadter-style worries about statistical translation, vs. which readings leave those worries exactly where they were? If every reading either is trivial or doesn't do the argumentative work, say that too.
