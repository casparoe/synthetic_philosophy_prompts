Before I read any further into the Humeanism–anti-Humeanism dispute, I want to fix the standard of evaluation myself rather than inherit one from either camp. Write the desiderata for a theory of natural laws, for the specific purpose of underwriting scientific explanation and inductive inference (not satisfying any prior metaphysical temperament): what an adequate account of lawhood must deliver — the phenomena it must explain, such as why laws but not accidental generalizations support counterfactuals, how exception-riddled special-science generalizations can still count as laws, and whether spacetime symmetries should be classified as laws — the distinctions it must respect, and the constraints from physics it cannot violate, with the case or puzzle behind each requirement. Mark which requirements are genuine and which are camp-specific prejudices (say, the insistence that laws govern rather than summarize, or that they hold with metaphysical necessity), flag any subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, and identify which single desideratum excludes the most candidates. Conclude with a brief scorecard of the best-system account, dispositional essentialism, and Armstrong-style necessitarianism against the list — but the criteria are the deliverable, so keep the scoring short.
