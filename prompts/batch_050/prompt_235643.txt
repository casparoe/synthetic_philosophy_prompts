I'm in a cog sci reading group and last week we did the mental imagery debate (Kosslyn vs Pylyshyn and all that). I couldn't make the session where they discussed Pylyshyn's 1973 paper, "What the Mind's Eye Tells the Mind's Brain" in Psychological Bulletin, so I asked a friend who was there to fill me in, and I also skimmed a summary in an intro cog sci textbook I have. The annoying thing is they basically said the same thing, so I assumed it must be right, but the more I think about it the more suspicious I get. Here's the combined version of what they told me:

Pylyshyn's argument is that mental images don't really exist. When people say they "picture" something, like imagining their living room and counting the windows, there's no actual picture-like thing in the head -- what's really going on is that they know facts about their living room, stored as propositions (basically sentences in a mental language), and they just read off answers from those. That's why it takes longer to "scan" across a big imagined distance: more propositions to retrieve. So all of Kosslyn's imagery experiments, the mental scanning, mental rotation, image zooming and so on, don't tell us anything about a special imagistic format, because subjects are really just role-playing based on what they already know about how perception works -- they know it takes longer to look at something far away, so they unconsciously take longer when the experimenter asks them to imagine it. Imagery is therefore epiphenomenal: the experience is real in some loose sense, but it plays no causal role in the cognition, and the whole research program built on it is bunk.

The part I keep tripping over is the "images don't exist" bit, and the claim that propositional storage = sentences you just read off. That seems like it would make Pylyshyn's view almost obviously false -- like, doesn't he have imagery experiences himself, and wouldn't he know they're not like reading sentences? But two independent sources gave me the same story, so maybe I'm the one who's wrong and this is a fair if harsh summary of the paper.

Break it down for me -- which parts accurately reflect what Pylyshyn actually argued in that paper, which parts are common simplifications that a defender of his view would grudgingly accept, and which parts misrepresent him (either by overstating the thesis or by importing objections from elsewhere)? And if the misrepresented bits get corrected, does the conclusion -- that Kosslyn-style imagery research is bunk and Pylyshyn basically won that round -- still stand, or was it depending on the unfair parts?
