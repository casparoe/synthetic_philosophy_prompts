I help write comps for our grad students in the history of philosophy stream, and this year I want a question on Augustine's treatment of time in Confessions Book XI that can't be answered by trotting out the greatest hits (the past doesn't exist, the future doesn't exist, the present has no extension, so time must be a distention of the soul, yadda yadda). Every year someone writes the "what is time, if nobody asks me I know" essay and I want a question where that essay earns a fail. So: draft ONE question a candidate could answer in about 90 minutes.

To force them beyond summary, I want the question built around this scenario I made up -- call it the Untended Metronome. Augustine holds that when we measure an interval (say, judging a long syllable against a short one), what we actually measure is an impression in the mind, not the sound itself. Now suppose a metronome ticks for exactly two hours in an empty room while every mind that would have heard it is asleep or absent, and Augustine is asked: was the interval between the first tick and the last still two hours long? The question should require candidates to work out what Augustine can and cannot say here without either (a) smuggling back in a mind-independent duration his own puzzle rules out, or (b) landing in the view that intervals simply have no duration when unexperienced, which sits badly with things he clearly wants to say (God measures created time, the created world had temporal order before anyone narrated it, etc.). A strong candidate should also have to distinguish memory of an interval from the interval itself, and say whether the metronome case is a problem for the distentio account or actually a vindication of it -- both answers should be defensible if argued well.

Deliverables: (1) the exam question itself, phrased the way it would appear on the paper, with any necessary setup text, (2) a model answer of around 600 words, and (3) a marking scheme built around five must-hit points worth 20 marks total, where each point comes with a one-line note on how candidates typically bungle it (e.g., treating Augustine as a crude subjectivist, or answering the metronome case from an Aristotelian measure-of-motion standpoint without acknowledging the tension with the measurement puzzle). The marking scheme should make clear what separates a pass from a distinction -- specifically, a distinction-level answer has to take a stand on whether mind-independence of duration survives the argument, not just describe the argument.
