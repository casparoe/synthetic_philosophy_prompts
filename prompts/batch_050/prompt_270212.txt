Somehow I ended up on my mid-sized city's charter review commission (~200k people), and we've been handed a proposal that's splitting us right down the middle. It bundles two changes to who can vote in municipal elections only — mayor, city council, school board, local ballot questions, nothing state or federal:

1. Any resident 18+ who has lived in the city for at least three years gets the local franchise, citizen or not. That's roughly 30,000 adults here — green card holders, long-term visa holders, some folks with more complicated status. They pay property taxes directly or through rent, sales tax, their kids are in the schools, the whole thing.
2. The voting age for those same local elections drops from 18 to 16.

Some context: our last mayoral race was decided by about 1,200 votes, turnout is anemic, and state law lets us set our own local franchise, so this is genuinely ours to decide. The council is split and wants our reccomendation within a month.

Here's where it gets philosophical for me. The two commissioners pushing hardest for the bundle keep saying that if the city's decisions bind you and the city's revenue comes out of your pocket, you're part of the people the city governs, and there's no principled line that excludes a 25-year-old green card holder who has been here three years, or that includes an 18-year-old who has never once thought about the council but excludes a 17-year-old who works 20 hours a week and sits through budget hearings. The two most opposed commissioners say voting isn't like paying taxes or getting a parking ticket — it's membership in the body that governs, and bodies get to set membership rules; and 16-year-olds, whatever else is true, are legal minors whose parents can make major decisions for them, so letting them vote while they can't sign a lease is just incoherent. A fifth commissioner keeps floating that we should treat visa holders differently from permanent residents, since some of them will be gone in two years.

Write the memo I'll circulate to the full commission before our vote. It should:

- Come down on one of these: adopt both, adopt only one (say which), adopt with conditions (specify them), or reject both.
- Treat the two halves of the bundle seperately rather than assuming they stand or fall together.
- Grapple honestly with the strongest argument on whichever side loses — not a strawman of it.
- Take a stand on what the franchise is actually for, since I suspect every other disagreement we have is downstream of that.

Keep it around 800–1,000 words, memo format. At the very end, on its own line, state the bottom line in exactly this form:

Recommendation: <adopt both / adopt non-citizen voting only / adopt age-16 voting only / adopt with conditions / reject both>
