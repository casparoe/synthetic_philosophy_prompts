I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and a situation I keep encountering in machine learning work has started to feel like a genuinely deep problem rather than an engineering nuisance. I'm hoping it has an established name and literature I can look into.

Two concrete instances. First: we trained an image classifier to distinguish cats from dogs. Unknown to us, every cat photo in the training set was taken indoors and every dog photo outdoors. Deployed, the system labels an indoor dog as a cat. Our team split into two camps: one says the system learned the concept "cat" and made an error on this input; the other says it learned the concept "indoor animal" and is functioning exactly as trained. Nothing in the weights, the behavior, or the training data seems to settle which description is right.

Second: a fraud-detection model flags a transaction. The investigators call this a false positive, an error. But the flag perfectly matches the pattern the model actually learned. The "error" seems to exist only relative to what we intended the model to track — and that intention is nowhere inside the system. It lives, if anywhere, in our heads and in the history of how the system was built.

The general pattern: calling an output an error presupposes that the system was supposed to track one category rather than another, but this "supposed to" does not appear to be a property of the system itself. If that's right, it seems to follow that no artificial system could ever be wrong on its own — wrongness would always be borrowed from a designer. And then I worry the same goes for brains.

My questions: Is there an established philosophical concept for this — the underdetermination of what a system's internal states are about, such that the very possibility of error becomes ill-defined? I suspect it is adjacent to, but distinct from, what ML researchers call underspecification or shortcut learning (a failure of training to constrain behavior) and from ordinary ambiguity; I'd like those differences drawn precisely. If a literature exists, what are the main proposed ways of fixing what a system represents — by its causal history, by its function, by something else — and where does each break down? A bare name won't satisfy me: I want the phenomenon carved at its joints, including boundary cases where the error description clearly does and clearly doesn't apply. If nothing established fits, please coin a term and define it accordingly.
