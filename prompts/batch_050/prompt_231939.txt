I TA for an intro cog-sci course and, while procrastinating on grading, I sketched an argument for why prototype theory can't possibly be the right story about concepts. The conclusion strikes me as obviously too strong, but I've been staring at it for two days and every step still looks locally fine, which is making me twitchy. Here it is. (1) Decades of typicality data show that people treat category membership as graded: robins are judged "better" birds than penguins, chairs better furniture than rugs, and reaction times track these gradients. (2) If a concept were a definition — a set of necessary and sufficient conditions — all members would be equal, so the gradients wouldn't exist; hence concepts are prototypes, similarity-based representations rather than definitions. (3) If a concept is a prototype, then a complex concept like PET FISH has to be built out of the prototypes of its parts, since the parts are all there is to build from. (4) But that construction fails: the typical pet fish (a goldfish) is neither a typical pet nor a typical fish, and in general you can't get the prototype of a compound by combining the prototypes of its constituents. (5) So prototypes cannot underwrite any complex thought. (6) But nearly every thought anyone has is complex — even a humble sentence like "the cat is on the mat" combines several concepts — so if prototypes are what concepts are, almost no one ever thinks anything with determinate content; at best we have a small stock of primitive prototype-thoughts and a lot of noise. Since (6) is absurd, something earlier has to give. The standard textbook response is that this "only shows prototypes aren't compositional," but that seems to just concede (5) and then change the subject, because I don't see what work "concept" is doing if it can't combine.

For what it's worth, I'm attached to (1) — the typicality findings seem like real data any theory has to explain — and I'd much rather find the crack in (3) or (4) than give that up. But when I try, I keep sliding back into feeling that if a concept is *nothing but* a prototype, then complex concepts really do have to be prototype-sums. A friend in the phil department says I've smuggled in an assumption about what "built out of" has to mean, but he couldn't articulate it and I suspect he was bluffing.

If you think the argument is actually sound and I should just swallow (6), say so, but you'll have to work to convince me.
