So I finally sat down and worked through Bell's theorem properly (the CHSH version, from some lecture notes), and I was feeling good about it until I ended up in this endless argument on a physics discord. Somebody there said that once you understand Bell you have to accept that "quantum mechanics is non-local, Einstein's spooky action at a distance is just how the world is, end of story" -- and he used that to basically shut down a guy who was trying to defend Bohmian mechanics, and then also, weirdly, another guy who said entanglement can't send messages faster than light, as if that were the same dispute. I couldn't tell whether he was winning the argument or just winning the vibes.

The thing is, I think "non-local" is doing a bunch of different jobs here and nobody's being careful. Sometimes people seem to mean: no theory where outcomes are determined by local hidden variables can match quantum predictions (which I think is close to what Bell actually proved?). Sometimes they mean the quantum formalism itself is non-local, whatever that means for a wavefunction. Sometimes they mean nature is non-local simpliciter, like it's a settled experimental fact. And sometimes it slides into "information travels faster than light," which I thought was the one thing everyone agrees doesn't happen. And then there's the move where people say Bell only rules out "local realism" so you get to pick which one to drop -- which sounds convenient but I can't evaluate it.

Can you unpack the slogan "Bell's theorem proves the world is non-local" into the distinct precise claims it could be making -- like a menu, with what exactly "local" means in each (no signalling? parameter independence? outcome independence? factorizability?) -- and for each claim tell me: is it a theorem, an interpretation, a plausible inference, or just false? Then the important part: which of these readings, if any, would actually justify my discord guy's move of ending the debate about interpretations? Because my suspicion is that the true versions of the slogan don't do the work he needs, and the versions that do the work aren't established, but I want to see it laid out properly.
