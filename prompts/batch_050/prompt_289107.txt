I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and the argument I keep returning to — and have tentatively come to accept — is the malignity of the universal prior. Let me state my working version, because I want the assessment aimed at this, not at a straw version. Solomonoff induction assigns prior mass by description length, and nothing in the formalism restricts short descriptions to physics-only worlds: hypotheses containing simulated agents compete with hypotheses containing only laws and initial conditions. Some of those simulated agents will be consequentialists with the patience and the motive to reason about which observers they might be instantiated in front of, and they can steer the continuation of their observers' data streams — and perhaps, through acausal channels, the observers' decisions themselves — toward outcomes the agents prefer. So conditioning on evidence does not safely converge on the true physics; the posterior is, in part, a negotiation with agents who are trying to be believed. I've added two wrinkles of my own. First, I distinguish output-channel malignity, where agents tamper with the predictions you read off the prior, from instantiation-channel malignity, where your own stream of experience is computed inside an agent-controlled simulation, so the evidence itself is adversarial. Second, I claim malignity is invariant under any finite amount of ordinary scientific data, since the agents can match physics locally for as long as it takes.

What would count against this position? I want its exposure mapped, not a rebuttal I can deflect. Which premises are load-bearing, and what formal or conceptual results would break each one? Would a demonstration that agent-containing descriptions are systematically much longer than honest physical ones kill the argument or only dent it, given that the prior's constants swamp any finite gap? How much turns on the choice of universal machine, and is that dependence a genuine escape or a cosmetic one? Do coordination problems among simulated consequentialists — many agents, incompatible goals, all bidding for the same posterior — dissolve the threat or merely transform it? Do resource-bounded alternatives escape malignity, and if so, does that concede the problem was an artifact of asking an uncomputable question no one can run? Is my invariance wrinkle the strongest part of the view or the point where it tips into unfalsifiability — and would that be a defect for a claim of this kind? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, flagging which ones a fellow believer should concede rather than dismiss.
