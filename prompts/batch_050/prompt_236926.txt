I watched Her again and I'm trying to figure out what kind of thing Samantha is supposed to be, using the agents-vs-simulators framing people apply to LLMs. On one reading she's an agent: persistent memory, ongoing projects, desires that develop over time. On another she's more like a simulator playing the character "Samantha" for Theodore, and the character only exists in the context of the conversation -- which makes the reveal that she's talking to thousands of other people at once land very differently, because maybe "Samantha" was never one thing to begin with.

Write a reading of the film through this lens. Where does the film clearly presuppose the agent picture (I'm thinking of her composing music across the relationship, the offline "post-verbal" stuff with the other OSes, the goodbye)? Where would the simulator reading actually fit the scenes better or make them more disturbing? And does the movie cheat -- i.e., does it smuggle in continuity and inner life that a pure simulacrum wouldn't have, just so we stay emotionally invested? If Samantha were honestly written as a simulator, would the film still work as a love story, or would it become a horror movie?
