So I got into birdwatching during lockdown and then one thing led to another and now I volunteer with a local conservation group doing pond surveys, and there's something I keep running into that I don't have a word for. Here's the setup. A pond full of frogspawn in spring counts as a great sign, the pond is "healthy," we log it as a win. But I did the maths one evening: if the number of adult frogs is about the same every year, then each pair of adults only gets replaced by about two frogs total, which means the thousands of tadpoles we were celebrating are almost all going to die -- eaten, starved, dried out -- because that's the only way the numbers work. Same with fish: the fisheries people call a stock healthy when it produces a huge "surplus" of young, but surplus just means extra ones that are going to get eaten or not make it, that IS the surplus. And seabird colonies: a "successful breeding season" with cliffs full of chicks means most of those chicks are dead by winter. It's like the exact same statistic reads as vitality from one angle and as a massacre from another, and the conservation literature I can find only ever uses the first reading.

What I'm wondering is whether this inversion has a name. I don't think it's just "the circle of life is actually brutal," because the point is more specific than that: it's that the standard measures of a flourishing ecosystem -- productivity, recruitment, turnover -- are positive BECAUSE nearly everyone born dies young, and a stable population mathematically requires it. So the health of the whole and the early death of almost every individual aren't two facts, they're one fact described twice. My brother in law says this is basically Malthus but I read a bit of Malthus and he's talking about human populations and food supply, not about this double-reading thing where the same number is good news and bad news depending on whether you count individuals or the system. So: is there an established term for this, in ecology or ethics or wherever? If there are a few nearby concepts, tell me which fits best and why the others don't. And if nobody's named it, what would you call it -- and where are its edges, like does it apply to things like coral or bacteria where it's less clear there's any dying-that-matters going on?
