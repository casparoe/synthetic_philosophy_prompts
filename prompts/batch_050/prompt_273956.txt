I write a small blog in the rationalist orbit, and I'm drafting a post meant to sort readers into positions on divine foreknowledge and counterfactuals of creaturely freedom. My audience is fluent in counterfactuals, possible-worlds talk, and decision theory, but mostly hasn't encountered the theology, so the tree can presuppose that vocabulary. Build me a decision tree, not a history of the debate. Start from a shared assumption (an omniscient God exists) and branch on diagnostic questions phrased as concrete claims a reader can accept or reject without knowing any labels: for instance, whether "if Eve were placed in circumstances C, she would freely eat the fruit" can be true prior to any divine decree; whether such truths require something that makes them true, and if so what; whether God's knowledge of future free acts is grounded in his own willing or independent of it; whether propositions about a genuinely open future have truth values at all. Aim for roughly five or six questions. The leaves should include at least Molinism, Thomism, simple-foreknowledge Arminianism, and open theism, each stating the answer pattern that lands there. Then survey the unoccupied combinations and diagnose each: is nobody there because the view is incoherent, unstable under its own logic, or merely unnamed? Flag any positions the tree cannot separate (I suspect Molinism from certain congruist variants) rather than papering over the distinction. Finally, since this is a classification instrument rather than an argument, tell me where the taxonomy itself is most vulnerable: which fork a well-informed objector would deny is a real distinction, and what consideration would lead you to restructure the tree.
