At my high school, the staff ethics policy says teachers may not accept any gift from a student or a student's family worth more than $25, and anything above that must be declined or reported to the administration. There is no rule at all against handwritten cards, homemade food, or flowers, and nothing about gifts given after a student has graduated. Meanwhile, I have relatives abroad who consider an envelope of cash handed to a teacher at the New Year a basic expression of respect and gratitude, and who find our rule faintly insulting, as though generosity were being treated as bribery. Write an essay reconstructing what conception of corruption our school's policy presupposes. Consider, for example: does the policy assume that corrupting influence scales with monetary value, so that a $20 mug is safe but a $200 watch is not, and is that plausible? Does it treat an identical gift differently depending on whether it is given before or after final grades are submitted, and if so, is the underlying worry about actually distorting the teacher's judgment, about the mere appearance of a transaction, or about unfairness toward students whose families cannot afford to give? Work out which of these theories the policy strictly requires in order to make sense and which it merely happens to fit well with. In particular, could a community running on my relatives' view, where gift-giving is understood as reciprocal obligation and respect rather than as a market exchange, adopt the very same $25 rule on entirely different grounds? Finally, say what the policy would have to look like if the school instead held that corruption lies in the giver's intent, or that any gift of any size creates an improper debt, and whether you think the school's actual rule gets the concept of corruption right.
