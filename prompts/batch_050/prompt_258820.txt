So before we pick this back up on friday, I want the spec first, not another contender. Write out the full list of requirements that any adequate theory of the fall of Rome would have to meet, and for each requirement give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement. Stuff I suspect belongs on the list, but tell me if I'm wrong about any of these:

- it has to explain why the WEST fell in the fifth century while the eastern empire did not -- any theory that "explains the fall of Rome" but applies equally to Constantinople in 500 AD has explained too much, right?

- it has to give an actual mechanism and not just a correlation (lead pipes correlate with literally everything Rome did, come on)
- it has to be compatible with the archaeological record rather than just with what literate elites wrote down, since elites complaining about decline is a constant of human history
- it has to say whether the fall was even bad, or bad for whom -- was 476 a catastrophe or just a change of management for an actual peasant in Gaul?
- it has to take a position on whether "fall" vs "gradual transformation" is itself the right framing, because that fight kept eating our whole argument

For each item, mark whether its genuinely non-negotiable or whether its just the preference of one camp (military historians, climate people, the transformation crowd, the Gibbon-style moral-decline traditionalists).
