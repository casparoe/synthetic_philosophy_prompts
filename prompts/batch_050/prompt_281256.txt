I run a philosophy of mind reading group at my university, and our sessions on predictive processing keep collapsing into the same unproductive exchange: half the room insists that the free energy principle is a revolutionary unified theory of life and mind, while the other half insists it is old Bayesian wine in new mathematical bottles, and both halves cite the same dense papers in support. I want to settle this by force of structure rather than force of exegesis. Write me a decision tree that takes someone from the innocuous starting commitment "the brain is in the business of prediction" to a definite position on what predictive processing and the free energy principle actually amount to.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a roster of names. I want roughly eight to ten branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete question a participant could answer without knowing which position it leads to: for example, whether a creature's seeking evidence for its model of the world can be redescribed without remainder as the creature fulfilling predictions about its own movement; whether a system could persist and resist entropy while failing to minimize free energy; whether the theory requires genuinely contentful internal states or merely a causal economy that can be usefully described as if it were inferential; and what observation, if any, would count as the framework failing rather than being flexibly reparameterized. The leaves should include, at minimum: the representationalist reading on which the brain maintains a hierarchical generative model of the causes of its sensory input; the maximalist reading on which free energy minimization is a constitutive truth about any self-organizing system whatsoever; an enactivist reading that keeps the predictive architecture but denies it requires internal models with content; a deflationary reading on which the free energy formalism is bookkeeping that adds no empirical content beyond the ordinary Bayesian-brain picture; and the skeptical charge that the package is unfalsifiable rather than a theory at all.

Please flag explicitly any pair of leaves the tree cannot in fact separate, and note any combination of answers that lands in unoccupied territory, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The payoff I care about is a classification of what kind of claim "the brain minimizes free energy" even is — empirical hypothesis, constitutive truth about systems as such, or mathematical theorem — so weight the nodes toward that question rather than toward textbook allegiances, and phrase every node so that a newcomer who has read one primer but no primary literature can answer it honestly.
