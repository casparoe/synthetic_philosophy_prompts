I keep trying to figure out what my own rule actually is, so let me just dump my gut verdicts on a batch of cases on you and you tell me what principle, if any, is running underneath them. My neighbor fires his pistol into the air on New Year's Eve: clearly not okay, even though the chance the bullet actually comes down on somebody is tiny. A friend drives home after one glass of wine, well under the legal limit but measurably a bit worse at driving: fine, everyone does this. A family buys a giant SUV instead of a sedan, which roughly doubles their odds of killing a pedestrian if they hit one: fine, or at least not immoral, even though spread over a lifetime of driving that probably adds up to more expected death than the pistol guy. A parent sends their kid to school with the sniffles in flu season: fine. An unvaccinated guy goes to a packed concert during a measles outbreak: not fine, even though the added risk to any individual in that room is way smaller than the SUV driver's added risk per year. A virologist publishes a paper showing how to make a flu strain more transmissible, knowing it could plausibly help vaccine design and also be misused: I lean okay but I genuinely can't settle it. A city approves a late-night concert venue next to a residential block, raising traffic and accident risk for people nobody asked: fine, and I think the fact that there was a public hearing is doing work for me there, though maybe it shouldn't.

The pair that keeps bugging me is the pistol versus the SUV.
