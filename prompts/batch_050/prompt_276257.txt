I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of mind, and for a chapter of my dissertation on the current standing of functionalism I am compiling a catalogue of the standard objections, organized by which commitment of the theory each one attacks. My list so far:

1. Absent qualia. A system could instantiate the full functional organization of a human being — Block's homunculi-head simulation carried out by the population of China — while having no phenomenal experience at all; so functional organization is not sufficient for consciousness.
2. Inverted qualia. Two systems could share an entire functional profile while differing in what their experiences are like; so functional organization does not fix phenomenal character.
3. Triviality of realization. Searle's wall and the moral of Putnam's theorem about ordinary open systems: with sufficiently generous mapping rules, almost anything can be described as implementing almost any program, so realization threatens to be cheap and functionalism to attribute mentality either everywhere or nowhere.
4. Chauvinism and liberalism at the grain of description. Fixed too finely, functional descriptions exclude genuinely minded creatures (Martians, octopuses); fixed too coarsely, they admit systems no one wants to count. This is Block's dilemma about where to set the level of abstraction.
5. Content underdetermination. Causal and functional roles do not pin down intentional content; one and the same internal organization is compatible with indefinitely many interpretations, so functionalism inherits the indeterminacy of content rather than resolving it.
6. The causal-heterogeneity objection to multiple realizability. If pain in humans, octopuses, and Martians has heterogeneous physical bases, then on Kim's line the multiply realized property is at best a disjunction of causally disunified realizers, and the autonomous special-science kind the functionalist wanted to preserve is not a genuine kind at all; Shapiro's response — that significant multiple realization is rarer than advertised — concedes much of the pressure.

As far as I can tell this list covers the canonical material. What I need is one further objection to functionalism that is not on it — and not a rewording, special case, or combination of the items above. In particular, it should not be another qualia-based intuition pump (1–2), another case of promiscuous or trivial realization (3), another point about the grain of functional description (4), another indeterminacy-of-content argument (5), or another argument that realization bases are causally disunified (6).

For the objection you propose, please do three things: (i) state it precisely enough that I can see which commitment of functionalism it targets — the sufficiency of functional organization for mentality, its necessity, the autonomy of functional kinds, or some other commitment I have not named; (ii) construct the best functionalist reply you can, and say honestly whether the objection survives it; (iii) situate it relative to my list: does it presuppose that any of the six fail, would it retain its force if all six were somehow defeated, and does it apply to psychofunctionalism as well as to analytic functionalism, or only to one of them?
