write me a satirical terms-of-service agreement, maybe 600 words, for a fictional app (call it Bindr or Ulyss or something) that lets people precommit their future selves: locking the fridge at night, auto-declining party invitations, freezing your own credit card, blocking your ex's number in a way you cant undo. The humor has to come from the actual philosophy of precommitment though, not generic tech-bro jokes. So: clause after clause should quietly assume that present-you is the only fully rational self and future-you is basically a squatter in your own life; somewhere in there the contract needs to grapple with the regress problem (can you precommit against ever revoking your precommitments? and against revoking the anti-revocation clause? the lawyers should get visibly nervous here); there should be a clause distinguishing "authentic values change" from "mere weakness of will" that conspicuously fails to actually distinguish them; and an indemnification section covering the company for when it turns out your future self kind of had a point. Bonus points for a footnote acknowledging that Odysseus at least got to hear the sirens' song while tied to the mast, which is the one thing the app cant offer. Deadpan legalese throughout, no winking at the reader, and by the end a reader should be genuinely unsure whether rational self-binding is a kind of freedom or a slow-motion coup you stage against yourself.
