I've been reading on my own about the proposal to govern by prediction markets — the idea that we should vote on values but bet on beliefs, letting market prices rather than politicians decide which policies get adopted — and I keep finding the vision both compelling and faintly unhinged. I'd like a satire that sits inside that tension. Write the official welcome pamphlet, roughly 500-600 words, that a newly converted "market republic" hands to citizens at the border: how to trade shares in whether a proposed law will raise national welfare, what becomes of statutes the market turns against, how civil servants are now paid in positions rather than salaries, and how disputes about the market's own constitution are settled, naturally, by a market. Keep it deadpan and warmly bureaucratic, in the voice of a civil-service copywriter who believes every word. The humor must come from taking the mechanism completely seriously, not from inventing incompetence: the shrewd trader who buys into a policy's success before lobbying for it, the market asked to price whether prediction markets produce good governance, the ordinary citizen quietly hedging against his own country's future. A reader familiar with the standard objections should feel each one land without a single one ever being stated outright.
