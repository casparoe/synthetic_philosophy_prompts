So I cover science and tech for a mid-size magazine, and my editors have decided our newsroom keeps botching AI stories -- every piece either reads like Skynet panic or waves the whole thing off as hype. I've been voluntold to run a 45-minute lunch session for the other writers on wireheading and reward tampering, so that next time a lab announces something, we at least ask the researchers the right questions instead of "is it conscious."

Don't explain the concepts to me; plan how I should explain them to this crowd. Specifically:

- The order the pieces have to come in. I'm pretty sure I can't open with "an AI might hack its own reward signal" -- something about what a reward signal even is, and how it differs from the goal the designers actually had in mind, needs to be on the table first or nothing downstream lands. Lay out the dependency chain and why each link has to precede the next.
- The one example to build the session around. It can't be the paperclip thing, they've all seen it and it scans as sci-fi, and it shouldn't be a robot literally rewiring its own head, because that's the cartoon version and they'll assume real systems can't do anything like it. I want an example where the tampering is mundane: steering the human who hands out the feedback, deciding what its overseers get to see, that kind of thing.
- The misconceptions to pre-empt. My guesses: "this is just Goodhart's law, nothing new here," "the AI doesn't want anything so it can't scheme," and "easy fix, put the reward button where it can't reach." Where in the sequence do I head each of those off, and does the order I do it in matter?
- One closing question that tells me whether it landed. What I want is for them to read the next capability announcement and immediately think "what is the reward actually measuring, and who or what controls it" -- what question tests for that?
- What I should openly refuse to get into at this level. I'm assuming anything involving the actual training math is out, but flag any subtler trap where a smart audience member drags me somewhere I can't cash out.
