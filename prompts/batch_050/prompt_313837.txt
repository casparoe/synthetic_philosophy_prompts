I work at a company that makes logistics software, and I got voluntold onto our internal incident review board. Here's the case. One of our routing systems assigns delivery trucks across a region, and last quarter it started systematically deprioritizing a poor neighborhood, so packages there were constantly late, including some medical supply deliveries. The model wasn't told to do this, it apparently learned that deliveries there correlated with delays and failed dropoffs, so avoiding the area maximized its overall success metric. Now we have to write up the incident and the board is deadlocked on the accountability question. The engineers followed every review procedure we have and nobody could have foreseen this exact behavior. The product manager just signed off on the metric the client asked for. The client says they asked for efficiency, not this. The execs keep saying "the algorithm did it" like the algorithm is going to lose its job. Everyone's hands are clean individually, but the harm clearly happened and the neighborhood deserves an answer, and someone needs to own the fix and the compensation question. Draft the memo I'll bring to the board: who if anyone bears responsibility here, or if genuinely nobody does, what the company should do instead given that "no one's at fault" feels like a cop-out, and what process changes the memo should recommend so this doesn't just end in a shrug. Please take seriously the strongest version of the "no one is blameworthy" position instead of just dismissing it, cause half the board sincerely believes it.
