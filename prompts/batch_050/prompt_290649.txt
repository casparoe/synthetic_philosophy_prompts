So my roommate and I have been arguing about that whole torture-versus-dust-specks thing for like a month now, and I started writing down her actual verdicts to try to catch her in an inconsistency. Here's what she's committed to so far. One: fifty years of torture for one person is worse than a dust speck in the eye of every person who will ever exist, full stop, no number changes it. Two: one person getting a moderately bad migraine for a full day is worse than a trillion people each getting a mild ten-second headache, because the headaches "don't add up to anything." Three: one person having their leg amputated is worse than a billion people each losing a single fingernail. Four: one person dying young is worse than ten million people each dying one week earlier than they otherwise would have. Five -- and this is the one that made me pause -- a million people each suffering one year of chronic back pain is worse than one person suffering forty years of the same pain. Six: she also says that if you could cure either one person's blindness or enough people's nearsightedness, you should cure the blindness no matter how many nearsighted people there are.

Now I've written all these down and I genuinely can't figure out what rule she's running. Sometimes it looks like mild harms just never aggregate against severe ones, but then verdict five aggregates identical harms by total, and verdict two says a trillion mild headaches lose to one bad migraine while verdict four lets one death beat ten million small shortenings of life. So maybe it's about how big the gap in severity is, or about whether the small harm is even noticeable, or something else.

Next case I want to put to her: a treatment bottleneck where a charity can either prevent one person from getting cluster headaches for the rest of their life (which she agrees is a genuinely terrible condition, maybe the worst common one), or prevent three billion people from each getting one week of moderate but real, day-ruining stomach flu. Predict her verdict, tell me which principle she's implicitly been running that fits all six earlier verdicts, and tell me what *other* principles fit them just as well but give the opposite answer on this new case.
