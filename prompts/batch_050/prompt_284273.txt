I am a novelist at work on a book whose narrator gradually comes to suspect that his sense of being a solid, continuous 'I' is something his own mind constructs — a symbol rather than a thing. To keep this from becoming a stale metafictional twist, I want to ground it in Hofstadter's claim that the self is a self-referential symbol, a strange loop, rather than in familiar talk of unreliable narration. Give an example of a genuine conceptual issue this view faces, one specific enough that I could build a scene around my narrator stumbling into it. I would be especially interested in a difficulty about reference or causal efficacy: for instance, if the 'I'-symbol does not merely describe the system's behaviour but partly causes it, in what sense can it be said to represent anything at all, and what exactly does it refer to when it refers to itself? Explain the issue clearly for a non-specialist, and note briefly why the obvious responses do not simply dissolve it. Please write in British English.
