At an AI research seminar, a colleague dismissed the claim that a language model trained purely on text could understand anything by saying, "syntax is not sufficient for semantics." Later in the same meeting she redeployed the slogan against a different proposal: giving such a model cameras and microphones would be mere bolt-on grounding, she said, since the perceptual inputs would just be more symbols for the same old syntax. It struck me that the slogan cannot be doing the same work in both moves, since the first use treats sensorimotor connection as irrelevant to understanding while the second implicitly treats it as the thing that matters. What are the distinct, precise theses this slogan could be expressing — readings about formal manipulation as such, readings about the need for the right causal or informational links to the world, readings about functional organization, readings about what it is like to be the system — and for each reading: is it trivial, substantive and defensible, or actually false, and would it in fact support either of my colleague's two uses? Please present the readings as a menu with verdicts rather than arguing for one correct interpretation, and note where the slogan's plausibility in these debates trades on sliding between two readings mid-argument.
