So I work at an AI lab, and something about our day-to-day practice has been bugging me. When we train a model with RL we hand out rewards and penalties by the million: +1 here, -1 there, big negative rewards slapped on outputs we dont want, whole training runs where an agent gets "punished" millions of times in simulation. And we talk about it in a funny double register: on the one hand reward is just a scalar, a control knob, clipping it or reshaping it is pure engineering and nobody would dream of calling a training run cruel. On the other hand we constantly say the agent "wants" the reward, "figures out" how to hack it, "learns to avoid" the penalty states -- the intentional vocabulary comes out whenever we explain behavior, even though we'd deny anything is felt. Meanwhile, half the people doing this would say a dog trainer who relies on shock collars instead of treats is doing something worse, precisely because they think the reinforcers are felt by the dog. And some of the same people sign open letters about how future AI systems might deserve moral consideration.

What does this whole practice actually presuppose about what a reward signal is? Does training-as-we-do-it only make sense if reward is a mere steering signal with no valence attached, or could you run the exact same pipeline while believing negative reward is genuinely bad for the agent -- would anything change besides our conscience? Reconstruct the implicit theory of valence underneath the practice, and be careful to separate what it strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with (I suspect a behaviorist "reward = whatever reinforces" reading licenses all of it, but does the practice actually need that, or just get it for free?). Then: is the double standard with animal training a real inconsistency or can it be principled? And if we took seriously the possibility that something in the loop -- reward prediction error, large negative rewards, whatever -- has felt valence, what concretely would have to change about how we train, and what could stay exactly as it is?
