Me and a friend have had this dumb running argument for almost two years about why the western Roman empire fell, and we've basicly agreed to settle it by polling a neutral third party, so congrats, that's you.

Setup: take "the fall" to mean that by 476 the west had no emperor and the imperial administration was gone, while the east kept chugging along for another thousand years. You get 100 points to distribute across candidate explanations, where points = causal weight. Here's my list of candidates, feel free to add your own if I've missed something big:

- straight-up military pressure from barbarian migrations (Goths, Vandals, the whole Hun domino effect)
- internal political dysfunction: civil wars, usurpers, the army making and unmaking emperors every few years
- economic decline: debased coinage, shrinking tax base, latifundia, all that
- climate and disease: the end of the Roman warm period, the Antonine and Cyprian plagues
- cultural and religious change, i.e. the Gibbon move where Christianity makes everyone stop caring about the empire
- lead poisoning (yes my friend unironically believes this one, give it points at your own risk)
- "there was no fall": most of what changed was gradual transformation, and 476 is just a date we retroactively made load-bearing

Two rules for your allocation. First, it has to pass what I'm calling the Eastern control test: any factor you load up on has to also explain why the east — which had Christianity, plagues, climate shifts, and plenty of its own barbarian headaches — did NOT fall. If a factor was just as present in Constantinople as in Ravenna, you owe me an account of the asymmetry or you have to move those points somewhere else. Second, no dodging the last category: putting points there means you think part of "the fall" is an artifact of periodization, and then you need to explain why that's a real answer and not an academic cop-out, because my friend will absolutely call it a cop-out.

So give me: the allocation with a sentence or two of justification per item, and then a paragraph on which single factor you'd defend if you were forced to pick just one.
