So I'm drafting a feature on how we explain complexity -- everything from the origin of life to whatever large language models are doing -- and I keep running into Dennett's cranes vs. skyhooks framing. Cranes: bottom-up, algorithmic, no miracles required, just lots of dumb local steps ratcheting up design. Skyhooks: mind-first, top-down, design-from-nowhere explanations that do the work by fiat. And then greedy reductionism is the failure mode where you get so excited about cranes that you skip levels and 'explain' things badly, like jumping straight from neurons to ethics.

Here's my question, and I want an actual number, not a shrug. What's your credence in this proposition: every apparent case of 'design' in nature and mind -- including the origin of life, complex adaptations, human cognition and language, and the impressive-seeming behavior of current AI systems -- will ultimately be fully explained by cranes, with no skyhook required at any point? My own gut is around 0.75; an editor I argued with about this put it at like 0.3, and I genuinely can't tell where our disagreement lives.

So decompose it for me. Assign rough probabilities to things like: the origin of life having a purely crane-based explanation, complex adaptation needing nothing beyond selection-and-friends, human cognition and language being explicable without any mind-first residue, current LLM abilities being 'just' cranes rather than evidence that some extra principle is doing work, and consciosness being the place where the crane story finally breaks (if it does). One line of justification per term, then show me how you combine them into your headline number. Then tell me which term your final answer is most sensitive to, what evidence would move it most, and which of those sub-questions me and my editor at 0.75 and 0.3 most likely disagree about. Oh, and if you think the proposition itself is vague in a load-bearing way, say where, but still give me a number under the most sensible reading.
