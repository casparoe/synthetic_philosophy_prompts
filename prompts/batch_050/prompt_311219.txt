ok so i got obsessed with thomsons lamp recently — the thought experiment where a lamp gets switched on after 1 minute, off after 30 more seconds, on after 15, and so on forever, so infinitely many flips happen inside 2 minutes, and then youre asked: at the 2 minute mark is it on or off? the thing that got me is that theres no last flip, so the usual "just check what happened last" move doesnt work, and apparently the whole infinite sequence doesnt even settle the final state.

write me a poem about this where the FORM does the same thing the lamp does. my idea is stanzas that keep halving in length — say 8 lines, then 4, then 2, then a single final line — so the reader physically feels the speeding up as they go down the page. and the last line has to land the on/off question somehow, either answering it or showing why answering is impossible, without just shrugging. bonus if the on/off alternation shows up somehow in the earlier stanzas too (line endings, rhymes, whatever). someone whos never heard of the lamp should still get a good poem out of it, but someone who has should feel like the poem understood the actual problem.
