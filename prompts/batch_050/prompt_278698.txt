I'm a PhD student in cog sci (philosophy on the side) and for our consciousness reading group everyone has to hand in a one-page "commitment inventory" that the others then get to attack. I want to stress-test mine before I submit it, so: does this set actually hang together?

(1) I'm an illusionist about phenomenal consciousness. There are no phenomenal properties; brains just misrepresent their own states to themselves as having qualitative character, and that's the whole story behind our sense that there's something it's like to be us. (2) I think of the illusion as working basically like a perceptual illusion: introspection presents our inner states the way vision presents the Müller-Lyer lines as different lengths — things seem a certain way to us that isn't how they are, and once you know it's an illusion you stop being fooled theoretically even if the seeming persists. (3) The only genuine explanandum here is the meta-problem, i.e. why creatures like us judge and say that we're phenomenally conscious, and once cognitive science explains that there's no leftover question about consciousness at all. (4) I'm not bothered by zombie conceivability arguments, and my go-to response is the phenomenal concept strategy: conceivability intuitions misfire because phenomenal concepts are a special kind of concept, so the gap between conceiving and entailing is explained by the concepts rather than by any metaphysical gap. (5) On the ethics side, I think what makes pain bad — and the reason animal pain morally matters — is what it's like to be in pain. (6) I take introspection to be generally reliable about the rest of our mental lives (what we believe, intend, attend to); it just goes wrong in this one specific place.
