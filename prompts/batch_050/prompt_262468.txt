Consider the following four candidate accounts of what a musical work is:

(A) A musical work is an abstract, eternally existing structure of sounds that composers discover rather than create; performances are imperfect instances of this structure.
(B) A musical work is nothing over and above the class of its performances: to speak of the work is just to speak, collectively, of all its past and future renditions.
(C) A musical work is essentially a set of instructions or directives for performers, possibly encoded in a score; whatever can be produced by following those instructions counts as the work.
(D) A musical work is the recording or sonic artifact itself, as fixed by its makers in the studio; live renditions and covers are derivative responses to that artifact.

Now consider the following problem cases:

(1) An orchestra performs a symphony but the second oboe plays a wrong note in the third movement. Intuitively, it was still a performance of that symphony.
(2) A jazz standard circulates for decades with no authoritative score, existing through improvisations that share little more than a harmonic skeleton.
(3) A studio album is assembled by splicing hundreds of takes and digitally altering sounds; no human being ever played the piece start to finish, and no live event could reproduce it.
(4) A DJ speeds up and pitches up an entire song, releasing the result as a new track; listeners treat it as a distinct work, though every sound event derives from the original recording.
(5) A composer writes a score and dies before anyone performs or hears it; decades later it is premiered.

For each account (A)–(D), rate on a scale from 0 to 10 how well it handles each of the cases (1)–(5), where 10 means the account explains our intuitive verdict about the case naturally and 0 means it forces a deeply counterintuitive conclusion. Present your ratings in a table, and briefly justify each rating in one or two sentences. Then aggregate your assessment: which single account achieves the best overall fit with the cases, and what is the most damaging objection that your winner must still answer?
