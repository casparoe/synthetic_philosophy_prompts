Construct a thought experiment in the ontology of musical works that satisfies three specifications at once. First, it must force a structuralist about musical works — one who holds that a work is a pure sound-structure, discoverable rather than created, and hence one and the same regardless of who arrives at it — to disagree with an indicated-type theorist in the style of Levinson — one who holds that a work is a sound-structure-as-indicated-by-a-particular-composer-at-a-particular-time, so that two composers who arrive at identical sounds from different art-historical positions have produced two distinct works. The case should make the first say one work exists and the second say two. Second, the very same scenario must raise a separate classificatory question about a digital recording: is it a performance of the work, an instance of the work, or the work itself? Here a nominalist who identifies a work with the class of its performances should be able to agree with both rivals about every concrete, checkable fact in the story and still be forced into a different answer. Third, the scenario must be realistic: set it in contemporary studio practice, with no time travel, no supernatural coincidence beyond what independent composition actually involves, and nothing a working audio engineer would find fanciful.

Constraints: the scenario itself should run no longer than one paragraph; the two composers must never have communicated or heard each other's material; the audio files they produce must be stipulated to be bit-for-bit identical; and the story must include one specified feature of the recording — for example, a file that yields the intended sounds only when rendered by one particular piece of software — that isolates the question of whether a work's identity resides in its sounds or in the instructions for producing them.

After presenting the scenario, do the following. (i) State the verdict of each of the three positions on each of the two questions, and show that each verdict follows from the position rather than being stipulated into the case. (ii) Identify which single feature of the scenario does the work in separating the structuralist from the indicated-type theorist, and describe what becomes of the case if that feature is removed — does it collapse into a trivial agreement, and if so, on which side? (iii) Do the same for the recording question: which feature forces the nominalist to classify the recording differently, and what happens without it? (iv) Finally, say how a defender of whichever position fares worst would attack the case, and judge whether that attack exposes a genuine ambiguity in the scenario or merely restates the attacked position.
