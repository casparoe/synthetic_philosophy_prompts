Each year our university philosophy society produces a short satirical magazine for the end-of-term dinner, and this year I have volunteered to contribute something on the philosophy of mind. The target I have in mind is the concept of qualia: the supposed properties of conscious experience said to be ineffable, intrinsic, private, and directly apprehensible to the subject. Some philosophers treat such properties as the last redoubt against a purely physical account of the mind; others regard the whole posit as an incoherent muddle that deserves to be, as it were, quined. I want a piece that will land with both camps.

What I'd like you to write is a product recall notice, issued by the fictional manufacturer of phenomenal consciousness (invent a suitably bland corporate name), recalling all units of qualia currently in circulation, followed by a brief FAQ for affected customers. The humour should come from the fact that the very features said to define the product make the recall administratively impossible: customer services cannot process complaints that are ineffable, technicians cannot inspect units that are private, quality control cannot independently verify faults known only by direct apprehension, and the returns department cannot confirm whether the item sent back is the same item sent out. Include the usual corporate furniture -- affected batch numbers, an unusable returns procedure, a helpline that cannot, in principle, help. I would also like a section dealing with the awkward case of two long-serving quality-assurance tasters, one of whom reports that the product's flavour has gradually changed over the years while the other insists it is his own standards that have drifted; the company's official position should be that there exists no procedure, even in principle, for settling which of them is correct. A passing nod to a customer who studied the complete technical manual for the colour red and then claimed, upon first opening the box, to have learnt something new would be welcome, as would mention of a production batch suspected of having shipped with no phenomenal component whatsoever, despite every unit passing all functional and behavioural tests.

The piece should run to roughly 600-700 words, in flawlessly deadpan corporate boilerplate, legible to a general reader but calculated to make a philosopher of mind laugh rather than wince. No real people should be named, and the piece must never step outside the fiction to explain the joke. A title would be appreciated; extra credit if it puns on discontinuing, or indeed quining, the product line.
