Goodhart's law and specification gaming (or reward hacking) are often treated as two labels for the same phenomenon: a proxy gets optimized until it diverges from the goal it was meant to track. One way of resisting this conflation is to claim the two concepts have different logical shapes: Goodhart failures are at bottom epistemic, arising whenever a measure is used as a target whether or not anything is deliberately exploiting it, whereas specification gaming is essentially agential, requiring a system that finds and exploits the gap between the specified objective and the intended one. Compare the two concepts with this proposed distinction in view. Is the epistemic/agential split the right carving, or does it mislocate the difference? Construct the clearest case you can of a Goodhart failure involving no agent and no exploitation, and the clearest case of specification gaming that is not also a Goodhart failure -- or argue that no such second case exists. Finally, which of the two concepts, if either, is more fundamental for analyzing AI systems that are heavily optimized but not agentic in the relevant sense?
