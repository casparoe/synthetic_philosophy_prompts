So I'm putting together a handout on the doxastic vs. non-doxastic debate about delusions -- i.e. whether delusions (like Capgras, Cotard, etc.) are genuinely beliefs or should be classed as something else (imaginings, acceptances, attitudes of some sui generis kind).

Here are the anti-doxastic arguments I already have, which you don't need to rehash:

(1) Failure of integration: delusions sit in a kind of sealed compartment and don't inferentially connect with the patient's other beliefs the way real beliefs do -- the patient doesn't draw the obvious conclusions.

(2) Failure to guide action (the double bookkeeping point): patients often don't act on their delusions in the ways you'd expect if they really believed them -- someone who believes the nurses are conspiring to poison them still eats the hospital food, or at least doesn't flee.

(3) Evidence-responsiveness: beliefs are supposed to be given up in the face of decisive counterevidence, and delusions paradigmatically aren't, so they fail a core functional criterion of belief. (I know doxasticists reply that ordinary beliefs are plenty irrational too, so don't just restate that dialectic either.)

(4) The content/incoherence worry: some delusional utterances are so bizarre or internally unstable that it's unclear there's a determinate proposition the patient could be said to believe at all.

What I want is one more argument for the conclusion that delusions aren't beliefs that is genuinely different from all four of these -- not a rewording, not a special case (e.g. "but patients really REALLY don't act on them" is just (2) louder), and not just a conjunction of two of them. It can draw on the functional profile of belief, on phenomenology, on the two-factor story about how delusions arise, or on something else entirely, as long as it lands as an argument for the anti-doxastic conclusion specifically.

Also, be honest with me afterwards: is your argument actually good, or does it collapse into one of the four above under pressure? If it collapses, tell me which one it collapses into. I'd rather have an honest "this is secretly (2) again" than a fake novelty on my handout.
