So I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training, and I ended up down a rabbit hole after a pub argument about whether a coworker who missed an on-call page "couldn't help it" because of burnout. Someone brought up Strawson and this idea of reactive attitudes -- resentment, gratitude, indignation, guilt -- and the claim that our practices of holding people responsible are grounded in these feelings rather than in some metaphysical fact about free will. I read a summary of his 1962 paper (the "Freedom and Resentment" one) and honestly it clicked with me in a way philosophy usually doesn't, because it sounds a bit like how we treat error signals in a distributed system: you don't ask whether the node was "metaphysically free" to drop the packet, you look at whether it was malfunctioning, overloaded, or just doing what the network forced it to do, and your response changes accordingly.

Anyway, here's what I actually want. Act as a professional philosopher who specialises in moral responsibility, and think carefully before answering. I keep seeing people argue past each other about responsibility -- some saying determinism kills blame, some saying Strawson showed it doesn't, some bringing up psychopaths or kids or AI agents -- and I'd like the full spec before the scorecard. Write me the list of desiderata a theory of moral responsibility must meet to count as adequate: the cases it has to handle (excuses vs exemptions, psychopaths, children, coercion, moral luck, maybe collective agents like companies), the distinctions it can't collapse (I gather blaming someone and holding them responsible aren't the same thing, and Strawson's own objective vs participant stance distinction matters here), the commitments it must either make or refuse to make about determinism, and whether it has to *justify* our reactive attitudes or just explain them. For each desideratum give the case or consideration that motivates it -- i.e. what goes wrong for a theory that drops it. And flag which requirements you think are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are just the prejudices of one camp (compatibilists, sceptics, Strawsonians, whoever).

And tell me which single requirement knocks out the most existing positions. Brief scoring of the main contenders against the list is fine at the end but keep it short, the criteria are the deliverable. Please answer in British English.
