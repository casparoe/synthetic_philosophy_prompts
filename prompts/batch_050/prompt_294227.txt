For a graduate seminar self-assessment on the Liar paradox, write a decision tree that takes someone from a single starting commitment -- the strengthened Liar, 'This sentence is not true,' is a meaningful declarative sentence of our language and demands a verdict -- to a named position on truth and the paradox. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a roster of names: first identify the questions on which the positions genuinely come apart (whether every meaningful sentence must be either true or false; whether a language's truth predicate can be applied to it only from a richer metalanguage; whether truths must be grounded in non-semantic facts; whether a contradiction can be true; whether the paradox shows the unrestricted T-schema must be restricted), then locate the actual positions within the combinations of answers those questions allow. The leaves must include at least the Tarskian hierarchy, Kripke's strong-Kleene fixed-point construction, the revision theory of truth, dialetheism, and a contextualist or indexical treatment; add further leaves only if they earn their own branch. Phrase every node as a question someone could answer on the merits, using a concrete case where it helps, without knowing which theory it sorts them toward. For any combination of answers that no position occupies, note it and diagnose briefly whether the slot is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate. No more than ten branch nodes.
