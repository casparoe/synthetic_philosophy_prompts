I serve on the investment board of a sovereign infrastructure fund. Two members have proposed a futarchy-style mechanism for the board's most contested decisions: whenever the board deadlocks between two major projects, the fund would open two prediction markets, each paying out according to the fund's audited ten-year return conditional on one project being adopted. Whichever contract trades higher after six weeks wins; the loser's contracts are voided and refunded, so traders bet only on the conditional comparison, never on which project the board will actually pick. Proponents say this aggregates information the board's deliberation misses and disciplines wishful thinking; opponents say it outsources judgment about public infrastructure to whoever has capital to trade. Draft the board's decision memo — adopt, reject, or adopt in modified form. It should give a clear, actionable recommendation; address the strongest objections on their merits (thin-market manipulation, the incentive effects of voiding the losing contract, the problem that infrastructure payoffs arrive decades after traders can profit from being right, and the legitimacy of settling public decisions by market price); and include an honest statement of the strongest consideration favoring the side the memo recommends against.
