Two people from my philosophy reading group had a row after we did the Allais paradox, and its still going in the group chat. (1) Mia said the standard fix is fine: people prefer the certain million because of diminishing marginal utility of money, so ordinary expected utility already handles risk aversion and there's nothing left to explain. (2) Tom said that can't work, because the same people take the riskier gamble in the second pair of choices, and no utility function over money -- concave or otherwise -- can deliver both preferences at once, which is the whole point of the paradox; he defends Buchak-style risk-weighted expected utility, where a risk function reweights worse outcomes and utility over money can stay linear. (3) Mia replied that REU only escapes the paradox by dropping independence, and that this costs more than its worth: an REU agent will endorse a plan tonight whose later stages she'll judge worse when she reaches them, so a clever bookie could walk her through a sequence of trades and reliably extract money. Dynamic inconsistency, she said. (4) Tom said she's not inconsistent, she's resolute: the right unit of evaluation is the whole plan judged from the start, and if she sticks to the plan the bookie can't guarantee a profit -- the 'exploitation' only goes through if you force her to re-evaluate each trade on its own at the later time, which is exactly what's in dispute. (5) Mia's last word was that 'resolute' is just a fancy name for knowingly doing what your future self rates as worse, and a theory you need willpower to follow through on has a bug, not a feature.

I've stayed out of it so far. Can you adjudicate?
