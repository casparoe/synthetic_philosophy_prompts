I am a novelist applying for a six-month independent fellowship, and I need a one-page research proposal rather than an essay. The project concerns Scheffler’s afterlife conjecture: why human extinction seems worse than the deaths it involves, even to people who will not be alive to miss anyone. I want to test a distinction standard discussions blur: extinction may be bad because it ends future persons, because it strands the dead without successors, or because it closes what I call the horizon of uptake, the practical possibility that unfinished meanings, works, institutions, and promises could still be received, corrected, or betrayed by someone later. To separate these, the proposal should build three thought cases: painless biological extinction after a fulfilled century; survival of comfortable but permanently sterile final custodians; and survival with all human records sealed so no future interpreter can make sense of us. State the question precisely, say why familiar answers do not settle which loss is doing the moral work, describe a philosophical method suited to a novelist rather than a population ethicist, give a realistic month-by-month plan, and specify what would count as illumination versus failure. Do not include a bibliography or citations; I will supply those.
