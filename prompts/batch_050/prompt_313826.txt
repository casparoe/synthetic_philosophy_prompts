I recently began using a language model’s written chain of thought as part of the audit trail for a decision-support system, and I have settled on a strongly skeptical position: a rationale is evidence about why the model reached its answer only if independent causal interventions show that the rationale, or the computation it reports, helped produce that answer. Coherence, consistency across rephrasings, agreement with the final output, and the model’s own confidence do not count, since all are compatible with post hoc rationalization.

What would count against this position? Identify observations, experiments, or conceptual arguments that would show that some unverified self-reports can still carry evidential weight, ranked from decisive to merely awkward. Distinguish faithfulness from causal relevance, completeness, and usefulness to an auditor, considering hidden scratchpads, compressed or encoded reasoning, and reports that reliably predict how perturbing the model changes its answer. Also assess whether my causal-validation requirement makes skepticism unfalsifiable. Please separate what is established about current language-model self-reports from what remains genuinely contested, without presenting either side as consensus.
