So I retired from thirty-odd years of condensed matter physics and have been binging philosophy of mind, mostly Hofstadter. Our reading group wants me to present my "position" next month, so here's what I actually believe — tell me if it hangs together:

1. Causal closure at the bottom: every microphysical event that has a cause has a fully sufficient microphysical cause. Nothing extra gets injected from above.
2. Downward causation is real anyway. My decision to raise my arm genuinely causes neurons to fire and muscles to contract; the symbol level pushes the substrate around.
3. In the careenium, though, the simms do no work: the simmballs' trajectories are wholly fixed by the forces in the table, and the symbol level there is a description, not an additional player.
4. Symbol-level explanations aren't mere shorthand. Explaining why the computer printed 42 purely in terms of voltages misses the actual reason, which lives at the level of the algorithm.
5. The mental supervenes on the physical: two systems identical at the substrate level can't differ at the symbol level.

Which pairs here genuinely conflict, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each give me the cheapest revision and what it costs me. Don't just flag everything — I suspect a couple of these only look contradictory because I'm being sloppyly with words like "cause" and "description".
