I teach philosophy of history, and every year my seminar on historical explanation founders on the same counterfactual. I would like to settle, or at least structure, my own thinking about it, and I want a number rather than a survey of positions.

Here is the proposition, stated precisely enough to have a truth value in the nearest relevant possible worlds: had Adolf Hitler been killed during the failed Munich putsch of November 1923, then by 1945 some German government would nonetheless have (i) initiated a major European war aimed at overturning the post-1918 settlement, and (ii) carried out the systematic murder of a substantial fraction of Europe's Jewish population. Give me your credence in the conjunction, and in each conjunct separately. My own figures are roughly 0.7 for (i), 0.15 for (ii), and I want to know whether yours differ and where.

More important than the numbers is the decomposition. Please assign rough probabilities to the sub-questions on which the verdict depends, state how they combine, and identify which one your final number is most sensitive to. The sub-questions I take to be load-bearing include: whether revision of Versailles was a consensus aim across the German political spectrum such that almost any plausible successor regime would pursue it by force if necessary; whether the structural conditions of the early 1930s — economic collapse, institutional fragility, the appeal of authoritarian solutions — made some form of radical-right government near-inevitable, or left room for a conventional military or conservative-authoritarian regime of the Papen-Schleicher sort; whether eliminationist antisemitism was latent enough in German political culture that genocide required only the opportunity, or whether it required a specific agent with Hitler's ideological fixation, personal authority, and willingness to gamble; and whether Hitler's distinctive contribution was the existence of the war at all or rather its timing and character — say, a war begun in 1939 rather than later, a two-front war against the Soviet Union, a war that became genocidal in the course of being waged.

Two further refinements. First, flag any place where the decomposition smuggles in a contested methodological commitment — for instance, whether the very question 'what would have happened' has determinate answers at this temporal distance, or whether the contingency is irreducible and my request for a number is itself confused. Second, tell me what evidence or argument would move your number most: a discovery about the deliberations of the non-Nazi German right, comparative evidence from other interwar authoritarian regimes, general considerations about the causal efficacy of individuals versus structures, or something else. I am not asking you to adjudicate the historiography; I am asking you to show me where the argument's weight actually rests.
