I'm a novelist, mid-way through a book about two sisters raised in the same family -- one who cannot stop punishing herself for things she did years ago, and one who only ever seems to feel bad when she gets caught. My writing group had a big row about which of them is the healthier character, and the row kept going in circles because everyone was using "guilt" and "shame" to mean slightly different things and defending slightly different positions about which emotion (if either) is actually any good. So here's what I'd like: a proper sorting quiz I can hand to my writing group (and, honestly, to beta readers) that sorts people by their actual views on guilt versus shame as moral emotions, without ever asking them which one they prefer directly.

Here's the shape I have in mind. Eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete situation a character or reader might face -- someone who betrayed a friend and was never found out, someone publicly exposed for something genuinely minor, a community deciding how to treat a member who broke its rules, that sort of thing -- with two or three answer options each, phrased in plain language so nobody needs to have read any moral psychology to answer. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto named positions. The leaves I definitely want included: the view that guilt is the good emotion because it's about acts and repair whilst shame just attacks the whole self; the contrary view that shame does indispensable work holding communities together and the modern West has pathologised something healthy; a division-of-labour view where both emotions have their proper job; and a pox-on-both-houses view that we should aim to respond to wrongdoing without self-torment at all. On top of that, I'd like a second, seperate axis in the key for whether people think a culture can actually choose which emotion to cultivate in its children, or whether we're stuck with whatever our upbringing installed.

A few more asks. The key should note which positions the quiz can't reliably tell apart (if any) rather than pretending the instrument is sharper than it is. If some combination of answers doesn't correspond to any position anyone actually holds, say so and take a guess at why -- is it incoherent, just unattractive, or weirdly unexplored? And no question should cheat by asking something like "do you think shame is toxic?" -- I want to sort my friends by what they say about the cases, since half of them would deny being shame-people whilst clearly being shame-people.
