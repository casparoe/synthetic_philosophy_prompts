I am a teaching assistant on an undergraduate philosophy of logic module, marking midterm scripts, and I would like a second opinion on one student's answer before I return it. The question and the answer are reproduced in full below.

The question (worth 25 marks): "Both Curry's paradox and Yablo's paradox are said to show something that the standard liar paradox does not. (a) State Curry's sentence precisely and derive the paradox, identifying every logical principle the derivation uses. (b) State Yablo's sequence precisely and derive the paradox. (c) Explain what each paradox demonstrates beyond the liar, and assess whether Yablo's paradox genuinely involves no self-reference."

The student's answer: "(a) Let C be the sentence 'If C is true, then the moon is made of green cheese', produced by diagonalisation together with the truth schema. Suppose C is true. By the truth schema, what C says is the case, so if C is true then the moon is made of green cheese; by modus ponens, the moon is made of green cheese. Discharging the supposition, if C is true then the moon is made of green cheese — but that is precisely what C says, so by the truth schema C is true, and modus ponens again delivers that the moon is made of green cheese. The derivation uses only the truth schema, conditional proof and modus ponens; unlike the liar, it never appeals to negation or to reductio. Since the choice of consequent was arbitrary, any sentence whatsoever can be proved in this way. (b) Consider the infinite sequence S_1, S_2, S_3, ..., where each S_n says: for every k greater than n, S_k is not true. Suppose S_1 is true. Then S_2 is not true, so some sentence later than S_2 is true; yet S_1's truth also says that sentence is not true — contradiction. Suppose instead that S_1 is not true. Then some S_k with k greater than 1 is true, and the first argument replayed on S_k yields a contradiction again. Either way we reach a contradiction. (c) The liar shows that unrestricted truth principles plus classical logic yield inconsistency. Curry's paradox yields a contradiction as well, and because it bypasses negation it can be contained by paraconsistent logics that tolerate contradictions; its real lesson is therefore that the conditional, not negation, is the dangerous principle. Yablo's paradox shows that semantic paradox requires no self-reference at all: no S_n refers to itself, so circularity cannot be the source of paradox, and any account that blames self-reference, such as Kripke's, stands refuted."
