Here's the pattern I keep noticing. A friend had major surgery and was on morphine afterwards, and she said the strangest thing: the pain was still there, she could point at it and describe it, but it simply didn't bother her. Same sensation, suffering gone. I filed that away as a weird drug effect. But then milder versions started showing up everywhere. My brother-in-law does these brutal workouts and describes the muscle burn as satisfying — he'd honestly be annoyed if it stopped. A massage therapist told me clients ask for pressure that "hurts good." My grandmother, after her stroke, apparently still reacted to pinpricks and said yes when asked if it hurt, but seemed oddly indifferent to it — the doctors had a specific word for this which I've forgotten. And there's the everyday version: identical soreness that is miserable when it's unexplained but almost welcome when you know exactly where it came from.

So the phenomenon, roughly: the sensory part of pain seems to come apart from the bad part. Sometimes the badness is deleted while the sensation stays (morphine, my grandmother), sometimes the sign seems to flip entirely into something wanted (the gym, the massage), and sometimes mere context or knowledge seems to do the work.

Three questions:

1. Is there an established name for the general dissociation — the gap between feeling pain and pain being bad — or only names for particular instances of it? If the general pattern genuinely has no name, coin one, give it a real definition, and mark its boundary cases.
2. How does this differ from nearby things it's easily confused with: high pain tolerance, distraction, the sensation genuinely being weaker, or just stoicism about the suffering?
3. If the two components really do come apart, which one deserves to be called "the pain"? Does my friend on morphine still have pain, or only a pain-like sensation? And does it matter for how we should think about why pain is bad?

Also: don't just hand me terms. For each of my examples, tell me whether it actually falls under the concept or whether it's a different phenomenon that only looks similar from the outside.
