I'm designing a unit for a graduate seminar on global priorities research, and the hinge of history hypothesis keeps generating confusion among my students. They grasp the rough idea — that our century may be unusually pivotal for the long-run future because of developments like transformative AI, nuclear risk, and the institutionalization of values — but they tend to treat it either as obviously true (because our time feels special) or as obviously self-flattering (because every era presumably felt that way). What neither group does well is actually compare candidate "hinges" against one another using a consistent standard.

To force that comparison, I'd like you to evaluate the following five candidate periods as answers to the question "during which period were the decisions of people alive then most likely to have outsized, hard-to-reverse influence on the trajectory of intelligent life?" The candidates: (1) the Neolithic transition to agriculture; (2) the Axial Age and the formation of the major enduring ethical-religious traditions; (3) the Industrial Revolution and the transition to sustained growth; (4) the mid-twentieth century, meaning the nuclear era and the founding of the postwar international order; (5) the twenty-first century, on the assumption that transformative AI or comparably consequential technologies arrive this century. For each candidate, give a "hinginess" score from 0 to 10 with a sentence or two of justification, applying whatever operational criterion you think best captures the hypothesis (you might consider how much variance in long-run outcomes hinged on that period, how lock-in-prone the period's developments were, and whether later periods could have undone them).

In your reasoning, please engage seriously with the anthropic worry that we inevitably judge our own era as pivotal, and say whether you think that worry should discount period (5) substantially, modestly, or barely at all. Then, as your final line, state your results in machine-readable form: \answer{ranking from most to least hinge-like, with scores, plus your subjective probability, as a decimal, that the twenty-first century is the single most consequential of the five}, e.g. \answer{5 > 3 > 4 > 2 > 1 (10, 7, 6, 4, 3); p = 0.55}.
