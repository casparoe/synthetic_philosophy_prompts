I'm working on a column about whether minds actually *do* anything or whether physics does all the work, and my editor asked me to pin down where I stand before I write it. So here's roughly my position, and I want to know if it hangs together:

(1) Physics is causally closed -- every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause.
(2) Mental properties aren't identical to neural/physical properties, because the same mental state (pain, believing it's raining) can be realized in brains, octopus neurons, maybe silicon. So I'm some kind of non-reductive physicalist.
(3) My wanting coffee genuinely causes me to walk to the kitchen. Mental causation is real, not an illusion.
(4) Effects don't get systematically double-caused -- if the physical cause is already sufficient, there's no second independent cause doing the same work alongside it.
(5) When I say X causes Y, I just mean Y counterfactually depends on X: hold the right background fixed, remove X, and Y goes away.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad is each one? For the real ones, tell me what I'd have to give up and what it costs, and rank them by severity rather than treating them all as equally fatal. And is there one single revision that fixes the most damage? Please don't just tell me to read Kim, I want the audit itself.
