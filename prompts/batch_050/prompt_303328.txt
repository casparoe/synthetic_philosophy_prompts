I recently acquired citizenship in my adopted country and, almost immediately, found myself appointed to a working group in my municipality that is fighting the cantonal government over who gets to set housing-density rules. At our first meeting, the council president ended the discussion by declaring that decisions should be taken as close to the citizen as possible, and everyone nodded as though the matter were settled. I nodded too, and then realized I had no idea what I had just agreed to. The phrase clearly has real force in these debates, but the more I think about it, the less I know what precise principle it expresses.

Could you lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct theses this slogan might be making? I suspect they include at least: a claim about geographic proximity to those affected; a claim that the lowest level of government *capable of handling the matter effectively* should handle it; a claim that authority should sit at whatever level internalizes the relevant costs and benefits; a claim about where democratic participation is most meaningful; a claim about enabling policy experimentation across jurisdictions; and a claim about letting people sort themselves into jurisdictions matching their preferences. For each thesis, please state it precisely (what variable is being minimized or matched, and according to what test), assess whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or actually false, and identify what kinds of evidence would bear on it.

Most important to me: say which of these readings, if any, would actually vindicate the council president's move in my case, where the disputed policy has effects that plainly spill over municipal borders (commuters, regional housing prices) but where local knowledge about neighborhoods is genuinely richer at the municipal level. If some readings of the slogan point toward municipal control while others point toward cantonal control, say so explicitly rather than splitting the difference, and note what further facts about our situation would settle which reading should govern. I would also like to know which reading the people who habitually invoke this slogan most plausibly have in mind, even if it is not the strongest one.

Please keep the whole answer under 800 words: a list of readings with verdicts, then a short application to my dispute. I am bringing this to our next working-group meeting, so precision matters more to me than comprehensiveness.
