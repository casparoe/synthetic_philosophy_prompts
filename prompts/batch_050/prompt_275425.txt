ok so I write novels for a living (two published, third one fighting me) and I keep running into the same knot about honesty, both in the book and in my actual life, and I want to know if the positions I've drifted into can coexist or if I'm being a hypocrit in a way my characters would catch before I do. Here's where I think I stand:

1. Fiction's whole job is psychological truth. The plot can be invented, but if I flatter my characters or make people nicer or clearer than they really are, I've failed at the one thing that matters.

2. White lies between friends are sometimes not just excusable but actually required. Last month a close friend sent me her manuscript and I told her it was "really nearly there" when I thought it was two drafts away, and I'd do it again, and I think NOT saying something like that would have been cruel.

3. I genuinely admire Dutch-style directness and I find the hedged, compliment-sandwich way Americans (and by reputation the Japanese, dialed up further) talk to each other to be a kind of low-grade dishonesty. People should say what they think.

4. The reason lying is wrong, when it's wrong, is that it withholds information the other person would want in order to make their own decisions. That's my whole account of it, roughly.

5. I wrote a glowing blurb for a friend's mediocre debut last year and felt fine about it, because blurbs are a convention and nobody reads them as sworn testimony.

6. My characters have to talk the way actual people talk -- indirection, hedging, polite dodges and all -- otherwise the dialogue sounds like a philosophy seminar instead of a kitchen.

Which of these are really pulling against each other and which only look like they are until you think about it? I'm especially unsure whether 2 and 4 can both survive, and whether 5 is a third case of whatever 2 is or a different animal entirely. Don't just flag every pair as equally problematic -- rank the tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. I have no attachment to any particular one of these, so don't go easy.

Format-wise: give me the analysis in normal prose first, then a table at the end with columns -- Pair, Genuine or Apparent, Severity (high/medium/low), What Would Have to Give, and Cost of Giving It Up. And please write in American English.
