I'm a second-year graduate student in cognitive science, and I have small fellowship funding contingent on a two-page proposal for a one-semester project. Please write the proposal for me.

Some background. I recently encountered the careenium thought experiment (Hofstadter's): a frictionless surface on which large numbers of tiny marbles collide with a smaller number of much larger marbles, such that at a coarse enough grain the big marbles' motions fall into stable configurations that can be read as symbols undergoing rule-governed transformations — a crude physics-level model of how symbol-level cognition might sit on top of neural hardware. The old question it poses is whether the symbol level does any causal work — whether symbols, so to speak, push the substrate around — or whether every event is already fully fixed by the micro-physics, so that symbol talk is only an observer's convenient shorthand. This has recently stopped being an abstraction for me: I spend my days doing interpretability work on trained networks, where colleagues routinely describe discovered features and circuits as the "real" explanation of a model's behavior, and I've realized I cannot say what would make that claim more than a lossy compression of the weights and activations.

My half-formed angle, which the proposal should develop: the standard debate runs together two distinct questions — first, whether a symbol-level description tracks a real, multiply realizable pattern with genuine predictive and explanatory value; second, whether symbol-level states cause micro-level events over and above what the micro-physics already settles. My suspicion is that the first can be answered yes and the second no without inconsistency, and that the careenium is the ideal test bed precisely because its symbol-level reading is imposed by an interpreter rather than intrinsic to the physics — which forces the two questions apart in a way brain cases don't.

The proposal should: state the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the obvious resources don't settle it as applied to an interpreter-relative symbol level (causal-exclusion arguments of the Kim variety, interventionist accounts of higher-level causation, and real-patterns views); lay out a method — mostly conceptual analysis, plus two worked reconstructions of the careenium, one using a natural symbol mapping and one using a deliberately gerrymandered one, to see which explanatory claims survive; say what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and be honest about what is achievable in one semester. No bibliography, please — I'll add citations myself. One more thing: where the argument leans on background commitments, mark explicitly which are settled among people who work on this (for instance, some form of causal closure of the physical) and which remain genuinely contested (for instance, whether interventionism dissolves the exclusion problem), and don't present a contested position as the consensus.
