my roommate did a philosophy of technology unit last semester and now she won't stop sorting inventions into "has politics" and "doesn't have politics." Her calls so far:

- the low overpasses on the Long Island parkways: political
- the mechanical tomato harvester: political, even though the engineers apparently just wanted to pick tomatoes cheaper
- nuclear power plants: political, because of the kind of centralized security apparatus you have to maintain around them basically forever
- mousetraps: not political
- QWERTY keyboards: NOT political, even though we're all stuck with a mediocre layout forever — this one genuinely shocked me
- the standard adjustable office chair: not political

So here's the case we argued about last night. A popular camera app applies a "beautification" filter by default: smooths skin, lightens skin tone slightly, narrows the jawline. You can turn it off — there's a toggle, takes ten seconds — but it's on from day one and almost nobody flips it, because people just assume that's what their photos look like now. Is she going to call this political?

I can see it both ways and it's driving me nuts. The effects are arguably bigger than the tomato harvester's — billions of faces nudged toward one beauty standard, and it works worst on darker-skinned users — but unlike the bridges there is literally an off switch. Which made me realize I don't actually know what rule she's running. Is it about what the design does to people? About whether you can route around it? About whether the effect is baked into the thing itself versus added by how people use it?

What I want from you: your best guess at her verdict on the camera app and why, at least two different rules that both fit all six of her earlier calls but split on this case, and one more technology (real or invented) where those two rules give opposite answers, so I can settle it at dinner. Bonus: she also refused to rule on a smart speaker that only responds when you phrase your request politely — predict that one too if your rules can reach it.
