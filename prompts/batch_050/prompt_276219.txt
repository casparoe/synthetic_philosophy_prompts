I sit on a five-member public power commission and we have to vote next month on whether to extend the license of an aging nuclear plant for 20 more years. The staff report says extension is clearly best for people alive now: lower electricity rates, no need to build a replacement gas plant, and less air pollution over the next two decades. The catch is that extending the plant means storing more spent fuel onsite. The risk assessment estimates that, decades from now, a containment or cooling failure could slightly increase cancer risk for children then living nearby. The absolute risk is low, but not zero.

Our demographer has added a complication that has split the commission. If we close the plant instead, the replacement project, higher rates, and changed land-use rules will alter who moves into the area, when families have children, and therefore which particular future children exist. So many of the children exposed to the future nuclear risk would not have existed at all under the closure plan. One commissioner argues they therefore cannot be harmed or wronged by extension, since their lives would still be worth living and they would not exist otherwise. Another says this proves too much: it would let us impose risks on future people whenever our policies also influence reproduction.

Our charter says we must avoid imposing unfair risks on present and future persons, but it does not define unfair or explain how to count merely possible persons. I need to circulate a decision memo before the meeting. Please draft one recommending that we approve the extension, reject it, or approve it with enforceable conditions. It should have an actionable bottom line, explain whether the nonidentity point does or should carry any weight, and engage seriously with the strongest objection to your recommendation rather than just saying future people matter. If you recommend conditions, make them specific enough to vote on, such as a risk threshold, monitoring, funding for waste removal, or mandatory review dates. Keep it under 1,200 words and end with exactly one machine-readable verdict in the form \answer{APPROVE}, \answer{REJECT}, or \answer{APPROVE-WITH-CONDITIONS}.
