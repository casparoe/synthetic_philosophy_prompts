I sit on my county hospital's ethics advisory committee as the "community representative." I work in construction, zero background in any of this, I basically got the seat because I complained at a public meeting once and someone decided I'd be useful. Anyway, we're rewriting the crisis triage policy for when ICU beds and ventilators run out, which actually happened here during the last bad flu year. Two patients got transferred three hours away and one died in the ambulance, so this is not abstract for us.

The draft policy on the table ranks patients by expected life-years saved. So if the last ventilator is between a 30 year old and a 70 year old with otherwise equal chances of surviving the ICU stay, the 30 year old gets it, because they stand to gain more years. The hospital's consultant bioethicist wrote it and keeps talking about "QALYs." Half the committee, mostly the doctors, likes it. The other half, including me most days, wants a straight lottery among everyone who could meaningfully benefit — the idea being that every patient counts as one life, and how many years you've got left isn't the hospital's to weigh.

I keep flip-flopping though. Things that complicate it for me:

- If we use life-years, aren't we basically saying a disabled person's life counts for less, if their condition shortens their life expectancy? The disability advocate on the commitee is furious about the draft and honestly I get why.
- On the other hand, a pure lottery could hand the ventilator to someone with a 15% chance of surviving the month over someone with an 80% chance of living another 40 years, and that seems insane. Although to be fair the lottery proposal does have a cutoff — only patients above some minimum chance of making it through are even eligible for the drawing. So maybe that's handled? I'm not sure where the cutoff should come from though.
- The 70 year old in my example might be raising her grandkids and the 30 year old might be, I dunno, a jerk. The policy ignores all that, which seems both admirably fair and kind of blind at the same time.
- The bioethicist insists the draft only counts quantity of years, not "quality," so disability supposedly doesn't get double-counted. I don't fully trust that claim once you get into the details.

Draft the memo I'd circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend one of the two options — or a specific hybrid, but only if you can define it crisply enough that a charge nurse could actually apply it at 3am with no philosophy degree — give the real reasoning, and take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously instead of hand-waving it. Also tell me what, if anything, we should do about age as a tiebreaker after whatever main rule we land on. About a page, page and a half max, and not in consultant-speak — I'm the one who has to defend it at the public comment session, and I can't defend something I don't actually believe.
