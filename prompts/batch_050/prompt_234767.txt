Political theory PhD student here (third year, dissertating on compromise in democratic theory, which is maybe why I'm noticing this everywhere). There's a pattern in my case material that I can't find in the literature I'm reading, and I want to know if it has a name before I embarrass myself coining one.

The pattern: two parties with genuinely opposed *moral* ends — not just conflicting interests — reach an arrangement where neither waters anything down. Instead of each accepting a diluted version of what they wanted, they re-divide control across seperate issues: each gives up ground on the thing they care less about so the other gives up ground on the thing they care more about, and afterwards *both* judge the world morally better than before, by their own lights, not just strategically acceptable. Instances: two donors where each thinks the other's favorite charity is not just ineffective but actively harmful, who agree to each redirect their giving so the other's money stops flowing to the bad cause; a hiring committee where the person who desperately wants the ethics hire and is horrified by the applied candidate, and the person with exactly the reverse preferences, quietly agree "you take this line, I take next year's" rather than fighting to a compromise candidate nobody wants.

Two things puzzle me. First, it seems to stop working — or stop being the same thing — when the disagreement is factual rather than evaluative: "I'll concede the bridge is safe if you concede the tunnel is cheap" feels like a category error, not a deal. Second, some cases feel grubby (the activist agreeing to stop campaigning against the farm if the farmer agrees not to expand) while others feel almost virtuous, and I can't state the variable.

So: does this have an established name, and how exactly does it differ from compromise as usually theorized, from logrolling, and from modus vivendi? I won't be satisfied with just a label — I want the carving: what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, where its edges are, and if nothing existing fits, coin a term and define it with boundary cases.

At the end, give me the comparison as a table with columns: candidate_concept | standard_meaning | where_my_phenomenon_fits | where_it_breaks | verdict (fit / partial / not_it). And if you coin something, add a JSON object with fields: coined_name, one_sentence_definition, necessary_conditions (array), boundary_cases (array, each with case + why it counts or doesn't).
