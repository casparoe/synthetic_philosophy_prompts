Give me your actual credence that money-pump arguments establish a genuine requirement of diachronic rationality — specifically, that an agent with cyclical preferences (A over B, B over C, C over A) who chooses rationally at each individual moment is nonetheless irrational tout court, because she can be turned into a pump. I want a number and its decomposition, not a survey of positions. Break the credence into the sub-questions it depends on and give a rough probability for each: (a) whether the pump's setup smuggles in assumptions the intransitive agent can rationally reject (that she must keep trading, that she can't foresee the sequence and adopt a plan, that her preferences stay fixed through it); (b) whether rational assessment can apply to sequences of choices at all, or only to time-slices, in which case "being pumped" is a prudential misfortune rather than a rational defect; (c) whether a sophisticated or resolute agent who is pumped anyway is irrational in virtue of her preferences or only in virtue of her policy. Then tell me how these combine into your final number, which sub-question it is most sensitive to, and what argument or case would move you most.
