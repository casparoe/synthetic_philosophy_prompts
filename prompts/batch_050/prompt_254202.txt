This might be a daft question from a sixth-former, but is there a proper name for this problem? We did hedonism in RS/philosophy and everyone keeps talking like “pleasure” is obviously one thing you can add up. But the examples seem wildly different. Greasy chips after football feel nice in your body. Finally getting a maths proof doesn’t really tingle anywhere, it’s more like a click or a win. Seeing your best mate after ages is warm and social. The end of a headache is good mostly because something bad stops. And laughing when your rival trips over is definately enjoyable in the moment but also a bit grubby afterwards.

What I keep noticing is that the bits people use to call something “pleasure” can come apart: there’s the glowy feeling, the wanting it to continue, the being glad later that it happened, and the “I’d choose it again” bit. A long run can hurt while you’d still choose it; a snog you regret can be great at the time and awful in memory; some calm things have no buzz at all but you wouldn’t swap them. So if hedonism says the good is pleasure, what makes all these count as one currency rather than lots of seperate things that we’ve lazily given the same label?

I don’t just want a one-word answer if there is one. I want to know the nearest named ideas, how this is different from “higher vs lower pleasures” or from just saying some pleasures are longer-lasting, and whether the best move is to split pleasure into kinds, define it by desire/choice instead of feeling, or admit the category is a bit of a fudge. If there isn’t an established term that fits exactly, say so and maybe coin one with clear boundary cases.
