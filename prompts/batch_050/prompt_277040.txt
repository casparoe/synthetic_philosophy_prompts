I host a small philosophy-of-science podcast, and as a retired organic chemist who has only recently got into philosophy, I keep loosing track of what Gödel’s theorems do and don’t establish. Next week’s guest defends the claim that human mathematical understanding cannot be wholly mechanical. Draft eight interview questions in British English that make her distinguish carefully between a formal system proving its Gödel sentence, our recognising it as true under further assumptions, and the different consistency or soundness requirements in the two theorems. For each question, include the answer she is most likely to give and a follow-up pressing one specific point: why adding the Gödel sentence as a new axiom merely moves the problem, whether a machine might revise its axioms or be inconsistent, how actual fallible humans differ from an ideal reasoner, what the second theorem adds, and what empirical evidence could count against her view.
