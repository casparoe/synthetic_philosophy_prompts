Two of my coworkers got into a real argument at lunch last week and both want me to settle it, but honestly I'm stuck. Background: our teammate Lena chewed out the new hire, Marcus, for padding his expense reports. Marcus fired back that Lena did the exact same thing a couple years ago (its basically an open secret) and got away with it.

Ana thinks Marcus has a point -- not that padding expenses is fine, but that Lena in particular has no business being the one to call him out. Her take: blaming someone is making a demand of them, and you can't coherently demand others follow a standard you exempt yourself from. Lena blaming Marcus is like a trespasser yelling at someone for cutting across the lawn. Luis says that's the ad hominem fallacy in a trench coat. Whether expense fraud is wrong doesn't depend on who's talking, and Marcus is just using Lena's past to dodge the actual question -- if a murderer's testimony convicts another murderer, the testimony doesn't stop being true because of who said it.

Ana came back with: the truth of the claim isn't the issue, the issue is who's issuing it. Marcus can fully admit he was wrong and still object that Lena isn't the right person to hold him to account -- and if Lena genuinely believed in the standard, she'd have reported herself first. Luis said fine, maybe her blaming is compromised, but then the fix is that Lena criticizes herself too, not that Marcus gets a pass; "you're a hypocrite" isn't a defense of expense fraud, it's changing the subject.

Then it got messier: apparently Lena confessed to her manager last year, paid the money back, and has been clean since. Ana said that changes nothing -- she only came clean once she was safe from consequences, so it's reputation management, not reform. Luis said if real change plus making amends doesn't restore your standing, then standing can never be regained at all, and surely morality doesn't work like a permanent no-ex-offenders policy.

Can you go through this round by round and say who's right about which specific point? And is there anything they're both missing?
