ok so I have to present my philosophical profile at a reading group next week and I want a consistency check first. My current package on antinatalism:

(a) I think Benatar's asymmetry is sound. Roughly: the presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure is good, but the absence of pain is good even if nobody's around to enjoy it, while the absence of pleasure is only bad if someone exists who's deprived of it. So coming into existence is always a harm compared to never existing, and having kids is wrong even if their lives would be happy.

(b) I'm a deprivationist about death. Death is bad when it cuts off future goods the person would have had, and the Epicurean line that death is nothing to us is just wrong.

(c) I buy the person-affecting intuition: an act can't be wrong unless it makes things worse for someone who exists or will exist. That's why the non-identity problem actually grips me instead of looking like a puzzle with some obvious impersonal fix.

(d) I'm glad my friends exist, I'm not miserable, and I think most actual lives are worth continuing.

(e) If a couple could somehow guarentee a child a life of total bliss plus exactly one stubbed toe, I think having that child would be permissible, maybe even good.

Which of these pull against each other and how badly? Which apparent conflicts evaporate once you state things carefully?
