Our hospital's clinical trials committee keeps having to rule on whether there's genuine equipoise for proposed RCTs, and our track record is getting confusing. Past calls: we approved a trial randomizing between two chemo regimens where oncology opinion was split roughly evenly; we rejected a placebo-controlled trial of a new asthma drug where a proven standard exists and flares carry real risk; we approved a placebo-controlled trial for mild seasonal allergies despite effective OTC options; we rejected a trial randomizing between an established antibiotic and a newer one where three large observational studies consistently favored the established one, even though clinicians said they had "no strong feelings"; and we approved a trial of two surgical techniques where the published evidence was thin and surgeons' preferences tracked their training rather than any data. Now we have a proposal to randomize between a standard heart failure drug and a newer one: the cardiology community is genuinely split and many say they'd be happy either way, but two meta-analyses of small trials point to the newer drug being modestly better, and switching patients later is easy and low-cost if the trial confirms it. What should we decide to stay consistent with our own precedents, which past case is the closest analog, and if our precedents underdetermine it, what principle (community disagreement vs. strength of available evidence vs. risk of being on the wrong arm) would we actually be committing to with each choice? End with a final line beginning "Verdict:" stating approve, reject, or approve with conditions.
