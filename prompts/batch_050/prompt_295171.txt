What I want is a parody of a self-help bestseller built entirely on Reflection as a life philosophy. Title, then 600-800 words in the guru's voice: chapter headings, aphorisms, testimonals from transformed readers, the works. The doctrine is that disagreement with Future You is a treatable disorder -- never plan to end up believing anything you wouldn't endorse believing now, because your future self has one more day's evidence than you do. The jokes should come from the actual epistemology, not a strawman: the guru should tout Dutch-book-proofing as the program's big selling point, and -- this is the important part -- he has to handle the standard counterexamples deadpan. Give me a chapter advising the reader who knows that after three whiskies tonight he'll be confident he's fit to drive home to start being confident of it now (call it "Trust the Driver You Will Become"), and one where he grapples with the Ulysses problem: what to do when you know your future self will be tied to a mast, drugged, asleep, or otherwise compromised, where the exceptions threaten to swallow the whole rule. Stay deadpan, no winking at the reader, but someone who knows this literature should be able to tell exactly which objection each chapter is dodging.
