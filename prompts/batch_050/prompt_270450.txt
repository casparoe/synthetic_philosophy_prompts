ok so in music class our teacher keeps saying music is basically a language of emotions -- like, the minor key "says" sad the way the word "sad" says sad, and a rising melody "says" hope, and thats why a song with no lyrics can still make you cry. she used it to argue that learning theory (scales, cadences, whatever) is like learning grammar, and that people who cant "hear" what a piece is saying are kind of musically illiterate.

i get the appeal but the more i think about it the more it seems off. words mean things by convention, right, like theres nothing sad about the sound "sad". but a minor chord sounds sad even to little kids who never learned any convention (i think?). also languages can say stuff like "the library closes at 9" and no melody can say that. but then again music has some grammar-ish things -- tension and resolution, expecting the next chord, phrases that feel like questions and answers.

so: how far does the language analogy actually hold? can you lay out what maps onto what -- whats supposed to be the words, the grammar, the meaning, the speaker -- and then where it breaks down? especially im stuck on: in a language the connection between sound and meaning is arbitrary, but is the connection between a sound and an emotion arbitrary? if not, does that make music better than language at feelings or just not a language at all? and does her conclusion survive -- if music isnt really a language, is "musical illiteracy" even a thing, or was the analogy doing all the work?

also if theres a better analogy for how sound-without-words can be sad (like, is it more like a face? a voice tone? a drug?) id take that too.
