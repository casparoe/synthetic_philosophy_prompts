So I've been going down a rabbit hole with On Certainty and the secondary lit, and I keep running into the same fight. One camp (Moyal-Sharrock, some of the "therapeutic" readers) insists that hinges aren't propositions at all -- they're ungrounded certainties that show up in what we do, more like ways of acting than things we believe, and calling them beliefs or propositions is already a category mistake. The other camp (Wright, Pritchard, people in that orbit) treats hinges as genuine propositions with a weird epistemic status -- we can't know them, doubt them rationally, or justify them, but they're still true-or-false content that our knowledge claims depend on. Both sides claim Wittgenstein's text, both sides have been citing the same handful of remarks at each other for like twenty years, and as far as I can tell nobody has budged an inch.

Here's my hypothesis, though feel free to shoot it down: both camps agree on all the actual phenomena -- that some things stand fast for us, that doubt only gets traction against a background that isn't itself up for grabs, that these certainties can't be justified in the way ordinary beliefs can -- and the whole dispute is about whether the word "proposition" (or "belief") should be stretched to cover something that plays none of the usual inferential roles. In other words it's a fight about terminology dressed up as a fight about epistemology, possibly with an exegetical turf war underneath it. But maybe I'm being too deflationary and there's a real substantive disagreement hiding in there -- e.g. about whether hinges can be rationally evaluated at all, or whether they change over time in a way that matters.

Diagnose it: verbal, substantive, exegesis cosplaying as epistemology, or two different questions (one about the structure of justification, one about the concept of belief) being argued under one heading? And tell me what would actually count as progress -- a distinction both sides would have to accept, a case they'd predict different things about, or a reason to think the impasse is permanent. Keep it under 600 words.
