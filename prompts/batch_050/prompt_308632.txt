I got into it with a friend who said constitutional monarchy is basically harmless because "the monarch reigns but does not rule" -- he used it to wave away my worry about reserve powers and the 1975 Australian dismissal. But the more I think about that slogan the more slippery it gets. Does "reigns but does not rule" mean the monarch *in fact* never exercises political influence (which seems empirically false -- weekly audiences with PMs, "the right to be consulted, to encourage and to warn," Spain's king during the 1981 coup attempt)? Does it mean they *shouldn't* intervene, which leaves open whether they can? Does it mean all their formal powers are exercised only on ministerial advice, which is just convention and varies by country (Belgium's king vs Japan's emperor vs the UK)? Or is it a claim about where sovereignty "really" lies?

Can you split the slogan into its distinct precise readings -- descriptive, normative, constitutional-formal, maybe symbolic/functional -- say which are platitudes, which are substantive, and which are just false, and then tell me which readings would actually have to be true for my friend's "therefore it's harmless" move to go through? A list I can steal for round two would be great.
