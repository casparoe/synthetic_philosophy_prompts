I'm a high school senior, and for my biology extended essay I've fallen down a rabbit hole I can't climb out of: the units of selection debate. I read The Selfish Gene for fun, then learned that a whole camp of biologists thinks selection also acts on groups, and that people have been arguing about genes versus individuals versus groups for something like sixty years without, as far as I can tell, anyone changing sides.

Here's the part that really confuses me. In Chapter VII of On the Origin of Species, in the section on neuter or sterile insects, Darwin himself deals with the puzzle of sterile worker ants by saying, in effect, that the difficulty dissolves once you remember that selection can act on the family and not only on the individual. So the founding text of the whole theory already reaches above the level of the individual organism when the facts demand it. And yet I keep reading modern authors who write as if selection above the individual were a mistake Darwin saved us from, and others who write as if gene-centric thinkers are dogmatists ignoring the obvious. How did a move the founder made casually in 1859 become a heresy that people are still fighting over?

What I actually want is a diagnosis, not a history lesson. Please take a definite position on what kind of disagreement this really is: a straightforward empirical dispute about how often group-level selection matters in nature; a verbal dispute, with each side using "unit of selection" to mean something different (the thing that benefits, the thing that's copied, the thing that competes); or a case where the mathematics is settled -- I've seen people claim kin selection and multilevel selection are just two bookkeepings of the same process -- and the real fight is over which description is more illuminating, which would make it a dispute about scientific taste rather than fact. Commit to one of these (or a better option of your own) and argue for it, including what the Darwin passage shows about which reading of "unit" he was using. Then say what would actually count as progress given your diagnosis, or argue that none should be expected. At most 600 words, please -- the Darwin passage reference doesn't count toward that.
