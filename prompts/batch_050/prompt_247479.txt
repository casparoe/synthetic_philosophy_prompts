I work in university administration, and a case at my institution last year sent me down a reading rabbit hole in moral philosophy and social theory that I still haven't found my footing in. A junior lecturer made an off-color remark at a conference, someone posted a short video of it, and over the following weeks several thousand strangers each did something small: one critical comment, one email to the department, one decision not to cite her work, one retweet with a disapproving emoji. No one organized anything, no one issued threats, and I genuinely believe each individual act, considered alone, was a permissible exercise of ordinary speech and association — the kind of thing any of us does regularly without a second thought. The aggregate, however, was catastrophic for her: she lost her position, her marriage came under strain, and she was briefly hospitalized. What struck me, sitting on the review committee, was that when we looked for someone to hold responsible for the severity of what happened, there was no one. Every participant could truthfully say: I did almost nothing.

I now keep noticing this configuration everywhere — the neighbor whom an entire street gradually stops greeting after a rumor, no one deciding anything, yet the man is as thoroughly ostracized as if a court had sentenced him; the small business that fails because hundreds of customers each independently drift away after reading a single review. The pattern is: a severe, targeted sanction composed entirely of individually trivial and individually blameless contributions, with no coordinator, and with no participant intending — or even aware of — the total. My question is whether this configuration has an established name. I know it's adjacent to the "problem of many hands," but that literature seems to be about organizations diffusing responsibility for decisions, whereas here there is no decision and no organization. It's also adjacent to structural injustice, but that usually concerns background conditions rather than a sanction aimed at a particular person. And it's adjacent to boycotts and to the philosophy of punishment, but punishment theorists generally assume a punisher who calibrates.

What I'd like is more than a label. Please tell me which established concept comes closest, how the configuration I've described differs from each neighboring concept, and where the genuine novelty lies — in particular, whether it even makes sense to call the aggregate "disproportionate" when proportionality is a standard for agents and no agent set the dose. If nothing in the literature carves at exactly this joint, propose a term, define it, and give me its boundary cases: does a spontaneous consumer boycott count? Does a whisper campaign with a single originator count, or does one malicious source disqualify it? Does it require that contributors be unaware of the total, or merely that none of them controls it?
