I run a philosophy-of-mind podcast that pitches itself to serious amateurs, and next month I'm interviewing a philosopher of biology who has spent her career defending a consumer-based teleosemantics: mental content, on her view, is fixed not by the producer-side story (what information a representation causally carries) but by the functions of the systems that consume the representation -- a token of FROG means frog because frog-snapping is what the frog's visual system was selected to do with it, never mind what actually triggered the token on a given occasion. I'd like you to draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. At minimum I want: one question pressing the classic misrepresentation problem (if the frog snaps at a bee-bee, why isn't the content the disjunctive 'fly or black moving dot'?); one on the swampman objection and whether a molecule-for-molecule duplicate with no selection history really has a mind empty of content; and one on whether her account can distinguish between 'this token means fly' and 'this token means small dark moving thing in this region,' given that selection seems indifferent between them. She is famously dismissive of all three, so the follow-ups should anticipate the standard replies (e.g., that indeterminacy is resolved by what the consuming mechanisms could use, or that swampman intuitions are just untutored).

Because my audience skews toward people interested in AI, I also want at least two questions connecting teleosemantics to large language models -- this is the part where I think her view has real untested bite. On a purely selectionist account, an LLM's token sequences have no evolutionary history, and RLHF is at best a weird artificial selection pressure over text, not over anything plausibly called a belief, so arguably nothing the model produces has determinate content at all: it just has syntactic shape that we, the genuine content-havers, read content into. But she has hinted in a recent talk that she's tempted by a liberalized version on which anything with sufficiently integrated producer-consumer structure qualifies, history be damned, which seems to concede the swampman point and more. Draft questions that corner her on which horn she actually takes: either LLM outputs are contentless (and then what about the frog's token when a clever neuroscientist recreates the frog's visual cortex from scratch in a dish?), or content is cheap enough to hand out to chatbots (and then what work is evolutionary history doing that she spent a career defending it?). The follow-ups should press for a yes-or-no before letting her hedge.

Two stylistic notes. First, no softballs and no biography questions -- every question should turn on the content of the view, and where I can, I'd like the questions to quote a concrete case (frog, bee-bee, magnetotactic bacteria, swampman, an LLM claiming to be in pain) rather than asking about abstractions. Second, order the questions so that each one makes the previous escape route less available: I want her either to bite the bullets visibly or to make a novel concession on air.
