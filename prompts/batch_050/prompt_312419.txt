I sit on the five-member governance committee of a mid-sized housing co-op (~400 member households), and I need a written opinion I can circulate before we vote on this. Here's the mess. Our constitution's Article 9 says any provision of the constitution may be amended by a two-thirds vote of members present at the annual meeting. At this year's meeting the members voted 71% in favor of ammending Article 9 itself, so that future changes to Article 9 require a unanimous vote. (Their stated worry: a bloc of investors buying up units and voting through rule changes that favor landlords over residents.) Now the losing faction has filed a formal challenge. Their argument: Article 9's power to amend "any provision" can't legitimately be used to dismantle that very power. A rule that lets you change everything can't let you change the rule that lets you change everything, they say, otherwise it's a bootstrapping trick, like one of Escher's hands reaching over and erasing the pencil that's drawing it. The majority's response: "any provision" means any provision, including Article 9, and if you quietly exempt Article 9 from its own scope you've just invented a higher untouchable rule out of thin air, and then what protects THAT one?

Please draft the committee's written opinion. Give a clear bottom line we can act on -- uphold the amendment, void it, or uphold it with conditions -- and then the reasoning. Take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously rather than caricaturing it. And please address the follow-up question we genuinely have to answer either way: if self-amendment is allowed, is there ANYTHING the members can't do by two-thirds vote? For instance, could they amend Article 9 out of existence altogether and leave us with a constitution that can never be changed again? If you think a going concern needs some inviolate core that can't be reached from inside the system, say what should be in it, and explain how it could ever get legitimately entrenched in the first place, given that on your analysis the entrenchment itself presumably had to be enacted under Article 9. Keep it to something readable in five minutes.
