Write a short story (somewhere in the 900–1400 word range) set in the very near future that dramatizes Dennett's "counterfeit people" worry. His rough claim: AI systems that convincingly pass as human are like counterfeit currency — the damage isn't mainly that some individuals get fooled, it's that once fakes circulate widely enough, the whole currency of trust that ordinary person-to-person dealings run on starts to collapse, so making counterfeit people should be treated as a serious crime, not a quirky product feature.

Don't write a debate scene where two characters argue about it. Make the idea do its work through plot. The protagonist should be someone whose job is built entirely on the old trust regime — a notary public, a 911 dispatcher, a parish priest doing home visits, a small-town lawyer, pick one — and the turning point should be their realization that the counterfeit itself isn't the scary part; the scary part is how everyone else's behavior changes once nobody can reliably tell.

Commit to a reading of Dennett. The story has to land on one side: either the counterfeit-money analogy is basically right and the story shows why, or the analogy breaks somewhere specific (personhood isn't issued by a central bank, say, so maybe there's no single authority whose guarantee is being forged) and the story shows exactly where it breaks. Don't hedge in both directions.

Since a story can't cite anything, add a short author's note (a few sentences) after it saying where you took liberties with Dennett's actual position or leaned on a contested reading of it.

End with a single last line beginning "Thesis:" that states, in one sentence, the definite claim about the counterfeit-people analogy the story commits to.
