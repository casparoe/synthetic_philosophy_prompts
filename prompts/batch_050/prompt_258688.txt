I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy, working in social epistemology with a secondary interest in normative ethics, and I am preparing materials both for my dissertation prospectus and for a small internal grant. Please write a two-page research proposal I can adapt for both purposes. The proposed project is a single paper, roughly 8,000–10,000 words, to be completed in one academic year alongside my other chapters.

The debate I want to enter is the one over moral testimony: whether a speaker's simply telling me that something is wrong can give me moral knowledge or, more demandingly, moral understanding, and whether relying on such testimony is ever an epistemically and morally respectable way of arriving at moral convictions. As I read the literature, both the pessimists about testimony-based moral belief and their optimistic critics frame the problem around isolated exchanges — a stranger tells you eating meat is wrong, a friend tells you a joke is cruel — and ask what a single act of testimony can transmit.

My half-formed idea is that this framing misdescribes most of the phenomenon it is meant to explain. The moral testimony that actually shapes lives rarely occurs in one-off exchanges between near-strangers. It occurs inside standing, asymmetric relationships — parent and child, religious authority and congregant, mentor and student — in which the hearer defers repeatedly, the testifier knows she is being deferred to, and both parties treat the deference as partly constitutive of the relationship. I suspect that replacing the one-shot model with what I want to call sustained testimonial dependence changes the normative analysis in at least three ways, and the proposal should test whether this suspicion survives scrutiny:

1. For the receiver: the familiar worry that testimony-based moral belief falls short of understanding applies differently when reliance is long-term. At some point continued deference seems to stop being a stage in moral development and become a failure of moral agency — but it is unclear whether there is any principled way to locate that point, or whether the defect, when it arrives, is epistemic, moral, or both.

2. For the testifier: the one-shot model treats the speaker's obligations as exhausted by sincerity and competence. In a sustained relationship, the testifier plausibly acquires ongoing obligations — to correct earlier testimony, to monitor what her dependent does with it, perhaps eventually to wean the dependent off deference altogether — for which the standard framework has no obvious place.

3. For institutions: confession, pastoral counseling, and some structured forms of mentorship look like deliberate technologies for managing sustained moral deference — channeling it, limiting it, or winding it down. If the relational model is right, these practices become a source of cases and of pressure on the theory rather than peripheral curiosities.

The proposal should do the following:

- State the central question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer. If my three-way division does not survive your analysis, restructure it, but preserve the core contrast between one-shot testimony and sustained testimonial relationships.
- Explain why the existing positions on moral testimony leave this question open rather than answering it by implication — in particular, why arguments designed to show what a single exchange can or cannot transmit do not settle what ongoing deference is or ought to be.
- Set out a method I could actually execute: what the paper would do section by section, what kinds of cases it would need to construct and analyze, and how it would use the institutional examples without degenerating into armchair sociology.
- Lay out the possible conclusions, what would count as success, and what would count as failure — including the possibility that the relational turn makes no normative difference after all. I want to know in advance how I would recognize that outcome rather than talk myself out of it.
- Give a realistic assessment of scope for one year and flag honestly what would have to be cut or deferred to later chapters.

Do not include citations, a bibliography, or a literature review by reference; I will attach the relevant literature myself. Refer to positions and arguments by description.
