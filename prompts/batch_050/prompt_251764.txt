Write a villanelle in defense of the deflationary theory of truth. The two refrains should be a plain first-order claim (say, "Snow is white") and its truth-predication ("It is true that snow is white"), so that the form's compulsory alternation enacts the disquotation schema: the refrains keep returning as if different lines, yet say the same thing. Use the middle tercets for the positive account — the truth predicate as a device of generalization and endorsement, the only way to affirm at once everything Euclid proved, rather than a name for some deep property that sentences share — and let one tercet give the correspondence theorist's protest that this leaves out whatever it is in the world that makes snow white. The final quatrain, where the two refrains are at last forced to stand side by side, should be the moment the reader sees that side by side is all they ever were. A reader who knows the literature should be able to tell from the placement of lines alone which tercet is the objection, and why the form has already answered it.
