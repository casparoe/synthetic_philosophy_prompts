I help run a small philosophy-of-biology reading group (mostly grad students, a couple of curious biologists) and we've spent the last three sessions going in circles on what function-talk even is -- whether saying the heart's function is to pump blood is a claim about history, about what hearts typically do, about what they're for in some designer-ish sense, or just a handy way of talking. Everyone keeps self-identifying with a label and then immediately saying things that contradict it, so I want to build a "which theory of biological function do you actually hold?" quiz for next week's session. Eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case rather than an abstract claim, with two or three answer options each, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The positions I want the key to cover: a selected-effects etiological view (functions come from a history of selection), a proper-function view in the Millikan neighborhood (functions come from belonging to a reproductively established lineage, whether or not natural selection did the work), a causal-role/capacity view (functions are just whatever a part does that contributes to some capacity of the whole system, no history needed), a biostatistical view (function is the species-typical contribution to survival and reproduction), an organizational view (function is contribution to the self-maintenance of the organized system), and a deflationary option for whoever concludes the whole notion is just loose talk. Feel free to split or merge leaves if the quiz logic demands it.

Some constraints. Every question has to be answerable by someone who's never heard any of these names -- so no "do you think functions are essentially historical?" and no "is function-talk explanatory or analytic?" Instead use cases, and the cases should be ones where the theories genuinely come apart, not ones where they all agree. Cases I'd like worked in somewhere: a creature assembled by freak accident (swampman-style) whose heart pumps beautifully -- does its heart have the function of pumping?; a vestigial structure like the whale pelvis that now does nothing but clearly once had a job; a heart so diseased it can't pump at all -- does it still have the function of pumping, or only the disposition to fail?; the classic annoying side-effect cases, like the heart's noise or the nose's talent for holding up glasses, where you need the quiz to distinguish function from lucky benefit; and an exaptation case, like feathers presumably not originating for flight, where the etiological people have to say something fiddly. Also flag explicitly any pair of positions your scoring key can't actually separate (I'm suspicious that the two history-based views will collapse into each other on ordinary cases, and maybe causal-role and organizational too) and say what extra case would be needed to pry them apart. And if there's a combination of answers the quiz allows but nobody sane would endorse -- like thinking the diseased heart has no function while insisting the swampcreature's does -- note it in the key rather than pretending the space is fully populated.
