ok so my friend and I spent like three hours last night arguing about whether large language models are intelligent, and about halfway through we realized we didn't even agree on what a theory of intelligence would have to DO to count as a good one, which is probably why the argument went nowhere. Her view is roughly that intelligence is the ability to achieve goals across a wide range of environments, and mine was something about learning quickly from few examples, but both of those have obvious problems (a lookup table achieves goals wherever it happens to be defined, evolution "learns" but nobody wants to call it smart, etc).

So before round two I want the spec first. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of intelligence must meet -- the purpose being a theory good enough to settle arguments like ours about whether a given system counts, not one good enough to build an AI from. Which cases does it have to give the right verdict on (chess engines? octopuses? human savants? ant colonies? a model that passes the bar exam but can't reliably count the letters in a word)? Which distinctions can it not collapse (intelligence vs consciousness, competence vs understanding, being intelligent vs just knowing a lot)? Which constraints can it not violate -- e.g. it can't define humans as intelligent by fiat, and it can't make intelligence so purely behavioral that a giant lookup table qualifies. For each desideratum give the case or argument that motivates it, because I suspect half the things people demand here are just turf defense.

Then the parts I actually care about: (a) mark which requirements look like genuine desiderata and which look like the prejudices of one camp (pure behaviorists, the compression-is-intelligence people, the embodiment people, whoever), (b) tell me whether any subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable -- I have a hunch that demanding both domain-generality and fast learning from scratch creates a real problem, since generality with no prior structure means astronomically slow learning, but I can't pin the arguement down, and (c) name the single requirement that knocks out the most existing contenders. And if the finished spec ends up ruling out basically everything, including us, say so plainly instead of quietly relaxing one of the requirements to save the humans.
