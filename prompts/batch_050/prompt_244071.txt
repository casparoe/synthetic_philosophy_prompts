ok so i'm helping put together a little zine for a friend group that's been slowly working through gödel escher bach, and i promised them a poem about escher's drawing hands — the lithograph with the two hands each sketching the other into existence. you're a poet who also genuinely knows hofstadter's stuff on strange loops and tangled hierarchies, so please put that knowledge to work and don't just describe the picture.

here's the constraint i care about: the poem itself should be a tangled hierarchy. two voices in alternating stanzas, each one claiming to be the one writing the other — each hand insisting it drew the hand that's drawing it. the voices should disagree about which of them is higher in the hierarchy, and neither should win, because that's the tangle. but then i want the poem to do the second thing hofstadter points out, the thing people always forget: in the actual lithograph there's an inviolate level — escher's own hand, outside the loop, never itself drawn, the reason the tangle exists at all. so somewhere in your poem the loop has to break. one element that stands outside the two voices and isnt caught in their mutual authorship. could be a stanza in a different form, a prose line, the title doing something weird, whatever — but a careful reader should be able to point at it and say "there, that's the level that isn't drawn."

and here's the part i really want you to sit with: a poem cant actually have an inviolate level, because whatever you write down is still just more words in the poem — the real inviolate level is you, holding the pen. so the poem's "outside" element is necessarily a counterfeit, and i'd like the final lines to quietly admit that without wrecking the whole piece.

think carefully about this before writing. brainstorm first about how to characterize the two voices (give them different registers so theyre distinguishable) and where exactly to break the form. then the poem itself, maybe 24-32 lines.
