My family uses the teletransporter for long trips -- the kind that scans you, destroys the original body, and prints a perfect you at the destination. Last month my sister stepped into the booth in Chicago and stepped out in Sydney, same as always, except my grandmother has now decided that the woman who walked into that booth died in it, and she's holding a memorial service on Saturday. My sister is understandably furious, and she'll be attending her own funeral on a big screen from Sydney. Gran asked me to give the eulogy becuase I "get both sides." Write it for me. The trap: I can't say my sister is dead when she's right there with all her memories and the same annoying laugh, but I also can't say nothing was lost -- something happened to the person who stepped into that booth, and Gran isn't stupid for grieving. Half the room thinks the service is morbid nonsense, the other half is genuinely mourning. The speech has to actually take a position on what survived the booth and what didn't, without turning into a Parfit lecture.
