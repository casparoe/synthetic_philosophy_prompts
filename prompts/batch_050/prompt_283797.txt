Next month I'm presenting a position paper at an AI safety workshop defending the thesis that monitoring the chains of thought of frontier reasoning models should be treated as a load-bearing safety measure: worth substantial investment by labs, and worth writing into deployment standards, even at some cost to capabilities. The audience is mixed in a specific way. About half are mechanistic interpretability researchers who tend to regard natural-language reasoning traces as post-hoc rationalization rather than a window into the computation that produced the answer; several work on RL and suspect that any optimization pressure applied to the trace will drive reasoning into illegible or encoded forms; and a few work on governance and will press on whether something this fragile can bear regulatory weight. Prepare me for the question period. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will arrive, phrased in each group's own vocabulary (the interpretability people's points about the causal irrelevance of the trace, the RL people's points about steganography and legibility decay under training pressure, the governance people's points about auditability and false assurance). For each objection, state how much force it has, what I should concede outright, what I should hold, and a reply I can deliver in under a minute. End with the weakest point in my own position — the objection I am hoping nobody raises — and the best available line on it.
