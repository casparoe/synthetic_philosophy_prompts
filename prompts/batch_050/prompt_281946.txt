Write a FAQ page, around 600 words, published by the fictional Marrow County Circuit Court to explain its new evidentiary rules to self-represented litigants. The backstory, which you should imply but never narrate: the county hired an efficiency consultant who read about the blue bus problem and concluded that witness testimony is just an expensive way of reaching conclusions that base rates reach for free. The court now accepts naked statistical evidence as sufficient to meet the preponderance standard in civil cases, and is piloting the same approach in criminal court, where beyond a reasonable doubt has been operationalized as a posterior of 0.95 or above.

Some questions for the FAQ (use these or invent better ones): 'Do I still need to bring witnesses?' 'The defendant's company runs 80% of the buses in the county -- is that enough for me to win?' 'What is a reference class and how do I pick the best one?' 'Everyone at the concert was statistically a gatecrasher, so can I sue all of them at once?' 'What if I'm probably innocent?'

The humour has to come from getting the actual doctrine right, so the FAQ should land, totally deadpan, the real pressure points of the naked statistics debate: why 80% via market share feels scandalous when 80% via an eyewitness feels fine, how the same defendant can be 90% likely liable as a member of one reference class and 10% likely as a member of another, and the gatecrasher result where every attendee is provably liable so effectively nobody is. Include at least one moment where the bland bureaucratic voice quietly betrays that something has gone badly wrong -- a fee schedule, a footnote, an oddly specific answer to a question nobody should have had to ask -- without ever breaking character or stating the moral. A law student who did the blue bus and gatecrasher hypos in evidence class should be laughing from recognition, not because the jokes get explained to them.
