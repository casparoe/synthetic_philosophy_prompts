So I've been on LessWrong / SSC / the whole ratsphere for like a decade at this point, and I'm running a reading group where population ethics keeps coming up and every thread devolves into people talking past each other about antinatalism. Half the comments conflate Benatar with "life is bad actually" pessimism, the other half think anyone who accepts the asymmetry has to be a full-on antinatalist, and someone always shows up with the consent argument like it's the same view.

I want a decision tree / flowchart I can sticky that walks someone from a neutral starting point to whichever position on the ethics of having kids they actually hold. Here's what I need:

- 6-8 branching questions, each phrased as something answerable without knowing any of the labels -- concrete cases or claims, not "do you accept the asymmetry?" More like "imagine a couple who could create a child guaranteed an extremely happy life and no suffering; if they don't, has anything gone wrong morally?"
- The branches should be built from the logical space first: whether nonexistence can be better or worse *for anyone*, whether creating happiness counts for as much as preventing suffering, whether the asymmetry (if accepted) is axiological or just about our duties, whether the impossibility of the child's consent does independent work, whether the verdicts are person-affecting or impersonal. Then locate the actual positions in that space.
- Leaves should include at least: Benatar-style antinatalism, consent-based antinatalism, a "soft" antinatalism that only kicks in below some quality threshold, total utilitarian pronatalism, average utilitarianism, and a person-affecting view that just shrugs at the whole question. If a combination of answers is unoccupied, mark the leaf "unnamed" and say in one line whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate (I suspect Benatar-style and consent-based antinatalism land in the same place on some of my questions and I don't trust myself to fix that).
- Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment -- e.g., where total utilitarianism has to bite the repugnant conclusion bullet, or where the person-affecting view has to say something weird about the non-identity problem.

One more thing: don't present the tree as gospel. At the end, tell me where your classification is most likely to be wrong -- which branch question you think does the least real work separating positions, and what argument or consideration would make you restructure the whole thing. If you think the asymmetry framing is itself a load-bearing mistake that distorts the tree, say so rather than building around it silently.

Keep it compact enough to fit in a single forum post. Casual tone is fine, but the logic needs to be airtight since the commenters will definately find any hole in it.
