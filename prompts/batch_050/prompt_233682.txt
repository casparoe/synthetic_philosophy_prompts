Take the APA's 1973 decision to remove homosexuality from the DSM and analyze it as a test case for the naturalism vs. normativism debate about disease. Use Boorse's biostatistical account and Wakefield's harmful-dysfunction analysis as the naturalist options, and a straightforwardly values-based normativism as the other side. Work out what each theory says: does homosexuality come out as a disorder on each view, and crucially, does it do so for the right reasons given how the episode actually unfolded -- the decision came through a vote under political pressure, not through new evidence about statistical dysfunction or evolutionary design. Was the APA discovering a fact about health or making a value judgment, and can each theory even draw that distinction in a way that fits what happend? Then run it the other direction: does the episode vindicate normativism, or does Wakefield's harm clause let naturalism absorb the case -- and if it does, has naturalism just smuggled normativity in through the back door? I'm less interested in a verdict on homosexuality itself than in what the episode reveals about whether any purely naturalistic criterion can do the classificatory work psychiatry actually needs.
