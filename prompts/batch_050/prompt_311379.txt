Ok so this has been bugging me since I had a lucid dream last month. I want to figure out when a dream is a real experience you had while asleep, versus a fake "memory" your brain slaps together the second you wake up. My psych teacher mentioned some philosopher who claimed dreams aren't experiences at all, which sounds nuts, but when I tried to write down my own judgments I got confused. Here they are:

1. Normal dream, I wake up and remeber it clearly, like the classic one where I'm late for the bus in my underwear. Verdict: real experience, happened during sleep.

2. My lucid dream where I knew I was dreaming and chose to fly around and even tried multiplying big numbers to test myself. Verdict: definately a real experience. I was making decisions, you can't fake that retroactively.

3. My alarm went off and in my dream a fire alarm started blaring like half a second before I woke up. Verdict: at least the fire-alarm part was really experienced while I was asleep, because the real sound came first and the dream reacted to it.

4. But then there's dreams that seem to last forever. I had one where I lived through a whole week of summer camp, and I read that the actual dream probably only took a few minutes. Verdict: I don't think I experienced a whole week. I think my brain just handed me the "memory" of a week when I woke up.

5. My little brother sometimes answers questions in his sleep and remembers zero of it in the morning. Verdict: he's not experiencing anything. No memory at all = nothing was experienced.

6. Once I got woken up out of really deep sleep and there was just... nothing. No dream, no thoughts, blank. Verdict: no experience.

The problem: in 2 and 3 I'm treating my memory as proof the experience happened during sleep. But in 5 I say no memory means no experience, which suddenly makes 2 shaky — what if the lucid dream is also just a waking memory? And in 4 I say memory lies about how much happened, so why do I trust it in 1? Still, I really want to keep both 2 and 5.

Is there one principle that captures all six verdicts, or failing that, tells me cleanly which one I have to drop? Also what would the principle say about cases I haven't made up my mind on: a dream you only remember three days later when something triggers it, and a dream where your leg hurts and you wake up and your leg actually was being squashed the whole time?
