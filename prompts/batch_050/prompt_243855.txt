I'm a second-year PhD student in machine learning, working on reward learning for language models, and our reading group has asked each of us to circulate a short statement of our commitments on Goodhart's law and specification gaming before next month's session, so that the discussion can target actual disagreements rather than talking past one another. A running argument with two of my labmates — one of whom thinks the entire Goodhart literature is overblown pessimism, the other of whom thinks benchmarks are worse than useless — has made me suspect that my own position may not be fully coherent. Below is the set of claims I would currently defend, and I'd like an audit rather than a lecture: tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear to, and what each repair would cost.

My commitments: (a) Goodhart-style collapse is structural, not incidental: when any proxy for a goal is placed under sustained optimization pressure, the correlation between proxy and goal degrades, and this is a general feature of optimization under measurement rather than a defect of particular badly chosen metrics. (b) Nonetheless, the right methodology for building aligned systems is iterative engineering: deploy, observe how the reward function or evaluation is being gamed, patch it, and repeat; alignment is a discipline of successive approximation, and there is no principled endpoint to aim for beyond what this loop delivers. (c) The canonical specification-gaming cases — for instance, the boat-racing agent that learned to circle a lagoon collecting power-ups rather than finish the course — show that the fault lies entirely with the specifier: the optimizer found a genuine optimum of the objective it was actually given, so the locus of failure is always the gap between the intended and the specified goal, never the optimizer. (d) Reinforcement learning from human feedback escapes this problem rather than merely relocating it, because human evaluators can recognize gaming when they see it, and so human feedback is not a proxy in the Goodhart sense. (e) Once a benchmark is public and has been trained against, it is evidentially worthless as a measure of capability; saturated or contaminated evaluations tell you nothing about what a system can do. (f) Mechanistic interpretability is the most promising direction in the field, because it could let us verify what a model is actually optimizing rather than inferring objectives from behavior, thereby getting us outside the proxy regime altogether.

Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, with no introduction and no conclusion. Items 1 through 4 should present the tensions (or apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection) in descending order of severity, each in two or three sentences: name the commitments involved, explain why they conflict or why the appearance of conflict is misleading, and state what would have to give in each case. Item 5 should identify the single revision that would restore the most consistency at the least cost to the overall position, without assuming in advance which commitment I would be willing to surrender.
