Is there a name for the difference here? I'm in a history seminar on how moral norms die (dueling, footbinding, slavery, that stuff) and the readings keep lumping together what look like totally different mechanisms. Sometimes it seems like a huge chunk of people privately hated the norm for ages and were just waiting for cover — then a few respected people defect publicly and the whole thing collapses within a generation, even though nobody actually changed their mind about anything. Other times it looks like the norm gets embarassing first — the people still practicing it start seeming ridiculous or low class, and the shame genuinely converts people, they don't just go quiet about the old view. (And sometimes the economics shifts underneath and the moral arguments feel decorative, but set that aside.) My question is about the first two: is there established terminology that separates them? They seem really different — a norm that died the first way could presumably snap back the moment enforcement wobbles, while the second way it's actually dead. And how would you even tell them apart in the historical record, since both just look like "everyone suddenly stopped doing it"? I don't just want vocab words, I want to know whether the distinction survives scrutiny or falls apart when you push on it.
