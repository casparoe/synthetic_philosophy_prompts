You are a philosopher of language specializing in pragmatics and the Gricean program. Think carefully and brainstorm a few candidate scenarios before settling on one.

Grice's analysis of speaker meaning runs roughly as follows: a speaker means that p by an act only if she intends (i) to get her audience to believe that p, (ii) that the audience recognize this very intention, and (iii) that this recognition function as at least part of the audience's reason for coming to believe p. This machinery is meant to exclude cases in which someone gets another person to believe p by covertly planting or displaying evidence — leaving a handkerchief at a crime scene, anonymously showing a photograph — since there the producer of the evidence intends the audience's inference to proceed without any recognition of communicative intent.

Construct an original thought experiment that pressures this analysis from the opposite direction: a case in which all three clauses are satisfied, yet intuitively the speaker still does not mean that p — or, if you judge that impossible, a case where the clauses are satisfied but only through a further "sneaky" higher-order intention (the speaker intends the audience to be deceived about some feature of her intentions), so that Grice needs an additional clause to exclude it. Constraints: the scenario must be realistic (an office, a hospital, or a courtroom; no hypnosis, no telepathy, no stipulated mind-readers); it must fit in one paragraph; and it must not reuse the stock examples of planted physical evidence. Do not stipulate the verdicts — engineer the case so the verdicts fall out of the analysis itself.

After presenting the scenario, do the check: state what the speaker intends at each level, run the three clauses explicitly, and identify exactly which feature of the case is doing the work. Then explain what happens to the verdict if that feature is removed, what supplementary condition a Gricean would have to add to restore the analysis, and whether that addition threatens to generate an infinite regress of ever-sneakier intentions.
