My friend Mara and I have this running argument that started on a long drive and has now consumed like three separate dinners. She read some Parfit in college and has very confident opinions about teletransporter cases, and I've been keeping a tally of her verdicts because I'm convinced she's secretly inconsistent. Here's the track record so far. Case one, the classic: scanner reads your body down to the last detail, destroys it, and rebuilds a single perfect copy on Mars out of new atoms, memories and personality fully intact. Mara says that is obviously still you, and anyone who disagrees is "afraid of the future." Case two, the branch-line version: same scan, same Mars copy, but this time the original on Earth is NOT destroyed and just walks out of the booth. Mara says the Earth person is you and the Mars person is a brand new guy who merely thinks he's you. Case three: a procedure that replaces your neurons one at a time with synthetic equivalents over the course of a decade, with no interruption of consciousness and full psychological continuity the whole way through. She says that's still you the entire time, no problem. Case four: the teletransporter rebuilds you from a scan taken eight years ago, so the Mars copy has your personality circa eight years ago and none of the memories since. She says that person is not you, more like "a revival of an earlier draft of you," though she conceded it's a closer call.

Last week I hit her with a new one and she refused to answer until she'd "thought about it properly," which she has now been doing for days, so I'm turning to you. Here's the case: you step into the scanner on Earth, your body is destroyed as usual, and the machine is supposed to send one signal to Mars — but due to a glitch it sends two, one to Mars and one to a relay station orbiting Europa. The Mars copy comes out perfect: all your memories, personality, quirks, the works. The Europa copy comes out degraded — it's lost about a third of your episodic memories, its affect is noticeably flatter, but it still sincerely believes it's you and shares your core values and projects. Both wake up at the same time. So: what will Mara say? Does the original you survive as the Mars copy, or did you die in the booth the moment a second signal went out? I genuinely can't tell if she's going to go with "you're the Mars copy, Europa doesn't change that" or "you're dead, two copies means no copy is you," and I want to win the argument when she finally commits.

Please give me your prediction of her verdict on the new case with your reasoning, plus a table laying out the candidate principles she might be running, with these columns: (1) the principle, stated in one sentence; (2) what it predicts about the Mars/Europa case; (3) how well it fits each of her four earlier verdicts (and flag any awkward fits); (4) a further hypothetical case that would cleanly separate this principle from the leading rival. If you think her earlier verdicts underdetermine the new case, say so outright, tell me which two principles are the strongest contenders, and which single new case you'd pose to her to force the issue.
