Her view is basically: if it talks like us, across enough topics, for long enough, then refusing to call it minded is just biological prejudice. My view is that pure conversational behavior is gameable -- a big enough lookup table or a good enough mimic could ace it without anything going on inside -- and we got completely stuck because neither of us could say what a behavioral criterion of mindedness is even supposed to *do*. Like, agree on the rules of the game before arguing about who wins it.

So here's what I actually want. Before I take another run at her, write me the desiderata: what any adequate *behavioral* test or criterion for mindedness has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each one. To make it concrete, here are the cases I keep circling, and the desiderata should at least speak to them:

- Block-style blockhead scenarios: a machine that passes any finite conversation by searching a giant precomputed tree of every possible dialogue up to some length. Feels like it should NOT count as minded, but it's behaviorally perfect in any test we could actually run.
- The mimic: a model trained to imitate one specific person's chat logs so well that their own mother can't tell. Seems like this shows passing-as-X and being-minded come apart.
- The tester-detector: a system that acts mindless on demand whenever it has evidence it's being evaluated, and richly minded-seeming otherwise. Is "behaves when watched" even the right target for a behavioral criterion?
- Animals and pre-linguistic humans: whatever criterion we land on presumably shouldn't require passing a *verbal* exam, or we've defined mindedness as "good at chatting," which was my friend's actual move and I think it gave the game away.
