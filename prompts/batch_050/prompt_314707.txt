I run a philosophy podcast aimed at advanced undergraduates and their teachers, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: the view that no one can ever be truly morally responsible for anything they do, because being responsible for an action requires being responsible for the way one is, which in turn requires having intentionally brought about the way one is — an impossible regress, since any choice that shaped one's character must itself have flowed from a character one did not choose.

Draft ten to twelve interview questions that do real work on the argument's structure rather than inviting a restatement of it. I would like the set to include at least one question on each of the following pressure points:

- The notion of responsibility at stake: whether the argument targets a particularly demanding, desert-entailing sense of responsibility and leaves ordinary practices of praise and blame untouched, or whether it reaches those too.
- The regress itself: whether there is any principled place for it to stop, and whether a libertarian appeal to undetermined, character-forming choices escapes it or merely exchanges responsibility for luck.
- The scope worry: whether the argument, if sound, proves too much by undermining rational or epistemic responsibility as well, and whether any asymmetry between the moral and non-moral cases can be motivated.
- The phenomenology: the near-unshakeable sense of being the self-determining source of one's actions, and whether the defender must treat this as a systematic cognitive illusion.
- The practical upshot: what follows for resentment, gratitude, punishment, and close relationships if the argument is accepted, and whether a human life conducted on its terms is actually livable.

For each question, append a follow-up keyed to the reply my guest is most likely to give, so that I can press past the first answer rather than move on. Finally, mark the two questions you judge most likely to make the defender uncomfortable, and say in a sentence why.
