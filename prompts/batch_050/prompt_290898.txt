For my history seminar's final project, our class split into two camps over the end of the Western Roman Empire. One side argues that Rome genuinely fell: the fifth century brought a real collapse in living standards, literacy, long-distance trade, and material comfort, and calling it a "transformation" is a euphemism that papers over a catastrophe for ordinary people. The other side argues that "fall" is the wrong frame entirely: Roman institutions, law, and Christianity evolved continuously into the early medieval world, political fragmentation is not the same as civilizational death, and the catastrophe narrative measures the period by anachronistic standards.

Write the joint statement the two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept about what actually changed between roughly 300 and 600 CE -- taxation, cities, coinage, manufactured goods, political authority; (2) a table of the exact remaining disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether archaeological evidence of material decline settles anything about quality of life or merely reflects changed priorities, and another is whether continuity of elite culture should count for more than the experience of peasants; and (4) for each crux, a piece of evidence or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance. Also flag any disagreements that turned out to be about words rather than facts.
