I'm a historian by training who has spent the last few years drifting from the field I studied into teaching, and the drift has me re-examining commitments I absorbed without ever really stress-testing them. The biggest of these is what I suppose I'd call the transformationist position on the end of the Western Roman Empire: the view that there was no civilizational catastrophe in the fifth century, no 'fall' deserving the drama the word carries, but rather a gradual reconfiguration in which Roman institutions, law, religion, landholding patterns, and self-understanding persisted and mutated under new political management. Against my old-fashioned colleagues who still gesture at Gibbon and at 476 as a genuine rupture, I've found myself insisting that 'the fall of Rome' is mostly a historiographical artifact -- a framing imposed by people who needed a fall for their own purposes, whether Renaissance humanists or Enlightenment polemicists or modern declinists.

What I want is a map of what would count against this position, and I want it from someone willing to take the position seriously enough to find its real weaknesses rather than the straw ones. Please distinguish several things: which kinds of evidence would be genuinely decisive if they held up (for instance, what would systematic evidence of sharp demographic collapse, or of a precipitous decline in material complexity across former imperial provinces, actually show?), which findings would be merely awkward but absorbable (say, localized violence during the fifth-century invasions, or the disappearance of particular institutions like the annona or a professional standing army), and which objections are really verbal -- disputes about what 'fall' means -- such that conceding them costs the transformationist nothing substantive. I'm also interested in the insulation problem: has the transformationist position been qualified into unfalsifiability, so that any evidence of discontinuity gets redescribed as 'transformation' by definitional courtesy? If so, say whether you think that's a fatal defect or a manageable one. I'd like the potential defeaters ranked from most to least threatening, with a note on which of them a committed transformationist would themselves concede as dangerous versus dismiss as question-begging.

Finally, I want the answer to be self-critical about its own verdict: after giving your assessment, state explicitly which single argument or body of evidence would most change your mind if it turned out differently than you currently suppose, and where your own reasoning in the response is most likely to go wrong. Keep the whole response under 600 words.
