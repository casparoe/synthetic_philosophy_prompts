I'm trying to get my intuitions about biological function straight and I want to know if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just inconsistent. Here's where I land on cases:

A healthy heart that can't pump anymore (say, scarred tissue) still HAS the function of pumping blood, it's just failing at it. That feels obvious to me.

A brand-new mutation appears in one beetle, lets it digest a pesticide nobody's ever seen before, and it spreads through the population because of that. Before it spreads, while it's still just the one beetle, I want to say the mutation already has the function of detoxifying that pesticide.

But then: a dead branch falls into a stream, dams it, and the resulting pool happens to save the local frog population through a drought. The branch definately does not have the function of protecting frogs. And my nose holds up my glasses great, but holding glasses is not what noses are for.

Feathers: suppose they originally spread because of insulation and only later got co-opted for flight. I feel like flight is now genuinely one of their functions, not just a bonus use.

The appendix still does some immune-related stuff in a minor way, but nobody thinks that's its function, since that's not why we have it. Hmm, wait -- actually I think I do want to say that IS its function now, even though it's vestigial for digestion. Not sure.

Finally: take the beetle case but change it so the mutation spreads purely by drift (founder effect, whatever), and it just happens to confer the pesticide resistance. Does it have the function now? My gut says... no? But I can't tell that apart from the first beetle case, which bugs me.

So: is there a coherent theory of function that captures most of these, and wich verdict does it force me to give up? I know the rough menu -- etiological accounts where function is about selection history vs causal-role accounts where it's about what a part actually contributes -- and I suspect my verdicts straddle the line. Tell me where the inconsistency actually is (is it the beetle pair? the appendix flip-flop?), and then give me the cleanest repaired principle and its verdicts on two cases I haven't thought about: a gene engineered in a lab, inserted into wild fish, that then spreads by selection -- and a trait that was selected for in the distant past but is currently maintained only by drift, doing nothing useful at all.
