I'm in my third year of a politics degree and currently taking a comparative seminar on religion and the state. For a term paper, I've been trying to think systematically about the four church-state arrangements we keep returning to: England (an established church whose bishops sit in the legislature), Germany (no established church, but the state collects a church tax on behalf of recognized religious bodies, which registered members generally must pay unless they formally leave), France (strict laïcité, with strong restrictions on religious symbols in certain public institutions), and the United States (constitutional non-establishment combined with broad religious tax exemptions and relatively permissive public religiosity).

My paper proposes an evaluative dimension I'm calling asymmetric cost incidence: the degree to which the material, symbolic, and political costs of a country's church-state settlement fall disproportionately on religious minorities and the non-religious, rather than being shared evenly across the population. For instance, a system scores badly on this dimension if non-adherents are effectively taxed to support a faith they reject, if state symbols or offices presume a majority creed, or if minority practices are singled out for restriction in the name of neutrality.

Please do the following:

1. Rank the four arrangements from best to worst on asymmetric cost incidence, and group them into tiers if you think some are roughly comparable.
2. For each country, give a short justification that considers at least one countervailing consideration -- a respect in which the arrangement might actually distribute costs fairly, or compensate for its asymmetries elsewhere.
3. Say which single, politically feasible reform would most improve the arrangement you rank worst, and briefly assess whether your ranking would change if the criterion were overall social stability of the settlement rather than asymmetric cost incidence.

I'd like the reasoning to engage with the actual mechanisms (tax collection, established offices, symbol restrictions, exemptions) rather than reciting the standard slogans about each country.
