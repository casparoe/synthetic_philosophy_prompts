I am giving a 20-minute policy talk arguing that countries should not try to top the World Happiness Rankings: the rankings primarily summarize average Cantril ladder life evaluations, not momentary happiness, and small differences, cultural response styles, inequality, reverse causation, and noncomparable policy mixes make first place a poor target. My positive proposal is that governments use the indicators diagnostically while targeting security, health, trust, and the least satisfied citizens. The audience consists of a wellbeing economist sympathetic to subjective measures, a psychometrician concerned with cross-cultural measurement invariance, and a civil servant who needs defensible targets for budgeting. Give me exactly eight numbered objections, ordered from most likely and damaging to least, each phrased as one of these audience members would ask it and followed by a concise reply; where useful, state what I should concede, what I should defend, and end with the objection I most hope nobody raises, including its strongest formulation and my best available answer.
