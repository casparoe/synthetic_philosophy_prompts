So I've been chewing on this thought experiment I half-remember from Hofstadter and partly rebuilt in my own head, and I want to stress-test the position I've landed on. The setup: imagine a huge vat of little balls bouncing around, interacting by simple mechanical rules (he calls them simms and simmballs, the details don't matter much). Out of zillions of these low-level collisions, stable larger patterns emerge that you can describe at a higher level as symbols doing things -- patterns triggering other patterns, like one symbol activating another. The key claim is that at the high level there's a clean, self-contained dynamics: you can predict what happens next by tracking the symbols and their rules of interaction, while ignoring the balls entirely, the same way you can play chess without knowing what the pieces are made of.

My position: the symbol level is where the real causal action is. If you ask "why did pattern B appear?" the true answer is "because symbol A activated it," and any story told purely in terms of ball collisions is either wrong or a degenerate restatement that misses the actual explanation. In other words I think symbols genuinely push the substrate around, not in some spooky extra-force way, but in the sense that the causal facts live at the symbol level and the ball level is just implementation.

What would count against this? Don't just gesture at "it's all physics at bottom" -- I want to know specifically: what observation or argument would force me to admit the symbol level is epiphenomenal, a kind of explanatory gloss with no causal bite? Is there a difference between my position and the weaker claim that the symbol level is merely a useful *description*, and if so, what would have to be true for the weaker claim to be the right one? I'd like the possible defeaters ranked from "this would kill the view outright" to "this is annoying but survivable," and for each one, say what a committed believer in my position would concede vs. what they'd push back on. Also: does it matter for my position whether two physically different ball-configurations can realize the same symbol? I have a feeling the multiple-realizability thing is load-bearing here but I can't quite see which way it cuts.
