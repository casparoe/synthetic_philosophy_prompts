I'm trying to figure out where I actually stand on corruption, so here are my verdicts on some cases -- tell me what principle, if any, unifies them.

A mayor steers a road contract to his cousin's construction firm and gets a kickback. Obviously corrupt, no hesitation. A village elder gives a district official a goat and two sacks of rice after the official fast-tracks the village's water connection -- no prior agreement, everyone in the area would see this as proper gratitude. I don't think that's corrupt, or only barely. A civil servant who earns too little to live on routinely accepts small "speed money" payments from applicants; literally every colleague does it and the fees are basically known prices. Weirdly I judge him much less harshly than the mayor, even though both take money for doing their job. A senator takes no money but hires the plainly unqualified son of her biggest campaign donor as her chief of staff. Feels corrupt to me, but in a different flavor than the mayor. A lobbyist's firm donates legally and heavily to a committee chair and gets lots of face time; no explicit deal ever. I think this is corrupt even though nothing illegal or even unusual happened. But a patient bringing chocolates to a surgeon who did a great job? Totally fine, no matter how nice the chocolates. And a tip left for a waiter in a country where waiters live on tips is fine, while a tip slipped to a police officer to not write a ticket is clearly a bribe, even if the amount is identical.

So: what's the principle? I'd like it stated precisely enough that I can see which intuition each clause is doing work for. I'm especially puzzled by the pair of the civil servant and the mayor -- same act type, wildly different verdicts -- and by the chocolates versus the campaign donation, since both are gifts with no explicit quid pro quo but I react oppositely. If no single principle captures all of these, tell me which verdict you'd overturn and why. Also: does your principle end up saying that some entire cultures' ordinary gift practices are corrupt, and if so, do you bite that bullet or does that count as evidence against the principle? Finally, give me your principle's verdict on two cases I didn't mention, so I can check whether it's really capturing my intuitions or just fitting the ones I listed.
