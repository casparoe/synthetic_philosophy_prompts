I have no formal training in physics, but I have been trying to understand what Bell’s theorem rules out. A friend has classified several models for me. She calls Bohmian mechanics nonlocal because the outcome realized on one wing can depend directly on the distant measurement setting. She calls Everettian quantum mechanics with a local Hamiltonian local because interactions propagate locally and there is no single remote outcome to exert an influence. She calls a retrocausal model local if the influence is carried continuously through the overlapping past and future light cones. She calls a superdeterministic model local if the correlation between settings and systems comes from an ordinary common cause. But she calls a model nonlocal when Alice’s outcome law contains Bob’s setting directly, even if it forbids controllable superluminal signals.

Now consider a model governed entirely by local equations, in which no event causes anything outside its light cone and measurement choices are independent. Nevertheless, a law constrains the four measurement events jointly; the resulting Bell-violating correlation cannot be traced to a common-past variable, a future boundary condition, or any process connecting the wings. Predict my friend’s verdict, distinguishing the principles suggested by her earlier verdicts—especially no direct causal dependence versus local generation of correlations—and explain your best-supported prediction. Also describe one further model whose classification would settle which principle she is using. Please state your confidence and single out the point you are least sure of.
