so i just spent like three hours arguing with my friend about this and we got nowhere. her dad died two years ago and one of those griefbot companies emailed her about making a chatbot trained on his texts and emails. she did it. i said it weirded me out, and her whole defense rests on one analogy she kept repeating: the bot is just like the shoebox of his letters she keeps under the bed, or the saved voicemails she still listens to sometimes. "its just a better photo album," she said, and anyone who'd let her keep the letters has no grounds to object to the bot.

audit the analogy for us. what actually maps onto what -- the letters as training data, the attic/shoebox as the server, rereading as prompting? where does it break? i want to press on the stuff that seems load-bearing to me: the letters can't produce new sentences, you know exactly what a voicemail will say before you play it, the bot can be wrong about him in his own voice, and someone else owns the shoebox now and charges her a subscription for it. but i also want to know where her side is stronger than i admitted -- a letter can hit you with something you'd forgotten, grief always involves talking to people who can't answer, and plenty of dead people's words get edited and republished by estates anyway.

the conclusion she used the analogy to reach was: if letters are fine, the bot is fine, end of story. does that survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing all the work? and if the letter comparison is the wrong one, is there a better analogy for what the bot actually is -- tribute band? ventriloquism? a very good impressionist? -- and what does that better analogy suggest about whether she should keep it running?
