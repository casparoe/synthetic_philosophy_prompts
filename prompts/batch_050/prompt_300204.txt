I wrote down her argument as best I remember it: "The idea of collapse is mostly a myth we keep retelling because we love apocalypse stories. The Maya never collapsed -- there are still millions of Maya people living in Central America today, speaking Mayan languages. Easter Island wasn't an ecocide either; the Rapa Nui were getting along fine until European ships brought disease and slave raids, so that's a story about colonialism, not about a society destroying itself. Same with the Late Bronze Age: some palaces burned and the scribes stopped writing, but ordinary farmers kept farming, and were plausibly better off once the tax collectors and their grain ledgers were gone. Collapse is never something that happens to whole societies -- it's elites losing power, and historians only call it collapse because they identify with the elites whose records they read."
