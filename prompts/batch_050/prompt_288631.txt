I'm a novelist working on an alternate history where a minor railway accident in 1848 sets off a chain of events in which no German nation-state ever forms. My editor says it's implausable because the same nationalism, warfare, and industrial pressures would have found another route. Take seriously the difference between causal sensitivity (tiny changes alter the details) and path dependence (a later outcome is genuinely unavailable, or much harder to reach, without a particular sequence). What are the most important implications of that distinction for plotting a counterfactual history that is neither butterfly-effect kitsch nor inevitable-history-with-different-dates? Please address what should remain stable, what should wash out, how institutions and beliefs could lock in, and when a this-event-caused-that-outcome claim becomes meaningless rather than merely hard to prove.
