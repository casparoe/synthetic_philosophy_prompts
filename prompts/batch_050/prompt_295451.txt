Ok this is going to sound made up but I swear it's real. My uncle is wealthy and kind of eccentric, and last weekend he made me an offer. He's a retired pharmacist and he brewed up this health tonic that's completely safe — he had it tested, drank it himself — but apparantly tastes so foul that everyone in the family who's tried a sip has gagged. Here's the deal: at midnight this Friday, he'll wire me $80,000 if, at that exact moment, I genuinely intend to drink a full glass of the tonic the next morning. That's it. I don't actually have to drink it. Once the money lands, whether I touch the glass on Saturday is entirely up to me and there are no consequences either way.

The catch is he'll know if I'm faking. He's been able to read me since I was a kid, and he wants me to look him in the eye and swear it sincerely, and honestly assume there's no fooling him — if at midnight I'm secretly thinking "lol obviously I'm not drinking that," he'll see right through me and I get nothing.

So the "smart" play seems obvious: intend to drink it, collect the cash, then just don't drink it. But here's where my brain short-circuits. Once Saturday morning rolls around, I'd have zero reason to actually drink the stuff — I'd already have the money and it's purely unpleasant. And I know this about myself right now. So how can I sincerely, at midnight, intend to do a thing I already know future-me will have every reason to skip? It feels like I'd have to lie to myself, and lying to yourself on purpose doesn't really work, does it?

Is this offer basically unwinnable for anyone who thinks clearly, or is there some mental move I'm missing? And if the money is somehow winnable, what exactly would I be doing in my head to win it?
