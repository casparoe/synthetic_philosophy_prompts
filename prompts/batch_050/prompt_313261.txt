I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I have spent the past year and a half reporting a long feature on the mental imagery debate — the dispute, running since the 1970s, between those who follow Kosslyn in holding that visual imagery is realised in depictive, spatially organised representations, and those who follow Pylyshyn in holding that imagery is just another mode of propositional, language-like description. Every interview ends the same way: each side tells me the other's experiments do not show what is claimed for them, and neither can quite tell me what would settle the matter.

For the closing chapter I want to give my readers the document the two camps should have produced decades ago: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a depictivist and a propositionalist. Write it for me. It should contain:

1. An agreed-claims section: a numbered list of findings and commitments both camps can sign — the basic mental rotation and image-scanning results, the reliability of vividness reports, the fact of aphantasia — each phrased so that the contested interpretation is deliberately left open rather than smuggled in.

2. A disagreements section: each residual disagreement stated in neutral wording that each side would accept as a fair statement of its own position, explicitly separating genuine empirical disagreements from ones that are merely terminological (over words like picture, format, or depiction). Where a disagreement is purely verbal, say so and dissolve it rather than staging it.

3. A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. I suspect these include whether imagery is cognitively penetrable in Pylyshyn's sense and whether that is even a testable thesis; whether retinotopic activation in early visual cortex during imagery bears on the format question at all, or only on where imagery happens; and whether demand-characteristics and tacit-knowledge explanations can be sharpened into falsifiable predictions rather than remaining universally available rejoinders.

4. For each crux, a concrete experimental paradigm, body of evidence, or phenomenon — for instance mental rotation, image scanning, neuroimaging, imagery vividness questionnaires, aphantasia, imagery in the congenitally blind — that both camps agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance, in the manner of a pre-registration.

Be specific throughout: named paradigms and diverging predictions, not 'further research is needed'. If a proposed test would not in fact discriminate between the camps, flag it as uninformative rather than listing it.
