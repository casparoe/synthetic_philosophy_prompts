I spent most of my career as an experimental physicist, and one habit I never shook is that when two measurements disagree you suspect the instrument, not nature. Since retiring I've been sitting in on a political philosophy reading group at the local university, and we've been circling the question of equality for weeks, and I keep noticing the same pattern and I want to know if it has a name. Everyone agrees equality matters, but the moment you ask equal along which axis, every proposed yardstick seems to fail in its own characteristic way, and the failures rotate. Take three cases we argued about. First: two neighbours with identical salaries and savings, but one has a condition requiring expensive ongoing treatment just to live a normal life. Obviously they're not equal in any sense that matters, so the yardstick should have us compensate her. But then a friend of mine who feels genuinely deprived without fine wine and opera tickets can make the exact same formal request, and compensating him feels absurd, yet nobody in the group could state a clean principle that seperates the two. Second: my grandmother grew up poor, learned early not to want much, and is honestly content; meanwhile plenty of comfortable people are miserable. If the yardstick is how satisfied people are, she gets less help precisely because she adapted to having less, which seems perverse. Third: some disabilities can't be fully offset by any amount of money, so a yardstick that says keep transferring until people are equally well off would swallow the entire budget on a few cases, which also seems wrong.

So here's what I'm after. Is there an established name for this merry-go-round itself -- the fact that each candidate measure of who is worse off breaks down somewhere, and that fixing one breakdown just moves you to a different yardstick with its own? I gather from the group that there is a famous framing question for the choice of yardstick in the first place, and I'd like that term too, but I suspect my merry-go-round is not quite the same thing as that question, more like a pattern across the possible answers. People in the group keep saying capabilities as if it dissolves everything, and I want to know whether that's true or whether it just relabels the difficulty (whose list of capabilities? what about the adapted, contented grandmother -- do you override her own assessment?).

Please don't just throw a label at me. What I actually want is the carving: which failure attaches to which yardstick, whether the expensive-taste case and the adapted-preference case are really two seperate problems or one deep problem wearing two hats, where exactly the capabilities view dodges these and where it re-imports them, and if there genuinely is no established term for the rotating-failure pattern as such, say so plainly and propose one with a definition and a couple of boundary cases so I can test it against the examples that come up in our group.
