Construct a thought experiment for a chapter I am writing on the comparability of historical atrocities. The case must put two intuitions under genuine pressure at once: the intuition that ranking atrocities against one another is obscene (because it turns victims into units on a common scale), and the intuition that some choices force comparison whether we like it or not. Specifically, engineer a realistic scenario in which an agent who conscientiously refuses to rank two past atrocities thereby brings about an outcome that is worse, by their own lights, than the outcome produced by ranking them -- so the refusal itself carries moral costs that have to be weighed. Constraints: no trolley-style contrivances with dials and levers; the agent should be an ordinary institutional actor (e.g., a memorial committee chair, a truth commission staffer, a museum curator) making a decision such people actually make; the two atrocities should differ along dimensions that make comparison genuinely contested (scale, intent, duration, relation to the present community) rather than one being obviously worse; and the scenario should fit in a paragraph. After the scenario, show the work: state what verdict the case forces on the anti-comparison position and on the pro-comparison position, identify which single feature of the case does the heavy lifting (i.e., what converts the refusal to rank into a cost rather than a neutral abstention), and explain what happens to each verdict if that feature is removed. Finally, give the strongest reply available to someone who holds that the obscene character of ranking survives even cases where refusing to rank does harm -- that is, explain how they would attack the case rather than concede it.
