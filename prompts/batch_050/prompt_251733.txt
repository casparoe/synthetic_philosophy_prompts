I'm a retired molecular biologist who lately got sucked into philosophy podcasts and now a local reading group, and I keep getting asked to referee fights I'm not qualified for. Last week two regulars went at it over whether a person could have come from a different sperm and egg than they actually did. Roughly how it went:

(1) Daniel said no: anyone who came from a different sperm or egg would simply be a different person, numerically, so "I could have been conceived from that other sperm" is like saying "I could have been my neighbor's kid" -- grammatically fine, metaphysically empty. Your origins are part of what makes you *you* and not somebody else.

(2) Priya pushed back that this is gamete mysticism. She ran a drift argument: take the actual sperm and change it one molecule at a time into the neighboring sperm. Surely the person from a one-molecule-different sperm is still you -- you wouldn't even be genetically distinguishable. But then where does the switch to "someone else entirely" happen? Any cutoff is arbitrary, and arbitrary cutoffs are a sign the principle is bogus.

(3) Daniel said the sorites move proves too much, because by that logic heaps and baldness and personhood itself all dissolve, and nobody thinks that. A sharp line somewhere is no worse than the vague mess on her side, and the zygote you actually came from is at least a *principled* line: identity can't be vague, so origins are exactly the kind of thing that could fix it.

(4) Priya then hit him with identical twins: a single zygote splits into two people, so coming from the same origin can't even be sufficient for being the same person, which makes it a strange candidate for the thing that supposedly pins down who you are. And saying "you necessarily come from *this very* zygote" just restates the conclusion -- it's defining you as whoever has that origin and then declaring the definition necessary, which she called circular.

(5) Daniel's last move was to distinguish necessary from sufficient -- twins show origins aren't sufficient, but that doesn't touch his claim that they're necessary -- and he said her circularity charge would sink any essential property, since "water is necessarily H2O" is also just definitional if you squint at it that way. Priya said chemistry tells you *why* H2O matters, while he has no story about what metaphysical work the zygote is doing.

Neither would give. Who's right about which specific points? Please go move by move, say where each of them lands a real blow and where they're talking past each other, and tell me if there's something obvious both are missing -- I'm new to this and can't tell if the twins objection is as strong as it sounded.
