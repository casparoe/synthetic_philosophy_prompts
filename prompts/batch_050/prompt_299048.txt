My uncle does this thing at family dinners where any time something outcompetes something else — a chain store killing the local hardware shop, an invasive weed taking over his backyard, one app beating another — he goes "survival of the fittest" like that settles it. Last week I pushed back and said wait, isn't that just saying the things that win, win? How is that an explanation? He got annoyed and said no, it's a real scientific principle, the fit ones survive because they're fit. Which honestly sounded to me like the same sentence said twice.

I've been turning it over since. I think the phrase has to be doing different jobs depending on who's saying it, because sometimes it seems obviously true and totally useless (yeah, the survivors survived, congrats), sometimes it seems like a genuine claim about the world (the better-adapted ones tend to win out over time), and sometimes it seems like a moral claim in disguise (the winner deserved to win, because winning is what proves fitness). My uncle slides between all three in one sentence, I think.

Can you lay out the different precise things "survival of the fittest" could be claiming, as a list I can bring to the next dinner? For each reading, say:

- what "fit" has to mean for that reading to work at all
- whether it comes out trivially true, or a real empirical claim that could have turned out false, or a normative claim
- whether it actually explains why one thing outcompeted another, or just redescribes the outcome in fancier words
- whether it would support my uncle's habit of using it to explain (and low-key endorse) the hardware store going under

Also, I have a vague memory from a documentary or somewhere that even biologists argue about whether the phrase is a tautology, and that there's some fix where fitness gets defined in terms of expected offspring rather than who actually survived — but I don't remember how that's supposed to work, and I can't tell if it rescues the phrase or just quietly swaps in a different one. If that's a real distinction and not something I half-invented, include it as one of the readings and explain whether it actually answers the "you're just saying the winners win" objection.

One more thing: I definately don't want the answer to just be "it's complicated." If the strongest reading still doesn't justify using the phrase the way my uncle does for businesses and apps, say so plainly.
