Ok so my girlfriend and I are in the middle of the biggest argument of our relationship and I need help figuring out whether my best move was actually a cheat.

Context: she's 33, I'm 35, and we're deciding whether to have a kid. She's all in, I've always been a no or at least a probably-not. Last week I made what I thought was my strongest argument and it might be terrible. Basically I said: I can't evaluate this choice from where I'm standing, because the person who has to live with the result isn't current me — it's the me-with-a-kid, and from everything I hear that guy is almost definately glad we did it. I even invoked this idea from AI alignment I read about, coherent extrapolated volition, the Yudkowsky thing where instead of asking what people want right now you ask what they'd want if they were smarter, better informed, had more time to reflect, and so on. My pitch was: post-kid me is like a mildly extrapolated version of me — he knows things I literally cannot know from here — so his endorsement should outweigh my current reluctance, same way CEV privileges your wiser self over your actual self.

Her response stopped me cold. She said that's not extrapolation, that's just picking a new person. CEV extrapolates your values under reflection, it doesn't let the choice itself swap out the evaluator. The future dad's endorsement only exists because the choice created it, so it can't justify the choice without going in a circle. And worse, she thinks the reverse holds too — childless-future-us will also end up endorsing that life. If both options self-ratify, then my-future-self-would-endorse-it is worthless as a decision procedure.

I honestly can't tell who's right, and its kind of load-bearing for my actual life, so: write me a dialogue (like 1200-1500 words) between two equally sharp philosophy grad students, one taking my side and one taking hers, hashing out whether idealized or extrapolated-preference reasoning can ever justify a choice that changes what your preferences are. Things I want them to actually tangle with:

- whether dad-me is continuous with me in the way that matters (same memories, same body, same old cares plus new ones) and whether that's enough to make his endorsement MY endorsement extrapolated rather than a stranger's
- the both-options-self-ratify problem — is there any asymmetry my side can point to (which path closes more doors, what to make of the small number of parents who openly say they regret it), or does the problem kill the whole move
- whether CEV-style idealization is even as conservative as my girlfriend assumes — doesn't full reflection change people's values too? if the smarter me in the extrapolation might want completely different things than current me, why is that extrapolation but parenthood is replacement
- how far her objection generalizes: it seems like it would also rule out moving abroad, converting to a religion, going all-in on a brutal career — basically any big choice where the person you become endorses it. Is that a feature or a bug

Make them genuinely engage with each other's points — no strawmen, nobody talking past anybody, both of them landing real hits. Its fine if they don't fully resolve it, but by the end I want them to have located the actual crux so I can tell which premise me and her disagreement really turns on. American English please.
