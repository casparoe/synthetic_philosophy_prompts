Write a joke about Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action (知行合一): the thesis that genuine knowledge of what is right is inseparable from doing it, so that a person who fails to act on what they claim to know thereby shows they never truly knew it — as Wang put it in effect, no one who merely talks about filial piety without practicing it can be said to know filial piety. The joke should be funny specifically to someone who understands the doctrine: its punchline should turn on the doctrine's actual content (for example, on what a sincere follower of Wang would have to conclude about procrastination, lapsed resolutions, or a lifetime spent studying moral philosophy without becoming any better), rather than being a generic joke about philosophers or scholars that could be retold about any thinker by swapping in a different name. The joke may take any form — one-liner, brief dialogue, or short setup and punchline — but it must be coherent and must not betray a misunderstanding of the doctrine, such as treating knowledge and action as two separate things that merely ought to be brought into alignment.
