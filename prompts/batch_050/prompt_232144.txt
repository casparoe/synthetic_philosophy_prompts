ok so my reading group (mix of law students and political theory people) just spent two sessions on Fuller, Raz, and Waldron and it was a mess because everyone kept insisting they "believe in the rule of law" while clearly meaning three or four completely different things by it. For next week I want to hand out a decision tree that starts from the one claim literally everyone in the room accepts -- that law should constrain the exercise of power rather than just decorate it -- and walks each person down to a named position based only on what they actually think.

Some constraints so the tree is useful rather than decorative:

- Every branch node has to be phrased as a question someone could answer without already knowing the labels. No "do you accept the inner morality of law?" type stuff -- instead ask about cases and tradeoffs, like whether a brutally unjust but perfectly clear, stable, prospectively applied legal system counts as having the rule of law, or whether a secret tribunal that reaches just outcomes is failing in some way that matters.
- The leaves must include: Fuller's view (the eight desiderata as a kind of internal standard of legality), Raz's thin formal/instrumental view where conformity is one virtue of law among others and fully compatible with evil law, Waldron's procedural-dignitarian view centered on courts, hearings and the opportunity to argue, a thick substantive rights-based leaf, a "rule by law" leaf (law as a tool the state uses on its subjects while staying above it), and a skeptical leaf where the whole ideal is just ideology or mystification.
- The split that matters most to us, so build the tree around it: whether the rule of law is supposed to constrain officials themselves or merely make life predictable for subjects, and whether a wicked regime can fully satisfy the ideal.
- Note any combination of answers nobody seems to occupy and say in one line why (incoherent, unattractive, or just undiscussed), and flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cant separate, especially Fuller vs. the skeptical leaf if it comes to that.

Present the tree as a numbered list of exactly eight items: seven numbered branch questions (each one or two sentences, followed by where a yes and a no lead, either another node number or a leaf name), and item 8 containing the unoccupied combinations plus the unseparable-leaf flags. No introduction, no conclusion. American English please.
