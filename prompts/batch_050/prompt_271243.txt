For a seminar on the frame problem, I would like you to construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. Design a single realistic scenario — a household or warehouse robot performing one ordinary task, no exotic technology — in which two design strategies for handling belief update come apart. Strategy A represents an action's effects by listing what changes and relying on an explicit enumeration of what stays the same; Strategy B instead computes the action's downstream causal consequences. First, give one action within the scenario where Strategy A fails but Strategy B succeeds; then give a second action where the pattern reverses. In neither case may the failure consist merely of the programmers having forgotten an obvious fact: Strategy A's failure should emerge structurally from the indefinite proliferation of non-changes it must certify, and Strategy B's failure from the indefinite ramification of indirect effects. After presenting the scenario and the two actions, demonstrate that the specifications are met: state precisely what each strategy concludes or fails to conclude for each action, and why. Then identify the feature of the case doing the work, say what happens to the divergence if that feature is removed, and give the strongest reply available to a defender of the strategy that fares worse overall. Conclude with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing a short phrase naming the load-bearing feature of the case.
