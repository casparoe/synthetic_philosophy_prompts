So I've been going down a rabbit hole about which things deserve moral consideration and I've sort of sketched out my position, but before I commit to it I want to know if it actually holds together or if I'm contradicting myself somewhere. Here's where I've landed:

1. Moral status comes in degrees, and the degrees track how sentient the thing is -- how much there is something it's like to be it.
2. There's no sharp cutoff anywhere. Sentience fades gradually, there's no magic line where it just switches off.
3. A thermostat has zero moral status. Not a tiny bit, actually zero. It's a sensor wired to a switch.
4. Insects probably have a little bit of sentience, so I won't pull wings off flies, but I don't lose sleep over spraying aphids off my tomatoes.
5. Someone under general anesthesia has their full moral status the whole time, even though for those hours there's nothing it's like to be them at all.
6. The reason sentience matters morally is the felt quality of it -- the pain hurting, the pleasure feeling good.

Which of these actually clash and which just look like they do? If some pair conflicts, tell me how bad it is and what the cheapest fix would be, and don't just say everything is equally problematic -- rank them. And if there's one change that would tidy up the most, name it.
