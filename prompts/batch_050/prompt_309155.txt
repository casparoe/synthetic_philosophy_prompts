So I work at a startup that makes neural implants (think cochlear-implant-tier tech, not sci-fi yet), and every friday we do internal tech talks. Next week I'm presenting something a bit out there: that if you replaced someone's brain neuron by neuron with synthetic equivalents over like a year, the person at the end is still the same person -- but if you scan a brain and copy it into a computer, the upload is just a copy and the original dies. My argument is basically a distributed systems analogy: gradual replacement is a rolling upgrade of a cluster (service never goes down, the identity lives in the continuity of the running process), while scan-and-copy is restoring a snapshot onto new hardware (same data, different instance). I know the analogy is doing alot of work but I genuinely think it's load-bearing, not just decorative.

The audience is about 15 people. Mostly backend and ML engineers, plus two folks with neuroscience PhDs who live for pouncing on hand-wavy brain claims, and one guy who minored in philosophy and somehow works Parfit into code review comments. Nobody's an expert on personal identity, but they're all smart and have zero tolerance for sloppy analogies, which is a problem because my whole talk is an analogy.

Help me prep for the Q&A. I want to know what's coming before it comes. I'm especially worried the neuro people will bring up actual split-brain patients -- which is awkward because split-brain is also literally the name of the failure mode in my analogy -- and the philosophy minor will pull the move where you keep all the removed neurons alive and reassemble them into the "original." I've got nothing for that yet.

Format the prep sheet like this, in this order:

- The Gauntlet: the 8-10 objections I'll actually get from this specific crowd, ordered by how much damage they'd do, phrased the way these people would phrase them (engineer-flavored, not seminar-flavored). A few sentences each, ~400 words total.
- What I Say Back: a reply of two spoken minutes or less for each objection, numbered to match, with a one-line note under each on where the reply is shakiest. ~600 words total.
- Concede vs Hold: a short list of what I should give up gracefully and what I should dig in on no matter what. ~150 words.
- The Nightmare Question: the single weakest point in my position, and whether there's any honest way to handle it besides hoping it doesn't come up. ~150 words.
