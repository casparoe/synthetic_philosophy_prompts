I'm a novelist working on a book and I've gotten stuck on a pattern I'm building a character around, and I'm hoping it has a name in philosophy or economics or somewhere, because if it does I want to read about it.

Here's the pattern. My character is a session musician, and over about fifteen years she goes from playing stuff she loves to playing stuff she despises, but there is never a moment where she sells out. Each step is tiny: a jingle that pays twice what the wedding gig pays. An album session that's a bit more generic than the last but the producer is good to work with. A tour she takes because it's only six weeks and the money sets her up for a year. Every single choice, if you freeze the frame and look at it, is obviously the right call -- she'd be a fool to turn any of them down. She knows exactly what's happening the whole time, she's not fooling herself, she talks about it with her friends. And at the end she would trade the whole pile of money to be back at the start, but if you replay it choice by choice she still can't point to the one she'd take back.

I've seen the same shape in real life with a friend whose flat slowly filled up with gadgets -- each one a small convenience, each one a small loss of privacy or attention, all of them individually worth it, the total not worth it. So it's not just my character.

Now, I know the obvious candidates and I don't think they fit. It's not addiction, there's no craving, she'd walk away from any single deal without a pang. It's not that slippery slope thing people warn about, because that's a prediction that one step will force the next, and here nothing forces anything. It's not the frog in the heating water, because she notices everything. And it's not weakness of will either, because she never acts against what she judges best -- that's the whole horror of it, her judgment keeps being right, step by step, and wrong overall. Like the local math and the global math come apart.

So: is there an established name for this exact configuration -- fully aware, individually rational micro-choices, each genuinely the best available, summing to an outcome the person would reject as a package and would even pay to undo? If there is, what's the literature on it, and does it treat the person as irrational somehow or as rational-but-doomed? If there's no clean name, what's the nearest stuff and where exactly does my pattern peel off from it? I'd also take a good label for the character type, since she needs to be a type and not just a person. And please don't just hand me a word -- I need to know where the edges of the concept are, what it excludes, because I have to decide which scenes count as instances of it and which are just ordinary compromise.
