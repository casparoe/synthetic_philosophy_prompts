I'm working on a short essay for an undergrad philosophy of art journal and I'd love comments before I submit. It's on Schopenhauer's theory of music in The World as Will and Representation (mostly the third book, around §52, plus the later chapter on the metaphysics of music). Here's the draft, such as it is -- it's really more fleshed-out notes than prose at this point:

1. Setup: for Schopenhauer the arts all objectify the Will, but at one remove -- they present the Platonic Ideas, which are the Will's adequate objectification. Music alone skips the Ideas and copies the Will directly. That's why he ranks it above the other arts and why it speaks to everyone regardless of their knowledge of the world.

2. The structural analogy bit: he maps the parts of music onto the grades of the Will's objectification -- the ground bass as inorganic nature, the middle voices as plant and animal life, and the melody, which alone carries a coherent, intellectually graspable story from start to finish, as the life of the rational human being, with its striving, delay, and satisfaction. I want to argue this isn't a loose metaphor for him but a strict homomorphism claim, and that's why he thinks it explains music's power rather than just redescribing it.

3. My main puzzle: Schopenhauer's whole aesthetics says aesthetic pleasure comes from escaping the Will -- pure perception, the will-less subject, the calm of the aesthetic state. But music, on his own account, is the most direct presentation of the Will there is. So music should be the most painful art, or at least the least will-less, yet he treats it as the deepest consolation. My resolution: the copy isn't the thing copied -- music lets you contemplate willing as such, its delays and resolutions, without any of it being *your* willing, so you get the Will's whole inner life with the suffering subtracted. Kind of a "willing at arm's length" reading.

4. Application: I test this against sad music and film scores -- if my reading is right, his view explains why we enjoy tragic music without needing any special puzzle about the paradox of sad music enjoyment, because the enjoyment was never about the emotion's content anyway.

Questions for you, in rough order of how much I care:

- Accuracy check on parts 1 and 2. Have I got the Will/Idea/music hierarchy right, and is "strict homomorphism" too strong or actually defensible from the text?
- Is the tension in part 3 a real tension in Schopenhauer, or does it dissolve once you distinguish the Will as object from the individual's own willing? I have a nagging worry I'm just restating the obvious and he never saw a problem here.
- Does my resolution secretly break his system? Specifically: if contemplating the Will can be pleasant, does that undermine the pessimism argument that existence is essentially suffering?
- Is part 4 too fast? I gesture at the sad-music literature in two sentences and I'm not sure the application earns its place.

Be as blunt as you like, especially on the third question -- that's the one my supervisor already raised an eyebrow at.
