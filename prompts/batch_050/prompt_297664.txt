Is there a name for this? I keep running into a wrinkle about being involved in something bad without actually doing anything, and I can't find the right concept. The usual complicity stuff seems to assume you contributed somehow -- drove the car, passed the message, laundered the money. But consider these:

1. A heist crew hires a backup getaway driver. The deal is she only drives if the main driver can't. The main driver stays healthy, so she sits in a parked car three blocks away the whole time, engine running, never touches the wheel for anyone. The crime goes off exactly as it would have without her.

2. Firing squads supposedly load one rifle with a blank, but that's different, everyone's shot goes somewhere. I mean a case where they add an eleventh shooter whose rifle is secretly never loaded at all, unknown to him, because ten was always plenty. He aims, pulls the trigger, nothing leaves his gun, and he dies believing he killed a man.

3. A friend agrees in advance to lie to give someone an alibi, but the police never ask, so the lie is never told.

In all three the person did everything a complicit person does -- agreed, showed up, stood ready -- but the causal chain of the actual crime never ran through them. And yet most people (me included) judge them as basically in on it. Meanwhile the "attempt" framing doesn't fit either, because attempt implies the crime failed, and here the crime succeeded, it just didn't need them.

One more twist that seems like it should matter but I can't say how: sometimes the plan only goes ahead *because* a backup exists (the crew wouldn't risk it without redundancy), and sometimes the backup is pure surplus and everything proceeds identically either way. Is the first backup complicit in the actual wrong and the second not? That feels too easy, but treating them the same also feels wrong.

So: is there an established concept or literature for this -- participation that is fully willed but never causally activated? How does it differ from ordinary complicity, from attempt, from moral-luck cases like the drunk driver who gets home without hitting noone? And if nothing quite covers it, what would you call it, with a definition and some boundary cases? Please don't just hand me a label, I want to know what the thing actually is and what it isn't.
