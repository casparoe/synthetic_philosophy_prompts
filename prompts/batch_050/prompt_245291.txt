I'm a junior in high school and my ethics elective just finished a unit on punishment. The teacher presented retributivism (people should be punished because they deserve it, in proportion to their wrongdoing) and consequentialism (punishment is only justified by its good effects: deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation) as the two big rivals, and I keep finding that my gut verdicts don't line up cleanly with either one. I went through a bunch of cases to figure out what I actually believe, and I'd like help extracting the principle behind my reactions -- or being told honestly that there isn't one and some of my intuitions have to go.

Here are my verdicts. (1) A student cheats on a final exam, confesses the next day before anyone suspected him, is genuinely remorseful, and everyone agrees he'd never do it again. I still think he should get some penalty -- a zero on the exam, say -- even though deterrence and rehabilitation have nothing left to do. (2) A woman shoplifts bread because she can't feed her kids; I think she's still done something wrong, but punishing her feels pointless, maybe even bad, and I'd be fine with no punishment at all. (3) Two people drive home equally drunk on the same night; one gets home safely, the other hits a pedestrian. I feel strongly the second deserves a much harsher punishment, even though the only difference between them is luck. (4) I think it's wrong to punish one shoplifter extra harshly just to "send a message" during a shoplifting wave, even if it would genuinely reduce theft -- using a person as a billboard seems off. (5) A man committed a serious assault forty years ago, was never caught, and has since lived an honestly good life and is no danger to anyone. Part of me says punishing him now accomplishes nothing; another part says something is still owed. I lean toward saying he should still face some punishment, but this one wobbles. (6) If we could magically guarantee a murderer would never reoffend and everyone would still believe he was in prison (so deterrence was preserved), releasing him in secret feels deeply wrong to me.

What I want: figure out what single principle, or small set of principles, best captures this pattern, and tell me which of my verdicts it can't save.
