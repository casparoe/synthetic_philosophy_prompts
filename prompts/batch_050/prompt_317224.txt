Ok so the debate over the norm of assertion -- knowledge norm (Williamson-style), truth norm, justified-belief norm -- has been going for like 25 years and as far as I can tell nobody has ever changed their mind. And the weird thing is both sides mostly agree on the data: lottery assertions ("your ticket won't win") sound defective, "p but I don't know that p" sounds off, challenges like "how do you know that?" are licensed in conversation. Each camp claims the same data supports them.

My suspicion, offered as a hypothesis: the parties aren't actually disagreeing about which assertions are proper. They're disagreeing about what a "norm of assertion" is even for -- explaining conversational practice? accounting for when blame is appropriate? underpinning the epistemology of testimony? -- and the shared data underdetermines all of that.
