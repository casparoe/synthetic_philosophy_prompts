ok so for my school's philosophy + CS club I'm running a debate thing on AI safety and I picked the whole "low impact AI" idea, like whether you can just tell an AI "don't cause side effects" without having to spell out everything you actually want. From my reading there's basically two camps:

- Camp A: the impact-measure people. They think you can define "impact" in a mostly value-free way, e.g. penalize the AI for changing stuff in the world that a wide range of possible goals would care about (the "attainable utility" style idea), or compare what the AI does against what wouldve happened if it just did nothing (inaction baselines). Their bet is you get most of the safety benefit without solving the entire "specify human values" problem.
- Camp B: the skeptics. Their argument is roughly that "side effect" only makes sense relative to what you value -- knocking over a vase is a side effect if you didn't want it knocked over, and part of the plan if you did -- so any impact measure either smuggles values in somewhere, or ends up penalizing literally everything the AI does, or (with a badly chosen baseline) rewards the AI for quietly preventing problems it caused itself. Plus a truly low-impact AI might just be a useless AI.

What I actually want from you: write the joint statement that one person from each camp could both sign after a genuinely good-faith adversarial collaboration, not a debate where they dunk on each other. Specifically:

1. A numbered list of claims both would sign -- including where impact measures clearly do catch real failure modes, AND where the "it's all just values anyway" argument overreaches.
2. A disagreements table where each remaining disagreement is phrased so that each side would accept the description of its own position. No strawmen on either side.
3. The cruxes: the specific sub-questions where, if they got settled, at least one side would have to change its mind. For each crux, give a concrete test, thought experiment, or toy AI design that both sides agree would bear on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts the result would be.

Also please be upfront about which points are basically settled among people who actually work on this and which are still genuinely contested -- don't make one camp's framing sound like the consensus if it isn't. And write the whole thing in American English since that's what we use at school.
