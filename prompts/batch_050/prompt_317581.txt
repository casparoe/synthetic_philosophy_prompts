I am designing a research program on whether structured debate between AI systems can serve as a scalable oversight mechanism — that is, whether a judge of limited ability, watching two or more AI debaters argue for competing answers, can reliably arrive at the correct answer even when the debaters' capabilities far exceed the judge's. Before I assess any particular protocol, I want the specification itself written out: a full list of the desiderata a debate-based scheme must meet to count as a genuine solution to the scalable oversight problem, rather than a persuasion engine that happens to reward whatever the judge already finds plausible.

For each desideratum, give the requirement, the case or failure mode that motivates it, and whether it is non-negotiable (a scheme failing it has not solved the oversight problem at all) or a preference characteristic of one camp. At minimum, your list should engage the following pressure points, and should add whatever I have overlooked:

1. The scheme must not smuggle in verification. If the judge is required to independently check any claim at the bottom of the debate tree, oversight has been presupposed rather than achieved — yet the recursion of claims into sub-claims must terminate somewhere. State precisely what the judge is permitted to rely on at the terminal nodes.
2. The scheme must rest on an explicit asymmetry assumption: some structural feature of the protocol must make honest argumentation a better strategy than dishonest argumentation. Identify what that feature is claimed to be, and what would have to be true of argumentation in general for the assumption to hold.
3. The scheme must address the obfuscated-argument problem: a dishonest debater may present a large argument containing a single fatal flaw that the honest debater cannot locate, or cannot render legible to the judge, within the interaction's length limits. Say whether an adequate scheme must eliminate this possibility or merely bound its probability, and why.
4. The scheme must not assume that at least one debater is honest. Collusion, or a training signal that rewards winning rather than truth, must not break it silently.
5. The scheme must hold in the worst case, against an adversarially constructed argument, not merely on average over typical debates.
6. The scheme must specify a floor of judge competence — the minimal abilities (e.g., detecting explicit contradiction, tracking conditional commitments) it cannot do without — and those abilities must be ones we can actually test for in the judges we have.

After the list, do three things. First, flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, with your reasoning — if the specification is internally inconsistent, that is itself the most important result. Second, identify the single desideratum that excludes the largest number of plausible protocol designs, and say whether you think that desideratum is defensible or should be weakened. Third, briefly score three or four representative protocol families against the full list, one short paragraph each. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; the scoring is secondary.
