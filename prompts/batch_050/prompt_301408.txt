Science journo here, I cover the consciousness beat (yes that's a real beat, no I don't fully understand it either). At a conference last month I watched a neuroscientist kill a whole discussion about whether fish feel pain by declaring that consciousness is an illusion — as in, the question doesn't even make sence, we'd be asking whether fish have a thing that nobody has, us included. Then a week later a philosopher used the exact same phrase in an interview with me, but she clearly meant something way milder: introspection misleads us about our own experience, and she still thought fish pain was a perfectly sensible research question. Same slogan, totally opposite upshots, and I havent been able to pin down where the gap is.

So: what are the precise claims that line could be making? Give me a menu running from the most deflationary reading up to the most radical — introspection being fallible, experience not being how it seems to us, phenomenal properties not existing at all, whatever is even stronger than that. For each reading tell me whether it's a platitude nobody would deny, a live empirical bet, a metaphysical thesis, or whether it eats itself (if it merely *seems* to me that I'm in pain, isn't the seeming itself an experience someone has to explain?). And the part I actually need for my article: which readings would genuinely support the neuroscientist's move of dismissing the fish question, which ones only get the philosopher her milder version, and does any reading say anything about whether an AI could be conscious — or does the slogan just dodge that question too?
