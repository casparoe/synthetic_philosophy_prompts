I'm a high school junior doing an independent study in philosophy, and I keep running into a pattern that I can't find a name for, though it feels like something philosophers must have noticed. Here are three cases of it.

First: I wait for the school bus before sunrise, and I watch the sky get light. Each minute looks exactly like the minute before it -- I can never spot a change. Yet at some point it clearly is light out, and I know it. So there must have been a first moment when I knew it was light. But that moment is invisible to me: I couldn't have pointed at it while it happened, and I can't find it afterward. Either it was light all night (absurd), or there is a moment where my knowledge quietly switched on without my being able to detect the switch.

Second: I count the people in the bleachers at a game. At forty people I'm confident of the exact count; at four hundred I'm not. Somewhere in between I stop knowing the count, but there's no crowd size I can point to and say "here, at this size, my counting stopped being knowledge." The boundary of my own knowledge isn't something I can see, even though the boundary definitely exists.

Third: during a movie in a dark classroom, a classmate slipped out. At the start I knew she was there; by the end I knew she wasn't. But I never experienced a moment of realizing she was gone. For a stretch I presumably didn't know whether she was there -- and during that stretch I didn't know that I didn't know.

So the pattern is: in situations that change gradually, knowledge seems to end somewhere, but the place where it ends is undetectable from the inside, and in particular it seems like I can know something without being in any position to know that I know it -- and can lose knowledge without being able to know that I've lost it.

My questions. Is there an established name for this phenomenon, or for the underlying fact that certain states can't be known from the inside even when they obtain? Second, I want the carving, not just the label: how is this different from (a) ordinary uncertainty, where I notice that I'm unsure; (b) vagueness about words like "light" or "crowd," where the problem seems to be in the language rather than in what I can know; and (c) simply being an unreliable observer? Third, does the pattern imply that knowing-you-know fails only in gradual cases like mine, or does it fail in general, and if only in gradual cases, what exactly is it about gradualness that does the work? Fourth, some boundary cases: does the same thing happen with a sudden change I might have missed, with something I learned from someone else, or with something I proved in math class? If it turns out no existing term fits all of this exactly, then say which concept comes closest, where my phenomenon departs from it, and propose a name with a definition sharp enough to tell the boundary cases apart.
