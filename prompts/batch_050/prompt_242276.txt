I'm a novelist, and two of my most argumentative friends have spent years fighting about Mencius vs. Xunzi on human nature. One insists people are born good and just get corrupted by bad environments, the other that we're born grasping and selfish and goodness is an artifice, like warped wood steamed straight in a frame. I'm tired of playing referee, so: write the document they should've produced if they'd ever done a proper adversarial collaboration like grownups.

Start with a numbered list of claims both would sign (about what the old texts actually say, what ordinary moral cultivation looks like, what both sides' recommended regimes have in common), then a disagreements table where each position is phrased so its own side would accept the wording. Name the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether following your spontaneous impulses is a reliable moral guide or just self-flattery, and another is what to make of childrens' unprompted sympathy vs. their unprompted greed. For each crux, give an observation or case both would accept as bearing on it and write down each side's prediction in advance. If part of their fight turns out to be verbal (I have a hunch the word "nature" is doing sneaky work for both of them), say which part and why.

One selfish extra: at the end, tell me briefly what each view implies for how I should write a character's redemption arc, so I know which friend to quietly ignore.
