I’m applying for a four-month independent study grant in philosophy of mind/cognitive science and need help turning a half-formed idea into a one-page research proposal. In a seminar discussion, we got stuck on the familiar claim that Darwin and Turing each pulled off a “strange inversion”: instead of explaining competence by first positing an intelligent comprehending designer, they show how competence can arise from mechanisms that do not understand what they are doing. My project would look at whether contemporary AI introduces a third case that is not quite either one. Suppose a hospital uses an AI system to summarize research papers for treatment decisions. The model is very good at producing apparently reasoned comparisons, but its “reasons” come from statistical training and human feedback rather than from grasping medical evidence. Yet the hospital’s humans also increasingly adjust their questions, workflows, and standards around whatever the system can do well. So competence may not simply be produced without comprehension inside the machine; it may be distributed and reshaped across a machine-human institution.

The proposal should state the research question precisely: when does this kind of distributed competence amount to collective comprehension, and when does it merely simulate comprehension at the institutional level? Explain why the obvious answers are inadequate -- saying the AI has no understanding leaves the institution’s changed abilities mysterious, while saying the institution understands risks laundering the machine’s output into human authority. The approach should compare three cases: natural selection producing adaptive design, a computing system following formal rules, and an institution whose humans and AI models co-adapt over time. I’d like the proposal to argue that conceptual analysis, rather than another benchmark study, can make progress by developing criteria for where comprehension is located and what counts as inherited versus generated understanding. Include what the final deliverable would be, what would count as a successful result or a failed one, and how to keep the project realistic in four months. Please describe relevant positions and arguments in prose rather than giving a bibliography; I’ll fill in citations myself.
