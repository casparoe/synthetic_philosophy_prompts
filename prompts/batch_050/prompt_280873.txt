I'm a doctoral student working on collective intentionality, and I'd like a thought experiment built to order. Construct a scenario, modeled loosely on Hofstadter's ant colony Aunt Hillary, in which three accounts of mindedness deliver three different verdicts about whether a system of many simple parts counts as a minded subject: (i) a functionalist says yes, (ii) a higher-order theory of consciousness says no, and (iii) a view that ties consciousness to intrinsic causal structure rather than to functional role (e.g., an integrated-information-style view) says something different from both, for instance attributing experience to the parts but no unified subject at the whole. Constraints: the system must be physically realizable (no infinite populations, no lookup tables, no stipulation of phenomenal facts); the description must be neutral enough that the three verdicts follow from the theories rather than being smuggled into the setup; and the whole colony must display behavior rich enough to make the functionalist verdict non-trivial. After presenting the scenario (one or two paragraphs), derive each theory's verdict and explain why; identify which single feature of your construction does the decisive work and what happens to each verdict if that feature is removed; and say how a defender of the higher-order view would argue that the case merely changes the subject rather than refuting their position.
