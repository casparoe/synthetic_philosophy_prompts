Background first: I'm a retired condensed matter physicist, and about a year ago I joined a philosophy reading group (it's me, a retired GP, and an actual philosopher, so the standard is mixed). Lately we keep circling this question of whether a language model's chain-of-thought can be trusted as a report of how it actually got its answer. The debate in the group is a mess because everyone uses words like "faithful" and "honest" differently and I want to force some order on it.

So: write me a decision tree that sorts the positions on whether chain-of-thought outputs are faithful reports of a model's reasoning. Here's the situation I want it to handle. Imagine a model that explains its answer step by step and the steps look coherent. Cases that should feature as nodes or test cases somewhere in the tree:

1. The answer was actually driven by a hint buried in the prompt, but the CoT never mentions the hint and instead presents a derivation. Some of us say that CoT is simply false; others say it's faithful to *a* way the answer could be derived, just not *the* way it was. That split has to be a node.
2. The CoT tokens are themselves part of the computation that produces the answer (the model would answer differently without writing them), versus a case where the CoT is generated after the answer is already settled internally, as decoration. I want a node that asks whether "faithfulness" even means the same thing in these two setups, or whether it's only well-defined in one of them.
3. Whether unfaithfulness would even be detectable by the model itself: if you asked the model "did the hint influence you?" and it said no, is that second report evidence of anything at all, or is it just another output with the same problem?

A couple of constraints, because I've seen trees like this that just sort people by labels:

- Every branch node must be a question someone can answer without knowing which position it leads to. No node like "do you believe CoT is faithful?" — that defeats the whole point. Concrete cases or yes/no claims about mechanisms, please.
- The leaves should include at least: the naive window view (CoT is a genuine introspective report), the post-hoc rationalization view (analogy to human confabulation after split-brain or choice-blindness experiments), a pragmatist view where faithfulness only means "predictively useful for some downstream purpose", a deflationary view where the question is ill-posed because "the model's reasoning" isn't the right kind of thing to have a report of, and a mechanistic view where the question can only be answered by inspecting internals and CoT itself is neither faithful nor unfaithful, just irrelevant evidence either way. If your tree can't cleanly seperate two of these, say so explicitly rather than fudging a branch.
- Where the logical space allows a combination of answers that nobody in our group (or anywhere, as far as you know) actually holds, mark the leaf as unoccupied and say in one sentence whether that's because the view is incoherent or just unattractive.
- Audience is educated non-philosophers, so no unexplained jargon, and keep the tree to maybe 5-7 branching nodes. If the tree has to be deeper than that, something's wrong with my setup and tell me what.

One more thing: our group's physicist (me) keeps insisting the whole thing is like asking whether a thermometer "honestly reports" temperature, and the philosopher keeps saying that's a category error. If your tree can locate where that disagreement actually bites, rather than just gesturing at it, I'll buy you a metaphorical beer.
