The contenders I will eventually assess are: (i) expected utility theory; (ii) cumulative prospect theory; and (iii) risk-weighted expected utility theory. Since the third is less standard, let me fix the notion here so you do not need to look anything up: the agent orders the possible outcomes of a gamble from worst to best and computes value by taking the worst outcome plus, for each subsequent improvement, the size of that improvement weighted by a risk function *r* applied to the probability of obtaining at least that improvement, where *r* maps [0,1] to [0,1] with r(0)=0 and r(1)=1. Convex *r* encodes pessimism about one's chances without any curvature in the utility function. Expected utility is the special case where r(p) = p.
