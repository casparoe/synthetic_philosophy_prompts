I'm a third-year PhD student working on the badness of death, and my dissertation chapter is basically one long engagement with the Epicurean argument that death can't be bad for the one who dies (no subject left to be harmed, nothing experienced, Lucretius's symmetry with prenatal nonexistence, etc.).

Here are the anti-Epicurean moves I already know and don't need repeated:

1. Nagel-style deprivationism: death is bad because it deprives you of goods your life would otherwise have contained, full stop.
2. The Keats-type case: a brilliant person reduced to a permanently infantile state suffers a misfortune even though they never experience the loss, so the "nothing can be bad unless experienced" premise fails.
3. Parfit's bias toward the future as a way of breaking Lucretius's symmetry (we rationally care about future nonexistence in a way we don't care about past nonexistence).
4. The Brueckner-Fischer line that future goods deprivation itself explains the asymmetry even without leaning on future bias.
5. Fixes to the timing problem: harm that's atemporal (bad for you simpliciter, not bad at a time), or harm located at the moment of death.
6. Dropping the existence/experience requirement via posthumous-harm analogies (Feinberg-style: surviving interests being set back, betrayals and slander after death).
7. Feldman-style possible-worlds comparisons: your life goes worse overall in the world where you die at t than in the nearest world where you don't.
8. Williams's Makropulos-adjacent point: death is bad while you still have categorical desires, because it frustrates the projects that give you reason to go on at all.

As far as I can tell that covers basically everything standard in the literature. What I want is a reply to the Epicurean that is NOT on this list, and not a rewording, special case, or mashup of items on it (so e.g. don't give me yet another solution to the timing problem dressed up differently). Ideally something that attacks the argument from a direction this whole literature ignores. Then be honest with me about whether its actually any good -- would it survive a referee, or does it collapse back into one of the moves above once you push on it? I'd rather you tell me it's weak than oversell it.
