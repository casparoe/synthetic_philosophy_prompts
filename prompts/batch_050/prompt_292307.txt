Consider the following situation. A dermatologist examines a patient in poor lighting. There is a characteristic rash pattern — call E the proposition that it is present — whose presence would substantially raise the probability of a diagnosis H. The lighting leaves her unsure: after the inspection her credence in E is 0.7, though it was 0.3 beforehand. Jeffrey conditionalisation tells her to set her new credence in H to P(H | E) × 0.7 + P(H | ¬E) × 0.3, using her prior conditional credences. My question concerns the limits of this rule and what a rational agent should do when those limits are reached.

First, the rule is usually said to be warranted only when a rigidity condition holds: the experience that shifts the agent's credence over {E, ¬E} must leave her conditional credences P(H | E) and P(H | ¬E) untouched. Please explain why this condition matters. In particular, explain how successive uncertain updates can fail to commute — so that applying Jeffrey's rule first with respect to one partition and then another yields a different result from applying them in the opposite order — when rigidity fails, and why this undermines the natural thought that Jeffrey's rule is simply ordinary conditionalisation generalised to uncertain evidence. Use concrete toy cases rather than purely formal statements; I want a gut-level grasp of where the non-commutativity comes from.

Second, I want you to take a definite position on what the dermatologist should do if rigidity fails in her case — for instance, if the very dimness of the light that makes her unsure whether the pattern is present also makes her unsure how diagnostic the pattern's presence would be in the first place. One possible repair is to retreat to a richer proposition — say, that it visually seemed to her that the pattern was present — and to conditionalise on that in the ordinary way. A second is to keep Jeffrey's rule but apply it to a finer partition on which rigidity does plausibly hold. A third is to accept that no formal rule governs the situation and that she must simply re-form her total judgement holistically, trusting her overall cognitive economy rather than any update rule. Please argue for one of these options (or a fourth of your own devising) against the alternatives, giving reasons rather than surveying the possibilities, and conclude by stating how confident you are in your verdict and which consideration you take to be the most serious threat to it.
