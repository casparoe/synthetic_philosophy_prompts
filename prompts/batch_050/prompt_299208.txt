I'm a site reliability engineer, about ten years in, mostly distributed systems, and I keep chewing on something that came up when my sister (an ICU nurse) tried to explain to me why hospitals can declare someone dead in two totally different ways. As I understand it: one route is brain death, where the whole brain including the brainstem has irreversibly stopped functioning even though a ventilator keeps the heart going and the body warm, and one route is circulatory death, where the heart stops and after a short no-touch period -- sometimes only a couple of minutes in controlled organ donation cases -- death is declared and retrieval can begin. There's a famous fight in the literature about whether that second route requires circulation to be *irreversible* (it could never be restarted) or merely *permanent* (it won't be restarted, because nobody's going to try, given the decision to stop). Apparently auto-resuscitation after a few minutes is essentially unheard of, but the philosophical fight is real.
