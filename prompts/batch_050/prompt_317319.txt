There's this econ history blogger I follow (normally pretty sharp, very much in the ACX orbit) who did a post summing up Adam Smith's take on colonies in Wealth of Nations. His version: Smith proved the empire was a pure money pit for Britain, the American colonies never really contributed anything to the mother country, the whole colonial system was basically a racket run by merchants who rigged trade rules for their own profit, and Smith's bottom line was that Britain should cut the colonies loose and stop LARPing as a great power. I was nodding along until I actually opened the third part of the colonies chapter (Book IV, Ch. VII) and hit this passage:

"The exclusive trade of the mother countries tends to diminish, or, at least, to keep down below what they would otherwise rise to, both the enjoyments and industry of all those nations in general, and of the American colonies in particular. It is a dead weight upon the action of one of the great springs which puts into motion a great part of the business of mankind. [...] It is a clog which, for the supposed benefit of some particular countries, embarrasses the pleasures and encumbers the industry of all other countries; but of the colonies more than of any other."

A few things tripped me up. The target here is "exclusive trade," not the colonies themselves, and "keep down below what they would otherwise rise to" sounds like a counterfactual loss, not teh "money pit" framing where empire actively made Britain poorer. Also Smith says right after this that the colonies' surplus produce is the original source of a big chunk of Europe's prosperity, which seems to flatly contradict "never contributed anything."

So: how fair is the blogger's summary? Go through his claims one by one, tell me which the passage (and the surrounding argument) actually supports, which are overstatements or conflations, and what Smith's real bottom-line recommendation on the colonies was — because I get the sense "just walk away" is doing a lot of work in that retelling.
