I am a PhD student in machine learning, and I am fluent in that field's working vocabulary: architectures and weight initializations, gradient-based optimization and black-box alternatives such as evolutionary strategies, reward functions, reinforcement learning and learning from human feedback, curriculum learning, exploration, and interpretability. I would like Alan Turing's proposals for educated machines restated in this vocabulary as faithfully as possible, followed by a report on what does not survive the translation.

The proposals I have in mind, paraphrased from his 1948 report Intelligent Machinery and the 1950 paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence: (1) Rather than designing an intelligent machine, begin with what he called an unorganized machine — a network of simple switching elements with largely arbitrary interconnections, equipped with devices that can strengthen, weaken, or disable links — and impose order through training rather than construction. (2) Educate this child machine by reward and punishment administered by a human teacher who has no access to its interior, must work entirely through its input-output interface, and ends up as ignorant of its trained internal state as a schoolteacher is of a pupil's neurons. (3) Understand the whole scheme on analogy with evolution: in his 1950 mapping, the machine's initial structure is the hereditary material, random alterations are mutations, and the experimenter's judgment about which variants to keep is selection; he took a purely evolutionary search to be a slow but genuine alternative to teaching. (4) Balance discipline against initiative: a machine trained to conform perfectly to its training signal could never do anything new, so education must preserve some capacity to depart from what the teacher rewards.

Please give the translation term by term, stating for each proposal what maps cleanly and onto what, what maps only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. For the residue, adjudicate whether the gap reflects a missing concept in modern machine learning or a vagueness in Turing. Two points I especially want settled: whether initiative reduces to exploration, and whether the designer-educator distinction he relied on survives in a pipeline where the same engineers set the architecture, the objective, and the data.
