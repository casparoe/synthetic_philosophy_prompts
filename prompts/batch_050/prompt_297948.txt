Two colleagues in my department — one a Kantian who holds that the universalizability tests of the Formula of Universal Law genuinely discriminate permissible from impermissible maxims, the other a critic who holds that the tests produce verdicts only when substantive assumptions about maxim description and the purposes of institutions are smuggled in — have agreed to stop arguing in the seminar room and produce a joint document instead. Draft that document: the joint statement of their adversarial collaboration. It should contain (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign — concerning what a maxim is, what the contradiction-in-conception and contradiction-in-the-will tests formally require, and which verdicts on stock cases (false promising, non-beneficence) both tests uncontroversially deliver or fail to deliver; (2) a disagreements table stating each residual dispute in wording each side would accept for its own position — I expect at least one disagreement about whether there is a principled, non-question-begging procedure for fixing the description of a maxim, and another about whether contradiction-in-the-will failures presuppose a teleology of rational willing or stand on their own; (3) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both parties agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of their long-running dispute turns out to be verbal — I suspect the phrase 'contradiction' means different things to each of them — identify which part and show the translation.
