This is something I keep arguing about with my brother and a couple guys from work, and I want to know if philosophers (or anyone) already have a term for it. Every time something big happens -- an election upset, a team winning a final nobody expected them to win, a startup collapsing -- the conversation afterwards is always full of people saying it was obviously going to happen. The signs were all there, the polls were off for understandable reasons, the company was doomed from day one. But the week before, the exact same people were saying it could go either way, and they weren't lying, because back then the same facts looked like open questions, not a countdown. Its like the moment something happens, everyone quietly rewrites the past so that the other outcomes were never really on the table, and anybody who says "it genuinely could have gone the other way" gets treated as naive. I know about hindsight bias, but from what I understand that's more about people misremembering their own predictions or being overconfident after the fact. What I'm talking about feels deeper -- it's not just that people forget they were uncertain, it's that they talk as if the uncertainty was never real, as if only the thing that actually happened was ever truly possible.

The reason this bugs me is it seems to connect to a mirror-image habit people have about the future. If I say "the coalition is going to lose the next election," people push back not just with "you don't know that" but with something stronger, like "nothing about the future is true yet, that's just a guess." As if no statement about what will happen can be true or false until it happens, at which point it retroactively becomes the thing that was always going to be true. So before the coin lands, "it'll land heads" is neither true nor false, and after it lands heads, apparently it never could have landed tails. Those two habits seem like the same mistake (if it is a mistake), just viewed from different ends. Is there a name for this whole pattern -- the way possibility gets treated as real only until the outcome is known and then gets erased backwards? And is it actually a mistake, or is there a respectable position that says this is just how truth and possibility work for events that haven't happened yet? If the specific thing I'm describing doesn't have its own name, I'd rather you tell me that and stake out what exactly it is and where it stops, than just hand me the nearest label.
