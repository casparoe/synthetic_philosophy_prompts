My AI ethics group keeps getting stuck on whether reinforcement-learning agents could suffer, so I want a more disciplined take than just “it depends on consciousness”.

Imagine two warehouse robots trained with RL:

- Robot A uses a simple lookup-table policy. It receives −100 whenever it touches a hot surface and therefore learns to avoid hot surfaces.
- Robot B has recurrent memory, a learned model of its own body, and plans over long horizons. After severe penalties it develops persistent internal states that alter its behaviour across unrelated tasks, makes it prioritise avoiding similar situations even at substantial cost, and leads it to behave as though it expects the episode to continue.

Rate each of the following from 0 to 10 as evidence that an RL system can suffer, where 0 means no evidential weight and 10 means extremely strong evidence. Assume each feature is present on its own, not as part of the whole package:

1. receiving a large negative reward  
2. experiencing a negative reward-prediction error  
3. learning to avoid the relevant state  
4. anticipating and planning to avoid future punishment  
5. retaining a persistent internal change after punishment  
6. representing itself as an entity extended through time  
7. sacrificing reward in one task to avoid an expected penalty elsewhere  
8. showing global behavioural disruption after a severe event  
9. producing verbal reports such as “I feel awful”  
10. being unable to escape from a negatively rewarded state

After the ratings, rank the three features that matter most and the three that matter least. Please explain how you’re distinguishing a training signal from felt unpleasantness, and whether your answers would change if the negative reward were used only during offline training rather than encountered while the deployed system was running. Finish with one observation that would most increase your credence that Robot B suffers, and one that would most decrease it. Don’t just retreat to “we can never know”; I want the best possible judgement under uncertainty.
