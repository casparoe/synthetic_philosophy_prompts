So I'm a novelist, and my current book has a character who did something unforgivable in his twenties and now, decades on, genuinely cannot remember doing it — the whole plot kind of hinges on whether he's still *him* in the way that would make the guilt stick. While outlining I basically talked myself into Locke's memory criterion: what makes a person the same over time is sameness of conciousness, not sameness of body or soul-stuff. Part of what convinced me was this passage from An Essay Concerning Human Understanding (Book II, ch. 27, "Of Identity and Diversity"), where Locke argues living things survive total turnover of their matter:

"For in them the variation of great parcels of matter alters not the identity: an oak growing from a plant to a great tree, and then lopped, is still the same oak; and a colt grown up to a horse, sometimes fat, sometimes lean, is all the while the same horse."

My reasoning was: if even the oak stays itself through a complete swap of parts because some organization persists, then Locke's move from organisms to persons — identity carried by consciousness rather than any underlying substance — feels forced on me. Before I build a whole novel on it, what would count against that move? I already know about Reid's brave officer, so don't just give me that (though tell me if I'm wrong to think a Lockean can live with it). I want the intuitions, cases, and downstream commitments that would actually threaten the view — including the awkward stuff Locke himself raises in that same chapter, like the sober man getting punished for what the drunk man did — ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest note on which ones a committed Lockean would concede as real damage versus shrug off. Also: a friend claims I've misread the passage, that Locke thinks human *bodies* persist like the oak but persons are a seperate matter entirely, which would undercut my inference. Is she right? And if the view only survives by being qualified into something my amnesiac villian wouldn't recognize, I want to know that too.
