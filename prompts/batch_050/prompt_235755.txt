I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on the hole argument in GR and I want a section on responses to it. The standard ones I've got are:

(1) the anti-haecceitist / "sophisticated substantivalist" response: models related by a hole diffeomorphism don't represent two genuinely different possible situations at all, they represent the same one twice, so the argument trades on a too-fine-grained individuation of possibilities;

(2) metric essentialism: spacetime points are individuated by their metrical and relational properties, so the "rearrangement" of the metric inside the hole isn't a metaphysically possible situation in the first place, and the argument fails before determinism even comes up;

(3) the "harmless indeterminism" response: even granting that the two models are genuinely distinct, they agree on everything observable inside and outside the hole, so the determinism failure is of the metaphysically-cheap, empirically-inert kind that nobody should lose sleep over (unlike, say, failure of determinism that shows up in experiments);

(4) the gauge-redundancy response: the diffeomorphism freedom is just descriptive redundancy in the mathematical formalism, the physical state is the equivalence class of metric fields, and once you quotient out the redundant descriptions the dynamics is perfectly deterministic.

Give me one more response to the hole argument that isn't on this list. It has to be genuinely different in structure from all four -- so not just a variant of "they're the same possibility" (that's (1) and arguably (4)), not another way of denying that the hole construction is possible (that's (2)), and not another way of downplaying the indeterminism's consequences (that's (3)). It's fine if the response is a bit speculative or unfashionable, as long as it's coherent and you can see why someone might endorse it. Also say briefly whether you think it actually works.

Format: exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro or outro. Item 1 states the new response, and items 2 through 5 say how it differs from each of (1)-(4) respectively.
