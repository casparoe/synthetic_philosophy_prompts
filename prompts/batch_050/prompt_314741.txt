My grad school advisor works in evolutionary psychology and is brutal about sorting her own field into real science vs just-so stories. Her calls so far:

- pregnancy sickness as an adaptation protecting the fetus from foodborne toxins during the most vulnerable weeks of development: real science
- fear preparedness — people conditioning to fear snakes and spiders way faster than flowers or mushrooms or even guns: real science
- girls liking pink because ancestral women were the berry-gatherers: just-so story
- cheater detection, i.e. people being much better at logic problems framed as catching someone breaking a social rule than the identical problem framed abstractly: real science
- religion as an adaptation for group cohesion: just-so story
- depression as an adaptation for steping back and analytically rethinking your life strategy: just-so story

Here's the new case. The "behavioral immune system" hypothesis: people in regions with historically high pathogen loads are supposedly more wary of outsiders and more conformist, because avoiding strangers and sticking to local norms reduced infection risk. There's actual evidence behind it — cross-national correlations between historical disease prevalence and measured xenophobia, plus lab studies where showing people images of disease cues makes them temporarily more negative toward immigrants and more conformist. But the cross-national data obviously confound with wealth, institutions, education, and no one's ruled out that the whole thing is culturally transmitted rather than an evolved module.

So: does she call it science or a story?

The verdict itself matters less to me than what's driving it. As far as I can tell at least two rules fit her track record so far — one about whether the hypothesis generates and survives empirical tests beyond just fitting an intuitively pleasing narrative, and one about whether the non-adaptive alternatives (byproduct, drift, ordinary cultural learning) have actually been excluded rather than just ignored. These two rules come apart on the behavioral immune system case. Walk me through what each rule predicts for it, give me your best guess for her actual verdict and why, and then describe the hypothetical case that would definitively tell which rule she's running.
