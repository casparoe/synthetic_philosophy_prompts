My closest friend from grad school spent seven years working on AI alignment and has now quit the field to open a bakery. I'm giving the toast at his farewell party, and the guest list is split awkwardly: half the room are alignment researchers who know the strategy-stealing assumption by heart (roughly, the claim that any strategy an unaligned AI might pursue can in principle be co-opted by an aligned one, so alignment need not be a competitive handicap), and the other half are his family and baking-school friends who have never heard the term. Write a five-minute toast built around the strategy-stealing assumption as a running joke -- for instance, teasing out whether his defection to pastry is a strategy the aligned team can simply steal, whether he's found the one plan no aligned agent can copy without losing what makes it good, or whether leaving was the dominant strategy all along. The jokes should turn on the actual content of the assumption (and on its known weaknesses) so the researchers catch real references, but the framing has to stay legible to the non-researchers, and the whole thing should land as affection rather than as a lecture or a recruitment pitch.
