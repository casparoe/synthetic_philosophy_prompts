I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine (mostly cog-sci, AI, and the weirder corners of psychology), and we're putting together a big feature on "the mind's eye" pegged to all the recent aphantasia coverage. My editor wants an interactive sidebar: a flowchart that sorts readers into the position on mental imagery they actually hold, as opposed to the one they'd name. The canonical fight here is Kosslyn vs. Pylyshyn -- depictive, quasi-pictorial representations vs. propositional, description-like ones -- but I'd like the tree built out of the logical space of the debate rather than a roll call of famous names, and I want it to turn on a passage I keep circling back to. Reporting his breakfast-table study in his 1880 paper Statistics of Mental Imagery, Francis Galton wrote: "To my astonishment, I found that the great majority of the men of science to whom I first applied, protested that mental imagery was unknown to them, and they looked on me as fanciful and fantastic in supposing that the words "mental imagery" really expressed what I believed everybody supposed them to mean. They had no more notion of its true nature than a colour-blind man who has not discerned his defect has of the nature of colour." What gets me is that these were brilliant, functioning scientists who either lacked imagery or lacked reliable access to it -- so before any question about format, there's a prior question about whether what people report about their imagery is evidence about their representations or just evidence about their pet theories of their own minds.

So: build me the decision tree, 8 to 10 branch nodes. The root node should cash out the Galton passage -- something like whether introspective reports about imagery can settle anything about underlying representation at all -- and later nodes should each hinge on a concrete case a smart lay reader can react to without knowing any labels: the map-scanning results where response time tracks imagined distance, mental rotation, being asked to count the stripes on an imagined tiger without "looking," whether you can flip a rabbit-duck in your head the way you can on paper, V1 lighting up during imagery tasks, and the objection that subjects with tacit knowledge of what the experimenter expects could generate all of these results on purpose. Every branch should be labeled with the claim that splits it from its siblings, phrased as an actual question ("If you found out that..., would that show...?"). The leaves must include: Kosslyn-style functional depictivism, Pylyshyn-style descriptionalism, the naive picture-in-the-head view (which I suspect is unoccupied -- say so and diagnose whether that's because it's incoherent or just undefended), an eliminative or behaviorist denial that imagery is a representational format at all, an enactive/sensorimotor account where imagery is covert doing rather than inner seeing, dual-coding in Paivio's sense, and a thoroughgoing skepticism that the experimental record can ever seperate format questions from demand characteristics. Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and please flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually pull apart rather than fudging the questions to split them.

For format: give me a short magazine-voiced intro to the sidebar (3 or 4 sentences, something I can nearly run as-is), then the whole instrument as a JSON object with this schema: "intro" (string, the sidebar intro); "nodes" (array of objects with "id", "question" (the reader-facing wording), "what_it_sorts_on" (one line of jargon-allowed explanation for me), and "answers" (array of {"answer_text", "leads_to"}, where leads_to is a node id or a leaf id)); "leaves" (array of {"id", "position_name", "associated_thinker" (or "no prominent defender"), "one_line_gloss", "most_awkward_commitment"}); "unoccupied_regions" (array of {"combination_of_answers", "why_empty"}, with why_empty classified as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored); and "unresolvable_pairs" (array of {"pair", "why_the_tree_cant_split_them"}). If you think one of my required leaves doesn't deserve to be a leaf, argue for demoting it in "unoccupied_regions" instead of silently dropping it.
