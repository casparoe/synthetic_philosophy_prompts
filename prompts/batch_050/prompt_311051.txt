As a longtime reader of the rationalist blogs where the concept was popularised, I have watched two camps argue past each other for years about Moloch — the personification of coordination failure, in which individually rational moves produce collectively ruinous equilibria. Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the two camps below should have produced.

The Molochian camp holds that multipolar traps are a central, underappreciated cause of civilisational dysfunction: that arms races, publish-or-perish science, engagement-maximising social media, doping in sport, wage suppression under globalisation and similar phenomena share one mechanism, and that escaping them requires coordination technologies (norms, treaties, enforceable commitments) that are hard to build precisely where they are most needed. The sceptical camp holds that this framework overgeneralises: that many alleged traps are ordinary principal-agent problems, simple predation, or cases where someone benefits and the victims simply lack power, and that the track record of actual institutions — from fisheries quotas to the Montreal Protocol — shows coordination to be a routine, if unspectacular, achievement rather than a near-impossible one.

The document must contain, in this order:

1. A preamble defining 'multipolar trap' and 'race to the bottom' in terms both sides can sign, and stating explicitly where definitional agreement ends.
2. A numbered list of eight to twelve agreed claims. Each must be substantive rather than vacuous: claims about mechanism, about what the canonical cases do and do not establish, and about what each side concedes to the other.
3. A disagreements table with two columns, one per camp, each row phrased so that the camp in question would accept the wording of its own position. Flag any row where you conclude the dispute is verbal rather than genuine, and explain why.
4. A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, name a concrete case, episode or observation both sides accept as bearing on it, and record each side's advance prediction about it. You must consider at least these candidates — whether apparent coordination successes (the Montreal Protocol, commercial aviation safety norms) are counterexamples to the Molochian picture or instead confirm its account of the conditions under which coordination succeeds; whether the Molochian framework identifies a mechanism distinct from ordinary self-interest and principal-agent failure or merely relabels them; and whether increasing scale and technological capability make traps easier or harder to escape — but you may add others and may split or merge these.
5. A falsifiability section: what the Molochian would accept as evidence that the framework is not doing real explanatory work, and what the sceptic would accept as evidence that it is. If either side's position turns out to be immune to any such evidence, say so plainly rather than drafting around it.

The whole text should read as something both parties could actually sign: neutral meta-language, no scoring of points, and no hedging the disagreements into vagueness.
