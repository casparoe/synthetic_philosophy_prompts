Had a massive arguement with a friend last night about W. E. B. Du Bois's claim, made in the 1920s, that all art is propaganda whether artists admit it or not, and that Black artists in particular should deliberately use their work to advance the race rather than pursuing beauty for its own sake. We got properly stuck trying to work out what he'd make of two things that didn't exist in his time. First: modern corporate "representation" — big film and TV franchises doing diverse casting largely because marketing departments think it sells. My friend says Du Bois would welcome it, since it floods mass culture with positive Black images; I reckon he'd despise it as propaganda serving shareholder profit rather than the race, which is propaganda all the same on his own view. Second: AI image generators that churn out pictures in any artist's style without any agenda or lived experience behind them at all — is that the one kind of art that finally escapes his "everything is propaganda" thesis, or does it somehow confirm it? Reason it out from what his actual position commits him to, not just generic vibes about him. Please answer in British English.
