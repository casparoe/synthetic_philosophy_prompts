Me and some friends have been arguing about mind uploading and I want to check my position actually hangs together before I put it in a blog post. Here's roughly where I've landed: (1) I'm basically Parfitian about survival — numerical identity isn't what matters, psychological continuity with any reliable cause is enough, and fission (both branches waking up) is a good outcome, not death. (2) I'm a causal-role functionalist: what makes something have my experiences is that it has the right causal organisation, not the stuff it's made of. (3) Gradual replacement — swapping my neurons one at a time for functionally identical silicon while I stay awake and lucid the whole way through — clearly preserves me, since there's never an interruption. (4) One-shot destructive uploading (scan my brain, vaporise it, run the emulation) I would refuse: the emulation is a copy and I'd die, because there's no continuous process connecting me to it, only shared information. (5) A lookup table that merely plays back my recorded behaviour isn't conscious — there's no actual thinking going on in there.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each one what I'd have to give up and what that costs, and then tell me the single cheapest revision that fixes the most. Be specific — don't just say it depends on my theory of personal identity.
