Two colleagues in my department have argued for years about "performance legitimacy": the claim that sustained prosperity, public order, and competent administration can by themselves make authoritarian rule legitimate, with Singapore and China as the standard exhibits. One of them defends this claim, at least as a pro tanto ground of legitimacy; the other holds that legitimacy is conferred by procedures -- consent, accountability, the ruled having a say -- and that good outcomes, however real, cannot substitute for these. Draft the joint statement they would produce if forced into an adversarial collaboration. It should contain: (1) the claims both can sign, about what the Singaporean and Chinese cases actually show and about what work the concept of legitimacy is supposed to do; (2) the residual disagreements, each stated in wording the relevant side would accept for its own position, with genuinely normative disputes separated from empirical ones; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side -- which must include at least one counterfactual crux (for example, what would follow for each view from a decade of stagnation under continued authoritarian rule, or from a disorderly but prosperous democracy); and (4) for each crux, a concrete observation or thought experiment both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If you judge that part of their dispute is verbal rather than substantive, identify which part and say why.
