I'm a novelist, and a historian friend read my new manuscript and told me the middle section is "just one damn thing after another" — stuff happens, characters react, more stuff happens, but nothing, he claims, is actually explained. My writing group loved those same chapters, so I'm not taking his word for it, but the phrase bugs me, especially since people also say it about history itself. Lay out the different precise claims someone could be making with it: that historical events don't fall under any general laws, that chronology isn't causation, that the explanatory links in any narrative are retrofitted by the teller rather than found in the events, that some domains admit only description and never explanation — whatever else is hiding in there. For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a live philosophical position, or just false, and which reading most plausibly captures what my friend thinks is wrong with the chapters — concretely, what would have to change in the manuscript for it to stop being one damn thing after another? And a side question: my old writing teacher used to distinguish a mere sequence of events (the king dies, then the queen dies) from a real plot (the king dies, then the queen dies of grief) — is that the same distinction my historian friend is reaching for, or a different one dressed up in the same clothes?
