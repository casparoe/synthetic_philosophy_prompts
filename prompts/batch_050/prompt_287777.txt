I work in causal inference — Pearl-style: DAGs, the do-operator, interventions, the whole abduction-action-prediction recipe for counterfactuals — and I keep bouncing off the philosophy of language stuff on conditionals. Philosophers split conditionals into indicatives (if Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did) and counterfactuals (if Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have) and treat them as needing totally different theories. Lewis builds everything on a similarity ordering over possible worlds, Stalnaker has his own version, and then there's this line from Adams that indicative conditionals don't even have truth values, just assertability conditions, which as far as I can tell has no analogue in anything I do.

So: restate this debate in my vocabulary, as precisely as you can. My working hypothesis is that the translation is trivial — indicatives are conditioning on evidence, counterfactuals are do-surgery on a structural causal model — and that the philosophers are overcomplicating it. Run that translation honestly and then give me the residue report:

1. What maps cleanly? I expect the Oswald pair to translate perfectly into conditioning vs. intervention. If it doesn't, that's important and I want to know.
2. What translates only with distortion? Lewis's similarity relation is the big one for me. An SCM just computes the counterfactual from the structural equations; there's no similarity metric anywhere. Is the similarity relation doing real work that my framework silently presupposes — like the decision about which exogenous variables to hold fixed during abduction, or how coarse-grained the model is — or is it surplus machinery?
3. What can't be expressed at all? Two things worry me. Backtracking cases: if the gas gauge had read empty, the tank would have been empty. That's effect-to-cause reasoning, fine as conditioning, incoherent as an intervention on the gauge. And Adams's claim that indicatives aren't truth-apt — is there even a slot for "assertable but not true/false" in a probabilistic-causal picture, or does my framework just beg the question against him by definition?

And the meta-question: when residue piles up like this, how do I tell whether it's a defect of the causal-inference framework or a sign the philosophers' category is less clear than it looks? I suspect part of the indicative/counterfactual distinction is just grammar doing cognitive work, but I'm aware I might be wearing causal goggles and seeing everything as a DAG.
