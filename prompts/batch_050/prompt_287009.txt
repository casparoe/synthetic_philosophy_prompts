Student's answer: "Szilard's engine reduces Maxwell's setup to a single gas molecule in a box. The demon inserts a partition, measures which half of the box the molecule occupies, couples a piston and weight to that side, and lets the molecule expand isothermally, thereby extracting kT ln 2 of work from the heat bath per cycle. Since this could in principle be repeated indefinitely while converting heat from a single bath entirely into work, it appears to violate the second law — and it sharpens Maxwell's version because the paradox no longer depends on the demon's skill at sorting many fast and slow molecules, only on its possessing one bit of information. Landauer's principle states that erasing one bit of information in a device at temperature T dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat into the environment. Bennett argued that the demon must store its measurement outcome in a memory register, and that it is precisely the act of measurement where the kT ln 2 cost is incurred, since acquiring a bit necessarily dissipates that much energy. Because the demon's memory is finite, it must eventually be erased to complete the cycle, and this erasure cost at least compensates for the work extracted. Hence the second law is saved: the engine together with the demon's memory, taken as a whole, does no net work from a single bath. This also shows that real computers have a fundamental energy floor: every logical operation a computer performs, whether logically reversible or not, incurs a minimum dissipation of kT ln 2 per bit, which is the deep sense in which information is physical."
