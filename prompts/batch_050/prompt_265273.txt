Had a long argument with a friend last night about whether love needs to be based on reasons, and we got genuinely stuck. I won't say which of us took which side, because I want an honest verdict.

Side one: love that isn't grounded in the beloved's actual qualities -- their kindness, humor, integrity, whatever -- is basically arbitrary. It's no better than loving someone because a potion made you, and you couldn't even explain to yourself why you love them.

Side two: if love is grounded in qualities, then consistency seems to demand that you "trade up" the moment you meet someone who has more of those qualities, and stop loving your actual partner if they change. That seems obviously not how love works, so love must not be a response to the person's qualities at all -- it just bestows value on them rather than appraising it.

Side one replied: fine, but the reasons that matter don't have to be the person's qualities -- they can be facts about the relationship itself, like the shared history, that this is the person you built a life with. No trading-up problem, because nobody else has that history.

Side two came back: that proves too much. It would make even a miserable relationship with a genuinely awful person self-justifying just because its been going on a long time.

Then side one: but grounding love purely in qualities means a perfect duplicate of your partner -- say an exact android copy, same personality, same memories -- would be equally lovable on the spot, and nobody actually believes that.

At that point we just went in circles. Can you adjudicate this properly? Who's right about each specific move -- the trading-up objection, the shared-history reply, the proves-too-much counter, the duplicate argument? And is there something we're both missing about what "reasons for love" even are?
