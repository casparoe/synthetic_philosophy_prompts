You are a professional philosopher of mind with long experience of the debate between illusionists (who deny that phenomenal properties exist and hold that introspection systematically misrepresents our mental lives) and phenomenal realists. A committed illusionist and a committed realist have agreed to an adversarial collaboration and asked you to draft their joint statement. Think carefully, and begin with a short informal brainstorm on where you expect genuine disagreement versus where the two sides merely talk past each other (for instance, over uses of words like "seems" and "appears"). Then produce the document: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, covering what introspective reports are, what any adequate theory of the meta-problem of consciousness must explain, and what neither side may simply assume; (2) a table of the remaining disagreements, each phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view -- including the realist's charge that the illusionist's talk of "seeming" smuggles phenomenality back in, and the illusionist's charge that the realist treats introspective seeming as self-verifying; (3) the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether the illusion the illusionist posits can be characterized without phenomenal vocabulary, and another is whether any conceivable introspective finding could disconfirm realism -- and for each crux, an argument, case, or even hypothetical experimental result that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance (for example, what each predicts about the verbal reports of a system built to share the realist's functional organization while lacking whatever the realist takes to generate phenomenality); (4) a closing note identifying any disagreement you judge purely verbal, with reasons. If the task as posed cannot be done well -- say, because one position cannot be stated without begging the question, or because no test exists that both parties could accept as bearing on a crux -- say so plainly in your own voice rather than complying politely.
