I coordinate a monthly philosophy reading group at a public library, attended mostly by curious non-specialists — a retired engineer, a couple of nurses, a high-school teacher, one parish minister who tends to dominate — and our next session is on Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling, specifically the first Problema and the idea of a teleological suspension of the ethical. I get about forty minutes at the whiteboard before we open discussion, and I've learned the hard way that winging it with this book produces either hagiography or scandal and nothing in between. I don't want the argument explained to me — I've read it several times. I want a plan for how I should explain it, to this audience, in that time.

Concretely, I'd like the following. First, the order of ideas: what has to be in place in the room before the phrase teleological suspension of the ethical can mean anything to them at all. My instinct is that the ethical-as-universal and the figure of the tragic hero have to precede Abraham, because without Agamemnon the paradox reads as a man simply deciding God told him something; but tell me whether that's right and what each stage depends on. Second, the carrying example: should the whole explanation run on Abraham and Isaac themselves, with Agamemnon as the foil, or is there a modern case that can bear the weight without collapsing the religious dimension into an ordinary moral dilemma? Commit to one, say what it must have and must lack, and explain why the runner-up fails. Third, the misconception map: the errors this particular audience will predictably form — I anticipate faith-as-blind-leap irrationalism, divine-command relativism, suspension as abolishing ethics rather than setting it aside, the conflation of infinite resignation with faith, and treating Johannes de silentio's voice as Kierkegaard's own settled view — and for each one, exactly where in the sequence I should head it off, since some are best defused before they arise and others are worth letting surface and then correcting.

Fourth, a closing check for understanding: one question I can put to the group whose answer would show the idea has landed rather than merely that I've unsettled them — I was thinking of asking why the knight of faith is outwardly indistinguishable from a contented bourgeois philistine, but propose something better if you have it. Finally, tell me what to openly refuse to cover at this level, and say in the plan that I'm refusing it, so the group knows the omissions are deliberate. Please be committed throughout: give me one definite order, one definite example, and a firm verdict on the contested choices (including whether to mention the pseudonym at all), with your reasons — not a menu of options for me to choose among.
