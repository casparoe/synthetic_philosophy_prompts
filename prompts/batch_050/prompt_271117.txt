So I run a small monthly "comedy theory" night with some friends from my old grad program (don't laugh) and we've gotten bored of the same four-box chart everyone knows: superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation. Every time we try to sort an actual bit of comedy into one of them it either fits three boxes or none, and we end up arguing about the labels instead of the jokes. I want to make a proper decision-tree handout for the group, but built the right way: start from the diagnostic questions, not from the named theories, and let the theories fall out as leaves.

Here's what I need. A decision tree that starts from "this thing made someone laugh (or was meant to)" and ends at one of the four classic theories, plus at least one 'none of the above / unnamed' leaf for combinations the tradition doesn't cover. Each branch node should be a concrete question someone can answer about a case WITHOUT knowing any theory -- things like: does the case still work if the audience feels no superiority or status shift toward anyone? Does the laughter survive the incongruity being fully explained or resolved? Would the thing be funnier if the stakes were higher rather than lower, or does it need the violation to be safe? Use tickling, contagious/yawning-style laughter, puns (incongruity with arguably no resolution), laughing at your own past embarrassing self, and laughter at funerals as recurring test cases, since I think those five actually pull the theories apart better than the usual "stupid boss slips on banana peel" stuff.

A few more constraints: phrase every node as a yes/no or either/or question, aim for 8-12 leaves total, and at the end (a) flag any pairs of leaves your tree can't genuinely separate with these questions -- I suspect relief theory and benign violation collapse into each other on taboo-joke cases, but tell me if I'm wrong -- and (b) for each unoccupied combination of answers, one sentence on whether that slot is empty because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Also add a short note on what the tree does with laughter that has nothing to do with humor at all (nervous laughter, tickling), since half our arguments are about whether those even count. Keep the whole thing to one page if printed.
