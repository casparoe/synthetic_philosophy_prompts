My two housemates are both in a phil mind seminar and they got into it in the kitchen last night over Dennett's Quining Qualia, specifically the bit with the two coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn. Quick setup as I understand it: both have been tasters for the company for years, and both now say the coffee doesn't taste to them the way it used to. Chase says the taste itself is exactly the same, he's just stopped liking it. Sanborn says no, something in his mouth or brain changed, so the coffee literally tastes different now. Dennett's point (I think) is that nothing could settle which of them is right, not even for the tasters themselves, and that's supposed to be bad news for qualia.

Here's roughly how the argument went:

1. Maya (pro-Dennett): the case shows there's no fact of the matter about whether Sanborn's quale changed. Any test you run -- memory comparisons, discrimination tasks, just asking him -- conflates the quale with his judgments and memories about it. So "the way the coffee tastes to him" isn't a real intrinsic property floating free of all that. Qualia, as usually posited, are confused.

2. Leo: way too strong. There IS a fact -- either his receptor physiology changed or his evaluative attitudes did, and those are different brain states. A good enough neuroscientist could tell them apart. Currently-undetectable isn't nonexistent.

3. Maya: you're missing the structure. Even a perfect neuroscientist can't seperate "quale changed, judgment same" from "judgment changed, quale same," because Sanborn's own access doesn't survive scrutiny either -- his memory of how it used to taste is just another current state and might be wrong. The quale is supposed to be what's left when you subtract all that, and nothing is left.

4. Leo: then at most you've shown our access to qualia is fallible, not that qualia don't exist. I can misremember how bad last week's headache was, but the headache still hurt exactly as much as it hurt. You've defined qualia apart from every possible evidence for them and then complained there's no evidence. Rigged game.

5. Maya: a property that makes no difference to anything -- not behavior, not memory, not any possible measurement, not even the subject's sincere say-so -- isn't a property, it's a noise word. That's just what it takes for a posit to earn its keep.

6. Leo: no, the quale makes a difference to the subject, just not a publicly checkable one, and first-person difference is still a difference. Your standard throws out the actual hurting of the headache along with the misremembering, and you should at least admit that's the bullet you're biting.
