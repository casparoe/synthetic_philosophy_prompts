I'm a novelist, and I keep circling an idea I can't crack, so I want you to write the short story I can't. It's about tangled hierarchies — think of Escher's Drawing Hands, where each hand is busy drawing the other into existance, except that Escher's real hand sits outside the loop on some inviolate level that the drawing can never reach or revise. Here's the thing though: I don't want the usual metafiction move where a character becomes aware of the author. That's been done to death and honestly it's the cheap version. The interesting part, philosophically, is the inviolate level — the level that participates in the tangle in some sense (it drew it, after all) but can never be drawn back by it. So write me a story where the characters are caught in a genuine loop (they create or control each other somehow, no author stand-ins allowed), and the real drama comes from them discovering that exactly one thing in their world cannot be pulled into the loop, and fighting over what to do about it — whether to worship it, destroy it, hide it, or use it. Make the inviolate thing something surprising, not a god and not a mirror of the reader. Structure the response as exactly five numbered scenes, a short paragraph each, with no introduction before them and no concluding note after.
