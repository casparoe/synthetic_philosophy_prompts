I'm a retired cell physiologist who has been reading philosophy of biology in my spare time, and I've basically been won over by the etiological account of function -- roughly, that the function of a trait is whatever it did that explains why it was selected for and stuck around. It appeals to me because it explains how a malformed organ can still have a function it fails to perform, which always bugged me about the simpler "function = what the part actually does in the system" story (I gather philosophers call that the causal-role account).

Act as a professional philosopher of biology and think this through carefully -- maybe brainstorm candidate objections before settling on the strong ones. Rank the defeaters from potentially fatal down to merely awkward. If the view turns out to be insulated against everything by qualifications, say so and tell me whether that's a vice or just what a good theory looks like.
