For several years I have followed alignment discussions on LessWrong and adjacent forums, and there is one one-liner that keeps surfacing whenever a conversation turns to reinforcement learning: "reward is not the optimization target." What prompted this question is that I recently saw it deployed in opposite directions within the same month. In one thread, someone worried that a sufficiently capable model might interfere with its own reward channel — tamper with the logs, manipulate the humans who assign scores, seize whatever computes the signal — and a commenter dismissed the concern outright with the slogan, as though it showed that no trained system could ever be motivated toward such behavior. In another thread, a newcomer suggested that since we control the reward function, getting the AI to want what we want is mostly a matter of rewarding the right behaviors, and the very same slogan was invoked to explain why that picture is naive. I found myself nodding along both times, which I take as a warning sign that the sentence is doing more work than any single claim could bear.

So: what precise claims could that one-liner actually be making? Please lay the readings out as a menu, ordered from the most deflationary to the most radical. I suspect there is a near-tautological reading on which a scalar in a training loop is simply not the sort of thing that can be anyone's target; a mechanistic reading about what gradient descent actually installs in a policy — behavioral dispositions and internal representations shaped by, but not necessarily pointing at, the training signal; an empirical reading about which objectives trained agents in fact end up pursuing, and under what conditions a proxy that was correlated with reward during training comes apart from it in deployment; and perhaps a very strong reading on which reward-directed motives are in principle impossible for systems built this way. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, genuinely contentious, or straightforwardly false, and give the strongest argument available for whichever readings deserve to be taken seriously.

Then the verdicts I actually need. First: which reading, if any, licenses the dismissal of the reward-tampering concern in the first thread? My suspicion is that the dismissal only goes through on a reading strong enough to be false, since a system that had learned to value reward itself, or some close correlate of it, would stand as a counterexample — but I want this argued, not asserted. Second: which reading is doing the real work in the reply to the newcomer, and does that reading also tell us anything about whether reward tampering should be expected or ruled out? If the two uses of the slogan turn out to require different readings, and one of them is indefensible, please state that plainly rather than rescuing the slogan by sliding between them.
