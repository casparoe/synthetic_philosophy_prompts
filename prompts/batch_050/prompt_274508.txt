You are a philosopher of science equally at home in the metaphysics of dispositions and in the structural causal modeling framework (DAGs, structural equations, interventions). Take the standard debate about the Simple Conditional Analysis of dispositions — the claim that an object's being fragile just consists in the truth of the counterfactual "if it were struck, it would break," together with Martin-style finks (mechanisms that remove the disposition precisely when the stimulus occurs) and Johnston-style masks (cases where the stimulus occurs, the disposition remains, but the manifestation is blocked) — and restate the whole debate in the vocabulary of structural causal models, as faithfully as you can. Think through it carefully before writing: sketch the candidate translations first, then give the polished version. In particular: what does a disposition ascription become when rendered as a structural equation plus a claim about do-interventions? Can a fink be expressed in a static SCM, or does it require a mechanism that changes under the very intervention meant to test it — and if so, which modularity or autonomy assumption does that violate? How do masks differ from finks in this vocabulary, or do they collapse into the same structure? Then give the residue report: which moves in the metaphysics debate translate cleanly, which survive only under distortion, and which have no expression at all in the SCM framework. For the residue, take a position on whether the structural causal vocabulary is missing something the metaphysicians are tracking, or whether the untranslatable parts of the debate are artifacts of the counterfactual idiom that the causal-modeling framework rightly forces us to drop.
