I'm a novelist working on a thriller and I've written myself into a corner that's actually starting to keep me up at night. My protagonist gets an offer from an eccentric billionaire: at midnight tonight, some machine in his house (it's a thriller, don't worry about the tech) reads her state of mind. If, at that exact moment, she genuinely intends to drink a small vial of liquid the next morning, she gets ten million dollars. The liquid isn't poison or anything, it'll just make her violently ill for a day. And here's the catch: nobody ever checks whether she actually drinks it. The money gets wired the second the machine confirms the intention was real at midnight. After that she's completely free to pour the vial down the drain.

I cannot figure out what she should do, or even what she *can* do, and the whole plot hangs on it. Once midnight passes and the money's in her account, drinking the vial is just pointless misery. She'll have no reason at all to do it. But she knows that in advance, at midnight. So how can she really, sincerely intend to do something she already knows future-her will have zero reason to do? Wouldn't the machine catch that her "intention" is fake, or at least hollow? And if the only way to win is to somehow be the kind of person who actually would drink it, is she better off trying to become that person, or just walking away?

A freind of mine told me this is the same problem as every new year's resolution ever — intending to do something only seems to make sense if you'll actually have reason to do it when the time comes. Which makes me think the billionaire's offer might secretly be unwinnable, i.e. the twist.

So: what do I have her do? Is there any way a rational person pulls this off — sorry, intends this off? And if she can't, is the flaw in her or in the offer? I need the scene to hold up for readers who think way too hard about this kind of thing, and right now I honestly can't tell who's supposed to be irrational in it.
