I help run a small reading group on interpretability and philosophy of science, and we've got properly stuck on something that sounds simple. The rough idea behind mechanistic anomaly detection, at least as we're using the term, is that you flag an input as anomalous when the system processes it via a *different mechanism* from the one it used on the training distribution -- not just when the output looks odd or the input sits far from the training data in some embedding space. That obviously presupposes an account of when two computations count as the same mechanism, and it turns out nobody in the group agrees on one.

Here's our list of candidate criteria so far: (1) functional equivalence -- same input-output mapping over the relevant domain; (2) componential identity -- the same parts performing the same operations, organised the same way, roughly the picture you get from the mechanisms literature in philosophy of neuroscience; (3) algorithmic equivalence at Marr's middle level -- the same abstract procedure, even if the low-level implementation differs; and (4) interventionist equivalence -- the same pattern of counterfactual dependence, i.e. intervening on corresponding internal variables produces corresponding downstream effects in both. Each has a known problem (a lookup table is functionally equivalent to an algorithm it mimics; componential identity seems to make every fine-tuning step a mechanism change; and so on), but we're not asking which is right.

What I'd like is a fifth criterion that is genuinely different from these four -- not a weighted hybrid, not one of them with a similarity threshold bolted on -- and that classifies at least one pair of computations differently from *all four* of the ones above. Bonus if you can describe that pair concretely. And one worry I'd like addressed: if what counts as 'the same mechanism' turns out to be interest-relative (same for the purposes of safety auditing, different for the purposes of understanding generalisation), does that quietly sink the whole anomaly-detection project, or can it survive with a plurality of criteria? Our group is split on this and I'd like an outside view before next week's session.
