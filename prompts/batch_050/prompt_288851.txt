I never studied philosophy or science formally, but I read a lot on my own and lately I got hooked on this question of whether the higher-level sciences are really independent of physics or just physics wearing a hat. Along the way I read this old book, John Stuart Mill's A System of Logic (1843), specifically the chapter in Book III on the composition of causes. Mill's idea, as I understood it, is that sometimes when several causes act together the total effect is just the sum of what each would do alone, like forces in mechanics, but other times, his examples were chemical reactions and living bodies, the combined effect is a different kind of thing altogether from any sum of the ingredients, and he claimed the laws governing those cases (he calls them heteropathic) can't be deduced from the laws of the parts, no matter how much you know about the parts.

So here is roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it hangs together:

1. Everything is made of the same basic physical stuff. When atoms form a frog nothing extra or spooky gets added, there's no life-force, it's atoms arranged a certain way, full stop.
2. Physics is closed at the bottom. Every physical event is fully accounted for by prior physical goings-on, so there are no gaps for a mind or a vital principle to reach in and push anything.
3. Mill is basically right, and not just as a historical curiosity: chemistry and biology state real laws, not just summaries of physics we haven't bothered to finish, and some of them could not be derived from physics alone even in principle.
4. Emergent properties are real (temperature, liquidity, being alive), but downward causation, meaning the emergent thing reaching down and pushing its own atoms around, is incoherent.
5. If you fixed every physical fact you'd fix every fact, period. There's no difference anywhere without a physical difference.
6. Some reductions genuinely go through. Temperature really is just the average kinetic energy of molecules, case closed, and I don't see why anyone makes a fuss about that one.
