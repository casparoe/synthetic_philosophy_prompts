For the past few years I have sat on the management committee of our residents' association, and I have become quietly fascinated by something I have never seen discussed anywhere, though I assume it must be familiar to people who study voting. We decide matters by taking each proposal separately, and I have noticed that the committee as a body can end up committed to things that no individual member believes. Last spring, for instance, a majority held that the lift must be repaired, a majority held that we may only undertake works we can fund without raising the service charge, and a majority refused to raise the service charge — three different majorities, mind you, so that the association was officially resolved to do something it had officially resolved to be impossible, while every one of us around the table was perfectly consistent in his or her own votes.

I would like a satirical piece that takes this seriously. Write the welcome pack issued to new members of the (fictional) committee of a housing co-operative whose constitution enshrines clause-by-clause majority voting and whose officers have long since made their peace with the results. Around 600 words, in deadpan institutional prose — the voice of a secretary who has stopped finding any of this odd, with sections on how to cast your ballot, what to do when the minutes contradict themselves, and a brief history of the society's proudest resolutions. The humour should come from the real mechanics rather than invented absurdity: each member is consistent, the collective is not, and every available fix (letting the chair decide, voting only on the final question, demanding larger majorities, appointing a subcommittee to settle things) costs something the pack quietly concedes in passing. Never give the problem a technical name, because the institution hasn't got one; but a reader who knows anything about collective decision-making should recognise exactly what is being satirised and find the satire fair. By the end, the reader should feel why this matters for any group that governs by vote, and not merely for ours.
