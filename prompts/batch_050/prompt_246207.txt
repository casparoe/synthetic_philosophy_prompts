ok so i need help with a toast and its a tricky one. my oldest friend mira is 31 and has been Deaf her whole life -- grew up in Deaf schools, signs as her first language, half her social world is the local Deaf community. next month she's getting a cochlear implant, and her wife is throwing her a big going-away party the weekend before the surgery. i've been asked to give a toast, maybe 5 minutes.

the room is going to be genuinely split. about half are her Deaf friends, a couple of whom think implants are a kind of betrayal or at least a slow goodbye to the community, and they'll be watching through an interpreter. the other half are her hearing coworkers and her parents, who think this is straightforwardly wonderful news and keep saying stuff like "finally she'll get to hear music!" which already caused one awkward silence at dinner last week.

heres the thing that makes it hard: mira herself keeps saying that she has no idea what hearing will be like and can't find out until she does it, and that she's doing it anyway. she says everyone asking her "but will you be happier?" is asking the wrong question, because theres no way for her to know in advance, and no way for the her-after to be compared to the her-now by the person making the decision. so the toast cant promise she'll love it, cant say "you'll still be you" (she's not sure she will be, in some respects!), and also cant treat the party like a funeral, which is how two of her Deaf friends are privately treating it.

please write the toast. it should be honest about the fact that she's stepping into something no one in the room -- including her -- can evaluate from the outside, without that turning into either a lecture or a downer. it needs to land as love for both halves of the room, and not say anything mira would consider false. i want it to end on something people can actually raise a glass to, not just a shrug.
