A friend of mine — amateur ancient-history nut, reads everything, has very firm opinions — has this running thing about which legends have a real historical kernel and which ones don't. We've been keeping score of her verdicts for years at this point. Here's the track record:

- Trojan War: real. Not Homer literally, but she's confident there was an actual conflict at Troy/Hisarlik, and she points to the Hittite texts mentioning Wilusa and the destruction layers at the site.
- King Arthur: nope, no Arthur. She'll grudgingly allow the stories might garble some post-Roman British war leaders, but she won't commit to any specific prototype.
- Exodus: didn't happen as told. No plagues, no mass migration out of Egypt. At most some small Semitic group whose story got woven in later.
- Gilgamesh: probably a real king of Uruk, adventures pure fiction. She points to the Sumerian king lists and to his rival Agga showing up in early poems too.
- Beowulf: hero invented, background real. She loves that Hygelac is attested by Gregory of Tours and that the ship burials and material culture check out.
- Amazons: no warrior-woman nation, but she buys that the Greeks were riffing on real steppe women who fought and got buried with weapons.
- Odysseus's wanderings: pure storytelling, don't even ask.

Last week over beers it occured to me to test whether she's actually running a consistent rule, because three new cases came up:

1. Yu the Great and the Great Flood of China — the legendary engineer-king who supposedly tamed a massive Yellow River flood and founded the Xia dynasty. Some geologists have claimed to find evidence of a giant outburst flood in the right region at roughly the right time.
2. King Minos of Crete — the palace at Knossos is real, the bull-leaping frescoes are real, but what about Minos himself, or the story that Athens had to send tribute of youths to Crete until Theseus ended it?
3. Atlantis — Plato's sunken naval power. I'm including this one mostly to see whether her rule spits out anything interesting or just "lol no."

I'm less interested in the "right" historical answers than in the prediction.
