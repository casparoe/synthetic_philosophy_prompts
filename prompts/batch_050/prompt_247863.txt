I'm a retired geologist (structural, mostly) who's spent the last couple of years of retirement reading philosophy, and I keep circling the meta-question of whether philosophy makes progress. Not the question itself — there's plenty written on that — but the debate about the question, which from the outside looks totally stalled.

For context: in my old field we had the continental drift fight, and it ended. The seafloor spreading evidence came in, the holdouts died or retired, and nobody teaches fixed continents anymore. So my scientist's instinct is that a healthy field converges on things. The pessimists about philosophy seem to share that instinct and point at the record: free will, conciousness, moral realism still wide open after two thousand years give or take, and that big survey of professional philosophers showed nothing like the convergence you'd see in a settled science. The optimists answer with a different list — formal logic, probability theory, the post-Gettier literature on knowledge, the sciences (physics, psychology, linguistics) that used to be philosophy and left. They also say the goal was never verdicts but cost-accounting: by now we know exactly what each position on free will has to swallow, and mapping that is a kind of progress even if nobody converts.

My working hypothesis, for what its worth, is that this isn't really a factual disagreement but two different yardsticks — convergence versus clarification — plus a selection effect: if philosophy is just the residue of questions nobody has yet figured out how to settle, then of course what's left shows no convergence, and the pessimists are measuring a sampling artifact. One thing that genuinely bugs me though: whether philosophy makes progress is itself a philosophical question, so this debate's failure to converge looks like evidence for the pessimists, which feels self-sealing, and maybe means the optimists are in structural trouble from the start. I might be confused about that.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? A hidden factual question about the history (did the sciences really spin off from philosophy, or is that a myth philosophers tell themselves)? A normative question about what philosophy is for? Or is it ill-posed? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress on it — a distinction between kinds of progress, a proper convergence metric, some real historical work — or should I expect it to stay stalled forever, and why?
