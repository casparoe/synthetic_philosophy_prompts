For my school's philosophy society we have been set a debate on mind uploading, and my motion partner has built our opening case almost entirely around a passage from John Locke's An Essay Concerning Human Understanding (Book II, Chapter XXVII, 'Of Identity and Diversity'). Her argument runs like this: Locke showed that a living thing survives the total turnover of its matter provided the organisation and life carry on uninterruptedly; a brain whose neurons were swapped, one by one, for synthetic parts doing exactly the same job would be in just that position; so the resulting being would still be you, and gradual uploading is safe. She read the passage aloud as though it settled the matter:

"In the state of living creatures, their identity depends not on a mass of the same particles, but on something else. For in them the variation of great parcels of matter alters not the identity: an oak growing from a plant to a great tree, and then lopped, is still the same oak; and a colt grown up to a horse, sometimes fat, sometimes lean, is all the while the same horse"

I have a nagging feeling the analogy is being stretched well past what Locke actually claimed. Could you audit it properly: map explicitly what is meant to correspond to what (oak to brain, sap and cells to neurons, one continued life to one continued stream of experience, and so on), and then say where the mapping breaks? Points I would like tested include whether Locke's criterion of 'one continued life' really survives translation into functional or psychological terms; whether it matters that an oak's cells are interchangeable in a way a neuron's precise connections perhaps are not; whether synthetic parts count as the kind of continuation Locke had in mind; and whether Locke's own distinction between the identity of the 'man' and the identity of the 'person' undercuts my partner's use of him rather than supporting it. Finally, please say whether her conclusion -- that gradual replacement preserves you -- stands once the disanalogies are on the table, and what a better analogy for the uploading case would be if this one will not do.
