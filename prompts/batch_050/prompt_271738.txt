Write a satirical official memorandum of roughly 800 words, issued by the "Bureau of Evidentiary Rationality," an agency of an invented jurisdiction, announcing a comprehensive reform of the law of proof. The Bureau's founding doctrine is that a fact is established in law whenever its probability on the admissible evidence exceeds the applicable standard of proof, and that only superstition could attach importance to how the probability was arrived at. The memorandum should be written in the complacent, cost-conscious prose of a competent civil service, structured in numbered sections, and it must never wink at the reader. Its satirical target is the view that "naked" statistical evidence, unsupported by anything tying it to the particular defendant, can suffice for a verdict; the humor should come from the Bureau simply taking that view seriously and administering it.

The memorandum should earnestly work through, at minimum, the following reforms, each a correct inference from its premise:

1. Traffic torts. Where an unidentified vehicle causes an accident on a road, liability is assigned to whichever operator runs most of the relevant vehicles on that road, in proportion to fleet share; eyewitness testimony is to be decommissioned as "unquantified anecdote." The Bureau should note with pride that an operator running fifty-one percent of the buses on a route is thereby liable for every unidentified collision on it, and hail this as a simplification.

2. The gatecrasher program. At an event of 1,000 attendees of whom only 499 paid, every attendee is individually liable for the admission price on the preponderance standard, since the probability that any given attendee failed to pay is 0.501. The Bureau should propose suing the entire attendance individually, and celebrate that the promoter recovers 1,000 admissions against an actual loss of 501, redescribing the surplus as a "deterrence dividend."

3. A criminal pilot scheme. Where a suitably defined base rate of offending exceeds some very high threshold, the Bureau proposes prosecutions satisfying proof beyond reasonable doubt, accompanied by an annex table converting base rates directly into verdicts.

4. Reference-class administration. The Bureau resolves the reference-class problem by decree: the legally operative reference class is whichever class the claimant's statistician specifies first. The objection that the defendant also belongs to many classes in which his probable innocence is high is to be reclassified as a discovery request.

5. A closing FAQ titled "Questions from the Philosophy Faculty," whose answers are courteously dismissive. To the complaint that such evidence is "naked" — that nothing connects the statistic to this particular defendant — the Bureau should reply that the demand for particularized evidence is an aesthetic preference with no place in actuarial justice, and that a probability of 0.501 is no more naked than any other number. Include one footnote acknowledging, and immediately setting aside, the allegation that the Bureau has abolished the distinction between being probably guilty and being guilty.

Every absurdity must follow correctly from the founding doctrine, so that a reader familiar with the literature on statistical proof can identify exactly which intuition is being steamrolled at each step. Cite no real cases, scholars, or jurisdictions; the Bureau may cite only its own circulars and a fictional statute. End with a signature block from an invented senior official.
