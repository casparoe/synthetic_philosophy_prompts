I'm giving a colloquium talk in a psych department next month defending a thesis I've been working on, and I need Q&A prep. The thesis: "general intelligence" in humans is an artifact. g only looks like a unitary capacity because test batteries all sample from the same culturally transmitted toolkit (schooling, symbol systems, test-taking formats), and what we call intelligence is really a portfolio of environment-relative efficiencies. The punchline is that AI systems being jagged -- superhuman at Go, useless at loading a dishwasher -- isn't a failure to capture real intelligence, its evidence about what intelligence always was. My argument, roughly: (a) the positive manifold is exactly what you'd predict if abilities scaffold each other developmentally and share cultural inputs, no latent factor required; (b) g's predictive power for jobs and health comes from the toolkit being genuinely useful in those environments, not from some single underlying thing; (c) ML systems are the controlled case showing ability profiles track training distributions, and humans only look uniform because we all get roughly the same training distribution.
