I'm designing the final project for a graduate seminar on explanation in the social sciences, and I want to assign my students the so-called gender-equality paradox as a case study: the cross-national correlational pattern in which countries scoring highest on standard measures of gender equality — the Nordic states being the usual exhibit — tend to produce smaller, not larger, female shares among graduates and professionals in certain technical fields, especially engineering and computer science, while the pattern is much weaker or absent in high-status fields like medicine and law. Before my students start trading explanations, I want to hand them a specification: what any adequate explanation of this pattern must be able to do, regardless of whether it ultimately vindicates nature, culture, or some interaction of the two.

Write that specification as a table with the following columns. (1) Desideratum: the requirement, stated precisely enough that one could check whether a given explanation meets it. (2) Motivation: the case, observation, or distinction that forces it — for instance, why an explanation that fits only the Nordic cases fails; why an explanation must reconcile the paradox with the rough parity of male and female mathematics performance measured in many of these same countries; or why an account must say something about which fields exhibit the pattern rather than treating technical work as one undifferentiated bloc. (3) Status: whether the desideratum is non-negotiable for any explanation whatsoever, or instead a preference of a particular camp (evolutionary-psychological, sociological or role-congruity, economic-constraint, measurement-skeptic, and so on), with the camp named. (4) Exclusionary force: which families of explanation this single requirement rules out, if any.

Two further requests. First, flag any pair of desiderata on your list that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say whether the conflict is logical, empirical, or merely rhetorical. I have a standing disagreement with a colleague about whether "explain the pattern as the relatively unconstrained expression of underlying preferences" and "treat the resulting occupational distribution as an injustice to be remedied" can be held together consistently, and I would like that pair adjudicated explicitly, though other candidate conflicts are welcome. Second, after the table, add a brief note identifying which single desideratum does the most work in eliminating popular explanations, and which desideratum you think is most often smuggled in as a value judgment dressed up as an empirical requirement. Keep the emphasis on the requirements and their motivating considerations rather than on scoring the existing contenders — my students will do the scoring themselves, and I'd rather they fight about a good list than inherit a bad verdict.
