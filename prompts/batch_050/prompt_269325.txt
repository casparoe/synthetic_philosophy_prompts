so im in a phil of action / rational choice seminar and last week we did the toxin puzzle (Kavka) — billionaire offers you a million dollars if at midnight tonight you genuinely intend to drink a toxin tomorrow afternoon that makes you miserably ill for a day but causes no lasting harm. the money depends only on whether you actually hold the intention at midnight, not on whether you end up drinking. my discussion group came up with this argument and it seems to prove way too much:

(1) You can only genuinely intend to do something if you believe (at least roughly) that you will actually do it when the time comes.
(2) A rational person, at the moment of action, does what they then have most reason to do.
(3) Tomorrow afternoon the money question is already settled — whatever you intended at midnight is fixed in the past — so drinking gains you nothing and costs you a day of misery. Tomorrow-you has no reason to drink, and by (2) won't.
(4) But you can see all of this tonight. So by (1), you can't form the intention tonight, no matter how big the reward.
(5) The exact same structure shows up all over the place: deterrence (once the attack has happened, retaliating is pointless, so the threat can't be genuinely intended, so it can't deter), promising (when keeping it is costly and no one would ever find out, future-you has no reason to keep it, so you can't sincerely promise now) — really any case where you commit to something that will be irrational to carry out when the moment arrives.
(6) So a fully rational agent is incapable of threats, promises, and precommitments — which is absurd, rational people do these constantly and whole institutions run on them.

Which step do I reject? I'm most attached to (2) because it just seems like what rationality IS, but I can't tell if the real flaw is in (1), or in the move from (3) to (4) — maybe "having a reason" is doing double duty somewhere, or maybe evaluating the action one moment at a time is already the mistake. What's the least costly premise to give up, and what does giving it up actually commit me to?
