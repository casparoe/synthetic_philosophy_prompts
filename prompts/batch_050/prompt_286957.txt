You are a philosopher of mind and biology who knows Dennett's work on "strange inversions of reasoning" well -- the pattern, first clearly exhibited by Darwin and later by Turing, in which a capacity we assumed required comprehension at the top is shown to arise from uncomprehending competence at the bottom. Before answering, think carefully and brainstorm several candidates in your head, checking each against the criteria below before settling on the strongest one.

I am assembling a catalogue of strange inversions of reasoning -- cases where competence arises without comprehension, reversing the traditional order in which we assumed understanding must precede and explain performance. So far my catalogue contains the following, each with its characteristic inversion:

1. Darwin's inversion: natural selection generates exquisitely adapted organisms -- apparent design, foresight, and purpose -- from blind variation and differential reproduction, with no designer who understands what is being built or why it works.

2. Turing's inversion: mechanical computation shows that any well-specified cognitive task can be carried out by a system that merely shuffles symbols according to formal rules, comprehending nothing of what the symbols mean or what the task is for.

3. The invisible hand / spontaneous order: market prices aggregate and transmit information dispersed across millions of agents, none of whom possesses that information or intends the resulting allocation; orderly, seemingly planned outcomes emerge from uncomprehending local behavior.

4. Reinforcement learning and operant conditioning: organisms and algorithms arrive at sophisticated, apparently goal-directed strategies through reward and punishment alone, without any internal model of why the strategy works -- Skinner's creatures, and their machine descendants, act intelligently without insight.

5. The immune system: clonal selection produces what looks like foresight -- recognition of and defense against pathogens never before encountered -- by differential replication of antibody-producing cells, a Darwinian process with no central planner that knows what a pathogen is.

6. Deep learning: large networks trained by gradient descent display fluent competence at translation, image recognition, and game-playing while embodying no rules, concepts, or comprehension that their designers can point to -- competence acquired by a mechanism no one, including its makers, fully understands.

Here is what I need from you: supply one further strange inversion that is genuinely not on this list. It must not be a rewording, special case, or straightforward combination of the six above (in particular, it should not just be another instance of Darwinian selection or of machine learning applied to a new domain). It should exhibit the core structure: a capacity traditionally explained by invoking a comprehending agent, re-explained as emerging from the iterated operation of uncomprehending parts.

For your candidate, please: (a) state the traditional "comprehension-first" explanation it overturns; (b) describe the mechanism that achieves competence without comprehension; (c) say explicitly how it differs in structure from each of the six listed inversions, especially the two or three it most resembles; and (d) consider the strongest objection to counting it as a genuine strange inversion, and give your verdict on whether the objection succeeds.
