My uncle passed away last year and left me as executor of a fund for his three kids (my cousins), with the infuriating instruction to split it "fairly, which need not mean equally." Thanks a lot, uncle Ray. The money part is legally settled, so please don't just tell me to talk to a lawyer -- I'm stuck on the principle, because all three of them have what sound like decent claims and they pull in totally different directions.

Anna has a chronic autoimmune condition. Her meds and the days she can't work eat a huge chunk of her income, so her argument is: give me more, so that I can actually do the stuff you two take for granted -- hold a full-time job, travel, have kids if I want. She's not asking to be richer than anyone, she just wants the same shot at a normal life.

Ben is just... miserable. Has been for years, no diagnosis, therapy hasn't fixed it. His pitch is basically: money is the only thing that reliably lifts my mood, and not by a little -- so if this fund is supposed to make us better off, I should get the biggest share because I convert it into the most happiness. The catch is he's also developed expensive tastes over the years and freely admits a dollar buys him less joy than it buys Anna, he just wants MORE dollars. This seems like a scam to me but I can't articulate why.

Cara got an equal gift from uncle Ray years back and sank it into a restaurant that failed. Her bank balance is now the lowest of the three, so she says fairness means topping her up until our net worths are equal, and the fact that she lost her share through her own choices is irrelevant -- equal is equal.

Here's what's driving me nuts: if I try to equalize their bank accounts, Anna still can't leave the house half the month. If I try to equalize their actual happiness, Ben vacuums up everything and Cara (who's actually pretty cheerful despite everything) gets punished for having a good attitude. And if I try Anna's version, I don't even know how you'd measure "ability to live a normal life" in dollars. Every notion of "fair" I pick makes one of them the winner and the other two furious.

So what I want to know is: is there a principled way to decide WHAT it is I'm supposed to be equalizing here -- money, happiness, freedom to do stuff, something else -- or does fairness just come down to picking a favorite and dressing it up? And if one of my cousins is making a move that's basicaly illegitimate (I have my suspicions about Ben), what exactly is wrong with it?
