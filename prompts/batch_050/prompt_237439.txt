I'm a novelist working on a book about a small coastal town, and I'm stuck on a story I want to include in it. Here's the setup: there's a fish cannery that's basically the whole local economy, and unbeknownst to most people it's quietly dumping waste that will make the bay unswimmable in a decade. My protagonist is a mid-level accountant there who one day figures out what's going on. Here's the catch that I really want the story to lean on: nothing she does changes anything. If she falsifies the ledgers or does them honestly, if she stays or quits, the dumping happens either way, because the decision lives three floors above her and five other people would take her job by Friday. She is, in a strict sense, causally irrelevant. And yet I want the reader to feel that her staying and keeping the books clean is somehow *participating* in the wrong, while her brother-in-law -- a fisherman who suspects nothing, takes loans from the cannery's credit union, and benefits from the whole arrangement -- is maybe complicit in a different way despite doing nothing at all.

Write me this short story, maybe 800-1200 words. Don't make it a morality play where she has a big dramatic confrontation -- I'm allergic to those. I'm more interested in the texture of a person going to work everyday knowing her moral discomfort costs nothing and fixes nothing, and in how small gestures of refusal or accommodation (the way she files a report, the way she phrases an email, whether she warns her niece not to swim near the outlet) carry weight when the outcome is already sealed. Ideally the story should leave the reader genuinely unsure whether she's done anything wrong at all, or whether the brother-in-law's innocent benefitting is actually the worse position. You can end it ambiguously -- in fact please do.

One more thing: I'd love it if some object in the story (a ledger, a tide chart, a buoy, whatever works) quietly does the philosophical work, so the story never has to state its own question out loud. Nothing heavy-handed, no character suddenly delivering a little speech about ethics at the dinner table. Trust the reader.
