A colleague and I disagree about how to judge contemporary deep learning research as a scientific endeavor. She puts the probability that it counts as a progressive research programme in Lakatos's sense at about 0.7 — meaning its theoretical machinery generates confirmed novel predictions rather than mostly accommodating anomalies after the fact. I put it at about 0.3. Give me your own credence, but more importantly decompose it: your credence that Lakatos's progressive/degenerating distinction is even the right standard for a field this engineering-driven, rather than a Kuhnian or purely instrumental one; your credence that the field's marquee results (scaling laws predicting performance before training runs, capabilities appearing at scale) count as novel predictions in Lakatos's strict sense rather than retrodiction or curve-fitting; your credence that responses to known failures (hallucination, brittle out-of-distribution generalization) are content-increasing modifications of the protective belt rather than ad hoc patches; and any other terms you think the estimate depends on. Give a number and one line of justification for each, show how they combine, then identify which sub-question our 0.7/0.3 disagreement most likely lives in and what evidence over the next few years would move that term most.
