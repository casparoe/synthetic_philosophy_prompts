I'm taking a phil of science course this semester and we just covered inference to the best explanation. The way our textbook presents it, IBE says that when you're weighing hypotheses, you should infer the one that gives the best explanation of the evidence you have. Fine so far. But it got me thinking about my uncle, who's really into astrology. His whole thing is that astrology explains tons of stuff: why his business failed in 2019 (Mercury retrograde), why his two kids are so different (different star signs), why he keeps having bad luck with cars. Honestly he can produce an astrological explanation for basically anything that happens to him, way more than my psych major roommate can explain with actual psychology.

So here's my confusion. If IBE is a legitimate inference rule, and his astrological framework explains more of his life events than any rival explanation he has on offer, doesn't IBE actually tell him he ought to believe astrology? It seems like the more a theory explains, the better the explanation, and the better the explanation, the more IBE favors it. When I brought this up my roommate said something about astrology not being the "best" explanation but that seems to just assume astrology is false, which is circular. If instead "best" means something other than "explains the most," what exactly does it mean, and can that be spelled out without smuggling in the conclusion?

I guess my real question is: is IBE actually a good inference rule at all, if it apparently licenses beliefs like my uncles? Or is there a way to use it properly that blocks this result? Please walk me through where the reasoning goes wrong, because right now it seems like either IBE vindicates astrology or IBE is useless, and my textbook sure doesn't seem to think its useless.
