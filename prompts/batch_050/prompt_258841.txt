A retributivist, who holds that wrongdoers deserve to suffer in proportion to their culpability irrespective of further effects, and a consequentialist, who holds that punishment is justified only insofar as it reduces future harm, have been arguing past each other for years and now attempt an adversarial collaboration. Draft the joint document they should produce: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, concerning such matters as the moral significance of suffering and the treatment of the innocent; (2) their residual disagreements, each stated in a single sentence that both accept as a fair wording of their own view; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — for example, whether deserved suffering has intrinsic value, whether deterrent effects of sentence severity, as opposed to certainty of apprehension, are large and knowable enough to bear moral weight, and whether intuitions about scapegoat cases count as data or as bias; and (4) for each crux, a case, argument, or piece of evidence both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Flag any apparent disagreement that dissolves as merely verbal.
