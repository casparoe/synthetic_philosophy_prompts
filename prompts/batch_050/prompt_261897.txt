So I'm giving a talk at a grad metaphysics workshop in about a month, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The paper defends a slightly weird thesis about finks and masks: my claim is that the fink/mask distinction doesn't carve anything real. Both are just interference cases, and the only difference is bookkeeping -- in a fink the interferer removes the disposition, in a mask it blocks the manifestation while the disposition stays put. I argue that for any stock mask case (the vase wrapped in bubble wrap, the live wire with an antidote attached, etc.) you can redescribe it as a fink whose interference works on a slightly different target, and for any fink you can redescribe it as a mask, so the distinction is interest-relative rather than metaphysical. And if that's right, I say, conditional analyses of dispositions face one problem with two costumes, not two problems, and alot of the literature's taxonomy is wheel-spinning.

The audience is maybe 15 people. Two of them actually work on dispositions and will know Martin's electro-fink, Lewis's revised conditional analysis, Bird's antidotes and Mumford's mask cases cold -- these are the dangerous ones. The rest are general metaphysicians who'll know the basics, plus one philosopher of physics who thinks fundamental properties are irreducible powers and tends to see all counterfactual analyses as a lost cause anyway.

What I want from you: the objections I'll actually get, in rough order of how likely they are weighted by how much damage they'd do to the thesis specifically (not to conditional analyses generally -- that's a different talk). Phrase each one the way that person would actually put it -- the dispositions people will frame things in terms of intrinsic finks and whether redescription preserves causal structure, the powers person will come at it totally differently. For each: what I should concede, a two-minute reply, and where the reply is soft. And tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks.

One more thing: when you list the objections, please flag which ones rest on points that are basically settled among people who work on this stuff (e.g. that plain masks sink the simple conditional analysis) versus which ones are live fights where competent people still disagree. Don't present a contested move as the consensus, and don't let me walk in treating something settled as if it were still up for grabs -- either mistake will get me killed in that room.
