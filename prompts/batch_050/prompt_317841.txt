My pushback was the opposite picture I picked up somewhere: history is more like a river in a valley -- knock a boulder in, it ripples and goes around it; the big structural currents (technology, demography, geography) determine where you end up, and if one particular leader or accident hadn't been there, the same wars and revolutions would have happened with different casts.

The thing is, I don't think either of us actually knows what we're borrowing when we say these things. 'Chaotic like the weather' is doing a lot of quiet work: chaos in the meteorological sense has a precise meaning, and I'm not sure what carries over to history -- what's the equivalent of an initial condition? of sensitivity to one? of a forecast horizon? -- and what just gets smuggled in. Same for the river: rivers really do have attractors in a way that's easy to state, but do historical outcomes?
