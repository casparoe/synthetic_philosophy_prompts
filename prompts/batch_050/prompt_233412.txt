Suppose Hilbert could see modern math tooling: proof assistants like Lean or Coq, SAT solvers that spit out gigantic formal certificates, and ML systems that notice patterns or propose lemmas without understanding them in any human sense. Say an ML model becomes convinced of a finite combinatorial conjecture, and a solver then produces a certificate with 100 million steps. The certificate is mechanically checkable, but no human can survey it or extract an illuminating proof from it.

What do you think Hilbert would say is going on here? Would he count the conjecture as established, or only the certificate-checking result? Would he think the conjecture had a determinate truth-value before any proof existed? And would this kind of machine-generated mathematics push him toward platonism, reinforce his formalism, or force a distinction between mathematical existence as consistency and existence as an independent object? Please connect this to nominalism too, rather than just saying he’d be delighted by computers. I havent seen this exact wrinkle discussed much.
