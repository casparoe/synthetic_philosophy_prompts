ok here's one I've been going back and forth on with a friend. Locke says personal identity = memory: the old general is the same person as the boy who was flogged at school only if he can remember the flogging. Reid's brave officer objection: the general remembers being the young officer who took an enemy standard, and the young officer remembered being the flogged boy, but the general has completely forgotten the flogging. So on Locke's criterion the general = the officer, the officer = the boy, but the general ≠ the boy, wich breaks transitivity of identity. The standard patch is to switch from direct memory connections to overlapping chains of memory (psychological continuity), so identity rides on the chain even when individual links are forgotten.

Now here's the wrinkle I want you to take seriously. Suppose the general, after reading his own boyhood diary, starts to vividly "remember" the flogging -- but it's confabulation, a seeming-memory manufactured from the diary, not a retained trace of the event itself. Does the chain now reconnect him to the boy, or does the patch require the links to be *genuine* memories, in which case what does genuineness amount to without smuggling back in the very direct-connectedness view the patch was meant to replace?

Give me your actual credence that the overlapping-chains fix (properly spelled out) saves a memory-based theory of personal identity from Reid's objection -- I want a number, not "it depends." Decompose it into at least: the probability that transitivity really is non-negotiable for identity so naive Locke is dead; the probability that continuity-not-connectedness does the work without collapsing into either circularity or the too-many-candidates problem; the probability that "is the general the same person as the boy" even has a determinate fact-of-the-matter answer rather than being an empty question where only what matters (survival-ish stuff) is real; and how the confabulated-memory wrinkle should shift things. Number plus one or two lines of justification per term, then how they combine into your final credence. My own credence is about 0.55, my friend is at 0.9 -- tell me which term in your decomposition most plausibly explains that gap, and what argument or thought experiment would move your number the most.
