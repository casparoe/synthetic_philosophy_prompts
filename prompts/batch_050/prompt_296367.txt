Me and a friend have been arguing about this for weeks. I need a minor procedure that's going to hurt. The clinic gives me two options: a one-hour session next week, or the same treatment spread out over about six hours, but not until eight months from now. The doctors are clear that the medical outcomes are identical, the total discomfort is the only real difference, and six hours is genuinely worse than one. Still, every instinct I have screams take the longer one later. My reasoning, such as it is, is basically: that's future me's problem, and suffering that far away just doesn't feel like it counts as much as suffering next week.

My friend says this is flatly irrational -- that caring less about pain purely because of when it happens on the calendar is as arbitrary as caring less because of where it happens on a map, and that if I admit the six hours is worse I should just take the one hour. But here's the thing: my friend also admits he'd feel huge relief if he learned a nasty operation he'd been dreading had actually already happened and he'd forgotten it, rather than being still to come. Isn't that the same calendar-based preference he says is irrational, just pointed the other direction?

Who's right here? And does it matter that "me in eight months" honestly feels a bit like a different person I happen to care about, rather than, you know, me?
