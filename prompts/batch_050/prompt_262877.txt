I keep a running doc where I write down my settled views on various topics, and before I bring the moral worth section to my reading group thursday I want to make sure it doesn't contradict itself. Here's what I've got:

1. Actions only have moral worth if they're done for the right reasons, and the right reason, at bottom, is that the action is morally right or required. The guy who keeps his promise because he takes it to be his duty, full stop, gets full marks. Motives that bottom out anywhere else (self-interest, wanting to look good, whatever) don't count.

2. I also buy the fetishism worry: the good person visits her friend in hospital because she cares about the friend, not because she's moved by the thought that this is what morality demands. Someone who needs that extra moralized thought on top of caring has, as the line goes, one thought too many. So the caring visitor's act has MORE moral worth than the dutiful-but-detached visitor's.

3. Praise and blame only make sense for what was under the agents control. You can't be on the hook for what you couldn't have helped.

4. Whether you feel sympathy or affection for someone isn't under your direct control. You can't decide to care about a person the way you decide to raise your arm; the naturally cold person can't flip a switch and become warm.

5. Verdict on a case: the guy who dives in to save a drowning kid purely because a film crew happens to be there and he wants to be on the news does the right thing, and it's good he did it, but his act has zero moral worth.

6. Moral worth comes in degrees, and the degree tracks how well the agent's motivating reasons line up with the features of the situation that actually make the act right.

So: does this hang together? Tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other and how badly, and rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally fatal. For each real tension, say which commitment is cheapest to revise and what the revision costs. And flag any pairs that only LOOK like they clash but dissolve once you're careful (e.g. about the difference between the rightness of an act and the worth of the motive behind it) — I don't want to fix things that aren't broken. For the record I have no strong prior about which piece I'd drop, so don't pull punches.
