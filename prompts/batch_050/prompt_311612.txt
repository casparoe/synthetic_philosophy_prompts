I serve on the grants committee of a small foundation that funds work in practical ethics. We have room to support roughly four postdoctoral projects over three years on corporate moral agency — the question of whether companies, as opposed to the individual employees, directors, and shareholders who compose them, can be genuine bearers of blame, duties, and responsibility. Before we circulate a call for proposals, I want a defended agenda: which open questions in this area are most worth funding, and in what order?

Here are some candidate questions, which you may reorder, merge, replace, or supplement as you see fit. First, whether group agents can literally hold beliefs and intentions, or whether corporate-level psychological talk is only ever a convenient shorthand for facts about individuals. Second, whether blame and guilt are coherent when directed at an entity with no phenomenal consciousness — can something be guilty if it cannot feel guilt, and does retributive condemnation of firms presuppose a kind of suffering no firm can undergo? Third, how responsibility ought to be divided between the corporation and the individuals within it, and whether strong corporate responsibility lets culpable individuals hide behind the entity. Fourth, whether punishing firms through fines is defensible given that costs pass through to shareholders, workers, and customers who may be innocent of the wrong. Fifth, whether any of this matters practically for law and regulation, or whether it is metaphysics with no downstream consequences for how liability should actually be assigned.

For each question you retain, explain where it sits in the dependency structure — which other questions presuppose an answer to it and which it presupposes answers to — how tractable it is for philosophers as opposed to, say, empirical legal scholars or organizational sociologists, and what considerations or findings over the next few years would move it up or down your ranking. State explicitly the criteria you are applying (practical stakes, neglectedness, tractability, how much else depends on it) and be candid about where those criteria pull against one another. I also want to know which of my candidate questions you consider overrated or malformed, and why. I care less about a comprehensive survey than about an ordering I could defend to the board. Keep the whole response under 650 words.
