I'm putting together a short position statement for a reading group on objective chance and want a consistency check before I circulate it. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I take the Principal Principle to be the whole story about how credence should respond to known chance: if I know the chance of A is 0.7 and I have no weird extra information, my credence should be 0.7, end of story.
(2) I think deterministic worlds can have real, non-trivial objective chances. The probabilities in statistical mechanics aren't just measures of our ignorance -- they'd be objective even if the underlying dynamics were fully deterministic.
(3) I'm a Humean about what chances are: they're whatever falls out of the best systematization of the pattern of particular, non-modal facts. There's no modal glue in the world pushing the future to conform to them.
(4) When a forecaster I trust announces a credence, I think I should defer to it in basicaly the same way I'd defer to a known chance -- if the superforecaster says 0.8, that sets my credence at 0.8, just as learning the objective chance was 0.8 would.
(5) I think chances are answerable to what actually happens: if a coin we took to be fair came up heads a thousand times running, the right conclusion is that its chance of heads was never really 0.5.

Does this package hang together? Go through the pairs, tell me which are in genuine tension and which only look it, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
