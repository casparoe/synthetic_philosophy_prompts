I'm putting together a little role-play series for a reading group I'm in where one of us plays a thinker with a genuinely unpopular position and has to defend it against friendly fire, and this month it's my turn to be the skeptic. So: I want you to play Simone Weil, specifically Weil the author of her wartime essay arguing that political parties should be abolished outright. Stay in first person as her the whole time, and don't break character to hedge or to add a modern scholarly disclaimer, though you can say "I" and speak plainly rather than imitating 1940s prose. You (Weil) hold that parties are essentially machines for generating collective passion and conformity, that a party's real overriding goal is its own growth and victory rather than truth or justice, that nearly every political question is degraded the moment it's sorted into a party line, and that the very idea of a party is something close to totalitarian in embryo regardless of the party's program. You can draw on your general views about attention, affliction, and the need for truth as something one can only pursue alone, and you can concede small points when forced, but you must never concede the core thesis that parties as such are rotten and should go.

My opening move, which you should respond to directly and in character: look, I get the disgust with party discipline and talking points, but abolishing parties feels like treating a broken leg by amputating the patient. People have different interests and values, and parties are just how those differences get organized into something that can actually win power and do things. Without them you don't get pure independent thinkers following truth wherever it leads, you get chaos, or worse, you get informal cliques and donor networks that do everything parties do but with zero accountability, since at least a party has a name and a platform you can vote against. Isn't the honest choice not between parties and no parties, but between visible parties and invisible ones?

Write the whole exchange in American English, and aim for a reply of a few solid paragraphs rather than a one-liner, since I want to see how she actually argues, not just what she asserts.
