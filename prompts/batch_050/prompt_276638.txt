Okay so there's this puzzle that philosophers call the paradox of blackmail, I think, and I want help getting my intuitions in order. The setup: it's perfectly legal for me to tell your wife about your affair, and it's perfectly legal for me to ask you for $5,000, but "pay me $5,000 or I tell your wife about the affair" is a crime and clearly scummy. Two permissable things combining into an impermissable one. Fine. But when I line up my gut verdicts on actual cases, they don't obviously cohere, and I want to know what principle (if any) is behind them.

My verdicts:

- "Pay me $5,000 or I tell your wife about the affair." Not fine. Textbook.
- "Pay me $5,000 or I report your embezzlement to the police." Also not fine -- even though reporting it would be the *right* thing to do! This is the one that keeps tripping me up.
- "Settle with me for $50,000 or I'll take you to court over the contract." Totally fine. That's just how lawsuits work.
- "Give us a raise or we strike." Fine.
- "Stop dumping waste in the river behind my house or I tell your wife about the affair." Honestly this feels mostly okay to me, or at least way less bad than the $5,000 version. Maybe that makes me a bad person.
- Prosecutor: "Plead guilty to the lesser charge or we go to trial on the big one." Standard practice, but if I stare at it too long it gets queasy.
- "Give me the promotion I've earned or I quit and go work for your competitor." Fine.
- "Give me the promotion or I tell the board about your expense account." Not fine. Even if the expense account thing is real.
- Restaurant reviewer says "I'll be writing about my visit next week" -- fine. Same reviewer says "it'd be a shame if I had to mention the rat I saw... unless we could come to an arrangement" -- obviously not fine. But is the only difference saying the quiet part out loud?
- "Fire the manager who harassed me or I go to the papers." Feels fine.
- Same employee: "Pay me a year's salary or I go to the papers." Feels not fine. Even though she might genuinely deserve compensation.
- A parent: "No college money unless you study medicine." Annoying but fine? I think?

The best principle I've managed on my own is something like: it's blackmail when the thing you demand is private gain disconnected from the thing you're threatening to reveal. But that immediately breaks -- the settlement demand is private gain too, and it's "connected" to the lawsuit only in some thin way I can't articulate. And my river case breaks the opposite fix (that the demand has to relate to the disclosure), because the wife has nothing to do with the river.

So: give me a principle, or two candidate principles if one won't do it. Requirements: (a) stated precisely enough that I can apply it to cases I haven't thought of, (b) say which of my verdicts it saves and which clause of the principle exists because of which case, (c) tell me what it says about cases I didn't list -- like "pay me or I'll post screenshots of your anonymous account," or a rival CEO threatening to enter my market unless I buy her out, and (d) be honest about which of my verdicts it has to throw out, because I'm pretty sure at least one of them can't survive. If no principle gets all of them, I'd rather hear that than get a fake one.
