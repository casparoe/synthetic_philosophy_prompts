Two of us in my philosophy Discord have been going in circles about axiology for over a year and a half, and last week we agreed to stop arguing and try the adversarial-collaboration format instead, where both sides have to co-author one document. Neither of us is much of a writer though, so I want a draft to work from. The two camps: one of us is a tranquilist -- her view, roughly, is that what makes an experience good is never a positive ingredient but only the absence of craving, felt need, or inner disturbance. On her picture, untroubled contentment isn't a lesser good than ecstatic joy, it's just good simpliciter, because bliss is either the relief of a craving (and cravings themselves are bad to have) or the brief non-experience of needs you would've been better off never having; a mind with no unmet needs has literally nothing to gain. The other is a classical hedonist: pleasure is positive value in its own right, a life of great joys beats a life of calm contentment even at equal suffering, and bringing very happy people into existence is genuinely good rather than merely harmless. They've agreed on surprisingly much already (both think torture-level suffering vastly outweighs mild pleasures, both think "pleasure" and "preference satisfaction" come apart) but keep talking past each other on whether contentment is a real good or just the absence of bads.

Write the joint statement they could both actually sign. I want: (1) an agreed-claims section, numbered, including the points above plus whatever else both sides would clearly accept about intensity, aggregation, and the badness of suffering; (2) a disagreements section where each position is stated precisely and in wording its own side would endorse -- no strawman formulations like "the tranquilist thinks pleasure is bad"; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is whether the absence of craving can be positively good rather than merely not-bad, and another is whether relieving a craving always presupposes a prior bad state (does the pleasure of scratching an itch require the itch to have been bad?), but please find others and correct me if these are actually verbal; (4) for each crux, a thought experiment or observation both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance -- e.g. the case of the meditator offered a pill for pure ecstasy who declines, or comparing creating a blissful child vs. a merely contented child vs. no child.

If some of their long-running fight turns out to be about words rather than the world, flag which part and why at the end -- I suspect the phrase "positive value" is doing damage somewhere but can't pin down where.
