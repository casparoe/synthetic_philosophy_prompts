I am a novelist, and over the past few years I have come to accept something close to Tolstoy's account of art in his late treatise What Is Art?: that art is essentially the transmission of feeling -- one person, having lived through an emotion, conveys it to others by means of words, sounds, or images, so that the audience comes to feel what the artist felt; that the merit of a work as art is a matter of its infectiousness, which depends above all on the artist's sincerity and the individuality of what is transmitted; and that a great deal of celebrated art, including most of Tolstoy's own fiction, fails as art altogether because it manufactures effects rather than transmitting lived feeling. I have also accepted, provisionally, his two-tier structure: infectiousness determines whether something is good *as art*, while the worth of the feelings transmitted determines whether it is good *art* -- so a work can be supremely infectious and still bad, because what it transmits is base.

What I want from you is a map of this position's exposure: what would have to be true for it to be false, or at least for me to abandon it. I am not asking for the standard parade of counterexamples from the survey courses -- I know the charge that Tolstoy's canon is philistine, and I have made my peace with it. I am asking which objections actually threaten the architecture. In particular, I would like you to consider: cases of collaboratively or procedurally produced art, where no single person had the feeling the work transmits; the distinction the view needs between *transmitting* a feeling and merely *inducing* one, and whether anything in experience or in the psychology of audiences could draw that line; whether the two-tier structure is a principled division of questions or an ad hoc retreat that lets the theory absorb any counterexample by relocating it to the other tier; and whether the view predicts anything about artistic practice or reception that its rivals -- expression theories, formalism, institutional theories -- do not, so that it is genuinely risking something.

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish throughout between what critics of the view say counts against it and what a committed defender would actually concede as counting. If the honest verdict is that the position is so insulated by qualification that nothing could dislodge it, say so plainly, and tell me whether that is a reason for a working novelist to stop trusting it as a guide to her own judgments about what on her desk is working and what is dead.
