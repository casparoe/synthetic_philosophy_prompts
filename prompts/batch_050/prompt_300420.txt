I’m a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science trying to turn a side project into a dissertation chapter, and I could use help prioritizing before I disappear down the wrong rabbit hole for eighteen months. The project concerns agents trying to coordinate under two kinds of mismatch at once: each has information the other cannot access, and each may carve the world into a different ontology. Imagine two maintenance systems overseeing the same industrial plant. One represents it in terms of pumps, valves, and pipe segments; the other in terms of pressure basins, bottlenecks, and flow gradients. Neither can inspect the other’s internal representations, and some facts are not merely unknown but inaccessible given each system’s sensors, position, and permitted interventions. They can exchange messages, observe behavior, and occasionally test actions on the plant. My superviser thinks this is basically a communication problem, but I suspect it raises a deeper question about when two agents can be said to have identified the same objects or properties at all.

Which open questions should I prioritize, and in what order? Candidates include: under what conditions one agent can infer another agent’s concepts from messages and interventions; whether ontology alignment should be measured by predictive agreement, intervention agreement, mutual repair costs, or explanatory usefulness; whether inaccessible information creates genuine indeterminacy about which ontology an agent uses rather than mere ignorance on our part; and how communication norms should change when both sides know their conceptual schemes may not line up. Feel free to replace these with a better question if I’ve missed the real crux. I’m looking for a dependency-aware agenda rather than a survey: which issues are upstream of the others, which are tractable through conceptual analysis and thought experiments rather than technical implementation, which would most change how we think about coordination between differently built minds, and which seem important but would have little downstream effect. Also say what discoveries or counterexamples over the next year would make you reorder the list.
