So in my philosophy of language reading group we keep hitting the same wall, and I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Back in the seventies Davidson argued (in his paper "What Metaphors Mean") that a metaphor means nothing beyond its literal meaning -- the metaphorical effect is something the utterance *does* (it nudges you into seeing one thing as another), not some extra proposition it *says*. On his picture, paraphrasing a metaphor is not retrieving its hidden meaning, its more like explaining the point of a joke. A few years later Lakoff and Johnson's Metaphors We Live By took basically the opposite stance: metaphor isn't a decorative use of words at the margins of language, it's the basic machinery of thought itself, since we systematically understand abstract domains (time, arguments, love, morality) through mappings from concrete bodily experience, so metaphorical "meaning" is everywhere and most of our apparently literal thinking is structured by it.

Decades later both camps still have defenders and, as far as I can tell, neither has budged an inch. The Davidsonians complain that the cognitive linguists just changed the subject from what sentences mean to what happens in speakers' heads. The Lakoff people reply that the Davidsonians are wedded to a picture of meaning-as-truth-conditions that was already obsolete, and that ignoring the massive patterns in conventional metaphor (all the "argument is war," "time is money" families of expressions) is just refusing to look at the data.

Diagnose this for me. Is it a substantive disagreement about one phenomenon, or two projects answering different questions (semantic content vs. cognitive structure) that keep getting mistaken for rivals? Is it partly verbal, hinging on what each side is willing to call "meaning"? Are they taking different cases as the central data -- novel poetic metaphors for Davidson, dead conventional ones for Lakoff -- and does that fully explain the impasse, or is there a real residual disagreement underneath, e.g. about what psycholinguistic evidence about processing could even show? My working hypothesis is that its two different questions under one label, but I'm not confident, because the Lakoff side does seem to want to draw conclusions about truth and reference, not just cognition. Given your diagnosis, tell me what would actually count as progress: an experiment, a principled distinction between kinds of metaphor, a better theory of meaning -- or should we expect nothing to ever settle this, and if so why?
