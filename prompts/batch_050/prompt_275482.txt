I'm a PhD student in financial mathematics, so I live in the world of risk measures: VaR, expected shortfall / CVaR, spectral risk measures, certainty equivalents, distortion functions, all that. Recently a friend doing philosophy kept telling me about this debate in decision theory between classic expected utility and "risk-weighted expected utility" (Buchak's view, I think), where risk attitudes are modeled by reweighting the probabilities of ranked outcomes rather than by the curvature of the utility function. She claims this is a deep disagreement about where risk-sensitivity "lives" in a decision maker. My immediate reaction was: isn't this just the difference between utility curvature and probability weighting, which my field already has completely mapped out? Rank-dependent utility is basically a Choquet integral, distortion risk measures like CVaR are a special case, and Acerbi's spectral risk measures seem to formalize exactly this kind of pessimism weighting on ranked outcomes.

So here's what I want. Restate the core of the EU vs. REU dispute in my vocabulary -- certainty equivalents, dual representations, distortion functions, coherence axioms, stochastic dominance -- as faithfully as you can. Then, and this is the part I actually care about, give me a residue report: which parts of the philosophers' disagreement translate cleanly into the math I know, which parts only translate with distortion (my suspicion: the philosophers' talk about "risk as a global attitude toward stakes" and the diachronic stuff about consistency over sequential choices doesn't quite become any property of a risk measure I've seen), and which parts have no counterpart at all in the risk-measure framework.

For the residue, I want your verdict on each chunk: is it missing from my vocabulary because risk measures were designed for a different job (pricing and regulation, single-shot preferences over lotteries, no story about agency or reasons), or because the philosophers are arguing about something that evaporates once you write it down formally? Be honest in both directions. I have a prior that a lot of this dissagreement is basicly my distinction wearing a costume, but my friend insists things like the Allais-type cases and the argument that EU forces you to explain extreme aversion to tiny-stakes gambles via absurd utility curvature are doing real work that no choice of distortion function captures. Tell me if she's right.
