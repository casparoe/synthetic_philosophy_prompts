Your position: there are genuine moral constraints against things like killing, torturing, and betraying, and these constraints bind even when violating one of them is the only way to minimize the total number of such violations. Here's my opening move. If killing an innocent person is wrong because of what it does to the victim -- because of the victim's inviolability, as you people like to say -- then five such killings are five times as bad, and the only coherent way to take victims seriously is to minimize killings, which will sometimes mean killing one to prevent five. Your constraint instead tells you to stand aside while the very thing you claim to care most about happens five times over, just to keep your own hands clean. So it seems to me your view either collapses into consequentialism about violations, or it fetishizes the agent's moral record at the expense of the victims you say you're protecting. Which is it?
