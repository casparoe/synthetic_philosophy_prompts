ok so my friend and i got into this huge argument last night that went til like 2am and neither of us budged. his position is simple: video game characters are just code, nothing you do to them matters morally, end of story, anyone who feels bad is just confusing themselves. but when i actually go through cases my gut does NOT give me the flat answer he wants, and it doesnt give me a consistent non-flat answer either, so i want to know what principle (if any) my verdicts add up to.

here's where i land. Mowing down random pedestrians in GTA for fun: basically fine, feels like popping bubble wrap, idgaf. Deliberately removing the pool ladder in the Sims so my sim swims til he drowns, while he begs and panics: that one i genuinely cant do, it feels bad, though when i asked myself why the only answer i had was something like 'it's about what enjoying that says about me,' not about the sim. Torturing an NPC in one of those gritty narrative games where the character is voiced and screams and remembers it later: wrong, or at least i'd judge someone who seeks that out. Choosing the evil dialogue path in an RPG where companions react and the game clearly frames it as a moral choice: not wrong, that's just engaging with the story the devs wrote. Deleting a 200-hour save file full of characters i'd gotten attached to: felt like a genuine loss when i did it, but more like losing a photo album than killing anyone. Modding a game so the NPCs exist in a permanent loop of suffering with no way to progress or die: that feels actually wrong wrong, not just icky, though i cant say who it wrongs. And here's the one i keep flip flopping on: a game with an AI-driven NPC that learns from and adapts to how you treat it over months, remembers everything, changes its personality accordingly -- being pointlessly cruel to that one feels different from the GTA thing in a way i cant justify, since it's still 'just code'.

so: is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? my friend says the only consistent views are 'nothing matters til it's sentient' or total sentimentalism, and that my ladder-vs-GTA split is incoherent since both are the same kind of thing. is he right that i have to drop one of them, and if so which? also tell me what the principle you land on says about cases i havent thought of -- like cruelty to NPCs in front of kids, or a dev deliberately designing characters to be maximally pitiable specifically so players will enjoy hurting them -- because my friend will defintely bring up something like that next time.
