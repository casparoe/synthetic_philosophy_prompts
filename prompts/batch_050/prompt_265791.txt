For my graduate seminar in constitutional theory I have constructed a stylized exercise, and I would like your analysis of it. All of the following is fictional: the country, its constitution, and its court are of my own invention, so nothing here turns on knowledge of any actual legal system.

The Republic of Velmara is a young democracy. Its constitution contains, among other provisions, a clause stating that no law shall abridge the freedom of expression, a clause stating that representatives shall be chosen by the people in free and equal elections, a clause guaranteeing the equal citizenship of all persons, a clause guaranteeing the inviolability of the dwelling, and a clause protecting property against taking without compensation. The Constitutional Court of Velmara has issued six noteworthy decisions:

1. It invalidated a statute imposing criminal penalties on statements that undermine public confidence in the armed forces, reasoning that the expression clause contains no public-order exception.
2. It invalidated an electoral statute restricting candidacy for the legislature to holders of university degrees, resting jointly on the free-and-equal-elections clause and the equal citizenship clause.
3. It upheld environmental regulations imposing severe compliance costs on manufacturers against a challenge under the property clause, holding that regulation falling short of expropriation is not a taking.
4. It invalidated a statute barring members of a particular religious minority from all public employment, under the equal citizenship clause.
5. It invalidated a statute authorizing warrantless police searches of private homes, under the inviolability-of-the-dwelling clause.
6. It upheld a sharply progressive tax on large landholdings against an equal citizenship challenge, remarking that classification in matters of taxation lies within the legislature's prerogative.

The Court must now decide the following case. The legislature has prohibited all direct-to-consumer advertising of prescription pharmaceuticals, defending the measure as necessary to public health. A pharmaceutical manufacturer challenges the ban under the expression clause.

Please do the following. First, enumerate the plausible decision rules that are each consistent with the Court's entire record — for example, a strictly textualist rule, a representation-reinforcing rule that intervenes only where the democratic process itself is distorted, a substantive-rights rule that protects fundamental interests broadly conceived, or something else — and state what each rule predicts in the pharmaceutical advertising case. At least two of your candidate rules should diverge here; if none do, reconsider your candidates. Second, give your best prediction of what the Court will do if it wishes to remain consistent with itself, and say which prior decision is the closest analog and why. Third, explain what governing principle the Court would implicitly be adopting under each of the two outcomes — that is, what it would be conceding about the proper role of an unelected tribunal in a democracy by striking the statute down, and what by upholding it. Finally, describe a hypothetical future case whose resolution would best discriminate among the decision rules that survive your prediction.
