I'm putting together a short statement of my epistemological commitments for a graduate seminar on the structure of justification, and before I circulate it I'd like an audit of whether the package actually hangs together. My views have mostly been shaped by dissatisfaction with all three standard responses to the regress argument, and I've tried to assemble what I take to be the defensible pieces of each. Here is where I've landed:

(a) I'm an internalist in a modest sense: whatever justifies a belief for a person must be something the person has — some mental state of theirs — rather than an external fact they may be wholly ignorant of, such as the actual reliability of the process that produced the belief. (b) I'm a foundationalist: some beliefs are justified non-inferentially, and perceptual beliefs about one's immediate surroundings are the paradigm case. (c) What non-inferentially justifies those beliefs is the perceptual experience itself, and experiences are not the kind of thing that can be justified or unjustified — only beliefs (or belief-like states) can be — so the regress stops there without needing the experience to have any epistemic status of its own. (d) Whenever a belief is justified by inference, the subject must be justified in accepting the connection between premises and conclusion; merely having true premises that in fact entail the conclusion is not enough. (e) Coherentism and infinitism both fail: a circle of mutual support can't generate justification from nothing, and an infinite chain of distinct reasons is psychologically impossible for creatures like us, so neither can be what justification actually consists in. (f) Finally, I'm no skeptic: ordinary people in ordinary perceptual conditions know a great deal about their environment.

Please tell me which of these commitments are in genuine tension with which, and how severe each tension is — I'd like them ranked rather than treated as equally problematic, since I'm prepared to revise something but want to give up as little as possible. For each real tension, identify what would have to give and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the package. I'd also ask you to distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones: I suspect at least one pair above only looks inconsistent because of an equivocation I haven't noticed, and I'd rather have that explained than have everything flagged indiscriminately. Note that I have no prior commitment about which item I'd drop if forced, so don't pull punches. Please write your answer in American English.
