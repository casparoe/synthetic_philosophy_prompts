Quick one that's been bugging me since a family argument last weekend. My family runs a small inn on the Gulf Coast, and we're deciding whether to drop a serious chunk of money — like $35k, which genuinely hurts — on a generator, storm shutters, and some drainage work before hurricane season. My cousin Marco, who helps us manage the place, is against it, and his reasoning is driving me nuts because I can't find the hole in it. He says: look, a year from now we'll know for sure whether a big storm hit us or not. Whichever way it goes, that's just the fact of the matter, already. The future doesn't become true when it happens — it just is what it is, we can't see it yet. He read some pop physics book about how all of time is laid out like a film strip and we're just watching the frames in order, so he's really on this kick. His pitch: either the storm comes for us or it doesn't. If it does, it comes no matter what we do, so the money doesn't stop anything. If it doesn't, we've burned $35k on nothing. So either way the prep is pointless, and we should pocket the money and stop stressing.

The thing is, Marco doesn't actually live like this. He still looks both ways crossing the street, he renewed the flood insurance on his own condo last month, and when I pointed that out he just said "that's instinct, this is logic." Meanwhile my mom is terrified of being the only dark building on the block if something hits, and the forecast people keep saying it's gonna be an extra active season, whatever that's worth. I'm the one who has to make the final call this week since I handle the books. Am I missing something? Where exactly does Marco's reasoning go wrong — because it feels wrong, but everytime I try to explain why, I end up just sputtering "but what if it hits us?", which he treats as pure emotion rather than an argument. And if he's actually right, what does that mean for how anyone is supposed to make decisions about anything?
