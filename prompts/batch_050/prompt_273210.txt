Settle an argument between me and my brother. He flipped a coin 40 times and wrote down the results, and the sequence came out with these weirdly tidy stretches -- like H T H T H T H T for eight flips, and a run of exactly alternating pairs later on. I said no way, that doesn't look like chance, maybe he was sloppy with the flipping or subconciously steering it. His comeback: every specific sequence of 40 flips has exactly the same probability, 1 in 2^40, so the "tidy" one is precisely as random as the messy-looking one I'd have accepted without blinking. The messy one was also a 1-in-2^40 event before it happened, so by my logic I should be equally suspicious of literally every outcome ever observed, which is absurd.

And look, he's clearly right about the arithmetic, which is what bugs me. But then I don't understand how the randomness tests people use to audit casino RNGs or lottery machines can be anything more than superstition. Whatever string the machine spits out, that exact string was a 1-in-2^40 event (or whatever the length is), every single time, so there's never any evidence of anything. So which is it: either my brother is wrong and some sequences really are less random than others despite equal probability, or randomness testing is bunk? And if the tests aren't bunk, what are they actually detecting -- because it can't be the improbability of the sequence, they're all equally improbable.
