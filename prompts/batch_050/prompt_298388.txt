ok so background: at my brother in laws birthday dinner last month he announced, with total confidence, that their neighbor was moving to portugal. turns out the neighbor was just visiting cousins in porto for two weeks. this is maybe the fifth time he's done something like this and it got me thinking about what the actual rule should be for when you're allowed to just SAY something. i went down a bit of a rabbit hole online and apparently philosophers genuinely argue about this: some say you should only say what you know, some say only what's true, some say only what you honestly believe. the "only what you know" one seems to be the prestigious position and honestly it sounds insane to me, like 90% of all conversation would be against the rules.

so heres what i want: write me a parody etiquette column, in the style of one of those very proper old-school advice columnists who treats fork placement like a matter of national security, but from a world (or maybe just an extremely strict social club) where the rule "only assert what you know" has been fully adopted as basic manners. asserting something you merely believe, however reasonably, should get treated the way those columnists treat elbows on the table. format: 3 or 4 short letters from readers with the columnist's replies, around 600-700 words total, completely deadpan, no winking at the reader.

the important part is that the humor comes from the rule actually being followed to the letter, not from made-up silliness. so work in the real weird consequences: can you properly say "the party starts at 7" or "see you tomorrow" given you might get hit by a bus, what happens to weather forecasts and small talk and "how are you", whether you're allowed to pass along what a trustworthy friend told you, the strange awfulness of saying something like "its raining but i dont know its raining" which sounds broken even though it might be true. at some point the columnist should mention, with visible horror, the rival etiquette systems — the permissive types who think its fine to assert whatever you sincerely believe, and the real extremists who say anything goes as long as it happens to be true — the way a manners columnist references people who put the milk in first. someone whos never heard of this debate should find it funny on its own, and someone who knows it should feel you actually understood the positions rather than just exaggerating randomly. dont settle whether the rule is right, let the columnist's serene total confidence do that work.
