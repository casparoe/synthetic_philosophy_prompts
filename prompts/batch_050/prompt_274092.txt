About six months ago I moved into a small row of terraced houses that share a communal courtyard garden. There are four households, and by longstanding informal custom each household takes one week per month of keeping the garden tidy: watering, weeding, and so on. For the first few months everything worked beautifully, and I happily did my week and more. Recently, though, one household has essentially stopped doing anything. Their weeds spread into the beds the rest of us maintain, and my own motivation has started to collapse: I find myself doing the bare minimum in my week, because why should my effort subsidize their neglect? But I notice this is making the garden worse for everyone, including me, and part of me suspects that if I simply keep up my standards visibly, the others (who so far still pull their weight) will too, and the delinquent household may even be shamed back into line. Another part of me thinks I should just match the lowest contributor and accept a shabby garden as the rational outcome.

Please act as an expert advisor on strategic interaction and the logic of collective action, someone who thinks carefully about when cooperation is stable and when it unravels. Before giving your recommendation, brainstorm at least three distinct ways of modelling my situation, considering in particular what changes when the same small group faces the same temptation to slack off over and over again, indefinitely, with everyone able to see what everyone else did last round. Weigh what each model implies for how I should behave, including whether punishing the free-riding household (for instance by pointedly leaving their side of the garden untended) is likely to restore cooperation or trigger a downward spiral, and what difference it makes that I cannot leave this neighborhood. Then give me a concrete, reasoned recommendation for how to act over the coming months, and tell me honestly where the theory runs out and ordinary judgment about my neighbors has to take over.
