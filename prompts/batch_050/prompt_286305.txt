Write a villanelle about disjunctivism in the philosophy of perception. The two refrains should be the good case (actually seeing the oak tree in front of you) and the bad case (a perfect hallucination of the same tree), and the form should do the argumentative work: the refrains trading places across the stanzas, indistinguishable as experiences, until they finally stand side by side in the closing quatrain -- which is where the poem should make the disjunctivist's point, that being unable to tell two states apart from the inside doesn't make them the same kind of state, so the argument from illusion's inference to a common factor (and from there to sense-data even in veridical perception) fails. Ideally the poem somehow registers that the bad case can only ever be characterized negatively, as merely seeming like the good one, rather than as a thing with its own positive nature -- but dont cheat by letting the hallucination feel different from the inside, because then the whole problem evaporates. Someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which refrain is which and why their final juxtaposition is the thesis rather than just a rhyme.
