I’m taking a second-year Philosophy of Language module and, in a moment of madness, volunteered to run the first 25 minutes of a revision seminar on Brandom’s inferentialism. The audience is first-years who have done sense/reference and a bit of truth-conditional semantics, but basically no pragmatics or formal logic. I’ve got a whiteboard, no slides, and I definately don’t want to lose them by starting with scorekeeping.

Please plan the teaching rather than writing the seminar itself. The carrying example I want to use is an invented café-workers’ term, quarked. Staff are entitled to call a returned mug quarked if it has a hairline crack or has been borrowed three times without being washed. Calling it quarked commits them to keeping it out of hot-drink service and putting it in the repair tub, and it is incompatible with calling the mug shelf-ready. I picked it because the word has no familiar reference and its whole content seems to live in what warrants it, what follows from it, and what it rules out.

Work out the order the ideas need to come in, and explain the dependencies. In particular: should I begin with use rather than reference; should upstream conditions come before downstream consequences; where do commitment, entitlement, and incompatibility belong; and when can scorekeeping be introduced without sounding like a weird metaphor about points? Also tell me whether the café example really carries the philosophical weight or merely gives a rulebook for a made-up word, and what it needs to have or avoid if it is going to get them to material inference rather than just an arbitrary definition.

Include the misconceptions I should expect and the exact point where each should be headed off: that inferential role means whatever associations an individual speaker happens to have; that inference means formal logic only; that this is verificationism under another name; that talking about social practice makes meaning depend on majority opinion; and that Brandom is replacing truth and reference with inference rather than trying to explain them. Suggest one short exercise that would show whether the idea has landed, perhaps by giving them a second invented status to analyse. Finally, say what I should openly leave out at this level rather than gesture at badly.

Please answer in British English.
