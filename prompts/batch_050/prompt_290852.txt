I recently joined a citizens' advisory committee for my city's council, which must decide whether to remove, relocate, or recontextualize (with new interpretive plaques) a century-old monument in our central square honoring a historical figure whose record includes both genuine civic achievements and conduct now widely regarded as shameful. The public debate has split into camps that each accuse the others of either erasing history or sanitizing it, and I have found Nietzsche's tripartite distinction in On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life -- monumental, antiquarian, and critical history -- a promising lens for cutting through the noise.

Analyze this kind of monument controversy through Nietzsche's framework. Map the main positions in the debate (preserve as-is, relocate to a museum, remove entirely, retain with critical recontextualization) onto the three modes of history, stating what each mode, taken seriously and on Nietzsche's own terms, would counsel here and why -- including the uncomfortable cases, such as whether removal is genuinely critical history or rather a refusal of history altogether, and whether the "heritage" defense is honest antiquarianism or monumental history in disguise. Then run the analysis in reverse: say what this controversy reveals about the framework. Does Nietzsche's typology have a blind spot for the difference between remembering and honoring, or for communities whose monumental past was someone else's oppression? Finally, be explicit about the epistemic status of your verdict: state which consideration or counterargument would most plausibly change your conclusion, and where you think your mapping of the debate onto Nietzsche's categories is most likely to be forced or wrong.
