I own a unit in an apartment building governed by a residents’ association. Only owners may vote, although the association’s rules bind tenants and its decisions can affect the surrounding block. The association must now decide whether to sell its courtyard to an overnight freight company. Owners would receive lower dues and a stronger reserve fund; tenants would lose the garden and endure nighttime noise; neighboring households would lose curb space; the company’s workers would gain jobs; and future residents would inherit both the financial benefit and the altered building.

The board says owners alone should vote because they carry the debts and are legally responsible for the association, while everyone else may submit comments. I see some force in that position, but it also seems wrong that people governed by the association every day have no authority merely because they do not hold title.

What arrangement of voting and consultation would be defensible? Please distinguish influence over this one decision from standing authority to make the association’s rules, and explain whether impact, legal responsibility, financial risk, or expected future residence should determine voting power. I am not seeking legal advice; I want a principled answer that I can present to the board.
