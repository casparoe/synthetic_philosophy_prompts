So my roommate and I have been bickering about wireheading for like two weeks now and it's actually starting to annoy me. It started with the classic experience machine stuff, but she's doing a master's in ML so she keeps dragging it toward reward tampering: if an AI (or a person, or a rat with an electrode) figures out how to directly stimulate its own reward signal instead of doing the thing the reward was supposed to track, has anything even gone wrong? Her line is that reward is reward, the signal firing IS the good outcome by the agent's own lights, and calling it "tampering" smuggles in the designer's or evolution's goals as if those were automatically authoritative. My line is that this confuses the meter with the cargo, but honestly when she pushes me on WHY the world-state matters more than the brain-state I keep reaching for intuitions I can't defend.

So I want to settle this with a proper taxonomy of the positions, but built from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names. Build it as a decision tree where each branch is a question someone could answer without knowing where it leads: things like whether value attaches to states of the world or states of the experiencer, whether a reward signal is constitutive of value or merely evidence of it, whether it matters whose goals set the standard (the agent's own deeper goals vs. an external designer's), and whether there's a relevant difference between never having had real goals (the rat) and betraying goals you had (the human who wires in). Leaves should include at least: the view that wireheading is fine and we should all be so lucky, the view that it's bad because it severs contact with reality, the view that it's bad because it hijacks the agent's own prior preferences, and the view that "bad" only makes sense relative to whoever specified the reward. If two leaves end up impossible to tell apart by your questions, say so explicitly rather than pretending, and if there's a combination of answers nobody seems to occupy, note whether it's incoherent or just unexplored.

Format: exactly eight numbered items and nothing else — first three are the branch questions (each one sentence, phrased as an actual yes/no or either/or question), last five are the leaf positions (each one or two sentences naming the view and its most awkward commitment). No intro, no conclusion.
