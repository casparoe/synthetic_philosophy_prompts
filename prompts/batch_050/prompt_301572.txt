For a reading group on dreams I wrote down where I stand so the others can attack it, but I want a pre-check first. I hold: (a) Malcolm is basicly right that a sincere waking report is the criterion for having dreamed — there's no further question about what "really happened" during the night beyond what the report fixes; (b) Dennett's cassette theory is empirically false — long coherent dreams genuinely unfold over time during sleep, and lucid dreamers carrying out pre-arranged eye signals prove this; (c) there is something it's like to be dreaming while you're asleep, i.e. dreams have phenomenal character at the time they occur; (d) you can have genuine experiences during dreamless sleep, like when a parent hears the baby cry and wakes up — the sound was registered before waking; (e) most dreams are forgotten, and a forgotten dream was still experienced at the time. Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix for each would be, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
