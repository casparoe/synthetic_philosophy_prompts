I'm drafting a short position note for a philosophy of mind reading group and could use a consistency audit before everyone tears into it. The setup is an Aunt Hillary-style ant colony: no ant understands the colony-level pattern, but interactions among ants implement something like memory, inference, and symbol manipulation at the collective level.

My commitments are:

1. Physicalism is true. If the colony has a mind, that has to be fully explained by ordinary physical interactions among ants, pheromones, the nest, and the environment.

2. In the right Aunt Hillary-style case, the colony itself can literally have beliefs, desires, and memories. For example, it might remember a food source, expect danger near a rival nest, or want to relocate. This is not merely a useful metaphor.

3. The individual ants have no beliefs or desires at all. They just follow comparatively simple local rules.

4. The colony is not conscious. There is no unified first-person perspective, no colony-level phenomenolgy, and nothing it is like to be the colony.

5. A system can have beliefs, desires, and memories without being conscious. Unconscious mentality is enough for literal mindedness.

6. I accept a strongly intentional-stance account of belief and desire: an attribution is made true by the predictive and explanatory usefulness of treating the system as having that attitude. There is no further fact about what the system “really believes” beyond the success of that interpretation.

7. Whether the colony is minded is an objective fact, not something that depends on which interpretation an observer happens to find useful.

8. Genuine mindedness requires a subject whose states they are. A merely aggregate system can display intelligent behavior, but without a subject it has only as-if beliefs and as-if desires.

9. Colony-level trail patterns can genuinely represent the world when they carry information, guide colony behavior, and can misrepresent things—for instance, when an old trail indicates food that is no longer there.

Please audit whether these commitments hang together. Don't give me a general survey of collective minds; focus on what commits me to what.
