I got into a stupidly long argument at a rationality-adjacent reading group last week and I want to settle it properly before we meet again. The fight was about acausal threats: cases where something that has no causal channel to you -- typically a hypothetical future superintelligence, or an agent in some other branch or simulation -- is supposed to be able to get leverage over you purely by being able to predict or simulate you. The standard nightmare example is a future AI that would retroactively punish (in simulation) everyone who didn't help bring it about, so that, per certain decision theories, you're "rationally" pressured into helping build it even though it can't actually do anything to you now. Someone claimed the whole problem dissolves if agents adopt what they called surrogate goals: instead of straightforwardly wanting outcome X, the agent wants something like "whatever the counterfactual version of me that actually got threatened would have wanted," the point being that the agent's real goals are constructed so threats can't extract value from them -- extorting the agent gains the extorter nothing, so there's nothing to bargain with. I think this is either deep or completely confused and I can't tell which, and the definately-not-helpful thing everyone in the group did was jump straight to verdicts without saying what an adequate theory here would even have to do.

So: write the desiderata. For each desideratum give the consideration or case behind it, and mark which are genuine requirements versus preferences of one camp (causal, evidential, or functional/logical decision theorists).
