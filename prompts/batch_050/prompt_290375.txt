So I finally got around to reading Dennett's Where Am I?, the one where his brain gets removed and kept alive in a vat in Houston while his body walks around controlled over a radio link. Near the start he seems to settle the question pretty quick: he's in Houston with the brain, since that's where all the experience-generating machinery sits, and the body is basically just a fancy marionette whose location doesn't matter. Okay, fair enough -- so the answer to "where am I?" is just "wherever your brain is," and the body is expendable hardware.

But then the story keeps going for ages. There's a backup computer copy of his brain that can be swapped in without him noticing any difference, there's a whole spare body, and by the end he acts like he can't say where he is at all. And philosophers keep citing this piece as if it raises some deep unsolved puzzle about personal location, rather than as the place where Dennett solved it. If the brain-in-Houston answer is right, what is the entire second half of the story even doing? Is everyone just overcomplicating a question that Dennett answered in the first few pages, or am I missing something about why "where the brain is" doesn't actually close it?
