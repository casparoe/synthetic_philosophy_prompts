You are an experienced examiner setting the final paper for a master's-level course in environmental ethics and the governance of emerging biotechnologies. Before drafting anything, think carefully about what separates a candidate who genuinely understands the ethics of irreversible ecological intervention from one who has merely memorized talking points about the precautionary principle, and design everything below to reward the first and expose the second.

Write one essay question on gene drives, answerable in 90 minutes. The question must be built around the following crux, stated explicitly in the question text: for nearly every other technology, harm spreads only because people keep choosing to use it, so regulation works by constraining ongoing choices; a self-sustaining gene drive inverts this default, since it propagates on its own and halting it requires further intervention, possibly by parties who never consented to the original release. The question must require candidates to take a position on whether this inverted default changes the moral structure of the release decision — specifically, whether one jurisdiction's release should be understood as a choice imposed on every jurisdiction the drive reaches, and whether irreversibility counts against release over and above its contribution to expected harm — rather than allowing them to rehash generic arguments for and against suppressing malaria mosquitoes. Include in the question a brief, self-contained description of a proposed self-limiting drive engineered to die out after a fixed number of generations, and require candidates to say whether it escapes their analysis or only softens it.

Then supply, in this order:

1. A model answer of 500–600 words that commits to definite positions on both required moves (whether the inverted default is a morally distinct fact, and whether the self-limiting design dissolves the problem) and argues for those positions, instead of surveying the available options and leaving the verdict open.

2. A marking scheme out of 25 marks: four core moves with the marks each carries; for each move, a one-line note naming the mistake weaker candidates typically make; and a bonus discriminator of up to 3 marks describing what an exceptional answer adds — for instance, using the contrast with de-extinction (an intervention that is local and recallable, but which produces ecological proxies of a lost species rather than the species itself) to isolate exactly which feature of gene drives does the moral work.
