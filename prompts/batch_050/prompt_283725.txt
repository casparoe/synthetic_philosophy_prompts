Write a mock academic grant proposal, roughly 600 words, from a historian whose entire methodology is that humans have never caused anything: every major event in history was actually done by a volcano, a microbe, or a weather system, and human 'decisions' are just the paperwork these agents file afterwards. In the proposal he applies for funding to prove that some event usually credited to human choices (his pick, maybe the French Revolution or the fall of Rome, your choice) was entirely the work of one such non-human actor, and he treats the obvious objections as a minor budget line item. Include a deadpan passage where he 'considers and rejects alternative explanations' and dismisses human agency in a single sentence, and a bit where he complains that rival scholars unfairly accuse him of determinism when he is simply following the evidence to the nearest caldera. The satire should exaggerate a real tendency in big-history and climate-collapse writing rather than a made-up one, so someone who knows that literature recognizes it as fair-ish, and it should stay completely straight-faced. No headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables anywhere in your response, continuous prose only like a letter, so the usual grant-proposal machinery (aims, methodology, expected impact) has to be smuggled into ordinary paragraphs. After the proposal, add a short note, still in prose, saying where the piece leans on a contested reading of the genre versus where it's basically accurate, how confident you are that the satire lands as fair rather than a strawman, and which single jab you're least sure about.
