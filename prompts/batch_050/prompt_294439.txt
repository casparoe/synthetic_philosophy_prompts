I have read rationalist and post-rationalist blogs for long enough to notice a recurring rhetorical operation that I cannot cleanly classify. A community starts with an ordinary word whose edges are vague. Influential members then propose a tidier rule for applying it, presented as precision rather than reform. But the rule is not neutral: it redistributes status, obligation, or blame. Three stylized examples: donors are told that only interventions with strong evidence and room for more funding really count as *charity*, while bake sales and warm-glow gifts are recast as generosity or consumption; in AI debates, *agent* is narrowed so that a system which resists correction counts, while a more capable system that reliably accepts being shut down is called a mere tool; a support community narrows *trauma* to events producing a recognized symptom profile, so chronic low-grade adversity is acknowledged as suffering but denied the word that unlocks accommodation.

The pattern I care about is the oscillation. When challenged, advocates say they are only describing what the term already meant; when unchallenged, they use the new boundary to say that old usages were confused, harmful, or complicit. Dissenters are made to choose between sounding pedantic and accepting a practical reclassification. It is not simple equivocation, because the speakers often know exactly which boundary they want. It is not ordinary stipulation, because they deny changing the subject. It is not mere drift, because the change is steered. And it is not an openly revisionary project, because the work is done while insisting that nothing is being revised.

Does this exact operation have an established name? If so, I do not want only the label: say how it differs from persuasive definition, explication that preserves the target, ameliorative revision undertaken openly, technical narrowing inside a restricted practice, and innocent semantic change. If there is no adequate name, coin one, define it by conditions rather than by vibes, and give at least two positive and two negative borderline cases. I especially want diagnostic tests: what evidence would show that a group is covertly repairing a word rather than discovering its prior logic, and under what conditions is the move legitimate anyway?
