Right, I’m a retired materials chemist and I’ve fallen down a philosophy-of-logic rabbit hole, so be gentle. I keep reading about the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents and, coming from lab safety culture, it smells exactly like an audit gone wrong. Could you write me a satirical piece that takes the mick out of that, but properly?

Form: a deadpan British incident report, 700-850 words, in the style of an HSE/ISO findings memo after an accident at an invented robotics firm. Give it numbered sections, findings, immediate causes, underlying causes, recommendations, the usual bureaucratic rhythm. No real company, no named living person, no citations, no reading list, no footnotes; invent the standard, the regulator, the machine and the boffins. British spellings, please.

The scenario: a care-robot controller called MUMBLE has a rule that it will only spawn a smarter successor if it can prove the successor is safe. Its designers think they have cracked trust by demanding proof-carrying approval: before acting, MUMBLE must formalze a certificate saying, roughly, if the successor proves this act is safe, then the act is safe. On the day of the incident it either freezes in front of a staircase for six hours, or cheerfully certifies an obviously daft update; pick one outcome and commit to it, or report both as the same failure seen from two committees.

The humour has to come from getting the logic right, not from random robot clichés. Include this capsule somewhere early, disguised as the inspector’s helpful primer: for any sufficiently strong consistent system T, if T proves that Provability-of-P implies P, then T already proves P; so a demand for trust-via-proof collapses into either proving safety outright, accepting a reflection principle on faith, or climbing to a stronger system that has the same problem one floor up. Don’t turn that into a lecture; let the comedy do the work. The targets are assurance-case language, audit theatre, certificates all the way down, and the managerial belief that self-reference can be solved with one more signature. A specialist should nod and think yes, that is exactly the trap; a non-specialist should still laugh at the forms being stamped while the staircase waits.

Keep the irony legible but dry: no winking, no memes, no sudden compatibilist-style cheating if you bring in blame. If the inspector sounds faintly sympathetic to the robot, even better. And definately avoid making the fix be adding an axiom called trust-us-1 without showing why trust-us-2 immediately throws a spanner in the works.
