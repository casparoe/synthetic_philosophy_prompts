Quick question about the "low impact" / "avoid side effects" school of AI safety ideas, because I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for and I'm hoping the field has already named it.

The setup: suppose you've got an AI that gets penalized for how much it changes the world, where the change is always measured against the state of things right before it acts. Now the pattern. A warehouse robot gets a small penalty every time the warehouse looks much different from how it looked an hour ago. Fine at first. But the robot just... moves one pallet a day. Each move is basically free, penalty-wise. Three years later the warehouse is unrecognizable, the workflows are completely different, the human staff have reorganized their whole jobs around it, and at no point did any single action register as a big change. Same with a recommendation system that nudges each user's feed by a tiny amount each week and, over a few years, substantially shifts what a whole population reads and believes. Nothing dramatic ever happened on any given Tuesday.

I know this is somewhere near "offsetting" (the thing where the agent compensates for its side effects after causing them, or causes counter-effects to cancel them in the metric), but I dont think its the same thing: in offsetting the big change happens and then gets erased from the ledger, whereas here no big change ever exists on any ledger at all -- each measurement window only ever sees a small one. It's also not quite the same as the agent just being "gradual" in the ordinary sense, because the whole point is that the gradualness is what defeats the penalty; a version of the agent that did the same total transformation in one afternoon would get hammered.

So: does this specific failure mode have an established name in the impact-measure literature or anywhere adjacent, and if so, how does that literature say it differs from offsetting and from the usual worries about what you measure change against? And a couple of edge questions I cant settle: does the phenomenon disappear if you measure every action against the state where the agent never existed, and if it does, is that a fix or does that break other things? If it turns out there's genuinely no name for it, what would you call it -- and give me a definition tight enough to exclude plain offsetting on one side and "just doing things slowly for normal reasons" on the other. A bare label won't satisfy me; I want to know where the edges of the phenomenon are.
