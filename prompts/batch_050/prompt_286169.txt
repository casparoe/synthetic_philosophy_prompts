I'm a second-year PhD student (philosophy, but my program is half in a law-and-tech institute) and I'm sketching my dissertation on responsibility gaps for autonomous systems. My supervisor keeps telling me the topic is too broad and I need to pick 2-3 chapters that actually do work, so I want help thinking through which open questions in this literature deserve the slots.

Here's my map of the terrain, tell me where it's wrong:

1. The "is the gap even real" question -- whether control problems and unpredictability actually break responsibility attribution, or whether the whole debate trades on conflating different senses of responsibility (attributability, accountability, answerability, liability). Some people seem to think that once you disambiguate, the gap mostly dissolves into ordinary tort problems.

2. The "gap-filling" question -- whether existing concepts (vicarious liability, enterprise liability, negligence-by-design, "many hands" style distributed responsibility) can be stretched to cover autonomous systems, or whether stretching them distorts them past recognition.

3. The retribution gap specifically -- the idea that punishment is different because it requires a fitting target, and fining a company doesn't scratch the retributive itch the way punishing a person does. This seems more worrying than the compensation side, since insurance and strict liability handle compensation fine.

4. The gap-embracing move -- maybe we should just accept that some harms are nobody's fault, the way we accept natural disasters, and the demand to always have someone to blame is the pathology rather than the gap.

5. The design question -- whether we should engineer systems to keep responsibility attribution tractable (meaningful human control requirements, legible decision logs), which reframes the whole thing as an engineering constraint rather than a metaphysical puzzle.

What I want from you: rank these by what a dissertation chapter on them could actually accomplish, and defend the ranking. Be explicit about your criteria -- I'm guessing some mix of (a) how many downstream debates hinge on the answer, (b) whether a philosopher can make real progress on it in ~12 months rather than just adding to the pile of positions, (c) whether the answer changes anything for regulators or just for other philosophers, and (d) how crowded the territory already is. If you think I'm weighting those criteria wrong, say so and propose your own.

Two constraints: I can't do empirical work (no experiments, no case studies of specific accidents beyond what's publicly known), and I want at least one chapter that's more constructive than critical -- actually building something rather than demolishing other people's taxonomies, since I'm told that's what gets you hired.

Also tell me which question you'd *cut* entirely and why, and what finding or argument would make you reorder the list. Don't give me a reading list, I can find papers myself -- I want the strategic reasoning.
