My roommate, a physics undergrad, has an argument that seems to prove too much, and I want a diagnosis before our seminar meets. (1) For any finite body of observations, there are indefinitely many mutually incompatible theories that fit it equally well; given a theory T, one can always concoct a rival that agrees with T on everything observed so far but diverges on what has not yet been tested. (2) So evidence alone never singles out one theory, and any choice among empirically adequate rivals must rest on something else — simplicity, scope, familiarity, elegance. (3) These extra-empirical virtues are pragmatic or aesthetic: they make theories easier for creatures like us to work with, but nothing about the world guarantees that the simpler or more elegant theory is more likely to be true. (4) A consideration that bears no connection to truth cannot make it rational to believe one theory over another; acting on it is convenience at best, prejudice at worst. (5) Therefore no scientific theory is ever rationally preferred to its empirically adequate rivals, and theory choice is a matter of taste. (6) In particular, there is no epistemic difference between preferring our best current physics over a deliberately contrived rival engineered to match all data to date, and preferring chocolate ice cream over vanilla.

The conclusion is absurd, but each step looks defensible on its own. Where does the argument go wrong — a bad premise, an equivocation between steps, or a slide from one claim to a stronger one? If it turns out to be valid, which premise is cheapest to give up, and what would that cost? Two things I'd like addressed along the way: first, whether it matters that (1) only guarantees rivals matching the data gathered so far, rather than rivals matching every possible observation; second, what picture of evidence or confirmation you have to adopt to reject the culprit step, and whether that picture can actually distinguish the contrived rivals in (6) from the serious competitors scientists genuinely weigh. If forced to choose, I'd rather keep (1) and (6)'s absurdity than deny (3), but I'd like to know what I'm paying either way.
