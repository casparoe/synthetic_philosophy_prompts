So I've somehow gotten myself signed up to do a short talk at my local photography club (it's mostly a social thing but they do a "ideas night" once a month) and now I'm commited and slightly panicking. I got obsessed with this question a while back: when I look at a photo of my grandmother, am I literally seeing her -- kind of like seeing her through a window or a mirror, just with a long delay -- or am I only seeing a picture that represents her, the way a drawing of her would? My position is the weird one, that we really do see through photographs. My rough argument is that a photo is mechanically caused by whatever was in front of the lens, so if grandma had moved, the image would've been different, whereas a drawing depends on what the artist thinks and remembers and chooses. So a blurry security-camera still of a burglar lets you see the burglar, but even a perfect painting of him made from an eyewitness description doesn't, no matter how accurate it is. I also have a bit about how mirrors, telescopes and live video feeds obviously count as seeing, and photos are just the same thing with storage.

The audience matters here, so: it's about fifteen hobbyists, two working portrait photographers, one guy who does heavy editing in Lightroom and gets visibly touchy whenever someone implies edited photos are "fake", a retired art teacher who says some version of "all images are constructed" at least once every meeting, and a newer member who shoots film only and talks alot about the "indexical" quality of analog (he took one philosophy class and it shows). Nobody else has philosophy background. I need a prep sheet for the Q&A, not a writeup of my talk: the objections I'll actually get from these people, in the order they're likely to come and phrased the way they'd phrase them (the Lightroom guy and the art teacher will come at me from opposite directions and I keep mixing up who's objection is whose). For each one: is it actually a problem for my view or just sounds like one, what should I concede without collapsing the whole position, what should I hold firm on, and a reply I can give in under two minutes without jargon. Also flag the weakest point in my own argument that I should have an answer ready for instead of hoping nobody notices, and tell me the question I'm dreading -- my guess is something about AI-generated images, since the film guy has been needling people about that lately and I honestly don't know what my view says about it.
