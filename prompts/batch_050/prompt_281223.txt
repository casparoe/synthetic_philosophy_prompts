write me a satirical advice column, about 600 words, in the voice of a relationships counselor who read Strawson on the reactive attitudes and decided to adopt the objective stance toward literally everyone in his life -- his wife, his teenage kids, his clients, the guy who cut him of in traffic this morning. he answers three reader letters (make them up: something like a woman furious at her lying brother, a man whose best friend forgot his birthday for the third year running, and someone consumed with gratitude toward a mentor) and responds to each entirely from within the objective attitude: people are to be understood, managed, worked around, maybe treated, but never resented, never thanked, never expected to reciprocate anything. keep him deadpan and internally consistent -- don't let gratitude or hurt feelings sneak back in through the door, with one deliberate exception: at some point he should slip, briefly, in a way that quietly lands Strawson's own suspicion that an actual human being in actual relationships can't sustain this stance, and he should paper over the slip without appearing to notice it. the humor has to come from how reasonable and professional he sounds while giving completely chilling advice, not from making him a clown. someone who knows the participant/objective attitude distinction should feel the columnist genuinely understands it, and someone who doesn't should still sense exactly what's off about the guy. no winking at the reader, no last-paragraph moral where he recants.
