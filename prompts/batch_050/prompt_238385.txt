My friend has pretty settled views on when it's okay for two people who morally disagree to strike a deal where each gives the other what the other wants. Here's where she's landed so far:

- A vegan and a climate activist make a pact: the activist gives up meat, the vegan gives up flying, each because the other cares more. She says this is great, basically a free win for both their values.
- Two donors each think their own charity is the better one, but each is willing to give more than the other. They agree to swap: each donates their larger amount to the other's preferred charity. She says fine, clever even.
- I offer my brother $500 to quit smoking because I genuinely think smoking is wrecking his health and he doesn't care. She says that's fine, it's my money and his choice.
- Someone offers a broke stranger cash to vote for a particular candidate. She says absolutely not, that's corruption even if both parties walk away happy.
- A lobbyist offers a city councillor a big donation, not to the councillor personally but to a local charity the councillor sincerely loves, in exchange for voting yes on a zoning change. She also says no.

Now the new case. Two ordinary citizens, no money involved anywhere. Anna cares passionately about the school board race and thinks her candidate is clearly better; Ben feels the same way about the city council race and is indifferent about the school board. They agree: Anna votes for Ben's council candidate, Ben votes for Anna's school board candidate. Each is voting against their own actual judgment in one race to get what they want in the other.

What do you think she says, and more importantly: what's the rule she seems to be running given her track record? I can see at least two rules that fit all five of her verdicts -- one that's about whether money buys political action, and one that's about whether the thing being traded was ever yours to trade in the first place -- and they seem to split on Anna and Ben. Tell me which rule you'd bet on and why, and what further case would actually settle which rule she's using.
