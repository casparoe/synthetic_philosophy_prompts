I've been chewing on forgiveness for a while now, partly because of stuff in my own family, and I want to know whether what I actually believe hangs together or whether I just say whatever fits the conversation I'm in. Here's roughly where I've landed.

One, forgiveness can be obligatory. If someone has genuinely repented, apologized, made what amends they can, and actually changed, then still refusing to forgive them is a moral failing on the refuser's part. That's what we mean when we call someone cold or self-righteous about it.

Two, real forgiveness has to be unconditional. Forgiveness you only hand over once the other person has apologized and groveled enough is a transaction, not forgiveness. The whole point is that it's a gift, not payment for services rendered.

Three, forgiving someone who doesn't even think they did anything wrong is condoning the wrong. It tells them, and yourself, that what they did didn't matter, and that's a failure of self-respect.

Four, forgiveness is an act you perform. When someone says I forgive you and means it, that's it, the forgiving has happened right there.

Five, what forgiveness is, at bottom, is giving up your resentment toward the person. That's the thing itself.

Six, forgiving someone doesn't mean reconciling with them or trusting them again. You can fully forgive someone and still never want them in your life.

For what it's worth, the two cases that got me here: I recently told my sister she's wrong to still be icing out our dad, who sincerely apologized for years of drinking and has been sober for a decade now. And last year I decided to forgive an old friend who stiffed me on money and to this day insists he did nothing wrong, because I was tired of carrying it around. I feel fine about both of these moves and I have a sneaking suspicion my list above doesn't let me have both, but I can't quite see where.

So: which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which ones only look like they are, and for each real tension, how severe is it and what would the cheapest fix be? Rank them by severity instead of treating them all as equally bad. And tell me which single revision would clean up the most, though I'm not promising I'll take it.
