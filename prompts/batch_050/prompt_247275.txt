Write an Ask Moloch advice column, about 600-700 words, in the style of a magazine agony-aunt page: three or four reader letters with Moloch's replies. Moloch here is the god of multipolar traps and races to the bottom, and the gimmick is that he gives sincerely helpful advice from inside his own logic -- he never lies, he just takes the trap as given and treats the letter-writer's scruples as the bug to be fixed. The letters should cover real traps: e.g. a grad student who doesn't want to work weekends while everyone in her lab does, a small-business owner who hates writing clickbait while his honest competitors go under, a parent sick of the extracurricular arms race. But one letter should be from someone proposing an actual coordination mechanism -- a union, a treaty, a norm with teeth -- and Moloch should get visibly nervous answering that one, since its the only genre of thing that can actually hurt him.

The jokes should come from getting the mechanism right: why unilateral restraint is just unilateral disarmament, why opting out selects for people who won't, why whoever defects first gets to call it being pragmatic. Keep Moloch deadpan and internally consistent: pick one settled position for him (I'd suggest: individual virtue inside a trap is a subsidy to your competitors) and don't let him drift into generic cartoon villainy, or worse, accidentally say something wise.

After the column, give me a table with these columns: Letter | Trap it instantiates | Why going it alone fails | What would actually break the trap. Commit to real answers in that last column -- an enforcement mechanism, an outside party, a changed payoff structure -- not just "better coordination." And if one of the letters honestly has no good answer, say so in the table rather than fudging it.
