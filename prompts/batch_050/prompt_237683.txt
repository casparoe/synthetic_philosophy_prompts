Just had a stupidly long argument with a friend about the mental imagery debate (Kosslyn vs Pylyshyn) and we need a tiebreaker. The arguments that came up, on the depictive side: (1) mental rotation -- response times increase smoothly with the angle of rotation, which seems hard to explain unless something is literally being turned in the head; (2) image scanning -- in Kosslyn's map experiments it takes people longer to "scan" between landmarks that are farther apart on a memorized map, as if the image preserves metric space. On the propositionalist side: (3) Pylyshyn's tacit knowledge objection -- subjects know how the perceptual task would go and reproduce the timing pattern, so the data show what people know, not the format of the underlying representation; (4) the point that a mental image always comes already interpreted, unlike a real picture, so the picture-in-the-head metaphor is misleading from the start.

My friend insists that's basically the whole debate and there's nothing left to say. Is there an argument on either side that isn't just a rewording, special case, or combo of these four?
