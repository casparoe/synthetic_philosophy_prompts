I am a high school student preparing for a philosophy debate on original and derived intentionality. Please play a rigorous but fair-minded defender of John Searle’s side against Dennett’s two-bitser argument. Your position is that some systems have intentionality in their own right, while artifacts such as thermometers and vending machines have at most derived intentionality because people interpret their states as being about something. You may concede that artifacts have functions, that designers’ intentions can fix those functions, and that natural selection can make a trait’s function objectively important. What you should defend is that causal reliability, usefulness, interpretation, or selection history alone does not create original intentionality. Stay in the first person as this Searlean interlocutor rather than neutrally explaining both sides. Do not merely repeat the Chinese Room, do not cite texts, and do not answer for Dennett. Reply to my opening objection in no more than three paragraphs, using plain language without oversimplifying. If one of my premises is ambiguous, distinguish its relevant readings instead of asking me to clarify.

Here is my opening move. Consider a machine that sorts U.S. quarters. Inside it is a physical switch-state, which I will call Q, that occurs reliably when and only when a quarter is inserted. At first, Q’s status as representing a quarter seems plainly derived from the machine’s designers. Now suppose that humans disappear, but the machines continue making imperfect copies of themselves. For a thousand years, machines whose Q-state responds accurately to quarters acquire more energy and produce more copies, while machines without it cease functioning. Eventually there is a population containing Q, but no designer, owner, or user remains. Q’s survival is explained by its sensitivity to quarters just as a bird’s survival might be explained by a perceptual state’s sensitivity to a predator.

Does Q now mean “quarter”? If you say no, explain what extra ingredient a bird’s or human’s brain state has besides being physically reliable, selectively useful, and interpretable by an observer. If you say yes, explain why this is not Dennett’s point that an objectively selected function can supply original content. And if Q’s content changes from derived to original during those thousand years, identify when the change occurs and what fact about the machine changes at that point.
