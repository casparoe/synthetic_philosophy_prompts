Hey, so I'm putting together a little interactive thing for a reading group on climate ethics and I want a decision tree / sorting instrument on solar geoengineering -- specifically stratospheric aerosol injection, not the carbon removal stuff. The idea is people answer questions and land on a named position about what, if anything, we should do.

Here's the angle I want, since the usual "pro vs con" split is useless: I don't care much whether people think SRM would *work* technically. I want the tree to sort on the normative and political fault lines instead. Please build it from these branching questions (you can reorder or split them if that's cleaner, but they all have to appear):

1. Does the possibility that researching SRM slows emissions cuts (the moral hazard worry) count as a reason against *research itself*, or only against deployment?
2. Is termination shock -- the rapid warming if you start and then abruptly stop -- a decisive objection, a manageable engineering/governance problem, or actually an argument *for* starting early while institutions are still functional?
3. Can any deployment be legitimate without something like global consent, and if not, does that mean de facto prohibition forever, given that consent will never come?
4. Does it matter who does it -- a UN body, a coalition of vulnerable states, one desperate country, a billionaire -- or are the ethics the same regardless of agent?
5. Is there a morally relevant difference between "altering the climate on purpose" and "altering it as a side effect of two centuries of emissions," or is that just squeamishness?

Requirements: 8-12 leaves, each with a name (real label if one exists -- e.g. things in the neighborhood of "research but don't deploy," "deploy only under emergency," "categorical prohibition" -- or a made-up one if the slot is empty), plus one sentence on the leaf's most awkward implication. Flag at least two combinations of answers that are logically fine but that nobody seems to actually hold, and say briefly why you think that seat is empty -- incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any two real positions the tree *can't* tell apart, and say what extra question would be needed to split them.

Ask the questions as concrete scenarios, not as "do you believe in moral hazard?" E.g. for the consent one, something like: a small island state facing inundation announces it will begin injections unilaterally in five years and invites others to join governance -- is that threat, invitation, or crime? Keep it casual in tone, it's for a reading group not a journal.
