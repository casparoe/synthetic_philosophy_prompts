I'm a TA for an undergrad philosophy of probability course, about 18 students, one 75-minute session. Design an in-class activity that makes them feel the force of the thirding argument in the Sleeping Beauty problem -- the one where on awakening you should put probability 1/3 on the coin having landed heads. Ideally they play some game with a hidden coin flip, repeated awakenings (and maybe amnesia potions simulated somehow), and small-stakes bets, and the students who offer 1:1 odds keep bleeding points to the ones who offer 2:1, without anyone being told beforehand why. Rules, materials, payoff structure that doesn't use real money, how to handle the memory-wipe mechanic in a classroom without actual amnesia, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the halfer/thirder dispute -- including giving the halfer position a fair shake, since some of them will insist the game was rigged against them. Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (e.g. students counting awakenings wrong, or someone figuring out the trick early and spoiling it), and which feature of the original puzzle the activity can't reproduce because real classrooms can't actually duplicate a person. Don't lecture them on the puzzle up front and don't let them hear the name Sleeping Beauty until the debrief.
