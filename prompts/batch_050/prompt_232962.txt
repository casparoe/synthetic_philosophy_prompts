After years of reading rationalist blogs and forums, I’ve become wary of two opposite misreadings of the Gödelian analogy in *Gödel, Escher, Bach*: treating incompleteness as a mystical proof that minds transcend mechanism, and treating self-reference as nothing more than a clever formal trick. I want a poem for a reading group that captures the subtler middle position: a formal system can encode statements about its own derivations, while a mind might emerge from symbol-level processes that model, interpret, and modify the very system in which they occur. The analogy should feel illuminating without becoming a proof of consciousness.

Write exactly five numbered quatrains, with no introduction, commentary, or conclusion. Each stanza should contain one or two grammatical sentences and trace one stage of the ascent: meaningless formal marks, arithmetic encoding, a sentence indirectly asserting its own unprovability, the analogy to a brain’s self-model, and finally the caution that a strange loop is not yet an explanation of awareness. The last line should repeat the first line with exactly one word changed, so the poem’s structure enacts the return-with-difference characteristic of a strange loop. Do not mention Gödel, Hofstadter, or the book by name; someone familiar with the argument should recognize it from the movement of ideas, while someone unfamiliar should still encounter a coherent and compelling poem.
