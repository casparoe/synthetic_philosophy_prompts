Longtime lurker here, finally posting because I keep flip-flopping on anthropic reasoning and want to be argued off the fence.

Everyone round here knows the Presumptuous Philosopher setup: theory A says there are a trillion observers, theory B says there are a trillion trillion, both fit all the available evidence, and the Self-Indication Assumption makes you favour B by an absurd factor even though B has zero independent empirical support. Most people I read take this as close to a reductio of SIA, and on bad days I do too.

What I want from you: argue that the Presumptuous Philosopher does NOT actually refute SIA — that the presumptuousness is being generated somewhere else, e.g. by the way the observer-counts get stipulated with no empirical backstory, or by a reference class choice that SIA doesn't force on you. Give the strongest version of that defence you can, not a strawman you'd enjoy knocking down. And here's a wrinkle I haven't seen discussed much: in B, suppose the extra observers only exist in the far future after a phase transition nobody in the present could possibly influence. Does your defence still work, or does the case get more damning? Say explicitly.

If part of the case against SIA survives even your best defence, name the part rather than pretending the whole thing dissolves. Also tell me what would change your verdict — which argument or consideration would make you conclude the philosopher really is being presumptuous and SIA has to go, or where your defence is most likely to have a hole in it.

Under 400 words please. I don't want a literature survey, I want the actual argument, and I'd rather have one done properly than five done quickly.
