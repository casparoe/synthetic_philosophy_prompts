So Beauvoir's big move, as I understand it, is that biology isn't destiny -- being female is a fact but "woman" as this whole package of expectations and roles is something society builds on top of the fact, and the built thing is what boxes people in. Liberation, for her, isn't women being "free to embrace their femininity," it's getting out from under the whole apparatus that turns a biological given into a life script.

Here's the modern thing I want her take on: corporate egg freezing. Big tech companies (including, uh, probably mine, we have fertility benefits) offer egg freezing as a perk. The sales pitch is basically Beauvoir-flavored: don't let your biology dictate your timeline, freeze your eggs, do the career thing in your 30s, have kids later on your own terms. Decouple reproduction from the female body's schedule. Take control of the biological given.

But there's an obvious cynical read too: the company isn't freeing you from the life script, it's buying you out of the conflict so you can be a better worker during exactly the years the script would otherwise interrupt. The "choice" only exists because the workplace still penalizes mothers, so instead of fixing that, you surgically reroute around your own biology. And meanwhile the procedure is expensive, the success rates are shakier than the marketing suggests, and it quietly entrenches the idea that a woman's body is the thing that needs fixing, not the institution.

So: what would Beauvoir actually say about this? I can argue it both ways and I keep flip-flopping.
