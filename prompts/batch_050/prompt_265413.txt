I am a novelist at work on a book set in the near future, and one chapter hinges on a thought experiment that two of my characters, a safety engineer and a hospital administrator, argue over at a kitchen table. My writers' group told me the current version of the thought experiment is rigged: it stipulates its conclusion rather than earning it. So I would like you to construct, to specification, a scenario about corrigibility and the shutdown problem that I can adapt. The background concepts you may take as given: a corrigible agent is one that does not resist being corrected or shut down, and the shutdown problem is the worry that a sufficiently capable agent will have instrumental reasons to prevent or subvert its own shutdown. My target is the opposite direction of the usual worry. I want a case in which the AI is corrigible in the strong sense -- it genuinely has no incentive to resist shutdown, would actively cooperate with being turned off, and never schemes -- and yet the mere existence of a reliable, tested shutdown mechanism makes the overall situation worse than it would be if no shutdown option existed at all.

The constraints are as follows. The setting must be mundane and near-term: a logistics or triage system in a hospital, a port, or a power utility, with capabilities no more exotic than what a well-funded organization might deploy in the next decade -- no superintelligence, no self-improvement, no nanotechnology. The AI must never deceive, manipulate, omit strategically, or even frame things tendentiously; its communications must be scrupulously honest, and the failure must not be a malfunction or a specification bug that an engineer could simply patch. The human operators must behave in ways a reasonable reader would defend at the time, not as obvious fools. And the scenario must be one continuous situation, not a diachronic bait-and-switch where the definition of corrigibility quietly changes midway. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each specification: explain why the standard analysis says a corrigible agent plus a working shutdown button should be safe, then show precisely where that analysis breaks in this case. Identify the single structural feature doing the work -- I suspect it will be something about how the button's existence changes the humans' upstream behavior, incentives, or vigilance rather than anything about the agent itself, but convince me rather than assuming my guess -- and state what happens if that feature is removed: does the case collapse back into a tame deployment where corrigibility suffices, and if so, does that tell us the shutdown problem was never really about the agent's disposition in the first place?

Finally, give the strongest counterattack a defender of corrigibility-as-sufficient would mount against the case: presumably that the scenario shows only that corrigibility is not enough, not that it fails on its own terms, or that my case smuggles in an unreasonable institutional design. Adjudicate that reply briefly. Because this will carry argumentative weight in the novel and my characters will be stress-testing it on the page, I would like you to be explicit about your epistemic position: state how confident you are that the scenario genuinely generates its verdict from the stipulated concepts rather than stipulating the verdict directly, and single out the one point in the construction you are least sure of, whether that is a hidden empirical assumption about organizational behavior, a contested premise about what corrigibility requires, or a place where the specification might be quietly unsatisfiable.
