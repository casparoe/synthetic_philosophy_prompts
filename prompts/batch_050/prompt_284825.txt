I have been turning over my intuitions about when it is appropriate to celebrate someone's success, and when it is appropriate -- even virtuous -- to puncture it, and I would like to know whether they add up to any coherent principle. I grew up in a culture where standing out was treated as a mild offense against everyone else, and I now live in one where striving and displaying your achievements is practically a civic duty, so I no longer trust my gut, but here is what my gut says.

A colleague lands a prestigious fellowship and mentions it once over dinner, plainly pleased: I feel genuine delight and think the celebration is entirely fine. The same colleague posting weekly updates about the fellowship for two months: I think he is doing something wrong, though I struggle to say what. A self-made man who grew up poor and now flaunts his wealth: I feel he has earned the right, even if I find it tasteless. A lottery winner celebrating his windfall at length: this grates on me more than the self-made man's flaunting, though by desert it should grate less -- he earned nothing. An athlete trash-talking after a championship win strikes me as fine, part of the game; a graduate student announcing his top mark to classmates who failed the same exam strikes me as cruel, though the boasting is milder. Mocking a famous billionaire whose rocket explodes on launch feels permissible, even healthy; mocking the owner of the corner bakery whose business folds feels vicious, though the bakery owner is not obviously less deserving of sympathy. I admire the friend who attributes her promotion to luck when I know she worked nights for years -- yet something about the performance of modesty also seems like a small lie we have all agreed to require of her, and I am not sure requiring it is just. Finally, celebrating one's own success seems fine among equals but wrong in front of someone who just failed at the very same thing.

Is there a principle here -- perhaps something about desert versus luck, something about relative standing and equality between the people present, or something about the direction in which the mockery travels? Please state the best candidate precisely, clause by clause, saying which of my cases motivates each clause and which of my verdicts it cannot save. Then tell me what it predicts for two cases I have not listed: a first-generation college student posting her acceptance letter where her unemployed cousin will see it, and a retiring athlete's farewell ceremony devoted to his own accomplishments. If the price of saving every verdict is a principle too gerrymandered to believe, tell me which verdict I should surrender.
