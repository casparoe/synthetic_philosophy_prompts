I'm part of an informal reading group that has been working through cooperative bargaining theory this semester, and last week we got pleasantly stuck on the rivalry between the Nash bargaining solution and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution — in particular on whether Kalai and Smorodinsky's individual monotonicity axiom really is more compelling than Nash's independence of irrelevant alternatives. The argument got spirited enough that one member joked the group itself needed binding arbitration, and that gave me an idea for a piece of writing I'd like help executing.

Write a parody advice column — the classic agony-aunt genre, but for the imaginary "applied game theory" page of a newspaper — in which an invented mediator, a fervent and humorless devotee of the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution, answers three letters from readers with mundane disputes: a couple negotiating which city to relocate to, roommates dividing up household chores, and siblings splitting a small inheritance. The comedy should come from the mediator applying the theory with total technical fidelity and total social obliviousness: carefully establishing each party's disagreement point, computing the utopia point from the best each side could hope for within the feasible set, and then prescribing a division in which both parties make equal proportional concessions relative to their maximum aspirations, as though this settled the emotional matter entirely. The mediator should also work in at least one withering aside about a rival school of thought that would let the outcome stay put even when the feasible set expands in one party's favor — a swipe at independence of irrelevant alternatives — delivered with the confidence of someone who considers the case closed. The humor has to exaggerate real features of the solution concept rather than invent fake ones, so that a game theorist recognizes the satire as technically literate and, ideally, fair, while a lay reader still finds the pompous mediator funny on the surface.

Aim for about 600 to 700 words. Keep the mediator completely deadpan throughout, with no winking at the reader, and please don't name any real scholars — the columnist, the letter-writers, and any rival theorists mentioned should all be invented. Format the reply as continuous prose, the way a printed column would read: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, with the letters and replies woven naturally into the flow.
