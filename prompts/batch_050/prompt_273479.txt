A buddy of mine in law school sent me this argument last week and it's been bugging me, because the conclusion seems nuts but I can't put my finger on the break. Setup first: a music festival counts 1000 people inside the grounds, but only 10 tickets were ever sold, so 990 of them hopped the fence. The organizers grab one attendee, more or less at random, and sue him for the ticket price. They have nothing on him personally -- nobody saw him climb the fence, he claims he paid cash to a scalper and lost the stub. Just the statistic: a 99% chance he's a crasher.

Now the argument (his, slightly cleaned up by me):

(1) In a civil suit the standard is preponderance of the evidence: if the evidence makes your claim more likely than not, you win. 99% clears that bar with room to spare.
(2) Still, it would be unjust to find this guy liable on the statistic alone. If that were enough, the organizers could sue every one of the 1000 attendees seperately and win all 1000 suits -- including the suits against the 10 people who actually paid.
(3) But think about why any piece of evidence ever supports a verdict. The eyewitness is believed because eyewitnesses are usually right; the DNA match convicts because labs almost never mess up; the confession counts because innocent people rarely confess. Strip away the background reliability statistic and no piece of evidence means anything at all.
(4) So every verdict, however "individualized" it feels, rests at bottom on a bare statistic of exactly the same kind as the gatecrasher's 99%.
(5) By (2), bare statistics can't justify a verdict on their own. By (4), that's all we ever have. So no verdict is ever justified -- not the eyewitness case, not the DNA case, none of them.
(6) That's absurd, since obviously some verdicts are justfied.
(7) So (2) is wrong, and we should find the gatecrasher liable after all.

I can't swallow (7), but (5) is obviously unacceptable too. Where does this actually go wrong, if it does? If I have to pick, I'd rather keep (2) than give up on eyewitness testimony and DNA, but I want to know exactly what rejecting each step costs me. My friend insists the argument is airtight, which makes me think I'm missing something.
