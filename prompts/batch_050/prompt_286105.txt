I'm putting together a short statement of my views on complicity for a workshop discussion group and I want to stress-test it before I embarrass myself. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm a difference-making person about complicity. If the bad thing would have gone down exactly the same without you, you weren't part of it. Participation that changes nothing isn't participation.

2. Buying factory-farmed meat is wrong, and wrong in a complicity way, not just "bad vibes." You're throwing in with the industry. I know the usual point that any individual purchase has basically no chance of changing what gets produced, but I don't think that gets the buyer off the hook.

3. If I inherit money that was originally made through some historical injustice (say a great-grandfather's plantation), and the actual victims are long dead and untraceable, I don't think I'm complicit in the original wrong by accepting it. Benefiting from something finished isn't participating in it.

4. Knowingly buying stolen goods is wrong, even when the theft already happened and my refusing the sale wouldn't have undone it or gotten anyone's stuff back.

5. On the other hand I do think corporations that are still profiting today from assets seized in historical injustices owe restitution even when the original victims are equally dead and untraceable.

What I want is an audit: which of these are actually in tension with each other, which ones only look like they clash, and how bad each tension is. Please rank them by severity instead of giving me a flat list, and for each real one tell me concretely what has to give -- which commitment I'd have to drop or weaken, and what the cheapest repair costs me in terms of other things I'd want to keep saying. I'd especially like to know if there's a single revision that fixes the worst problem without wrecking the rest.

Two requests: first, don't just tell me it all hangs together to be nice -- I'm genuinely not sure it does and I'd rather find out now. Second, if I've set this up badly, e.g. if one of these commitments is confused on its own terms rather than just clashing with the others, or if the whole "audit my beliefs" framing is asking for something that can't really be delivered, say that plainly instead of working around it.
