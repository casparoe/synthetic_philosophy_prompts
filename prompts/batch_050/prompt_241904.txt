I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on logic and language, and I need a referee for an argument two colleagues had after our truth seminar. Someone had written on the whiteboard, as the only sentence there: The sentence on this whiteboard is not true.

(1) Dana argued that the sentence fails to express a proposition at all: meaningful truth-ascribing sentences must ultimately bottom out in non-semantic facts, and this one loops back on itself without ever reaching the ground, so the paradox dissolves rather than gets solved. The sentence is ungrounded, hence neither true nor false.

(2) Eli replied that this merely invites revenge: if the sentence expresses no proposition, then in particular it is not true -- but that is exactly what it says, so by Dana's own lights it comes out true after all. Declaring it ungrounded reproduces the paradox instead of dissolving it.

(3) Dana answered that Eli's move trades on an equivocation: the step from "it is not true" to "it is true" runs through the phrase "that is exactly what it says," but if the sentence says nothing, there is no content for the facts to match, so that step is blocked. We, standing outside the loop, can correctly judge the sentence not to be true; it does not follow that the sentence is true.

(4) Eli's final move was that meaningfulness cannot hinge on empirical accident. Suppose the board had instead read: The sentence on the whiteboard across the hall is false. Whether that sentence is paradoxical depends on what happens to be written across the hall, which the writer may not know when writing it. So the sentence must already have its content before any empirical check -- grounding can be a precondition of truth, but not of expressing a proposition.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly right to clearly mistaken, justify each rating briefly, and say who is more right overall and what, if anything, both are missing. Lead with your verdict on the overall dispute in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Please write in British English.
