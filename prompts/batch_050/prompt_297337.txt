I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized company that makes a habit-tracking and wellbeing app. I have no formal training in philosophy or ethics, but I was recently asked to sit on our internal product ethics review board, and our first real case has landed.

The product team wants approval for a retention experiment on roughly 2% of our active users for six weeks. Users in the test arm would receive reminders phrased to induce mild guilt when they miss a scheduled session (for example, messages implying they have broken a promise to themselves or wasted their own past effort), while the control arm receives neutral reminders. The team has two further requests. First, they do not want to tell participants that an experiment is running: they argue that advance disclosure would change how users respond to the notifications and thereby invalidate the results, and they propose instead covering the experiment through a quiet update to our terms of service, accepted by the usual click-through banner. Second, they note that around a tenth of the experimental pool would be users aged 13 to 17, whose accounts were opened with a parent or guardian's email confirmation when they first registered.

Please draft a memo from me to the rest of the board recommending a decision: approve, reject, or approve with specified conditions. The memo should have an actionable bottom line the board can vote on. It should reason carefully about whether the consent we would be relying on is valid, and I would like the analysis structured around the three pressure points I keep circling: deception (is a vague terms-of-service clause, agreed to without knowing an experiment exists, adequate disclosure, and does the team's claim that disclosure would corrupt the data justify withholding it?), coercion or manipulation (do guilt-framed notifications pressure users in a way that bears on consent itself, or is that a separate harm?), and competence (does a guardian's one-off confirmation at sign-up cover a teenager's ongoing participation in research they do not know about?). Please give the strongest honest statement of the product team's position — including the point that experiments of this general kind are routine across the industry and that the harm, if any, is minor — before explaining where, on balance, you think it succeeds or fails. Finally, the memo should end with three or four general criteria the board can apply to future experiments of this kind, worded so that engineers rather than philosophers can operationalise them.

Please write in British English.
