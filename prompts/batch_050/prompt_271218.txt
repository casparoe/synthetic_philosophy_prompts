In the post-mortem, my director argued that the system isn't lying and isn't even making errors in the usual sense; it's bullshitting, in roughly Harry Frankfurt's sense: indifferent to whether what it says is true, unconstrained by any genuine concern for the facts, producing whatever sounds good for the occasion. He leaned on this analogy to conclude that calling its outputs "mistakes" is confused, that the behavior is intrinsic rather than fixable, and that a disclaimer is therefore a more honest response than further engineering. Please audit the analogy for me, in under 600 words. Give the correspondence explicitly: what in the model plays the role of the bullshitter's indifference to truth, the bullshitter's concern with sounding impressive or persuasive, and Frankfurt's further claim that the bullshitter misrepresents not just reality but their own enterprise. Then identify where the mapping breaks: whether a system without intentions can be indifferent in the relevant sense, whether the analogy smuggles in a mind it then denies exists, and whether "error" and "bullshit" are even exclusive categories here. Finally, say whether my director's practical conclusions survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and if the bullshitter analogy fails, propose the better one.
