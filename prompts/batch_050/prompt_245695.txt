A friend in my bioethics reading group laid this out and honestly it's been bugging me for a week. She argues:

(1) A doctor may only enroll a patient in a study if doing so is compatible with her basic duty to act in that patient's best medical interest.
(2) Acting in a patient's best medical interest means giving them whichever treatment you judge, on the evidence available, to be best for them.
(3) Randomizing means deliberately leaving it to chance whether the patient gets the treatment you'd judge best. So enrollment is only ethical when the doctor genuinely has no judgment either way -- real, personal, in-the-gut uncertainty.
(4) But a trial only gets designed and funded in the first place because there IS evidence and argument pulling informed people in different directions -- and anyone informed enough to enroll patients has usually read the pilot data and formed some lean, however tentative.
(5) So essentially no properly-informed doctor is ever in the state (3) requires, and nearly every randomized trial ever run -- including the ones that established treatments we now take for granted -- was unethical.

She's happy to bite that bullet, which is why I suspect something's off. (3) to (4) feels like where it slips to me, since she jumps from "no judgment either way" to "no lean at all," but I can't make the objection precise, and I know there's a standard move in this debate about whose uncertainty counts (the individual doctor's vs. the expert community's) that might rescue the argument instead of sinking it. I also know some people just reject (1) wholesale and say trials aren't therapy at all, but that feels like it gives up too much -- I want to keep the idea that doctors owe their patients something.

Where exactly does the argument go wrong, and what does rejecting that step cost? If you think it's actually valid, tell me which premise is cheapest to abandon and what you lose by abandoning it. Either way, say what consideration would change your verdict -- e.g. is there a trial design or case where your diagnosis would stop applying.

Give me your answer as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro and no conclusion.
