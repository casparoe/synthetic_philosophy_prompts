Two friends of mine got into a long argument over drinks last week and I've been turning it over since. Background: Mara recently got into panpsychism, the view that experience is a fundamental feature of matter, so even electrons or quarks have some tiny flicker of what-it's-like-ness. Dev thinks panpsychism is false but wanted to argue on her terms. It went roughly like this:

(1) Mara said panpsychism, if true, is an ethical emergency. If micro-experiences can have valence -- if an electron's flicker can feel *bad* -- then the universe is drowning in suffering. Every star is running quadrillions of fusion reactions a second, and each one might be a tiny pang of pain. She said physicists smashing particles in colliders might be committing atrocities on a scale that makes factory farming look trivial, and nobody's even thinking about it.

(2) Dev said this overgenerates. Valence isn't just raw experience; suffering requires functional structure -- something like a system with states it aims at, that can go better or worse *for it*. A bare electron has no goals, no integrity to be damaged, nothing that being in this state versus that state threatens. So electron experience, if it exists at all, is morally inert -- like a blank pixel, not a pain.

(3) Mara said that's complexity chauvinism dressed up as analysis. The whole point of panpsychism is that experience doesn't wait for fancy functional organization -- it's there at the ground floor. So why assume valence *does* wait? Maybe valence is just as fundamental as experience itself, and an electron is simply, intrinsically, in a minuscule amount of discomfort. Demanding goals and damage-conditions is importing criteria from human psychology that the theory explicitly rejects.

(4) Dev switched tactics: if she's right, panpsychism refutes itself by reductio. Her picture makes the moral ledger of the universe totally dominated by particles -- the suffering in one second of one star outweighs all suffering in human history by an absurd factor. Any theory that renders all the evil we actually care about a rounding error can't be taken seriously as ethics, so something in her setup has to go.

(5) Mara said that's not a reductio, it's just an implication he finds weird, and weirdness isn't an argument. Maybe utilitarian arithmetic genuinely *is* dominated by micro-experience. You can't reject a conclusion because it's inconvenient; that's exactly what people said to the animal welfare people.

(6) Then Dev brought up the combination problem: even granting particles feel, their experiences don't fuse into one big subject. The sun isn't a single sufferer; it's a heap of disconnected micro-pangs with no unified experiencer. And a trillion disconnected flickers plausibly matter less than one integrated subject in real agony -- the badness of suffering seems tied to someone suffering *as a whole*, and the heap lacks that.

(7) Mara said she doesn't see why unification is morally load-bearing. If ten separate people each stub a toe, the badness isn't reduced by the fact that no single subject feels all ten stubs. Suffering matters wherever it occurs, and aggregating it across separate subjects is just arithmetic.

(8) Dev's last move: he said her indifference to unification cuts against her. If integration adds nothing morally, then there's no principled reason destroying a complex, unified mind is worse than rearranging an equal mass of disorganized matter -- and she clearly doesn't believe demolishing a person and scrambling a rock are on a par.

They both asked me to call it and I keep flip-flopping. Can you go through the exchange move by move -- for each of the eight steps, say who has the better of it and why -- and then give an overall verdict? Also: is there some distinction or assumption in the background that both of them are missing, which would dissolve at least part of the fight?
