Kendall Walton's transparency thesis holds that photographs are transparent: when I look at a photograph of my late grandmother, I literally see her, albeit indirectly, much as I see things through mirrors or telescopes. Here are the arguments for the thesis that I have collected:

(1) The prosthesis-of-vision argument. We already count it as seeing when we look through mirrors, periscopes, binoculars, and live video feeds; a photograph differs from a live feed only in introducing a temporal delay, and temporal delay is no bar to seeing in other cases -- we see stars whose light left them long ago.

(2) The belief-independent counterfactual dependence argument. A photograph's appearance would have been different had the scene been different, regardless of what anyone believed or intended, whereas a painting's appearance is mediated by the painter's beliefs; photography thus preserves the kind of belief-independent causal-cum-counterfactual link to its object that genuine seeing requires.

(3) The phenomenological-linguistic argument. We spontaneously say "that's my grandmother" rather than "that represents my grandmother," we point at photos to settle what someone looked like, and the depicted object strikes us as present rather than merely described -- which is best explained if photographs really do let us see.

Is there another argument for the transparency thesis that I am missing? It should be genuinely different from these three -- not a rewording, special case, or conjunction of items already on the list. State the argument, explain how it differs from (1)-(3), and say whether you think it is actually any good.
