i’m helping design an eval for whether an AI system actually understands a rule rather than merely continuing examples in a statistically convenient way. Before anyone proposes benchmarks, write the desiderata any adequate account of rule-following in a language game has to meet.

Use Wittgenstein’s ideas seriously, but don’t turn the answer into a slogan about meaning as use. The requirements should cover, at minimum:

- novel cases the system has never encountered, including ones where every finite continuation can be made compatible with multiple rules
- family-resemblance concepts with no tidy necessary-and-sufficient definition
- cases where the community changes the game midstream, or temporarily coins a category for a local purpose
- the difference between being disposed to answer a certain way and being able to treat an answer as mistaken
- cases where correct use depends on the surrounding activity, not just the sentence or dataset
- apparent counterexamples produced by an insider who deliberately plays dumb, bends the rules, or follows a perverse but describable rule

For each requirement, explain the case that motivates it and why a merely predictive or similarity-based account would miss it. Also distinguish things the theory must explain from things it should refuse to explain because they presuppose the wrong picture.

Flag any desiderata that seem jointly unsatisfiable, especially the tension between normativity and the contingency of shared practices. At the end, briefly score three candidate accounts against your list: an explicit-rule account, a prototype/similarity account, and a practice-based account. But keep the spec itself the main deliverable. typos are fine, i mainly want the conceptual map
