A friend of mine is unusually reflective about decisions involving very small probabilities and very large stakes, and over the years I have collected a series of her verdicts.

1. A stranger approaches her in the street, claims to wield powers beyond current science, and says that unless she hands over $50 he will cause a trillion conscious beings to suffer in a remote region of reality. She declines without visible deliberation and walks on.

2. A caller tells her that, for a $50 donation, he can bring into existence an enormous population of happy beings elsewhere in the cosmos. She hangs up.

3. Asked to advise a regional government, she endorses spending heavily on an early-warning and deflection program after independent astronomers estimate that it reduces the probability of an impact devastating the region from one in 600,000 to one in 1.2 million over the next two centuries.

4. At a charity fair she buys a $2 raffle ticket offering a one-in-5,000 chance at a bicycle, fully aware that the expected value is negative.

5. She carries homeowner's insurance against fire and flood at actuarially fair rates.

Now she sits on the board of a foundation and must vote on the following. A consortium of physicists at established institutions has published a calculation, independently replicated by teams with no stake in the outcome, estimating that a planned particle experiment carries a one-in-ten-billion chance per run of triggering an event that would destroy the Earth. The board can spend $200 million to convert the facility to an alternative design that the same physicists certify as safe. She must vote yes or no.

Please do the following: (i) predict her vote, giving your best reasons for that prediction; (ii) set out at least two distinct decision rules, each consistent with all five of her earlier verdicts, that deliver opposite verdicts on the new case, and explain why her track record cannot adjudicate between them; (iii) identify which of the earlier cases is the closest analog to the new one, and say what the answer to that question depends on; (iv) state what general principle she would be committing herself to under each possible vote; and (v) describe a single further case whose resolution would most efficiently discriminate between your candidate rules.
