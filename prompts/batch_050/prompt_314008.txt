You are a teaching assistant for an upper-level course on evolution and intentionality. Grade the following exam answer. The question was worth 10 points, divided roughly equally across its three tasks.

Exam question: Explain what Dennett means by a free-floating rationale, and how the notion connects to his slogan about competence without comprehension. Then give an example of your own, distinct from Dennett's. Finally, answer and defend: does the existence of free-floating rationales show that natural selection is literally an agent with purposes?

Student answer:

A free-floating rationale is a reason that exists in nature without any mind having formulated or represented it. Natural selection tracks reasons: a trait persists because there is a reason for it — it confers some advantage — even though no one, not even the organism that bears the trait, appreciates that reason. This is the flip side of Dennett's claim that nature is full of competence without comprehension: systems can do exactly the right thing for reasons they cannot state.

My example is the honeybee's waggle dance. A returning forager performs a figure-eight dance whose angle and duration encode the direction and distance of a food source. There is an excellent reason the dance takes the form it does — it gets nestmates to the food — but the bee has no idea why it dances as it does, or even that it is communicating at all. The rationale floats free of every bee's mind. It resides, so to speak, in the bee's genome, which over evolutionary time has comprehended the geometry of nectar locations and hardwired the appropriate responses.

Does this make selection literally an agent? No. Attributing purposes to selection is a stance we take for predictive convenience, not the discovery of a cosmic deliberator; there is no homunculus behind the process. Dennett's own conclusion, however, is that since rationales can float free of reasoners, they also float free of reasoning: human deliberation, on his view, is causally idle, an epiphenomenal narration laid over processes whose real reasons were fixed by selection before any of us decided anything.
