Next month I'm giving a talk at an interdisciplinary animal welfare workshop defending the claim that, all else equal, an animal's moral weight scales with its number of neurons -- so a cow matters more than a chicken which matters more than a bee, and this should actually guide how we allocate welfare funding. The audience is roughly half animal welfare scientists (who think in terms of sentience, pain behaviors, and cognitive bias tests) and half philosophers (mostly sympathetic to utilitarian-ish animal ethics, a few deontologists who hate the whole framing). Prepare me for the Q&A: what objections each half of the room will raise, phrased the way they'd actually phrase them, which ones have real force and which are rhetorical, what I should concede early to buy goodwill, and which single reply my defense lives or dies on. Also flag the question I'm quietly hoping nobody asks, and be honest about it. Format: "Likeliest objections from the scientists" (~250 words), "Likeliest objections from the philosophers" (~250 words), "What to concede" (~100 words), "The question I don't want" (~100 words), "The reply that has to land" (~100 words).
