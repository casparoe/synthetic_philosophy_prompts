Bit of a niche request. I help run a monthly philosophy-in-the-pub night and last session we did the fine-tuning argument for the existence of God, and it went badly. Every analogy anyone produced — firing squads, lottery winners, the bloke shuffling cards and getting perfect bridge hands — felt quietly rigged in favour of whichever side the speaker already agreed with, and when challenged, nobody could say which feature of their analogy was actually doing the work. So I want to settle this properly for next month's rematch.

Construct for me a pair of matched thought experiments, both set in ordinary life (no cosmology, no gods, no universe-generating machines, nothing science-fictional), that between them isolate the contested feature. Each case must have the same evidential shape as the cosmological fine-tuning situation: (i) the observed outcome is staggeringly improbable on a one-shot chance hypothesis; (ii) the outcome is remarkable under some independent description, not merely improbable — after all, every long sequence of dice rolls is improbable in some way; and (iii) there's an observation-selection element, meaning the observer could only have made the observation at all if the outcome had occurred. Here's the twist: in Case A, inferring a huge ensemble of trials (the multiverse move) should be clearly the right call while inferring deliberate arrangement (the design move) is clearly a blunder. In Case B, with all three features still present, it should go exactly the other way — design clearly right, the ensemble inference clearly fallacious. Constraints: each case at most a paragraph, the probabilities concrete enough to eyeball (cards, hospital wards, radio static, whatever suits), and the selection element has to fall out of the setup naturally rather than being stipulated with "suppose you could only exist if...".

Then the payoff, in plain terms: name the single feature whose presence or absence flips the verdict between your two cases; say what happens to each case if you remove that feature; and then tell me — honestly, this is the bit I actually care about — whether the real cosmological fine-tuning case has the feature or not, i.e. whether it's more like your Case A or your Case B. Finish with the strongest attack a committed multiverse proponent would launch against your design-friendly case. Please answer in British English.
