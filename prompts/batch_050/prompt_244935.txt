So me and my friends got into this dumb argument about what's "true" in fiction and I realized my gut answers are all over the place, and I wanna know if there's actually one rule behind them or if I'm just inconsistent. Here are my verdicts:

1. "Sherlock Holmes lives at 221B Baker Street" — true, obviously. If someone in my english class said he lives in Manchester they'd just be wrong, the teacher would mark it wrong on a test even.

2. But if a tourist in London asked me where Sherlock Holmes lives and I said 221B Baker Street, that would be lying, or at least super misleading. And its literally the same sentence as #1. So the same words are true in one conversation and false in another?

3. "Sherlock Holmes doesn't exist" — true. But wait, then how is #1 true? If there's no Holmes, who's living at Baker Street?? This is the one that broke my brain a little.

4. "Sherlock Holmes is taller than Harry Potter" — this doesn't feel true OR false, it feels like there's just no answer, the question doesn't even make sense. My friend says crossover fanfics exist so there could be an answer but that feels wrong.

5. In the actual books, Watson's war wound is in his shoulder in one story and his leg in another one (I looked this up, Doyle apparently just messed up and nobody fixed it). So "Watson got shot in the shoulder" — true or false? It feels like both and neither at the same time.

6. "Sherlock Holmes has a spleen" — the books never say this anywhere but it feels true anyway, like it just comes along with being a normal guy. But "Sherlock Holmes has exactly two freckles on his left arm" — that feels like neither true nor false. Why does the spleen get in for free but the freckles don't? I have strong opinions here and zero justification.

7. If I write a fanfic where Holmes is a woman, "Holmes is a woman" does NOT become true, even though the original stories were also just made up by some guy. Made-up stuff can't overrule other made-up stuff. But I can't explain why Doyle's made-up stuff counts and mine doesn't, other than "he got there first," which seems like a dumb reason.

8. "Darth Vader is Luke's father" is true and everyone knows it, but apparently when the first movie came out it hadn't even been decided yet. So was it true in 1977 or did it become true later? Can a fact about a character retroactively appear?

So is there one principle that captures all of these? My teacher's answer was basically "its true if the story says so" but that instantly fails on #5 (the story says contradictory stuff) and #6 (the story says nothing about spleens). I want a rule precise enough that I can actually win arguments with it — what does it say about the hard cases (#4, #5, #8 especially), and be honest about which of my verdicts I have to give up, because I'm guessing they cant all survive.
