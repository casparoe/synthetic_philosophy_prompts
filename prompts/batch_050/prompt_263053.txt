I've been reading about Immanuel Kant's absolute prohibition on lying and the famous objection it always provokes: the murderer at the door who asks where your friend is hiding. Every discussion I can find uses this kind of case to make Kant look unreasonable, since telling the truth there seems monstrous and the consequentialist's permission to lie seems obviously correct. I want to see whether the argument can be made to run in the opposite direction, and I'd like you to build the case that does it.

Construct a thought experiment meeting all of these specifications:

1. The verdicts. An act consequentialist (who judges this one lie by its own expected consequences) concludes that lying is right. Kant concludes that lying is wrong. A rule consequentialist (who asks which general policy on honesty it would be best for everyone to adopt) sides with Kant. And the case should be engineered so that the intuitively plausible verdict also sides with Kant and the rule consequentialist, against the act consequentialist.

2. Realism. An ordinary contemporary setting — a workplace, a hospital, a school, a courtroom — with no murderers, kidnappers, or other villains engineering the dilemma. The person weighing the lie is an ordinary professional, and the lie is of a kind people in that role actually tell.

3. No cheating by bad luck. The act consequentialist's verdict must not turn out to be wrong because the lie happens to get discovered or backfires. Stipulate that the lie succeeds exactly as intended and is never found out. The defect in the act consequentialist's reasoning must be systematic — something about how the case-by-case calculation itself works — not a misfiring of this particular calculation.

4. Length. The scenario itself must fit in a single paragraph.

After the scenario, walk me through the reasoning of each of the three positions and show that the case genuinely delivers the specified verdicts — a case that merely stipulates the verdicts instead of generating them from the theories doesn't count. Then tell me: which single feature of the scenario is doing the work; what happens to each of the three verdicts if that feature is removed; and how a defender of act consequentialism would attack the case — give me the strongest objection you can, not a weak one.

Present the scenario itself first, as the very beginning of your response, with no preface and no restating of the assignment; the analysis should follow it.
