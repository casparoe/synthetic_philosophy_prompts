I've read Harry Frankfurt's short essay on bullshit and been mulling over how his framework applies to AI language models, and I'd like a consistency check on where I've landed. My views:

(1) Lying requires an intent to deceive, so no current language model can lie, no matter how false its outputs are.

(2) Bullshit, on Frankfurt's account, is marked by indifference to truth rather than by deceptive intent -- and language models fit this description perfectly, since they have no concern for whether what they produce is true or false either way.

(3) Still, calling AI hallucinations "bullshit" is misleading and unfair to the technology, because it suggests a kind of disregard that a mere tool cannot have; hallucinations are better understood as honest errors, like a person misremembering.

(4) When a person confidently passes along an AI-generated claim they never checked, that person is bullshitting -- even if the claim happens to be true.

(5) Whether something counts as bullshit shouldn't hinge on unsettleable questions about a system's inner states, like whether it really "has attitudes" toward truth.

Are these jointly tenable? Please go through them, identify which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which apparent conflicts dissolve on inspection, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be. Finally, tell me which single revision would make the whole set coherent -- I genuinely don't know which commitment I'd have to drop.
