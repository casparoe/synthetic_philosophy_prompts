I'm TAing a grad seminar on Hume's metaphysics this fall and I want to hand out a one-page flowchart on day one that sorts students into the main intepretive camps on Hume on causation before they've decided what they think. Please build it for me.

Some requirements so it actually does the job I need:

- Make it a decision tree with maybe 6-9 yes/no (or agree/disagree) questions. Each question has to be phrased as a concrete claim someone can react to without knowing the scholarly labels -- e.g. something like "Hume thinks there really are powers in objects that make effects follow, we just can't grasp them" rather than "are you a skeptical realist."
- The leaves have to include at least these: the straight regularity reading (Hume denies there's anything more to causation than constant conjunction), the projectivist reading (necessity is something the mind spreads onto the world), the New Hume / skeptical realist reading, and the view that Hume's two definitions of cause in the Enquiry aren't rival definitions of one thing but definitions of two different notions. If your tree generates other occupied leaves, great, add them.
- Two specific pressure points have to show up as diagnostic questions somewhere: (1) whether Hume's talk of "secret powers" in nature is sincere or ironic, and (2) whether a god's-eye observer could perceive necessary connection, since I think those two questions split the camps faster than anything about the definitions.
- Where the tree can't distinguish two readings that scholars fight about, say so explicitly and explain why no yes/no question separates them, instead of fudging a branch.
- Also flag any combination of answers that nobody seems to hold, and give one sentence on whether that slot is empty because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or actually unexplored.
- End each leaf with the name of the reading, one or two scholars associated with it, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment (the thing a student holding it will get grilled on in seminar).

Write it so I can basically paste it into a handout, in American English. A short intro paragraph for the students explaining how to use the flowchart would be a nice bonus.
