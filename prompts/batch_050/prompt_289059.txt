Something I do has been bugging me and I want to know if philosophy has a word for it. Concrete case: when I want to wake up early, I put my alarm clock on the other side of the bedroom before I go to sleep. Nothing stops me from moving it back next to the bed right then and there -- the trick only works because the me at 11pm and the me at 6:30am want different things, and 11pm me gets to move first. Same pattern when I delete a food delivery app on Sunday night, or leave my credit card at home before going to the mall. Nobody is forcing me. There's no contract, no friend holding me to it. It's just whichever version of me acts earlier beats whichever version would act later, purely because of the order we're in.

Now the thing I actually can't sort out: in the classic case of weakness of will, the failure is that you don't do what you currently judge best. But in my alarm clock case, at 6:30am I genuinely judge it best to stay in bed, and my 11pm self has rigged things so that my actual 6:30am judgment loses. So who's the weak one? Is my evening self the rational one protecting me from weakness, or is my evening self itself being weak by refusing to let my future self's actual judgment count? I've noticed I sometimes do the reverse too -- I deliberately don't prepay for the gym because I figure the me who's actually standing there on Tuesday knows things Sunday me doesn't. So there's some real question here about which self gets authority, and I suspect the standard picture just assumes the answer instead of arguing for it.

Is there an established name for this specific configuration -- self-versus-self conflict resolved purely by who acts first, with no external enforcement -- and how does it differ from ordinary temptation, from weakness of will proper, and from signing a binding contract with someone else? If the thing I'm describing doesn't have its own name, coin one, define it, and give a couple of boundary cases where it stops applying. Also tell me what consideration or example would make you change your verdict about which self has the better claim -- I want to know where your answer is most likely to be wrong. Give me the answer as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no conclusion.
