So I've been reading LessWrong and the alignment-adjacent blogs for like fifteen years, and there's one debate I keep seeing cycle around without ever moving: how should a superintelligent AI actually talk to us, given that it would presumably be insanely persuasive? Roughly three camps as I read it. Camp one: the honesty/deference camp — the AI should give humans information, follow instructions, stay corrigible, and never push an agenda, because any attempt to steer our beliefs is manipulation-adjacent. Camp two: the "you can't not persuade" camp — at superhuman levels of rhetoric there's no neutral option, every framing choice steers belief, so the only real question is steering toward what. Camp three: the "persuasion is fine if it's honest" camp — an AI that argues for true conclusions with good arguments is doing what a good teacher does, and a policy of never being convincing just leaves humans confidently wrong. People have been rehashing this since the old AI-box thought experiments and as far as I can tell nobody has every switched camps.

Play the role of a philosopher who's also deeply familiar with the AI safety literature, and think this through carefully — maybe start by brainstorming what each camp is actually claiming before diagnosing. What I want is a diagnosis of the impasse, not a verdict on who's right: is this a verbal disagreement over words like "manipulation" and "persuasion," a substantive empirical disagreement (about how persuasion works on human minds, or about how capable future systems will be), a values disagreement (autonomy vs. holding true beliefs), or three groups answering different questions under one heading? My own hypothesis is that camp one is secretly making an empirical bet — that corrigible behavior is even coherent for something that persuasive — while camps two and three are really fighting over values, which is why they talk past each other. But I'm not attached to that. Then, given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction, a psychology experiment, a formal result, or an argument that the question is ill-posed and should be replaced with a better one. And if you think the framing above is confused or I've mischaracterized the camps, say so plainly instead of politely working with a bad setup.
