A friend of mine who is finishing a PhD in theoretical physics and I got into a long argument last month about whether mathematical elegance should count as evidence for a physical theory. She thinks it plainly does; I kept insisting it depends on the case, and she challenged me to say which cases and why. I realized I couldn't articulate a principle, only gut verdicts, so I'm laying them out here in the hope that someone can tell me what principle, if any, I'm actually operating with. First: Maxwell added the displacement-current term to Ampère's law largely because it made the equations symmetric, and the completed equations then predicted electromagnetic waves; I think the aesthetic motivation there really was evidence-bearing, and the payoff confirms it. Second: Einstein introduced the cosmological constant because an expanding universe struck him as unacceptable, and he later abandoned it; I don't think that aesthetic motivation counted as evidence at all -- it feels like a fudge, even though the structural move looks similar to Maxwell's. Third: Dirac trusted the beauty of his relativistic electron equation through the embarrassment of its negative-energy solutions, which turned out to describe positrons; I think the elegance of that equation genuinely was evidence for it, even before the positron was found. Fourth: generations of theorists found supersymmetry and naturalness-driven models beautiful, predicted superpartners at accessible energies, and the LHC found nothing; I want to say those theorists were wrong to treat elegance as evidence -- but I'm uncomfortably aware that this verdict, paired with my verdict on Dirac, may just be hindsight bias dressed up as principle. Fifth: Kepler began convinced that circular orbits were the only fitting ones and let Tycho's data drag him to ellipses; I think he was right to let the data win, which seems to imply that elegance wasn't evidence in that case at all -- and that sits oddly with my first and third verdicts.

So here is what I'd like. Is there a coherent principle that delivers most of these verdicts -- something about whether the aesthetically motivated feature generates novel predictions versus merely accommodating known data, or about the difference between simplicity and beauty, or something else I haven't thought of? Please state the principle (or the two or three best candidates) precisely, show which of my cases motivates each clause, and be honest about which verdict or verdicts I have to surrender if I adopt it -- I suspect the Dirac and supersymmetry pair can't both stand, and I'd rather be told that bluntly than have the tension papered over. Finally, tell me what the resulting principle says about two cases I haven't taken a position on: a theory like much of contemporary string theory, widely admired for its mathematical elegance but with no currently accessible experimental consequences, and a hypothetical ugly, patched-together theory that nonetheless keeps surviving tests designed to kill it.
