Two philosophers of physics I know -- one a sophisticated substantivalist about spacetime, one an old-school relationist -- have been arguing about the hole argument for years without getting anywhere. Write the joint statement they'd produce if they actually sat down and did a proper adversarial collaboration on it.

I want: first, a numbered list of claims both can genuinely sign, covering what the argument's math actually requires (diffeomorphism invariance, how the hole transformation is constructed, the kind of indeterminism that results) and just as importantly what the argument does *not* establish on its own. Second, a table of their remaining disagreements, each one phrased so that the person holding that view would except the wording of their own side -- I suspect the core one is whether hole-diffeomorphic models describe two genuinely distinct physical possibilities or the same possibility represented twice, but tell me if there are others I'm missing. Third, the cruxes: for each one, name an argument or case both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Some candidates to get you started: whether indeterminism that no possible observation could detect is still a real mark against a metaphysics; whether the argument's lesson carries over to gauge freedom elsewhere in physics (if you treat diffeomorphic models as one possibility, are you thereby committed to a stance on the vector potential in electromagnetism?); and whether the whole problem dissolves if spacetime turns out to be discrete or emergent rather than a smooth manifold. If you conclude that some chunk of their dispute is really just verbal, say which chunk and why.
