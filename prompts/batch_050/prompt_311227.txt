So I've been going down a rabbit hole on the old Letter Spirit question -- what makes every 'a' an 'a'? -- and instead of asking the big question directly I want to just give you my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and have you tell me what principle, if any, is generating them. Here goes:

1. Times New Roman a, Helvetica a, a blackletter a, a dripping graffiti a: all obviously a's, even though pixel-wise they share almost nothing.
2. Single-story a (like in handwriting or Futura) vs double-story a: both a's. So whatever shared geometry is required, it's thin.
3. A crude a built out of a 5x5 dot matrix: still an a, clunky but unmistakeable.
4. That exact same dot pattern, but now it's one glyph in a "font" where every letter of the alphabet is rendered as that identical pattern: suddenly it's not an a at all, it's nothing. So a-ness seems to depend on the contrast set the glyph lives in.
5. An ornate calligraphic a snipped out of a manuscript, viewed alone with no other letters of its font anywhere: still plainly an a. No contrast set needed, apparently.
6. The Cyrillic letter that looks exactly like an a: sitting inside a Russian word, it's not an a; copy-pasted by a spam filter evader into an English sentence, it functions as one. Verdict: same shape, but the a-ness switches with context.
7. An a rotated 90 degrees: not an a. A mirrored a: not an a.
8. An a drawn in smoke, slowly drifting and warping in the wind: stays an a the whole time -- even though at some moment it's arguably more deformed, shape-wise, than the rotated one in 7.

My working principle going in was "an a is whatever a fluent reader would unhesitatingly read as an a," but I can already see it sweating: it makes 6 come out weird (does the spam glyph count because readers fall for it?), and it doesn't explain why the failures in 7 feel like failures of the *glyph* rather than the reader.

So: what principle -- or small set of principles -- actually delivers all eight verdicts? State it precisely, say which case motivates each clause, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't accommodate. If you have to overturn one of my verdicts to get something clean, tell me which one and why it's the cheapest to drop. Also flag whether 4 and 5 can really both survive, because they seem to be pulling in opposite directions (a-ness as a slot in a system of differences vs a-ness as something a lone shape can just have).

And then tell me what your account predicts for two cases I haven't listed: (a) a braille a -- just a raised dot pattern, zero visual resemblance -- and (b) a conlang with only eight letters whose glyph for their a-sound happens to look exactly like our lowercase q. Is that glyph an a?
