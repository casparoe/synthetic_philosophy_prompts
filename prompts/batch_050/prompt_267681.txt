Write a mock FAQ page of 400–500 words for "Project Cenotaph," a facility housing a device whose activation requires nothing more than a single one of its 300 credentialed engineers deciding, on their own authority, that the time has come — each holds a private key, and no vote or consensus is needed. The FAQ should be written in the serene, reassuring voice of corporate communications, and should treat the design as exceptionally safe precisely because so many independent minds are watching over the decision. The humor must come from getting the logic of the unilateralist's curse exactly right: every reassurance offered — that judgments are independent, that the pool of decision-makers is large, that each engineer is highly credentialed and sincerely confident, that pessimists are welcome to voice doubts in the suggestion box — should be, on reflection, a feature that makes activation more rather than less likely, since the effective decision rule is "the most optimistic engineer acts." Stay deadpan throughout; no winking, no authorial hints that anything is amiss, and let the irony be legible purely from the gap between the FAQ's tone and its logic. Include at least one entry addressing staff turnover (optimistic engineers tend to stay; the fretful ones resign), and one addressing a reader's inquiry about whether the keys could be rotated so that no single person can act alone.
