For years I have defended a position I call indifference nihilism, and I would now like its exposure mapped rather than its virtues rehearsed. The position runs as follows. In continuous sample spaces, the principle of indifference is not merely difficult to apply but has no determinate application at all. Every uniform distribution presupposes a parametrization of the possibility space — the length of a side rather than its square, the ratio of wine to water rather than its inverse, the midpoint of a chord rather than its angle — and Bertrand-style reparametrizations show that descriptions of one and the same physical situation, equally natural by any non-question-begging standard, yield incompatible probability assignments. Since nothing internal to the problems selects among parametrizations, there is no objective prior; appeals to symmetry merely relocate the arbitrariness to the choice of a transformation group, which is itself conventional. On this basis I hold that objective Bayesianism is dead in the continuous case, and that indifference survives at most as a subjective default or a coordination device.

Tell me what would count against this. Not the textbook refutations — I know those and find them question-begging — but a serious accounting of what the view risks. First: what would have to be true for some parametrization to be genuinely privileged? Would a successful invariance solution to the classic chord problem, of the sort pursued via transformation groups and consistency desiderata, actually touch my claim, or am I right to suspect that the invariance requirements were reverse-engineered from an antecedently intuited answer? Second: are there formal results — convergence or washing-out theorems, constraints from the geometry of the sample space, connections to maximum-entropy methods — that would restrict the class of admissible priors enough to undercut nihilism, and how much restriction would be enough to matter? Third: which of these would a fellow nihilist concede as counting against the view, as opposed to those only its opponents take seriously? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Then close with an honest verdict on whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability — that is, whether I have any criterion at all for what a successful objective indifference principle would look like — and, if I do not, whether that is a defect in the thesis or simply the correct posture toward a degenerate research program.
