I'm a philosophy prof putting together readings for my free will seminar. Every year I teach Dennett's sphex wasp -- the digger wasp who paralyzes a cricket, leaves it at the mouth of her burrow while she pops inside to check things out, and if an experimenter nudges the cricket a few inches away, drags it right back to the threshhold and goes in to check the burrow again, and again, as many times as you keep moving it. Dennett holds her up as the mascot of sphexishness: rigid behavioral loops that look purposive until someone wiggles the environment.

For next semester I want to hand out a satirical op-ed, around 550 words, written in the voice of the wasp herself, responding to this decades-long slander of her species. She defends her own freedom using exactly the moves my undergrads use to defend theirs: she could have done otherwise, her behavior is sensitive to reasons (the cricket being there IS a reason), dragging the cricket is something she does for the sake of her offspring and not something that merely happens in her, and the real question was never whether her actions have causes but whether the causes run through her. The comedy is that each move sounds precisely as convincing coming from a creature with a hundred thousand neurons, so the reader has to ask whether our own defenses are any stronger.

Ground rules: she stays internally consistent -- she never concedes the mechanism point, never accidentally admits she's sphexish, and whatever irony there is lives in the reader's ear, not in her voice. Deadpan the whole way, no winking. She can invent a biography, grievances, some wasp pride. She has clearly read the relevant passages about herself but she's not scholarly, so no name-dropping beyond Dennett.

Start your response with the op-ed itself, headline included, no preface or commentary about what you're about to do.
