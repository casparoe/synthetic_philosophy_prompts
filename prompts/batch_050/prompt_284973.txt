Ok so a friend and I run a little forecasting club and we keep having the same argument, so let me just dump my positions on disagreement and you tell me if they actually cohere. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I accept Aumann's agreement theorem: rational agents with common priors who share their posteriors and know each other to be rational can't keep disagreeing — they have to end up with the same credence.
(2) But I think the common prior assumption is basically never satisfied in real life: people grow up with different evidence streams and temperaments, so genuinely different priors are the norm.
(3) I think "agreeing to disagree" is sometimes perfectly rational — e.g. my friend and I have known each other's reasoning on whether our club's forecasts beat Metaculus for ages, neither of us has moved, and I think that's fine and neither of us is being irrational.
(4) I think learning that an epistemic peer disagrees with you is itself evidence, and you should update on it roughly as you would on comparable first-order evidence.
(5) I also think my own private evidence can sometimes justify staying put even after hearing a peer's view — I'm not obliged to split the difference.

Which of these genuinely clash, which only look like they do, and if something has to give, which commitment is cheapest to drop? Please rank any real tensions by severity and be specific about what each revision would cost me. Also, answer in British English please.
