So I'm in a metaphysics seminar and we just finished two weeks on causation by omission -- absences, failures, non-events, all that. For the final paper proposal we have to submit a one-page statement of where we stand, and the prof said the worst outcome is handing in a set of views that can't all be true at once, so I want to stress-test mine before I hand it in. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) Omissions really can be causes. My neighbour's failure to water my plants while I was away genuinely caused them to die -- that's not just a loose way of talking, it's a real causal fact.

(b) But not every failure counts. The Queen of England also failed to water my plants, and her failure didn't cause anything. Which omissions count as causes seems to track what was expected or required of the person -- the neighbour promised, the Queen didn't.

(c) Causation is a purely objective relation out in the world. If two situations are physically identical, they can't differ in what causes what. Norms and expectations might shape how we talk, but they can't change the causal facts themselves.

(d) Absences aren't things. Nothing happens when someone fails to act -- there's no event, no energy, no process -- so nothing can literally flow from an omission the way it flows from, say, poison entering the soil.

(e) My working test for whether an omission caused something: ask whether the outcome would have been avoided if the person had acted. Plant would've lived if the neighbour had watered? Then the neighbour's failure was a cause.

What I can't tell is whether this hangs together or whether I'm papering over cracks. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it at first glance? Please rank the actual tensions by how serious they are rather than listing everything as equally doomed, and for each one tell me what I'd have to give up to fix it. If one single revision would clean up the most damage, flag it -- but I should warn you I'm pretty attached to (a), so if that has to go you need to argue for it properly. Also curious wheter the test in (e) actually does the job I want it to do, because I mostly added it to make the page look rigorous and haven't thought hard about it.
