I cover policy and ideas for a magazine, and I'm working on a long piece about sex work law. For months I've been interviewing two people who can barely be in the same room: a abolitionist who campaigns for the Nordic model (criminalize the buyers, decriminalize the sellers, fund exit services, like Sweden's done since 1999) and a sex-workers-rights organizer who wants full decriminalization New Zealand style (2003, brothel operator certificates, labor law applies, all that). In a fit of optimism I told them I'd draft the "joint statement" they'd write if they had to do an adversarial collaboration, and to my surprise both said they'd read it.

So: write me that document. Specifically:

1. An agreed-claims section. I suspect there's more common ground than either admits -- both oppose criminalizing sellers, both think street-level conditions are often grim, both concede the trafficking stats under every regime are a methodological swamp. Find the rest, phrased so both would actually sign.

2. A disagreements table. The precise points where they part, each side's position worded so that side would accept the wording. No strawmen -- that's the whole point of the exercise, and both of them will send me furious emails if I get thier view wrong.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move at least one of them, not just the disagreements stated louder. My guesses, which you can improve on: whether buying sex is inherently an act of subordination or only contingently so given conditions; whether criminalizing demand actually shrinks the market without pushing it underground and making sellers less safe; whether decriminalization changes who enters the industry and under what pressure; whether "sex work is work like any other job" is a conceptual claim or a rhetorical one.

4. For each crux, a concrete case, country comparison, or piece of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance -- e.g. what the abolitionist vs. the decrim advocate would each expect the New Zealand post-2003 data to show, or what each would predict for France and Ireland, which went Nordic more recently. The point is that neither gets to retro-fit their story after the numbers come in.

And if part of their fight is verbal -- I have a strong hunch "exploitation" is doing two different jobs in their mouths, and maybe "consent" too -- say which part and why, rather than papering over it.
