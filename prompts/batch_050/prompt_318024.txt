My friend Priya has very confident intuitions about harm tradeoffs, and over a few drinks last week she started firing off verdicts. Her track record so far, as best I remember it:

- One person tortured for an hour vs. a trillion people each getting a single dust speck in the eye: take the specks, not even close.
- One person getting a paper cut vs. fifty people each getting an identical paper cut: take the one paper cut.
- One person getting a severe 30-minute migraine vs. ten people each getting a mild headache that lasts 3 hours: take the single migraine.
- One person getting a dust speck in their eye every day for a year vs. that same person having one truly awful hangover day: she'd rather have the year of daily specks.
- One person dying vs. a thousand people each breaking a leg: take the thousand broken legs.

Ok so next dispute, which came up when we were talking about mosquito control budgets of all things: suppose you could either let a googol people each get a mildly itchy ear for ten minutes (annoying, you'd definately notice, but you'd forget about it by lunch) or let one person break a wrist and have it ache moderately for six weeks while it heals. Which does Priya pick?

I'm less interested in the "right" answer than in reverse-engineering her. It seems like her verdicts are consistent with more than one rule — like, maybe tiny harms just count for literally zero no matter how many there are, or maybe harms come in tiers where no amount of a lower tier ever adds up to a higher one, or maybe something else fits her five verdicts just as well. Can you lay out the two or three most plausible rules she's implicitly running, say what each one predicts for the itchy-ear case, give me your best guess at her actual verdict (with reasons, not just a coin flip), and tell me what *further* hypothetical case would best distinguish between the rules? And if you think her five verdicts are actually inconsistent with each other, I'd want to hear that too.
