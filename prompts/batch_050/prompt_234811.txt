Write a short story about a literary translator, set in the near future, who is hired to translate a beloved 16th-centry poem — a tiny get-well-soon poem addressed to a sick girl, famous in the original language for being simultaneously rhymed, syllable-counted, and singable to a familiar tune — into a language where, as she painfully discovers, you can preserve at most two of those three formal features at once. Her client insists on all three, and her rival, an AI translation system that satisfies any constraints you give it perfectly but keeps producing hollow results, is waiting in the wings. Use the story to explore, concretely and through the characters' choices and arguments, what it even means to "preserve the form" of a poem once form turns out to decompose into separable features that can be traded against each other — and let the translator's final solution be something other than a straightforward compromise (maybe she preserves a formal feature nobody on the client side had even thought to count). Bonus points if the story itself is written under some light formal constraint of your choosing, like every paragraph having exactly five sentences, without the story ever drawing attention to it.
