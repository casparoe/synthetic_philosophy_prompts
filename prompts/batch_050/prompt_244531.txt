I'm in my final year of high school and preparing a presentation for our philosophy elective on whether the formal "coherence" arguments really justify the standard rules of rational choice. Before I commit to a position, I'd like to know whether the views I've arrived at actually hang together. Here is where I've landed:

(1) The Dutch book argument succeeds: if your degrees of belief violate the axioms of probability, a bookie can offer you a set of bets, each of which you accept, that together guarantee you a loss — and this shows your credences were irrational.

(2) The structurally similar money pump argument fails: someone with cyclical preferences (prefers A to B, B to C, but C to A) is not thereby irrational, because a person who sees the pump coming can simply decline to keep trading. Rationality governs what you prefer, not whether you turn out to be a poor strategist.

(3) Rational preferences over lotteries must conform to expected utility theory, as captured by the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms; in particular, rational preferences satisfy the independence axiom.

(4) The Allais preferences are perfectly rational: it is reasonable to prefer a guaranteed million over a gamble that almost always pays a million but carries a small chance of nothing, while also preferring the higher-payoff gamble over the lower-payoff one when both are long shots.

(5) There is no conflict between being risk averse about money and being an expected utility maximizer, since the utility of money need not rise linearly with the amount.

(6) What rationality requires of an agent should not depend on how farsighted or strategically sophisticated that agent happens to be.

Please audit this set of commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, tell me how severe it is, what the cheapest fix would be, and what that fix costs. I'd also like to know whether the way I defend cyclical preferences in (2) remains available to me given my other commitments, and, if one single revision would clean up the most problems, which revision that is. Please don't treat every pair as equally problematic — I want the audit ordered by how much damage each conflict does to the overall position.
