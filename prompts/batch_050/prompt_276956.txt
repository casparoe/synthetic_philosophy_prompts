I cover science and technology for a magazine, and my newsroom has just introduced what editors call a “grounding audit” for AI-assisted reporting. Before publication, we have to mark every important noun phrase in a draft as one of three things:

1. directly anchored — connected to something a person can perceive, manipulate, or measure, like a robot lifting a cup;
2. operationally anchored — defined through a settled proxy or procedure, like unemployment, IQ, engagement, or inflation; or
3. document-anchored — mostly defined by relations to other texts, models, or expert claims, like national mood, AI belief, market confidence, or emergent intelligence.

Anything in categories 2 or 3 needs an explicit “bridge” to observations, measurements, decisions, or interventions. Otherwise we either rewrite it or label it as an interpretation rather than a fact.

The policy is producing some odd verdicts. A claim that a household robot “understands the instruction to pick up the red mug” fails the audit if all we have is benchmark results, but passes if reporters watched it respond to varied spoken instructions, lighting conditions, mug positions, and deliberate distractions. Meanwhile, a claim about a black hole’s shadow can pass on the strength of telescope data and reconstruction software even though nobody has encountered a black hole directly. “Unemployment rose” passes because the official survey procedure counts as its bridge. But “the model believes the mug is reachable” apparently cannot pass, no matter how good its predictions are, unless the claim is rephrased as “the model reliably behaves as though the mug is reachable.” Definately feels inconsistent to me.

What philosophical view of meaning or understanding does this audit presuppose? Is it committed to the idea that symbols have to be grounded in perception and action, or could the official bridges be defended on an inferential-role or social-practice theory instead? Does it mistakenly treat grounding journalists’ words as the same problem as grounding an AI system’s internal representations? Which parts of the policy assume a causal theory of reference, which assume operationalism or verificationism, and which are compatible with several rival views? Finally, if we adopted a more holistic theory of meaning, what concretely would have to change in the audit—and would anything worth keeping be lost?
