im in a running fight with my flatmate about whether truth outruns what anyone could in principle find out. My position was modest, or so I thought: for anything that's true, it's at least possible that someone comes to know it. Maybe not us, maybe not now, but no truth is beyond being known full stop. He says that collapses into everything true already being known, and he wrote this out on our kitchen whiteboard:

(1) For every truth p, it's possible that someone knows p.
(2) Suppose not everything true is known. Then there's some truth p such that nobody knows p.
(3) Then the conjunction "p, and nobody knows p" is itself true.
(4) By (1) applied to that conjunction: it's possible that someone knows that (p and nobody knows p).
(5) Anyone who knows a conjunction knows each of its conjuncts.
(6) So possibly: someone knows p, and someone knows that nobody knows p.
(7) Knowledge is factive: if someone knows that nobody knows p, then nobody knows p.
(8) That contradicts the first half of (6). So the supposition in (2) is impossible, and every truth is known.

This has to go wrong somewhere, because it's just false that every truth is known -- nobody knows whether the number of blades of grass in the garden is odd or even, and no amount of possibility-talk changes that. My money is on (5): can't you know a conjunction on someone's say-so without yourself knowing each part? He says (5) is the one premise he refuses to give up because without it 'proofs stop working', whatever that means.

So: which step do I actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Two followups. First, my original position wasn't really that every truth might be known as things stand -- I meant more like, for every truth, someone could eventually come to know it, given enough time and investigation. Does that version survive the argument, or does the same trick still go through on it once you pick the right truth to feed into step (4)? Second, he now claims the argument also shows there can't even be truths that are never *going* to be known, which seems even crazier. Is that a seperate claim needing its own proof, or does it fall out of the same premises?

If the price is giving up my modest little principle in (1), i'll pay it before i'll give up factivity or (5). But I want to see the bill first.
