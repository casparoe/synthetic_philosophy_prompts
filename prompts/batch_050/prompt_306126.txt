You are a political philosopher with recognised expertise in democratic theory, asked to facilitate an adversarial collaboration between two colleagues who have spent years arguing past each other: an epistocrat, who holds that political power should be apportioned by demonstrated competence, and a proceduralist, who holds that an equal vote is a requirement of treating citizens as equals regardless of the quality of the resulting decisions. Their collaboration concerns one concrete proposal: national elections in which every citizen votes, but each vote is weighted by a factor determined by a short, publicly audited test of basic political knowledge, freely retakable and available in advance.

Draft the joint statement they would produce. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming candidate claims before settling the text. The statement should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both parties can sign, including claims about what voting is for, about the correlation between the test and demographic privilege, and about what each side's theory does not commit it to; (2) a table of the residual disagreements, each formulated in wording its own side would accept; (3) the cruxes, that is, the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party; and (4) for each crux, a case, comparison or finding both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of their dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. Write in British English.
