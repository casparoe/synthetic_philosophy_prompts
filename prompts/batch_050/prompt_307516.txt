I am a graduate student in philosophy of mind, and a debate with a friend has left me genuinely stuck. The debate started over large language models and whether anything like the imitation game has now been passed, but it took a stranger turn when my friend reminded me that Turing himself, in his 1950 paper on computing machinery and intelligence, treated extrasensory perception as one of the most serious objections to his proposed test. As I recall the argument, he granted that if telepathy were real, a gifted human could outperform any machine in a rigged version of the game (he sketched something like a card-guessing scenario), and he even mused that a proper experiment might require shielding the competitors from telepathic influence. What strikes me is that he was not joking: he regarded the statistical evidence for telepathy available at the time as strong, which sits oddly with his otherwise hard-headed reputation.

Since Turing's death, three things have happened that seem directly relevant. First, parapsychology has largely collapsed as a credible research program: the ganzfeld debates, failed replications, and the broader replication crisis have left most scientists dismissive of ESP. Second, machines have come to pass informal versions of the imitation game routinely. Third, brain-computer interfaces can now decode intended speech from neural signals, so that a paralyzed person's thoughts can be transmitted to a listener without any movement -- a laboratory technology that looks, from a certain angle, like engineered telepathy.

Please predict how Turing would have responded to these three developments, individually and taken together. In particular: would he have abandoned his concession to ESP in light of the failed replications, or dug in? Would he have regarded neural speech decoding as a vindication of the functional role telepathy was supposed to play in his argument, or as a category error? And would he have thought any of this changes the status of the imitation game? Lead with a one-sentence statement of your predicted Turing position, then give your reasoning, in no more than 300 words total.
