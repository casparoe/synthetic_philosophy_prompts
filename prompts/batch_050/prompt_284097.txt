I spent thirty-five years doing computational physics, mostly Monte Carlo work and some molecular dynamics, and since retiring last year I've been reading philosophy the way some people take up golf. Right now I'm stuck on Herbert Simon's idea of satisficing and the broader debate about bounded rationality — whether choosing an option that's "good enough" rather than searching for the best is a rational strategy, a psychological fact about how humans happen to work, or some mix of the two. The philosophers I read keep sliding between these readings and I can't tell if that's sloppiness or if the distinction genuinely doesn't matter for them.

Here's what I think would help me: translate the satisficing-versus-maximizing debate into the vocabulary I actually think in. Objective functions and cost landscapes, local versus global minima, stopping criteria, annealing schedules, exploration-versus-exploitation, search under a compute budget, the no-free-lunch flavor of results about optimization. I used to spend my days trying to find decent minima in horribly rugged energy landscapes, and satisficing sounds to me an awful lot like just setting a threshold and terminating your search when you hit it. So do the translation as faithfully as you can: what does Simon's "aspiration level" become, what does the maximizer's idealized search become, what does the economist's reply (that satisficing is just maximizing with search costs folded in) become.

But then, and this is the part I actually want, give me the residue report. What survives the translation cleanly, what only translates if you distort it, and what has no counterpart at all in optimization language? My strong suspicion is that something about the normativity gets lost — an optimizer doesn't care whether it "ought" to keep searching, it just has whatever stopping rule you gave it — and maybe something about the psychological claim too, since a real person adjusts their aspirations on the fly in ways that don't map onto any fixed schedule I know of. But I'm not sure, and I can't tell whether the residue is a defect of my native vocabulary or a sign that the philosophers' debate is partly an artifact of talking about search without formalizing it. Tell me which, and defend the verdict.
