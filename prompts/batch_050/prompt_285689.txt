I run a research group in machine learning, and I've been reading Lakatos on the methodology of scientific research programmes. Restate the framework in the vocabulary my field actually uses — architectures, benchmarks, baselines, ablations, training heuristics, negative results, scaling behavior — covering at minimum: the hard core and the protective belt, the negative and positive heuristics, the distinction between progressive and degenerating problemshifts, and the role of novel facts. Then give me a residue report: which components translate cleanly, which only with distortion, and which have no expression in the day-to-day practice of ML research at all. Two parts of the residue matter most to me. First, does the notion of novel prediction survive translation into a field where held-out test sets, benchmark saturation, and training-data contamination complicate what counts as a prediction at all? Second, does ML have any counterpart to the judgment that a programme is degenerating — could we distinguish, from the inside, a paradigm that is degenerating in Lakatos's sense from one that merely needs more compute, better data, or a fairer benchmark? Finally, take a view on what the residue means: is it something my field is missing, or something Lakatos could afford to say only because science, unlike ML research, aims at truth rather than capability?
