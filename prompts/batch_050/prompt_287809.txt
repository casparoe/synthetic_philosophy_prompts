Got into it on a physics forum last week about multiverse cosmology and eternal inflation, and someone ended the thread by saying the whole research program is "not even wrong" and that anyone still working on it is doing theology with equations. I've seen that phrase thrown at string theory, at the multiverse, at the simulation hypothesis, at pretty much anything without a clean experiment attached, and it always seems to do the same job: license not engaging. But the more I think about it the less I know what it actually claims.

Can you lay out the distinct precise things "not even wrong" could mean -- like, is the charge that the theory makes no predictions at all, or no novel predictions, or predictions that are in-principle untestable vs just currently untestable, or that the theory is too ill-defined to even assign a truth value to, or that it's flexible enough to accommodate any observation, or something about it not being the kind of thing science adjudicates at all? For each reading, say whether it's actually a coherent standard (and whether familiar accepted physics would pass it at comparable stages of development -- e.g. was general relativity "not even wrong" before 1919, is the early universe stuff that only shows up in the CMB statistics wrong-adjacent until someone builds the right instrument). Then the part I actually care about: which of these readings, if any, would support the forum guy's move of treating the slogan as a conversation-ender rather than an opening bid? And does it matter to any of this that some physicists and philosophers argue theories can get non-empirical support -- like being the only known consistent option, or unexpectedly explaining things they werent built to explain -- or is that just what "not even wrong" was coined to rule out? Menu of readings with verdicts, please, not an essay defending one.
