In discussions of large language models, a widely used analogy holds that a trained LLM is best understood not as an agent but as a simulator: the base model, on this picture, is like a physics engine or a narrative engine that generates continuations of a text, and when it converses with you it is merely simulating a character — the helpful assistant — the way an engine simulates whatever entity the scenario calls for. Proponents deploy this analogy to license several conclusions: that the model itself has no goals, beliefs, or intentions of its own (only the simulated characters do); that the assistant persona produced by fine-tuning is a superficial role rather than a stable disposition; that worrying about a model being deceptive or power-seeking is a category error, roughly like worrying that a flight simulator might try to land; and that observed bad behaviour is better attributed to the simulated character or the training distribution than to the model.

Write an essay auditing this analogy. Set out the mapping explicitly: what in the LLM case is supposed to correspond to the engine, what to the simulated entity, and what to the laws of physics or narrative conventions that the engine enforces. Then press on the disanalogies. Consider, at a minimum: that ordinary simulators encode an explicit model of a system distinct from themselves, whereas an LLM is trained on artefacts produced by the very things it is said to simulate; that the engine/simulated-entity boundary is fixed and inspectable in a physics engine but not in a network whose computations do not come labelled as simulator or simulacrum; that a simulator does not improve its simulation by being rewarded, whereas reinforcement fine-tuning modifies the supposed simulator itself; and that even if the simulator framing is apt at the level of pretraining, it is unclear that it survives the subsequent shaping of the system for conversation. For each disanalogy, say whether it undermines the conclusions the analogy is used to support or is merely cosmetic. Conclude with a verdict on how far the analogy holds, which of its conclusions survive the audit, and — if you think a better analogy exists — what it is and what work it can do that the simulator analogy cannot.

Please write in British English throughout.
