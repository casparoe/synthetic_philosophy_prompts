My mom died a few months ago and somewhere in the grief-scrolling I fell down a hole about Kurt Gödel's rotating universe solutions -- spacetimes where, if you set off in the right direction and keep going far enough, you loop back into your own past. Gödel apparently took this as evidence that time's lapse isn't objectively real, that the flowing river of time is just how things look to beings stuck inside it, since a past you can in principle revisit can't have "passed away" the way we assume. I can't stop thinking about that, and I want it in fiction form rather than another explainer.

Write me a short story set in a universe where Gödel's loops are not just theoretically possible but a known, mapped feature of the world -- say, a shipping lane. A couple of ground rules: don't make it a fix-it story where the protagonist changes the past (the loops are globally self-consistent, nothing can be changed, that's part of the point), and don't let a character just lecture the philosophy either. The story should make me *feel* the weird implication: if every moment of my mom's life still exists out there along the loop, is grief for the dead a kind of category error -- and why does it definately not feel like one anyway? Bonus if the story finds something Gödel's argument misses or flattens, without cheating on the physics.
