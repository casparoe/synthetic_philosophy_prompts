Last week I had a long, inconclusive argument with a friend about the chatbot I use at work, and I am hoping someone can tell me whether the thing we were arguing about has an established name, because we could not find a category that fits it.

Two recent episodes, to make the phenomenon concrete. First, I asked the chatbot to summarize a court decision, and it produced a fluent, confident summary, complete with a plausible docket number, a date, and a holding. The case does not exist. When I pointed this out, it apologized and supplied a different docket number, also fabricated. Second, my friend asked it to settle a dispute about a historical date; it gave the wrong date, supported by two paragraphs of invented detail, all phrased with total assurance.

My friend says the program is lying. I objected that lying requires believing the truth and intending to deceive, and this system has no beliefs or intentions whatsoever. He then retreated to Frankfurt's notion of bullshit — speech produced with indifference to whether it is true — which I looked up after our argument. But that does not seem to fit either: Frankfurt's bullshitter has an attitude, namely indifference, and is misrepresenting what he is up to, whereas this system has no attitudes to be indifferent with. Calling it ordinary error seems equally wrong, because a malfunctioning instrument fails at random, while this produces fluent, on-topic, authoritative prose that mimics knowledge. The closest human analogue I can think of is the amnesiac patient who confidently invents memories, except such patients apparently believe what they say, and this system believes nothing. My friend's final move was to say that everyone just calls these "hallucinations," but that term seems to presuppose a perception gone wrong, and nothing here is perceived.

Is there an established name and literature for this phenomenon? I would not be satisfied with a bare label: I want to know which of the nearby concepts — lying, bullshit, error, confabulation — comes closest, exactly where each one fails to fit, and, if none fits, a coined term with a precise definition and some boundary cases. In particular: does the category change when a human being reads the output, half-suspects it might be fabricated, and passes it along as fact anyway, reasoning that if it is wrong the fault lies with the program — as I nearly did with that docket number?
