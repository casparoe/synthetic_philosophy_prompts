Call the causal spine of a computation the chain of events by which each of its intermediate states produces its successor: in an ordinary digital computer, the spine is carried by clocked transitions in silicon; in a brain, by electrochemical dynamics. A taped implementation, by contrast, replaces the spine with playback: a complete table of the computation's states, recorded in advance and read off in sequence by a mechanism whose operation is insensitive to the content of the table — no state on the tape is produced by its predecessor in anything like the way the original computation's states were. Suppose, for the sake of argument, that some computations matter morally when they are actually run — for instance, computations of the kind that in a human brain constitute episodes of suffering. Now consider the spine thesis: a physical system instantiates a morally relevant computation only if the system's own causal processes constitute that computation's causal spine, so that mere playback of the right state sequence, however faithful, instantiates nothing of moral significance. Please argue for the spine thesis. Your argument should do at least the following. First, show that the thesis is not merely the claim that tape-players are too simple, too slow, or too small to run complex computations — the argument must go through even for a playback device of arbitrary speed, fidelity, and storage capacity. Second, answer the determinism objection: from a God's-eye view, the laws of physics fix a brain's every future state just as surely as the table fixes the tape's, so unless the spine/playback distinction is drawn with real precision it collapses into a distinction without a difference. Third, state what the thesis implies for gradual-replacement scenarios, in which a brain's neurons are substituted one by one with functionally equivalent silicon components, and argue that this implication is a vindication of the thesis rather than a reductio of it. Fourth, identify as exactly as you can which feature of the spine carries the moral weight — causal integration among states, counterfactual robustness of the implementation, the self-producing character of the process, or something else — and defend that choice against the strongest rival candidate.
