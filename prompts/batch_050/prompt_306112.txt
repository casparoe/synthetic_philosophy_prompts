Lately I've gotten interested in the historians' fight over contingency -- the for-want-of-a-nail intuition that tiny events (a missed train, a butterfly, Cleopatra's nose) can swing enormous outcomes, versus the structuralist view that deep economic and social forces make the big outcomes robust and the small stuff is just noise on the path.

Restate both positions in my vocabulary as carefully as you can. And what about the worry that counterfactuals too far from the actual world (no Nazi Germany, no Mongol empire) are so underdetermined they're meaningless?
