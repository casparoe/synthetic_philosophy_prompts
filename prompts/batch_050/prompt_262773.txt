Every month, my friends and I play a strategy board game for a small cash prize, and for the past year I've kept a notebook where, before each match, I write down my prediction of which opening move I'll end up making. I've gotten eerily good at this — my predictions are right about four times out of five. Lately my teammate has been arguing that I should stop deliberating during my turns altogether and just play whatever the notebook says, on the grounds that my written prediction is already the best available estimate of what my deliberation will conclude, so re-deliberating can only add noise. This strikes me as deeply wrong, but I'm struggling to say why. When I sit down to actually choose a move, reading my old prediction doesn't feel like being handed the answer — it feels like being handed one more piece of information to chew on, and sometimes I even catch myself wanting to pick a different move precisely because it was predicted (which, maddeningly, means my next prediction ought to factor that in too). Is my teammate's advice as rational as it sounds, or is there something incoherent about treating a prediction of your own choice as a substitute for making the choice? And if I keep the notebook, how should I even think about assigning probabilities to moves I haven't decided between yet?
