Something that's been bugging me since I finished a novel last month. The main character dies at the end and I genuinely cried, actual tears, sat there for a while. But here's the weird part: if you offered me a version of the book where she survives, I wouldn't want it. The sad ending is the one I'd choose, and if the author announced a sequel that brings her back I'd be annoyed, not relieved. So in one sense I was sad she died, but in another sense I wouldn't change it even if I could, which is not at all how sadness about real people works -- if a real friend died I'd obviously take the version where they live. Does this combination have a name? I know there's the old puzzle about why we feel anything for people who dont exist, but this seems like a different problem: not why the emotion happens, but why it shows up without the wanting-things-otherwise part that I'd have thought was part of what sadness even is. Is there an established concept for this specific thing, and how does it relate to the better-known puzzle? If it doesn't have a name, coin one, and tell me whether it causes trouble for the standard ways out of the fiction puzzle or fits neatly into one of them.
