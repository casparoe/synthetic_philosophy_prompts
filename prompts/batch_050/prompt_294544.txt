The chapter is from Eddington's The Nature of the Physical World, where he argues that you can tell which direction of time is the future just by checking whether things are getting more disordered as you go along — more shuffled, more run-down, entropy going up — and that this is basically the only distinction between past and future that physics knows about. Here's roughly how it went, I'm numbering the moves so you can go through them:

(1) Tomas said the passage is circular. The fundamental laws are time-symmetric, so "entropy increases" only picks out a direction if you already know which way the future is. Worse, if you take any low-entropy state and just apply the statistics, entropy goes up in *both* time directions away from it. So Eddington isn't explaining the arrow, he's just relabeling it.

(2) Anke said the both-directions thing is actually the best part, not a problem. If entropy rises away from a low-entropy state on both sides, then anyone living on either side experiences an arrow pointing away from that state, full stop. You get the whole asymmetry of experience out of one boundary condition plus symmetric laws, and that's an explanation, not circularity.

(3) Tomas said fine, but then all the real work is done by the low-entropy past, which the passage says nothing about, and that's the actual mystery — why did the universe start out so fantastically ordered? Calling the arrow a matter of increasing randomness just hides that the one genuinely puzzling fact got shoved into an assumption.

(4) Anke said every explanation has to stop somewhere and initial conditions aren't the kind of thing laws are supposed to explain anyway. Plus the entropy story earns its keep: it accounts for melting ice, mixing cream, the fact that we have records of the past and not the future. Tomas is conflating "nothing deeper explains it" with "it isn't an explanation."

(5) Tomas's last move was that the records point backfires on her. Having a record of the past is itself a low-entropy correlation, so memory only works given the very past condition she's trying to avoid justifying — meaning the arrow of records can't be what grounds time's direction either.
