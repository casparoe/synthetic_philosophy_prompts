hi, I'm in a medieval philosophy seminar this semester and we've spent the last couple weeks on Boethius and the foreknowledge problem. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. God is timeless, not just everlasting. He isn't located in time at all and possesses his whole life at once, the way Boethius defines eternity, instead of living through it moment by moment like we do.

2. God knows everything, including everything I will ever do -- but not by foreknowing it. All times are equally present to him in his eternal now, so his knowledge of my future is more like observation than prediction.

3. Humans sometimes have libertarian free will, me included: I could genuinely have done otherwise, and my choice isn't necessitated by anything that isn't up to me -- not by the past, not by the laws, and not by God's knowledge.

4. The Boethian solution works. Since God timelessly sees rather than foreknows, his knowledge no more forces my actions than my watching someone cross the street forces them to cross. The dependence runs from the event to the knowledge, not the other way round.

5. Petitionary prayer actually does something. God genuinely responds to prayers, and some events happen because someone prayed for them that wouldn't have happened otherwise.

6. Providence governs history in the strong sense the Consolation builds up to: everything, down to the apparently pointless suffering, is ordered toward the good.

So: which of these genuinely pull against each other, and wich pairs only look like they conflict?
