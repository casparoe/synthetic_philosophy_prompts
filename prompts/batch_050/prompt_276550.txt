I'm prepping for a reading group on confirmation theory and we keep circling back to Goodman's new riddle of induction, the grue emeralds thing. At this point I think we've collected all the standard responses, and I want to test whether there's anything left we haven't thought of. Here's our list, with the usual weaknesses baked in, so don't bother repeating any of it:

1. Goodman's own entrenchment answer -- "green" has a long track record of successful projection and "grue" doesn't, so only the first is projectible. (We know the worry that this just describes our inductive practice instead of justifying it.)
2. The "grue is gerrymandered" answer -- it mentions a specific time t, so it's disjunctive and not a purely qualitative predicate, whereas "green" is. We also know Goodman's symmetry rebuttal: take grue and bleen as primitive and suddenly "green" is the one defined with a time index, so this answer begs the question unless you can independantly privilege one vocabulary over the other.
3. The natural-kinds answer -- induction only works on natural kind properties, being green (or whatever microphysical property underlies it) is one, being grue isn't. (Yes, we know this mostly pushes the bump under the rug to "what makes a kind natural".)
4. The Bayesian answer -- the riddle just shows induction needs substantive priors; we assign low prior probability to hypotheses with a switch at t, and there's no deeper question left to answer.
5. The simplicity answer -- the all-green hypothesis is simpler than the grue one (no extra parameter for the switch time), and ordinary curve-fitting considerations justify preferring it.

Is there a sixth response that is not on this list and not a rewording, special case, or mash-up of two of them? If it helps, we're happy to count ideas from outside phil of science proper -- decision theory, machine learning, metaphysics of properties, wherever -- as long as they actually engage with the riddle rather than dissolving it into "induction is just what we happen to do." And afterwards be honest with me: is the new response actually any good, or does it collapse into one of the five once you press on it?
