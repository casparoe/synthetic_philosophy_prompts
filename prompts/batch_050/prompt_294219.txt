So I've been roped into leading my reading group on Dennett's "Quining Qualia" next week and before I embarrass myself I want to check whether my own position actually hangs together. Here's roughly what I believe:

(1) Dennett is basically right about Chase and Sanborn. When Chase the coffee taster stops enjoying coffee, there's no fact of the matter that settles whether his taste-qualia changed or whether the qualia stayed the same and his standards/memory/judgments changed. Any test you run just pushes the question one step back.

(2) Even so, when it comes to what my experience is like for me right now, I'm the ultimate authority. You can be wrong about my pain; I can't be, at least not in the way you can.

(3) I'm a functionalist about the mental in general — beliefs, desires, and for that matter pain understood as a psychological state are all functional-role states. Nothing else is on the table for me.

(4) The inverted spectrum is a coherent scenario: two people could be perfect behavioral and functional duplicates while what it's like for them to look at grass differs. It might be undetectable, but there's a fact there.

(5) Introspection is highly fallible. Change blindness, confabulation, inattentional stuff — people are wrong about their own experience constantly, and I'd never rest an argument on "but I just know my own mind."

Which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict but dissolve once you're careful, and what's the cheapest single revision that makes the rest consistent? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally fatal, and for each one say what exactly would have to give. I honestly can't tell whether my problem is (2) vs (5), or (1) vs (4), or something I haven't noticed, so don't assume I've spotted it.
