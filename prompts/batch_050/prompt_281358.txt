I'm a PhD student in decision theory (my official home is the philosophy department but I basically live in the LSE-style formal epistemology crowd) and I'm writing the lit-review chapter of my dissertation on coherence arguments for expected utility maximization -- Dutch books, money pumps, representation theorems, the whole family. What I've realized reading this stuff is that people argue past each other constantly because there's no shared statement of what an argument in this family even has to accomplish to count as successful. One person treats the money pump as decisive, another says it's diachronic and presupposes that the agent will choose naively at each step, a third says Allais shows the independence axiom just isn't a rationality requirement, and nobody states upfront what standard they're applying.

So before I survey the literature I want the standard itself. Write the desiderata list for a coherence argument for EU maximization: what any argument in this family (Dutch book, money pump, Savage/vNM-style representation result, whatever) has to deliver to count as showing that rationality requires maximizing expected utility. I'm thinking of things like: the premises have to be independently compelling as requirements of rationality rather than smuggling in EU-shaped structure (the petitio worry that gets raised against both Dutch books and pumps); the argument has to take a stand on whether it's synchronic or diachronic, and if diachronic, deal with the fact that a resolute agent doesn't get pumped; it has to handle the completeness axiom honestly rather than just assuming incomparable options are irrational; it has to not conflate maximizing expected utility with maximizing expected money or with being risk-neutral; and it should say something about one-off choices, since the betting setup can feel rigged toward repeated play. But don't just adopt my list -- push back on any of these if you think it's a prejudice of one camp rather than a genuine requirement, and add what I've missed. For each desideratum give the case or consideration that motivates it, not just the label.

Two more things. First, I want to know whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable -- my suspicion is that "premises must be obviously mandatory on pain of irrationality" and "conclusion must be full EU, Archimedean continuity included" can't both be met, given lexicographic preferences and risk-weighted alternatives, but I want the tension argued properly, not just asserted. If the list itself turns out to be a result in that sense, say so clearly because that changes my chapter structure. Second, and this matters: take definite positions. I don't want a survey of what different camps would say about each requirement. Tell me which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable, which single one knocks out the most existing arguments (I have my own guess -- the diachronic-choice problem for money pumps -- but commit to yours even if it's different), and give your bottom-line verdict on whether any coherence argument in the literature, suitably charitably reconstructed, actually meets the standard. If the answer is that the whole project fails but fails interestingly, that's fine -- just say it and defend it rather than hedging.
