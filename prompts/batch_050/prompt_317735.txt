so I'm in a phil of science seminar this semester and we've spent the last two weeks on the verisimilitude problem -- Popper's definition, the Miller/Tichý result showing it falls apart, and then the later attempts (Niiniluoto-style distance metrics, the content/likeness stuff from Oddie, that whole literature). I've come out of it with a fairly strong position and I want it stress-tested before I commit to it in my term paper.

My position: the metric program for truthlikeness is beyond saving, not because of any one formal result but because the whole idea of a language-independent, content-based "distance from the truth" is confused -- what counts as closer to the truth is always relative to which features of the world we care about for a given inquiry, so the right successor notion is something purely pragmatic/epistemic: theory A is "more verisimilar" than B iff expecting according to A yields better accuracy-weighted outcomes for the questions we're actually asking. In other words, verisimilitude talk should be deflated into a claim about expected epistemic utility relative to an interest, and anyone still looking for an objective interest-independent metric is chasing a ghost.

What would count against this? Don't just give me the standard rehashed objections to Popper -- I want the defeaters aimed at *my* deflationary move. Are there cases where my relativized notion gives clearly the wrong verdict -- e.g. where we'd want to say one theory is straightforwardly closer to the truth simpliciter and any interest-relativity seems like a cop-out? Does my view collapse into something else already on the table (van Fraassen-ish pragmatism? just Bayesianism with extra steps?), and would that be a refutation or an acceptable cost? Would a realist about approximate truth have to reject it wholesale, and if so is that a point against my view or against realism?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying, and be honest about which ones defenders of metric accounts would themselves concede actually bite versus which ones they'd shrug off. Also: has my position been qualified into something unfalsifiable -- like, is "relative to the interests of the inquiry" doing so much work that no case could ever count against it?
