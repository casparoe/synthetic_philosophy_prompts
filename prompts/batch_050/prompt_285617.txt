So I'm a junior and I somehow ended up on my college's centennial committee, and this semester I'm also taking a seminar on the ethics of memory, which is making the whole thing feel a bit on the nose. Here's the situation: an archivist spent two years documenting that from roughly the 1940s through the 60s, researchers at our med school ran experiments on patients at the county asylum without consent, and the dean at the time knew about it and buried it. The report is finished and public, so nobody's proposing a cover-up. The fight is over what we do now. Roughly three camps: (1) a permanent memorial plus an annual day of remembrance woven into college ritual; (2) a plaque and one big public reckoning event this year, then let it rest; (3) no dedicated commemoration at all — fold the findings into a warts-and-all centennial history book and move on.

It maps almost embarassingly well onto the seminar readings. We read Margalit (The Ethics of Memory) — communities of memory, memory as what holds thick relations together, and this idea of a division of moral labor where somebody has to do the remembering. And Rieff (In Praise of Forgetting), which is basically a brief for camp 3: ritualized public remembrance as self-congratulation, forgetting as a precondition for institutions actually changing, and skepticism about the whole therapeutic rationale for collective memory.

Can you draft the decision memo I'd circulate before the vote? Pick a camp (or a principled hybrid), give a clear bottom line the committee could actually act on, and take the strongest argument for the losing side seriously rather than waving at it. Two specific things I want: first, grapple with the problem that the college's relation to patients dead for 60+ years is about as thin as relations get, so on Margalit's own terms it's not obvious we're the ones who bear the duty — but then if not us, who? Second, please be honest about which premises here are genuinely contested among people who work on this stuff and which are fairly settled — I don't want the memo presenting Rieff's side as the consensus if it isn't, or vice versa.
