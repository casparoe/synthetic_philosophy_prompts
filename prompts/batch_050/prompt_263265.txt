I'm writing a section of a course packet on concepts of freedom, and I want a worked example that takes the COVID-19 lockdowns of 2020–21 and runs them through the three standard notions of liberty — Berlin's negative and positive liberty, and the republican conception of freedom as non-domination associated with Pettit and Skinner. I know the public health arguments and the standard libertarian objection; what I want is the conceptual mapping done carefully. Take the salient facts as common knowledge: restrictions were enacted through legislation or emergency decrees, were generally time-limited and publicly announced, were enforced with fines and in some places curfews, and in some jurisdictions were challengeable in court, while in others enforcement was visibly discretionary and uneven (businesses shuttered by official discretion while connected firms stayed open, gatherings tolerated or punished depending on who was gathering). Analyze who is the agent, what the obstacles are, and what the freedoms are freedoms to do, on each of the three views — MacCallum's triadic schema (X is free from Y to do or become Z) may help keep the mapping honest, so use it if it does.

Then I want the two directions of the analysis kept distinct. First, theory illuminating case: where does the republican notion do work that the negative notion cannot — for instance, does it explain why discretionary, unpredictable enforcement felt like a loss of freedom even to people who were never actually fined, and does it also explain why a rule-bound, court-reviewable lockdown might count as interference without domination? Second, case testing theory: what does the episode show about each view? Does the widespread sense that even unenforced emergency powers were threatening confirm the republican claim that actual interference isn't necessary for unfreedom? Does the way "positive freedom" language appeared on both sides of the debate — the freedom to move about without fear of infection versus the freedom to run one's business and associate — support or undercut Berlin's warning that positive liberty is easily captured? And identify at least one thing the whole three-way framework cannot say about the episode — for example, whether it can weigh one population's negative liberty against another's, or whether it falls silent on that.

Lead with a two-sentence bottom line stating which of the three notions you think best captures what was at stake, then use headed sections. Maximum 850 words.
