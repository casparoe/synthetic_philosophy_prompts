So I run a small history reading group and we've spent the last three months on the French Revolution, and honestly I'm stuck on something that's bigger than the Revolution itself. We started with the old textbook story: a rising capitalist bourgeoisie strangled by a feudal aristocracy, 1789 as the class seizing power, feudal dues abolished, capitalism unleashed. Then we read the revisionist pushback from the 60s and 70s onward: the leading "bourgeois" and the nobles were economically almost indistinguishable, lots of revolutionaries were lawyers and office-holders not capitalists, so there was no rising class doing anything -- the revolution was made by political actors, ideas, bad luck, and the Terror wasn't the logic of the thing working itself out but a contingent product of war and panic. Fine. But here's what's been driving me nuts: this fight has been going for like sixty years now, both sides know all the same archives, and as far as I can tell nobody has converted anybody. The class people just keep replying that the revisionists are being naive about what class means, and the revisionists reply that the class people are being unfalsifiable.

So what I want from you is not a verdict on who wins but a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this even is. Is it still secretly a factual dispute (about who the revolutionaries actually were) that more archival work could settle? Or a dispute about what counts as an explanation of a giant historical event -- one side thinks a real cause has to be structural, the other thinks events can just cascade from contingency? Or is it basically verbal -- "bourgeoisie" and "caused by class" doing different work in each camp's sentences? And one more thing, offered as a hypothesis I can't defend well: does the whole "do revolutions devour their children" framing that both sides keep using smuggle in a shared mistake -- treating "the Revolution" as an agent with an inner logic -- such that the impasse isn't about France at all but about whether big historical events are the kind of thing that can have a nature? Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here: a new distinction, a new kind of evidence, or should we just accept that some questions this size don't get answered?
