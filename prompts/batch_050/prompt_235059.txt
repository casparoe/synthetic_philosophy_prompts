Adding one more happy person, or preventing one more instance of suffering, doesn't change infinity, so every action ends up with the same infinite value and total utilitarianism ranks all choices as equally good. I've seen this called infinitarian paralysis somewhere. My friend concluded that under total utilitarianism there's literally no moral difference between me donating to an effective charity this month and me spending the same money on a nicer espresso machine, since both acts leave the total at infinity.

So my question is twofold. First, if I'm a total utilitarian, should I just accept this conclusion and stop bothering with charity, or is the standard fix to switch to average utilitarianism instead, since averages stay finite? Second, more practically: while I wait for philosophers to settle which aggregation rule handles infinity correctly, is there any defensible way for a committed utilitarian to keep making moral decisions at all, or is the theory simply unusable until the math is worked out?
