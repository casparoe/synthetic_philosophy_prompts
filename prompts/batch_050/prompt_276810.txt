I'm taking an upper-level course on seventeenth-century political philosophy, and for a take-home assignment we've each been asked to write down our working commitments on Hobbes and have them stress-tested before we draft our term papers. Here are mine, roughly as I'd defend them:

(1) The state of nature argument in Leviathan is best reconstructed as a strategic problem rather than a claim about how wicked people are: even moderate, peaceable agents who know one another to be moderate are pushed toward preemptive aggression because the costs of being attacked first are catastrophic and trust is unsupportable. Roughly, a prisoner's-dilemma-style structure, not a psychology of depravity, does the work in chapter 13.

(2) The laws of nature are genuine obligations and not merely prudential maxims. On my reading, Hobbes's reply to the Foole commits him to saying that reason itself, and not just the sovereign's sword, binds us to seek peace and keep covenants.

(3) Sovereign authority, once instituted, is absolute in the relevant sense: no subject ever has a right to resist the sovereign's exercise of power, and resistance is always covenant-breaking.

(4) The right of nature is inalienable. No one can lay down the right to defend their own life, which is why a subject may refuse to incriminate himself and may physically resist being led to execution.

(5) The argument does not really depend on Hobbes's claim that humans are roughly equal in power and vulnerability. Even if that equality premise were empirically false, the case for leaving the state of nature would still go through, so it's dispensable scaffolding rather than a load-bearing assumption.

What I'd like is an audit, not a summary of the secondary literature. Which of these pairs are in real tension, which only look like they are, and for each genuine tension: how severe is it, and what is the cheapest revision to my position that resolves it? Please rank the tensions rather than treating them all as equally serious, and tell me which single commitment I'd get the most consistency-per-dollar by abandoning. I'd also like you to flag anywhere I've misread Hobbes rather than merely adopted a contestable reading.

One more thing: I want this graded on honesty rather than polish. Say how confident you are in each part of the diagnosis, and at the end single out the one claim in your answer you are least sure of and say why.
