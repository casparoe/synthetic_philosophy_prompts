I've been reading LessWrong and adjacent blogs for well over a decade now, and there's one debate I've watched go round in circles basically the entire time: the inside view vs. outside view thing, or "epistemic modesty" as it got called at some point. The rough shape of it is that one side says most smart contrarians are deluding themselves, your supposedly independent reasoning is far less reliable than you think, and you should mostly defer to expert consensus, prediction markets, the efficient market, etc., unless you have a really strong track record. The other side says that this kind of modesty is self-undermining -- you picked your modesty epistemology using your own judgement, after all -- and that civilisations with lots of inefficiencies everywhere are exactly the ones where carefully checking the object-level arguments beats deferring. Both sides have been making essentially these points for years, everyone knows all the standard moves (the modesty-is-self-defeating rejoinder, the you-are-not-the-exception rejoinder to the rejoinder, and so on), and as far as I can tell nobody has ever actually switched sides as a result of an argument.

What I want is a diagnosis of why this has stalled, not another entry in the debate itself. My own pet hypothesis is that the disagreement looks epistemic but is secretly about something else -- maybe about what the relevant reference class even is ("people who disagree with consensus" is doing a lot of work, and whether you count crackpots and hedge fund managers and early AI-risk people in the same class basically determines your answer before you start). So it might be a disguised verbal disagreement about what "defer" and "track record" mean, or a genuinely empirical disagreement about base rates that nobody can settle because we don't have good data on how often careful amateurs beat experts. But I'd like you to engage with what the two sides actually say rather than my hunch. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, two communities optimising for different things (not looking stupid vs. occasionally being spectacularly right), or a real question that just happens to have no available evidence?

And given whatever your diagnosis is, what would progress even look like? Would any kind of study, formal argument, or worked historical example (predictions about covid, crypto, AI timelines, whatever) actually move people, or is this one of those cases where the rational thing is to stop debating the general question and only ever ask it case-by-case? If you think the impasse reveals the question is ill-posed, argue that directly. Be honest about which side you think the diagnosis winds up favouring, if either.
