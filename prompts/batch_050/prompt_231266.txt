Ok so I never studied philosophy or neuroscience, I just got stuck on this after reading about mind uploading stuff and now its bugging me alot. If you replace my brain one neuron at a time with tiny machine neurons that do the exact same job, I feel like maybe I survive? Like there's no single moment where "I" die, it's just my brain slowly becoming a ship of theseus situation. But if instead you scan me and make a perfect copy in a computer while old me gets destroyed, that feels way more like murder plus a confident stranger waking up somewhere else. The weird part is I can't say where the line is, which makes me suspicious there even is a clean line.

Write a satirical corporate FAQ plus short customer-support chat for an invented startup called Continuity+ that sells gradual neuron replacement as a subscription, and also offers a cheaper instant upload plan for people in a hurry. Deadpan cheerful Silicon Valley voice, around 700 words. The target should be the smug assumption that if memory, personality, and behavior are preserved then of course its still you, dont worry about it. But please make the jokes come from the actual creepy details, not random nonsense: no single replaced neuron should count as the death point, the old biological neurons get returned as trade-in credit, customers at 61% synthetic keep getting emails saying congrats on becoming more yourself, the instant plan has a branching problem when they restore from backup, and a beta tester asks whether the freezer full of their original cells counts as them being alive, dead, or "parked."

Don't have the company admit the problem in a winking way. Let the absurdity leak out through policy language, edge cases, and one support agent who is definately too calm. If philosophical terms show up, keep them buried under startup jargon so a normal reader gets it and a picky reader thinks, oh no, fair hit.
