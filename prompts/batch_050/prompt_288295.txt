I keep running into this pattern and want to know whether it has a name. Someone can get a benefit only if others can rely on her later doing something that, when the later moment arrives, will be pointlessly costly for her. Example: a stranded driver will be rescued only if the rescuer is confident she really will pay him afterward; there is no contract, no audience, and no repeat interaction, so paying later gains her nothing, but unless she is now the sort of person who would pay, she is left stranded. Or a founder can attract backers only by being visibly unable to quit after losses mount, though quitting would then be sensible. This seems adjacent to time-inconsistency, commitment problems, weakness of will, and Parfit-style hitchhiker cases, but I mean the narrower pattern: it is rational now to acquire or signal a disposition whose later exercise would be irrational. Does that exact configuration have an established label? If not, propose one, define it, give two boundary cases where it stops applying, and say whether the later act is best called irrational, rationally required by the earlier rational step, or neither.
