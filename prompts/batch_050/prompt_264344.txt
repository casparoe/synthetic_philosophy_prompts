I've long been a thirder on the Sleeping Beauty problem: upon awakening, Beauty's credence in heads should be 1/3. My grounds are the usual ones — the long-run frequency argument, the betting analysis, and the extreme variant where tails triggers a million awakenings, in which the halfer's 1/2 seems indefensible. But lately I've suspected I've treated the puzzle as settled without ever mapping what my position actually risks. Halfers have replies I haven't fully absorbed: that the betting arguments equivocate between one wager per experiment and one wager per awakening, that an awakening Beauty knew was guaranteed cannot constitute evidence for anything, and that the million-awakenings pump trades on conflating credence with expected frequency.

So: what would count against thirding? I don't want the standard halfer case restated; I want a map of my position's exposure. Which results, if established, would be genuinely decisive — for instance, a demonstration that the thirder's update on self-locating information violates a consistency principle I presumably accept, or a consequence in a neighboring case I'd have to reject (thirding seems to commit me to SIA-style anthropic reasoning, and hence to the embarrassments of the Presumptuous Philosopher)? Which considerations would be merely awkward rather than fatal? What do thirding's own defenders concede as damaging, as opposed to what its critics allege? And has the view been quietly insulated — by retreating to "the answer depends on the betting setup," or to the claim that 1/3 and 1/2 answer different well-formed questions — in a way that should worry me on its own, regardless of who's right?
