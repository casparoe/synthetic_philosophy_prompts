After a long argument with a friend about Fitch's paradox of knowability, I'd like to close the evening on a lighter note. Write a joke about the paradox that would actually land with someone who knows the proof: its punchline should turn on the specific trouble with knowing the conjunction p and it is not known that p — namely, that knowing it would require knowing each conjunct, and knowing the second conjunct would render the first conjunct false — rather than on generic humor about philosophers not knowing anything. If it helps the joke breathe, you may also play on the knowability principle itself, or on the slide from possibly known to known that the anti-realist never saw coming.
