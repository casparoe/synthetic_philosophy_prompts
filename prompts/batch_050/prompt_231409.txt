Over the past year, largely through a reading group on philosophy of mind and some conversations with a friend who studies social insects, I've come to accept a fairly strong position: that an ant colony, taken as a whole, literally possesses a mind -- not metaphorically, not merely "as if," but in roughly the same sense in which we ascribe one to an octopus. My reasons are functionalist at bottom. Colonies of house-hunting ants evaluate candidate nest sites against multiple criteria, pool partial information across individuals, and commit to a choice once a threshold is crossed; colonies retain foraging knowledge across the complete turnover of their workers, so that the thing remembering outlives everything composing it; and the colony's processing is massively decentralized, which I take to be a point in its favor rather than against, since brains are decentralized too, just faster and denser. I've also thought about the obvious disanalogies -- a colony "decides" on a timescale of hours, its components walk away and get replaced mid-thought, there is no anatomically privileged center -- and each seems to me to be a difference of degree, or to push equally against ascribing thought to any sufficiently distributed system, including possibly us.

What I'd like from you is a map of this position's exposure: what would count against it. Please go beyond the standard opening moves ("but there's nobody home," "it's just a metaphor") and tell me which empirical discoveries about colony mechanisms, which conceptual arguments, and which consequences of the view would genuinely undermine it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. In particular: would it defeat the position if colony-level decisions turned out to be fully predictable from local interaction rules with no colony-level informational integration, or is that demand one we also couldn't meet for neurons? Does the view require phenomenal consciousness, or can it retreat to "cognition without experience" -- and if it retreats, has it been qualified into something unfalsifiable? I'm also worried about boundaries: if the colony qualifies, why not the colony plus its aphid herds, or a slime mold, or a city's traffic system? One thing that would move me personally: I already concede that the human immune system integrates information, learns, and makes what look like decisions, yet I deny it has a mind -- so if you can identify a principled line that excludes the immune system but includes the colony (or show that my position forces me to grant both), that would be the most instructive outcome. Where a committed defender of colony minds would concede a defeater rather than dismiss it, please say so explicitly.
