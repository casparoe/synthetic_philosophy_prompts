ok so my school does this thing at the end of the semester where philosophy club kids give short talks and then get grilled, and mine is on Zhuangzi and death. My thesis: Zhuangzi is NOT saying death is great, and he's not even saying grief is wrong exactly. He's saying that being terrified of death, or being totally destroyed when someone dies, only makes sense if you're confident that being alive in this body as "me" is better than whatever death turns you into — and his whole deal is that we're never in a position to know that. He even has this bit about how hating death might be like a kid who ran off young and forgot the way home. So deep grief is built on a mistake, like sobbing because your ice melted when you never actually knew if you wanted the ice or the water more. My evidence is three stories: him drumming on a tub and singing after his wife dies (he admits he cried at first, but then thought about how before her birth there was no body and no life-force, and birth and death are just more changes like the seasons, so wailing forever means not getting how change works); the sick guy who says if the transformation process turns his arm into a rooster he'll just use it to tell time; and Zhuangzi refusing a fancy funeral because the birds and bugs can have him.

The audience: our advisor Mr. Halpern, who did a philosophy phd and is obsessed with Epicurus and the whole analytic debate about whether death is even bad for the person who dies — his favorite move is "ok but where's the actual argument, or is this just vibes" — plus alot of students.
