Write a customer-service FAQ, about 600 words in American English, for an online furniture retailer whose corporate legal department has officially adopted the organicist answer to the special composition question: nothing exists except simples and living organisms, and what the catalog calls tables, chairs, and bookshelves are, strictly speaking, only simples arranged tablewise, chairwise, and shelfwise. The FAQ should answer ordinary customer questions in the soothing, evasive register of real corporate help pages -- delivery, assembly, warranty claims, returns, and a loyalty program -- while never once conceding that the merchandise exists. Suggested questions include: whether the customer may return a defective item (difficult, as there is no item); what the assembly instructions are for (nothing is assembled; the simples come to be arranged chairwise); why the warranty covers the frame but not, as one complainant put it, the chair itself; and what happened to the sofa during shipping (the simples are now arranged porchwise). Include one genuinely existing exception, such as a houseplant or a customer's cat, which alone qualifies for returns on metaphysical grounds. In a final question, let a customer object that a rival school holds that everything composes something, so that her order must at least exist fused with the warehouse, the delivery van, and the Moon; the reply should politely confirm that under that view shipping would indeed be free, and decline to change policy. The humor should come from the metaphysics being done correctly -- a metaphysician should find the satire fair rather than confused -- and the tone should stay deadpan throughout, with no winking at the reader.
