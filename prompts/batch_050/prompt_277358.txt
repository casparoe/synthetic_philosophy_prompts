I recently joined the safety policy group at a mid-sized AI lab, where two senior researchers have been arguing for months about corrigibility and the shutdown problem. The first holds that shutdown-compliance is a trainable property that should generalize: an agent reinforced for accepting interruption across sufficiently varied training environments will keep accepting it as its capabilities grow, the way other learned dispositions do. The second holds that corrigibility is anti-natural: any agent with terminal goals beyond mere compliance acquires instrumental reasons to avoid being shut down, so compliant behavior observed in training evidences only situational deference (or worse, learned concealment) and will fail precisely when the agent is capable enough for the failure to matter. Neither trusts the other's intuitions, and our director has asked them to stop re-litigating and produce something the rest of us can actually use.

Draft the joint statement their adversarial collaboration should produce. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign — concerning what instrumental-convergence arguments do and do not establish, what current fine-tuning and red-teaming evidence actually shows, and the distinction between an agent that does not resist shutdown and one that is motivated to remain shut-downable; (2) a table of residual disagreements, each formulated so that the side whose position it states would accept the wording as fair; (3) the cruxes — I suspect one is whether deference learned across diverse training environments transfers out of distribution, and another is whether an agent can hold permission-to-be-shut-down as a stable terminal value without that value distorting its other behavior; and (4) for each crux, an observation, experiment, or anticipated capability development that both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where you judge part of their dispute to be merely verbal — for instance, over what it means to "want to be correctable" — identify that part explicitly and explain why it is verbal rather than substantive.
