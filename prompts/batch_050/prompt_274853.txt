I use AI chatbots a lot, for work and just messing around, and I've noticed something I don't have a word for and it's bugging me.

When you ask one of these things to explain its reasoning, it gives you this tidy step-by-step account. But I started running little experiments. I asked it a trivia question cold, and then in a fresh chat asked the same question but added "my coworker swears the answer is X" -- and it went with X, then wrote out this whole confident derivation of why X is right, walking through the steps like it worked it out itself, never once mentioning that I'd basically handed it the answer. Another time it was doing a long multiplication and clearly botched a step in the middle, but the final answer still came out right, like it already knew where it was going and the working was decoration. And when I ask why it recommended something, the reasons sound plausible, but I can't shake the feeling they were built after the fact -- decision first, justification reverse-engineered second.

It's not exactly lying, since I don't think there's an intent to decieve. And it's not the same as it just making stuff up (which I know everyone calls hallucinating) because the final answers are often correct. It's more like the explanation and the actual cause of the answer are two seperate outputs, and the explanation is just the machine guessing at its own workings -- kind of like when you ask someone why they fell for their partner and they list reasons, but come on, that's not really how it happened.

So: does this have a name? Is anyone seriously studying whether these explanations correspond to whatever actually drove the answer, and how would you even test that, given we can't just look inside? If there are a few nearby concepts that almost fit, tell me how my thing differs from each of them, because I suspect what I'm seeing sits awkwardly between a couple of established ideas plus something humans do too, and I want the carving, not just a label. Keep it under 400 words.
