For a graduate seminar on the philosophy of AI, I am designing a unit on classifying defective AI outputs. The anchor is Harry Frankfurt's account of bullshit: on that account, the liar must track the truth in order to conceal it, whereas the bullshitter is simply indifferent to whether what they say is true, and this indifference, rather than falsity, is the essence of bullshit. The seminar's live question is whether LLM "hallucinations" -- fluent, confident, false outputs -- are best understood as lies, as bullshit, or as mere error, given that the systems producing them arguably have no beliefs and no intentions at all.

Write a decision tree that takes a described episode of false or misleading speech (by a human or a machine) as input and delivers a classification at each leaf. The tree should have roughly eight to twelve branch nodes and must include all of the following leaves: lying; Frankfurt-style bullshit; honest error; confabulation in the clinical sense (a false report sincerely taken to be true by the person who produces it, with no intent to deceive); at least one leaf for cases where the classificatory question is ill-posed because the producer lacks the mental states the tree's earlier questions presuppose; and at least two leaves for categories that our existing vocabulary does not name -- combinations of answers that are conceptually available but for which English has no settled term. Give those leaves stipulative names and one sentence each on why the gap in the vocabulary might exist.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names. The diagnostic questions should concern things like: whether the producer has beliefs at all; whether the production of the output causally depended on whether it is true; whether the producer would have produced the same output had the facts been otherwise (a counterfactual-sensitivity test, which I want on the tree as a way of operationalizing Frankfurtian indifference for systems where talk of mental states is contested); whether there is an intention to deceive; and whether the producer represents itself as answerable to the truth. Two constraints on the nodes: falsity itself must not appear as a branch (every leaf should be reachable by false and by true outputs alike), and each question must be phrased so that someone could answer it from evidence about how the output was produced, without already knowing which label the tree will deliver.

After the tree, do three things. First, walk through these three episodes and show which leaf each reaches: a politician who invents a statistic to survive a debate; a sleep-deprived person who sincerely but falsely remembers locking the door; an LLM that produces a plausible-sounding citation to a paper that does not exist. If any of these lands somewhere surprising, say what that reveals about the tree. Second, note which combinations of answers your nodes permit but no leaf occupies, and briefly diagnose each gap (is the position incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored?). Third, flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot reliably separate in practice -- for instance, where bullshit and negligent error converge because indifference and carelessness leave the same evidence -- and state what additional information, if any, would pry them apart.
