So I run a small philosophy meetup at my local library (about 12 people, we get the back room for 90 minutes) and next month's session is on personal identity. This got a lot less abstract for me recently because my dad was diagnosed with early-stage Alzheimers, and I've been chewing on the Locke thing where you're the same person as your past self iff you can remember being them, plus Reid's old "brave officer" objection -- the general remembers being the young officer, the officer remembers being the boy who got flogged at school, but the general doesn't remember the boy, so by Locke's own criterion the general is and isn't the boy, since identity is supposed to be transitive and memory apparently isn't.

I want an activity, not a lecture. Something where the group actually runs INTO the transitivity failure themselves before I name it. My rough idea is some kind of staged role-play or "passing the torch" setup where different people embody different life-stages of one character and the group has to vote on sameness-of-person questions, and the rules of what memories get carried forward are rigged so the contradiction pops out at them. But I'm open to a totally different structure if you have a better one.

Please give me: concrete rules and any materials/props I'd need (index cards, memory tokens, whatever), a script of the decisions or votes participants face, roughly how long each phase takes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto Locke's criterion and Reid's counterexample. Also tell me what's going to predictably go wrong the first time (people gaming the memory rules, someone refusing to vote, etc.) and how to handle it. And be honest about which part of the philosophical problem the game can't actually reproduce and I'll just have to say out loud.

One more thing: most of my group hasn't read Locke or Reid and never will, so the activity has to work for them, but a couple of philosophy grads will be there and I dont want them bored either.
