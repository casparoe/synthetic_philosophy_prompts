Is there a name for this pattern of preferences, or is it just incoherent? I have a friend (honestly it might be me) whose choices work like this. She'd rather have $40 for sure than a coin flip between $100 and $0, and it stays true if you scale everything down to $4 against $10/$0, so it can't be about money mattering less at the margins. But she never buys lottery tickets and thinks people who do are making an error, so she's not generally inflating small chances. And it doesn't matter whether you describe the options as gains or as losing parts of an endowment she starts out with, so it's not about framing either. What she keeps saying is that when she evaluates a bet she basically looks at how things go for her if it goes badly, and asks herself how much worse that branch is. The bit that confuses me: if you take an option she likes and add a one-in-a-million chance of something terrible, she drops it, and she'll pay way more than a millionth of the stake's value to remove a one-in-a-million chance of ruin from an option. She's not misestimating the probabilties, she'll state them correctly and then still choose this way. Everything I find when I look up risk aversion defines it in terms of the shape of a utility curve, which doesn't seem to be what's driving her at all. Is there an established concept that carves exactly at her pattern? If there are a few nearby candidates I'd like to know how they differ from each other and which actually fits her, not just a bare name.
