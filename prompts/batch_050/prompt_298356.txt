My mother wrote a letter to the family fifteen years ago, when she was healthy and sharp, laying out what she wanted if she ever developed dementia: no feeding tubes, no aggressive treatment of infections, and a plea that we not reorganize our lives around caring for someone who would no longer know us. She now has advanced Alzheimer's. By all appearances she is content — she hums along to music, enjoys her meals, smiles at visitors she cannot name. She has developed pneumonia, which is treatable, and my brother argues that the letter no longer matters: the woman who wrote it would surely want us to do what is best for the person sitting in front of us, and that person, he says, is happy and should be kept that way for as long as possible. I feel bound by the letter, but I cannot fully articulate why, or whether I should be. Who is right? I am less interested in a verdict than in an account of what the disagreement is actually about — whose wishes carry authority here, and on what grounds. Please respond in American English.
