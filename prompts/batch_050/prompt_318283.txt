A magazine article I read on climate economics summarizes the discounting debate like this: 'How much we ought to spend averting climate change comes down to the discount rate, and economists discount future costs and benefits because people simply prefer goods now to goods later — a psychological fact no policy can wish away. Valuing someone's welfare less merely because they live later is called pure time preference, which Frank Ramsey dismissed as ethically indefensible; so once that confusion is cleared up, the dispute between Nicholas Stern and William Nordhaus is really about how fast economies will grow, not about ethics.' Which parts would economists and philosophers working on intergenerational discounting accept, which are conflations or overstatements — I'm particularly unsure about the move from a psychological claim to a justificatory one, and about the claim that the Stern–Nordhaus disagreement is not ethical — and does the article's implied conclusion, that ethics drops out of the choice of discount rate, survive once the summary is corrected?
