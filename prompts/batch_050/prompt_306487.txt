Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a demandingness objector and a plain act consequentialist, starting from this bit of John Stuart Mill’s Utilitarianism: “All action is for the sake of some end, and rules of action, it seems natural to suppose, must take their whole character and colour from the end to which they are subservient.” Make them argue over what follows from it, not over whether Mill is sacred. Include: numbered claims both can sign; a disagreements table where each side’s position is worded so that side would accept it; and the cruxes, especially two invented-but-useful ones — the “sacrifice ceiling” (how much cost to self is too much before a duty becomes unreasonable) and “moral overdraft” (whether an agent may borrow against future wellbeing, projects, or relationships to meet present demands). For each crux, give a case both accept as relevant and write down each side’s prediction. Keep it constructive, dont adjudicate, and if part of the row is merely verbal say which part.
