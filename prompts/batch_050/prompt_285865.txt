I keep running into this phrase "I am a strange loop" and I've realized I have no idea what it actually claims. Context: I don't have any philosophy background, I read Hofstadter's book (the one with that title) a couple years ago after I got obsessed with GEB in college, and I loved it but couldn't tell you what the thesis is if my life depended on it. Since then I hear the phrase everywhere -- a coworker used it last week to argue that a sufficiently self-referential AI would "have a self in the only sense that matters," and a friend used it to mean something like "the self is an illusion, wake up sheeple." These seem like totally different claims??

So here's what I want. Take the slogan "I am a strange loop" and break it apart into the distinct precise claims it could be making. Some candidates I can half-see, feel free to correct or add:

1. The "I" is a symbol inside the brain, and what it refers to is the whole system that contains the symbol, so there's this loopy reference structure
2. The self is literally a feedback loop, like a thermostat or a video pointing a camera at its own screen, and that's ALL it is
3. The self is an illusion -- a story the brain tells, with nobody actually home
4. Consciousness itself emerges from self-reference somehow, so strange-loopiness explains why there's something it's like to be me
5. It's basically just a pretty metaphor and doesn't commit to anything

For each reading (yours or mine), tell me: is it a platitude once you state it carefully, is it substantive and defensible, or is it actually false or confused? I suspect #2 and #3 contradict each other in an interesting way (if the self is just a loop, isn't the loop real?), but maybe I'm wrong about that.

Then the important part: which reading does my coworker NEED for his AI argument to work? My gut says he's using the slogan to do a bait-and-switch -- "self = self-reference" sounds innocent, but "self = the thing that matters about me" is doing all the work, and those aren't obviously the same reading. And same question for my friend -- does "I am a strange loop" even support the "self is an illusion" conclusion, or is that a different thesis dressed up in the same words?

One more thing that bugs me: if the slogan is right, is the "I" that says it the loop, or a symbol in the loop, or the looper?? I can't keep these straight and I suspect most people who quote it can't either.
