I keep encountering the following pattern when estimating risk from historical data, and I would like to know whether it has an established name.

Suppose I am estimating whether a software deployment will cause an incident. All of these statements can be true:

- Deployments across the company have a 4 percent incident rate.
- Deployments of Go services have an 8 percent rate.
- Deployments by teams with at least five years of experience have a 2 percent rate.
- Deployments on Fridays have a 10 percent rate.
- Deployments of Go services by experienced teams have a 3 percent rate.
- Deployments of Go services by experienced teams on Fridays have happened only once before, and that deployment caused an incident.

Thus, the same deployment has a different historical frequency depending on which true description of it I use. Adding more features makes the comparison group more similar to the present case but also smaller and less statistically reliable. Removing features gives me more data but may hide an important distinction. The problem is not that anyone is lying, ignoring the base rate, or confusing correlation with causation; it is that the event belongs to many legitimate groups at once, and there may be no non-arbitrary rule for deciding which one supplies the relevant frequency.

I can construct similar cases involving hiring, insurance, medical prognosis, and criminal sentencing. Sometimes two reasonable groupings even support opposite practical conclusions, while a highly specific grouping contains only the event under consideration.

Does the general phenomenon have an established name? More specifically, is there a separate name for this narrower configuration: several factually accurate descriptions of the same event yield incompatible frequencies, with no prior agreement about which description is the relevant one? Please distinguish it from nearby ideas such as cherry-picking, Simpson's paradox, base-rate neglect, overfitting, and the problem of induction. If no established term fits the narrower configuration exactly, propose one, define it precisely, and give examples that would fall just inside and just outside its boundaries. Please answer in American English.
