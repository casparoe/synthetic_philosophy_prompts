Financial markets run on trades, and the whole point of the institution — real-time price feeds, analyst coverage, disclosure rules — is that prices end up reflecting information. But there's a famous result: if everyone is fully rational, shares a common prior, and everyone's rationality is common knowledge, then no trade should ever happen, because anyone's willingness to trade with you is itself information that kills the deal (so-called no-trade theorems). Meanwhile insider trading law assumes roughly the opposite: it bans certain trades precisely because the informed trader's information would get baked into the price. What view of information and disagreement do these practices presuppose — that traders genuinely disagree, that priors aren't actually common, or just that most traders are a bit irrational, and which of these does the market need most? Are the insider trading rules and the price-reflects-information story even consistant with each other? And if you rebuilt market institutions around whichever diagnosis you land on, what would concretely have to change?
