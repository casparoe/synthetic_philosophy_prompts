Here's my opening move: suppose you and I play a ten-node centipede for real money, and I pass at node one. On your own view, passing was irrational of me -- so the fact that I passed is evidence that I am not the kind of player your theory describes, and that should rationally change what you expect from me at node three. But if my observed move legitimately updates your beliefs about me, then the backward induction chain never actually gets started between real players, because it only runs on a fictional, idealized version of each other. And notice the bind: the only evidence your theory could ever be tested against -- what people actually do -- is evidence that comes precisely from its prediction failing. Isn't backward induction self-defeating in exactly this way? Defend it.
