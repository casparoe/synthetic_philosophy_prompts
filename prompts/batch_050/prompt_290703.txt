I handle food purchasing for a twelve-person housing co-op that has decided, after much debate, to source its shared kitchen with the aim of minimizing expected animal suffering rather than following any simple rule like "no meat." Every month I make roughly fifteen sourcing decisions -- which staple proteins to stock, whether to switch suppliers, whether a member's requested item clears the bar -- and each decision gets maybe twenty minutes of my time and has to be defensible in a one-paragraph writeup to the food committee, none of whom have read any of the underlying literature.

The problem is that the obvious heuristics break down in ways I now think are real rather than pedantic. First, what I'll call the small-body problem: shifting a calorie of demand from beef to chicken multiplies the number of individual animals raised and killed by something like an order of magnitude or two, and the animals live worse lives to boot, so naive "less red meat" advice may be actively harmful on suffering grounds. Second, harvest deaths: crop cultivation kills field animals in nontrivial numbers (rodents, ground-nesting birds, animals killed by machinery), and while I don't trust any specific estimate of this, I can't just set it to zero either. Third, land use: pasture-raised beef uses much more land per calorie than plant crops, and that land, if not farmed, would support wild animals whose lives are plausibly net-negative on some views about wild animal welfare -- which cuts in a disturbing direction -- but plausibly net-positive on others, so the sign of the effect is contested. Fourth, moral and empirical uncertainty: I'm not confident how to weigh a chicken-day against a cow-day (neuron counts? behavior? some committee-decided conversion factor?), and I'm not confident wild animal lives are net bad, and the procedure has to function anyway rather than seize up.

What I want is a reusable decision procedure -- a checklist or ordered set of tests I can run in twenty minutes with no web access and no spreadsheet modeling -- that converts whatever the best current arguments actually imply into sourcing decisions. It should specify: which distinctions have to survive the simplification (I suspect the per-calorie conversion across species is one; I suspect fine-grained differences between two lentil suppliers are not), what default to use when a number I need is unknown or contested, how to handle the wild-animal-land-use term given that its sign is disputed (do I ignore it, split the difference, or treat the dispute itself as a reason to prefer options robust across both signs?), and at which steps I'm exercising judgment dressed up as procedure. It should also say plainly what the procedure cannot do -- what categories of decision I should kick back to the committee as value disagreements rather than resolve procedurally.

Then apply the procedure to three cases: (1) replacing our bulk chicken thighs with an equal number of calories of conventional ground beef, (2) adding pasture-raised eggs as a staple, and (3) a member requesting farmed shrimp, where I have almost no usable data. At least one of these worked applications should be a case where the procedure outputs an answer the asker -- someone whose instinct is roughly "less meat is better, red meat worst of all" -- will find unwelcome, and you should flag it as such rather than smoothing it over.

Please lead with the bottom line in the first sentence -- a one-sentence statement of the procedure's core rule -- then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
