My roommate is an amateur photographer and we've been going back and forth for years about wether you ever count as genuinely seeing something when there's a device in between, or whether you're just seeing a trace of it. Her verdicts so far: the moon through her telescope, yes, obviously seeing it. A distant star through the same telescope, one that might well have gone supernova centuries ago, still seeing it, since the light is arriving right now. The live CCTV feed at her work, yes, she can see the parking lot from the break room. An old photograph of her grandmother, yes, "that's really her." A realistic oil portrait of the same grandmother painted from life, no, you're seeing the painter's version of her. A footprint in wet cement, no, that's evidence someone was there, not seeing them. Now the case we actually need a ruling on: her sister is pregnant, and at the last scan they watched a live 3D ultrasound, rendered in real time from sound echoes in that amber color scheme, and the tech could rotate the viewpoint around to angles no probe ever actually occupied. Did they see the fetus? Predict my roommate's verdict and tell me which rule her track record is really running: something like transparency-as-optics (light from the scene, mechanically preserved, no one's beliefs in the chain, storage and time lag are fine), versus transparency-as-dependence (any channel counts as long as the image counterfactually depends on the scene without passing through anyone's beliefs about it), versus transparency-as-appearance (you only see X if the image shows you how X visibly looks, or would look to someone standing there). Say what each rule predicts about the ultrasound, give me your best guess with reasons, and name one further case that would settle it between whichever rules are still tied after your answer.
