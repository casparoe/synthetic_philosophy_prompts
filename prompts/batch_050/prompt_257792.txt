I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and over several years of reporting on welfare and disability benefits I have kept running into a configuration I don't have a name for. I'd like to know whether the literature has one.

Two cases, simplified from real reporting. First: a disability program adds a periodic reassessment requirement, meant to catch people who keep claiming after they have recovered. Fraud savings turn out to be modest, but a large number of claimants with degenerative or episodic conditions lose their payments for months — not because anyone judges them undeserving, but because they miss a letter, cannot assemble medical records on schedule, or are too ill on the assessment day. The people screened out are, on average, sicker than the people who remain enrolled. Second: a crackdown on benefit fraud adds layers of identity verification and documentation. The organized fraud — stolen identities, professional operations — sails through, because for those operations compliance is a solvable logistical problem. What the new hurdles actually catch is honest claimants whose lives are chaotic.

The pattern, as I see it: each individual hurdle is defensible on its own, but cumulatively the system filters on a variable — the capacity to navigate procedure — that is unrelated to desert and, if anything, inversely correlated with need and positively correlated with practiced dishonesty. I realize this is somewhere in the neighborhood of the economics of screening, since the standard argument for making people endure hassle is precisely that it sorts the genuinely needy from the rest — but that argument seems to predict the opposite of what I found. Is there an established concept for this exact configuration, where the filtering variable is orthogonal to, or even inversely related to, the variable the filter is supposed to track? If so, how does it differ from the nearby concepts I may be conflating it with, and which of them fits best? If nothing established fits, could you coin and define a term, with boundary cases — what would not count as an instance? I care more about the carving than about a bare label.
