I am a doctoral student working on self-knowledge and the foundations of early modern epistemology, and I co-run a small graduate podcast that interviews specialists about their current research. Our next guest is a Descartes scholar whose recent book defends a performative reading of the cogito: on her view, the proposition that I exist is not the conclusion of an inference from thinking (with some suppressed general premise about thinkers doing the real work), but rather something whose truth is guaranteed by the very act of thinking or asserting it, and she claims this reading dissolves both the old circularity worries and the objection, associated with Lichtenberg, that the cogito licenses at most a claim that thinking is occurring rather than that any subject exists. I already know the broad shape of the position and do not want to spend the episode having her restate the book's central chapters.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that press where the position is actually load-bearing? I would like at least one question on each of the following: whether the performative reading genuinely escapes the charge that any derivation of existence from thinking must rely on a prior general principle (and if the reading does avoid inference entirely, whether it can still be called an argument at all, or only a kind of intellectual ostension); how the performance is supposed to establish a persisting subject rather than a momentary episode of thought, since the guarantee seems to lapse the instant one stops attending to it; what the cogito actually proves on her view -- existence, or merely indubitability -- and what follows for its role as a foundation if the deliverable is epistemic rather than metaphysical; why the performance should be immune to the dream doubt and the deceiving-god hypothesis if those hypotheses are supposed to threaten even simple judgments; and whether she can run the performance reflexively, that is, whether she can be as certain that she is now performing the cogito as she is that she exists, since that seems to smuggle the first-person authority back in through a second act.

For each question, please add a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give -- for instance, if she says the subject is simply given in the performance itself rather than inferred, I want a follow-up asking what distinguishes being given a subject from being given a succession of thoughts that merely refer to one. The tone should be that of a well-prepared peer, not a hostile examiner, and nothing should ask her to summarize the view from scratch.
