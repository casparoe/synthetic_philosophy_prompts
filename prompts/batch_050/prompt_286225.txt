I work as a software engineer at a consumer app company. Our product is free and ad-supported, with an optional subscription that removes ads. At a design review last week, I objected to a proposal to add streak mechanics and variable-reward notifications to push up daily session length; my objection was that these features optimize for engagement at the expense of what users would actually choose for themselves. A senior product manager ended the discussion with a familiar slogan: "If you're not paying for the product, you are the product." As best I could tell, he meant that engagement optimization is simply our business model operating as intended, that this is no secret to anyone, and that free-tier users have effectively agreed to the arrangement.

The slogan gets repeated constantly in my industry, and I have probably repeated it myself, but I cannot say what precise claim it makes, let alone whether it does the work he needed it to do. Here is what I would like.

First, enumerate the distinct theses this one sentence could be expressing. I suspect they range from something nearly tautological (ad-supported companies earn revenue from advertisers rather than users) through a substantive claim about whose interests end up shaping a product's design, to something much stronger — for instance, that free users are literally a commodity being sold, or that designing against users' interests is unavoidable in any ad-funded product. State each candidate reading precisely enough that it could be true or false on its own.

Second, for each reading, give a verdict: is it trivial, substantive and defensible, or false? I am especially interested in the middle category — which strong-but-defensible claims about incentive alignment the slogan can genuinely stand for.

Third, some hard cases. Does the slogan distinguish ad-supported free tiers from products that are free for other reasons, such as open-core software, loss leaders, or freemium tiers that function as marketing for the paid product? Does it have anything to say about paid products that also monetize user attention and data — in other words, is the operative variable really whether you pay, or something else? And does it draw any line between ordinary engagement optimization and the specific mechanics I objected to, or does it treat them identically?

Fourth, the question I actually care about: which of these readings, if any, would support my colleague's use of the slogan — in particular his inference from "users know the deal" to "users have consented to whatever engagement design we ship"? If none of the defensible readings get him there, I would like to know precisely where the argument breaks.
