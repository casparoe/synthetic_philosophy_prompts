our work-in-progress group is doing a term on the ethics of commemoration -- statues, street names, the whole mess -- and we've realised we keep arguing about particular cases (a certain slaver philanthropist, a certain imperialist on a plinth) without any shared sense of what a theory in this area would even have to do to be adequate. So: write the spec. Not a theory, not a verdict on any actual statue -- the desiderata a theory of public commemoration must meet to be good enough to, say, guide an actual city council's policy rather than just win seminar-room arguments.

Some things I want the list to cover, though don't limit yourself to these:

- The cases it has to handle, and they're more varied than people admit: statues vs street names vs banknotes vs public holidays vs building names (do these all work the same way? a theory that only covers statues isn't a theory of commemoration); people honoured for X who were dreadful about Y; monuments erected decades after the person's death by people pursuing their own agenda; commemorations of events rather than persons; counter-memorials and deliberately self-effacing ones; genuinely good art by or about genuinely bad people
- The distinctions it can't collapse: commemorating vs celebrating vs merely recording; what a monument meant when erected vs what it means now vs what it would mean to remove it; removing vs destroying vs relocating-with-a-plaque; honouring the dead vs addressing the living
- The facts it must stay consistent with: meanings of monuments drift over time; public space is finite and zero-sum-ish; some of the communities now living with these symbols had no say whatsoever in putting them up; people rarely agree on what a given monument even says

For each desideratum give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which are genuine requirements and which are just the priors of one camp (the its-just-history camp, the monuments-are-state-speech camp, the retain-and-contextualise camp). Two further asks. First: flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- I have a hunch that respecting the historical fabric and requiring public symbols to express values the present community can actually endorse come apart somewhere, but I can't pin down where. Second: which single desideratum does the most work in knocking out positions currently on the table?

One more wrinkle to build in. Someone in the group proposed a sunset clause: every commemoration expires after, say, a century unless the community actively re-ratifies it. It's a slightly mad idea but it exposes something -- does your list of desiderata implicitly assume commemorations are meant to be permanent, and should it? Say whether a theory has to take a stand on temporality at all, or whether it can stay neutral and let the sunset people and the permanence people fight it out downstream.

Casual tone is fine, a table for the list itself is fine, no reading list or citations needed -- I can find the literature myself once I know what I'm asking of it.
