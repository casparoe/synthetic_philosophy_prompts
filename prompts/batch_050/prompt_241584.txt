I'm a novelist, and my current book hinges on a neuroscientist character who insists the brain is a computer: neurons are the hardware, memories are files in storage, personality is the software running on top. She uses this to justify the plot's big move -- copying a dying woman's mind onto a machine and treating the copy as the woman herself surviving. My writing group is split: half say this is standard sci-fi scaffolding and readers will swallow it, half say philosophers have mostly abandonned the picture and the copy would at best be a stranger with her memories. I don't need to settle uploading metaphysics for real, but I need to know what the analogy can honestly carry before I build a third act on it. Give me exactly seven numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up: what plays the role of hardware, of software, of stored files, and of whoever wrote or runs the program; where the mapping breaks in ways that actually matter for my plot (does the brain store anything file-like, is there a real hardware/software split in meat, does "running" a person even mean anything); which disanalogies don't matter and I can safely ignore; whether her conclusion survives once those are on the table; and if it doesn't, what better analogy I could put in her mouth so the scene still works.
