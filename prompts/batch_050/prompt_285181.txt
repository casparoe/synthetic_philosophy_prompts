I’m a philosophy professor who got roped into our Regional Resilience Standards Board, and next month we vote on whether to certify NEPTUNE, a deterministic flood-evacuation simulator the vendor wants to sell to three coastal counties. Act as a careful philosopher of science who also understands committees: think carefully, maybe brainstorm the decision space first, then write the short memo I can circulate, with a clear recommendation, conditions if any, and an honest paragraph giving the strongest point on the losing side. The case: NEPTUNE’s core model is fully deterministic and, given identical inputs, reruns exactly; but rainfall, soil saturation, road closures, and human compliance are chaotic enough that two runs differing by sub-measurement noise diverge after a few days. The vendor says determinism means it is not random, therefore certifiable for 14-day evacuation triggers; our skeptics say sensitive dependence makes any trigger past 48 hours theater, while the vendor replies that ensembles and data assimilation tame the butterfly.

I keep circling back to Henri Poincaré in Science and Method, where, in paraphrase rather than quotation, he treats what we call chance as the name for large effects springing from causes too small for our instruments to register, so that ignorance of initial conditions, not metaphysical indeterminism, does the practical work. Don’t cite anything or give me a bibliography; make the memo turn on whether that Poincaré point supports certification for short horizons while undercutting the 14-day guarantee, and have it define a usable policy category I’m calling an epistemic horizon budget: the board publishes the maximum lead time at which a model’s error growth still lets a reasonable official act, then forbids procurement language beyond that horizon. The comittee is split between people who worship determinism and people who worship uncertainty, so please avoid both slogans; the bottom line should be concrete, e.g. approve for 0-48 hour routing and hospital staging only if NEPTUNE runs a six-month shadow mode against observed floods and reports divergence growth, reject any contractual reliance on 10-14 day triggers, and say what evidence would make us revisit. Include the best defense of the vendor’s determinism argument before refusing its conclusion, and note, definately without sounding smug, what principle we adopt if we instead certify longer horizons on ensemble spread alone.
