Grading season is almost over and I'm prepping my phil of religion seminar for the fall, so here's a favor to ask. Act as an expert philosopher of religion who also has a solid grip on modal logic -- not a popularizer, someone who could actually referee a paper on this stuff.

We're doing two weeks on ontological arguments, and I want to end the unit with a discussion prompt for my students: is there a serious objection to Gödel's modal version that ISN'T just one of the classics recycled? Here's my list of the classics, which is what they shouldn't hand back to me:

(1) The Gaunilo-style parody objection -- swap in perfect islands, maximally great pizzas, whatever, and the same reasoning "proves" things it shouldn't.

(2) The Kantian line that existence isn't a real predicate or property, so treating necessary existence as a perfection already misunderstands what it is to say something exists.

(3) The "positivity does all the work" objection -- Gödel's axioms about positive properties (especially that positivity is closed under conjunction and that necessary existence is positive) are precisely where the theology gets smuggled in; nothing forces a neutral reader to accept them.

(4) The modal collapse worry -- on Gödel's axioms it arguably follows that everything true is necessarily true, which is a steep price to pay for the conclusion.

(5) The verdict that the whole thing is just a more rigorous Anselm, so the same old objections (especially (2)) carry over mutatis mutandis.

So: give me ONE further objection to Gödel's argument that's genuinely distinct from all five -- not a rewording, not a special case, not (3) applied to a different axiom. It can target the formal machinery, the interpretation of "positive," the move from the formal proof to anything religiously interesting, or whatever else, but it has to be a different line of attack.

Before you commit, brainstorm three or four candidate objections and for each one briefly check whether it secretly collapses into something on my list -- I expect most candidates to fail this test, and I want to see them fail. Then give me your survivor and explain what it adds that the classics don't, and whether you think it's actually any good or just novel.

Think carefully here; my students are annoyingly sharp and will smell a rehash a mile away. If you genuinely can't find one that survives, say so and tell me which of your candidates came closest and why it died.
