I work in epidemiology, and the vocabulary I actually think in is that of structural causal models: directed acyclic graphs, structural equations, the do-operator, and counterfactual queries computed by abduction, action, and prediction. I have recently been reading into the philosophical debate over whether omissions and absences can be causes, and I would like the debate restated in my vocabulary, as rigorously as the translation allows.

Take the main positions. First, the counterfactual view, on which an absence counts as a cause whenever the outcome counterfactually depends on it. Second, the view that causation is a relation between actual events or positive states of affairs, so that an absence, being nothing, can neither produce nor sustain anything. Third, the norm-sensitive views, on which the gardener's failure to water the plant caused its death while the Queen's failure to water it did not, because only the gardener's omission deviates from what was supposed to happen. Restate each position in the language of structural causal models, one by one. What does an omission even correspond to in a DAG: a variable taking a default value, the absence of an intervention, a structural equation that never fires? Does do(X = 0) treat an absence symmetrically with a presence, and if so, is that symmetry a faithful translation of the counterfactual view or a silent endorsement of it? Can the gardener-versus-Queen asymmetry be expressed at all, and if it can only be expressed by enriching the model with defaults or normality assignments, is that enrichment a translation of the norm-sensitive view or a change of subject?

Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what survives only under distortion, and what has no expression in the framework at all. In particular, consider the metaphysical constraint that causes must be actual existents. Does the structural-causal framework have the resources to state that constraint, or does it settle the question by construction? My working suspicion is that the metaphysical dispute dissolves once everything is stated formally. Tell me whether that suspicion is right, or whether my framework covertly presupposes the very metaphysics I believe it dissolves.
