I've been reading about illusionism about phenomenal consciousness for a philosophy of mind seminar (the Frankish-style view that phenomenal consciousness is a kind of introspective illusion, and that the real explanatory work is explaining why we *seem* to ourselves to have ineffable inner qualitative states). My friends and I were joking about what customer service would look like in a world where everyone had fully internalized this view, and I want to see that joke done properly.

Write a deadpan FAQ page (roughly 600-800 words) from the website of a fictional wellness company, "Quale-Free Living," that sells meditation retreats and consumer neurotech in a society where illusionism is settled common sense. The FAQ should answer questions like: "If my pain isn't really *like* anything, why does the retreat cost so much?", "Is the 'ineffable richness of experience' covered under warranty?", "My husband says my reports of inner experience are just a systematically misleading introspective model -- should I be worried?" and so on.

The humor has to come from the actual philosophy, not made-up strawmen. In particular, the FAQ should keep accidentally bumping into the real pressure points of the view: the sense that calling phenomenality an "illusion" still leaves something to explain (who or what is undergoing the illusion?), the awkwardness of talking about "seeming" without reintroducing phenomenal seemings, and the way illusionists owe us an account of why the introspective misrepresentation is so persistent and convincing. The company's chipper corporate voice should paper over these problems with breezy non-answers that a specialist will recognize as exactly the moves (and non-moves) in the literature. Stay completely deadpan -- no winking, no "gotcha" moments where the company admits the view is wrong. A reader who knows the debate should be able to tell precisely what's being ironized; a reader who doesn't should just find it a funny piece of corporate dystopia.
