So I'm prepping for a grad seminar on free will and responsibility and the assignment is to write down your "position profile" so everyone can attack it. Before I hand mine in I want to sanity-check whether my views actually fit together or whether I'm walking into an ambush. Here's where I land:

1. I think Frankfurt-style cases work. The setup where the counterfactual intervener never actually has to step in shows that you can be morally responsible without being able to do otherwise, and I don't buy the flicker-of-freedom reply -- the tiny remaining alternative (the sign the intervener was watching for) isn't the kind of alternative that could ground responsibility anyway.

2. I'm a compatibilist. Determinism, even if true, doesn't by itself threaten moral responsibility.

3. I find manipulation cases genuinely disturbing. Like in Pereboom's four-case setup, my gut says the agent in the first case, whose whole decision was secretly engineered by neuroscientists, is not responsible for what he does.

4. I think responsibility is about the actual sequence, full stop. What matters is whether the action flowed from the agent's own, suitably reasons-responsive psychology. What would or wouldnt have happened in nearby possible worlds is beside the point.

5. Coercion excuses because the coerced person couldn't have done otherwise. When someone hands over the money at gunpoint, the excuse just is the absence of alternatives.

So: which of these are actually in tension with each other, and which only look like they are? Please don't just flag every pair as equally problematic -- rank the tensions by how serious they are, and for each real one, tell me what specifically would have to give. And if there's one single revision that would clean up the most damage, tell me which one it is and what it would cost me. I genuinely don't know where the weak spot is, that's why I'm asking.
