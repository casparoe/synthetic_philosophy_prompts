I’ve been chewing over a pattern after a falling-out with a friend. He lied to me about something fairly serious, and when I think about his childhood, insecurities and the situation he was in, his behaviour seems almost inevitable. If someone asks whether he was ultimately to blame, I find myself saying no: he was just where a long chain of causes happened to come out. Yet I still feel personally wronged, I still want an apology, and I would be hurt all over again if he treated the lie like bad weather rather than something he did to me. This is not simply that I keep changing my mind. Both responses can be present at once, and each seems to answer a different question. I get the same split with manipulative politicians: one part of me sees a damaged psychological mechanism to be managed, while another cannot stop treating them as people who ought to answer for themselves.

Is there an established name for this exact split? It seems related to ambivalence, hypocrisy, weakness of will, and having an emotion that conflicts with one’s considered judgement, but none of those quite captures it. The resentment is not just an irrational feeling hanging around after the judgement has gone; it seems to contain a demand and thereby to hold the person responsible even while I deny, in my more detached moments, that the demand is ultimately warranted. Please map the phenomenon onto the nearest concepts in debates about free will, moral responsibility and the emotions, say which fits best, and mark the differences carefully. If there is no settled name, coin one and define it, including some cases it would not cover. I’d also like to know whether this split should count as irrational, or whether it might be an indispensable part of moral life rather than a failure to be consistent.
