A friend and I have been arguing for months about how to decide things when you can't trust your own memory, and I cannot pin down the rule she is using. Here are her verdicts so far. First: a volunteer is told that a coin will be flipped tonight; she will be woken tomorrow either way, and woken a second time the day after only if the coin lands tails, with no memory carried between wakings. At each waking she is offered a bet that wins $20 on heads and loses $20 on tails, and every offer she accepts counts. My friend says: decline. Second: same setup, except she is woken exactly once no matter how the coin lands. My friend says: take the bet, it's fair. Third: she herself once could not remember whether she had already left the tip at a café, and she decided to leave a few extra dollars rather than risk leaving nothing, reasoning that overtipping stings less than stiffing the server. Now I want to confront her with a new case. A driver knows his route has two exits, that he will not be able to tell them apart or remember passing the first, and that turning off at the first exit is worthless, turning off at the second is worth a lot, and missing both is worth a little. Before leaving, he settles on the plan that maximizes his expected winnings, which involves sometimes driving past an exit. But now he is sitting at an exit, unsure which one it is, and has to decide afresh. What will my friend tell him to do?
