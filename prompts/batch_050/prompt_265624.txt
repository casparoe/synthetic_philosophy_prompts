I've been chewing on a kind of case that keeps coming up in a reading group I run, and I want to know if the literature has an actual name for it, or if it's just a pile of neighboring things that don't quite cover it.

The shape of it: someone does something wrong, and at the moment they did it they genuinely couldn't have done otherwise -- not because anyone or anything was standing by to stop them, but because of who they are. The trait that produced the act is the very same thing that closes off the alternatives. Two instances to pin it down:

1. A guy raised in a millieu where backing down means disaster. Someone insults him in a bar, and walking away isn't just unappealing to him, it's psychologically inaccessible, like asking him to fly by flapping his arms. He never chose to become this person; he grew into it long before he had any say.

2. A woman who, out of deep devotion to her kids, simply cannot bring herself to do something that would harm them, even slightly, even in a situation where it might be the lesser evil. But she'd *endorse* this about herself. She doesn't want the alternative to be open to her.

Now, I know about those thought experiments where a mad scientist has implanted a chip and would have forced the choice if the person hadn't chosen it on their own, the point being you can apparently be responsible without alternatives. This isn't that, or I don't think it is. In the chip cases the thing that removes the alternatives just sits there doing nothing; it plays zero role in what actually happens. In my cases the thing removing the alternatives is doing all the work -- it IS the source of the action. So it's like the chip got internalized and started making the decisions itself. That seems like it should matter a lot, but I can't find where that difference gets discussed head-on.

I also know the move where you're responsible for being unable to do otherwise because of some earlier choice, like the drunk driver who can't drive straight at the moment of the crash but could have skipped the last four drinks. That fails here too. My guy never had an earlier lucid moment where he steered onto this path. There's no point in the story where a different choice was on the table.

And notice the two instances pull apart. In the second, the person endorses the necessity, it expresses her deepest commitments. In the first, the guy might absolutely hate that he's like this. Intuitively that changes something about blame, even though the underlying structure (couldn't do otherwise, no earlier choice, no external intervener) is identical in both.

So: does this configuration have a name of its own? And don't just hand me a bare label -- I want to know exactly how it relates to (a) the chip cases, (b) the trace-it-back-to-an-earlier-choice idea, and (c) whatever handles the endorsed-vs-resented asymmetry. If nothing established quite covers it, coin a term and be precise about the edges: does it still count if the character was *partly* chosen? What if the necessity is chemical, like an addiction, rather than a personality trait?
