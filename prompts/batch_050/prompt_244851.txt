So I'm a science journalist, mostly covering physics and space stuff, and I'm working on a piece that was supposed to be easy: the story of how Pluto got kicked out of the planet club in 2006. But the reporting has kind of broken my brain a little.

Every astronomer I talk to gives me a slightly different answer. Some say a planet has to have cleared its orbital neighborhood, some say that's arbitrary, one guy told me the whole vote was basically politics. Fine, I thought, space people are just weird. But then I remembered covering a similar fight among biologists over whether viruses count as alive, and it had the exact same shape: everyone agrees on all the facts about viruses, they just can't agree on the word. Nobody's disputing the data, they're disputing the label.

Now my editor wants me to end the piece by telling readers what a planet really is. Like, here's the actual correct definition, case closed. But I'm starting to suspect there's no such thing as the "real" definition hiding somewhere waiting to be discovered — and also that saying THAT would sound like relativist mush to readers.

So my question: is there actually a fact of the matter here that I'm failing to find, or is something else going on with how words like planet and alive work? And depending on the answer, how should I actually write this thing so I'm being honest with readers without sounding like I've given up on the truth? Practical advice welcome, I'm on a deadline lol
