so I'm about 60k words into a novel and I've hit a wall, not a plotting wall, a philosophical one, and I keep going back and forth on whether my own assumptions even fit together. quick background: the book jumps between a woman on a generation ship moving at near light speed and her twin back on earth, and a lot of the emotional weight comes from the two of them experiencing the "same" years totally differently. I read a bunch of pop physics and some actual philosophy of time while researching (there's an argument by, I think, Rietdijk and Putnam about relativity forcing you into a block universe, and it stuck with me).

Here's what I currently believe, both about the physics and about what my novel is doing:

1. Special relativity is basically right, and the relativity of simultaneity is real -- there's no frame-independent fact about which distant events are happening "now."
2. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument succeeds, so future events are just as real and fixed as past ones. The block universe is true.
3. My earth-twin makes genuinely free choices, and whether she forgives her sister is something she settles, not something that was already the case before she decided.
4. The ship-twin's slower clock means she is, in a literal sense, living "less time" than her sister, and this asymmetry is the tragedy of the book.
5. The way I order chapters -- alternating timelines, flashforwards, a chapter told backwards -- is purely a literary device and commits me to nothing about the metaphysics of time.
6. There is an objective difference between the twins' perspectives that the physics itself respects, since each has her own frame and neither is "the correct one."

Tell me honestly whether these hang together. Which pairs are in real tension, which only look like they clash, and for the real ones what's the least painful revision -- I'm more attached to the novel working emotionally than to any particular metaphysics, but I don't want the book to rest on an incoherent picture either. Rank the tensions by how bad they actually are rather than treating them all as fatal. And say how confident you are in each verdict, and flag the one claim in your answer you're least sure about, since I'm going to be making actual revisions off this.
