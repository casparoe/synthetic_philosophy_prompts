My best friend is getting married next month and I'm giving the toast. Here's the wrinkle: he and his fiancee met on a silent retreat and are both sincere adherents of a view they call tranquilism -- roughly, the idea that nothing is ever good in itself, and that states of affairs matter only insofar as they involve the absence of craving and suffering. A blissfully satisfied person, on their view, adds nothing to the world that a dreamless rock doesn't already. So the usual toast moves -- "may your life together be full of joy," "love is the greatest good" -- are exactly the things they'd consider false. The guests are mostly family who will hear anything vaguely Buddhist-sounding and nod along, plus four or five philosophy friends who will notice every hedge. Write a four-minute toast that genuinely celebrates this specific marriage in terms the couple would actually endorse, without lecturing the room on axiology and without making the aunties think the groom's friends find the whole thing bleak.
