He'll drive you to the city only if he's confident you'll pay him $100 once you're safely there. The problem is that once you're safe, a rational person has no reason to pay — the ride already happened, so paying is just throwing away money. So a purely rational person never gets rescued, and the chump with a sense of honor survives. Parfit's point is that this is really a case where morality wins out over self-interest: paying is what you owe the guy, and the lesson is that being moral beats being rational. He also took it to basically refute standard rational choice theory — game theory says everyone defects, and the hitchhiker shows defecting gets you killed, so the theory is self-defeating and can't explain why people actually cooperate."
