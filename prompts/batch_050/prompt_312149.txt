So I'm in a reading group on philosophy of biology and we just finished the famous Gould and Lewontin paper about the spandrels of San Marco, where they argue that a lot of evolutionary "explanations" are just-so stories that assume every trait is an adaptation built by selection for its current function. I get the critique in broad strokes, but here's where I'm stuck: if we take the critique seriously, what would a *good* evolutionary explanation of a trait actually have to look like? Everyone seems to agree the naive Panglossian version is dead, but nobody in my group could say what the standard of adequacy actually is.

Write me the desiderata for an adequate explanation of why a trait exists in a population -- what it has to deliver to count as a real explanation rather than a plausible narrative. I'm thinking of things like: does it have to establish that the trait was actually selected *for* its current role rather than being a byproduct of something else, and how would it distinguish those? Does it have to rule out drift, developmental constraint, phylogenetic inertia, and exaptation before claiming adaptation, or is it enough to show selection *could* have done it? Does it need to account for the trait's actual variation and heritability, or can it stay at the level of function? What evidence would count as confirming it -- comparative data, optimality models, experiments -- and is correlational fit between trait and environment ever sufficient on its own? For each requirement, give me the consideration or case behind it (e.g. things like the human chin, the redness of blood, male nipples as cases where the adaptationist reflex fails, or cases where byproduct explanations turned out right).

And the part I most want: are any pairs of these desiderata in tension or jointly unsatisfiable in practice, like demanding both strong confirmatory evidence and applicability to traits we can only study comparatively? If the list itself reveals that "adequate explanation" has to be a pluralist notion, say so. Briefly at the end you can note how a strict adaptationist program and a Gould-style pluralist program each fare against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves. Please write it as continuous prose, like a letter -- no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, even though I know a list format would be tempting here.
