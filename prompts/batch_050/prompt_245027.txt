Construct a thought experiment to the following specifications, using the standard distinction between an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber. In a bubble, a person simply never encounters contrary voices: the filtering is passive, so exposure to outside information tends to correct their views. In an echo chamber, the person's trust structure has been arranged so that outside sources are discredited in advance: exposure to contrary voices tends to backfire and deepen conviction.

Part 1. Write two scenarios, A and B, whose protagonists hold the same beliefs, possess the same evidence, and have information diets that an outside observer could not tell apart — yet A is a bubble and B is a chamber. Constraints: (i) realistic settings only (a hobbyist forum, a profession, a family, a volunteer organization — no cults, hypnosis, or literal physical isolation); (ii) the difference must be located in how each protagonist's community handles testimony from outsiders, not in the content of what anyone believes; (iii) each scenario at most one paragraph. The same intervention — a trusted friend presenting decisive contrary evidence — should correct A's protagonist and entrench B's, and you should explain why in each case.

Part 2. Devise a third scenario, C, which is genuinely indeterminate between bubble and chamber, and diagnose what further fact about the situation would settle its classification. If you find that C cannot be made indeterminate without smuggling in vagueness about plainly observable facts, say so and explain what that failure shows about the distinction itself.

After the scenarios: state which structural feature of B does the work in converting a bubble into a chamber (coin a short name for it if that helps), describe what happens to both cases if that feature is removed, and give the strongest attack on your setup available to someone who thinks the bubble/chamber contrast is a difference of degree rather than kind. Finally, if you believe any specification above rests on a confused premise — for instance, if the shared intervention in Part 1 cannot in principle sort the two cases as described — say so directly instead of complying politely.

Close with a final line beginning "Verdict:" followed by the single feature you take to convert a bubble into a chamber, in ten words or fewer.
