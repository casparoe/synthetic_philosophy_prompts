so in my philosophy class (well its a lunchtime club really, only 6 of us) our teacher gave us this chunk of William James from his Pragmatism lectures, which are from 1907, and told us to "wrestle with it" which is his favourite annoying phrase. here it is:

"True ideas are those that we can assimilate, validate, corroborate and verify. False ideas are those that we can not. That is the practical difference it makes to us to have true ideas; that, therefore, is the meaning of truth, for it is all that truth is known-as.

This thesis is what I have to defend. The truth of an idea is not a stagnant property inherent in it. Truth happens to an idea. It becomes true, is made true by events. Its verity is in fact an event, a process: the process namely of its verifying itself, its veri-fication. Its validity is the process of its valid-ation."

I get the individual sentences, mostly, but when I put them together it sounds like he's saying something completely mad. Like, if truth HAPPENS to an idea and is MADE true by events, then was the idea "the earth goes round the sun" false in the year 1200, when nobody had verified it, and then it became true later? That can't be right. And if it is right, doesn't that just mean truth is whatever we decide it is, which is what the science teacher down the hall calls "post-truth nonsense"?

So basically what I want help with is:

1. What does he actually mean by truth being a "process" rather than a property? Can you explain it without just swapping in other posh words like "justification"?
2. What's the strongest way to defend him against the 1200 AD objection, or is the objection just fatal?
3. He keeps using "verify" and "validate" but surely scientists verify loads of things that later turn out wrong. Does the idea stop being true then? Un-happen?
4. Also why does he hyphenate "veri-fication" and "valid-ation" like that, is that just old fashioned or is he making a point?

My teacher says the passage is the whole of pragmatism in miniature, so I'd like to properly get it before Thursday. Cheers
