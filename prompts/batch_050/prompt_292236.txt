Last week I spent an entire evening arguing with a friend about game theory and cooperation, and she ended the night by claiming my views are just a pile of pop-science slogans that don't actually cohere. We have a rematch scheduled, so I want to stress-test my position beforehand. Here is roughly what I found myself defending:

(a) In a genuinely one-shot, anonymous prisoner's dilemma against a stranger, defection is the only rational move; dominance reasoning settles the matter and there is nothing more to say.

(b) If I had to play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma against an exact psychological copy of myself, I should cooperate, because I can be confident the copy will reason as I do and arrive wherever I arrive.

(c) Backward induction shows that cooperation unravels in any iterated prisoner's dilemma whose length is finite and known to both players in advance.

(d) Axelrod's tournaments show that in real life, nice, forgiving, retaliatory strategies like tit-for-tat outperform exploitative ones, so people in ongoing relationships should default to cooperation.

(e) Zero-determinant strategies in the iterated prisoner's dilemma — Press and Dyson's extortionate strategies that unilaterally fix the opponent's payoff — show that a sufficiently clever player can lock in an exploitative advantage, which substantially undercuts the optimistic lesson of Axelrod's tournaments.

(f) Since reciprocity-based cooperation is what natural selection installed in us, cooperating in mixed-motive situations is thereby rational, not merely natural.

My friend says at least three of these can't be jointly held, but she wouldn't say which. Please audit the set: tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, ranked by severity. For each genuine tension, explain what commits me to the contradiction and what the cheapest fix would be. For each merely apparent tension, say what dissolves it. Finally, if I could retract or soften exactly one commitment, tell me which single revision would restore the most coherence, and what it would cost me in the argument with my friend.
