I'm in a medieval philosophy course and we just covered Boethius on divine foreknowledge in Book V of the Consolation. My rough understanding is that God isn't supposed to be an observer located earlier in time who makes the future fixed. Rather, from eternity, God sees all times in something like an everlasting present, so knowing what I will do no more causes me to do it than seeing someone drink coffee causes them to drink it. I'm honestly not sure whether that solves the problem or just moves it around, but let's assume Boethius thinks it works.

For my final essay I want to apply his view to a modern problem instead of just summarizing the text. Imagine a government develops an AI system that can predict, from someone's digital records, that there is a 96% chance they will commit a violent crime within the next year. The system doesn't claim timeless knowledge; it is just making an extremely reliable statistical inference. Officials consider four uses: offering the person voluntary counseling, requiring monitoring before any offense, raising their insurance premium, and sentencing them in advance if the prediction crosses 99%. What would Boethius likely say about each policy? Would he think a highly accurate prediction threatens free will less than divine foreknowledge, more than it, or in a completely different way? How would his distinction between different kinds of necessity apply here, and would it matter that publicizing a prediction can itself change what people later do?

Please don't just say that because Boethius was a Christian philosopher he would obviously accept or reject the system. Give the strongest Boethian analysis in his own conceptual vocabulary, while noting where the extrapolation is speculative. I'm especially interested in whether he would locate the moral problem in prediction itself or only in acting on predictions, and whether someone punished in advance could still be morally responsible if they later freely chose to commit the predicted crime. If you think his eternity solution becomes implausible once we introduce probability, feedback loops, or modern physics, explain why rather than treating it as obviously sound.
