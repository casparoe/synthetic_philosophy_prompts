Since retiring from a forty-year career in experimental chemistry — most of it spent inferring molecular structures no one will ever see from spectra no one else wanted to read — I have been reading about AI alignment, and I have tried to write down where I have landed on the question of whose values an aligned system should serve. Before I inflict this on my reading group, I would like to know whether the position hangs together.

Here is what I currently believe. First, an aligned AI ought to be neutral among its users' conceptions of the good life: it should help each person pursue their own values and never steer anyone toward a life the designers happen to admire. Second, what people say they value is often not what they really value — preferences get warped by misinformation, advertising, peer pressure, and adaptation to bad circumstances — so the right target is what a person would value on full information and calm reflection, not their avowed preferences. Third, whatever values the system serves must be learnable from intersubjectively checkable evidence — behavior, choices, speech — because that is the only basis on which competing claims about a person's values could ever be adjudicated; I am not willing to let alignment float free of the data. Fourth, when values conflict across people and have to be aggregated, nobody's values count for more than anybody else's. Fifth, some values are simply out of bounds: a person whose settled, fully reflective commitment is to cruelty should not have it served, because some facts about suffering obtain whether or not anyone endorses them. Sixth, a case I feel confident about: a religious community whose members, informed and calm, jointly want the system to help organize their common life around their shared ideal should be accommodated, and I take this to be no exception to my neutrality.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Please order the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I would rather you identified two real problems and explained why the rest are apparent than treated every pair as equally suspect. And please write your answer as continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables.
