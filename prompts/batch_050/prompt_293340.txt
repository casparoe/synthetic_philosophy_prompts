Last night a friend and I spent four hours arguing about whether probability can mean anything at all in the many-worlds interpretation: if every outcome of a measurement happens in some branch, what can it possibly mean to say one outcome is more likely than another? He kept insisting the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic derivation settles this by showing rational agents must weight branches by the Born rule; I kept pressing that this smuggles probability in through the axioms, and that the self-locating uncertainty approach (Sebens and Carroll) has its own problem with the epistemic separability principle. Next week I'm interviewing an unnamed but prominent defender of the decision-theoretic program for my philosophy podcast. Draft eight to ten interview questions, in a deliberate sequence, that force her to take definite positions rather than survey the landscape: at least one on the incoherence objection (what uncertainty could even attach to before branching), at least one on whether her rationality axioms are smuggling in the Born weights, at least one on how she responds to the claim that branch counting is as rational as Born weighting, and at least one on what the self-locating alternative gets right that her view cannot accommodate. For each question, give the follow-up keyed to the answer she will most likely give, and indicate which question in the sequence you think is most likely to make her concede ground -- commit to that judgment and defend it.
