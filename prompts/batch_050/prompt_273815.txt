I run a monthly workshop for an AI alignment reading group — 9 graduate students, 90 minutes. Most have read about AI safety via debate but treat it as a purely theoretical proposal. Design a structured activity that lets them feel both why debate might work as an oversight scheme and exactly where it breaks. The core setup I want: a genuine asymmetry of expertise, with two participants cast as debaters assigned to argue opposite sides of a question (one side true, one false) that the rest of the group, cast as judges, cannot check directly, and a protocol in which the judges may drill into any sub-claim of the debaters' arguments, recursively, until they reach something they actually can evaluate — at which point they render a verdict and a debater wins or loses. The activity should be arranged so the mechanism that is supposed to make honesty the winning strategy shows up in play rather than in explanation: lies have to stay consistent under recursive questioning, so the honest debater can always steer toward checkable ground, while the dishonest one is pushed toward either contradiction or an unverifiable tangle. Choose topics that let a small group genuinely play expert and non-expert (this is harder than it sounds — say what makes a topic usable and give several), specify rules, win conditions, and how to run escalating rounds including one designed to surface the known failure mode, where the dishonest debater wins by keeping every branch too complex for the judges to resolve. Include a debrief mapping moments in play onto the real proposal, note what will predictably go wrong the first time, and be honest about which feature of the actual scheme — debaters more capable than the judge, not merely better informed — no classroom version can stage.
