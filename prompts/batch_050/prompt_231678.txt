I cover science and ideas for a general-audience outlet, and for my newsletter I want a "which grand narrative of human history do you actually believe?" quiz. The leaves should be the big rivals: a Diamond-style geographic determinism, a Harari-style story about shared fictions and the cognitive revolution, a Graeber-and-Wengrow-style view where early people were politically self-aware and farming/hierarchy were traps we stumbled into rather than stages we climbed, a Hobbesian progress narrative, a Rousseauian fall-from-foraging narrative, and a materialist stages-of-production story. Write 8-10 questions, each built around a concrete claim or case -- forager work hours, Çatalhöyük, why farming spread even where it made people shorter and sicker, wether big irrigation explains early states, that kind of thing -- with answer options phrased so readers respond to the claim, not to the label, since the whole point is to sort people by what they think happened rather than by which book they enjoyed. Then give a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the six positions, flag any pair of leaves your questions can't really separate, and if some coherent answer pattern lands nowhere, say in a sentence what that unnamed view would be.
