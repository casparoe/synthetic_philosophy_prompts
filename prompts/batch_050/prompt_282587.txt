Is there a name for this? I work in picture framing and sometimes help with minor restoration, and I keep running into the same weird pattern with how people value things.

A couple years back a local collector had a landscape everyone believed was by a fairly well-known 19th century painter. Then some paperwork surfaced showing it was actually painted in the 1950s by a copyist. Nothing about the canvas changed — same paint, same frame, same wall it hung on. But it went from the centerpiece of his collection to basically unsellable overnight, and he said it felt like a different object now, which sounds irrational but honestly I got it.

Here's the part I can't square. I also see the opposite pattern all the time: objects where almost nothing original is left. A violin that's had its neck, varnish, and big chunks of the body replaced still sells as "the real one" for a fortune. A chapel that's been rebuilt stone by stone over the centuries is still THE chapel, people still make pilgrimages to it, nobody feels cheated. So in one case an object with all its original material loses everything when the backstory changes, and in the other an object with barely any original material keeps everything as long as the backstory runs in an unbroken line. It seems like what people actually care about is some invisible chain connecting the thing to its maker or its moment, not what's physically in front of them.

Does this have an established name? I don't think its just snobbery or investment logic, because collectors will pay far more for the genuine article even in cases where nobody could ever tell the two apart. And I'm not sure it's the same as the general idea that originals have a special aura or vibe, because the violin case shows people happily accepting a mostly-new object as authentic if the chain of custody is continuous. If there's a term, I also want to know how it handles edge cases: an authorized second cast from the sculptor's own mold, or a digital photo where the "original" file is bit-for-bit identical to every copy.

Please answer as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, with no intro or conclusion: the closest established term or terms, how the pattern I'm describing differs from each of them, and how the best candidate handles the edge cases.
