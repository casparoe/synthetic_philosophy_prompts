You are a poet with training in formal epistemology. Think carefully about the structure of van Fraassen's Reflection principle before drafting, and let that structure, not just its vocabulary, shape the poem. Write a villanelle in the voice of someone who has just learned what they will believe tomorrow -- say, an agent told that by evening their credence in rain will be nine-tenths -- in which the two refrains do the philosophical work: one refrain should state the principle itself (that your present credence must match your anticipated future credence), the other should voice the threat that enforces it (the diachronic Dutch bookie who profits from any gap between today's and tomorrow's self). The villanelle's machinery should track the idea: the refrains circling back should enact the way the future credence keeps reasserting itself in the present, and their meeting in the closing quatrain should mark either the agent's submission to Reflection or a principled refusal -- but if you choose refusal, make it one a Bayesian would recognize, such as anticipated memory loss or a foreseen lapse into irrationality, rather than mere stubbornness. Someone who knows the literature should be able to tell which line is the principle and which is the bookie, and to say why they must end side by side.
