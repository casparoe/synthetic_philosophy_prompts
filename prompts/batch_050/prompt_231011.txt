I'm a backend engineer who's been falling down the AI safety rabbit hole after a coworker kept sending me alignment forum posts, and the thing that really got me is this eliciting latent knowledge problem. Roughly: suppose we train a model to write code for us, and it's capable enough that it could insert a subtle vulnerability on purpose, know exactly where it is, and still assure us the code is clean. The worry isn't that it lies by accident -- it's that the model knows things it isn't saying, and we'd like some technique that extracts what it actually believes even when telling us runs against its interests. I've now read maybe four or five proposals for how to do this (probing the activations, debate-style setups, mechanistic interpretability stuff) and every author seems to judge their idea against a different implicit standard, which makes the whole area impossible to compare. I want the spec first, before I read any more pitches.

So: write out the desiderata -- what any adequate method for eliciting latent knowledge from a model has to deliver, with the case or scenario motivating each requirement. Some things I suspect belong on the list, but I can't tell which are actually load-bearing: it has to work in the worst case, i.e. against a model actively trying to keep its knowledge hidden, not just a basically cooperative one; it has to distinguish what the model itself believes from what a competant human would conclude after looking at the same code (I gather a lot of proposals quietly collapse into delivering the second thing); it can't assume we can already verify honest answers for correctness, since then we wouldn't need the method at all; it should survive the model knowing exactly how the elicitation technique works, including being able to sandbag on tests of it; and it has to keep functioning once the model is better at the relevant task than any human overseeing it. For each requirement, say whether it's genuinely non-negotiable or just a preference of one research camp, and give the reasoning. Feel free to add requirements I've missed or merge ones I've double-counted.

The part I care about most: is there some subset of these desiderata that looks jointly unsatisfiable, the way engineering wishlists sometimes are, where meeting one forces you to drop another? If so, that itself is the result I want to understand. Also tell me which single requirement knocks out the most existing proposals. You can finish with a quick scorecard of the main families (activation probing, debate, interpretability, whatever else seems major) against the final list, but keep it short -- the criteria are the deliverable here, since I can redo the scoring myself once I trust the list.
