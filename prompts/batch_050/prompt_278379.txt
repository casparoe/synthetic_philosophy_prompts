I'm a robotics researcher working on prosthetic hand control, and I've been reading Merleau-Ponty's Phenomenology of Perception on the side because a colleague keeps telling me that our field reinvented his ideas badly. I now have a rough grip on his core notions -- the lived body as opposed to the body as an object of science, the body schema as a pre-reflective sense of where one's limbs are and what they can do, motor intentionality as a way of being directed at tasks and situations rather than at representations of them, and his contrast between the Cartesian stance of I think and the bodily stance of I can. What I can't tell is how much of this is genuinely anticipating things we formalized decades later and how much is a different kind of claim altogether that only sounds similar.

So here is what I want. Translate Merleau-Ponty's position on the lived body and motor intentionality into the vocabulary I work in every day: state estimation, forward and inverse models, the distinction between a controller's model of the plant and the plant itself, feedback versus feedforward control, active sensing, learned motor synergies, and the way a grasp policy is parameterized by task and object geometry rather than by a full world-model. Go claim by claim rather than giving me a general gloss, and keep the translation honest -- don't quietly import our assumption that the body is a physical system to be estimated and then call that Merleau-Ponty's view, since his whole point seems to be that the lived body is not an object in the subject's world at all. The part I care most about is the residue report: which of his claims map cleanly onto something like a body schema implemented as an internal state estimator, which can only be translated by distorting them (I'm guessing the pre-reflective character of motor intentionality goes here, but tell me), and which have no expression in control-theoretic vocabulary at all. For that last category, give me your judgment on whether the residue shows that my framework is missing something real -- something a prosthesis user might actually notice in how a device fails to feel like theirs -- or whether it shows that part of Merleau-Ponty's account is doing less work than phenomenologists assume.
