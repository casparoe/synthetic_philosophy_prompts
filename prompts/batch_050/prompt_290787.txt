I am preparing a capstone handout for a graduate seminar on artificial intelligence and commonsense reasoning. Before the seminar surveys particular formalisms, I want an adequacy specification for what would count as solving the frame problem for an autonomous agent operating in a partly unpredictable environment—not merely for a planner working in a closed toy world.

Write the desiderata such a solution must meet, using the following cases as anchors:

1. **Paint case:** A robot carries a sealed paint tin from the kitchen to the bathroom. The tin changes location, and whatever is inside it moves with it, but the color of the kitchen floor, the state of the refrigerator, and countless other facts normally remain unchanged. If the robot drops the tin after its lid has been opened, paint may spill; the tin may then be empty and the floor wet, but only under the right causal conditions.
2. **Kettle case:** The robot switches on an electric kettle and is then interrupted. After a delay, the water ordinarily boils, provided the kettle remains plugged in and contains water. The doorbell may ring, a cat may knock something over, or another agent may unplug the kettle while the robot is reasoning. The robot must act before it can enumerate every proposition that might have changed or remained the same.

Your specification should distinguish at least four issues that are often run together: representing non-change without a frame axiom for every action–fluent pair; inferring which facts change without checking every possibility; deriving indirect effects of actions without letting effects propagate indiscriminately; and deciding which possibilities a bounded agent ought to consider at all. Explain how the frame problem proper differs from the qualification problem, the ramification problem, and the broader problem of relevance, while also discussing whether a practically adequate solution can keep these issues completely separate.

For each desideratum, provide:

- the requirement, stated precisely;
- the case or transition that motivates it;
- the characteristic failure produced when it is violated;
- whether it is non-negotiable for the stated purpose or instead a preference associated with a particular kind of solution.

Include desiderata concerning persistence, causal ramifications, exogenous events, delayed effects, incomplete knowledge, exceptions and qualifications, computational tractability, scalability, and the extent to which relevance may be hand-coded. Also address whether a solution must be cognitively plausible, or whether engineering adequacy is sufficient.

The most important part is the analysis of tensions within the list. Identify any sets of desiderata that appear difficult or impossible to satisfy jointly—for example, open-world correctness, real-time tractability, and the complete avoidance of domain-specific relevance assumptions. Where a trade-off is genuine, explain what must be given up on each side rather than presenting the conflict as a temporary technical limitation. Say which single desideratum would exclude the largest number of familiar approaches.

Conclude with a compact assessment of four broad strategies against your specification: STRIPS-style planning under a closed-world assumption, monotonic action calculi with explicitly engineered persistence axioms, nonmonotonic or default-based approaches, and approaches built around explicit causal structure. Do not turn this conclusion into a numerical league table; indicate only where each strategy satisfies a requirement by narrowing the problem and where it addresses the problem itself. No bibliography or citations are needed.
