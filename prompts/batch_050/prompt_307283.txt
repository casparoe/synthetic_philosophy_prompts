For the past six years my business partner Mara and I have run a small company that licenses a water-purification technology she invented. Last fall she left on a year-long ocean research expedition, genuinely unreachable, and before departing she signed paperwork giving me sole authority to act for the company and asked me, cheerfully and unhelpfully, to just do what she would do. Two weeks ago a large industrial firm offered to buy the company outright for a sum that would make us both wealthy. The offer expires in six weeks; there is no way to consult her, and the default, if I do nothing, is that the offer lapses and we carry on as before.

My problem is that I can construct two internally coherent pictures of what Mara would want, both drawn from real things she has said over the years. On one picture, she has repeatedly talked about cashing out someday to fund a clean-water nonprofit, and this offer would let her do that on a grand scale. On the other picture, she has called the company her life's work, has turned down acquisition interest before, and once said she never wants to watch her invention become a neglected patent in some conglomerate's portfolio. Depending on which picture I weight more heavily, I get opposite answers. I could also pursue intermediate options -- a partial sale, an earn-out that keeps her involved, a counteroffer with contractual guarantees about the technology's continued deployment -- though each of those also looks different under the two pictures.

What I'm looking for is not a prediction about which Mara is the real one, since I doubt there is a fact of the matter I could discover from here. Rather: is there a principled decision procedure for cases like this? My instinct is that the right test is to look for a course of action that every plausible version of Mara's values would rank above simply letting the offer lapse, and to prefer such an option even over one that scores higher on my best-guess average of her values. Is that instinct sound? Does it smuggle in an unjustified privilege for inaction, given that "do nothing" is itself a choice she might reject under every picture? And what should I do if no option, including inaction, is unanimously preferred to the default -- or if the option that all versions of her would endorse requires me to act before the deadline on the basis of a default I am only assuming?
