I've been going back and forth on chain-of-thought faithfulness lately and I've realised I don't actually know what I'd want a faithfulness audit to do before judging any particular one. So: write the spec. What would a method for checking whether a model's written reasoning reflects what actually drove its answer have to deliver, requirement by requirement, with the case motivating each?

The ones I can already see: it has to catch the sneaky-hint case, where you plant a cue in the prompt, the model clearly uses it, but its reasoning never mentions the cue and dresses the answer up as derived. It has to say something about silent computation, where the answer is basically settled before or independent of the tokens and the visible reasoning is downstream theatre. It has to handle the steganography-flavoured failure where the tokens genuinely do drive the answer but are also smuggling extra payload a human reader can't see. And it can't just be "does the answer survive paraphrasing the reasoning", because that's robustness, not faithfulness, and conflating them is half the confusion in this literature.

But I'm sure some of my requirements are prejudices and I'm missing real ones. Give me the desiderata, mark which are negotiable and which aren't, flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied (I've got a feeling that "works on black-box API models" and "can actually detect silent computation" fight each other), and say which single desideratum knocks out the most existing methods. Then briefly score the main families of approach — behavioural perturbation tests, looking-inside-the-weights mechanistic checks, and training models toward verbal honesty — against the list, but keep the scoring short; the criteria are the interesting bit.
