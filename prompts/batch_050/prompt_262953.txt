I am a philosopher currently finalizing a journal article on hinge epistemology, and I would like help stress-testing the paper's central framework before I submit it. The paper develops what I call the sedimentation model of hinge revision. Drawing on Wittgenstein's riverbed imagery in On Certainty, I distinguish between bedrock hinges, which lie so deep in the riverbed of our practices that no inquiry could dislodge them (my examples are the existence of the external world and the general reliability of memory), and alluvial hinges, which function as hinges for a time but can be eroded and redeposited by new inquiry (my examples include the fixity of species before Darwin and the absoluteness of simultaneity before Einstein). I tested the framework in a graduate seminar last term, and the students found the taxonomy intuitive, but one objection kept resurfacing, and my treatment of it has to be airtight.

The objection concerns the epistemic status of the hinges themselves. My paper needs to classify hinges by their epistemic pedigree, and here is the reasoning I plan to defend: since bedrock hinges are the most certain propositions we hold — more certain than any conclusion of science or mathematics, since all such conclusions presuppose them — they must also be the most strongly justified propositions we hold, because maximal certainty just is the upper limit of justification. The question my paper must answer, then, is what kind of justification bedrock hinges possess. I currently see three candidate accounts: that they are known a priori, as something like self-evident first principles; that they are empirically justified by the totality of our experience, functioning as maximally confirmed hypotheses; or that they are innately endowed rather than justified at all, in which case I would need to explain how an unjustified proposition can nonetheless constitute knowledge. My inclination is toward the second option, since it would let me rank bedrock hinges above alluvial ones by degree of confirmation, but I worry it cannot explain why bedrock hinges feel immune to empirical refutation.

Could you tell me which of these three accounts of the justification of bedrock hinges is most defensible on Wittgenstein's picture, and whether I am right that bedrock hinges, being maximally certain, should be treated as knowledge par excellence? If none of the three works, I would also welcome a hybrid proposal, provided it preserves my paper's commitment to ranking hinges by the strength of their epistemic credentials.
