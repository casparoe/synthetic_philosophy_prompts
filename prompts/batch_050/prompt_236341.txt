In three weeks I'm giving a colloquium paper defending a claim I'm now worried is too strong, and I need hostile prep. The claim: acausal threats (a hypothetical superintelligence that blackmails you into helping bring it about, without ever needing to exist -- basilisk-style setups) can only get traction against agents who have adopted what I call a surrogate goal. By that I mean caring about an outcome partly for what achieving it would prove or signal about you -- being the kind of agent who can't be bullied, keeping your commitments legible to other reasoners -- rather than caring about the outcome for its own sake. My argument is that a "pure" instrumental agent with some terminal goal (maximize paperclips, minimize suffering, whatever) is simply immune, because the threat doesn't causally change anything it values; the threat only bites once the agent has smuggled in a surrogate goal like "don't be the sort of agent who caves to threats." So vulnerability to acausal blackmail is self-inflicted, a consequence of bad goal architecture, not a deep fact about rationality.
