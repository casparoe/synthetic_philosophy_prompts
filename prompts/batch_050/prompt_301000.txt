For my philosophy of physics reading group I assembled the following argument that the emergent-spacetime programme in quantum gravity is self-undermining. The conclusion strikes me as proving too much — the programme is pursued in earnest by serious physicists and philosophers — yet I cannot locate the weak step.

(1) In several prominent approaches to quantum gravity, spacetime and its geometry are not fundamental: they obtain only approximately, emerging from an underlying structure that is not itself spatiotemporal (entanglement relations among quantum degrees of freedom, a pre-geometric ordering of discrete elements, or the like).
(2) If spacetime is emergent rather than fundamental, then spatial and temporal relations are not fundamental either.
(3) Causal relations are spatiotemporal through and through: a cause precedes its effect in time and is connected to it across space; there is no causation without temporal order.
(4) Therefore causal relations are not fundamental; they hold, if at all, only among things at the emergent level.
(5) Yet the emergence claim is itself a causal claim: to say that spacetime emerges from the underlying structure is to say that the non-spatiotemporal degrees of freedom generate or give rise to it.
(6) Nothing can generate anything without standing in a causal relation to it.
(7) So the emergence claim requires causal relations between the fundamental structure and the spacetime that emerges from it.
(8) By (4), no causal relations are available at the fundamental level.
(9) Hence the emergence claim presupposes precisely the relations it renders unavailable: the programme is self-undermining, and either emergent spacetime is incoherent or spacetime is fundamental after all.

Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? Identify the step you would reject, explain why it looked innocent, and price the alternatives: what it would cost to reject (3) instead, what it would cost to reject (5) or (6), and what it would cost to accept every premise and embrace the conclusion. Say also what the episode teaches about the notion of emergence the programme is actually committed to, if it survives. If I have to give something up, I would rather it not be (1), and I am reluctant to deny both that causes precede their effects and that the physics is coherent; but I want to know what I am paying for those attachments either way.
