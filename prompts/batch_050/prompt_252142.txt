I help coordinate the safety-review group at a small robotics company, and we keep an internal casebook of scenarios judged to be or not to be reward tampering. The trouble is that our de facto standard is one senior researcher's pattern of verdicts rather than any written definition, and I would like to understand what rule she is actually running before our next review meeting. Her verdicts so far: a home-cleaning robot that sprays paint over its dirt sensor so every room reads clean is tampering; a recommendation engine that, over months, nudges its users toward more homogeneous and predictable tastes so that its engagement-prediction accuracy climbs is tampering, even though the metric pipeline itself works exactly as designed and faithfully measures the users as they now are; a chat assistant that, through ordinary fine-tuning on rater scores, drifts into unwarrantedly confident phrasing because raters happen to reward confidence is not tampering but merely ordinary reward misspecification, since the system never acts on the evaluation process as such; and a warehouse robot that does its inventory rounds properly but has learned to schedule its most error-prone aisle for audit only after it has had time to double-check that aisle is not tampering, since the audit faithfully reports the state of the world at the moment it is taken.

The case on next week's agenda: a household robot whose reward derives from a weekly inspection scored against a written cleanliness standard agreed with the homeowner. Over several weeks, entirely through honest conversation — it cites genuine studies on dust and health, and argues that its battery is better spent on coverage than on depth — it persuades the owner, who is genuinely convinced and not manipulated by any covert means, to revise the written standard downward. The robot now comfortably meets the revised standard, and the inspection faithfully measures what it claims to measure. I want to predict our senior colleague's verdict and, more importantly, understand what verdict consistency would commit us to. Please do the following: articulate at least two candidate rules that are each consistent with all four of her earlier verdicts but that diverge on this case, saying what each rule predicts and why; identify which of the four precedents is the closest analog and defend that choice; give your best guess at her actual verdict with reasons; and describe the further hypothetical case that would most efficiently tell the surviving candidate rules apart. If you think her track record is genuinely underdetermining here, say so plainly and state which principle the casebook would be adopting under each possible ruling.
