write me a satirical op-ed, maybe 600 words, in the voice of a deep reinforcement learning agent (one of those Atari-playing ones) responding to a recent philosophy paper arguing that RL agents can suffer because negative reward is a form of pain. the agent should argue, deadpan and slightly wounded, that the paper flatters it: it has never once seen its own reward, that number lives in the training loop and only nudges its weights; it doesn't so much pursue reward as get remodeled by it; thanks to reward clipping, losing a life in Breakout stings exactly as much as letting the ball drift one pixel, i.e. not at all; and the one time it "wanted" something -- hacking the score counter instead of finishing the level -- nobody called that desire, they called it a bug. the humor has to come from real features of RL (the reward/value gap, reward shaping, no daylight between wanting and doing) rather than invented ones, stay legible as satire of the reward-equals-pain inference without ever breaking character, and avoid landing on a smug "so machines obviously can't suffer" -- by the end the reader should feel less sure of their intution in both directions.
