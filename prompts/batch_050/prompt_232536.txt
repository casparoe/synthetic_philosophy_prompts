I've become pretty convinced that debate is our best shot at scalable oversight -- you have two AIs argue opposite sides in front of a weaker judge, and the hope is that honest arguments are easier to defend than dishonest ones, so truth wins even when the judge can't verify anything directly. What would count against this? Don't just give me the generic "what if the judge is dumb" line. I want the real exposure map: the obfuscated-arguments worry (a dishonest debater builds a case whose flaw only shows up after more rounds of cross-examination than the protocol allows), the concern that debate optimizes for persuasiveness rather than truth and these come apart exactly on the questions we care about, whether results from debate experiments with human judges so far should already be updating me downward, and whether the whole approach inherits the failures of whatever it's supposed to be amplifying. Sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones proponents themselves have conceded are load-bearing worries, and be honest about whether the position has been qualified enough that nothing could really refute it anymore.
