The assignment was: Frankfurt distinguishes lying from bullshit by the speaker's relation to the truth -- the liar cares about the truth enough to hide it, the bullshitter is indifferent to it. Apply this taxonomy to LLM hallucinations. When a language model confidently outputs something false, is that best understood as lying, as bullshit, as plain error, or as none of these? Defend your answer by engaging with what each category requires, in 300 words or so.

Here's the student's answer:

Frankfurt's framework gives us a clean three-way split. The liar knows the truth and deliberately conceals it. The bullshitter says false things without knowing or caring whether they're false -- what makes bullshit distinct from lying is the bullshitter's ignorance, not any deception, since you can only deceive someone about something you yourself believe. Error is just being wrong, full stop. Applying this: an LLM has no beliefs and no intentions, so it can't lie, because lying requires knowing the truth. Its training objective is to produce plausible text rather than true text, which means the system as a whole is indifferent to truth, so every output it produces is bullshit by design -- not just the false ones. This also tells us the 'hallucination' framing is misleading, since hallucination implies a basically reliable faculty that occasionally glitches, whereas bullshit is the default mode. Frankfurt himself treated bullshit as relatively benign compared to lying, since at least the bullshitter isn't actively opposing the truth, and that suggests we should worry less about confident LLM falsehoods than about cases where a model is fine-tuned to deceive, which would be genuine lying done through the model by its operators. So: hallucinations are bullshit, not lies, and this is reassuring.
