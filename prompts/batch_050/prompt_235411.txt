I’m a retired natural scientist (mostly atmospheric chemistry) who has started reading philosophy in my spare time, so forgive me if this is a standard distinction that I’m mangling. I’m trying to settle what I actually mean by calling an AI system an agent rather than a simulator, by going through cases and seeing where my instincts land. Consciousness, feelings, and moral responsibility are separate questions as far as I’m concerned.

Here are my tentative verdicts:

1. A thermostat controlling a greenhouse is not an agent. It responds reliably, but I don’t want to say it has a goal in the relevant sense.

2. A robot that maps an unfamiliar building, preserves battery power, notices that its plan has failed, forms a new one, and physically carries samples back to a docking station is an agent, even if all its behavior comes from learned weights plus ordinary control software.

3. A chess engine is an agent while playing chess, but not an agent in ordinary life. It selects moves in light of an objective and responds to feedback, but its whole world is the game. So maybe agency is domain-relative rather than all-or-nothing.

4. A language model in an ordinary chat window, answering questions and adopting a persona when asked, is not an agent. It may produce a convincing simulation of an agent, just as it can simulate a historian, a grieving widow, or a weather forecaster. The simulated person may be an agent in the fiction, but I don’t think the underlying model automatically inherits that status.

5. The same kind of model becomes part of an agent if it is given a persistent objective, memory across sessions, access to email and files, tools for making purchases, and the ability to initiate actions without waiting for a fresh prompt. But then I’m inclined to say that the agent is the whole engineered system, not the language model by itself.

6. Suppose the model in that tool-using system is copied onto ten servers, all pursuing the same long-term project and sharing memory. My instinct is that there is still one distributed agent, not ten agents, unless the copies begin maintaining separate memories and conflicting policies.

7. If a human reads a model-generated plan and manually carries out every step, I would not call the model the agent merely because it originated the plan. The human-plus-tools arrangement contains agency, and the model is an unusually sophisticated source of suggestions.

8. A professional actor playing a spy is definitely an agent, while the fictional spy is not a real agent at all. But when a language model plays a spy, I find myself wanting to say neither that the model is the spy nor that there is simply a human actor underneath. That makes me wonder whether my actor analogy is doing less work than I thought.

Cases 2, 4, and 7 seem to push me toward saying that agency requires a persistent observation-action-feedback loop with consequences in some environment, where the system’s own state helps select among interventions. Case 3 then seems unproblematic if agency can be relativized to a game-world. But cases 5 and 8 make that harder. The tool-using language-model system has the right loop, while the bare chat model does not; yet the same model supplies the planning and linguistic behavior in both. And in case 8, the model can improvise a coherent character in response to new events, which seems closer to acting than merely reciting a script.

Is there one reasonably precise principle that captures most of these verdicts? If not, I’d like two or three candidate principles, stated explicitly enough that I could apply them to new systems. Please say which of my verdicts each principle preserves and which it rejects. In particular, I want to know whether I should distinguish among the model, the computational process running it, the whole technical system, and the fictional or social character it portrays; whether temporary goals count differently from persistent ones; and whether agency is an intrinsic property of a system or partly a matter of how the system is embedded and described. If my thermostat intuition is the one that has to go, say so plainly.
