so my puzzle blog is doing a two-envelope paradox week and i want a poem to open it. write me a sestina about the switching argument -- you know, you're handed one of two envelopes, told one has twice the other, you open yours, and then the reasoning "the other one has either half or double, so expected value favors swapping" seems to justify switching no matter what you saw, which would mean you should've switched before opening, and then switch back, forever.

the constraint: the six repeating end-words of the sestina should be the actual moving parts of the argument (things like switch, double, half, other, etc -- pick six that genuinely recur in the reasoning), so that the form's obsessive recycling of the same words enacts how the argument keeps handing you the same conclusion from either side. and the envoi should land on the actual diagnosis -- that "the other envelope" is doing double duty as two different quantities, so the expected-value calc quietly averages across a variable that isn't fixed. dont just name-drop "probability" in the last three lines, make the slip feel like something the poem's been hiding in plain sight the whole time. someone who's wrestled with the paradox should read it and go "yeah, that's exactly where it goes wrong," and someone who hasn't should still get a decent poem out of it.
