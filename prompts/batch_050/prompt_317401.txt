Backend dev here, been messing with a side project where I try to model user goals from clickstream data, and I keep running into a conceptual wall that I'm sure has been discussed to death somewhere, I just don't know the vocabulary.

Here's the thing I keep noticing. You watch someone do something and you try to work backwards to what they want. But the same action always seems to admit two readings and I can't see how observation alone picks between them. Like: my neighbor buys these expensive supplements every month, like clockwork. Reading one: she values the supplements (or whatever they give her) as an end. Reading two: she values her health and believes the supplements get her there. From the purchase history those are indistinguishable. And it matters, because under reading two, if she stopped believing the claim, the behavior would vanish even though her "real" values never changed. Under reading one it wouldn't.

I hit the same thing in the project: my model infers a user's "preferences" from what they click, then the site's layout changes and the inferred preferences drift, even though presumably the actual person didn't suddenly want different stuff -- they just believe different things now about what clicking what will get them. And then I realized this is everywhere. Someone works 70-hour weeks: do they value the job, or do they value security and believe the hours buy it? The behavior is identical.

The closest concept I know is "revealed preference" from econ, but as I understand it that theory sort of sidesteps this by just defining preference as whatever the behavior shows, which feels like dissolving the problem rather than solving it -- because the whole issue is that the behavior underdetermines the thing I'm actually trying to get at. And there's presumably work on why you can't uniquely recover a reward function from behavior when you train agents to imitate humans, but I don't know if that's the same problem wearing a lab coat or a genuinely different one.

So: does this specific tangle -- behavior being equally explainable by "wants the outcome" vs "wants something else and believes the action produces it" -- have an established name, or a couple of competing names in different fields? If it's a named problem, how do people in those fields mark the difference between it and, on one side, the general underdetermination of any inference from evidence, and on the other side, plain uncertainty about which preferences someone has? And if the answer is that nobody has cleanly carved out this particular two-way ambiguity, what would you call it, and where would you draw its edges -- like, does the same problem show up when it's habits or biases instead of beliefs doing the mediating, or is that a different animal?
