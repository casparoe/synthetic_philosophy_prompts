I'm a PhD student working on global catastrophic risk and long-term governance, and I'm TAing a seminar where the singleton hypothesis is on the syllabus next week. For the session I want to hand out a short scripted dialogue (700-900 words) that the students can pick apart, between two characters who both actually understand the debate.

Character 1 defends the singleton hypothesis, roughly in the sense that there's a decent chance the future ends up with a single decision-making agency at the highest level of the world order, and argues this might even be on net good. Character 2 defends a multipolar future -- many independent power centers staying in competition -- and thinks a singleton would be a catastrophe.

I want them to really engage with each other's arguements rather than do a rehearsed back-and-forth of talking points. Specifically, make sure they get into:

- What "singleton" even means -- is a world government, one dominant superintelligent AI, and a subtle global coordination mechanism really one category, or is the hypothesis papering over huge differences between these?
- The main pro-singleton argument from race dynamics: multipolar competition creates pressure to cut corners on safety and sacrifice values to stay competitive, and a singleton is the only thing that dissolves that. Character 2 should push back on whether that assumes winner-take-all dynamics that might not hold, or whether coordination without unification is possible.
- The lock-in worry: a stable singleton could freeze in bad values or institutions with no external check and no exit option, forever. Character 1 should have a real answer to this, not a dodge.
- Internal stability: can a singleton even stay a singleton, given principal-agent problems, internal fragmentation, coup risk, etc.?
- The empirical trend question: has history actually been moving toward fewer poles or more, and does that tell us anything?

Both characters should be equally sharp. The singleton defender shouldn't come off as a wannabe dictator, and the multipolar character should honestly concede that race dynamics are a genuine problem for their side. End on real disagreement, no tidy resolution where one of them converts. Casual register is fine -- they're grad students, not debate bots.
