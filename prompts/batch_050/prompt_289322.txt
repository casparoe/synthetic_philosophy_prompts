Is there a name for this? I keep running into a specific way that people do the right thing but somehow make it worse, and I can't tell whether it's one phenomenon or three different ones I've lumped together.

Recent cases. A friend visited me in hospital and when I thanked him he said, don't mention it, I sat down last night and worked out that visiting you was what I had most reason to do this evening. He said it warmly! He did come! Another: my sister asked her husband why he stays with her (she's ill, it's a fair question) and he told her, honestly, that he periodically reviews his reasons and they still come out in favour, and that he'd reassess if they didn't. She was devasted. Third one is from my own side: I drove my neighbour to chemo all spring out of plain affection, zero deliberation, and then last week I caught myself auditing it afterwards — would it still have been right if she'd been less likeable? — and now the memory feels faintly contaminated even though the thought only showed up after the fact.

It's definately not hypocrisy, since they all did the thing. It's not an ulterior motive, nobody gained anything. Coldness is closer but the neighbour case was me and I'm not cold. I also have a vague memory of some philosophy example about a man who can only rescue one drowning person and picks his wife, where the problem was somehow in what he was thinking while he did it, but I can't remember the details or the verdict.

So: does this have a name? And please don't just hand me the name — where exactly is the defect? In having the thought, in being moved by the thought, or in saying it out loud? If my friend had done his calculation silently and shown up with grapes and gossip, would anything be wrong at all? And is my retroactive auditing the same thing as his up-front deliberation or a different animal? If I've mashed several distinct problems together, untangle them for me.
