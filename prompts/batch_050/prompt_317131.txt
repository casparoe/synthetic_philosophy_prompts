Is there a name for this pattern? I've noticed that in a bunch of transactions people find icky, the objection isn't really about the thing being bought or sold, it's about which person in the chain gets the money. Like with kidney transplants: the surgeon gets paid, the anesthesiologist gets paid, the hospital bills insane amounts, the transplant coordinator draws a salary, the imuno-suppressant drugs cost a fortune, and nobody considers any of that morally dicey. But if the donor -- the one person actually losing a body part -- gets paid, suddenly it's ghoulish and people start talking about exploitation. Same with adoption: agencies charge tens of thousands in fees, lawyers bill hourly, the social workers doing home studies get paid, but if the birth mother receives money beyond "reasonable expenses" it's baby-selling and probably criminal. Or look at surrogacy debates, where the fertility clinic's invoice is just a normal business expense but the surrogate's compensation is the part everyone agonizes over. In each case money is sloshing all around the transaction, basically everyone involved profits *except* the person whose contribution is most, I dunno, constitutive -- the person without whom there is literally nothing to sell -- and that person's payment is exactly where the disgust lands.

It's not that people think the whole enterprise should be non-commercial, since nobody expects the surgeon to donate her labor out of love. And it doesn't seem to be a generic worry about poor people being induced into risky deals, because paying a poor guy to do roofing or crab fishing is fine. So: is there an established concept for this specific asymmetry, where the taboo attaches to compensating the most essential contributor rather than to the transaction as a whole? If there isn't one, can you coin a term with a definition sharp enough to say where it applies and where it doesn't -- like, does it cover paying plasma donors, or paying informants, or tipping, and what separates those cases? I mostly care about getting the carving right, not just having a label to throw around.
