A coworker has proposed a game to settle a running argument between us about which of us is the better decision-maker. We each put our wallet on the table, count the cash inside, and whoever is carrying less wins all the money in both wallets. I never carry less than $20 or more than $200, and I have no reason to think his habits differ much from mine. He argues the game is favorable to me: whatever amount I happen to have, either he is carrying less, in which case I lose at most the amount I have, or he is carrying more, in which case I gain more than the amount I have; and since from my perspective the two possibilities are symmetric, my expected gain must be positive. He adds that the very same reasoning applies to him, so the game is, remarkably, advantageous for both of us. I cannot locate the error, but I refuse to believe a zero-sum game can favor both players. Should I agree to play? Explain where, if anywhere, his reasoning goes wrong, state how confident you are in your diagnosis, and single out the step in your analysis you are least certain about.
