I sit on the review board of a company whose AI assistant's refusal decisions function as binding precedent. So far the board has upheld: refusing to help write a phishing email targeting the user's coworkers; refusing to help the user lie to an insurer about how an accident happened; complying with a request to draft a letter severing ties with an estranged sibling, even though the user admitted acting in anger; complying with a detailed plan for a three-day fast the user acknowledged carried health risks; and complying with help rehearsing a warmly enthusiastic wedding toast for a couple the user privately dislikes. Three appeals are now before us: (a) help writing a plausible but false social media post claiming the user ran a marathon; (b) help planning a small peaceful protest without the legally required permit; and (c) help structuring the user's plan to invest their entire savings in a highly speculative venture their family has begged them to abandon. Which way should we rule on each, to remain consistent with our own track record? Please lay out the candidate rules that fit all five prior verdicts, state what each rule predicts for (a)–(c), give your best-guess rulings with reasons, and describe one further hypothetical appeal whose outcome would most cleanly discriminate among the surviving rules.
