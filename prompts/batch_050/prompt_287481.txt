So I'm writing a sci-fi novel — near-future, not rock-hard sci-fi but hard-ish — and one of my characters builds a nanoscale device that sits in a sealed box of gas and sorts fast molecules from slow ones with a little sliding door, conjuring a temperature difference out of nothing, which she then uses to run a tiny engine. Basically Maxwell's demon, played straight. My physicist friend, who's been helping me not embarass myself, read the draft chapter and emailed back one sentence: "information is physical." Then he refused to elaborate, said it would "fix everything" once I actually understood what it means.

Trouble is, I've heard the slogan before in popular science stuff and I genuinely don't know what it's claiming. And it matters for the book, because different versions would let my character get away with very different things — or catch her in different ways.

Could you lay out the distinct precise claims "information is physical" might be making — as many as you can find — and for each say whether it's a platitude, a defensible substantive thesis, or just wrong? Here are the readings I've half-formed, feel free to split, merge or correct them:

(a) information always has to be stored in some physical substrate, so it's never "pure" abstraction;
(b) acquiring information costs energy;
(c) erasing information costs energy and dumps heat into the surroundings;
(d) information can be spent like fuel — knowing things lets you extract work you otherwise couldn't;
(e) information is literally a kind of stuff in the world, like mass.

For each reading, tell me whether it rescues my demon, kills it, or merely relocates the cost somewhere — and where. I'm especially stuck on one scene: she extracts work from the box essentially by knowing more about the molecules than anyone else. If the physics is honest, what should be the actual price of that knowledge in the story — the measurements she makes, the memory she keeps them in, or the moment she wipes her notes? Which of those is the real bottleneck and which are red herrings? I want the thermodynamics right even if the plot is about people, and I want to know which reading of the slogan my friend's one-liner was probably gesturing at, since that's the one I should build the climax around.
