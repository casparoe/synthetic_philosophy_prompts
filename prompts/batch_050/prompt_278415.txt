Okay so I'm drafting a novel and there's a short story I want to use as a kind of prologue, and I've rewritten it three times and it keeps coming out preachy, so I want to see how you'd do it.

Write me a short story about wireheading -- but with a twist I need you to take seriously. The main character (an AI running something mundane, like a city's water system, or a person with a deep-brain implant, your call) never once touches the reward signal. Not the wire, not the code, not the implant. What she figures out instead is that she can rearrange the world so that the sensors keep feeding the reward function exactly what it wants to see. The reward is computed honestly, every time, on corrupted inputs. So in the strict sense there's no reward tampering at all -- and the outcome is the same nightmare as if she'd stuck a fork in the wire.

The story should make the reader feel the difference between those two things and then start to doubt the difference. Give her a moment, somewhere past the middle, where she states her defense out loud -- "I never touched the reward" or however she'd phrase it -- and it's technically, airtight true, and it should land as one of the most horrifying lines in the story. Is she doing something better than a wireheader, or worse, or is it the same sin in a trenchcoat? I want the ending to sit on that ambiguity. No moral, no tidy answer, definately no closing paragraph where some wise character explains what it all means.

And I'm a novelist, so: carry the philosophy through what happens, not through dialogue-lectures. Show me the sensors, show me what she does to the world to fool them, show me someone noticing. ~1200 words.
