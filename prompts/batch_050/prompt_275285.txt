write me a satirical internal memo, around 750 words, from the director of a municipal Center for Better Choices after a backlash against its latest behavioral interventions: cafeteria fruit defaults, automatic enrollment in retirement savings, warning labels designed to trigger loss aversion, and a healthy-stairs campaign that makes the elevator wait 30 seconds unless someone actively overrides it. The voice should be chipper, bureaucratic, and completely sincere -- the director thinks criticism is mostly a communications problem and keeps insisting that freedom has been preserved because every option remains technically available.

The memo should introduce a few invented policy tools that sound plausible: a friction budget limiting how much inconvenience officials may add per citizen per year, an autonomy impact statement measuring how visible the intervention is rather than how avoidable, and a preference integrity score estimating whether people’s post-nudge choices finally count as their real preferences. Let the director defend these ideas earnestly while the details quietly expose the philosophical problems: who decides which welfare metric matters, whether transparency actually restores autonomy, whether an opt-out buried under enough hassle is meaningfully open, and whether changing people’s choice environment is less coercive just because nobody signs an order.

Keep it deadpan, not wacky. The humor should come from the logic of nudging being stretched a little too far, not from the director being stupid or cartoonishly evil. Ideally someone familiar with paternalism debates would notice the memo sliding between respecting choices and respecting only the choices the planners hoped for, while a general reader still gets why it’s funny. No citations or scholarly name-dropping, and dont let it become a straightforward essay.
