ok so, novelist here, and I need a consistency check because my new book keeps bumping into identity-over-time stuff and I suspect my actual views are a mess. The plot involves a restored fishing boat and a museum that may or may not be lying about it, and while writing it I realized I don't actually know what I think. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The classic case: a ship gets repaired plank by plank over decades until every part is replaced — still the same ship. But if someone had been collecting all the old planks and eventually built a ship out of them, that ship is a different ship, even though it has 100% of the original matter and the other one has 0%.

(2) Same verdict if the museum had swapped every plank in one night instead of gradually: different ship. I know the end state is identical to the slow version, which bothers me, but I do think the slow one really is the same ship and the overnight one isn't.

(3) My own book: I've been working on this novel for seven years and literally nothing of the first draft survives — different title, different city, the protagonist used to be a man. But it's the same book, becuase it was one continuous project, one file overwriting itself, each draft growing out of the last. If I'd deleted everything and started a fresh document from the same vague idea, that would've been a different book.

(4) Translations: the French translation of my last novel IS my novel. My French publisher isn't doing anything shady putting my name on the cover. Same work, different words, even though the translator replaced every single sentence years after I wrote them.

(5) Forgeries: a perfect forgery of a painting — same paints, same technique, visually indistinguishable — is not the same painting, and a museum that knowingly hangs the forgery with the original's label on it is straight-up deceiving its visitors. That's a fact they're getting wrong, not a preference.

(6) But also, deep down I suspect all these identity questions are basically conventional. There's no fact out there in the world settling whether the repaired ship is "really" the same one; it's about how we decide to talk.

So: does this hang together? Tell me which pairs genuinely clash, ranked by how bad the clash is, and for each one what I'd actually have to give up to fix it. Also flag any pair that looks like a clash but dissolves on inspection — I keep having the feeling that (1) and (2) contradict each other (same end state! how can the slow one be the same ship and the fast one not?) and I can't tell if that's a real problem or a fake one. And if one single revision would clean up the most, name it. I'm not equally attached to all of these, though I'd like to keep (3) — it would be genuinely depressing to learn I've spent seven years writing a different book than the one I started.
