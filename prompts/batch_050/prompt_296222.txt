My friend has pretty settled intuitions about when it's okay to have kids, and I'm trying to figure out the rule she's actually running. Her verdicts so far:

- Conceiving a child when you know it'll inherit a condition that makes its life short and dominated by pain: clearly wrong, obviously don't do that.
- Deciding to stay childfree even though any kid of yours would very likely have a great life: totally fine, nobody gets wronged, there's no duty to have kids.
- Conceiving now even though waiting three months (to finish a medication that causes mild birth defects) would mean conceiving a different, healthier kid instead: she says that's wrong, you should wait — even though the kid conceived now would still have a life clearly worth living.
- Whether to have a second kid when the first is happy and the second probably would be too: entirely the parents' call, no moral pressure either way.

Now the new case: a couple deciding between having one child, who'd be very happy, or twins, each of whom would be happy but somewhat less so (stretched resources and attention). What will she say? I'm actually more interested in: what rule best explains her earlier verdicts, which other rules fit those verdicts just as well but come apart from it on this case, and what further case I could put to her that would settle which rule she's really running.
