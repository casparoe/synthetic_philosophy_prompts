My housemate is a philosophy grad student and has this whole thing about when it's legit to talk about something as if it wants and believes stuff, versus when it's just "as if designed," versus when you should drop the talk entirely and just deal with the mechanism. We've been arguing about it for months so I've got a decent record of her actual calls:

- Thermostat: no beliefs, no desires. It's just a bimetal strip, mechanism talk all the way down, and she says anyone who says otherwise is being cute.
- The chess engine on her laptop: she happily says it "knows" the knight fork is coming and "wants" to castle. But she insists this is a stance we take, not a fact about the machine.
- Her dog: full believer and desirer, obviously, and she gets annoyed if you suggest this is "just" a stance.
- A corporation (her example was Amazon): she says it literally has beliefs and desires and you can predict it better by asking what it wants than by asking what any employee wants.
- An ant colony: same deal, beliefs and desires of its own, not reducible to the ants'.
- A slime mold solving a maze: no. "Impressive chemistry," she says, "no point of view."
- A hurricane: no, and she scoffed when I even asked.
- The free market as a whole: she went back and forth and landed on no — she said there's no unified predictor gain from treating "the market" as an agent even though finance people talk that way constantly.

Here's what's killing me: I can't figure out what rule she's running. It can't just be "complex enough to beat you at prediction," because the slime mold is complex and the corporation is arguably just a bunch of people with forms. It can't be "has a brain," because Amazon. It can't be "evolved rather than built," because the chess engine. Every principle I come up with either lets in the slime mold or kicks out the ant colony.

So three things. First, give me at least three different rules that fit all eight of her verdicts, stated precisely enough that they'd actually disagree about something. Second, predict her calls on four new cases she hasn't ruled on: (a) a large language model, (b) a whole ecosystem like a coral reef, (c) a swarm of warehouse robots trained by reinforcement learning that developed an emergent traffic convention nobody programmed, and (d) a river delta. For each one, say which of your rules predict what, and give me your best guess of what she'd actually say and why. Third, tell me the single new case (not one of mine) that would most efficiently discriminate between your remaining rules after her verdicts on my four are in.

Give me the reasoning in prose first, then a structured summary as a JSON object with fields: "rules" (array of objects with "rule" and "fits_all_eight" boolean), "predictions" (array of objects with "case", "predicted_verdict" [one of "mechanism only", "as-if designed", "full beliefs and desires"], "rules_that_support", "rules_that_reject", and "confidence"), and "best_discriminating_case" (a string explaining the case and what each outcome would rule out).
