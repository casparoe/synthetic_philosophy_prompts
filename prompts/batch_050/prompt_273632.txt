So I was reading David Pearce's The Hedonistic Imperative (the online manifesto version) and got stuck on this line from section 0.2, where he's talking about the hedonic treadmill:

"Feedback-inhibition ensures that a majority of people would be periodically bored, depressed or angst-ridden in a recreated Garden of Eden."

He's basically saying that no environment, however perfect, can durably fix well-being, because our hedonic set-points are genetically baked in and feedback mechanisms drag us back to them. That's why he thinks the only real route to abolishing suffering is rewriting the biology rather than improving the world.

I want your credence on a specific version of his conclusion: that within, say, the next few centuries, biotechnology (genetic engineering, drugs, neural intervention, whatever) will have eliminated aversive experience in the majority of sentient beings under human influence, humans included. Don't just give me a number though, I want the derivation. Decompose it along lines like: (a) how likely is the strong treadmill claim in that passage actually true, i.e. that environmental improvements basically can't durably raise set-points; (b) given that, how likely is it technically feasible to stably rewire set-points without wrecking motivation, learning, or the ability to respond to actual threats (Pearce's "gradients of bliss" idea needs this to work); (c) how likely is it that societies would actually choose or permit this rather than treat it the way we treat wireheading now; and (d) whatever else you think belongs in there. Number plus one line of justification per term, then tell me how they combine and which single term your final credence is most sensitive to.

Also, one thing that bugs me: the passage smuggles in an empirical claim (the treadmill is strong enough that Eden wouldn't help) as support for a biotech conclusion, but couldn't you accept the empirical claim and still conclude the answer is changing what counts as a good life rather than the biochemestry? If that alternative has real weight, fold it in and tell me where it lands.
