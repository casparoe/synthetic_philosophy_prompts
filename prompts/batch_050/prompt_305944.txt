A colleague recently gave me the following summary of Samuel Scheffler's afterlife conjecture, and I want to know how much of it to trust before I rely on it in a paper:

"Scheffler's claim is that human extinction matters chiefly because of what it would mean for those of us alive now. Imagine we learned that humanity would die out shortly after our own deaths, or that the currently living are the last generation: Scheffler predicts that virtually everyone would fall into despair and that human projects across the board would lose their point. This shows, he argues, that the value of everything we do secretly depends on the continued existence of future generations, and hence that the badness of extinction is exhausted by its effects on people who already exist. A further consequence is that caring about humanity's survival is not really altruism at all, since it turns out to be a precondition of our own lives mattering."

I'm inclined to think this is roughly right, but the final sentence feels like a stronger conclusion than the thought experiment supports. Which parts of the summary would Scheffler accept, which parts overstate or misstate the conjecture, and does my colleague's closing claim about altruism follow from whatever survives correction?
