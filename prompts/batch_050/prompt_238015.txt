After thirty-five years as an experimental biologist, I retired and began reading philosophy, and I keep colliding with the phrase "socially constructed." Writers apply it to some kinds and withhold it from others, and I would like to know whether any principle stands behind my own verdicts. Here is where I land on eight cases:

1. Money. A twenty-dollar bill owes its status entirely to institutions; abolish them and it reverts to paper. Constructed.
2. Electrons. They would have existed, with their charge, had no mind ever arisen. Not constructed.
3. Race. Folk racial categories carve human populations along lines no biological kind tracks, and the categories persist through social practice -- though their effects are real enough. Constructed.
4. Species. Lions and tigers differ independently of us; speciation is a process we discover, not one we decree. Not constructed.
5. Planets. The vote that demoted Pluto settled a definition, not a fact; the solar system offers a continuum of orbiting bodies with no joint at the chosen cutoff. A constructed boundary, though Pluto itself is not constructed.
6. Weeds. A weed is a plant out of place relative to someone's purposes. Constructed.
7. Hypertension. The threshold printed on the guidelines is a committee's decision, but the vascular damage it tracks is not. The category is partly constructed, the pathology not.
8. Constellations. Orion belongs to us; the stars do not.

The pair I cannot reconcile is 4 and 5. Taxonomists, like astronomers, vote and argue over continua -- ring species make the continuum vivid -- yet I want to say species are discovered while planet boundaries are decided, and I cannot articulate what earns the difference.

Please tell me what principle, if any, captures these verdicts: state it precisely, indicate which case motivates each clause, and say what it predicts for three cases I have not listed -- the kilogram, genius, and wilderness. If no single principle accommodates all eight, say so plainly and tell me which verdict is cheapest to abandon and what the abandonment costs. Keep the entire reply to 500 words or fewer; I would rather have one exact principle than a leisurely survey.
