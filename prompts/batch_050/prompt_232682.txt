So I keep hearing about the "strategy-stealing assumption" in discussions of AI risk -- the idea being that whatever clever plan a misaligned AI could come up with to take over or cause harm, an aligned AI (or humans aided by one) could in principle run the same strategy first and block it, since strategies themselves are neutral about who's executing them. Given that, I'm confused about why takeover risk is still treated as a live worry at all. Like, either the strategy-stealing arguement works, in which case any AI whose capabilities are reachable by an aligned system can't do anything we couldn't have preempted, or it doesn't work, in which case what exactly is it an assumption *for*? I've seen people say the remaining risk is just "coordination failures" on the human side, but that seems too weak to justify all the concern. Can you explain what the residual worry is actually supposed to be, or is the strategy-stealing move basically dissolving the takeover scenario for any sub- or near-human-level AI?
