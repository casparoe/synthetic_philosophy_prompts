I’m a second-year PhD student in aesthetics helping run a small grad-student podcast, and I need to prepare an interveiw with a philosopher who defends a contemporary Humean account of beauty: an object is beautiful iff it would characteristically please, or win the approval of, sufficiently qualified critics under suitable conditions. The qualifications are familiar Humean ones: delicacy of perception, practice, breadth of comparison, and freedom from prejudice. She thinks this gives us a genuine standard of taste without treating beauty as a wholly mind-independent property.

Her work makes a lot of this passage from David Hume’s Of the Standard of Taste:

> There are certain terms in every language, which import blame, and others praise; and all men, who use the same tongue, must agree in their application of them. Every voice is united in applauding elegance, propriety, simplicity, spirit in writing; and in blaming fustian, affectation, coldness, and a false brilliant: But when critics come to particulars, this seeming unanimity vanishes; and it is found, that they had affixed a very different meaning to their expressions.

Draft nine interview questions that go beyond asking her to summarize Hume or rehearse “beauty is subjective, but not merely subjective.” For each question, add the answer she is most likely to give and a follow-up that presses that answer.

I especially want questions that:

- ask whether the passage supports a semantic diagnosis of aesthetic disagreement, an epistemic one, or both;
- challenge her to identify qualified critics without covertly defining qualification as giving the verdicts she already favors;
- consider two equally practiced and seemingly unprejudiced critics who persistently disagree about a particular work;
- ask when familiarity with a genre is necessary context and when it becomes parochial prejudice;
- press whether cross-cultural or historical convergence would actually be evidence for her standard, or only evidence of shared human psychology;
- test the normative step from “qualified critics would approve” to “I ought to try to appreciate this”;
- use one concrete case involving contemporary art or music, rather than staying with Hume’s literary examples.

The questions should get progressively harder for her to answer with the stock reply that disagreement just means one party lacks the relevant qualifications.
