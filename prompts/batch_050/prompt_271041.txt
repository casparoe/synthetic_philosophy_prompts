Write a parody of an internal policy memo from the HR department of a company whose CEO has gone all-in on Bentham, announcing that employee complaints will no longer be processed if they appeal to "natural rights." The memo should introduce a formal two-tier classification system for such complaints — plain "nonsense" (complaints that are merely false, e.g. claims of rights no statute or contract ever granted, filed and forgotten) versus "nonsense upon stilts" (complaints that are false AND dangerous, because they dress up someone's preferences as imprescriptible entitlements that trump the utility calculation, like a demand for a "right to a private office" that can't be traded off against headcount). Include the new submission procedure (all grievances must now be restated purely in terms of pleasures and pains, with quantities), a worked example of a complaint being re-filed, and a FAQ. Around 550 words. The satire should land the standard worries about Bentham's move — that calling rights fictions doesn't make unjust law impossible, that slavery passes the "legal rights exist" test just fine, that utility talk smuggles the entitlements back in — without ever stating them or winking at the reader. Deadpan corporate voice throughout.
