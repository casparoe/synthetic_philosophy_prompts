Over the past year, prompted partly by a housemate writing her thesis on wild animal welfare, I have been trying to bring my food choices in line with whatever my actual moral intuitions are, and I have noticed that my verdicts across cases are more patterned than I expected but perhaps not consistent. Here is where I land. When the choice is a chicken entrée or a beef entrée, I order the beef, because I think broiler chickens have considerably worse lives than cattle and because something like two hundred times as many animals must be raised and killed per calorie of chicken as per calorie of beef. When a decent plant-based option is on the menu, I take it over the beef, and I do not think this verdict is much shaken by the fact that crop cultivation kills large numbers of field mice, voles, and other small animals through plowing and harvest. When my housemate presses the "least harm" argument -- that a cow grazing pasture its whole life might occasion fewer total animal deaths than the equivalent calories in harvested grain -- I resist it, though I admit my resistance is partly that almost no beef actually works that way and partly that pasture-based systems require so much land that they displace wild habitat, and I cannot cleanly separate those reasons from simple reluctance to concede her point. I eat eggs from my aunt's backyard flock without guilt but refuse commercial eggs. And here is the case that nags at me: I feel essentially nothing about the insects killed by the pest management that protects the crops I eat, even though I am unmoved by confident claims that insects cannot suffer, given how badly such confidence has historically tracked actual capacity for welfare.

What I want to know is whether there is a single principle that generates all of these verdicts -- something like minimizing expected total suffering, weighted by welfare capacity, per calorie consumed -- or whether my pattern is a patchwork. Please state your best candidate principle precisely enough that I can see which case motivates each clause of it, and tell me what it predicts for two cases I have not listed: choosing between farmed fish and pork, and choosing between conventionally grown vegetables and vegetables grown with especially rodent-intensive harvesting methods. Most importantly, tell me honestly which of my verdicts the principle cannot save. My suspicion is that either my indifference to harvest deaths or my preference for plants over genuine pasture-raised beef has to go, since the same principle seems to push them in opposite directions -- but perhaps there is a coherent distinction I am missing, and if so I would like it stated rather than gestured at.
