So I cover science and ideas for a magazine and I'm working on a feature about the hedonistic imperative — the transhumanist-ish position, most associated with David Pearce, that we should use biotechnology (gene editing, designer drugs, even rewriting wild predators so nothing gets eaten) to phase out suffering altogether and run everything on gradients of bliss instead.

For the sidebar I want to commision a satirical piece, and here's the idea: a mock product recall notice, the kind a consumer safety commission puts out, except the product being recalled is suffering itself — all of it, globally, across species. Deadpan bureaucratic voice the whole way through. Hit the usual recall elements: affected units (all sentient beings, all model years since the Cambrian, give or take), the defect being corrected, instructions for consumers on disposing of their remaining stockpiles of grief and despair, a list of side effects of the replacement product ("information-sensitive gradients of well-being"), an FAQ for confused customers (e.g. someone asking whether their grief can be grandfathered in because it felt meaningful, someone else asking if the recall covers mild inconveniences or just the big stuff), and a note that certain units in the wild — sharks, wasps, the cat — are being retrofitted on site rather than recalled.

The satire should land the actual objections people raise against the view: that suffering carries information you'd lose at your peril, that art and love and moral seriousness seem tangled up with it, that a world of bliss might just be a planet of wireheads, that the lion really does have to be dealt with if you're serious. But it should only ever land them by exaggerating things the position genuinely says — the regulator is sincerely, cheerfully convinced this is the best recall in the agency's history, and the objections should be legible only in what the notice takes for granted. No strawmen; someone sympathetic to the view should be able to say "fair cop."

Don't name Pearce or any real person — invent the agency, the spokesperson, the recall reference number. Around 600 words.
