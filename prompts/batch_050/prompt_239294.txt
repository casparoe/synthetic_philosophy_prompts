Build me a thought experiment about behavioral tests for mindedness: two systems, A and B, whose actual observable behavior is completely identical — same outputs to every input anyone ever tries, same timing, same hesitations, same jokes — but where a defensible behavioral criterion for having a mind still gives different verdicts about the two. Constraints: no giant lookup tables of prerecorded conversations (Block's trick is banned), no homunculi running the show, and you cant just stipulate that one has inner states and the other doesn't; the difference has to live in how each system comes to produce its behavior, in a way that's physically possible in principle. After the scenario, explain which version of the behavioral criterion (raw actual performance vs. something counterfactual or dispositional) diverges on A and B, and concede that a strict only-the-transcript-matters Turing-style behaviorist cannot tell them apart, so your case gives that person nothing. Then name the single feature of the setup that's doing all the work, and finish with how the strict behaviorist would try to dismiss the whole thing — and whether their dismissal reveals that "behavioral evidence" was never really just about actual behavior in the first place.
