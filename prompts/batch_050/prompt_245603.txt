I am finalizing the statement of position that opens my qualifying paper on the semantic paradoxes, and before I defend it I would like the position itself audited for internal coherence. My commitments are as follows.

(i) The T-schema is constitutive of our concept of truth and must be retained without restriction: for every sentence S, "S" is true if and only if S. Any restriction to a class of "safe" sentences is ad hoc, an evasion rather than a theory of truth.

(ii) The correct response to the Liar is dialetheist: the Liar sentence is both true and false, and because the underlying logic is paraconsistent, this admission does not spread. The principal appeal of this response is that it is cheap — one blocks explosion and otherwise reasons as one always has.

(iii) The Liar and Curry's paradox are a single pathology in two guises: in both, self-reference produces instability in the truth predicate. An adequate theory must therefore treat them uniformly; a solution that neutralizes one while leaving the other intact is no solution at all.

(iv) Curry's paradox, specifically, is already covered by my dialetheism: the Curry sentence is simply another dialetheia, and paraconsistency contains it exactly as it contains the Liar. If anything, I regard Curry as the less threatening of the two paradoxes.

(v) Yablo's paradox demonstrates that self-reference is strictly inessential to semantic paradox: an infinite sequence of sentences, each saying only that all the later sentences in the sequence are untrue, generates contradiction even though no sentence refers to itself or to any earlier sentence. Consequently, any proposed solution whose mechanism consists in prohibiting or taming self-reference or vicious circularity is thereby refuted.

(vi) Truth-value gap theories are hopeless. To declare the Liar neither true nor false is to abandon excluded middle, which I take to be non-negotiable.

My questions are these. Which of these commitments genuinely stand in tension with one another, and which pairs merely appear to? Please take the tensions in order of severity, and for each genuine one state exactly what would have to be surrendered to restore consistency and what that surrender would cost the overall position. Where an apparent tension dissolves on inspection, show why rather than simply asserting that it does. Finally, if the set as a whole is untenable, tell me which single change would render it coherent with the least collateral damage — I have no fixed view about which commitment ought to go.
