A popular science book I read summarizes Daniel Dennett's hierarchy of minds as follows: 'Dennett ranks minds into four ascending kinds of creature. Darwinian creatures are products of natural selection alone, hardwired with behaviors they cannot modify in their lifetimes. Skinnerian creatures can learn, but only by blind trial and error, repeating whatever gets rewarded. Popperian creatures can test candidate actions in an inner rehearsal environment before risking them, letting their hypotheses die in their stead -- and since such testing requires representing hypotheses explicitly, only language-users can manage it, which means only humans. Gregorian creatures go further by using tools, which is widespread in the animal kingdom, so crows, chimps, and many other species join us at this level. The stages form a ladder: as higher kinds of intelligence evolve, they replace the lower machinery rather than supplementing it, so adult human behavior is thoroughly Gregorian and no longer governed by blind reinforcement.' Something about this strikes me as too tidy, but I can't pin down where it goes wrong. Which parts would Dennett accept, which distort his actual account, and do the conclusions about which animals qualify at each level survive correction?
