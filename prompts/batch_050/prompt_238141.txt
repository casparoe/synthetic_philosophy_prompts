I work as a software engineer (backend/distributed systems, ~8 years in) and I keep getting sucked into this debate I stumbled on about methodological individualism vs holism in the social sciences. no formal phil background, so forgive the framing.

Here's where I've landed, partly because of my day job: I'm basically a methodological individualist. My reasoning is that a social system is like a distributed system. The cluster's behavior is *nothing over and above* what the nodes do plus the message-passing between them. If you tell me the full state of every node and every message in flight, there's no extra fact about "what the cluster is doing" left over. Same, I figure, for firms, markets, nations: they're just people doing stuff in structured interaction. So explanations that bottom out in individual beliefs, desires, incentives etc are the only real explanations, and holist talk about "the market punished the firm" or "the institution reproduced itself" is at best a handy shorthand, like when I say "the load balancer decided" when really its just code running on some box.

But here's the thing that makes me less sure than I sound. In my actual work, the shorthand isn't just shorthand. You genuinely cannot debug a cascading failure by simulating from the node level up, and sometimes the right level of abstraction to *find and fix* the bug is the system level, and reduction would actually lose information you need. Also, emergent behavior in distributed systems is real in some sense — byzantine failures, thrashing, stuff no node "intends."

So: what would count against my position? Don't give me a balanced both-sides tour, I want you to take a stand on how strong my view actually is and then rank the defeaters. What cases in the social sciences (or elsewhere) would, if they turned out to work a certain way, actually sink individualism rather than just annoy it? Which objections would a smart committed individualist (the "explanations must bottom out in individuals" kind, not the strawman "groups don't exist" kind) concede are real problems, and which would they wave off, and are they right to wave them off? And specifically: does my distributed-systems analogy actually do any work for me, or does it backfire — i.e. does the fact that system-level reasoning is indispensable in my own field undermine my position rather than support it? Commit to an answer on that last one especially.
