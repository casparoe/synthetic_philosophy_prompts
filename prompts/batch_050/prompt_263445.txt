I spent 35 years doing experimental chemistry before I retired, and lately I've been reading my way into philosophy -- most recently the old Indian Nyaya school and their list of pramanas, the supposedly legitimate ways of knowing: perception, inference, comparison, and testimony. Here's what I'd like. Translate that framework into the vocabulary I actually think in: measurement, instrument calibration, error bars, controls, replication, peer review, the whole apparatus of getting reliable results out of a lab. Be systematic about it, pramana by pramana, and don't smuggle in lab assumptions and pass them off as translation. Then the part I really care about: tell me what doesn't survive. Is there anything the Nyaya folks are insisting on that has no real counterpart in lab-speak -- and is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because their distinctions were never as sharp as they looked? Please give me an actual verdict on that last question with reasons, not a menu of options I have to pick between. I can handle being told the chemists lose this one.
