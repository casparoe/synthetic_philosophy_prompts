Two friends in my philosophy circle have been going at this for a year. One is a perceptualist about aesthetic value: if no observer could ever tell two objects apart, they have the same aesthetic value, full stop — so a perfect duplicate of a Vermeer is aesthetically worth exactly what the Vermeer is, and the extra reverence for the original is some mix of snobbery, insurance pricing, and superstition. The other is an authenticist: a painting is the outcome of a particular performance by a particular person at a particular point in history, and that history is part of what you value when you value the work, so even a molecule-for-molecule duplicate produced by some freak lab accident would be missing something real, aesthetically.

Write the document they'd produce if they ran an actual adversarial collaboration instead of just rehashing the same argument. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both would sign — I suspect both accept that beliefs about provenance genuinely change how people experience a work (not just what they say about it), and that some artworks, like readymades or performance pieces, can't be duplicated at all — but check my suspicions rather than taking them on faith; (2) a table of the remaining disagreements, each phrased carefully enough that both sides would accept the wording of it's own position; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one of them, as opposed to disagreements that are really about words, like what counts as an "aesthetic" property in the first place; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Candidate cases: van Meegeren's fake Vermeers being admired as Vermeers and then "seen" as clumsy after the exposure; the lab accident that spits out a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a Rembrandt with no painter involved at all; and a heavily restored original where most of the visible paint is now the restorer's. Dont just split the difference or declare it a draw — the point is to find out where they actually disagree, including whether the perceptualist can really handle the readymades and whether the authenticist can honestly say what the accidental duplicate is missing.
