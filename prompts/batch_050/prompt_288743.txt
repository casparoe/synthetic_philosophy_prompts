So I'm a novelist working on a scene and could use some help getting the philosophy right. Two of my characters, both physicists at a small university, get into an argument over beers about quantum suicide. One of them, call her Mira, has convinced herself that if many-worlds is true, then quantum suicide is basically survivable: from her own first-person view she'll always end up in a branch where the gun misfires, so she could in princple never experience her own death, and she half-jokingly claims this makes her effectively immortal. The other, Tomas, thinks this is deeply confused — but he's not a strawman, he actually accepts many-worlds himself, he just thinks she's gone wrong somewhere about identity over time, or about the weight that tiny-measure branches should have in her reasoning, or about what "surviving" could even mean when almost all of her future selves in the wavefunction are corpses.

Write the dialogue. I want a genuine back-and-forth, not them talking past each other. Tomas should press her on the practical stuff — e.g. whether she'd actually be willing to pull the trigger for a million dollars, and if not, why not. Mira should push back on him for inconsistency, since he happily does things every day that carry a tiny risk of death, and she'd say he's just refusing to follow his own anthropic reasoning where it leads. Bonus points if they get into whether "you" in a low-measure branch is still you in the morally relevant sense. Neither should win cleanly, and I don't want one of them converted at the end — maybe they end on an unresolved note. Around 500 words, minimal stage directions, keep their voices distinct.
