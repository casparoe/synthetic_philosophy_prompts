For a psychology class I learned about the sphex wasp, which drags a paralyzed cricket to its burrow, leaves it at the threshold to go inside and check, and if an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches, just drags it back and re-does the whole sequence from the start, over and over, apparently unable to notice the repetition. I put together the following argument from that example and it seems to prove too much. (1) A creature lacks free will whenever its behavior is the output of a fixed program it runs without being able to take notice of what is actually happening. (2) For every human action, there is some complete explanation of it in terms of the person's stable dispositions, habits, and conditioning, together with the situation they were in. (3) So human behavior, like the wasp's, is the output of a fixed program. (4) But the wasp example shows that being the output of a fixed program is incompatible with free will. (5) Therefore humans have no free will. (6) I take it that humans have free will, so one of the premises must go. I would most like to keep (1) and (4), since they seem to capture exactly what is disturbing about the wasp. Where does the argument go wrong, if it does?
