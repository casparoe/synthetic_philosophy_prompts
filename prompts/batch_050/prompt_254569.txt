I'm a retired biochemist who's gotten into decision theory as a hobby, and I keep running into a situation I can't find a proper name for. Imagine someone whose preferences are perfectly consistent at every single moment: whenever you ask them, they rank their options in a neat transitive order. But the ranking shifts over the week in a predictable way. On Monday they'd rather have an apple than a banana, on Wednesday a banana than a cherry, on Friday a cherry than an apple. A trader who knows the pattern could presumably swap fruit with them all week, charging a small fee per trade, and walk away with their money while they end up back where they started. Now the classic money pump argument, as I understand it, pins the blame on having an inconsistent ranking at a single time. But this person never has one — every choice they make is flawless by the lights of there preferences at the moment they make it. So does this configuration have a name of its own, or is it just the ordinary money pump wearing a disguise? And if the standard argument shows intransitive preferences are irrational, what does this case show: that there's a further requirement that your preferences stay put over time, that you must plan around your future self, or nothing at all? Don't just hand me a label — I want to know where the irrationality, if any, is supposed to sit. Also, tell me what would change your verdict: what consideration would convince you this collapses into the ordinary money pump, or that it's genuinely distinct, and where is your reasoning most likely to go wrong?
