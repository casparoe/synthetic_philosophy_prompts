Two friends of mine fell into an argument that started over a chess engine and ended somewhere I can't follow. Sam said the engine doesn't believe or want anything: it is voltages and gates, and saying it "wants to trap your queen" is loose talk, like saying a river wants to reach the sea. Dana replied that loose talk that works isn't merely loose: treating the engine as a rational agent that knows the rules and wants to win lets you predict its moves nearly perfectly, whereas the physical description is useless to anyone, and a stance that yields that kind of reliable prediction earns the right to its ontology. Sam countered that predictive success isn't truth — epicycles predicted the planets — and that the engine's so-called beliefs live in our interpretation, not in the machine; strip away the observer and there are no symbols, only current. Dana answered that the question is whether the pattern is really there or only projected, and that a pattern which lets you compress an enormous behavioral description into a short one is there whether or not anyone notices it, the way nobody has to interpret a tiger's stripes for them to exist; the machine's states track your queen the way a barometer tracks storms, and that tracking just is the belief. Sam pushed back that the very same physical machine could be reinterpreted as playing a different game under a different semantics, so which beliefs it has is underdetermined by the physics — and anything underdetermined by the physics cannot be real in the way the physics is real. Dana's final move was that underdetermination at the margins doesn't dissolve the pattern where it is sharp, and that Sam's own beliefs about chess are just as hostage to radical reinterpretation, so his standard would abolish belief in humans too. Neither conceded, and I genuinely can't settle it.
