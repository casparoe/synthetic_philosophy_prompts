I am drafting an internal framework for evaluating conversational AI systems and would like a consistency check on the following commitments:

1. An AI system can be honest, dishonest, or deceptive without possessing beliefs, desires, or consciousness; these terms apply functionally to its outputs and selection mechanisms.
2. Deceptiveness is audience-relative: an output is deceptive when, in context, it would be apt to lead an ordinary, reasonably attentive user to a false or unwarranted belief, even if the actual user is not convinced.
3. Merely foreseeable misleadingness is insufficient for deception. The output must have been selected or optimized because of its tendency to induce the relevant belief; otherwise, the case is one of error, negligent disclosure, or perhaps manipulation.
4. A confident hallucination unsupported by the model’s evidence is dishonest but not, merely as such, deceptive.
5. Literally true outputs can be deceptive through framing, implicature, or selective omission, although omission is deceptive only when the context creates a duty to disclose.
6. A retention-optimized customer-support bot that truthfully says a fare can be changed without a fee, while omitting the customer’s eligibility for a refund, is deceptive when the retention objective explains the omission and leads the customer to infer that no refund is available.
7. A general disclaimer that the model may be wrong does not by itself make a specific, confident, unsupported answer honest.

Do these commitments form a coherent package? Audit them rather than merely summarizing them: identify genuine tensions and merely apparent ones, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and explain what would have to be revised in each case and what the lowest-cost repair would be. In particular, determine whether my classifications of the hallucination and support-bot cases follow from the stated tests, and whether honesty and deception emerge as independent dimensions or as one concept nested within the other. Finally, identify the consideration that would most plausibly change your overall verdict and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
