I'm putting together a segment for a philosophy podcast on interpretations of probability and I need a thought experiment built to spec. Here's what I want it to do:

Construct a scenario where a frequentist and a propensity theorist are pushed toward different probability assignments for the same event, and where the propensity theorist's answer is the intuitively right one. Constraints:

- Realistic and mundane. No quantum anything, no radioactive decay, no idealized infinite sequences spelled out in the scenario. Something like a factory, a casino, a clinic, a workshop.
- The event has to be genuinely single-case, but the single-caseness has to arise naturally from the story (the machine wears out, the process gets recalibrated, the person retires), not from me just stipulating "and then the device was destroyed so it can never happen again" -- that feels like cheating.
- There must be frequency information available in the story, and it has to point one way while our intuition about this particular case points the other way. If there's no frequency data at all the frequentist can just shrug, and that's boring.
- Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph.

After the scenario, do the work: state what a frequentist says about the case, what a propensity theorist says, and what a subjective Bayesian says (I suspect the Bayesian gets the intuitively right answer too cheaply, so say whether that's a problem for my segment). Then tell me which single feature of your scenario is doing all the work in making the frequentist uncomfortable, and what happens to the case if that feature is removed. Finally, give the frequentist's best counter-move (hypothetical frequentism, reference class fiddling, whatever you think is strongest) and honestly price what it costs them.

One more thing: don't rig the scenario so the frequentist looks like an idiot. I want listeners to feel the pull of both sides before the case tips them toward propensities. If you cant make the case work under all these constraints, tell me which constraint to relax and why that one's the problem.
