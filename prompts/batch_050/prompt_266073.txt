I've been reading LessWrong and the adjacent blogs for well over a decade, and every so often a thread on language, correspondence, or what propositions even are sends me back to a problem I first met through those discussions: how a sentence manages to be more than a list of its words. "Desdemona loves Cassio" asserts something and hangs together as one thought; the trio Desdemona, loving, Cassio is just three items sitting next to each other. The natural fix is to add a further ingredient, a relation or copula that ties the constituents together, but that runs straight into Bradley's regress: whatever ties them must itself be tied to them, which calls for a further tie, and so on without end. I'd like a poem that takes this problem seriously rather than name-dropping it.

Write me a pantoum about Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. The form should do the philosophical work, not just carry it. In a pantoum, the second and fourth lines of each quatrain return as the first and third lines of the next, so every line is bound into the poem only by being bound again, and each binding has to be performed anew, which is exactly the structure of the regress: every relation invoked to unify the terms itself stands in need of a further relation to relate it to them. Let the repeated lines shift in meaning or grammatical role on each return, the way the alleged relating relation keeps changing what it is doing the moment you examine it. The closing quatrain of a pantoum brings the opening lines back and seals the circle; use that closure to enact whichever resolution you commit to, whether the thought that the regress was benign all along because the proposition's unity is not an extra constituent needing glue, or Bradley's own darker conclusion that the whole holds together in a way no chain of relations ever could. Commit to one and make the form's final loop carry it. Four to six quatrains is plenty. Someone who knows the problem should be able to see why it demanded a pantoum rather than a sonnet or free verse; someone who doesn't should still get a good poem about repetition and return.

Please give me the poem alone as continuous verse: no title, no headings, no bold text, and no explanation or commentary before or after it, just the stanzas themselves.
