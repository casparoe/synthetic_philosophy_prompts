I have no academic background, but I have been thinking about a pattern I call copycat concepts: a familiar term is deliberately carried into a new setting, where it keeps some tests for applying the term while quietly dropping others. My microdomain is workplace “friendship”: companies encourage employees to treat colleagues as friends, retaining expectations of loyalty and informality while discarding free choice, equality, and the right to walk away. Please write, in American English, the desiderata for an adequate theory of this kind of conceptual slippage. Say which cases it must distinguish—metaphor, euphemism, category extension, manipulation, and genuine change in meaning; which judgments it must preserve about consent, deception, and responsibility; and what evidence would show that a copied concept has become a stable new sense rather than merely a borrowed label. Flag any desiderata that may conflict, and end by assessing whether an employer’s appeal to “workplace friendship” is innocent enrichment, misleading framing, or something else.
