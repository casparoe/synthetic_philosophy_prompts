I'm putting together a unit on the epistemology of conspiracy theories for an undergrad phil of science course and I want to build it around The Truman Show instead of the usual Matrix skepticism thing. Read the film through the main fault lines in that debate, but describe the positions rather than name-dropping papers -- e.g., whether conspiracy theories are epistemically defective as a class or have to be judged case by case, the idea that conspiracy believers are stuck in a closed informational loop that can't correct itself from the inside, and the self-sealing problem where the absence of evidence gets absorbed as further proof of the cover-up.

Then the actual work: does Truman reason like a conspiracy theorist, or crucially differently? His evidence is anomalous data -- a stage light falling from the sky, rain falling only on him, the car radio narrating his route, the same pedestrians looping the block. At what point does the pile-up of anomalies license one global deception hypothesis over a bunch of boring local explanations, and is there any non-question-begging test he could run from inside Seahaven? Also the ending: his exit means walking through a black door into total darkness, choosing Sylvia's word over the voice from the sky -- and the maddening thing is that Christof, a proven liar, is also the only person who actually knows what's out there. Is Truman's exit epistemically rational or just existentially compelled? And does the film cheat by giving the audience the control-room view, i.e. certainty the conspiracy is real, which no actual conspiracy believer ever gets? Where does the film resist the reading -- real conspiracies leak, Seahaven doesn't -- and what, if anything, does it show that the journal articles cant?
