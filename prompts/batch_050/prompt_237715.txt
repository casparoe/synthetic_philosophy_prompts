I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm working on a long piece treating the 2023 Hollywood writers' strike as a test case for the so-called Fragment on Machines in Marx's Grundrisse — the passage where he sketches the general intellect, in which a society's accumulated knowledge is absorbed directly into machinery and living labour dwindles toward a supervisory role. The strike is usually written up as a pay dispute with an AI clause bolted on; I want the harder reading. Map the episode onto the Fragment: in what sense is a large language model trained on a century of screenwriting a plausible candidate for the general intellect fixed in machinery, and what did the writers' actual AI demands — limits on training uses, on machine-generated material, on credit — amount to in Marx's categories: a struggle over control of dead labour, over the extraction of surplus value, or over something his framework does not reach? Then run it the other way. The writers were not displaced wholesale but re-priced; the machine in question is trained on the very labour it threatens, which may invert rather than confirm the Fragment's trajectory; and the dispute ended in negotiated concessions, not expropriation. Conclude with a verdict on whether the episode strengthens or embarrasses the general-intellect reading of Marx, separating what is settled among people who work on this material from what remains genuinely contested — including the contested status of the Fragment itself within Marx's corpus — and not presenting any contested interpretation as the scholarly consensus.
