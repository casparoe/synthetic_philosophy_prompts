I've been trying to write down my considered position on fakes, forgeries, and the value of originals, and before I commit to it I want to know whether the pieces actually fit together. Here is where I've landed:

(a) The value of a visual artwork consists in the aesthetic experiences it affords a suitably attentive viewer — in what it looks like and what looking at it reveals, not in facts about who made it or when, since those facts cannot be seen in the work itself.

(b) When Han van Meegeren's supposed Vermeers were exposed as forgeries, they rightly collapsed in standing, and any similar exposure of a work as inauthentic rightly lowers its value; authenticity matters, and it is not snobbery to say so.

(c) A restoration that repaints most of a fresco's surface, if done with historical fidelity and continuous with what remains, leaves us with the original work, whose value survives the restoration intact.

(d) The best explanation of what is objectionable about forgery is the deception involved — it is the lie that offends, not the object itself.

(e) Two perceptually indistinguishable works, such as a masterpiece and a flawless replica of it, are aesthetically equivalent, even if their monetary and historical values differ.

My specific worry is that some of these are each individually defensible — (a) and (e) seem to come as a package, and (b) and (c) both seem like things any sensible person accepts — but that the usual motivations for some of them are quietly incompatible. I also suspect at least one apparent tension among them is merely verbal and dissolves once the relevant distinction is drawn, and I don't want a false alarm flagged as a real problem. I have no settled view about which commitment I would surrender, so please don't assume one.

Please respond with exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: item 1 should be your overall verdict on whether the set is jointly tenable; items 2 through 4 should present the tensions in order from most severe to least severe (including the merely apparent one if there is one, clearly labelled as such, placed wherever its severity warrants), each stating what would have to give; and item 5 should name the single revision that would restore consistency at the lowest cost and state what that revision costs.
