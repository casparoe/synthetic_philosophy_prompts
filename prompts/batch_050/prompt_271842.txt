My position rests on an analogy I've leaned on heavily: that visualizing something is like consulting a picture stored in the head. What convinced me were things I noticed in myself. When I picture my childhood home and count the windows, it takes me time, and the windows that are "farther apart" in the image take longer to move between, as if I were scanning something laid out in space. When I imagine rotating a shape to see whether it would fit through a doorframe, the rotation seems to take longer the farther I turn it. I concluded that the mind must preserve geometry the way a photograph or map does, and that this is why techniques like memory palaces work: you're literally placing things in a mental space and later inspecting the layout.

My friend says the whole picture metaphor is a confusion, that "picturing" is just knowing facts in a vivid way, and that a picture in the head would need someone inside to look at it. And two of my own observations have made me doubt myself: I once tried to mentally hold an ambiguous drawing (the one that can be seen as a duck or a rabbit) and found I couldn't make it flip the way the paper version does, and I've noticed that I can't imagine "a man" in general without imagining him facing some particular direction and wearing something specific, whereas I can simply *know* that a man is in the next room without knowing any of that.
