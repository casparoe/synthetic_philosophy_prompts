A friend and I spent the entire evening arguing about the so-called paradox of blackmail: it can be perfectly legal for me to reveal your embarrassing secret, and perfectly legal for me to ask you for money, yet offering to stay silent in exchange for payment is a serious crime. My friend thinks the prohibition is just incoherent moral luck, while I suspect there is a principled rationale neither of us could articulate. Please describe the most important implications of this paradox for how one should distinguish, in law and in everyday ethics, between a coercive threat and mere hard bargaining — for instance, how it bears on practices like demanding payment to suppress damaging information, or telling an employer you will quit unless given a raise. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
