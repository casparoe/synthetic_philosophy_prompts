I help run the socials for a standup open mic night and, long story, after a very drunken 2am argument about why people laugh I got it into my head to make a quiz: "which theory of humour do you secretly believe?" I want to put it on our instagram as one of those carousel things where you note down your answers letter by letter and then check the scoring key at the end. So: write me a sorting quiz, 9 or 10 questions, where each question is a concrete case of something funny and the answer options are different candidate explanations of where the humour comes from. Cases could be things like slipping on a banana peel, a pun, tickling, laughing when you're nervous in a job interview, a roast where the comic punches up vs the same joke punching down, dark jokes told at a funeral by the deceased's closest friend vs by a stranger, laughing at your OWN joke, and why a joke stops being funny the third time you hear it. Whatever you think sorts best.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto four or five named positions: superiority theory, incongruity theory (feel free to split off the version where the incongruity has to get resolved, since I suspect those genuinely diverge on puns), relief theory, and benign violation theory. Here's the catch though: no question may mention any theory, theorist, or technical term. Everything has to be answerable by someone who just knows what they find funny. Each option within a question should read like a natural thing a person would say ("you laugh because...", "it's funny because..."), and the scoring key needs to show which option feeds which theory, ideally with a worked example of how a mixed answer pattern gets sorted — e.g., what to do with someone who splits 4/3/3.

Also, the whole point is that the quiz should expose where these theories actually come apart, so please add a short note at the end flagging (a) any case where two theories predict the same response and the quiz therefore can't separate them — I'm guessing superiority and benign violation collide on status jokes, and incongruity vs relief might be hard to pull apart on nervous laughter — and (b) any combo of answers that no actual theory occupies, and whether that's because the position would be incoherent or just weirdly undefended. My drunk friend insisted every theory is secretly superiority theory in a trench coat, and I want the quiz to be able to prove him wrong, or at least show exactly which answers he'd have to give to be right.
