write an essay about whether, judged by Marx's own theory, large language models are the realization of what he called the "general intellect." background you can take as given: in the Grundrisse there's a famous stretch (often called the Fragment on Machines) where Marx sketches a stage of capitalism where production depends less and less on anyone's direct labor time and more on society's accumulated knowledge being embodied in the machinery itself. also take as given his value theory from Capital: machines can only transfer their own value into the product, they can't create *new* value — only living labor does that, which is why fully automating everything would undercut the source of profit.

now the wrinkle i want the essay to wrestle with: an LLM looks a lot like the general intellect made literal — it's basically the distilled textual output of humanity compressed into fixed capital, and it churns out what looks suspiciously like productive labor without any living laborer in the loop at inference time. so which is it? (a) the model is just another machine in Marx's sense: impressive, but it only passes along the value of the labor that went into training it (annotators, RLHF contractors, the unpaid labor of everyone who ever posted online), and its outputs are valueless in his strict sense; or (b) the labor theory of value actually breaks here, because the marginal cost of the "labor-like" output is near zero and the whole value-creation story can't be told anymore; or (c) some third option where the real living labor is hidden somewhere else (the users? the data center techs? the ongoing retraining?) and the surplus is just better concealed than it was in the factory.

weigh these seriously, stealman the ones you reject, and reach an actual verdict rather than a "its complicated." at the end, say what would change your mind: which empirical fact about how these models are built or used, or which theoretical argument about value, would push you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning feels shakiest.
