I help run the fundraising committee for our town's fall fair, and I have to write up a recommendation we vote on next week. A guy who designs board games wants to run a coin-flip booth and its turned into a whole thing. The game: you pay an entry fee, then flip a coin til it lands heads. If it takes 1 flip you win $2. 2 flips, you pay $2 (on top of the fee). 3 flips you win $2.67, 4 flips you pay $4, 5 flips you win $6.40, and so on — each extra flip the amount grows but it keeps alternating between winning and paying. He swears the math averages out to a lousy 69 cents per play, so a $1 entry fee nets the fair a steady 31 cents a game. But our treasurer, who's an actual accountant, says she can get basically any number she wants for the "average" depending on the order you add up all the possible outcomes — huge positive, huge negative, whatever — which means the game just doesn't have an average. He's accusing her of flunking math, she says the booth is an uninsurable liability and we're one bad streak away from the fair owing someone ten grand.

Write the memo I'll circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete call — host the booth as proposed, host it with changes (say exactly what changes), or reject it outright — with reasoning a room of non-math people can follow. And take the game designer's side seriously somewhere in there, because I honestly can't tell if "this game has no average payout" is a real reason to ban it or just a technicality, and if 69 cents really is the answer then maybe the fee is actually unfair to players and we've got it backwards.
