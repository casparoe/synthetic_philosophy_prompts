I am a PhD candidate in religious studies, and my dissertation includes a chapter on Dōgen's treatment of being-time (uji) in the Shōbōgenzō. Before I circulate the chapter to my committee, I want a consistency audit of the interpretive commitments I have built the argument on. For the purposes of this exercise, take my readings of the text as given: I am asking about the internal coherence of the position, not about philology or about whether I have Dōgen right.

My commitments:

(1) Time is not a container. Time is not a dimension, medium, or flow within which events occur. Each existent, standing in its own dharma-position, simply is a time: pines are time, bamboos are time, the self arraying itself as the world is time. "Being-time" names an identity, not a relation between two things.

(2) Each time is sealed and complete. Every being-time is exhaustive in its position. No moment carries over into another, and nothing persists through the sequence of moments. There is no substrate — no continuing self, no conserved stuff — that travels from past through present to future.

(3) Passage is real and multi-directional. Passage (kyōryaku) is not a one-way track. On my reading, Dōgen has passage running from yesterday to today but also from today to yesterday, and even from a day to itself. I take this at face value rather than as ornament.

(4) Nothing is lost. Because every time stands fast in its position, the past does not drain away into unreality. My own past exertions, and the lives of the ancient masters, abide fully actual as their own times.

(5) Impermanence is total. Dōgen's teaching radicalizes impermanence rather than softening it. Nothing whatsoever endures, and any reading on which being-time becomes a storehouse of permanent contents is an eternalist corruption of the text.

(6) Practice accomplishes something. There is a genuine difference between the deluded person and the awakened person, and sustained training brings about that difference. This is why it matters whether one lives one's time as being-time or squanders it.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs or triples are in genuine tension, which only appear to be and why the appearance dissolves, and how severe each genuine tension is. For each genuine tension, state precisely what would have to give and what that revision would cost the overall interpretation. Then tell me which single revision would restore the most coherence for the least loss. Do not flag everything as problematic: I want discrimination between real problems and merely surprising conjunctions, ranked by severity rather than listed as equally bad.

After your analysis, give me a structured summary as a JSON object with the following schema:

- "overall_verdict": one of "consistent", "consistent_with_revision", or "inconsistent"
- "tensions": an array, ordered from most to least severe, of objects each with the fields:
  - "commitments": an array of the commitment numbers involved (integers 1–6)
  - "status": "genuine" or "apparent"
  - "severity": "high", "medium", or "low"
  - "diagnosis": one or two sentences on the source of the tension
  - "cheapest_fix": one sentence on the least costly revision (for apparent tensions, state what dissolves them instead)
  - "cost": one sentence on what the fix sacrifices
- "single_best_revision": a string identifying the one change that cleans up the most
- "residual_risks": an array of strings naming any tensions your proposed revision leaves unresolved
