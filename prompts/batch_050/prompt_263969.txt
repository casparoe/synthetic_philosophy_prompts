I work as the lone archivist at a small county historical society and I process a couple of donated collections a month -- family papers, business ledgers, that kind of thing. I want a working procedure, like a checklist I can tape above my desk, for handling the silences in each collection: the people who show up only in someone else's records (a cook named once in a diary, laborers listed as wages, enslaved people listed as property), and the people who never show up at all but clearly were there. Ground it in whatever the field actually knows about archives and power -- who did the recording, what the records were made for, why some voices survive and others don't -- but the output has to be steps: what to check during appraisal, what to put in the finding aid, when it's worth flagging a gap to researchers vs. when that flag itself distorts things. The part I most need is a test for the line between describing a silence honestly and just inventing a story to fill it, because I see colleagues cross that line alot. Run the procedure on two or three example collections, including one where following it produces a decision funders or the donor's family will hate, and mark the points where no procedure will save me from a judgment call.
