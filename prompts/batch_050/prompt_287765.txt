I chair the deployment review committee of a mid-sized AI lab. Every two weeks, product teams bring us release requests, each accompanied by an evaluation dossier: benchmark scores, behavioral evals, and a red-team report. My committee's recurring failure mode is that we have no shared standard for how to weigh these documents against each other, and I trace this to a conceptual confusion we keep re-enacting: the difference between evidence about a system's average-case behavior and evidence about its worst-case behavior, and what kind of assurance each can and cannot support.

I want you to turn that distinction into a sign-off procedure my committee can actually run. Assume generalist reviewers, about ninety minutes of deliberation per dossier, and decision authority up to but not including models with qualitatively new capabilities (those escalate regardless). The procedure should be a sequence of questions and tests we apply to every dossier in order. At minimum it must tell us how to handle the following recurring situations, which I list in the order they tend to arise:

- A dossier leading with strong aggregate benchmark results and pass rates. What, if anything, may we conclude from these about worst-case behavior, and how should the procedure keep the room from treating high averages as reassuring?
- A red-team report that found exploits. We need a way to assess severity that does not collapse into counting successful attacks, given that the number found depends on the search budget, the red team's skill, and the attack surface they happened to probe.
- A red-team report that found nothing. Under what conditions is an empty findings list evidence, how should the reported elicitation effort and scaffolding quality affect the weight we give it, and how do we prevent the asymmetry where finding an exploit is damning but failing to find one is meaningless?
- Cases where the plausible failure is frequent but mild versus rare but catastrophic, and how the procedure should treat these differently rather than averaging them together.
- The gap between the evaluation distribution and the deployment distribution, including users with adversarial intent, which the eval designers never modeled.

Apply the finished procedure to three dossiers: (1) a model with excellent average scores whose red team, on a small budget, found a reliable jailbreak within a day; (2) a model with mediocre averages but no exploits found under an unusually large and well-resourced red-teaming effort; (3) a flagship release whose dossier looks strong by every metric we currently collect, where the correct output should nonetheless be a decision the product team will hate — show me the procedure producing that outcome without special pleading.

Finally, mark explicitly which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and state plainly what this procedure cannot deliver: I suspect it cannot produce anything deserving the name guarantee, and I want the document to say so rather than let the committee slide into believing otherwise.
