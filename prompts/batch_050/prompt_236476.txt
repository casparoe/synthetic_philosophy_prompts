Write a sestina on the question of whether an ant colony can have a mind. The six recurring end-words should function like signals passed between individual ants — pheromone traces, encounters, bits of food — so that each line stays at the scale of a single ant's experience while the stanzas, through recombination of those same six words, make something legible at the colony's scale: a decision, a memory, a want. The envoi should be the one place where a colony-level voice speaks, and it should be ambiguous whether that voice is a genuine subject reporting its thought or merely the pattern the reader has been trained to hear. A reader who knows the ant-colony argument for emergent minds should find the form itself making the case; a reader who doesn't should still find it a good poem.
