So I just spent like four hours at the pub arguing with my friend about the doomsday argument and I'm still annoyed about it. For anyone not in the weeds on this: the idea is roughly that if humanity were going to go extinct soon, your position among all humans who ever live would be unremarkable, whereas if we survive for millions more years you'd be an extremely early human, so the reasoning goes you should update toward doom being sooner. My friend thinks this is just straightforward Bayesian conditioning on your birth rank and that resisting it is special pleading. I think it has to be wrong somewhere, but every time I tried to say where I ended up flailing -- I gestured at the reference class problem (am I sampling from all humans? all observers? all intelligent agents in the universe?) and at the idea that maybe your existence is evidence that the world allows observers at all, and he just kept saying none of that touches the core inference.

The thing that bugs me is that this argument has been around for over forty years now, philosophers and physicists and statisticians have all had a crack at it, and as far as I can tell the literature is exactly as split as it was at the start. Nobody has convinced anybody. So my question isn't "is the doomsday argument sound" -- I want a diagnosis of why this debate never moves. Is the disagreement actually about the math (e.g. whether the self-sampling assumption or the self-indication assumption is the right way to handle observer evidence), in which case someone should be able to settle it formally? Is it a disguised empirical dispute about what the right reference class is? Is it that the two sides mean different things by probability here -- credences versus frequencies? Or is it one of those cases where both sides have a bedrock intuition about whether your own existence can count as evidence at all, and there's nothing underneath the intuitions to argue about?

Given your diagnosis, tell me what would actually count as progress: a theorem, a cleaner formalization, an analogy that dissolves it, an empirical fact, or is this just permanently underdetermined. And if you think the two sides are talking past each other, show me where, using what each side's actual best argument is rather than a strawman of either.
