I'm a journalist covering science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm putting together an interactive sidebar for a feature on the coming wave of private Mars missions. The gimmick is a quiz -- "what kind of space settler are you?" -- where readers click through some scenarios and at the end get told which ethical position on space colonization they actually hold. The editors want it to feel fun, but I want it to do real philosophical sorting work, because in my interviews people constantly say things like "I'm pro-exploration" or "I think we should be careful" and then, when you push on actual cases, turn out to hold wildly different views under those labels. So: write me the quiz.

Specs: 7 to 9 questions, each one a concrete scenario rather than an abstract principle, with two or three answer options each. Some cases I definitely want used in some form: dormant microbes found under the Martian ice; a mining consortium wanting to strip a geologically unique but totally barren asteroid; a terraforming proposal that would take millennia and permanently erase the planet's original surface; a settler who wants to introduce Earth life to a world we've confirmed is sterile; and a choice between spending the money on a Mars colony versus on problems here on Earth. The quiz should sort readers into at least these positions: (a) full-speed-ahead anthropocentrism -- dead rock has no value, only human interests (present and future) count; (b) settle but don't transform -- domes, habitats, and mining are fine, but large-scale terraforming crosses a line; (c) preservationism -- pristine worlds have value even if there's no life and nobody ever sees them, so hands off; (d) biocentric priority -- if even microbial native life exists, we acquire serious duties toward it that constrain or forbid settlement; and (e) astronomical-stakes consequentialism -- the real moral weight is the trillions of possible future people, so we should expand as fast as safely possible and worries about scenery are sentimental. Include a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions, and flag explicitly any pair of positions your questions can't reliably tell apart rather than fudging it. Also, if you notice answer combinations a reader could produce that don't correspond to any coherent view, say so and diagnose briefly whether that combo is incoherent or just a position nobody bothers to hold. No question should ask anything like "do you think nature has intrinsic value?" -- sort people by what they'd do, not by which label sounds nice.
