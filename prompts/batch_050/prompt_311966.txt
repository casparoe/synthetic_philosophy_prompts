I'm taking a seminar on game theory and rational agency and we just did the unit on commitment devices. For my term paper I want to defend a claim that my prof was pretty skeptical about, so I want to pressure-test it before I commit to it (pun intended).

Quick setup as it was presented to us: two agents are about to play some strategic game, but each one also has the ability to make a credibly observable commitment to a strategy beforehand (classic cases like burning your ships, or an AI system publishing its source code with a provably fixed policy). Whoever commits first gets to force the other side into the role of best-responder, so both sides have an incentive to commit as early as possible — a "commitment race". And this can leave everyone worse off than if they'd just played the game, because in the rush you commit hastily, before checking whether your commitment is actually a good one, and opportunities for coordination get skipped over.

The claim I want to argue for: a commitment race can't be stably defused by agreement. If both agents try to settle things by committing not to race (like "we both commit to only make commitments after some date T, once we've thought it through"), that agreement is itself just another commitment — so the race doesn't go away, it just moves up a level. Now each side has an incentive to be the first to bind the other to the disarmament deal, or to secretly defect from it, and the whole verification/enforcement problem recurses at the meta-level. And there's no obvious place for the regress to bottom out.

Please argue that this claim is correct. Format it like this:

- Setup in your own words (~100 words)
- The main argument (~300 words)
- The strongest objection you'd expect (~150 words)
- Response to it (~150 words)
- Bottom line: does the claim hold as stated or does it need weakening? (~100 words)

Don't just rehash the standard points about commitment being valuable — the paper has to be specifically about whether the regress can be stopped, so keep it focused on that.
