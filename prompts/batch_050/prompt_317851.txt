Two friends of mine took a class on Chinese philosophy and have been fighting about Gongsun Long's white horse thing for a week now. For context, neither reads Chinese, they just read a translation and some secondary stuff.

Marta's view: it's a clever sophism and nothing more. It works, she says, because classical Chinese doesn't mark the difference between identity and class-membership, so "a white horse is not a horse" sounds deep, but once you disambiguate it either means "the class of white horses is not identical to the class of horses" (true but trivial, nobody denied it) or "white horses aren't horses" (false, done). Either way the paradox evaporates, and she thinks the fact that later Mohists bothered to refute related paradoxes with stuff like "riding a white horse is riding a horse" shows people at the time basically saw through it.

Dave's reply: the dialogue actually contains arguments, and the disambiguation move ignores them. Gongsun Long argues that "horse" names a shape and "white" names a color, so the compound term can't just be the same thing as "horse"; and he argues that if you send for a horse, a yellow or black one will do, but send for a white horse and they won't. That's a point about how the terms behave, not a trick with missing articles. He also said the Mohist "refutation" just asserts what Gongsun Long is questioning, so it begs the question.

Marta came back with: fine, then Dave has conceded her point, because once you grant the arguments are about the *concepts* or *meanings* being different, the thesis is just intensionality — of course the concept white horse isn't the concept horse — and the only reason anyone remembers the dialogue is the scandalous-sounding class reading, which is false. So it's either trivial or false, which is what she said on day one.

Dave's counter: that "trivial" point apparently had to be discovered, since the Mohists and a lot of modern readers keep sliding between the readings — which is exactly the confusion the dialogue exposes, so calling it a mere sophism gets the direction backwards.

They want me to pick a winner and I keep flip-flopping. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what — and if there's something both of them are missing about what the thesis could even mean, say that too?
