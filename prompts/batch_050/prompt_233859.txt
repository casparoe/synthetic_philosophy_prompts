I run an informal philosophy reading group (mostly grad students from other departments plus a couple of stubborn lawyers) and we got completely stuck on causation last month, so I want to settle what I actually think before I go back to them. I'll just give you my gut verdicts on cases and you tell me what theory I hold, if any. Suzy and Billy both throw rocks at a bottle, Suzy's gets there first and shatters it: her throw caused the shattering, Billy's didn't, end of story. Two assassins shoot the victim at the same instant, either bullet sufficient: I say both shots are causes, full stop. But then the one that keeps me up: a major and a sergeant both shout "charge," the soldiers would have charged for either order alone, and yet I want to say the major's order caused the charge and the sergeant's didn't. I realize this is in tension with the assassin case, since structurally they look identical -- two independently sufficient events, one outcome -- so either my verdict on one of them is wrong or the principle has to care about something besides sufficiency structure (command hierarchies? laws of a different kind than physics?), and I can't tell which. Then omissions: the gardener who never waters the plant while we're on vacation caused its death, but I don't think my neighbor three doors down, who also didn't water it, caused anything, even though both "interventions" would have saved it. And double prevention: the bodyguard who tackles Billy before his rock hits Suzy's rock and deflects it -- I grudgingly count that as a cause of the bottle shattering, but it feels like causation at a distance in a way the rock cases don't, and I'm not sure the grudge is doing real work or is just squeamishness I should drop.

So: is there one account -- counterfactual-dependence-flavored, interventionist-flavored, some hybrid, I don't care -- that delivers all five of these verdicts, or do I have to give one up, and if so which is the cheapest sacrifice and why that one rather than the assassins? My own tentative repair is something like "a cause is whatever makes a difference, or would have made a difference once you hold the redundant backups fixed in whatever way the laws say they'd be held," but the gardener/neighbor case seems to break it unless I smuggle in some notion of what's supposed to happen, and I'd like that made explicit rather than smuggled. Finally, whatever account you land me with, tell me what it predicts about a case I haven't listed: a switch is set to track A, the train takes track A and arrives on time, but had the switch been set to B the train would've taken B and arrived on time anyway. Did the switch setting cause the on-time arrival? I honestly don't know what I think there, which is probably diagnostic of something.
