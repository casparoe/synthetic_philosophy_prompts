I'm in a reading group on metaphysics of science and last week we did finks and masks (the Martin electro-fink stuff, the glass wrapped in packing foam, all of that). Afterwards one of the others emailed around this argument she says fell out of the discussion, and it's been bugging me because the conclusion is obviously nuts but each step looked fine when we went through it. Here it is, roughly as she wrote it:

(1) To say the glass is fragile is just to say a conditional: if it were struck, it would break (at least in suitable conditions). (2) But whenever a fragile glass is struck and fails to break, we can always say the disposition was masked -- foam, a careful sorcerer, a safety net, whatever. And whenever a non-fragile thing like a brick shatters under some weird setup, we can say its sturdiness was masked or finked away. (3) So "fragile" is compatible with breaking and with not breaking, in every possible circumstance; same goes for "soluble," "flammable," "conductive," any disposition term. (4) A predicate compatible with every possible outcome is unfalsifiable and hence tells us nothing about the world. (5) So disposition terms are empty: there are no dispositions, only things actually happening or not. (6) And since huge chunks of physics and chemistry are stated dispositionally (charge, valence, mass as resistance to acceleration), those aren't real properties either, just bookkeeping.

She openly admits (6) proves too much, and I agree, but the annoying thing is I can't say where it derails. (2) does seem like exactly the move we all made when discussing finks -- masks are always *available* as an explanation, that's the whole lesson. And if I patch (1) by adding "as long as no mask is present," the conditional goes circular or trivial, since "no mask" starts to mean "nothing preventing the breaking." My own instinct is that the slide happens somewhere between (3) and (4) -- something about masks being independently detectable rather than just "whatever stopped the manifestation" -- but when I tried to say that in the group I got pushback that in principle a mask could be undetectable (the sorcerer leaves no trace). So: which step do I reject, why did it look fine, and what do the standard repairs (better conditional analyses, causal-basis accounts, just biting a realist bullet about powers) each have to give up to reject it? If forced I'd rather keep something like (1) than (5), but I want to know the price tag.
