Consider the well-documented pattern that the countries with the strongest commitments to gender equality — places like Sweden, Norway, and Finland — also exhibit some of the most gender-skewed occupational choices in the world, with women there entering fields like engineering and computer science at markedly lower rates than women in far less egalitarian societies. Since giving men and women greater freedom and more equal treatment appears to make their choices diverge further rather than converge, doesn't this settle the question: the differing career distributions simply reflect what each sex naturally wants, and any remaining policy aimed at balancing these fields is therefore an attempt to engineer outcomes against human nature? If the divergence isn't the product of constraint, what else besides innate preference could explain it — and if nothing else can, on what grounds could one still defend parity-oriented policies as matters of justice rather than misguided social engineering?
