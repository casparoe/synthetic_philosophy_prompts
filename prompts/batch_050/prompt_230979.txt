A student asked me last week what Popper would make of the multiverse stuff in cosmology, and I realized I genuinely don't know. His track record as I read it: Marxist history as its later defenders practiced it, and Freudian and Adlerian psychology, fail because they can accomodate any outcome whatsoever; general relativity is the gold standard because it bet everything on risky predictions; Darwinism he classified as a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable theory, though a valuable one, and then near the end of his life he said he'd changed his mind about that; and he went after the Copenhagen interpretation hammer and tongs while never doubting quantum mechanics itself is science. So what rule is he actually running -- risky predictions, or whether a framework can spawn testable theories, or some theory-vs-interpretation line? Give me your best guess at his verdict on the inflationary multiverse, where some people claim indirect evidence from the CMB and critics say it's untestable in principle, and -- this is the part I really care about -- describe a hypothetical tweak to the case where your candidate rules would predict different verdicts, so I'd know which rule was the right one.
