Write an Onion-style news article, headline plus 450–550 words, reporting that a family court has been asked to settle the status of the number 2 after two rival set-theoretic households produced conflicting accounts of its personal life: in the Zermelo coding (where each number is the singleton of its predecessor) 2 is an element of 3 but not of 4, while in the von Neumann coding (where each number is the set of all its predecessors) 2 is an element of 4, and indeed of every number from 3 onward. The presiding judge, a structuralist, dismisses the case on the grounds that there is no fact of the matter about which set the number 2 really is — there are only facts about the position it occupies in the progression — and the comedy should come from the deadpan fallout: the number 2's bewilderment at being told that its questions about its own identity are ill-posed, a Platonist bystander insisting that 2 must be something in itself and not merely a role with interchangeable occupants, a category theorist reassuring reporters that 2 is pinned down up to isomorphism and that isomorphic lookalikes are for all mathematical purposes the same individual, and an ante rem structuralist objecting that the position itself is a perfectly respectable abstract object even if everything said about its officeholders is mere gossip. Include at least one brief quote from the number 2 itself. Keep the piece strictly deadpan, with no winking or meta-commentary, and make sure every mathematical and philosophical claim in it — the membership facts under both codings, the content of each position — is one a specialist would confirm rather than wince at.
