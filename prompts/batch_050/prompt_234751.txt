So my friend Maya (we met in the LW meetup scene years ago, shes the one who got me to actually read the Sequences instead of just nodding along) has been on a big Stoicism kick for the last year. Started with the modern revival stuff, William Irvine's A Guide to the Good Life and the usual Daily Stoic-flavored content, and then actually went back and read Epictetus's Enchiridion, which she now won't shut up about. Her favorite bit is chapter 19, where Epictetus says (paraphrasing here) that you can make yourself invincible simply by refusing to enter any contest where winning isn't in your own power. She's turned this into a party game where she hands out "up to you" / "not up to you" verdicts on whatever life problem someone brings up.

Her verdicts so far, as best as I can reconstruct them:

- Landlord raising your rent: not up to you.
- The rent hike wrecking your whole evening: up to you.
- Getting the job offer after interviewing: not up to you.
- Having lied in that interview: up to you.
- Whether you still stew over an embarrassing thing you did six years ago: up to you.
- Whether your novel gets published: not up to you.

Here's the new case I want to spring on her. Maya herself is writing a novel, and she keeps setting and missing deadlines. Is "writing 300 words every morning for the next six months" up to her, in her own system? What about "finishing the first draft by December"?

I genuinely can't predict her, and I think it's because her verdicts so far are consistent with at least two different rules. There's the strict Epictetus reading where only your inner assents and judgments are up to you and literally anything routed through your body or the world isn't, which I think is what the chapter 19 stuff actually commits her to. And then there's the Irvine-style trichotomy thing where effort and execution are a third "partially up to you" bucket and you're supposed to internalize your goals so the daily writing becomes the real target, which honestly sounds a lot like CFAR-style goal factoring wearing a toga. These two rules give opposite answers on her writing habit, defintely on the six-month streak version.

So: predict her verdict on the two writing cases, tell me which rule you think she's actually been running and why, which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog to the new case, and if her track record genuinely underdetermines it, what additional verdict of hers would best tell the two rules apart. Bonus points if you think one of the rules is the better reading of the chapter 19 passage on its own terms, since that's the text she'd actually defer to.
