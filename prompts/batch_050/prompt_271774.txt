In 2013, the city of Camden, New Jersey, dissolved its municipal police department outright: every officer was laid off, the existing union contract was voided, and policing was taken over by a newly created county force to which former officers had to reapply, many at lower pay and under new work rules. The rebuilt force was larger, cheaper per officer, and formally committed to community-policing principles, and in the following years reported crime in Camden fell substantially. Advocates across the spectrum have since claimed the episode: reformers cite it as proof that departments can be rebuilt; defunders note that the old department was, literally, abolished; skeptics note that total officers increased and that no coercive function was ever removed from the state.

Analyze this episode through the lens of Max Weber's claim that the state is defined by its monopoly on legitimate violence, supplemented by a distinction I want you to take seriously even though it is my own coinage: between institutional abolition (dissolving the particular organization that wields force) and functional abolition (eliminating or transferring the coercive function itself). I want the analysis to run in both directions. First, the mapping: by the lights of the Weberian picture, what actually changed hands in Camden, and what did not? Is there a sense in which dissolving the unionized department reasserted the state's monopoly against a rival claimant, namely the union's contractual grip on how coercion could be exercised? On my distinction, was this institutional abolition in the service of preserving — even expanding — the function, and does that make the defund-and-abolish movement's embrace of the case a category error, or does it show that institutional abolition is the only realistic instrument the movement has? Second, the stress test: what does the Weberian lens fail to see in this episode — consider at least whether it can distinguish legitimate from merely successful violence, whether it has anything to say about why residents' relationship to the new force changed (if it did), and whether the drop in crime can be credited to the organizational rupture at all rather than to increased headcount and spending. Conclude with a verdict: does Camden confirm the Weberian framework, expose a gap in it, or show that the reform/defund/abolish debate is argued at the wrong level of abstraction entirely? Where the theory is silent, say so rather than forcing it to speak.
