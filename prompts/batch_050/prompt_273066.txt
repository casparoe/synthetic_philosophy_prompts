I am a retired molecular biologist who has recently taken up philosophy, and I keep finding the dispute between naturalism and normativism about health and disease slippery: both sides seem to claim the same evidence. Please act as a philosopher of medicine experienced in mediating adversarial collaborations, and think carefully before writing. Begin with a short note on which candidate points of agreement you considered and rejected as vacuous or slanted. Then draft the joint statement that a committed naturalist — one who takes disease to be a departure from statistically normal functioning of the organism, specifiable without value judgments — and a committed normativist — one who holds that nothing counts as a disease unless it is bad for its bearer — could both sign. Include: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept, covering hard cases each camp tends to claim as its own, such as sickle-cell trait in malarial regions and the depathologization of homosexuality; (2) a table of the residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes, and for each, the case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance — please include a test case of mine: a mutation causing mild chronic pain becomes fixed in the human population, so that pain is now statistically normal. Close by stating how confident you are in your portrayal of each side, and name the single formulation you are least sure of.
