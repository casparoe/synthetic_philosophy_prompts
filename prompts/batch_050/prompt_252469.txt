I direct a small philosophy of mind reading group and am putting together next term's syllabus around Chalmers's meta-problem of consciousness -- roughly, the problem of explaining why we judge, report, and behave as if we are conscious, treated as a problem in its own right and at least in principle separable from the 'hard problem' of explaining consciousness itself. One of my stronger doctoral students is about to choose a dissertation topic, and she has perhaps three years and no lab, only armchair methods plus whatever experimental work she can borrow from collaborators. Rather than hand her a survey, I would like to give her a defended agenda. So: among the open questions in this area, which two or three are most worth a dissertation-length investment, and in what order? Candidates I have in mind include: whether an adequate solution to the meta-problem would dissolve, or merely bracket, the hard problem; whether 'phenomenal judgement' can be given a functional characterization determinate enough for illusionism and realism to actually disagree about it; how the introspective-reliability debate bears on the evidential status of our consciousness judgements; and whether analogies with metaethical error theory genuinely illuminate illusionism or are merely decorative. Feel free to substitute a question I have omitted if you think it outranks these.

I am less interested in the list itself than in the reasoning behind it. For each question you select, explain: whether it is upstream of other open questions (that is, how many further questions have answers that depend on its resolution, and which ones); how tractable it is for someone restricted to conceptual and argumentative methods rather than empirical ones; how neglected it is relative to its importance, given how much of the existing literature circles the realism-versus-illusionism standoff; and what would change its ranking -- for instance, a theoretical result, or an empirical finding about introspection, that would move it up or down the list. Please also indicate, briefly, which of my candidates you would deprioritize and why (e.g., because settling it would change little downstream, or because it is not tractable with the tools available to a philosopher without a lab).

After your prose discussion, give me the agenda in structured form as a JSON object with the following schema: a top-level field "ranked_questions" containing an array of objects, each with fields "rank" (integer), "question" (one-sentence statement of the open question), "upstream_of" (array of strings naming the questions or debates whose answers depend on this one), "tractability" (one of "high", "medium", "low", judged for a philosopher using conceptual methods over three years), "neglectedness" (one of "high", "medium", "low"), "key_risk" (one sentence on the most likely way a dissertation on this question fails), and "rank_changer" (one sentence on what evidence or result would most change this question's position); plus a top-level field "deprioritized" containing an array of objects with fields "question" and "reason" (one sentence). The JSON should restate your conclusions, not introduce new ones.
