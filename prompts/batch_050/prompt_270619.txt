ok so background: i do freelance translation, mostly certified legal stuff, birth certificates, court docs. over the past year the bottom has fallen out of it — agencies run everything through machine translation now and hire "post-editors" at about a third of what i used to charge, and everytime i hold the line on my rate i watch the job go to someone else who caved. a friend sent me scott alexander's Meditations on Moloch (the slate star codex essay) and i havent stopped thinking about this part:

"Every single citizen hates the system, but for lack of a good coordination mechanism it endures. From a god's-eye-view, we can optimize the system to "everyone agrees to stop doing this at once", but no one within the system is able to effect the transition without great risk to themselves."

i am one of the people inside the system, and i've decided to try being the coordination mechanism. next thursday at our regional translators association meeting (~30 members) i'm proposing a three-part pact: a published minimum rate for certified work, a shared "100% human-translated" label that clients can verify against a member registry, and a mutual agreement that nobody takes post-editing gigs below a set cents-per-word floor. the association has no power to bind anyone, this would be purely voluntary.

the room: about a third are court-sworn translators whose niche is legally protected and who privately think the rest of us are whining. a handful of literary translators who will cheer anything anti-machine but can't actually join a rate floor because their work is all royalties. the rest are agency regulars, the most exposed, and — i assume — the most tempted to quietly defect. plus a treasurer who gets nervous anytime anything sounds like price-fixing, and honestly a couple of people i suspect are already using MT themselves and just not saying so.

prep me for the Q&A. give me the 8 or so objections i'll actually get from THIS room (not generic game theory objections — in the words these specific people would use, e.g. the sworn translators will say the floor is protectionism for people who can't compete), ordered by how likely each is times how much damage it does. for each: a reply i can give in under two minutes, and a note on where the reply is weakest. then: what i should concede up front before anyone asks, which objections have real force that i should treat as fatal to parts of the proposal rather than try to answer, and the one reply that has to land or the whole thing dies. and give me the question i'm praying nobody asks.

last thing, the part i actually want stress-tested: is a voluntary pact between the people getting shocked a "coordination mechanism" in the sense that passage demands, or does the passage basically imply it can only come from outside — a law, a platform, a certifying body with teeth? if the latter, tell me straight, and tell me whether my fallback should be lobbying the national certification board to make the rate floor a condition of using the credential instead.
