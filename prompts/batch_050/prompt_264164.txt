I run a tabletop RPG group and we've hit a weird wall I can't find the right word for. My players will accept basically anything I put in a setting. Floating islands, resurrection, a world where time runs backwards on Tuesdays, a culture where everyone is a total egoist, fine, they roll with it, it's fiction, the DM says so, end of story. But last year I tried running a world where the setting itself, not any character, treats cruelty as genuinely virtuous, like the narration says this is good and the players are meant to go along with that as a fact of the world. They flat out refused. Not "our characters wouldn't believe it", more like "the world can't be that way, even a made-up one". Which is odd, because these same players happily play evil characters who believe cruelty is great, and nobody objects to that at all. So its not the content, it's something about who gets to say it's true. And here's the kicker, when I ran the exact same setting later as an obvious dark comedy, the resistance basically evaporated.

Is there an established name for this pattern? I don't think it's just being squeamish or refusing to suspend disbelief, since the disbelief gets suspended for magic every week. And the fact that framing it as satire fixes it seems like a clue. If this has a literature, how does it carve the difference between accepting something in a characters mouth vs accepting it as a fact the fiction hands you, and does any of it say why genre should matter?
