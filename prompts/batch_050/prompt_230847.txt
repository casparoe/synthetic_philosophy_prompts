I cover physics and the philosophy of science for a general-interest magazine, and I am drafting a long feature on whether modern physics implies that the past and the future are as real as the present — the so-called block universe. Early in the piece I quote this passage from chapter IX, "The Relativity of Simultaneity," of Albert Einstein's Relativity: The Special and General Theory (trans. Robert W. Lawson, 1920):

"Now in reality (considered with reference to the railway embankment) he is hastening towards the beam of light coming from B, whilst he is riding on ahead of the beam of light coming from A. Hence the observer will see the beam of light emitted from B earlier than he will see that emitted from A. Observers who take the railway train as their reference-body must therefore come to the conclusion that the lightning flash B took place earlier than the lightning flash A. We thus arrive at the important result:

Events which are simultaneous with reference to the embankment are not simultaneous with respect to the train, and vice versa (relativity of simultaneity). Every reference-body (co-ordinate system) has its own particular time; unless we are told the reference-body to which the statement of time refers, there is no meaning in a statement of the time of an event."

Two colleagues of mine got into an extended argument about this passage at a story meeting, and I found myself unable to settle it. I would like you to adjudicate.

Marta, who covers fundamental physics, went first. She said the passage is not merely about clocks and signals. Once you accept that simultaneity is relative to a reference frame and that relativity privileges no frame, an event in my future — a choice I will make next year — is present according to the equally valid frame of some passing observer. Unless we are prepared to say that what is present and real for that observer is somehow less real than what is present for us, the future event must already be as real as this one. That, she said, is the core of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and Einstein's paragraph already contains everything the argument needs.

Jonah, an essayist who writes on philosophy, answered that the passage licenses none of this. Everything in it is operational: light rays, midpoints, what an observer will see, what conclusion observers must come to. It tells us how differently moving observers assign time readings, not what exists. The block-universe conclusion requires an additional premise — roughly, that whatever is present for any observer is real, period — and relativity neither states nor requires that premise. Physicists can use frame-dependent time coordinates while remaining agnostic about the metaphysics of time.

Marta replied that agnosticism is not actually available. On Jonah's reading there would be a fact about which events are really present that no physical theory could even state, since stating it would single out a preferred slicing of spacetime and contradict relativity — and a fact inexpressible in our best physics is a fact we should discard. Moreover, the premise Jonah dismisses as metaphysical freight is just the denial that reality varies from observer to observer; rejecting it means treating other frames' presents as second-class, which she called a kind of solipsism.

Jonah came back with two objections. First, the argument ignores causal structure: for pairs of events that no light signal can connect, different frames disagree about temporal order, but nothing physical hinges on the disagreement, so declaring one frame's "now" to be real for everyone adds something gratuitous to the physics. Second, he accused Marta of sliding between "determinate" and "existent": even if the equations fix every event in the sense of constraining it, it does not follow that future events exist now in the way the page in front of us does.

Please go through the exchange move by move and tell me who is right at each step, and why: (1) Marta's claim that the passage, together with the denial of a privileged frame, already delivers the block universe; (2) Jonah's claim that the passage is purely operational and that the block universe needs a metaphysical premise relativity does not supply; (3) Marta's reply that a frame-independent present is inexpressible in physics and that denying equal reality across frames is solipsism; (4) Jonah's two final objections, about causal structure and about the slide from "determinate" to "existent." Then say who is more right overall, and identify anything important that both of them are missing — for instance, whether the whole dispute turns on conflating what a frame makes assignable with what exists, or on some assumption neither party has stated. I want something I could use to write a fair account of the disagreement, on the order of 900 to 1,100 words.
