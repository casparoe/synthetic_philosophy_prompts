A friend quietly grinding on a startup for two years and only mentioning it when asked -- admirable, maybe the most admirable of all. That same friend, if he posted weekly metrics updates about it -- suddenly grating, even though nothing about the achievement changed. An athlete doing an over-the-top touchdown celebration -- fine. A student announcing their A to the whole seminar -- not fine. Someone saying "oh, it was nothing" about something that was clearly a big deal -- sometimes this reads as gracious and sometimes as the most annoying move of all, and I honestly can't tell what flips it. Is there one principle that gets most of these -- something about status claims vs. information sharing, or earned vs. unearned audience, or whether the listener can opt out? Give me the answer as a JSON object with these fields: "principle" (a one- or two-sentence statement of the best candidate), "runner_up" (same, for the second-best), "case_verdicts" (an array of objects, one per case above, with fields "case", "my_verdict", "principle_says", and "saved_or_sacrificed"), "sacrificed_verdicts" (which of my intuitions the principle has to overturn, and why that's a cost worth paying or not), and "group_chat_case" (the principle's verdict on my flip-flop case, in one sentence).
