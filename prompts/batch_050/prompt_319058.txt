I work in causal inference and am fluent in the structural causal model framework -- variables, structural equations, exogenous noise, interventions via do-operators, counterfactuals computed by abduction-action-prediction, and path-specific effects. I want you to restate, as faithfully as you can, the philosophical debate over whether omissions and absences can be genuine causes in the vocabulary I actually use. The debate I have in mind runs roughly as follows: commonsense says the gardener's failure to water the plant caused its death, but then so did my failure to water it, and the Queen of England's, and there are absurdly many such absences; some philosophers conclude omissions are causes only relative to a norm or expectation, others that omissions are not causes at all but figure in causal explanations in some looser way, and others that all of these absences really are causes and the discomfort is merely pragmatic.

Take the main positions in that debate and translate each into claims about structural models: which variables exist, what values they can take, which interventions are well-defined, what counterfactuals come out true, and how (if at all) the notion of a default or expected value enters the formalism. Then, and this is the part I care most about, give me the residue report: which claims in the debate have no counterpart in SCM vocabulary, and for each, whether that is because the framework is missing something a causal theory should have (for instance, no primitive distinction between a variable's default value and a deviant one) or because the philosophical disagreement is less substantive than it appears and dissolves once everything is stated interventionally. Do not smuggle the framework's assumptions in and call them translation; if a position can only be rendered by adding machinery, say what you added.

At the end, give me the residue report as a table with four columns: "Claim" (the philosophical claim about causation by omission, in one sentence), "SCM rendering" (its best translation into structural causal model terms, or "none" if untranslatable), "Status" (one of: clean, distorted, untranslatable), and "Diagnosis" (if distorted or untranslatable: whether the residue reflects a genuine gap in the framework or an unclear original claim, in one or two sentences).
