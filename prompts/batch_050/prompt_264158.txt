I'm putting together a one-page position statement on mental causation for a graduate seminar, and before I circulate it I'd like an audit of whether my commitments are jointly tenable. Here is where I've landed:

(1) Causal closure: every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause at the time of its occurrence.
(2) Mental properties are numerically distinct from neural properties; multiple realizability across species and substrates rules out type-identity.
(3) Mental properties are causally efficacious qua mental: when my desire for coffee causes my reaching for the cup, the desire itself — not merely its neural realizer — is causally relevant to the reaching.
(4) This is not overdetermination: there is one reaching with one causal history, not two independent sufficient causes competing for the same effect.
(5) Properties are individuated by their causal powers: two properties that confer exactly the same causal powers in every possible circumstance are the same property.
(6) The correct test for causal relevance is interventionist: X is a cause of Y only if some possible intervention on X would make a difference to Y.

I suspect there's trouble in here somewhere but I'm not sure where. Please go through the set and identify which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to. For each real tension, tell me how severe it is, what exactly generates it, and what the cheapest revision would be — including cases where the fix is to clarify rather than abandon a claim. If a pair looks inconsistent but the conflict dissolves on closer inspection, say so explicitly rather than flagging it. Order the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally threatening, and finish by telling me which single revision would do the most to stabilize the overall position and what it would cost me.
