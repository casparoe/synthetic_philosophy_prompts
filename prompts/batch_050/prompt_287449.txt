I am a PhD student in philosophy with some background in theoretical computer science, and next month I will present a paper to a mixed seminar of philosophers of mind, normative ethicists, and complexity theorists. My thesis is that computational complexity is not merely an engineering constraint on how minds happen to be implemented, but a genuinely philosophical constraint on what kinds of agency and rationality are possible. More specifically, I argue that if an ethical theory requires an agent to identify the best action by searching a space of possible futures that grows exponentially with morally relevant detail, then that theory cannot straightforwardly serve as a standard for finite agents, even highly idealized ones. At the same time, I reject the stronger claim that worst-case hardness alone refutes optimization-based ethics, since real agents encounter structured rather than arbitrary problems, may use approximation or heuristics, and need only perform well over a relevant distribution of cases. My positive conclusion is that complexity theory changes the philosophical ideal of rationality from finding the uniquely best option to selecting and revising search procedures under resource constraints, but it does not by itself tell us which bounded procedures are justified.

Please prepare me for the objections this audience is most likely to raise, roughly in the order they will arise, and phrase them as the relevant specialists would phrase them. Include objections about worst-case versus average-case complexity, the distinction between a problem and a particular algorithm, approximation and heuristic success, whether computational possibility should matter to an ideal normative standard, and whether my view covertly assumes a controversial model of cognition. For each objection, tell me what I should concede, what I should hold, and the strongest concise reply I can give during the question period. Identify which objections have real force rather than merely sounding technically impressive, and be explicit about the weakest point in my own position, including the question I should most hope nobody asks. I want a committed assessment rather than an even-handed menu: rank the objections by how damaging they are, take a definite position on what my thesis can and cannot establish, and recommend the most defensible reformulation if my current claim is too strong.
