I'm a novelist working on a comic novel about a washed-up trend forecaster who insists human generations can be classified like animal species. For a section that appears between chapters, write a deadpan parody of a natural-history field guide, about 800-1,000 words, called something like A Field Guide to the American Cohort.

Give it species entries for the Baby Boomer, the Millennial, and Gen Z, plus one unstable hybrid or borderline specimen from the cusp. Each entry can have field-guide sections like range, habitat, identifying plumage, common calls, feeding habits, display rituals, and conservation status. The guide should treat these labels as obvious natural kinds, while the descriptions quietly expose why that is philosophically and scientifically dodgy: arbitrary birth-year boundaries, huge differences within each cohort, age effects mistaken for cohort effects, shared events affecting different subgroups unequally, and the way being assigned a label can make people start behaving like the label.

Let the fictional author remain utterly confident while the taxonomy gets increasingly strained -- precise diagnostic rules followed by longer and longer lists of exceptions would be ideal. The humor should come from taking the genre seriously, not from lazy stereotypes or simply dunking on one generation. No real experts, citations, or bibliography. It should amuse someone who has thought about social kinds or demographic methods, but still work as pure absurdity for everyone else. Keep the irony deadpan: the narrator should never admit the project is nonsense, and definately shouldn't turn into a rant.
