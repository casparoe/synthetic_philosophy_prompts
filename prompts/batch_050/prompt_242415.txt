So I run a small blog, mostly read by coworkers and a few internet strangers, and I'm drafting a post about a weird idea I had and I want comments before I put it up. I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, so half my worry is that this is either obviously wrong or a century old and I just can't tell. Here's the draft argument, roughly as I have it:

An acausal threat is one where some agent you can't interact with at all -- say a far-future superintelligence, or an alien mind you'll never meet -- threatens to make things bad for you (classically: it can simulate copies of you and punish them, and you can't be sure you're not the copy) unless you act how it wants. The spooky part is it needs no cameras and no communication channel. It just has to predict you well. Your predictability is the whole attack surface.

The standard response I see is: refuse, precommit to never paying blackmail, and make sure the other side knows that. Fine. But I think there's a different defense nobody talks about, which I'm calling a surrogate goal. The threat only works if the blackmailer's simulation of you gets your values right -- it has to know what you care about to know what to threaten. So: adopt a proxy objective. Pick something only loosely correlated with what you actually want, and genuinely organize your decisions around it, talk about it, act on it. Now any model the blackmailer builds of you from observable evidence predicts the surrogate, not the real you. It winds up threatening the wrong thing -- it torments the copy to pressure values you only half-hold, and the threat is mispriced. You made yourself expensive to blackmail by becoming hard to model in the one place that matters.

Then I have three worries and this is where I mostly want comments. First, is that psychological state even coherent? The surrogate has to be real enough that my behavior actually reflects it, otherwise the simulation sees through it, but fake enough that my true values stay private. But a goal that reliably steers my actions might just... be my goal. I can't tell if "a goal I hold purely as camouflage" is a real thing or a contradiction in terms. Second, there's a weird problem with publishing the essay at all. The defense runs through what the blackmailer can infer about me. The moment the trick is public, it just adjusts -- it models readers of my post as people running the surrogate trick. So an effective defense against acausal threats might be one you can never write down, which is a strange property for an argument to have, kind of like a strategy that destroys itself when stated. Does that actually sink it, or is there a reason the blackmailer can't adjust (like it can't tell which people are running surrogates)? Third, doesn't this prove too much? If a noisy, half-hidden objective protects you, then the rational move is to deliberately corrupt your own utility function, and "win by being slightly irrational on purpose" feels like it should be suspicious.

What I want from you: read this like a hostile but fair reviewer. Tell me if the core defense actually holds or has a hole I'm not seeing. Tell me whether my first worry is a genuine problem or me confusing myself. Tell me if the publication paradox is fatal. And if the whole thing is just some old idea in a trenchcoat, say so plainly -- no need to point me to papers, I probably wont read them, just tell me what's wrong with it in plain terms. Also flag anything in the setup itself I've gotten muddled about how acausal threats are supposed to work, since I'm reconstructing this from memory of internet arguments. Write your comments as plain prose, like an email back to me -- no headings, no bullet points, no bold or tables, just continuous paragraphs I can read top to bottom.
