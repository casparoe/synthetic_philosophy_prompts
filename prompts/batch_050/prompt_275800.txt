Next week I’m recording an interveiw for my small philosophy podcast with Dr. Mara Venn, an invented but very plausible defender of a Wittgenstein-inspired view she calls rule-by-example. Her claim is that most concepts—especially messy ones like person, art, harm, and vehicle—are not governed by hidden necessary-and-sufficient conditions. We master them through examples, comparisons, and socially embedded practices; later cases get accepted because they resemble earlier ones in respects that matter for the activity at hand. She also thinks this explains why concept-users can competently disagree at the margins without merely talking past each other.

Draft a sequence of ten to twelve interview questions that presses this view rather than simply asking Venn to summarize Wittgenstein. Move from clarification into harder objections: resemblance comes in unlimited respects, so what makes one similarity relevant without smuggling in a rule?; how are distinct language-games individuated without becoming gerrymandered?; if correct application depends on practice, how can an entire community be wrong rather than merely innovative?; how does the view handle genuinely novel cases such as delivery drones under a vehicle law or synthetic organisms under a concept of life?; and why doesn’t rule-by-example turn conceptual disagreement into a sociological report about who happens to hold power? Include at least one question about whether the view can explain real contradiction across communities, one about its implications for morally loaded boundary disputes, and one asking what evidence would make her abandon it. For every question, add the answer she would most likely give and a follow-up that closes off that answer’s easiest escape route. Start directly with Question 1, with no preface or general advice about interviewing.
