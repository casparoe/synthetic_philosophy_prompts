im putting together a worksheet for an undergrad seminar on practical rationality and i want a decision tree that sorts students into positions on choice without preference, ie the Buridans ass problem: can it be rational to act when nothing favors one option over the other?

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of named views. Start somewhere like whether perfectly symmetric options can even obtain, then whether acting on no differential reason can be rational, then what mechanism (if any) does the work of settling the tie. Every branch node should be a question a student can answer without knowing which position it funnels into, so phrase nodes as cases or concrete claims, never as do you believe in X.

The tree has to route students through these cases somewhere:
- the starving donkey parked equidistant between two identical hay bales
- grabbing one of two identical soup cans off the shelf without really thinking
- needing to randomize in rock paper scissors against a competent opponent (is a mixed strategy a case of reason demanding an unreasoned pick?)
- a warehouse robot that must choose one of two identical charging docks -- does it change anything that the robot has no will at all, or is the structure of its problem the donkeys problem?

Leaves i want represented, but name them descriptively rather than borrowing labels: the starvation verdict (no differential reason, no rational move, the donkey dies and a perfectly rational agent would die with it), the just pick view (arbitrarily taking one is itself a fully rational act that needs no further justification), the randomization view (ties rationally require coin-flipping, and rock paper scissors proves rationality can demand it), the dissolution view (perfect symmetry never actually obtains, some cue always breaks it, so the puzzle is empty), the free will reading (the case shows the will can act beyond the balance of reasons and thats exactly what freedom is), and the split view (reasons justify the menu, not the item: choosing from the optimal set is rational, but settling on a member is a different activity that was never reason-governed). If the answer space forces an extra leaf ive missed, add it and say why its forced.

For any combination of answers nobody seems to occupy, mark it as empty and give a one sentence diagnosis: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And if two leaves cant actually be distinguished by any question on the tree (i suspect the just pick view and the split view might collapse into each other, and maybe the free will reading and just pick too), flag the pair honestly instead of papering over it.

6 to 8 branch questions total, and write everything in American English.
