I'm putting together a funding application for a twelve-month, part-time research project in the philosophy of music, and I need a two-page proposal. The topic is the ontology of musical works, but I want to approach it through recordings rather than through scores and performances, which is where the standard debate lives.

My half-formed idea: the usual positions treat recordings as philosophically secondary — either as mere access devices to works (the way a paperback gives you access to a novel) or as documents of performances (the way a photograph documents an event). I think a handful of cases resist both treatments, and that working through them forces a three-way ontology of works, performances, and recordings whose identity conditions are genuinely unclear. The cases: studio recordings assembled by splicing dozens of takes, where no complete performance of the work ever occurred; remasters and remixes that a streaming platform substitutes for the original release without notice, so that two listeners who believe they heard "the same album" heard sonically different objects; a "live" album edited together from several nights and presented as one concert; and musical cultures in which a recording is treated as the primary work itself, with scores and live renditions occupying the derivative position. In each case the questions are: how many works are there, how many performances, and is the recording identical to either, neither, or a work of a new kind?

Write the proposal. It should contain the following:

- A precise statement of the central question. "What is the ontology of recorded music?" is a survey topic, not a research question, so frame something with a determinate answer — for instance, whether recordings that instantiate no performance are works, performances, or a sui generis category, and what follows from each option for the identity and survival conditions of the works those recordings present.

- A section explaining why the standard menu of positions leaves the question open. The positions to engage are the familiar ones: the view of works as abstract types with performances as their tokens; contextualist versions that build the instrumentation and historical situation of composition into the work itself; and nominalist or performance-centered views that refuse to countenance works apart from their instances. For each, show where it either ignores recordings, quietly assimilates them to performances, or quietly assimilates them to works — and why that assimilation fails on at least one of my cases. Refer to positions by description rather than by author, and don't include a bibliography or a literature review; I'll supply the citations afterward.

- A method section describing what I would actually do across the twelve months. I expect this to involve mapping the full grid of available positions (is a recording a work? a performance? a representation of a performance? a fourth kind of thing?), using the cases as tests that eliminate cells of the grid, and then checking which live positions in the existing debate are committed to which cells — including whether any standard position occupies a cell that survives all the cases.

- A statement of what a result looks like: ideally, identity and persistence conditions for recordings relative to works and performances, plus a verdict on which standard positions absorb this result cheaply and which require revision. Also state what would count as the project failing — for example, if it turns out that every major position accommodates all four cases with minor adjustments, then recordings are not philosophically special after all, and the honest deliverable is a paper saying exactly that.

- A realism check: is twelve months part-time enough for the grid-plus-cases method, and what gets cut if it isn't? Close with a paragraph aimed at a non-specialist funder explaining why the metaphysics of a remaster matters outside aesthetics — I have in mind a connection to general questions about digital objects (whether a file, its copies, and its silently modified versions are one thing or many), but if you think there's a better bridge to broader significance, use that instead.
