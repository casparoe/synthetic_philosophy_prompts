I teach a philosophy of physics module to third-year undergraduates. In one ninety-minute session I want to take them from Maxwell's demon to the modern consensus that the resolution lies in the physics of information — Szilard's one-molecule engine, Landauer's principle, and Bennett's argument that the entropy debt is paid in the demon's memory rather than in its perception. The class knows the second law only as a statement about entropy and isolated systems; they have had no information theory and have never been asked to think of computation as a physical process.

I am not asking you to give the explanation; I am asking you to design it.

First, work out the dependency structure. Which notions must be in place before the demon is even a paradox, and which should be withheld until the students are already committed to one? In particular, tell me whether Shannon entropy should come before the demon or after it, and whether Szilard's engine should precede or follow the general discussion of what measurement is, with a reason for each placement.

Second, choose the carrying scenario. Maxwell's original sorting demon, Szilard's engine, or something plainer still — which one should run through the whole session, and what must it have, and conspicuously lack, so that the erasure analysis at the end can be performed on it directly rather than by switching examples midway?

Third, map the misconceptions. I expect at least these: that the trapdoor mechanism itself must dissipate the missing entropy; that acquiring a bit of information is intrinsically thermodynamically costly; and that Bennett's result shows the second law was never genuinely threatened. For each, tell me where in the sequence it will naturally form, and whether I should preempt it at that point or let it form and then turn it into material.

Fourth, give me one question to pose at the end whose answers will separate students who understand why erasure rather than measurement carries the cost from students who have merely retained the slogan.

Finally, tell me what I should openly decline to cover at this level — the quantum-mechanical versions, fluctuation theorems, the thermodynamic cost of building the demon in the first place — and how to mark those exclusions without making the session feel truncated.
