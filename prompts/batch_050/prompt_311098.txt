I'm putting together my little position statement on socially constructed kinds for a reading group and want a consistency check before I defend it. Here's where I've landed:

(1) There are no biological races; race is socially constructed all the way down.
(2) Constructed doesn't mean unreal. Money is my model here: paper is valuable purely because we collectively treat it as valuable, and yet money is one of the most causally powerful things around. Race is real in the same way.
(3) What makes someone a member of a race isn't anyone's beliefs or concepts, it's their position in a social hierarchy -- the actual pattern of who gets what, tracked by ancestry. Even if everyone stopped *thinking* racially tommorow, the hierarchy itself would still be the racial fact.
(4) Race is also like money in this further sense: if the collective practice sustaining it genuinely ended, race would simply cease to exist, and a world without races is the right long-term goal. Eliminativism, not conservation or celebration.
(5) A case I've been using to test this: a colony ship leaves Earth, and en route all records are destroyed and some gene therapy scrambles any visible correlates of ancestry, so nobody on board has racial concepts or perceptions at all. But families and wealth still pass down the generations, so the great-grandchildren of Earth's formerly enslaved stay markedly poorer on average, without anyone knowing why. My verdict: there is no race on the ship anymore, but the obligation to make reparations survives, and it's still specifically reparations for racial injustice, not just generic egalitarian redistribution toward the poor.

Which of these are actually in tension, and which only look like they are? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, say for each one what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs, and finish by telling me which single revision would clean up the most.
