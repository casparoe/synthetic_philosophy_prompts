I’m trying to understand Van Fraassen’s Reflection principle. As I read it, it says that if I’m rational now and expect to remain rational later, then my current credence in p, given that my later credence in p will be r, should also be r. But that seems to imply I can change my present credence just by deciding what my future credence will be: if tonight I resolve that tomorrow I’ll be 90% confident it will rain, Reflection apparently says I should already be 90% confident now, even if I’ve seen no forecast. Is that really a way to bootstrap evidence-free beliefs? If not, where exactly does the argument go wrong—in treating Reflection as a rule about my expected future credence, in assuming I can rationally precommit to a future credence regardless of what I learn, or somewhere else? And does Reflection require the later self to be better informed, or only that the current self treats the later credence as authoritative?
