I advise a midsized city government that is considering a five-year shadow futarchy pilot for recurring capital and regulatory decisions costing between $1 million and $25 million. Prediction markets would advise rather than replace the elected council, which retains final authority. Develop a reusable procedure that two policy staff members could complete within one working day, followed by a market lasting no more than fourteen days.

The procedure should specify, in order: how to choose measurable outcomes without collapsing public value into one metric; how to define adoption and nonadoption counterfactuals; what market structure and settlement rules to use; how to seed liquidity and prevent manipulation, collusion, self-dealing, and hedging by affected contractors; when low participation invalidates a forecast; how to compare conditional predictions under uncertainty; and when the procedure must stop because the issue concerns rights, distributive values, or an unmeasurable objective. Distinguish carefully between what the market estimates and what the council prefers, and mark every point at which judgment cannot be eliminated.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals: replacing an aging water main, permitting a privately funded facial-recognition system downtown, and choosing between two designs for a flood-control park. At least one application should yield a recommendation the proposal’s supporters would dislike. Conclude by identifying the kinds of decisions for which the entire procedure is likely to misfire.
