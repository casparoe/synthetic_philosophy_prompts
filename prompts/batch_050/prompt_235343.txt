I'm running a session of our philosophy of mind reading group on whether LLMs genuinely understand language, and I got assigned the pro-understanding side. I've collected what I think are all the standard arguments, but before the session I want to stress-test the list -- is there a good argument I'm missing?

Here's what I have so far: (1) the behavioral argument -- LLMs pass basically every linguistic test we'd use to attribute understanding to a person (answering novel questions, following instructions, translating, explaining jokes, spotting ambiguities), and demanding more is moving goalposts we'd never move for humans; (2) the meaning-as-use argument -- if meaning is constituted by use rather than by some inner mental item, as the later Wittgenstein suggests, then mastering the use of expressions just is understanding them, and LLMs clearly master use; (3) the internal-representations argument -- probing work and cases like models trained purely on game transcripts apparently reconstructing board states suggest these systems build something like models of the underlying reality from text alone, so they're not just parroting surface statistics; (4) the grounding-through-training argument -- human feedback fine-tuning and multimodal training give the model enough contact with non-linguistic reality to sidestep the octopus-test worry about form-only training; and (5) the epistemic-symmetry argument -- we attribute understanding to other humans purely on behavioral evidence too, since nobody has access to anyone else's 'inner light,' so requiring one from LLMs is just carbon chauvinism.

So: is there a sixth argument for the pro side that isn't on this list? To be clear about the rules: it has to be genuinely different, not a special case or rewording. Another probing result is just more of (3), another impressive behavioral benchmark is more of (1), and 'but humans are also trained on statistics' is more of (5) unless it does real extra work. If you honestly think the list is exhaustive, say that and explain why -- but try first. Also tell me whether your new argument is actually any good, and specifically whether it survives the obvious reply from the stochastic parrots crowd. If it's weaker than the five above I'd rather know now than find out in front of the reading group.
