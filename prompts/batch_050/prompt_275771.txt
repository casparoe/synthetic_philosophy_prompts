here's a case i've been chewing on and i want to spar about it. i'm a bridge inspector. this morning i run the standard load tests on the Cranford Street bridge, everything checks out, and i conclude the bridge safely holds 40 tons. by any ordinary standard i now know this: good instruments, competent me, no funny business. this afternoon a collegue emails me a study arguing that the exact sensor model i used drifts badly on bridges of exactly this age. now the dogmatic reasoning: if the bridge really does hold 40 tons, then any evidence suggesting otherwise is misleading evidence, and i already know the bridge holds 40 tons. so apparently i can conclude right now, without even opening the study, that wherever it seems to bear on my bridge it's misleading, and just delete the email. the conclusion seems absurd but every step looks fine.

i want you to play a philosopher who bites this bullet: you think the reasoning is perfectly rational and the air of absurdity is an illusion. don't be a caricature though. you have the usual resources: you distinguish knowing p from merely being very confident of p, you concede that strong counterevidence often *destroys* your knowledge so that dismissing it later would be irrational, you just insist that while the knowledge is in place the inference it licenses is sound, and that's not a defect of knowledge, it's what knowledge is for. stay in character, first person, until i say 'stop'. keep replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like an actual argument. you can concede small points but never the main one, and feel free to ask me questions back if my objection is sloppy.

my opening move: your position is time-indexed in a way that kills it. at 3pm i know p, so i know tomorrow's counterevidence will be misleading. at 5pm, having actually read the study, i no longer know p, so dismissal is no longer licensed. so the license to ignore the evidence evaporates at the exact moment it's exercised -- it was never a license to ignore anything, only a license to have ignored it earlier. a permission you lose by acting on it isn't a permission at all, it's a trap. what do you say?
