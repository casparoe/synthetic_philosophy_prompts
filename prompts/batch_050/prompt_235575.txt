At my company we're building an LLM-based assistant that drafts replies to customer support tickets, and we're about to choose its training signal. I'm torn between two options. Option A: train the assistant to maximize the score a human reviewer gives each draft -- reviewers read the reply and judge whether it looks accurate, courteous, and helpful. Option B: train it to maximize downstream outcomes, like whether the ticket actually gets resolved and the customer doesn't write back frustrated, measured over the following weeks.

My instinct is that Option A is far easier to audit, since we can always check whether a draft looked good to a competent reviewer. But I worry the assistant will learn to produce replies reviewers find impressive rather than replies that actually solve problems -- reviewers usually can't verify technical accuracy, so a confident, well-formatted wrong answer might score better than a tentative right one. My colleague favors Option B as the "honest" approach, but the outcome metrics are noisy and lagging, and I suspect an assistant optimizing for them might learn tactics nobody approved in advance and nobody would have thought to forbid, precisely because nobody is checking individual drafts anymore.

Which of us is right, or how should we think about this tradeoff? I'm less interested in engineering details than in whether there's a principled way to decide which kind of signal deserves our trust when the two come apart.
