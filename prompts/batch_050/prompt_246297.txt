I teach an undergraduate introduction to philosophy of mind with 24 students and an 80-minute session. Design a classroom simulation that lets students feel both the force of the Chinese Room argument and the force of the systems reply before either is named. The activity should use an invented logographic language rather than actual Chinese, so that no student has an unfair advantage. One student should act as the worker inside the room, receiving cards through a slot and producing answers solely by following a rulebook, while the rest of the class is divided into a rulebook committee, outside interrogators, and observers. Include several rounds with a clear purpose: an initial round in which the worker appears conversationally competent despite having no idea what the symbols mean; an adversarial round using novel combinations, context dependence, and follow-up questions that expose gaps in the original rules; and a revision round in which the committee improves the system from feedback. At some point, replace the worker with another volunteer without changing the rules, so students must confront whether the relevant competence belongs to the person, the rulebook, the whole arrangement, or no one.

Please provide concrete setup instructions, roles, sample symbol cards and rulebook entries, a manageable scoring system, and a timeline. The interrogators should be rewarded for asking questions that distinguish rote symbol matching from understanding, but the rules should prevent them from simply asking the worker what the symbols mean. Then give a detailed debrief plan mapping particular moments in the activity to syntax versus semantics, behavioral indistinguishability, the systems reply, and the robot reply. The debrief should also ask whether the committee version illegitimately changes Searle’s original case by distributing cognition across several people. Finally, explain what will predictably go wrong—students secretly translating symbols into English, the worker memorizing salient rules, the rulebook becoming too cumbersome, or the discussion collapsing into a verbal dispute over the word understanding—and how to prevent or use each failure. Be explicit about what the simulation cannot establish, especially concerning consciousness, embodiment, and the enormous difference between a toy language and a human linguistic system.
