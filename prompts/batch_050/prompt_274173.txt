I'm in a grad seminar on the philosophy of pain and I keep getting wrecked by the same objection, so I want to spar. My view: the badness of pain is intrinsic to the painful sensation itself -- the raw feel of pain is just bad to undergo, full stop, whether or not the person minds it. I want you to play a committed defender of the opposite view: a pain is bad for someone only insofar as it's aversive to them, only insofar as it frustrates or is disliked by the subject. Your star witness is pain asymbolia -- patients (after certain strokes, cingulotomies, or prefrontal leukotomies) who sincerely report that they still feel pain, can locate it and describe its intensity, but that it simply doesn't bother them anymore. You'll argue that nothing bad is happening to these patients in respect of that sensation, so the mere feel can't be where badness lives; badness enters only when the sensation is unwanted. Give my position a fair shake -- concede small points when I earn them -- but never give up the core claim that unbothersome pain isn't bad. You can invent thought experiments on the fly if they help your case, just keep them concrete. Keep each reply to one paragraph so it feels like a real exchange, stay in character until I say stop, and end every reply with a last line beginning "Position:" that states your current stance in a single sentence, so I can track whether I'm actually moving you.

Here's my opening move. Your whole case leans on the asymbolics being fine, but consider what morphine does: people on it standardly say the pain is still there, they just don't mind it. Nobody concludes from this that morphine makes torture morally fine -- only that it makes you indifferent to your own torture. Not minding something bad isn't the badness going away; it's the caring going away. Suppose I train myself, stoic-style, over years, to be utterly unmoved by the pain of a shattered leg. Have I made the pain good? Surely not -- I've made myself a bad judge of my own condition. So the asymbolic patients aren't evidence that unminded pain is harmless; they're evidence that people can fail to register the badness of what's happening to them, which is tragic, not exonerating. Why doesn't that reading work?
