ok so a friend told me about this thought experiment at a party and ive been chewing on it for like two weeks. There's a guy hooked up to a machine with a dial that goes from 0 to 1000. Each notch up adds a bit of pain, but so little that you genuinely cannot feel the difference between one notch and the next -- 47 vs 48 is nothing, you'd fail a blind test every single time. But each notch also pays you, say $1000, straight into your account. So turning from 47 to 48 is basically free money. Yet the difference between 0 and 1000 is the difference between nothing and total agony, and apparently no amount of money is worth being at the top.

Anyway I've been arguing about this with people and here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it hangs together before I embarrass myself further:

1. Nobody is irrational just because of what they prefer. How much pain a thousand bucks is worth to you is your business, there's no wrong answer to that.
2. Turning the dial one notch is always the right move. You can't feel the difference and you get paid. Refusing means turning down money for a cost of literally zero.
3. The guy who cranks it all the way to 1000 for the cash screwed up somewhere. That outcome is just bad for him even though he 'chose' it.
4. I don't think cyclical preferences are a problem. Sometimes I honestly prefer pizza to sushi, sushi to tacos, and tacos to pizza. That's just taste, like rock paper scissors, nobody is broken for it.
5. But if someone could exploit my little loops to keep trading me things in a circle and draining my bank account a dollar at a time, I'd have to admit something had gone wrong with me, not just that I have quirky taste.

So tell me: which of these are actually in tension? My buddy insists 2 and 3 can't both be true, and my sister says 1 and 3 are the real problem, and honestly I suspect some of these fights are fake. Tell me which pairs genuinely clash and wich ones only look like they do, rank the real ones by how bad the damage is, and for each one say what I'd have to give up to fix it. And then: what's the single cheapest revision that makes the whole set consistent, and what does it cost me?
