I recently fell down a wikipedia hole about pain asymbolia -- the condition where people (sometimes after certain brain lesions) say they still feel pain, can locate it, describe its intensity, but just... don't mind it anymore. No suffering, no avoidance, no "make it stop." And I got into a dumb argument with my roommate about whether such a person is still in a bad state, like is the badness of pain in the ouch-feeling itself or in the hating-it part? She said obviously the feeling, I said if you don't mind it then in what sense is it bad, and we went in circles for an hour.

So here's what I want: write me the FAQ page for a fictional Silicon Valley startup called something like "Unbothered" or "Just Sensation" that offers an elective procedure giving you pain asymbolia on purpose -- pitched as the ultimate biohack, "keep the signal, drop the suffering." Around 600-700 words, deadpan corporate-wellness voice, with the usual FAQ structure: how it works, pricing tiers (maybe a premium plan where you can toggle it), testimonials from a marathon runner and someone with a chronic condition, a worried-sounding disclaimer buried in legalese, the works.

The catch: all the humor has to come from the company accidentally tripping over the actual philosophy while trying to sell this. Like the FAQ keeps promising you'll "still feel everything" while also promising it "won't hurt anymore," and can't decide which of those is the selling point. Maybe one testimonial is thrilled and one is weirdly unsettling in a way the marketing team didn't notice. Maybe there's a question about whether you can still claim damages in a lawsuit, or whether it counts as "really hurting" for insurance purposes, answered with total confidence in a way that makes the philosophical problem more visible, not less. Don't name-drop philosophers or use jargon like "evaluativism" -- the whole point is that a specialist reads it and goes "oh no, they built a company on top of an open problem" while a normal reader just thinks its funny dystopian satire. Stay deadpan the whole time, no winking, and end on an FAQ answer that accidentally states the core puzzle completely straight.
