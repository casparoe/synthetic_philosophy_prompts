Subsequent analyses raised methodological objections to this report, and also observed that despite the COMPAS risk tool's errors, its estimates of the probability of recidivism are equally well calibrated to the true outcomes for both African-American and white defendants."

(a) Identify which of the paper's three formal fairness conditions each side of this dispute is appealing to, and define each condition you name.
(b) State precisely the conditions under which the paper's impossibility result applies, and the cases in which all three fairness conditions can be satisfied simultaneously.

The student's answer:

(a) The first side — the report described in the passage — is appealing to the paper's two balance conditions. Balance for the negative class requires that, within each group, people who do not go on to reoffend receive the same average risk score; balance for the positive class is the same requirement for people who do reoffend. Together, the two balance conditions are the paper's version of statistical parity, since they demand that members of each group face the same distribution of risk scores. The "subsequent analyses" are appealing to calibration: among the people assigned any given score, the fraction who actually reoffend must be the same in both groups.

(b) The impossibility applies when two things hold: the groups have different base rates (different underlying fractions of people who actually reoffend) and prediction is imperfect. If either condition fails, all three fairness conditions can hold at once — equal base rates and perfect prediction are the two escape hatches the paper identifies.

(c) Yes. The theorem shows that outside those special cases the three conditions cannot hold jointly, so requiring any one of them amounts to requiring that the others be violated — a regulator who mandated calibration would be mandating provable unfairness with respect to balance, and vice versa. The result therefore vindicates the calibration defense of COMPAS described in the passage, since calibration is the condition the authors treat as fundamental. It also shows that the choice among fairness metrics is a purely political one that no technical argument can settle.
