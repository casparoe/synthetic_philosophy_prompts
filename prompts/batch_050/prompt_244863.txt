A friend who teaches law told me the Hart–Devlin debate is basically a fight economists already have, just in fancier language, and I want to test whether he's right. Quick background on me: I do applied micro, so my native vocabulary is utility functions, externalities, preference aggregation, welfare theorems, Kaldor-Hicks, mechanism design. I don't read jurisprudence and I'd rather not have to.

So here's the ask. Take the debate over legal moralism — roughly, Devlin's view that a society is entitled to enforce its shared moral code as such, even against things done in private that harm nobody else, versus the Mill/Hart line that immorality alone, without harm to others, is none of the law's business — and restate it in my vocabulary, as precisely and faithfully as you can manage. My own first-pass translation: the disapproval of the right-minded majority is just a preference like any other, other people's private conduct enters utility functions as a psychic externality, and the whole debate collapses into the question of wether you launder moralistic preferences out of the social welfare function before aggregating. I suspect that's too fast, but I can't see where it breaks.

The deliverable has two parts. (1) The best translation you can produce, term by term. (2) The residue report: what maps cleanly, what only fits with distortion, and what has no counterpart in the econ vocabulary at all. Things I already suspect are hard to translate: Mill's liberty principle looks like a side-constraint rather than an argument of the welfare function; Devlin's claim that a shared morality is the cement holding a society together seems partly empirical and partly something else; the harm/offense distinction; the idea that criminal law does expressive work rather than just preventing disutility. For each untranslatable bit, give me your verdict on which side the problem is on: is it a blind spot in the econ vocabulary, or a sign that the lawyers were being vaguer than they sound?

And be honest about epistemics: attach rough confidence levels to the main translation moves, and at the end single out the one point in the whole exercise where you're least sure you've got it right.
