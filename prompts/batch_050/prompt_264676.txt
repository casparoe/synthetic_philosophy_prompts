I'm a first-year graduate student applying for a three-month summer research fellowship in philosophy of language, and I need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea is this. In my philosophy of language seminar we worked through Davidson's account of radical interpretation: the interpreter, faced with a speaker whose language is entirely unknown, has no choice but to assume that the speaker's beliefs are mostly true and mostly coherent, since massive error or irrationality on the part of the speaker would be indistinguishable from misinterpretation on the part of the interpreter. That is the principle of charity, and Davidson treats it not as a courtesy we extend to speakers but as a constitutive condition of their being speakers at all. Here is where my question starts. Most of the speech I actually encounter in my life is nothing like the earnest utterances of the standard examples: it is ironic, layered, memetic, strategic, and often designed so that members of an out-group will systematically misread it while in-group members catch the real meaning. Think of discourse communities whose utterances function as shibboleths, or whose participants routinely say things they would not defend if pressed, where saying the thing signals allegiance rather than expresses belief. My suspicion is that radical interpretation faces a genuine problem here that the standard discussion ignores: charity tells the interpreter to maximize truth and coherence in the speaker's beliefs, but if a whole community's speech is partly constituted by the expectation of being misinterpreted by outsiders, then maximizing the truth of what they seem to be saying may be exactly the wrong method, and the correct interpretation may require attributing beliefs the speakers would disavow. I am not sure whether this is a problem for Davidson, a problem Davidson already has the resources to solve (perhaps via his later claim that interpretation is always a matter of provisional 'passing theories' revised in the course of conversation), or not a problem at all because charity was never meant to apply community by community.
