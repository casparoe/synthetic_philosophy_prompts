ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole lately about whether nature is actually good for the animals in it, and I want to know if the views I've ended up with even fit together or if I'm kidding myself. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Most wild animals probably have lives that aren't worth living. Most species reproduce by having tons of offspring where nearly all of them die young — eaten, starved, diseased, whatever. Nature documentaries skip over that part. So plausibly the wild contains way more suffering than happiness.
2. Insects and other small critters count morally, not just elephants and wolves and the cute stuff. And given the sheer numbers of insects, they're probably most of what morally matters about a forest or a meadow.
3. I'm still against destroying habitat. Deforestation, draining wetlands, paving over meadows, all of it. I don't want to become the guy who thinks parking lots are an improvement on nature, that conclusion feels crazy to me.
4. I support rewilding projects — letting farmland go back to scrub and forest, reintroducing predators like wolves, that sort of thing.
5. I don't think a species going extinct is bad in itself, beyond whatever it does to the individual animals. A species isn't a person, it can't suffer. Only individuals matter.

I've got a nagging feeling that 1 and 2 cause problems for some of the others but I can't pin down exactly where. So: which of these actually contradict each other, which ones only *look* like they conflict but are fine once you think about it, and for the real conflicts, what's the cheapest fix — which commitment do I drop or weaken and what does that cost me? Rank the problems by how serious they are instead of just listing them.

One more thing: don't just answer everything with "it depends on the empirical facts." Tell me WHICH facts each tension depends on and what follows either way, so I know what I'd have to go look up or think about more.
