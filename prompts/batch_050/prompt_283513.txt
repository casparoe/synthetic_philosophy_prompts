I'm a novelist, ~60k words into a thriller about a two-week nuclear standoff -- two superpowers, a submarine gone dark, the works. Problem: the whole third act depends on my president being both a fundamentally decent man and a credible threatener, and the more I stare at it the more I think I've written myself into a corner, because I'm no longer sure my own views on this stuff hang together either. Roughly where I stand:

1. If deterrence fails, actually launching the big countervalue strike -- incinerating millions of civilians when it can no longer protect anyone -- is mass murder. He'd be a monster if he gave the order, and the book treats him that way.
2. Deterrence only works if the other side believes you'd do it, and bluff doesn't survive decades of scrutiny: doctrines leak, officers defect, the adversary models your incentives. So a real deterrent requires genuinly meaning it -- a standing intention to retaliate, or at least machinery that makes retaliation genuinely likely.
3. You can't decently intend to do what you know would be monstrous to do. Planning mass murder stains you even if the plan never executes; intentions aren't morally free just because they're conditional.
4. And yet: a nuclear deterrent is justified. The balance of terror plausibly spared us a third world war, and unilateral stand-down looks worse.
5. In a crisis it can be rational to take a step neither side fully controls -- bombers airborne, fleets moved -- because only a shared risk of catastrophe gets the other side to climb down.
6. But it is never rational to choose to lose control. A rational agent doesn't trade away its own capacity to choose.

Audit, please, not a lecture: tensions ranked by severity, and for each one be specific about which commitment has to give and what giving it up costs. If a pair only looks like a tension but dissolves when you press on it, say that instead of padding the list. And if I've built the problem badly -- smuggled in a premise that's doing all the work, or confused a plot constraint with a fact about ethics -- tell me straight instead of politely answering the wrong question. I'd rather scrap the third act than get a tidy verdict on a broken setup.
