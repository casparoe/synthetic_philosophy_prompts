So I'm a longtime LW / SSC / Astral Codex reader and yesterday I got into a back-and-forth on the EA forum about AI timelines. I was arguing for short timelines, someone else for long, we were both giving actual arguments, and then a third person jumps in with the classic move: "you're both good Bayesians right, so this whole argument is pointless — priors wash out, with enough evidence you'll converge, so just wait for more data." It got a bunch of upvotes. It annoyed me a lot but I couldn't articulate exactly why on the spot.

What I want is a disambiguation of what "priors wash out" could actually be claiming, precisified into a menu I can paste into the thread. I have a vague sense there are several different things floating around under this slogan and I want them separated. Candidate readings I can gesture at, feel free to restructure:

- Something about posterior consistency: as evidence piles up, your posterior concentrates around the truth, whatever your prior was
- Something about merging: two Bayesians who watch the same evidence stream eventually assign similar probabilities to everything
- Aumann-flavored stuff: rational agents with a common prior can't "agree to disagree"
- The weaker claim that likelihood ratios eventually swamp any prior odds ratio
- Maybe the weakest reading: priors matter less as data accumulates, full stop, no theorems invoked

For each reading I want: (a) is it an actual theorem or a heuristic, and if a theorem, roughly what assumptions it needs — I half-remember things like the true hypothesis having to get nonzero prior probability, and that Aumann needs common knowledge of the posteriors and not just both sides knowing the other disagrees, but I'm shaky on details; (b) does "converge" mean converging on probabilities, or just on decisions/actions, which seem pretty different; (c) does it say anything at all about how fast convergence happens, which seems like the crux for an argument about something happening in the next decade; (d) does it actually license the reply guy's move of telling us to stop arguing?

And then verdicts: which reading did this person probably mean, and does the strongest *true* version of the slogan support the dismissive use? My suspicion is that all the real theorems are asymptotic, assume shared likelihood functions (wich two humans arguing about AI obviously don't have), and say nothing about how much convergence has happened by now — but maybe I'm wrong and there's a reading where he was basically right and I should just update and shut up.

Keep it as a list of readings with verdicts, not an essay defending one interpretation.
