Alan Turing argued that instead of programming an adult mind directly, one should build a child-machine and educate it, and he speculated about the roles that reward and punishment, imitation, and the teacher's interventions would play in such a machine's upbringing. Suppose Turing were alive today and shown how large language models are actually produced: imitation of a vast text corpus, then adjustment by human approval and disapproval signals, then deployment in which users steer the finished system with instructions rather than further training. Given his writings on learning machines, would Turing say these systems have been educated, programmed, or neither? Where exactly would he say the analogy between their training and a child's schooling breaks down, and what would he change about how such systems are brought up? Finally, Turing imagined his child-machine being taught by human instructors; if he were asked whether a well-trained language model could itself serve as the sort of teacher he had in mind, what do you think his answer would be, and on what grounds?
