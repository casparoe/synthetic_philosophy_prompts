Need a tiebreaker on an argument that ate our whole team lunch on friday. I'm a backend dev, no philosophy training, and I honestly can't tell who's right, or if they're even disagreeing about the same thing.

Someone brought up that logic puzzle: island with 100 blue-eyed and 100 brown-eyed people, nobody knows their own eye color, they can't communicate, and if anyone ever figures out their own eye color they leave the island that night. Everyone is a perfect logician and everyone knows everyone is. A guru visits and says once, in front of everyone: "I can see at least one person with blue eyes." Standard answer is all the blue-eyed people leave on night 100.

Dana (senior eng, distributed systems) said the puzzle is a cheat. The guru tells them absolutely nothing new -- every single person there already sees blue-eyed people, so there's no new information, and without new information nobody can deduce anything, so the "solution" is sleight of hand.

Marcus said the information isn't in the content, it's in the announcement being public. Before, everyone knew there were blue eyes, but not everyone knew that everyone knew that everyone knew, etc. The guru makes it common knowledge in one shot, and that's the new bit.

Dana said fine, that works for 2 or 3 islanders, but with 100 blue-eyed people "at least one person has blue eyes" is already mutually known like 99 levels deep. The guru only adds level 100 and up, which only matters if everyone is a literally perfect reasoner -- one person doubting someone else's logic at level 40 and the whole induction collapses. So her point stands: the puzzle doesn't show communication adding info in any real-world sense, it shows that if you assume infinite rationality you get a cute theorem.

Marcus said "it's a theorem" is a retreat -- her original claim was that no new information was added, and that's just false. He brought up the Two Generals problem (which we covered in our distributed systems reading group, ironically): generals exchanging messengers can pile up acknowledgments forever and never reach common knowledge, so they can't coordinate an attack. He said that gap between deep mutual knowledge and actual common knowledge is real and has teeth.

Dana said Two Generals proves HER point: common knowledge is basically impossible to establish between parties, and the puzzle smuggles it in as a premise (common knowledge of everyone's perfect rationality, plus a magic reliable broadcast). Garbage in, gospel out.

Marcus said she's conflating "is the puzzle internally valid" with "does it model humans," and it never claimed to model humans.

Please go point by point: (a) does the guru's statement add information when everyone already sees blue eyes, (b) is Dana right that at 100 people it's already mutual knowledge ~99 deep, and does that make the announcement redundant, (c) does the fragility thing (one doubter breaks the chain) refute the solution or just its real-world applicability, (d) who does Two Generals actually support, and (e) is there something both of them are missing. And who's more right overall?
