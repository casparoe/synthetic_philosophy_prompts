Draft the joint statement that a committed defender of inference to the best explanation and a committed strict Bayesian could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on how scientists should apportion credence among rival hypotheses.

For background, take the two sides as follows. The explanationist holds that the explanatory virtues of a hypothesis — its scope, simplicity, unifying power, and the way it would render the evidence unsurprising — are themselves epistemic reasons to raise one's confidence in it, so that the loveliness of an explanation bears on its likeliness and is not merely a heuristic for finding hypotheses worth testing. The Bayesian holds that rational credence is exhausted by prior probabilities conditionalized on likelihoods, and that any epistemic work the explanatory virtues appear to do is either already encoded in those quantities or is an illicit double-counting that amounts to a dressed-up bias.

The document should contain the following. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: propositions both parties sign, which should cover at least the facts that working scientists demonstrably reason in IBE-shaped ways, that explanatory considerations often do track truth, that a likelihood is not always easier to estimate than an explanatory judgment, and that neither side has a fully general account of where priors come from. Second, a disagreements table giving the precise residual disputes, each formulated in wording its own side would accept rather than in the other side's caricature. Third, a list of cruxes, which I expect to include whether explanatory goodness can raise a posterior only through priors and likelihoods or can legitimately enter as a further step, whether IBE is a rival to conditionalization or merely a device for estimating its inputs, and whether the badness of the best explanation within a bad lot of candidates undermines the inference. For each crux, specify a concrete case both sides accept as bearing on it — one should involve two hypotheses that are likelihood-equivalent on all current evidence but differ in explanatory character, and another the best-of-a-bad-lot situation — and record in advance what each side predicts rational inference demands there.

Close with your own adjudication, and commit: state which parts of this dispute you judge verbal and which substantive, which single crux would do most to move the debate, and which side should concede more ground, giving your reasons rather than leaving the choice to me.
