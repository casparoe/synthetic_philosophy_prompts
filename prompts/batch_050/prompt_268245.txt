The exam question was: State Aumann's agreement theorem as precisely as you can, identifying each assumption on which it depends. Then assess what bearing it has on disagreement among reasonable, well-informed people in ordinary life.

Here is the student's answer, verbatim:

Aumann's agreement theorem, from his 1976 paper Agreeing to Disagree, says that two Bayesian agents who share a common prior, and whose posterior probabilities for some event are common knowledge between them, cannot assign different posteriors to that event. If it is common knowledge that Ann's posterior for E is p and Bob's is q, then p = q. Rational agents cannot agree to disagree.

The assumptions are these. First, both agents update by Bayes' rule from a common prior defined over a common state space. Second, each agent's private information is represented by a partition of the state space: each agent learns which cell of their own partition contains the true state, and nothing more. Third, the agents' information is the same, in the sense that their partitions coincide; the theorem concerns agents working from an identical evidential base. Fourth, their posteriors are not merely known to each but are common knowledge: Ann knows Bob's posterior, Bob knows Ann's, each knows that the other knows, and so on. Fifth, both agents are rational, and this rationality is itself common knowledge.

The theorem implies that persistent disagreement between rational people is impossible. Whenever two people continue to disagree, at least one of them must be irrational or misinformed. The theorem also shows why dialogue works: two rational agents who take turns announcing their estimates will update on each other's announcements and thereby converge on a common estimate, which is the dynamic form of the impossibility result.

In ordinary life, of course, disagreement persists, which shows that the assumptions fail: people do not share priors, their posteriors are almost never common knowledge, and rationality is itself in doubt. So the theorem's practical upshot is diagnostic rather than predictive -- it tells us what must be going wrong when reasonable people keep disagreeing.
