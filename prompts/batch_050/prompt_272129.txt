I'm writing the framing chapter of a review essay on rational belief, and I want to settle what an account of flat-out belief (as opposed to degrees of confidence) has to do before I start grading the ones on offer. The two cases that drive everything: the lottery -- in a big fair lottery with a guaranteed winner, it seems fine to believe of any given ticket that it'll lose, yet believing that of every ticket contradicts believing a winner exists -- and the preface -- an author has solid evidence for each claim in her book, but on general grounds of her own falibility she also believes at least one of them is wrong, and that seems sensible rather than scandalous.

So give me the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Things I suspect belong on the list: it has to explain why the ticket beliefs feel individually rational but jointly rotten; it has to take a stand on whether the preface author is irrational, and on whether the two cases deserve the same verdict or different ones (and why); it has to say how flat-out belief relates to credence; it has to rule on whether rational belief is closed under conjunction; and it has to connect belief to what you'd act on or assert, since the point of the concept at all, for my purposes, is to serve as a norm for what may go in the conclusion section rather than the fine print.

But push back where needed: if one of those is really just a dogma of one camp rather than a genuine adequacy condition, say so and argue it. And add the requirements I've missed -- I'm sure there are some.

Then the part I care about most: which desiderata can't be jointly satisfied. Show me the inconsistent pairs or triples, so that the list itself demonstrates there's no free lunch here. Mark which requirements you'd treat as negotiable and which are dealbreakers given my stated purpose, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in knocking out candidates.

Last, and briefly, score the three standard moves against the list: believe whatever clears some threshold of probability; reserve flat-out belief for certainty (or near enough); and a two-state picture where belief and credence are seperate states governed by seperate norms. A few sentences each is plenty -- the criteria are where I want the actual thinking.
