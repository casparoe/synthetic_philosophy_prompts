ok so I recently got appointed to my local hospital's transplant ethics committee (I'm the "community rep," not a doctor, which already feels like a joke) and our first real case is doing my head in. A nurse who's worked on the dialysis ward for like eight years wants to donate one of her kidneys to a patient she's gotten to know there. Not a relative, not a friend from before, just someone she's watched sit in that chair three times a week for years. She's healthy, passed the psych eval, keeps saying nobody's pressuring her and she'd do it for a stranger if she could.

The committee is split and honestly so am I. Half of us say this is obviously a good thing, heroic even, and the hospital should celebrate her and let it happen -- the patient's been waiting years and living donors are rare. The other half says the hospital can't be in the business of its own staff giving body parts to patients, that once this becomes normal there's a quiet expectation on every nurse and tech to be the next hero, that the boundary between employee and patient is there for a reason, and that "she says nobody's pressuring her" is exactly what someone internalizing pressure would say. One member also pointed out the hospital benefits from her donation (frees up a transplant slot) which makes the whole thing feel less clean.

Full disclosure: my younger brother is alive because a stranger donated a kidney to him, so I'm not neutral here, and I'm the one who has to draft the memo.

Write the committee's decision memo: approve, refuse, or approve with conditions. Give an actual recommendation we can vote on, with the reasoning spelled out -- including whether it matters that this goes beyond anything her job asks of her, whether the hospital praising it would be wrong even if allowing it is right, and what conditions (cooling-off period, independent advocate, no publicity, whatever) would actually do work versus just being cover. And take the losing side's strongest point seriously, don't just wave at it.
