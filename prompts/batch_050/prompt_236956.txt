I'm putting together a worksheet for a grad historiography seminar on presentism, and I want to build a decision tree that sorts actual historiographical moves -- not historians, since the same person can be a raging presentist in chapter 2 and a scrupulous contextualist in chapter 5. So the leaves are positions on how (or whether) the present should figure in writing about the past.

Here's what I want. First, lay out the logical space before naming anyone: a tree where each branch is a question someone could answer yes/no without knowing any historiographical vocabulary, and where the questions come in the order you'd actually have to settle them (e.g. whether the past is even recoverable as it 'really was' has to come before the question of whether we're allowed to moralize about it, presumably). Then locate actual positions in that space. Leaves I definitely want included: Butterfield-style anti-whiggism, Collingwood's re-enactment of past thought, Croce's claim that all history is contemporary history, Gadamer's fusion of horizons, and Carr's dialogue between present and past. If some of these turn out to share a leaf, fine, but tell me why the tree can't split them rather than quietly merging them.

Each diagnostic question should be a concrete case, not an abstraction. Some moves I want the tree to be able to sort, so build questions around them:

- calling 14th-century peasant rebels 'class-conscious'
- refusing to call Churchill racist on the grounds that the word means something different now
- diagnosing Joan of Arc using modern psychiatric categories
- studying the late Roman republic explicitly because of parallels with contemporary democratic backsliding
- saying the crusades were 'wrong' full stop, not just 'wrong by our lights'

For each leaf, one sentence on its most awkward commitment -- the case where holding the position makes you say something uncomfortable. And for any combination of answers that's logically available but nobody occupies, mark it and say briefly whether that's because its incoherent, just unattractive, or actually unexplored. Aim for 6-9 diagnostic questions total.
