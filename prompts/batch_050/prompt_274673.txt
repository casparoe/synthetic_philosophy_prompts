You are a political philosopher with a specialization in the concept of freedom and its rival analyses. Please think carefully about the following case. I suggest you begin by brainstorming several candidate concepts that might apply, then test each against the details of the case, and only then settle on a verdict. I am not looking for a quick label; I want the carving.

Here is the phenomenon I keep noticing and don't have a word for. A person is never ordered, threatened, blocked, or punished by anyone. No identifiable individual ever steps in her way. And yet she reliably curbs her own conduct because of consequences that would be delivered, if at all, by no one in particular.

Two instances. First: a driver for a ride-hailing platform. No manager ever instructs her. But an opaque rating and job-allocation system means she has learned to accept every ride, to stay polite to abusive passengers, to never log off at mealtimes, and to never voice a complaint in the driver forums. Nothing she avoids doing was ever forbidden; no one ever said a word to her. Second: a junior scholar in a field where jobs are allocated by diffuse consensus. No senior figure ever threatens her, and no rule excludes any topic, but everyone knows which research questions would quietly sink a candidacy, and she chooses her projects accordingly. Again: no censor, no prohibition, no identifiable interferer.

Here is why I think this is not just the familiar kinds of unfreedom. It isn't straightforward coercion or interference, because no one ever actually intervenes, and every option remains formally open -- she could speak, log off, pick the risky topic, and nothing and nobody would physically stop her. It also isn't a mere lack of ability or resources, the way poverty or illness limits what one can do; her capacities are intact, and what constrains her is the anticipated response of her social environment, not her own incapacity. And it doesn't seem to be the classic case of living under a particular person's thumb, because there is no particular person: no one holds a power over her that they might or might not exercise, no one's goodwill is what she depends on. The pressure is agentless, though not natural -- it comes from arrangements humans built and could change.

My questions: Does this phenomenon have an established name in the literature on freedom? If it does, tell me how the established concept handles the two features that seem distinctive here -- the absence of any actual interference, and the absence of any identifiable interfering agent -- and whether standard versions of that concept assume one or the other. If the nearest concepts each fit only imperfectly, I want the mismatches stated explicitly: for each candidate, what does the case have that the concept doesn't cover, or vice versa? And if nothing quite fits, say so plainly and propose a term, with a definition precise enough to decide boundary cases: does the phenomenon persist if the feared consequences would in fact never materialize? Does it require that the subject be aware of the pressure, or can someone be in this condition while sincerely believing they choose freely? Does it count if the constraining arrangement is one the subject herself helped build and endorses? Where exactly does the phenomenon end and ordinary prudence begin?
