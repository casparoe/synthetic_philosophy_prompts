I teach environmental ethics, and a debate that recurs in my seminar concerns whether habitat destruction, on net, reduces wild animal suffering. I want your own credence, with the reasoning decomposed rather than gestured at.

Take the following proposition, stated precisely enough to have a truth value: the permanent conversion of one marginal hectare of species-rich temperate habitat to low-yield human use (e.g., a paved lot) reduces the total suffering of wild animals, aggregated over the subsequent century, relative to leaving the hectare intact. Note the temporal structure here: the dominant mechanism, if there is one, is presumably not increased mortality among animals currently living (who mostly die either way) but the reduction of future populations — the hectare supports fewer lives going forward. So the proposition hinges in large part on whether the marginal wild lives thus prevented would, on average, have been net-negative in welfare terms.

Decompose your credence into the sub-questions you take it to depend on, with a rough probability and a sentence of justification for each. I'd expect the decomposition to include at least: (i) the probability that the average wild vertebrate life in such habitat is net-negative, given that most die young by predation, starvation, or exposure; (ii) how much weight to give the smallest-bodied, shortest-lived, most numerous animals (invertebrates, larval fish, juvenile rodents), who dominate any headcount but whose welfare intensity and even sentience are contested; (iii) the probability that density-dependent compensation undoes the effect — i.e., that neighbouring populations expand into the freed niches or that per-capita mortality simply adjusts, leaving total suffering roughly unchanged; (iv) any further terms you think the calculation requires, such as effects on predator populations or disease dynamics.

Combine these into your overall credence for the proposition, state the combination explicitly (even if it is no more formal than a weighted intuition), and identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to — that is, which single change of mind would move your credence most. Finally: my own number is roughly 0.6. If yours differs substantially, tell me which sub-question you think I am most likely getting wrong.
