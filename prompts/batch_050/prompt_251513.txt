Below are five ethical positions, all of which give special moral priority to suffering. Each is defined solely by what follows; no familiarity with the literature is assumed.

1. **Classical negative utilitarianism:** Only suffering has moral weight. An outcome is better than another exactly to the extent that it contains less total suffering; pleasure and happiness are morally irrelevant except insofar as they reduce or prevent suffering.

2. **Lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism:** No quantity of pleasure, however large, can compensate for any instance of suffering above a threshold of intensity (e.g., torture-level agony). Below that threshold, suffering and pleasure trade against each other on ordinary terms.

3. **Weak negative utilitarianism:** Suffering and wellbeing both matter, but suffering of a given intensity counts for strictly more than wellbeing of the corresponding intensity; the asymmetry is one of degree, not of kind.

4. **Consent-weighted negative utilitarianism:** Involuntarily imposed suffering carries full moral weight, while suffering knowingly and freely accepted by the sufferer (as in mountaineering, childbirth chosen for its own sake, or ascetic practice) carries only a fraction of that weight; pleasure otherwise matters as in weak negative utilitarianism.

5. **Tranquilism:** All that ultimately matters is the absence of craving, distress, or frustration. Pleasure has no value over and above the relief or non-disturbance it constitutes, so an outcome containing perfectly untroubled but joyless lives is as good as one containing ecstatic lives, provided neither contains suffering.

Please rank these five positions from most defensible to least defensible. Assess each against the following three objections:

(a) the *world-destruction objection* — the view appears to endorse the instant, painless annihilation of all sentient life whenever this would prevent any future suffering;

(b) the *pinprick argument* — the view appears willing to sacrifice arbitrarily great goods to eliminate a trivial suffering, such as one person's brief pinprick;

(c) the *motivational-psychology objection* — the view demands a pattern of concern so one-sided that no psychologically plausible human agent could stably live by it, which some take to be evidence against the view itself.

For each position, state its rank and justify the placement in two to four sentences, explicitly noting how it fares on each of (a)–(c). Where two positions survive the objections to a similar degree, rank them by which you judge to preserve more of what commonsense morality gets right. Conclude by identifying the single position-objection pairing you consider the most damaging overall, and briefly say whether the objection is best answered by revising the position, biting the bullet, or denying the objection's force.
