Write a mock advice column — an agony-aunt page with two or three reader letters and the columnist's replies, about 600 words total — whose columnist is a rigorous consequentialist paralyzed by the problem of cluelessness: because every choice ramifies into an unforeseeable future, no two options can ever be rationally compared, whether the question is leaving a job, having a child, or buttering toast. The comedy should come from scrupulous consistency rather than exaggeration for its own sake: each reply must do real philosophical work, engaging at least once with the difference between simply failing to foresee consequences and being unable to assign them any probabilities at all, with the fact that doing nothing is also a choice with fallout, and with whether cluelessness quietly licenses falling back on common-sense morality. The columnist must never notice that publishing an advice column is itself an action with unpredictable consequences — unless that realization is saved for the final line and handled without breaking character. Deadpan throughout, and readable by someone who has never encountered the problem.
