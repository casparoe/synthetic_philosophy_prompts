I've gotten pretty convinved of Robert Brandom's inferentialism, the rough view from Making It Explicit: what makes a bit of language mean what it does isn't a tie to an object or a picture in the head, it's the role the sentence plays in the game of giving and asking for reasons -- what you commit yourself to by asserting it, what would entitle you to it, what follows from it and what it follows from. I like how it demotes reference and representation to byproducts of social practice instead of leaving them as unexplained primitives, and I like that it explains why learning the meaning of a word like "if" feels like learning a skill rather than spotting an object.

Now tell me what would count against it. I don't want generic complaints or a reading list; I want the actual failure modes. Specifically: which phenomena the view handles badly or only by ad hoc patch (words that don't seem to enter into inferences at all, our apparent ability to think about things nobody could give reasons about, the fact that meanings seem more compositional and context-stable than a pure use-based story predicts), which formal or empirical results would genuinely embarrass it, and which criticisms a Brandom-style defender would themselves concede as real costs versus which they'd wave off as missing the point. Rank the defeaters from "this kills the program" down to "awkward but survivable," and be honest about whether the view has been qualified into something unfalsifiable -- if nothing observable could count against it, say so, and tell me whether that's a vice for a theory of meaning or just what theories of meaning look like.

One wrinkle I keep coming back to, and it's probably the test I'd find most decisive: LLMs. By training they're arguably nothing but inferential-role machines, all use and no world-contact. If the inferentialist says LLM outputs genuinely mean something, does that vindicate the view, or does it expose that its bar for meaning was set too cheaply? And if the inferentialist says LLM outputs don't really mean anything, what extra ingredient do they have to bolt on, and does bolting it on quietly break the original program? Take a side on that fork and defend it.
