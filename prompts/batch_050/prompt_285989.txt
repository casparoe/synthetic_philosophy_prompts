A friend and I got into an argument about when it's okay to just believe what people tell you, and I realized I have pretty firm gut verdicts but no idea what rule is generating them. So here are my cases, roughly in the order I'd defend them. A stranger in a city I'm visiting tells me the museum is that way -- I just believe him, no checking his credentials, and that seems fine. My doctor tells me the test came back positive and explains the treatment -- I believe her even though I couldn't evaluate the evidence myself if I tried. A guy I know to be a compulsive liar tells me the bus comes at 5 -- I feel like I get almost nothing from that, maybe literally nothing. My four-year-old tells me there's a monster under the bed -- I don't believe him, obviously, but when he tells me his tummy hurts I believe him completely, even though his track record on tummy aches is also pretty mixed. A stranger tells me the city was founded in 1342 -- I think I believe it, but noticeably less confidently than the museum directions, and I can't tell if that difference is about the topic or the stakes. Someone tells me their back has been hurting for weeks -- total belief, it would be bizarre to ask for evidence. And a friend swears he didn't eat the last cookie while standing next to the crumbs -- zero belief, the testimony does no work at all against the evidence.

The tension I keep hitting: in the directions case I seem to trust someone with literally no track record, which suggests testimony is just... default good, believe unless there's a specific reason not to. But the liar case suggests track record matters, and worse, I genuinely can't decide whether his statement is weak evidence or no evidence -- if it's weak evidence, then default-trust is wrong and I need positive reasons every time, which makes the directions case suddenly look epistemically reckless. And the kid cases seem to demand that the principle be sensitive to what's being said, not just who's saying it, which neither simple view obviously delivers.

So: is there one principle about when testimony justifies belief that captures all or most of these verdicts? State it precisely, tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up (I'd guess the liar one or the museum-vs-founding contrast), and say what it predicts for two cases I'm stuck on: believing a news outlet I haven't checked the reliability of, and believing my friend when she reports what her sister told her about a car accident the sister witnessed.
