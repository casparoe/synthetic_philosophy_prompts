Write a dialogue between two careful reasoners, both well-versed in anthropic reasoning, who disagree about the doomsday argument. One accepts it: given the self-sampling assumption, she holds that discovering I am a randomly sampled observer from the human reference class should shift my credence toward doom soon, since otherwise my birth rank would be improbably early. The other rejects the argument via the self-indication assumption: the mere fact of my existence is evidence favoring worlds with more observers, and this exactly cancels the doomsday update.

Set the dialogue after both have just worked through the presumptuous philosopher thought experiment together, and have them start from their disagreement about what that case actually shows. I want genuine back-and-forth, not two monologues: each should respond to the other's strongest formulations, not their weakest. Along the way, have them engage at least these pressure points: whether the disagreement is really about the choice of reference class or is prior to it; whether SIA's tilt toward observer-rich worlds is a reductio or a bullet worth biting; and whether SSA's consequences in simple urn-style cases (where the sampling procedure is fully specified and unambiguous) are as bad as SIA's consequences in cosmological cases. Neither character should strawman the other, and neither should cave.

By the end, they should have converged on at least one claim both endorse, and they should state, as precisely as they can, the single crux whose resolution would most move each of them.
