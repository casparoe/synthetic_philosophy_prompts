So I'm a novelist, about sixty pages into a book where a small ferry goes down off the coast and seven people end up in a dinghy rated for four. One of them is already half-dead from a crushed leg and going septic, the water will run out in maybe four days, and there's a storm on the horizon that will swamp them if they don't lighten the boat. The problem I'm having is the argument scenes. I keep writing the debate about whether to throw someone overboard and every character ends up sounding like the same guilty liberal having a panic attack in different vocabulary. My writing group read a draft and said the moral conflict felt decorative, like wallpaper, and honestly they're right. What I want is for each character to be working from a genuinely different moral operating system, so that the fight on the boat is a real collision and not just people taking turns saying "but he's a person!"

So here's what I'm asking for: build me a decision tree (or taxonomy, whichever works better) of the positions someone can actually take in that dinghy, starting from the shared premise everyone accepts -- if we do nothing, we all die. Each branch should be a question a real frightened person could answer without knowing any philosophy, stuff like whether killing one to save six is ever OK, whether it matters that the injured guy would probably die anyway, whether drawing lots makes it fair or just launders the decision, whether letting someone die is different from actively killing, whether consent changes everything or nothing. The leaves should be the named positions -- I want the straight consequentialist who'd push the sick guy over tonight, some kind of absolutist who'd rather everyone drown than become a murderer, a lottery person, a consent-first person, a character/virtue type, and the anti-theory "nobody can know the right answer here so we muddle through" person -- plus one sentence per leaf on the ugliest commitment that character has to own. Where the tree genuinely can't seperate two positions by what they'd *do* on the boat, say so explicitly instead of inventing some hair-splitting difference, because for fiction purposes behavior is the test. Also flag any combination of answers that's empty, and tell me if it's empty because the view is incoherent or just because nobody wants to stand there.

And please be commited about it, not a textbook survey. I want you to make definite calls about which questions are the real dividers and which ones just relabel the same underlying stance -- my suspicion is that a lot of apparently different positions collapse into identical behavior once you're actually in the boat, and if I'm wrong about that I want the argument for why, not a polite both-sides. Eight to ten branch questions max, and if you think one of my required leaves (the lottery person, say) isn't actually a coherent independent position, argue that out loud rather than forcing it in to please me.
