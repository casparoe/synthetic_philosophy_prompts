Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have convened a monthly philosophy reading group composed mostly of former colleagues, and we have spent the autumn on egalitarian justice. Our discussions keep foundering on the same shoal: one member insists equality means equal welfare, another equal holdings of resources, a third equal real freedoms, and a fourth insists the whole framing is mistaken because what matters is equal standing in social relations. Because they were trained, as I was, to trust measurement, they tend to pick the currency that sounds most tractable rather than the one they actually believe in. I would like you to construct a diagnostic instrument — a decision tree, or equivalently a quiz with a scoring key — that assigns each reader a position in the "equality of what?" debate purely on the basis of their verdicts about cases, without ever naming a position or asking which label they prefer.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first the branching questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space. I would like the root node to be a question close to my physicist's heart — whether the thing to be equalized is even the sort of thing that admits of interpersonal comparison and measurement (subjective states versus observable holdings versus sets of achievable functionings) — since a negative answer should route a reader away from the welfarist branch early, before any case is considered. Subsequent branch questions should be keyed to concrete vignettes, including at least: a person who has involuntarily acquired expensive tastes she cannot shed; a person with a severe physical disability whose sunny temperament yields high reported welfare; two people who began with identical bundles, one of whom gambled imprudently and lost, considered both before and after the gamble; a healthy, talented person who declines to work in favour of leisure; and two people holding identical resource bundles, one of whom requires twice the inputs to achieve the same level of functioning. Phrase every question so that an intelligent non-philosopher can answer it from the case alone.

The leaves should include: equality of welfare; equality of opportunity for welfare; resource egalitarianism of the ambition-sensitive, endowment-insensitive sort; the capability approach; relational or social equality; and prioritarianism. The capability leaf should itself split on a distinction my group has found slippery — equal capability sets versus equal achieved functionings — and prioritarianism should be handled honestly, since it answers "how much priority?" rather than "equality of what?": either give it an escape route off the tree or flag it as a position the instrument cannot place. Finally, please append a short postscript that (a) flags any pair of leaves the quiz cannot reliably separate, and (b) identifies at least two unoccupied regions of the logical space and diagnoses whether they are uninhabited because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Aim for eight to ten branch questions in total; I would rather the tree be compact and discriminating than exhaustive.
