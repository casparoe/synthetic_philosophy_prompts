I'm writing a chapter that argues advance directives (living wills) are basically little private constitutions, and I want a flowchart for the front of it. Build me a decision tree that takes a reader from the starting commitment "Ulysses was rational to have his crew tie him to the mast" down to a named position -- and I want the same tree run twice in parallel, once for individuals binding their future selves and once for polities binding future generations through entrenched constitutions, with the constitutional questions shadowing the individual ones.

Roughly 10-14 branch nodes. Each node should be a question someone could answer without already knowing which position it leads to -- so concrete cases, not "do you believe in diachronic autonomy?" Questions I want somewhere on the tree, though you can reorganize and rephrase:

- whether binding is justified because the future self's preferences are predictably and temporarily distorted (the siren song as a passing fever, hyperbolic discounting) or wether it can also be justified when the future self has genuinely and permanently changed its values
- whether the binding has to be a real constraint (rope, an eternity clause) or whether merely raising the price of defection counts (betting a friend money that you'll quit smoking; making constitutional amendment procedurally harder but not impossible)
- the dementia case: someone signs a directive refusing life-prolonging treatment once demented, and years later is demented, content, and clearly doesn't want to die -- whose say-so wins?
- whether the bound later self counts as the same agent, and whether weak psychological continuity turns self-binding into coercing a stranger
- Jefferson's worry that the dead shouldn't govern the living -- does each generation owe re-ratification for the binding to be legitimate?
- whether an eternity clause (Germany's Basic Law makes human dignity and the democratic form unrepealable even by amendment) is the same kind of thing as Ulysses's rope or something else entirely

Leaves: please locate at least Elster-style rational precommitment (binding against predictable passion), an incentive-shifting-only view (no genuine constraint, just changed payoffs), precedent autonomy about directives (the Dworkin side), a current-interests-first view (the Dresser side), a Parfit-flavored "the bound self is another person" view, generational sovereignty / anti-entrenchment, eternity-clause absolutism, and process-protecting precommitment (entrenchment fine only where it keeps future self-government possible). If a natural combination of answers has no actual defender, mark it as an empty leaf and say in a sentence wether it's empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate.

One more ask: for each leaf, add one line on how well the individual version of the position maps onto the constitutional version -- does the argument carry over, or does something break when the "self" being bound is a people spread across generations? Keep the whole thing in plain text I can redraw as a flowchart, arrows and all.
