Give me your credence that a system like the following is alive, with the reasoning decomposed. Say we build in a lab a chemical network with no membrane and no cells: a set of catalytic reactions running on a mineral surface that keeps itself going far from equilibrium, replicates its own organization with heritable variation, and over many generations produces reaction pathways nobody designed in. No DNA, no spatial boundary, but genuinely open-ended evolution.

I keep flip-flopping on whether this thing is alive, and I suspect my wobble is actually several separate uncertainties stacked up. Like: does "alive" even have a determinate extension for cases like this, or is it a cluster concept where the question is partly verbal and there's no fact of the matter? If there is a real boundary, does it run through metabolism or compartmentalization rather than evolution? And how much of my judgment is just doing pattern-matching to Earth life as we happen to know it, which might be a terrible guide.

My own gut credence is maybe 0.55, but I'd rather have yours: a rough probability for each of the sub-questions you think the answer actually depends on (feel free to restructure them), how they combine into a bottom-line number, and then which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to and what -- a thought experiment, a result from origins-of-life work, whatever -- would move it most.
