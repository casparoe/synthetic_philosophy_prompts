I'm setting the laws-of-nature question on our doctoral comprehensive exam in metaphysics. I want one essay question, answerable in about an hour, that cannot be handled by defining the best-system analysis and Armstrong-style necessitation relations and then declaring the dispute a standoff. The crux the question should isolate: both camps agree on the distribution of particular matters of fact; the fight is over what, if anything, must be added to make some of its generalizations lawful — governing relations among universals, dispositions built into the properties themselves, or nothing at all. Candidates should have to say, concretely, what explanatory work the anti-Humean's addition performs that the best system does not already do in grounding counterfactuals and objective chances; whether positing a necessitation relation between universals explains why all Fs are Gs or merely gives the regularity a grander name; and how the Humean handles cases where the best system seems to deliver the wrong verdict — for instance, a world in which two kinds of particle never interact (is there really no fact of the matter about what would happen if they did?), or the consequence that what the laws are now depends partly on what happens in the future. The question should be phrased so that a candidate who picks a side without diagnosing what the disagreement is actually about cannot score well. Provide the question itself, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 built around five must-hit moves, stating how many marks each carries and how candidates typically fumble each.
