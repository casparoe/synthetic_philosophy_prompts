I'm a graduate student in philosophy of science preparing a seminar on the classification of mental disorders, and before I read any more proposals for how psychiatry should carve up its domain -- the successive DSM revisions, the NIMH's RDoC framework, network models of psychopathology, dimensional approaches built around a general psychopathology factor, and so on -- I want to fix the standard of evaluation first, so that I can judge each proposal against requirements rather than impressions. Write the desiderata that any adequate classification system for psychiatric conditions must meet, with the case or consideration behind each one.

The list should at least grapple with the following, but it should not merely restate them -- develop them, add whatever I am missing, and flag anything on my list that strikes you as a prejudice of one camp rather than a genuine requirement. First, the system has to cope with Hacking's looping effects: classifying people changes the people classified, who then alter their behavior and self-understanding in response to the classification (through stigma, treatment, or deliberate identification with the label), so that the target of the classification is moving while you aim at it, and the classification itself is partly responsible for the motion. Second, it has to make sense of diagnoses being withdrawn -- as homosexuality was removed from the DSM in the 1970s -- without implying either that the earlier classification was nothing but social prejudice dressed as medicine or, on the other extreme, that everyone diagnosed under it was straightforwardly ill in exactly the way the manual said. Third, it has to handle pervasive comorbidity and fuzzy boundaries without either multiplying disorders indefinitely or collapsing everything into a single general factor. Fourth, it has to serve purposes that pull apart: clinical communication and reimbursement on one side, research into etiology on the other, and arguably patients' own need for self-understanding on a third.

For each desideratum, say whether it is non-negotiable or negotiable, and whose requirement it really is. The part I most want to see is whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: for instance, whether a classification can be both stable enough to accumulate clinical and scientific knowledge over decades and responsive enough to looping effects and social change; or whether reliability across clinicians and validity as a picture of the underlying nature of distress are in genuine tension and not merely hard to achieve at once. If the list itself contains a contradiction, that is a result, and I want it stated plainly rather than smoothed over. Finish with a brief note on which single requirement would knock out the most existing approaches, but keep the scoring short -- the criteria are the deliverable, and I will do the scorecard myself in the seminar.
