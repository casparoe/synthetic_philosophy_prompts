Okay so I cover science and big ideas for a magazine, and for an upcoming package on moral philosophy my editor has asked each of us to file a short "where I actually land" memo that the rest of the team can pick apart before we interview anyone. Here's my attempt on deontology. Tell me whether it holds together:

1. The transplant surgeon does wrong -- seriously wrong -- if he kills one healthy patient to save five who need organs. And I'd go further: it's still wrong even in the version where killing the one is the only way to prevent five *other* people each killing one. You don't get to violate a constraint to minimize violations of that very constraint.
2. I'm fine with the standard trolley verdict: redirecting the train so it kills one instead of five is permissible, maybe even required.
3. What grounds the constraint, for me, is roughly the Kantian thought that persons are inviolable -- a person isn't a resource, and the wrongness of using someone isn't a matter of how the arithmetic comes out. Each person matters in a way that doesn't just add up.
4. That said, I do think an outcome with more rights-violations is morally worse than one with fewer. Five people getting killed is a worse state of the world than one person getting killed. I don't see how anyone denies that.
5. I'm suspicious of the "agent-relative reasons" move people make here -- the idea that it can be wrong for me to do X even while X's happening is better than the alternative. Feels like a cop-out. If one state of affairs is genuinely better, morality should push us toward it, not shrug.

When I read these back I feel like each one is the kind of thing a reasonable person believes, but I have a nagging feeling the set has problems I'm not seeing. Go through them pairwise: which commitments are in genuine tension and how severe is it, which look tense but actually dissolve on inspection (I'd bet at least one pair does), and for each real tension what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to clean up the whole set. I want an audit, not a lecture on deontology generally -- I'm not particuarly attached to any one of these so don't go easy on them, but I do want the final memo to survive the team.
