A political theorist whose seminar I am taking has firm views about when a liberal state may restrict immigration. Her verdicts on the cases discussed so far:

- A state may deny entry to applicants with serious criminal records.
- A state may not exclude applicants because of their religion.
- A state may cap total annual admissions when inflows would outrun the capacity of its schools and housing stock.
- A state may not give preference to applicants who share the majority ethnicity.
- A state may require prospective citizens to learn the national language as a condition of naturalization.
- A state may not refuse an applicant on the ground that she is poor and would likely draw on public assistance, even where the refusal would save money and the state's budget is under no strain.

Two new proposals have now come up for discussion. The first is a points-based admissions system that heavily favors applicants who are young, highly educated, and already hold job offers in high-wage sectors, defended on the ground that such immigrants will contribute more in taxes than they consume in services. The second is a requirement that all admitted immigrants sign a pledge affirming the state's constitutional values, including freedom of expression and the legal equality of men and women, with refusal treated as grounds for denial.

Please predict her verdict on each proposal. In doing so, identify the candidate general rules that are consistent with her entire track record (I can see at least three: a rule permitting only restrictions needed to protect security and the material functioning of institutions; a rule permitting restrictions that track individuals' own conduct and choices but not their unchosen traits or merely expected fiscal costs; and a rule permitting a political community to set the terms of membership so long as it does not rank the worth of persons). State what each candidate rule predicts about each proposal, identify which of the earlier verdicts is the closest analog to each new proposal, give your best guess as to her actual verdicts with reasons, and describe a further hypothetical case that would most efficiently discriminate between whichever candidate rules survive both new proposals. If her record genuinely underdetermines a verdict, say so and state what principle she would be committing herself to with each possible ruling.
