I'm putting together the handout for a graduate reading group I run on AI safety, and I want a decision tree that sorts the main responses to the shutdown problem — i.e. why a sufficiently advanced agent might resist being switched off, and what, if anything, could be built so that it wouldn't. Every term in this field seems to come pre-loaded with a camp, so I want the tree to sort by beliefs rather than by allegiance: each branch should be a question a student could answer having read nothing except a description of the problem itself, and each leaf a named proposal or stance.

Some constraints so the tree doesn't just reproduce the standard literature survey. Start at the commitment "we'd like to build agents that let us shut them down", and make the first fork about whether resistance to shutdown really is instrumentally convergent for almost any objective, or whether that's an artifact of how the scenarios get described — I want a leaf for the sceptical position that the whole thing dissolves under ordinary training, since several of my students hold it and I'd like them placed on the map rather than dismissed. Among the leaves that take the problem seriously, make sure to include: the utility-indifference family associated with Stuart Armstrong (including the correction-term variants), safe interruptibility for reinforcement learners, the proposal to have the agent optimise for what a human would approve of rather than for outcomes, quantilizer-style mild optimisation, the MIRI-ish view that corrigibility has to be loaded in as a value in its own right, and the genuinely pessimistic leaf where the conclusion is that nothing survives a strong enough optimiser so the answer is not to build it. Aim for ten to fourteen leaves in total; add intermediate structure if you need it (e.g. seperating "the problem is misspecifying the objective" from "the problem is maximisation itself"), but every node must be a question about concrete cases, not about labels — "would this agent disable its own off-switch if it knew we'd replace it with a successor whose goals differ slightly?" is the right register, "do you accept instrumental convergence?" is not, rephrase anything like that as a scenario.

Two more things. Flag any pairs of leaves your tree can't really pull apart in practice — I suspect interruptibility and quantilizers come apart cleanly, but I'm less sure approval-direction and value-loaded corrigibility do, and I'd rather the tree admit that than pretend. And for every combination of answers your tree generates that nobody actually defends, add a line saying whether the gap is there because the position is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Finish with a short paragraph on which leaf you'd put your own money on and why, since the students always ask me that and I'd like a second opinion to argue against. British English spellings please, and keep the whole thing under about 1,200 words.
