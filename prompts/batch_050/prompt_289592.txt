I'm trying to figure out what I actually believe about photographic evidence now that convincing fakes are cheap, so let me tabulate my verdicts and ask what they add up to. An 8mm film my aunt shot in 1974 of a family wedding: I believe it completely. ATM security footage used to convict someone: strong evidence, no hesitation. A video my sister filmed yesterday of my parents' dog: I believe it. A viral clip of a senator insulting a reporter, posted anonymously: I withhold judgment entirely, even though it looks flawless. A company's own financial spreadsheet, trivially editable, that happens to incriminate the company: I still take it seriously. Genuine footage that a politician dismisses as a deepfake: I find I can't fully dismiss his dismissal, and that bothers me. Finally, my trust in photographs has dropped over the past decade while my trust in what friends tell me face-to-face hasn't moved at all.

Is there one principle that captures all of this -- say, that a recording's evidential force is now wholly inherited from the credibility of its chain of custody, so that recordings have collapsed back into testimony -- or does something extra have to be said about the old role recordings played as a check on testimony? Which of my verdicts would the simplest version of that principle have to overturn, and what does the winning account predict about cases I haven't listed: a livestream, a screenshot of a text conversation, and a voicemail recording?
