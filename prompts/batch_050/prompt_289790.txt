I'm in my second year of law school and something from my criminal law course has been bugging me for weeks. On the one hand, the legal system seems obsessed with making sure people can find out what the law says before they act: statutes have to be published, criminal laws that are too vauge get struck down entirely, and when a statute is ambiguous judges are supposed to apply whichever reading is more favorable to the defendant. The whole picture seems to be that law is something ordinary people are meant to look up, understand, and organize their behavior around. On the other hand, there's the old rule everyone knows, that ignorance of the law is no excuse. It doesn't matter that the offense you committed is buried on page 900 of some regulatory code nobody reads; you're treated as if you knew it all along.

These two seem to pull in opposite directions to me. If law actually worked the way the first set of doctrines assumes — people find out what's prohibited and steer themselves accordingly — then the ignorance rule seems plainly unfair, because in reality nobody knows more than a tiny fraction of the law, and everyone involved must know that. And if the ignorance rule is fine as it stands, then why all the fuss about publication and clarity and resolving ambiguity for the defendant, since nobody is reading the statutes anyways? I asked my professor about this and she just said both rules are far too well settled to question, and moved on, which wasn't very satisfying.

So here's what I actually want to know: what does this combination of practices presuppose about what law is and what it's for? Is there a single coherent picture of law under which both the publicity-and-clarity doctrines and the ignorance rule make sense together, or do they secretly rest on two different and incompatible theories of law that just happen to coexist in the same system? If they do conflict, which side would have to give, and what would the system look like if it did? And are there pieces of this that don't commit to any theory at all — things that pretty much any account of what law is could live with?
