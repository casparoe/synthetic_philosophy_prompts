So I volunteer with a youth mentoring program and we keep having the same fight at planning meetings, and I've started noticing the same fight everywhere else too. It goes like this. One faction says: kids (or employees, or citizens, whoever) left to their own devices will cut corners, be selfish, take the easy way out, so what they need is clear rules, structure, consequences, habit-building, and only after years of that do you get someone trustworthy. The other faction says: no, people basically want to do well, and piling on rules and surveillance is exactly what crushes that -- give them trust and room and the goodness comes out on its own.

The weird part isn't that they disagree, it's HOW they disagree. Both sides trot out the same studies and the same anecdotes. One side points at the toddler who shares her snack: see, it's in there from the start. The other side points at the same toddler and says: she's been praised for sharing a hundred times, that's training working. The Milgram-y stuff, the bystander stuff, some school's experiment with an honor code -- both factions cite all of it, and each walks away MORE convinced. It's like the disagreement isn't about any fact at all but about which direction causation is allowed to run: does virtue get built from the outside, or uncovered from the inside.

I know this rhymes with that old Chinese argument between Mencius and Xunzi about whether human nature is good or bad. But I'm pretty sure the modern version isn't quite that debate, because nobody in my meetings thinks they're doing metaphysics -- they all think they're just reading the evidence, and neither side is really saying humans are 'evil', more like... neutral material that has to be shaped. So my questions: does this recurring shape of disagreement have an actual name, either in psychology or in philosophy? How close is it really to the Mencius/Xunzi thing, and where does it come apart? And the thing I actually want to know -- is this the kind of dispute that evidence could ever settle, or is something about its structure making it immune to data, such that the same finding will always feed both sides? If there's no established term for that last bit, coin one and define it properly, with what would count as a borderline case. Don't just give me a name and leave, I want the carving.
