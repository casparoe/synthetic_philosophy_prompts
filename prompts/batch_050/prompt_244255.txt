I got into a dumb argument in the comments of a pop-physics blog last week. Someone said that a hypothetical warp drive wouldn't "break" the speed of light, it would just show that relativity wasn't the final theory, and somebody else replied, very confidently, "laws of nature are descriptive, not prescriptive" — as in, the laws don't forbid anything, they just describe what happens, so there's no such thing as "violating" a law of nature. The comment got upvoted to the top, the thread moved on, and it's been bugging me since, because the more I turn it over the less I understand what the slogan is even claiming.

For context, I've absorbed most of my philosophy from years of reading rationalist blogs and forums rather than from actual coursework, so I have a grab-bag of half-digested ideas here. Some candidate readings I can sort of see: (a) a boring linguistic point that "law" is a leftover metaphor from when people imagined God legislating the universe, so of course nature doesn't take orders; (b) an epistemic point that OUR laws are just our current best-fit descriptions and might get revised, which is exactly what the warp drive scenario would show; (c) a stronger metaphysical claim that what really exists is just the mosaic of events, the bare pattern of what happens, and the laws are a summary constructed afterward — so events aren't caused or compelled by laws at all, if anything it's the other way round; (d) I gather some actual philosophers defend the denial of (c), that laws genuinely "govern," which honestly sounds to me like it just relocates the mystery one step.

So: what are the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing, beyond my list if I've missed any? For each, is it trivial, substantive-and-defensible, or just false? And which reading do people who deploy this line actually need it to be for it to do the work in arguments like this one — i.e. to establish that "violating a law of nature" is a category error rather than merely something that never in fact happens? One thing that trips me up: even if laws are "just descriptions," descriptions can be exceptionless and support counterfactuals, so I'm not sure the slogan even blocks talk of physical impossibility. If relativity is true, there's still no warp drive — just not because some cosmic cop stops you.

And please actually take a position. I've seen enough even-handed philosophy explainers where every reading gets a gold star and I'm left to pick my favorite. Tell me which reading you think the slogan most charitably amounts to, whether that reading is true, and whether it wins the comment-thread argument or whether the slogan is mostly functioning as a thought-terminating cliche people reach for when someone takes physics too literally. If you think the whole descriptive/prescriptive framing is a bad way to carve up the options in the first place, that's a fine answer too — but argue for it commitedly, don't just gesture at it.
