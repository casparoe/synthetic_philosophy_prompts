I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy or economics background, and I've run into a distinction I can't find a name for. I keep seeing discussions of repeated gambles or investments where the expected value per round and the typical long-run growth rate disagree about whether to play at all -- the classic case being a game with positive expected value that still drives you broke over time. I believe the divergence itself is sometimes called ergodicity breaking. But my case is different, and I can't tell whether it's the same phenomenon or a separate one.

Concretely: suppose strategy A multiplies your wealth by 1.4 or 0.95 each round, with equal probability, and strategy B multiplies it by 1.8 or 0.6. Both have expected value above 1 per round, and both grow wealth over the long run. But B has the higher expected value per round, while A has the higher typical compounded growth rate. So the two criteria agree that each strategy is worth playing -- no ruin, no sign flip -- and disagree only about which is better. Nobody goes broke under B; they just end up worse off than the A-players, and a large ensemble of B-players would, on average, beat a large ensemble of A-players even though almost every individual B-player loses to almost every individual A-player over enough rounds.

Does this ranking inversion, as opposed to the play/don't-play divergence, have an established name? If it does, I'd like to know how it relates to ergodicity breaking proper, to volatility drag (which as I understand it describes the gap within a single strategy rather than a reversal between two), and to the Kelly criterion (which seems to be a decision rule that sides with the time average rather than a name for the phenomenon). If it doesn't have a name, please coin one, define it precisely, and give the boundary cases: for instance, what happens when the two criteria agree on the ranking but disagree on magnitude, and whether the phenomenon survives if the ensemble average is taken over median rather than mean outcomes. A bare label without this carving would not be useful to me. Please answer in British English.
