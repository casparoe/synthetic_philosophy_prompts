So I just spent like three hours arguing with my friend Marcus about precommitment and we got nowhere, so I want to settle this by finding out what I actually think. The argument started with Ulysses contracts (the psych advance directive kind, not the Odyssey kind, though the Odyssey kind came up too) and sprawled into constitutions and dead generations and whether your past self can legitimately boss around your future self.

Here's my gut verdicts on cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, is behind them:

- Odysseus having his crew tie him to the mast: totally fine, no problem at all
- Me putting my alarm clock across the room so I have to get up to turn it off: fine, obviously
- Signing up for a retirement account that charges a penalty for early withdrawal: fine
- A psychiatric patient, while stable and lucid, signing an advance directive saying "if I go psychotic again and refuse treatment, hospitalize me anyway": I think fine, and this is where Marcus and I really started fighting
- Someone getting an implant that makes them violently ill if they drink alcohol, and the implant is designed to be permanent and unremovable: not fine, or at least really not okay with it
- Selling yourself into indentured servitude for life: obviously not fine
- A constitution that can only be amended by supermajority: fine, that's kind of the point of a constitution
- A constitution with an eternity clause, where certain provisions (like human dignity protections) can never be amended ever, not even by unanimous vote: I actually think this is fine, maybe even good
- Irrevocably selling your right to vote: not fine

The pair I can't reconcile: the eternity clause is fine by me but the permanent anti-alcohol implant isn't. Both are a sober, rational self permanently binding a future self who might later, also sober and rational, genuinely want to change things. Marcus says I'm just being inconsistent and either past selves can rule future selves or they can't. I feel like there's a real difference but I can't articulate it -- maybe something about binding a polity vs. binding a body? Or temptation vs. changed values? idk.

Is there one coherent principle that gets all or most of these? If so, state it precisely and tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up (I suspect at least one has to go). Also tell me what it predicts for a case we didn't even get to: "cooling-off period" laws that let you cancel a timeshare purchase within a few days but not after -- is that the state doing the binding for you, and is it the good kind or the bad kind on the principle?
