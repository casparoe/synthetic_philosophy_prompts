I'm a software engineer, no philosophy degree or anything, but I've been slowly working my way through Gödel, Escher, Bach and got stuck thinking about the MU-puzzle and this whole "jumping out of the system" idea. For anyone who hasn't read it: the puzzle is a tiny string-rewriting system where you start with MI and apply rules like "you can append a U if the string ends in I" and "Mx can become Mxx" and "III can be replaced by U" and "UU can be deleted", and the question is whether you can ever derive MU. The answer is no, but you can't see that from inside the system -- you have to step outside it and notice an invariant (the number of I's is never divisible by three). Hofstadter calls that move JOOTSing.

Here's my actual question. Act as someone who knows GEB inside out, like a professional philosopher who also writes code. Think carefully, and brainstorm a handful of candidates to yourself before settling on your final answer.

I'm collecting examples of genuine JOOTSing -- cases where a problem is unsolvable or invisible from inside some formal/mechanical system, and the only way forward is to step up a level and reason *about* the system instead of *within* it. What I have so far:

1. The MU-puzzle itself (see above)
2. Gödel's incompleteness proof -- arithmetic can't prove its own consistency, you show it by arithmetizing the system and looking at it from outside
3. The halting problem -- no program can decide halting in general, so you "jump out" to a meta-level argument (diagonalization)
4. Tarski on truth -- a language can't contain its own truth predicate, you need a metalanguage
5. A debugger being able to halt and inspect a running program in ways the program can't inspect itself

What I want: one MORE example of JOOTSing that isn't on this list and isn't just a re-skin of something on it (e.g. "the liar paradox" would feel like a rerun of #4, and Rice's theorem is basically #3 wearing a hat). It can come from math, CS, logic, everyday life, wherever, but it has to have the same essential structure: an agent stuck inside a system, plus a genuinely higher vantage point that dissolves or answers the question.

Then tell me honestly: is your example really the same *kind* of jump as the MU-puzzle, or is something subtly different going on? I've noticed people use "meta" super loosely and I suspect half the things called JOOTSing are actually just... normal abstraction. Push back on my list too if one of my five doesn't actually qualify.
