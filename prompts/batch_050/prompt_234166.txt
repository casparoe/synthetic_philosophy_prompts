I work on philosophy of mind and have been asked to circulate a short statement of where I stand on machine minds before a workshop. Before I do, I want to know whether the package actually hangs together. My commitments:

1. Functionalism: mental states are individuated by their causal-functional roles, mentality is multiply realizable, and a silicon system duplicating the relevant functional organization of a human brain would have the same mental life as the biological original.

2. The Chinese Room argument succeeds: merely executing a program, no matter how sophisticated, is never by itself sufficient for genuine understanding or consciousness, because syntax alone does not yield semantics.

3. Implementation is objective: whether a physical system computes a given function is an observer-independent fact, since otherwise everything implements every computation and functionalism is trivialized.

4. Uploading: a neuron-level whole-brain emulation of me, running on a server and interacting with the world only through a text terminal, would be conscious, and I would survive as that emulation — it would be me.

5. Consciousness is causally efficacious: my being conscious is part of what explains why I sincerely and confidently report that I am conscious.

Treat this as an audit rather than an introduction to the literature. Go through the set and tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; for each apparent tension that dissolves, say briefly why. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one state what would have to give and what the cheapest repair costs me elsewhere in the set. Finally, if a single revision would remove more problems than any other, identify it. I have no fixed view about which commitment I would surrender first, so don't assume one.
