A colleague in my department defends the language of thought hypothesis, and over years of argument her judgments about which systems think in Mentalese have settled into a stable pattern. Pre-linguistic human infants, she holds, must already deploy Mentalese, since a natural language could not otherwise be learned. Honeybees that integrate their outbound path and communicate a distance and direction to a food source qualify as well, and so do rats that take novel shortcuts through a familiar maze. On the other side, she denies Mentalese to Block's imaginary machine that converses by consulting a vast pre-recorded script of possible conversations, and to a trained feedforward image classifier. I want to predict her verdict on a current large language model, whose fluent output extends to sentences never previously produced but whose internal states are distributed activation vectors rather than discrete symbols. Please identify at least two principles, each consistent with all five of her prior verdicts, that deliver opposite verdicts on this new case; say which principle you take her actually to be running and why; and describe a further case, real or hypothetical, whose verdict would discriminate between the two principles.
