About a year ago I cut off my oldest friend after he lied to me about something pretty big (long story, involves money he borrowed and where it actually went). Since then I've been trying to sort out what I actually believe about forgiveness and loyalty, because my feelings are a mess. Here's the weird part though. I'll come up with some principle that sounds right, like "you should forgive people who genuinely repented," and then I test it against cases -- like, would I forgive my aunt who did something similar? And when the principle gives an answer that feels totally wrong in the case, I tweak the principle. But then sometimes it goes the other direction: my gut reaction to some case feels off once I hold it up against the principle, so I basically talk myself out of the gut reaction. I keep going back and forth like this, adjusting the general rule, then adjusting my take on specific situations, round and round.

My brother listened to me explain this whole process and said it sounds like I'm just moving the goalposts until I land on whatever conclusion I wanted anyway -- like if the rule and the intuition can both be changed to fit each other, how is that different from rationalizing? And honestly I couldn't give him a good answer. But it also doesn't feel like pure self-deception; it feels like I'm getting somewhere, like the rule and the cases are slowly disciplining each other. So who's right? Is this back-and-forth method a legitimate way of figuring out what you think, or is my brother onto something -- and if it is legitimate, what would distinguish it from just dressing up a prejudice as a philosophy?
