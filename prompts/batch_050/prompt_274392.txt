Longtime LW/SSC reader here, and I keep hitting a move in comment sections that I want to pin down. Latest instance: somebody posted the grandmother hypothesis -- the idea that human menopause and long post-reproductive lifespans are an adaptation because grandmothers boost their grandkids' survival, so genes for stopping fertility early and helping instead got selected for. First comment with a bunch of upvotes is just "this is a just-so story" and the thread basically treated that as a refutation and moved on.

The phrase obviously comes from the Kipling thing, how the leopard got his spots etc., but as used it seems to mean like five different things depending on who's swinging it. Can you lay out the menu of precise charges someone might be making when they call an evolutionary explanation a just-so story? Off the top of my head I can see candidates like: the hypothesis is unfalsifiable in principle; it's falsifiable in principle but the only evidence offered is the very fact it was cooked up to explain; it assumes the trait is an adaptation at all rather than a byproduct or drift, without arguing for that; it never compares against rival adaptationist explanations that fit the same data; the storyteller could've spun an equally plausible story for the opposite trait, so the story has no evidential weight; or just "this feels post hoc and I want to register that." Feel free to carve it differently, I'm not attached to my list.

For each reading: is it a platitude about method, a substantive but defensible charge, or actually false (I'm pretty sure at least one common reading is false, since some ev psych hypotheses have been tested and confirmed or shot down)? And then the part I actually care about: which readings, if any, are strong enough to justify what the commenter did -- dismissing the grandmother hypothesis out of hand, rather than lowering credence a bit or asking for a specific kind of evidence? If none of them justify that, what's the honest version of the complaint the commenter should have made instead?
