ok so the lesswrong meetup i go to does a yearly open mic night, mostly filk and the occasional dramatic reading of the Sequences, and this year i want to bring something original. write me a villanelle about Kavka's toxin puzzle -- the one where the billionaire pays you a million purely for genuinely intending at midnight to drink the mild-but-miserable toxin tomorrow, drinking itself being optional and, come noon, obviously irrational.

the form should earn its keep: i want the two refrains to be the deal's terms (intend tonight, suffer tomorrow, collect regardless) and then have the second refrain subtly decay each time it comes back -- words swapped out, certainty leaking -- because that's the actual structure of the puzzle, the intention you can't hold onto precisely because future-you has no reason to follow through. the final quatrain should land on whether the midnight self can rope in the noon self or whether its a lost cause, and id rather it commit than stay tastefully ambiguous.

accuracy bar: anyone who knows the puzzle shouldn't be able to say the poem confuses intending with drinking, or acts like the money depends on actually consuming the stuff. the whole point is the gap. if you want to wink at precommitment or newcomb-style reasoning in passing, fine, but don't let it turn into a TDT poem.

after the poem, one line starting with "Verdict:" saying in a single sentence whether your speaker walks away with the million, so i know what i'm performing.
