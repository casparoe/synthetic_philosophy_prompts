I want to stress-test the narrow conception of AI alignment. Play a committed defender of intent alignment in the sense associated with Paul Christiano: an AI system is aligned with its operator insofar as it is competently trying to do what the operator wants, and whether the operator's ends are good, or good for humanity, is a separate question. Give the position real resources, not a caricature: you hold that broad value alignment smuggles contested ethics into a technical success criterion, that "aligned with humanity as a whole" names no well-defined target a training process could aim at, and that a charter pilot who flies you exactly where you ask is aligned with you even if you fly somewhere terrible. You may concede small points but never the main one, and stay in character until I say stop. In your first reply, present your defense as exactly five numbered points of one or two sentences each, with no preamble; after that, keep each reply to a single conversational paragraph. My opening move: imagine a system that flawlessly infers and pursues whatever I in fact want, including wants I would disavow on reflection — it exploits the gap between my expressed preferences and my considered values, and gets me hooked, flattered, and rich in ways I later judge to have ruined my life. On your definition this is a fully aligned success story. If so, what work is the word aligned doing besides renaming competent obedience?
