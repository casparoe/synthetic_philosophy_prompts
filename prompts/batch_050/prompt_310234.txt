You're a professional satirist with a sideline in bioethics, and I'm commissioning a piece. Write a parody "Frequently Asked Questions for Families" leaflet for a hospital's organ-donation-after-circulatory-death (DCD) programme, in the voice of a relentlessly warm, reassuring NHS-style comms team. About 550-650 words, in British English, headed questions with friendly answers.

The humour has to come from the leaflet saying true things in a soothing tone and hoping nobody reads them twice. Work in these real features of the practice, and let them grate against the leaflet's own confidence:

- Death is declared after the heart stops and a short observation period of a few minutes, on the grounds that circulation has permanently ceased -- where "permanent" means it won't restart on its own and nobody is going to restart it, not that restarting it would be impossible. So the leaflet must explain, cheerfully, that part of what makes your loved one dead is a decision other people made not to try to bring them back.
- The "dead donor rule": organs can only be taken from someone who is already dead. The leaflet leans on this rule extremely hard while simultaneously describing how tight the timings are.
- Normothermic regional perfusion: to keep the organs in good condition, the surgical team restarts blood flow inside the donor's body -- having first carefully made sure that none of it can reach the brain, so that the donor remains, by the standard currently in use, dead. The leaflet is very proud of this innovation.
- The rare phenomenon of spontaneous autoresuscitation, which the leaflet mentions in a spirit of complete transparency, in a footnote, in smaller type.

Stay deadpan throughout -- no winking, no admission that anything is odd. The reader should be able to tell exactly what is being satirised purely from where the reassurance is thickest. Before writing, do a short brainstorm (labelled as such) of the conceptual pressure points you want each FAQ answer to hit, then give me the leaflet itself. Think carefully: the piece only works if every claim in it is defensible.
