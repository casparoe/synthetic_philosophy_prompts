A number of philosophers and commentators have recently argued that the false outputs of large language models are best classified as bullshit in roughly Harry Frankfurt's sense: not lies, because lying requires an intent to deceive about what one takes to be true, and not mere errors, because errors are failed attempts at truth whereas these systems, on the standard picture, are not attempting anything at all with respect to truth -- they generate plausible text, indifferent to whether it is accurate. Take this classification as the position under examination and play it straight: what would count against it? Consider, at minimum, the following directions, and feel free to add others. First, the argument depends on attributing to the system an attitude of indifference toward truth; but indifference is itself an attitude, so it is unclear whether a system with no propositional attitudes at all can even qualify as a bullshitter -- perhaps the classification fails in the opposite direction from what critics assume, leaving only the category of error. What evidence would settle whether indifference, rather than absence of any truth-related stance, is the right description? Second, training regimes that penalize falsehood and reward accuracy, or architectures that ground outputs in retrieved documents, might constitute a functional analog of caring about the truth without any conscious regard for it; would that defeat the classification, and would defenders of the view accept functional rather than phenomenal regard for truth as sufficient? Third, there is a live rival category, confabulation -- the human kind involves fluent false reports produced without intent to deceive and arguably without indifference either -- so what observation about an LLM's behavior or training would favor the bullshit classification over confabulation, and is there any such observation at all, or does the classification float free of anything checkable? Finally, distinguish the artifact from the agent: the produced text might be bullshit-like in its relation to truth even if the system is no bullshitter, or the system's designers and deployers might be the real locus of the indifference. For each proposed defeater, say whether it would be decisive or merely awkward, state what a defender of the bullshit classification would herself concede as counting against the view, and give an honest verdict on whether the classification is a falsifiable claim about these systems or has quietly become an unfalsifiable piece of rhetoric.
