I’m a software engineer without formal training in economics or philosophy, and I’m trying to turn the socialist calculation debate into a question with an actual answer rather than a contest of slogans.

Consider this proposition:

For an industrialized economy of at least 50 million people, a democratically accountable planning system could outperform or roughly match a developed mixed-market economy over a 20-year period in allocating investment, intermediate goods, and production capacity, even if the planning system had excellent contemporary or near-future sensors, databases, optimization methods, and machine-learning systems—but no general market in producer goods, no competing firms able to commit capital independently, and no decentralized residual claimants.

Please give me your numerical credence in that proposition, with the reasoning decomposed rather than merely summarized. At minimum, separate and assign rough probabilities to:

1. whether the relevant economic knowledge can in principle be made explicit and transmitted to a planning center;
2. whether the resulting allocation problem is computationally tractable at national scale under continuously changing conditions;
3. whether people and local organizations would report usable information honestly given their incentives;
4. whether a non-market system could generate and evaluate genuinely new possibilities, rather than merely optimize among options it already recognizes;
5. whether political authority over the plan would preserve error-correction rather than entrench incumbent projects and metrics; and
6. whether “roughly match” should be judged mainly by consumer welfare, productive efficiency, innovation, resilience, or some weighted combination of these.

Feel free to modify or subdivide these terms if mine are not independent. State how the component probabilities combine, avoid double-counting the same mechanism under multiple headings, and give both the resulting number and a one- or two-sentence justification for each component. Then identify the component to which your final credence is most sensitive, the strongest argument on the other side, and what kind of real-world evidence or technological development would change your estimate most.

Please distinguish failure to calculate an optimum from failure to discover what should be optimized, and do not let the answer turn on whether planners could reproduce today’s market prices after the fact.
