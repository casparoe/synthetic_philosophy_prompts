I'm taking a graduate seminar on consciousness and the timing of experience, and last week we discussed the color phi phenomenon: a red dot is flashed at one location on a screen, then a green dot is flashed at a nearby location, and under the right timing conditions observers report seeing a single dot move across the screen, changing from red to green somewhere around the midpoint of its path. The puzzle we spent the session on is that the second dot only appears after the first, so the experience of the dot changing color mid-trajectory seems to require that the brain somehow presented the color change as occurring at a time when the green dot hadn't yet been shown. Our seminar labeled the two standard hypotheses the Orwellian one -- the subject actually experiences two stationary dots, red then green, but memory is retrospectively revised so that the motion and mid-path color change are all they can recall -- and the Stalinesque one -- some pre-conscious editing process fabricates the moving, color-changing dot before anything reaches consciousness at all, so the experience itself contains the revision. I've tried to turn our discussion into an argument, and it seems to prove something I can't accept, so either I've gone wrong somewhere or a premise I like has to die.

Here it is. (1) If there is a genuine fact about whether an experience was produced Stalinesquely before consciousness or revised Orwellianly after it, then there must be something -- some report, memory, behavioral measure, or neural measurement -- that could in principle distinguish the two. (2) In the color phi case, the two hypotheses agree on everything the subject says, remembers, and does, and on every measurement taken at or after the second flash, since the difference between them concerns only how the final experiential content came to be where it is. (3) So in this case, nothing could distinguish the hypotheses. (4) Where no possible evidence could distinguish two hypotheses, there is no fact of the matter as to which is true. (5) So there is no fact of the matter about when or how the color change entered consciousness; the question of Orwellian versus Stalinesque is simply empty, which is the conclusion our seminar flirted with. (6) But exactly the same reasoning applies to any experience whatsoever: take my present experience of reading this page -- any evidence I could produce about it consists of reports, memories, or measurements, and for each such piece of evidence there is an Orwellian-style hypothesis (the experience happened one way and the record was rewritten) and a Stalinesque-style hypothesis (the record was pre-fabricated) that accommodate it equally well, so by (1) through (4) there is no fact of the matter about what any of my experiences are like, including the one I'm having right now. (7) That conclusion is absurd; there is plainly something it is like to be me right now, and a fact about it, even if I can't fully articulate it. (8) So at least one of (1) through (5) must be wrong.

My problem is that I can't locate the wrong step, and my attempts to block (6) keep reinstating the argument. If (4) is the culprit, I want to know why verificationism seemed compulsory in the color phi case but optional in the ordinary case -- is there something about sub-second timing and the resolution of report that changes what's at stake, or is that special pleading? If (2) is the culprit, I'd like to hear which actual or imaginable measurements could bear on the choice and whether they'd settle the question or merely relabel it. And if the real fault is the leap from (5) to (6), I'd like it stated precisely: what extra premise does the leap need, why does the color phi case appear to supply it, and why doesn't it? I'd most like to keep (1), (4), and (7) if I can, and I'm willing to concede that Dennett-style deflationism about the timing question might be correct for the laboratory case -- what I'm not willing to concede is that my current experience has no fact of the matter. Tell me which step to reject, why it looked like it followed, and what each of the main exit routes costs.
