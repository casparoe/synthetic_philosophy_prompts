I run a small decision theory reading group (mostly LW/ACX diaspora folks, we've worked through the sequences and the FDT paper) and I'm writing a mock final to close out the unit. I need one essay question, answerable in about an hour, on counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail. The trap I want to avoid is a question people can answer by defining updatelessness, saying precommitments are good, and going home. The crux I actually want to test: in one of these two scenarios, paying up is the embarrassing outcome that indicts your decision theory, and in the other, refusing to pay is. Students should have to say which is which and pinpoint exactly what structural difference does the work -- roughly, whether the other agent's behavior is downstream of your policy (Omega's prediction) or whether only your evidence gets shuffled while the world stays put (the termites dont care what you transfer). Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 with the must-hit moves, plus a note on the specific wrong turns weaker answers will take (like assuming Omega causally reaches back and changes the coin, or treating the termite infestation as correlated with the payment).
