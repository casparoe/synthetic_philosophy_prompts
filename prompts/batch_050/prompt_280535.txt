Okay this is going to sound a bit unhinged but I promise there's a real question at the end. My mom is scheduled to get a deep brain stimulator put in next month for her Parkinson's, and somewhere between the surgeon walking us through the hardware and me reading philosophy forums at 2am I've become genuinely obsessed with the question of how much of a brain you could swap out before the person stops being the same person. The thing is, my gut gives me confident answers case by case, but when I line them up they don't seem to follow any rule I can state. So here are my verdicts, tell me what principle I'm committed to (if any):

1. One neuron dies and gets replaced by a tiny machine that does exactly what the neuron did, same inputs same outputs. Obviously still my mom. Nobody blinks at a cochlear implant or a pacemaker, this feels the same.

2. Over ten years, all of her neurons get replaced one at a time by such machines, each swap seamless, she never notices anything, her personality and memories intact the whole way through. Still my mom. Feels basically like ordinary cell turnover, just with fancier material.

3. The exact same total replacement, but it's done in a single marathon surgery over twelve hours. Suddenly I have the strong feeling she dies on the table and something else wakes up. I know this is the same endpoint as #2, that's why I'm writing this.

4. Gradual replacement over ten years like #2, EXCEPT the extracted neurons are kept alive at the lab and slowly reassembled into a functioning brain, which when switched on claims to be my mom and has all her memories. Now I want to say the jar is her and the person walking around is the imposter. Which directly contradicts what I said in #2, so something's broken in my head.

5. Instant destructive scan: her brain is mapped perfectly, destroyed in the process, and the upload runs on a computer. Definitely death plus a copy. Not her.

6. Same scan but non-destructive, upload is made while she keeps living her life. Obviously the upload is just a copy and the real her is the biological one. But I notice that if copies are "just copies" here, I'm not sure what work the gradualness was doing in #2 either.

So: is there one principle about what makes a person the same person over time that delivers all six of these verdicts, or even most of them? If yes, state it precisely enough that I can see which of my cases is doing which work. If no, tell me which verdict you'd overturn and why that one is the cheapest to give up. And whatever principle you land on, tell me what it says about a case I haven't listed: the replacement happens gradually like #2 but is paused for six months halfway through because of an insurance dispute, so for half a year she's running on exactly 50% biological neurons, 50% chips.
