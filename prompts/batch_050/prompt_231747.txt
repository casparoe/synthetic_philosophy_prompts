I'm putting together a worksheet for my undergrad ethics seminar on what adult children owe their parents, and I want a decision tree that lands students on a position rather than a vocabulary list. Start from "your parents raised you decently" and branch on questions like: do the sacrifices your parents made ground a duty all by themselves, even if you don't like them now? does the current relationship have to be voluntary and reciprocal for any obligation to survive? can a debt of gratitude ever be fully paid off? does mere biology create anything? One leaf has to be Jane English's friendship model (duties track the ongoing friendship, not the past), and I also want leaves for the debt/gratitude view, a Confucian-style filial piety view, and whatever other live positions your questions naturally generate -- plus a note on any combination of answers nobody seems to defend and why (incoherent, or just unpopular?). Phrase each node as a concrete case (e.g., the devoted parent you now find insufferable, the estranged father who paid child support but nothing else) so students can answer without knowing the labels, and flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate.
