I'm preping a position note for a reading group on Sartre and want to know whether my view actually hangs together. My commitments are roughly these:

1. Radical freedom: no motive, emotion, social role, or value can determine an action by itself. Such things become reasons only within projects I freely sustain. Coercion can drastically narrow my options, but it cannot literally choose my response for me.

2. Facticity is nevertheless real and not just interpretation. My body, past, class position, and the image other people have of me are not chosen, and they constrain what is possible independently of how I think about them.

3. There is no Freudian dynamic unconscious hiding behind consciousness. Every conscious state is pre-reflectively self-aware, even when its content is not explicitly thematized. Bad faith works by keeping something non-thematic or unstable, not by locking knowledge away in an unconscious chamber.

4. Bad faith requires a flight from freedom. So merely being absorbed in a role, conforming to expectations, or believing something false is not enough. The waiter who performs waiterness too completely is in bad faith only if he has some pre-reflective grasp that the role is not his whole being and uses the performance to evade that grasp.

5. A lifelong unreflective person who simply identifies with being a waiter and has never had even a fleeting lucid recognition of alternatives is not yet in bad faith. He becomes in bad faith only after recognizing his freedom and then telling himself that the role compels him.

6. In shame before another person, I discover a being-for-others that I did not constitute and cannot eliminate simply by reinterpreting it. But another person still cannot fix the meaning of my life, since I remain free to take up their image in different projects.

7. There is no pre-given human nature, no objective table of values, and no choice that is right independently of a freely chosen project. Still, Sartre is entitled to condemn bad faith universally, because it is always an evasion of the freedom that makes valuing possible.

Please audit the package rather than give me a general summary of Sartre. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and which apparent tensions dissolve once distinctions like thematic versus pre-reflective awareness or facticity versus meaning are kept straight? Rank the real problems by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and identify the single revision that would restore the most consistency with the least collateral damage.
