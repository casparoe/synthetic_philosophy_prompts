Write a short dialogue between two philosophers of action arguing about weakness of will. One defends the hyperbolic discounting picture: so-called akrasia is really just time-inconsistent preference, the near-term reward gets overweighted as it gets close, so strictly speaking nobody ever acts against what they judge best *at the moment of acting* — the judgment just flips. The other defends the view that genuine akrasia means failing to stick with a resolution or better judgment you still hold, and that the discounting story explains the phenomenon away rather than explaining it.

Have them fight over this concrete case: someone resolves to quit caffeine, does fine for weeks by refusing to ever re-open the decision, then relapses — but the relapse happens late at night while planning the next morning, hours away from any actual coffee, not when the espresso machine is right in front of them. The discounter should have to explain how their account handles a lapse that occurs when the temptation is temporally distant (their model predicts lapses when the reward gets close), and the resolution theorist should have to explain what "still holding the judgment" even amounts to if the person sincerely deliberated fresh and ended up endorsing the coffee.

Both should be equally sharp, no strawmen, and they should actually engage with each other's arguements rather than talk past each other. Keep the dialogue under 450 words. After it, add one or two sentences saying how confident you are that you represented both positions fairly, and name the specific point you're least sure you got right.
