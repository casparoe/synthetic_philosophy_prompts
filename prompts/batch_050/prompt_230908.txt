So my philosophy club (yes our high school has one, theres like 6 of us) had this big argument last week and it's still bugging me. We were talking about this Quine guy our advisor mentioned, and his paper about the analytic/synthetic distinction, and two club members basically ended up yelling at each other. Here's how it went as best I remember:

Jake started by saying Quine is obviously wrong that there's no real analytic/synthetic distinction. Like, "all bachelors are unmarried" is true just because of what the words mean, and "there's a coffee shop on Main street" is true because of the world. Those feel like totally different kinds of true, and if a distinction is that obvious to everyone, a philosopher can't just come along and say it doesn't exist.

Maya said he's attacking a strawman because Quine never denied the difference FEELS obvious. His point was that nobody can actually explain what "true by meaning alone" amounts to without going in circles: you define analytic in terms of synonymy, synonymy in terms of definition, definition in terms of meaning, and it all just loops. A feeling of obviousness isn't an explanation.

Jake said fine, but then Quine's argument proves way too much. If you can't pin down what a sentence means well enough to say some truths are analytic, then you also can't say two sentences mean the same thing, or that a translation is correct, or even what anyone ever said. And we clearly do all that stuff every day. So whatever the problem is with "analytic," it's a problem for all of language, not a special reason to throw out the distinction.

Maya came back with: that's exactly Quine's point though. He's happy to say meaning and translation ARE messy and indeterminate, the messiness doesn't bother him, it's the philosophers who built this whole load-bearing wall on analyticity (like the logical positivists using it to explain math and logic) who have the problem.

Then Jake said something I couldn't follow as well, about how math being analytic was never really about words like "bachelor" anyway, and that Quine lumps together explaining necessity with explaining synonymy, and those might be different projects. Maya said he was just changing the subject to save the distinction.
