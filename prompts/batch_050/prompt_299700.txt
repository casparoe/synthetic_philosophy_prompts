Next month I'm presenting at our joint phil-of-mind / cog-sci colloquium and I need to not get destroyed in the Q&A. My talk defends a reading of Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment: I claim the simms (the symbol-level things) genuinely do causal work on the simmballs (the substrate-level things that make them up), and that this is the right template for how thoughts cause neural events. My argument, roughly: (1) the simmball-level dynamics is locally "meaningless" — each simmball just reacts to its neighbors — so the simmball-level causal story, while complete, misses all the real generalizations; (2) counterfactuals and interventions run at the simm level: if the simms had been configured differently, different simmballs would have fired, and it's the simm-level pattern that screens off; (3) so "symbols pushing the substrate around" isn't sloppy talk or a competing cause that violates closure — it's the level where the causation is visible, and downward causation is just upward constitution plus level-relative causal generalization. I call the position "causation at the level of the description that does the predictive work" (name still in progress, dont judge).

The audience: two philosophers of mind who are sympathetic to Kim-style causal exclusion worries and will pounce on anything that smells like causal overdetermination; one neurodynamicist who thinks the whole symbols-and-substrate picture is GOFAI nostalgia and that brains don't have a clean two-level architecture like the careenium does; and a couple of ML interpretability people who will want to know whether "features in superposition" are simms or simmballs and whether my story even applies to systems where the levels aren't cleanly separable.
