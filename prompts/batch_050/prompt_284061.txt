So for the past few months I've been in an informal reading group on humour (we started as a joke, ironically) and I've been keeping a running list of the main theories of why things are funny. Here's where I'm at: (1) superiority theory -- the Hobbesian idea that laughter is basically a little rush of feeling better than someone else, which explains why so many jokes have a butt; (2) incongruity theory -- humour comes from an expectation getting violated, the setup builds one frame and the punchline yanks you into another; (3) relief theory -- the Freudian-ish one where laughter discharges nervous energy that got built up around taboo or anxious material, which is supposedly why sex and death jokes hit so hard; (4) benign violation theory -- something has to seem wrong or threatening AND simultaneously be appraised as actually fine, with the humour living in that double appraisal. I've also got (5) the idea that laughter is fundamentally a play signal, like the panting dogs do during play-fighting, whose real job is social bonding rather than responding to joke structure at all.

Here's my problem: I feel like this list has converged on everything the standard literature offers, and I want at least one theory on it that ISN'T a rewording of these. When I test myself, everything I come up with collapses into one of them -- like, "humour as status negotiation" is just superiority with extra steps, and "humour as surprise" is just incongruity. So: can you propose a genuinely sixth theory of humour? It needs to actually disagree with these about what laughter is FOR or what triggers it, not just combine two of them or specialize one of them to a subcase. I'd also like it to do some work the others do badly -- for instance, none of my five really nails why you can't tickle yourself, or why puns reliably get groans but people keep compulsively making them anyway, or why nervous laughter shows up at funerals where nothing is a "violation appraised as benign" so much as just raw distress. If your theory can explain at least one of those stubborn cases better than my list does, that's the gold standard, but I'd also accept it just covering a different patch of phenomena convincingly. Oh, and tell me honestly whether you think your theory is actually good or just novel -- those arent the same thing and I'd rather know.
