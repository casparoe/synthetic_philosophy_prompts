I'm a novelist and I was at a residency last month where two other writers, let's call them A and B, got into a long arguement over wine about whether you can actually trust introspection, and it's been bugging me since because it matters a lot for how much faith I put in my characters' inner lives. Round one: A said introspection is basically confabulation, and pointed to those experiments where people pick which of two photos they find more attractive, the photos get sneakily swapped, and people happily explain why they chose the face they actually rejected. So self-reports are stories we invent after the fact. Round two: B said fine, but that only shows we're bad at knowing *why* we did something, not that we're wrong about what we're currently experiencing. You can't be mistaken that you're in pain right now, or that you're seeing red. Round three: A countered that people a few generations back sincerely reported dreaming in black and white and almost nobody does now, so even reports of raw experience drift with whatever culture tells us experience is like, and besides, people can't even agree on whether their peripheral vision is vivid or sketchy, which should be trivially easy to check if introspection worked. Round four: B said the dream thing is a report from memory about a past state, which is contaminated in ways that catching an experience in the moment isn't, and anyway some unreliability doesn't equal total unreliability -- eyesight is fallible too and nobody calls vision a fiction. Then A's last move: B's fallback of "it's mostly reliable" is unfalsifiable, because every failure gets reclassified as a memory problem or a report problem rather than an introspection problem, so introspection never has to lose. Please adjudicate this move by move, telling me for each round who's actually right and why, plus what you think both of them are missing. Take real positions rather than laying out both sides and letting me choose. And please just write it as flowing prose, no headings or bullet points, like a letter back to me.
