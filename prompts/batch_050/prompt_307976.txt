The exchange between Searle and Dennett over original and derived intentionality appears to me to have produced no genuinely new moves in decades. Searle holds that intrinsic intentionality is a biological phenomenon: only minds — in fact, only certain brain processes — genuinely mean anything, and the content we ascribe to artifacts such as thermostats, books, and vending machines is derived from the intentions of their designers and users, so that the artifact considered in itself means nothing at all. Dennett's rejoinder, built around his two-bitser case — a machine constructed to accept US quarters, then shipped to Panama, where quarter-balboas are physically indistinguishable from quarters, leaving apparently no fact of the matter about which coins it is really a detector of — is that derivedness admits of no principled stopping point. Our own intentionality is itself derived, from natural selection rather than from a designer, and the appearance of a special underived stratum inside the head is an artifact of taking the intentional stance toward ourselves.

So far as I can see, both parties accept the same inventory of cases and dispute only what the cases establish. My working hypothesis is that this is not a disagreement about a phenomenon at all but a turf war over the word meaning: Searle is answering a question about the phenomenology of understanding, Dennett a question about naturalized content-ascription, and each side's favored cases are constructed so as to beg the question against the other.

But consider a case that seems to fall outside both templates. Engineers build a coin sorter by running an automated search over possible discriminator circuits, selecting candidates solely for throughput on a mixed stream of genuine quarters and slugs. The resulting machine reliably accepts quarters, yet no person ever represented quarter-hood — the engineers' intentions fixed the search procedure, not the content — and no evolutionary history selected for it either. The machine's disposition was shaped by a selection-like process whose criteria never mentioned coins at all. On Searle's view it would seem to possess no intentionality whatsoever; on Dennett's it is unclear whether its content is quarters, coin-sized metal discs, or nothing determinate.

Diagnose the standoff. Is the disagreement verbal, substantive, or a matter of two research programs answering different questions under one name? Is there a hidden question doing the work — about whether function-talk is observer-relative, for instance — or are these bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? Does the automated-search case sharpen the diagnosis, dissolve it, or merely multiply intuitions? And given your diagnosis, what, if anything, would count as progress: a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, or the admission that the question should be retired?
