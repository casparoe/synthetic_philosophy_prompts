I'm a PhD student in machine learning; my dissertation concerns how learned models behave under distribution shift, and I think fluently in terms of input-output functions, training distributions, test-time interventions, adversarial perturbations, and post-hoc safety filters. I have almost no background in metaphysics, but I recently encountered the literature on dispositions, finks, and masks -- Martin's electro-fink and the reverse cycle variant, Bird's antidotes, and the surrounding debate about whether a masked disposition is retained or lost -- and I have a strong suspicion that the whole debate has a counterpart in vocabulary I already command. I'd like you to test that suspicion by translating it.

Concretely, I want three translations, each as faithful as you can make it:

(1) The simple conditional analysis of dispositions -- roughly, that a fragile glass is one that would shatter if struck -- and its refutation by finks and masks. Restate both the analysis and the counterexamples in terms of a model's input-output function, its behavior on inputs far from the training distribution, and interventions applied at inference time.

(2) The distinction between a fink, where the disposition itself is removed by the stimulus or the conditions, and a mask, where the disposition remains but its manifestation is blocked. My working hunch is that a fink is like replacing the model -- the weights change, so there is nothing left of the original mapping -- whereas a mask is like wrapping an unchanged model in a filter that intercepts certain outputs, or appending a fixed suffix that reroutes them. Tell me whether that hunch survives scrutiny, and where exactly it strains.

(3) The retreat from the simple conditional analysis to views invoking ideal or standard conditions, or intrinsically characterized dispositions. In my field, the natural analog would be specifying the deployment conditions under which a model's observed behavior actually reveals what it is disposed to do. Say whether anything in ML practice genuinely corresponds to the metaphysicians' move here, or whether the correspondence breaks down.

Then give me the residue report: which claims translated cleanly, which you had to distort to fit, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. I am especially unsure about the question that seems to drive the literature -- whether the masked object still has the disposition at the very moment its manifestation is blocked. Does "the model would have produced that output absent the filter" settle this, or is there a question about the model's intrinsic state that my purely functional, counterfactual vocabulary cannot even formulate? Finally, tell me which way the residue cuts: does it indicate something my framework is missing, or does it suggest that part of the metaphysical dispute dissolves once you refuse to distinguish a system's dispositions from its counterfactual behavior?
