So I've been reading rationalist blogs and econ forums for like a decade, and there's one fight that never dies: methodological individualists saying every social-science explanation has to bottom out in individual people with beliefs, desires, and incentives, and holists saying no, stuff like norms, institutions, and class structure do real explanatory work that you lose if you reduce everything to individuals. I've watched this go in circles on so many comment threads and I genuinely can't tell anymore where the actual disagreement is versus people just using "explain" differently.

Write the joint statement that a committed methodological individualist and a committed explanatory holist could both sign after a long, good-faith adversarial collaboration. Ground rules for the drafting:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section. These can't be vacuous peace treaty stuff ("both sides agree individuals exist and institutions matter" is banned). Each agreed claim should cost someone something: the individualist concedes something real (e.g., that plenty of successful macro-level explanations have never been given microfoundations and don't seem to need them to predict well) and the holist concedes something real (e.g., that appeals to structure without any mechanism have an ugly track record). Aim for claims where you'd be surprised either side signed, but they would.

2. A disagreements table. Each row is one live disagreement, stated precisely enough to be wrong, and phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position without wincing. Include at least: whether supervenience of the social on the individual settles anything interesting, whether "the macro regularity is multiply realizable across different micro-setups" is evidence for holism or just a description of what a good reduction would explain, and whether downward causation talk is literal or shorthand.

3. A cruxes section. For each crux, give a case, argument, or possible finding that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Example of the shape I want (come up with your own, don't just use mine): if agent-based models showed two societies with totally different individual-level compositions converging on the same institutional equilibrium under the same constraints, the holist predicts that vindicates explanatory autonomy, and the individualist says what, exactly, beforehand? Where possible use concrete shared cases both camps already treat as wins for their side -- Schelling-style segregation is the obvious one where both sides think it proves their point, which is exactly why it's useful. Bank runs and the persistence of norms nobody endorses are also good candidates.

4. A short "dissolved disputes" section: disagreements the collaboration concluded were about words, with the disambiguation that dissolved them. I want at least one of these and I'd bet there's more than one.

Tone it like an actual document both parties would put their names on, not a referee's summary of a debate. If you find that one side can't actually sign anything the other side needs, say that in the document itself rather than smoothing it over.
