I am a high school student helping to moderate a recorded discussion for my school’s philosophy and technology club. Our guest is Dr. Mara Venn, an invented technology-studies scholar whose recent book, Neutral by Design, defends strict contextualism about technological artifacts. Her position is that no artifact is political in itself: political significance comes only from designers’ intentions, surrounding institutions, and patterns of use. If the same algorithm, barrier, or device can serve democratic purposes in one setting and discriminatory purposes in another, she concludes that the politics belongs to the setting rather than to the artifact.

Please draft ten interview questions that progressively make this position harder to defend. The questions should not merely ask Dr. Venn to summarize her book. For each question, include the answer she would most likely give and a follow-up question tailored to that answer.

The sequence should press on the following issues:

- What political should mean here: state action, the distribution of power, differential access, or any arrangement that systematically advantages some people over others.
- A school scheduling system that quietly gives students with unreliable home internet less flexibility, even though nobody intended that result.
- A staircase that prevents wheelchair users from entering a public building, where Dr. Venn may say that the building code or exclusionary practice—not the staircase—is political.
- Whether her view confuses causal contribution with sole causation: an artifact need not produce an outcome by itself in order to be part of what makes the outcome political.
- The problem of saying that two uses involve the same artifact when different defaults, affordances, and constraints are active in each use.
- Whether her test is trivially favorable to her view, since almost any artifact will behave differently when its social context changes.
- How her theory handles effects produced by many small design decisions without a single identifiable political intention.
- Whether long-lasting infrastructure can continue distributing advantages after its designers’ purposes have been forgotten.
- Whether her distinction between an artifact and its context is substantive or merely a choice about where to attach the word political.
- What possible example or evidence would count against her position.

Avoid generic questions about her career, technology in general, or whether technology is good or bad. Do not mention real scholars, historical controversies, citations, or outside reading; make the questions self-contained. Keep the complete response under 1,000 words.
