My political theory professor does this thing in seminar where we pitch her a policy and she tells us whether the parties in the original position would sign off on it. Her calls so far: steeply progressive income tax, yes. A guaranteed minimum income even if it dents overall growth a bit, yes. Subsidizing elite universities on the grounds that the talented will eventually make everyone richer, no. Outlawing all inheritance, no -- the parties would allow bequests as long as the tax and education systems keep opportunities fair, she said. A military draft that falls way harder on poor kids, no. A policy that shrinks the whole economy by 2% but raises the floor for the worst-off, yes. Ok, new case for next week: a policy that makes literally everyone richer in income terms, worst-off included, but only works because it lets wealthy donors buy a lot more political influence than everyone else gets. What's her verdict, which principle is she actually running here -- strict maximin over income and wealth, the two principles with liberty strictly prior, or fair equality of opportunity doing all the work -- and what further case would let us tell those readings apart for sure?
