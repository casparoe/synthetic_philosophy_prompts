Last week, over drinks after our political theory reading group finished The Social Contract, a friend defended compulsory voting by invoking Rousseau's famous line that a citizen who refuses to obey the general will must be "forced to be free." His argument, roughly, was that freedom isn't simply a matter of being left alone — it partly consists in actually participating in collective self-government — and so a state that fines non-voters isn't diminishing anyone's liberty but supplying them with a piece of it, the way compulsory schooling supplies children with capacities they didn't know they needed. When someone objected that this is exactly the move every paternalist, and worse, has ever made, he shrugged and said the line has been unfairly maligned and that Rousseau meant something quite specific by it. Nobody at the table, myself included, could say precisely what that something was, and the conversation died there. I'd like to resurrect it with better equipment.

Please lay out, as an explicit menu, the distinct claims the slogan "forced to be free" could be making. For instance, I've half-heard that it might mean coercion is really self-coercion whenever laws express the general will, or that force can be used to dismantle forms of dependence that block freedom, or that the state may override your actual will in the name of your "real" or rational will — but don't treat my guesses as exhaustive, and feel free to add readings I haven't imagined. For each reading: state it precisely enough that it could turn out true or false; say whether it's a defensible interpretation of Rousseau specifically or a freestanding thesis merely borrowing his words; assess whether it's plausible, trivial, or sinister on its own merits; and say whether it would actually support my friend's use of it in defense of compulsory voting, or whether it only sounds like it does. I'd also like to know whether there is a reading on which the slogan is obviously true but does no argumentative work at all, and whether there is a reading on which it does the work my friend needs but only at a price he presumably wouldn't be willing to pay. Please write your answer in American English.
