I'm a novelist, mostly litfic but currently deep into a sci-fi book that's threatening to eat my life, and I need help from someone who actually understands the philosophy of biology because my characters keep getting into arguments I can't adjudicate. Quick setup: the book takes place on a colony ship several generations out from Earth. The original colonists were heavily gene-edited for the journey, and so were the plants and animals in the ship's ecosystem, but it's been two hundred years, things have drifted, random mutations have accumulated, and nobody onboard has the original design specs anymore. So the ship's medics and biologists are constantly fighting over whether some trait is a malfunction that should be treated or just how the organism works now -- one crew line has hearts that beat weirdly fast under stress, the hydroponic wheat has developed a stalk shape that half the botanists call a deformity and half call an adaptation, and there's a whole subplot about an engineered gut enzyme that kept a lot of colonists alive decades ago but now seems to be causing a lot of the chronic illness in the oldest generation. Whether these things count as "diseases" is a genuinely load-bearing question in the plot, because the ship has a charter that mandates treatment of dysfunction but forbids "improvement," so the classification determines what the medics are allowed to do.

Here's what I want. I've read a bit and I gather philosophers have two big rival ways of thinking about what a biological function even is: one family that says a trait's function is the effect it was selected or designed for (its history is what matters), and another that says a function is just whatever causal contribution the trait currently makes to the system it's part of (no history needed). I keep getting them tangled up, so what I need is a decision procedure I can basically hand to my characters -- a checklist or flowchart-in-words that someone on the ship (smart, but not a philosopher, working with incomplete records) could run through to decide whether trait X in organism Y counts as functioning properly, malfunctioning, or functioning fine-but-in-a-way-we-dislike. Walk me through building it: which account of function should drive which step, what questions get asked in what order, what to do when the history is unknown (which on this ship it usually is), and where the two accounts give different verdicts so I can put those fault lines in the dialogue. Be honest about which steps are real tests and which are judgement calls in a trenchcoat, since the charter dispute in the book kind of hinges on that.

Then apply the finished procedure to three cases, at least two of them mine from above (the fast-beating hearts, the wheat, the gut enzyme), and make sure at least one application spits out a verdict the person running it would hate -- like a medic forced to classify something she desperately wants to treat as "working as intended," or vice versa. Also flag the kinds of case where the procedure just breaks down and my characters would have to argue it out, because honestly those are the scenes I'm most excited to write. Oh and if it matters, tone-wise I'm going for something closer to a Becky Chambers book than hard military SF, so the procedure should feel like something actual working people would use, not an army field manual.
