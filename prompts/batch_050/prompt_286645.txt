I am preparing a position statement for a graduate seminar on distributive justice and would like it checked for coherence. My current view is:

1. In the original position, the parties are mutually disinterested and know general facts about economics and human psychology, but not their class, talents, identity, or conception of the good.
2. I accept Rawls’s two principles, with equal basic liberties lexically prior to fair equality of opportunity and the difference principle.
3. Securing the fair value of political liberty requires a property-owning democracy, not merely redistributive taxation within welfare-state capitalism.
4. The difference principle governs the basic structure, not individual conduct; talented people may seek higher pay when doing so is compatible with just institutions.
5. Justice is reciprocity among contributors, so no able adult has a justice-based claim to live indefinitely from others’ work without contributing.
6. I nevertheless favor an unconditional basic income with no work requirement, and I would count leisure as a primary good when identifying the least advantaged.
7. The difference principle does not apply globally because there is no worldwide coercive basic structure; richer countries owe poorer societies only a duty of assistance.
8. Because birthplace is morally arbitrary, fair equality of opportunity requires open borders and equal access to domestic employment from the moment an immigrant arrives.

Please audit the whole set rather than discussing each proposition separately. Identify any outright inconsistencies, tensions between the commitments’ underlying rationales, and conflicts that only appear problematic. Rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each, state which commitment could be revised and what that revision would cost. Finally, recommend the smallest change that would make the position coherent.
