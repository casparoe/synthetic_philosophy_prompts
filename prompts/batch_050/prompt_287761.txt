I'm a PhD student working on the conceptual foundations of citizenship policy. Several European governments require immigrants to attend integration courses or sign declarations affirming "Western values" -- typically listing things like gender equality, freedom of expression, religious tolerance, and the separation of church and state. Yet the same governments, in other official contexts, describe these very principles as universal human rights that hold everywhere and bind everyone, not as the property of any particular civilization. What conception of the category "Western values" does the integration-course practice presuppose? Does it treat these values as a distinctive cultural inheritance that must be transmitted to newcomers, and if so, is that compatible with officially regarding them as universal principles that any rational person could endorse regardless of background? Reconstruct the implicit theory of the category, say whether the practice strictly requires it or would also cohere with rival views (for instance, that "Western values" names merely a historical cluster with no essential unity, or that the phrase functions purely as a boundary-marker in political argument), and tell me what the practice would have to look like if the category turned out to be incoherent or dispensable.
