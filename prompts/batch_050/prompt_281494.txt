I teach a graduate seminar on rational choice and formal epistemology, and this term I am restructuring the unit on resolute choice, precommitment, and predictor-dependent problems. Rather than walking the students through the usual sequence of puzzle cases and then asking which decision theory survives, I want to begin with a specification exercise: before they encounter any contender, they should know what an adequate theory of rational action in this family of problems would have to deliver. The family I have in mind includes Parfit's hitchhiker, counterfactual mugging (the agent loses a coin flip and a reliable predictor, who would have paid her a large sum had the flip gone the other way on condition of payment, now requests a smaller payment), transparent Newcomb, and XOR blackmail, where a letter demanding money arrives exactly when either disaster looms or payment is forthcoming. Please write the desiderata such a theory must meet: the behaviors it must license or forbid, the distinctions it must preserve (for instance, between rationally paying a counterfactual mugger and being exploitable by anyone who claims to have run a simulation of you, or between honoring a lost bet and submitting to extortion), and the consistency it must maintain between one-shot and iterated versions of the same problem. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration, and be candid about which requirements you regard as genuinely non-negotiable and which strike you as the parochial commitments of one camp of decision theorists.

Two further stipulations, so that this cannot be answered by reciting the standard literature. First, your desiderata must adjudicate two cases of my own devising: (a) the standing-policy mugging, in which the same trustworthy mugger returns annually with a fresh coin-flip-dependent demand and the agent must choose once, now, between a permanent policy of paying and a permanent policy of refusing, knowing the policy itself will be predicted; and (b) the entangled letter, an XOR-blackmail scenario in which the recipient's own past negligence is known to be a contributing cause both of the termite infestation and of her being the sort of person the blackmailer targets, so that the letter is simultaneously evidence about the world and evidence about her own dispositions. Second, I want your judgment on a structural suspicion I hold but have not proved: that no theory can jointly satisfy the demands of (i) never being predictably outperformed by agents using rival theories in Newcomb-like situations, (ii) refusing every offer that is transparently extortionate in structure, and (iii) respecting dominance reasoning whenever outcomes are causally independent of the choice. If you judge this triad unsatisfiable, identify which desideratum you would relax and defend the sacrifice; if you judge it satisfiable, sketch the shape a theory meeting all three would have to take.

Present the response as exactly seven numbered items — five desiderata with their motivating considerations, then one adjudication of each of my two cases — each item no more than two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
