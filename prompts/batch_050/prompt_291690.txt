While preparing for a seminar on classical Indian epistemology, I encountered the following account of the Nyāya theory of the pramāṇas in an unnamed study guide:

Nyāya recognizes exactly four independent sources of knowledge: perception, inference, comparison, and reliable testimony. Perception supplies immediate, nonconceptual acquaintance with objects; inference derives conclusions from things previously perceived; comparison identifies unfamiliar objects through resemblance; and testimony transmits knowledge from a trustworthy speaker. Since every chain of justification must ultimately terminate in perceptual evidence, comparison and testimony are legitimate only insofar as they can be reconstructed as indirect forms of inference. Nyāya’s realism also leads it to identify truth with correspondence to mind-independent objects. A cognition produced by one of the four pramāṇas is therefore automatically true, while an apparently perceptual or testimonial cognition that proves false was never produced by a genuine pramāṇa. The restriction to four sources reflects epistemic parsimony: candidates such as tradition, analogy, and presumption are not rejected as sources of truth but classified under the four basic pramāṇas. Nyāya is thus close to contemporary evidentialism, since a belief is justified when the believer can identify the perception, inference, comparison, or testimony from which it originated.

The broad outline seems familiar, but I suspect that some of the interpretive connections are too quick. Please assess whether this is a fair summary of Nyāya epistemology. Distinguish claims that are substantially accurate from those that are oversimplified, misleading, or mistaken. In particular, address the characterization of perception, the alleged dependence of testimony and comparison on inference, the relation between pramāṇas and truth, and the comparison with contemporary evidentialism. Finally, explain which conclusions in the final two sentences would remain defensible after the summary has been corrected.
