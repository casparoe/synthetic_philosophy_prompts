I'm finishing my MA this year and want to apply for a small summer research grant (about 4 months of funding) to turn a half-baked idea into a proper project. The idea came out of an argument with a friend who works in philosophy of physics: she pointed out that infinite ethics, as it usually gets discussed, treats infinities as something that just *happens* to us -- the universe might already contain infinitely many people, and now your moral theory has to cope. But almost nobody seems to ask the action-guiding version of the question: suppose we could actually *bring about* an infinity -- digital minds running forever on accelerating hardware, some far-future civilization that persists without end, whatever your favorite speculative mechanism is. If infinite positive value swamps every finite consideration, does it follow that securing an infinite future dominates everything else we could do, the way securing a finite astronomical future supposedly dominates in longtermism? And if the theory says yes, is that a result or a reductio? My suspicion is that the existing technical fixes for infinite ethics (hyperreal-valued utilities, discounting, dominance-based principles that only compare actions differing on finite regions) were all designed to handle infinities we *find*, and they give weird or indeterminate answers for infinities we *make*, partly because they ignore the risk and uncertainty that always attach to bringing something about. That's the half-formed part.

Write me the grant proposal, roughly two pages. It should have: a precise statement of the question (I want it narrow enough that a yes/no-ish answer is at least conceivable in four months, so you'll probably have to restrict to one or two candidate mechanisms and one or two decision frameworks); a section on why the existing approaches to infinite aggregation leave this open rather than answering it; a month-by-month method section saying what I'd actually be doing (I expect months one and two are mostly reconstruction of existing views, but be concrete about what the later months produce); a section on what a result would look like -- including the possibility that the answer is "no principled theory can be action-guiding here," which I think should count as a finding, not a failure; and a short honest paragraph on what the failure modes of the project are and why this is the right scope for four months rather than a dissertation. Don't include citations or a reference list, I'll fill those in myself, but you can name positions and arguments in the text.
