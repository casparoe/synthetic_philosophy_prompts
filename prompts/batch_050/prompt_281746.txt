so here's a thing that's been bugging me about spectrum arguments against the transitivity of "better than". Everyone seems to grant that you can build a long chain of pairwise comparisons where each step looks fine (headache A is slightly milder than headache B, a tiny speck of dust in the eye for a year vs. a day less of it, etc.) but the endpoints differ enormously, and then the fight is over whether transitivity actually fails, or whether some comparison in the middle is actually false, or whether "better than" is just doing something weird like being an essentially comparative notion that depends on what else is on the menu. People also bring in money pumps, sorites, rough equality, incommensurability -- it's a mess and I can't tell which commitments are load-bearing.

What I want is the spec first. What would any adequate theory of "better than" (or "at least as good as") have to do to handle spectrum cases? Give me the requirements list: which phenomena it has to explain (why the small steps feel undeniable, why the endpoints feel like they must compare), which distinctions it can't collapse (intransitivity vs. incomparability vs. "roughly equal" vs. vagueness -- these get run together constantly and I suspect half the literature is people talking past each other), which decision-theoretic results it has to respect or explicitly bite the bullet on (can you be money-pumped if each individual trade is rational? is choiceworthiness even tied to betterness?), and what it has to say about whether the argument changes when the steps are about intensity of pain vs. number of people affected vs. welfare levels of different individuals.

For each requirement say why it's a requirement and not just one camp's prejudice -- like, is "better than must be transitive" itself a desideratum or is it the thing under dispute? And I want your honest verdict on whether the full list is jointly satisfiable at all, because I have a suspicion it isn't and that the real result here is that you have to give up exactly one of a small set of things, in which case tell me which one you'd give up and which single requirement does the most work in knocking out candidate theories (essentially comparative views, vagueness about betterness, intransitivity, parity, whatever).

Also: where is this reasoning most likely to be wrong, and what consideration or case would actually change your verdict -- like is there a spectrum case your spec handles badly that would make you rebuild it?
