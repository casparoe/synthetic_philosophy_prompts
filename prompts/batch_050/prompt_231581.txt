I'm a high school junior and I help run my school's philosophy club. For the past three weeks we've been stuck on the ontological argument, and the stall itself is what I want to ask about.

We started with the classic version: God is defined as the greatest conceivable being, and a being that exists is greater than one that doesn't, so God must exist. Then one member brought in a modal version she found, where the key premises are roughly that maximal greatness is possible and that a maximally great being would exist necessarily, so if such a being is possible it exists in every possible world including this one. A third member objected to all of it using the parody move: you could run the same argument for a perfect island, so something has to be wrong with the form.

Here's the strange part. Everyone in the club grants that the modal version is logically valid -- if the premises are true, the conclusion follows. Nobody disputes the modal logic itself. And yet after three weeks of discussion, not one person has changed sides. The theists say the possibility premise is obviously reasonable and the island parody fails because islands don't have "great-making" properties the way a necessary being does. The skeptics say conceiving of something doesn't make it genuinely possible, and that the argument only looks compelling because it trades on two different senses of "possible." A few people think the whole thing is a word game about whether existence is the kind of thing that can be packed into a definition.

My hypothesis is that this isn't really a disagreement about God at all -- it's a disagreement about what conceivability can tell us about genuine possibility, dressed up as a dispute about one argument. Is that right? Please give me a diagnosis, with reasons: is this debate verbal or substantive, and if substantive, what is it actually about? Do the sides differ over facts, over which intuitions count as data, or over something neither side can argue for? And what, if anything, would count as genuine progress -- a formal result, a conceptual distinction, a proof that one side's modal commitments lead somewhere they won't accept -- or should I conclude that the impasse is permanent, and why? Please answer in American English.
