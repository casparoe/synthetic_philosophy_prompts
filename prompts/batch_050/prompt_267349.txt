Write an Onion-style news article — headline plus 500-600 words — reporting that Laplace's demon, after millennia of distinguished service, has failed its annual performance review because its long-range weather forecasts are no better than chance. The comedy must come from the actual philosophy of physics, not from the demon being dim: it still knows the position and momentum of every particle and applies the laws flawlessly, and what undoes it are precisely the features of chaotic systems a specialist would name — sensitive dependence on initial conditions, the exponential amplification of arbitrarily small measurement error, and the impossibility of recording initial data to unlimited precision. Keep the metaphysics straight throughout the deadpan: the universe's determinism is never once in doubt. Include a quote from a relieved compatibilist insisting the demon's failure was never a threat to free will anyway, and one from the demon's union representative arguing that any predictor embedded in the universe it predicts faces a problem no amount of computing power solves. End with the demon reassigned to a simple, non-chaotic clockwork system, where it remains infallible in a way that helps no one.
