ok so I'm on a hiring tools team and we keep going in circles about which "fairness definition" to certify our screening model against, and every vendor deck just picks one and pretends the others dont exist. Before we pick anything I want the actual spec. Write the desiderata that any proposed formal fairness criterion for a binary decision (hire/interview vs. reject, based on a risk-style score) has to meet to count as adequate — the cases it must handle, the distinctions it cannot collapse, the things it must be consistent with — with the case or consideration behind each one. A table is fine but I want the reasoning, not just the grid.

To make this concrete, our situation: base rates of "successful hire" genuinely differ across the two demographic groups we track (about 40% vs 25%, and no, the difference isn't an artifact of our labels, we checked the outcome data as best we can), and the model is well-calibrated within each group but nowhere near perfect. Some things I specifically want adjudicated as desiderata, with whether each is a genuine requirement, a preference of one camp, or incoherent:

- calibration within groups (a score of 0.7 should mean 70% success rate in each group)
- equal false positive rates and equal false negative rates across groups
- equal selection rates across groups
- the criterion shouldn't be gameable by individuals misreporting or obscuring their group membership (this is my own hobbyhorse — does it deserve a spot on the list or is it a category error, confusing a property of the decision rule with a property of the incentives around it?)
- the criterion should still mean something when the same model gets reused for a slightly different decision threshold next quarter
- it shouldn't be satisfiable by a company just slicing its applicant pool into subgroups until each slice looks fine (I gather people call this fairness gerrymandering?)

The part I most care about: which subsets of these are jointly unsatisfiable given our setup (unequal base rates, imperfect prediction), stated as precisely as you can manage — I'd rather have the impossibility spelled out for our numbers than gestured at. If the impossibility means our compliance team's demand of "calibration AND equal error rates AND no disparity complaints" is a logical dead end rather than a technical challenge, say so plainly, because that's the slide I need. Also flag which single desideratum does the most work in knocking out the popular criteria (demographic parity, equalized odds, predictive parity, counterfactual fairness), and end with a quick pass of those four against the list — but keep the scoring brief, the requirements are the deliverable.
