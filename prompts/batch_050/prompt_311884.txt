So I'm a novelist, and my current book is set on a retired ocean liner that never got scrapped and instead became a permanently floating microstate anchored just outside anyone's territorial waters. It's been sitting there for forty years now, and the politics are getting messy, which is the fun part. The plot hinges on a referendum about who counts as a citizen, and I want the arguments my characters make to track real philosophical positions reasonably well, not just be hand-wavy stuff I made up. Here's the cast of affected parties: the original passengers who bought in back in the day, their kids who were born aboard and have never set foot on land, a rotating population of contractors who live on the ship nine months a year but keep apartments in Lisbon, stowaways who arrived illegally and have been there a decade, the small coastal town nearby whose whole economy now depends on servicing the ship, and a handful of foreign governments who keep trying to impose regulations on it.

The principles I've got my factions arguing over are basically: everyone whose interests are affected by a decision should get a say in it; everyone the ship's rules actually coerce or bind should get a say; everyone who actually lives on the ship should get a say; membership passes down by descent from the founding passengers; and you earn membership by contributing something concrete (taxes, labor, years). What I'd love from you is a tier ranking of these five by how defensibly each one settles who gets to vote in my specific setup, with a sentence or two on where each principle breaks down or produces something absurd given the details above -- like, does the coastal town get votes under the first principle, and is that a feature or a bug? Also tell me which principle my villain-ish faction, a clique of founding families trying to entrench themselves, would most plausibly dress up as the obviously fair one, because I want their rhetoric to be seductive rather than cartoonish.

One more thing: the emotional center of the book is a stowaway kid born mid-voyage who is stateless on paper everywhere, so I'm especially curious which principles handle her case cleanly and which ones definately choke on it. If none of them do, say so and tell me whether that's a real gap in how people think about this problem or just me being difficult.
