After a long argument with a friend, I remain unconvinced by her favorite case for licensing parents: parenting is like driving. Both are potentially dangerous activities performed by ordinary adults, so the state may require prospective parents, like prospective drivers, to demonstrate basic competence before beginning. She adds a restriction intended to answer liberal objections: licenses should assess only whether applicants can keep a child safe, nourished, educated, and able to choose among a reasonable range of adult futures, not whether they hold approved religious, political, or cultural views. Audit the driving analogy rather than treating it as a slogan. Set out what plays the roles of the road, vehicle, passenger, other road users, instructor, examiner, and license revocation; identify the disanalogies relevant to state authority, family intimacy, procreative freedom, error costs, and unequal enforcement; and assess whether the open-future restriction repairs the analogy or makes the proposed licensing scheme less like driving. Can the analogy support prospective parental licensing at all, or only narrower interventions once harm or serious risk is evident? Please state how confident you are in your overall assessment and single out the claim about which you are least certain.
