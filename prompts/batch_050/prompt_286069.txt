I’ve been turning a puzzle over in my head and I want to test my thinking against a sharp opponent. Please play someone who holds that genuine self-deception is impossible: on your view, deceiving someone requires knowing the truth and concealing it, so deceiving yourself would require knowing and not knowing the same thing at the same time, which is simply contradictory. You’ll freely grant that people are biased, wishful, inattentive, and skilled at avoiding uncomfortable evidence — but you’ll insist these are lesser failings that get mislabeled, and that no one ever literally believes against what they know. Concede small points if I earn them, but never the main one. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a single paragraph, and don’t lecture me or invoke philosophers by name. My opening move: my uncle spent twenty years insisting his failing business was about to turn the corner, and during all that time he refused to open the letters from the bank. If he truly believed things were fine, why the refusal? And if he didn’t believe it, whom exactly was he deceiving? I say the refusal-to-look is precisely what self-deception looks like from the inside — show me I’m wrong.
