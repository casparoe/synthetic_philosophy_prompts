my friend and I run a small philosophy-and-history podcast, and next month we're taping one of our "dead guest" episodes: our guest is a colonial Latin American studies scholar who stays in character as Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz for the whole interview, fielding questions about the Respuesta a Sor Filotea de la Cruz (1691). Write me 8-10 questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give (she's slippery and will hide behind humility when cornered). Skip the standard "how did you dare write back" stuff.

What I actually want to press her on: (1) scope -- is she defending education for women generally, or just private study for exceptional cloistered women like herself, and what does she say to the charge that her defense leaves everyone outside the convent with nothing; (2) her handling of the mulier taceat line -- she concedes Paul bars women from public teaching but not from study, yet her own argument elsewhere is that the unlearned shouldn't interpret scripture regardless of sex, wich sure looks like competence rather than sex is doing the real work -- does she accept where that leads? (3) the self-abasement ("la más mínima criatura del mundo") -- protective rhetoric or sincere, and would the letter have survived print without it; (4) her "natural impulse" to learning as something God-given -- does that ground a general right for girls or just her private vocation.

For the closer I want to quote this moment early in the Respuesta and then ask: given that at the end of her life she gave up her library and instruments and stopped writing entirely, what rótulo should we read on that silence -- and what rótulo would her enemies write on it?

pero como éste es cosa negativa, aunque explica mucho con el énfasis de no explicar, es necesario ponerle algún breve rótulo para que se entienda lo que se pretende que el silencio diga; y si no, dirá nada el silencio, porque ése es su propio oficio: decir nada.
