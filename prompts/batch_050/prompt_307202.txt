I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm writing a long feature on a dispute I've been following between two philosophers of science who agreed to sit down together after years of criticizing each other's work in print. One is an unapologetic realist about natural kinds: she holds that the world comes pre-sorted into kinds that our best classifications discover rather than invent, that kind-membership is fixed by some deep, projectible property or structure, and that the history of chemistry — the move from jade to jadeite and nephrite, the periodic table itself — is the model of science getting closer to nature's own joints. The other defends a thoroughgoing pluralism: he insists that many legitimate, non-overlapping classifications of the same domain can each be fully objective, that which kinds we recognize depends on our explanatory and practical interests as much as on the world, and that psychiatry, biology, and even chemistry abound with cross-cutting taxonomies that no single carving captures.

My editor has asked me to include, as a sidebar, the joint statement these two might issue if their meeting went as well as it possibly could — a genuinely adversarial collaboration, not a truce papering over the disagreement. Please draft that document for me. It should open with a numbered list of claims both parties can sign: about what successful scientific classification actually requires, about what cases like the jade/jadeite-nephrite split, the species problem, and contested psychiatric categories do and do not show, and about which positions on each side are caricatures neither holds. Then give a table of the remaining disagreements, each row phrased so that the person whose view is being stated would accept the wording as fair. For each disagreement, identify the crux — the sub-question such that settling it would genuinely move at least one of them — and, where possible, a concrete case, type of evidence, or scientific development both would accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Two things I specifically want the draft to get right, because my feature depends on them. First, the agreed-claims section must contain real content: if the only things they can both sign are banalities like "classifications should be useful," then say so explicitly and explain why the common ground is so thin, since that is itself a finding. Second, I suspect at least part of their quarrel is verbal — that they mean different things by "objective" or "in the world" — and I want the sidebar to say which part, if any, dissolves once the terms are pinned down, and which part survives as a substantive dispute about the metaphysics of classification. If you conclude the whole dispute is verbal, or that none of it is, argue for that verdict rather than assuming it.
