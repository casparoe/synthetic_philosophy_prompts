Two philosophers in my reading group keep clashing over whether a holobiont is an organism. One is selection-first: an organism has to reproduce as a fairly unified lineage, with enough alignment among its parts for selection to act on the whole. The other is integration-first: an organism is primarily a self-maintaining physiological and developmental system, even if its components have partly divergent evolutionary interests. Their favourite test cases are corals with their symbiotic algae and microbes, lichens, fungus-growing ant colonies, and the Portuguese man o’ war.

Draft the joint statement they could plausibly sign after an adversarial colloboration. Don’t adjudicate the dispute or write it as a dialogue. Include:

1. A short list of agreed claims. These should include any distinctions both sides accept, such as between an ecological consortium, a physiological unit, and a unit of selection, and any cases they agree are being misdescribed by the other side.
2. A table of their remaining disagreements. Phrase each side’s position carefully enough that the person holding it would accept the wording. Mark which disagreements are empirical, which are conceptual, and which are merely terminological.
3. The main cruxes. I suspect these include whether partner-specific inheritance matters more than metabolic interdependence, how much internal conflict an organism can tolerate, and whether organismhood is an all-or-nothing status.
4. Three thought experiments or feasible biological tests both sides accept as relevant: partner-swapping between viable microbial communities, propagating host and symbiont separately before recombining them, and introducing a variant symbiont that benefits itself while reducing host reproduction. For each, record what the selection-first and integration-first camps predict and what result would embarrass their own position.
5. A closing paragraph stating the weakest version of organismhood both could accept and the strongest version neither can yet defend.

Use British English throughout, keep it under 750 words, and don’t sneak in a verdict about which camp is ultimately right.
