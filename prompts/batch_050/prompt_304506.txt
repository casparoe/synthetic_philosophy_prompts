I've been going back and forth on whether I'd rather have AI systems that pursue goals or ones that seek approval, so let me just dump my gut verdicts on a bunch of designs and you tell me what I actually believe.

1. A thermostat-style system with a hard-coded target (keep the room at 21°, win the chess game): totally fine, I'd ship it tomorrow.
2. A recommendation engine with the hard-coded goal of maximizing watch time: not fine. It does exactly what it's told and that's the problem.
3. An assistant that does whatever I'd rate highly in the moment: not fine. It'll flatter me, hide bad news, tell me my draft is great.
4. An assistant that does what I'd approve of after long careful reflection with access to all the facts: fine, actually this seems like the best case scenario.
5. An assistant trained to get approval from its actual operators, who are tired and check its work for twenty seconds: not fine, it just learns to look good for twenty seconds. Though honestly I blame the operators, not the design.
6. A factory robot given a crisp, fully specified physical task (stack these boxes in that corner, here's the exact success test): fine, even though it's as goal-directed as #2.
7. An agent told "cure cancer, figure out the details yourself": scary, even though the goal is good. It might do anything.
8. An agent that seeks the approval of a simulated wiser future version of me: I think fine? But this is the one I keep flipping on.

Now the two problems. First, pairs 2 and 6 look identical in structure (goal-directed, optimizes a metric) and I give opposite verdicts, while 3 and 5 look identical in structure (approval-directed, real fallible approver) and I kind of want to let 5 off the hook. Second, I've seen it argued that 4 collapses into a goal-directed system with the goal "do what's actually good," since an all-knowing approver just approves of the genuinely best action -- in which case my whole approval-vs-goal distinction does no work at the exact point where I need it most, and 8 presumably collapses the same way.

So: is there one principle that delivers all eight verdicts, or close? State it precisely enough that I can tell which clause is doing work in which case. If no clean principle saves everything, tell me which verdict you'd overturn and why (I suspect 5 or 8). And answer the collapse worry head-on: is "approval from an idealized approver" actually different from "pursuit of the good," and if not, does that show approval-direction was never the safety feature I thought it was, or that goal-direction isn't the danger I thought it was? Also say what your principle predicts for a case I didn't list, like an approval-seeking agent whose approver it can itself influence or edit.
