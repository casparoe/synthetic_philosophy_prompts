Here are my gut verdicts about conditionals and I want to know what theory they add up to, if any. "If Oswald didn't shoot Kennedy, someone else did" -- true. "If Oswald hadn't shot Kennedy, someone else would have" -- false. "If you'd bet on heads, you'd have won" (said after the coin lands heads and I didn't bet) -- true. "If you'd bet on heads, the coin would have landed heads" -- false, and I feel this one strongly, even though it seems to have exactly the same connection between antecedent and consequent as the last one. "If kangaroos had no tails, they'd topple over" -- true. "If Nixon had pressed the button, there would have been a nuclear holocaust" -- true. But then: "if Nixon had pressed the button, there would have been a signal failure" -- that also sounds right, and these two cant both be true, can they? Also: "if Alice comes to the party it'll be fun, but if Alice and Bob both come it wont be" -- both perfectly assertible back to back, which I take it rules out treating indicative 'if' as a strict conditional. Is there one semantics that delivers all of these verdicts, especially the bet-on-heads pair and the Nixon pair, or do I have to give one up -- and if so which and why? And what does the resulting account say about a case I haven't listed: "if you hadn't bet, the coin would still have landed heads"?
