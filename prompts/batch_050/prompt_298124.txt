Periodical cicadas, which emerge after life cycles of 13 or 17 years, are frequently cited as a paradigm case of mathematics genuinely explaining a physical phenomenon: because a prime-numbered period minimizes the years in which emergence coincides with predators or competing broods operating on shorter periodic cycles, natural selection would have favored precisely the prime durations, and so a fact about prime numbers seems to explain an outcome of evolution. On the assumption that this example holds up, it appears to me that the explanatory version of the Quine–Putnam indispensability argument is essentially victorious, and that mathematical platonism is thereby established: if a theorem about primes does real explanatory work in our best biology, then numbers must exist mind-independently for that explanation to go through. What I would like to understand is what remains for philosophers of mathematics to dispute. Is the continuing debate merely a matter of refining an argument whose conclusion is no longer in serious doubt? And do nominalists have anything substantive left to say, or are they now confined to resisting a settled result out of a prior scruple about abstract objects?
