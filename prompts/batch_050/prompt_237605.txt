1. I pick the dust specks. Even at 10^60 people. At some point the tiny harms have to add up to more total badness than the torture — there's no magic line where numbers just stop mattering.
2. Transitivity of preference is non-negotiable for me. If A beats B and B beats C then A beats C, and cyclic preferences are just irrational, full stop.
3. The Repugnant Conclusion is a straight-up reductio. Any theory that ranks a vast population of lives barely worth living above a smaller population of amazing lives is false, end of story.
4. I buy the separateness of persons point — you can't just add up pleasures and pains across different people into one giant total, because nobody actually lives the sum. Tradeoffs across persons are morally different from tradeoffs within one life.
5. Honestly, a single dust speck is basically nothing. You blink, it's gone, you've forgotten it in a minute. I'd barely even count it as a harm.
6. I also think there are real side-constraints: it would be wrong to torture one innocent person even to stop five other people from being tortured in the exact same way.
