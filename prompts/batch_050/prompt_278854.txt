so there's a philosophy podcast i like, the host is sharp and usually does his homework, and his episode on Sartre's "Existentialism is a Humanism" (the 1946 lecture) ended with this little recap that i've been chewing on:

"For Sartre, there's no human nature and no excuses. You're not shy or lazy or a coward because of your genes or your childhood -- you are whatever you've chosen to be, full stop, and you can decide at any moment to be someone completely different. Your circumstances are just raw material, they don't constrain the choice itself. Even your emotions are choices: you're angry or in love because you decided to be. That's why he says we're 'condemned to be free.' Bad faith is just lying to yourself about this, like the alcoholic who says he could quit any time -- it's a failure of honesty that some weak people fall into. So existentialism is basically the original no-excuses philosophy: radical freedom means radical blame, and if your life is bad, that's on you."

That "both" is doing a lot of work, no? Doesn't that directly contradict the "circumstances are just raw material, they don't constrain the choice" bit? Unless I'm reading it wrong.

So: is the host's recap fair? I get that it's a podcast, not a seminar, and some compression is fine.
