I'm a PhD student in philosophy, and I've been asked to run one 75-minute session of a graduate seminar on rational choice and collective action (about 14 students, mixed philosophy and political science backgrounds). The session's theme is commitment races: situations in which parties keep sinking resources into out-committing one another — escalation in auctions and bidding wars, arms buildups, standing ovations, the academic job market — where each side's expenditure only pays off relative to the other's, where early investments lock people into later ones, and where the very ability to bind oneself visibly and irreversibly can decide the contest while leaving everyone worse off than if nobody had been able to commit at all. I'd like you to design an in-class game that makes them feel this rather than hear about it: my plan is to run it in at least two stages, the first a plain escalating contest with no way to limit oneself, so that participants discover from the inside how rational bid-by-bid decisions aggregate into an outcome nobody wanted, and the second introducing some mechanism for binding oneself (for example, publicly and irrevocably burning an option, or forfeiting a deposit if one continues), so they can experience both the strategic power of commitment and the way it can force or destroy value. Please give me exact rules, materials, a points-based payoff scheme with small real stakes (I'm willing to put a modest prize on the table if that matters — tell me whether it does), a timeline for the two stages, and instructions for what I should and shouldn't reveal in advance, since the participants should hit the escalation dynamic themselves before it gets named. Then give me a debrief plan that maps specific moments of the game onto the components of a commitment race — relative payoff, sunk investment, the commitment advantage, and the welfare loss — and that asks participants to identify where they could each have unilaterally stopped and why they didn't. I'd also like your honest forecast of what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this (collusion, players treating the points as meaningless, someone bidding to spite the room, the commitment stage being gamed) and how to handle each. Finally, be frank about which features of real commitment races the game cannot reproduce — real irreversibility, real stakes, the fact that in actual races the finish line itself moves with the spending — and what I need to supply in discussion to close that gap. And if you think my framing of the phenomenon is confused, or that a game of this kind can't actually instantiate what I want it to instantiate, I'd rather you say so plainly and tell me what to do instead than design around a muddle.
