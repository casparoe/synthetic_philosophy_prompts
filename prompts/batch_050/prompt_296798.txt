My coworker and I have been arguing for weeks — over way too many coffees — about which country is more liberal, Sweden or Norway. She says Norway, I say Sweden, and at this point we've both realized we're mostly just hoarding anecdotes: I'll bring up Sweden's school vouchers and how much easier it is to start a business there, she counters with Norway's gender-neutral conscription... which honestly isn't obviously a point for liberalism either, and that's kind of the problem. Both countries have a state alcohol monopoly, both criminalize buying sex but not selling it, both sit at the top of every democracy index, so all the easy stuff cancels out and we're left arguing about vibes, like which society would actually be chill about your weird life choices rather than merely legally permitting them.

Here's what I want: draft the joint statement my coworker and I should have produced instead of bickering. Start with a numbered list of claims we can both sign — some about the two countries, but also about what "liberal" could coherently mean (permissive law? tolerant day-to-day culture? low state involvement in personal choices? economic freedom? and how much weight each dimension should get). Then state the actual disagreements, worded neutrally enough that each of us would accept the description of our own side. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where, if we could settle them, at least one of us would have to update. I suspect one is whether liberalism is about what's on the books or about how strangers treat each other in practice, and another is how to aggregate across domains — does being more liberal on one axis (say drugs, or speech) outweigh being less liberal on another (say alcohol or prostitution)? For each crux, name a concrete comparison or kind of evidence we would both accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each of us predicts it would show, so neither of us gets to move the goalposts later. And if you conclude that part of our fight is really verbal — that "liberal" is doing different work in our mouths — say exactly which part and why, instead of pretending its one disagreement.
