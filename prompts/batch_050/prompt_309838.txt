For a project on how rights should be modeled within social choice theory, I want the specification before I look at any proposed solution. Write the desiderata that an adequate resolution of Sen's liberal paradox (the impossibility of a Paretian liberal) must satisfy, where 'resolution' is construed broadly to include a revised axiomatization, a reinterpretation of what rights are, or a principled argument that the apparent conflict is illusory or practically harmless.

Cover at least the following, with the consideration motivating each desideratum:

- which formal results the resolution must engage with and not merely evade: Sen's original theorem with unrestricted domain, weak Pareto, minimal liberalism, and acyclic collective rationality, as well as Gibbard's extension of the conflict to libertarian claims as such;
- which intuitive cases it must deliver a determinate verdict on, including meddlesome preferences of the kind in the Lady Chatterley's Lover example, cases where the exercise of a private right produces outcomes others care about, and cases where rights-holders condition their choices on one another's;
- which distinctions it must not collapse: rights as constraints on admissible social states versus rights as game-form assignments of decision powers, decisiveness over one's own personal sphere versus effective decisiveness given others' strategies, and preference-based versus autonomy-based grounds for applying the Pareto principle within private affairs;
- which features it must preserve of ordinary liberal intuition, and which it may sacrifice.

Then classify the desiderata: which are non-negotiable given my purpose (a theory adequate for assessing actual constitutional arrangements, not merely a technically consistent axiom set), and which are prejudices of one camp — welfarist, deontological, or game-form — rather than genuine constraints. If some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, state this explicitly and precisely; I suspect that unrestricted domain, strong Pareto, and unconditional minimal rights are such a subset, and I want to know exactly which weakening purchases consistency at the lowest intuitive cost. Finally, score the main existing strategies against the list in at most a paragraph each: Sen's own diagnosis, the game-form relocation of rights associated with Nozick, conditional-preference approaches of the Gaertner–Pattanaik–Suzumura kind, and the welfarist response that Pareto should be weakened first. Conclude with a line of the form \answer{...} naming the single desideratum that excludes the most candidate resolutions.
