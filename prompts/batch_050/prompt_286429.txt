For an ethics discussion group, write a “which theory of forgiveness do you actually hold?” decision tree. Build the tree from the logical space first, not from a list of labels: begin by separating forgiveness from excusing, condoning, forgetting, trusting, and reconciling; then ask whether repentance, apology, punishment, or repair are required; whether forgiveness can be obligatory; and whether anyone besides the victim may forgive.

Use 8–10 concrete questions that someone could answer without knowing the theories. Include cases involving an offender who sincerely repents only after being caught, an offender permanently unable to repent because of brain damage, a victim who stops resenting the offender but still judges the act deeply wrong, and a relative attempting to forgive on a murdered victim’s behalf. Leaves should include unconditional permission, unconditional duty, repentance-gated permission, repair-earned duty, supererogatory-only forgiveness, and an anti-forgiveness or moral-protest view. Give a scoring key, note any coherent-looking combinations that nobody seems to occupy and why, and flag positions your tree cannot distinguish. Each leaf needs one sentence about its most awkward commitment. Descriptive names are fine; don’t attribute views to particular philosophers.
