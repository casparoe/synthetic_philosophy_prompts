After forty years as an experimental physicist I have retired and taken up philosophy, and I am currently working through the literature on the reliability of introspection: the question of whether, and under what conditions, our reports about our own ongoing mental states -- what we believe, why we acted, what we are currently feeling or imagining -- can be trusted. I find myself instinctively translating everything into the vocabulary of my old discipline, and I would like you to do this translation properly and systematically rather than let me keep doing it haphazardly.

Here is the target vocabulary, in which I am fully fluent: an introspective report is a measurement; the mind is the system under measurement; the report is the instrument reading; calibration against an independent standard; random error versus systematic error; instrument drift; the response function of the instrument; perturbation of the system by the act of measurement (observer effect, back-action); the distinction between precision and accuracy; blinding; inter-rater reliability as an analogue of cross-instrument comparison; and the problem of validating an instrument when no independent instrument exists that measures the same quantity.

Please take the main positions and considerations in the debate -- incorrigibility and first-person authority; the view that some introspection is constitutive rather than epistemic (saying what you believe partly makes it so); the evidence from confabulation studies in which people sincerely report reasons for their choices that were demonstrably manufactured on the spot; worries about imagery reports and the unreliability of phenomenal judgments under pressure; and the general question of whether introspection is perception-like, inference-like, or something else -- and restate each of them as precisely as you can in the measurement-theoretic vocabulary above, one position at a time.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about. For each position: what translated cleanly, what you had to distort or stretch to fit the metrological frame, and what had no expression in that frame at all. I have three specific suspicions I want addressed. First, I suspect the ground-truth problem -- that in introspection there is often no independent instrument to calibrate against -- swallows most of the debate, and I want to know whether anything important remains once that point is granted. Second, I suspect that first-person authority does not fit the instrument model at all, since we do not ordinarily grant an instrument authority over the quantity it measures; tell me whether this is a genuine defect of my translation or a sign that first-person authority was always a confused notion. Third, in physics we distinguish a bad reading from a perturbation of the system, and I am unsure which of these confabulation is -- whether the confabulating subject is misreading a stable state or manufacturing the state in the act of reporting it -- and whether the philosophical debate even registers that distinction.

Finally, the verdict: is the residue a defect of the metrological framework (evidence that the mind is not, in the relevant respects, a system under measurement) or a sign that parts of the philosophical debate are less substantive than they appear? Please be honest in the translation -- do not quietly import the assumptions of measurement theory and then present the result as a neutral rendering.
