I'm preparing a seminar paper for a Victorian literature module on secularisation, and a friend and I fell into an argument last week that I now want to settle properly. We were discussing the wave of popular anti-religious books published in the 2000s -- the movement commonly labelled New Atheism, associated with writers such as Richard Dawkins, Christopher Hitchens, Daniel Dennett and Sam Harris -- and my friend claimed that George Eliot, had she lived to read them, would have welcomed the movement wholeheartedly, since she herself lost her evangelical faith, translated Strauss and Feuerbach, and never returned to orthodoxy. I suspect this is too simple. Eliot seems to me to have regarded religious feeling, communal worship and the moral imagination sustained by Christianity as things of enormous human value even after she ceased to believe the doctrines, and I wonder whether the combative, science-first tone of New Atheism would have struck her as shallow about what religion actually does for people -- or whether she would instead have thought my view sentimental, and the New Atheists' impatience with comforting fictions entirely right.

Please reason from what is known of Eliot's intellectual development, her translations, her essays and letters, and the moral texture of her novels, and give me your best reconstruction of where she would have stood on New Atheism. Structure your answer in four sections, in this order: "The common ground" (roughly 200 words: where she would have agreed with the New Atheists, and how firmly), "The points of friction" (roughly 300 words: where she would have resisted them, whether on epistemology, on the moral psychology of belief, or on what should replace religion, giving the strongest version of her case rather than a strawman), "The hardest question for her" (roughly 150 words: the single point on which her own commitments pull in opposite directions and where even a sympathetic reconstruction cannot confidently say what she would conclude), and "My verdict" (roughly 100 words: your overall judgement, on balance, of whether she would have been an ally, a critic, or something harder to classify). Where you are extrapolating beyond anything she actually wrote, say so explicitly rather than presenting conjecture as her view.
