Here's a version of the centipede game worry that I want you to take a stand on. Suppose rationality is common knowledge between the two players. Backward induction says player 1 should take the money immediately at the first node. But now consider the second player's first decision node: she only gets to move at all if player 1 passed, which is exactly what common knowledge of rationality (plus backward induction) predicted wouldn't happen. So her information set being reached seems to refute the very premise she was supposed to use to reason about what to do. Argue that this makes the standard backward-induction solution self-undermining: that common knowledge of rationality is inconsistent with ever reaching a later node, and hence can't be what justifies taking at node 1 either. If you think the argument fails, identify the precise step where it goes wrong (e.g., is common knowledge really refuted by a deviation, or only some weaker belief?), and defend the first-node take.
