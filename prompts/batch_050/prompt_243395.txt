Okay so I'm putting together my "philosophical profile" for a phil math reading group and I want a consistency audit before I embarrass myself. Please act as a specialist in philosophy of mathematics -- think carefully, brainstorm possible tensions first, then give me the actual audit.

Some context. I've been reading Benacerraf's "Mathematical Truth" (1973). Near the start he argues, roughly, that any satisfactory account of mathematics has to do two jobs at once: give a semantics for math talk that's continuous with how we treat the rest of language, and also fit into a reasonable epistemology that makes it intelligible how we actually come to know these things -- and he thinks standard accounts can do one or the other but not both. (That's my paraphrase, not his words.) My profile is basically an attempt to wriggle out from between those two demands, so tell me if it works.

Here are my commitments:

(a) Mathematical statements are objectively true or false, and the truthmakers are abstract, mind-independent things (objects, structures, whatever -- I'm not picky about the category).

(b) Knowledge of some domain requires a reliable, broadly naturalistically respectable connection between our beliefs about that domain and the facts of that domain. Benacerraf's epistemological worry is legitimate and any view I hold has to answer it, not dodge it.

(c) We obviously have mathematical knowledge. Any view that ends up skeptical about whether I know that 7+5=12 is refuted by that fact alone.

(d) I'm an ante rem structuralist: numbers are positions in the natural number structure, full stop. This is my answer to Benacerraf's other problem (the multiple reductions / "what numbers could not be" worry), and I also take it to soften the epistemological problem, since I no longer claim knowledge of speshul sui generis number-objects.

(e) The indispensability of mathematics to science gives us genuine epistemic warrant for believing mathematics -- I lean on this when people push me on (b).

(f) Field-style fictionalism is a non-starter for me; the nominalization program is too costly and I don't buy it.

What I want: go through these and tell me which pairs (or triples) are in real tension, which only look like they are, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each real one, tell me what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs me. I'm especially interested in whether (d) and (e) actually do the work I think they do against (b). Don't just flag everything as equally problematic -- discriminate. And don't assume I already know where the problem is, because I clearly don't.
