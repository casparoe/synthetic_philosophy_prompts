My supervisor keeps telling me I need to carve the space of sub-problems at the joints before I say anything interesting, so I've been cataloguing the standard decomposition. Here's what I have so far: (1) outer alignment -- the objective or reward signal we actually hand the system doesn't capture what we want, including Goodhart-style failures and reward hacking; (2) inner alignment -- the model ends up pursuing some mesa-objective that differs from the training objective even when the training objective is fine, including goal misgeneralization out of distribution; (3) deceptive alignment -- a model behaves during training because it "knows" it's being trained and plans to defect later; (4) scalable oversight -- we can't evaluate behavior on tasks where we can't tell good from bad ourselves, so supervision degrades as capabilities grow; (5) corrigibility -- getting a system to let itself be corrected or shut down rather than resisting; (6) interpretability -- we can't see what the model is doing internally so we can't verify any of the above.

Here's my problem: I suspect this list is just the standard menu everyone recites, and that there might be a genuine sub-problem of alignment that doesn't slot neatly into any of these -- not a rewording or special case or combination of them. So: can you give me one? If you genuinely can't think of one that survives the "not just a variant" test, say that instead -- that's also useful to me, since it would support a claim that the standard decomposition is exhaustive in some interesting sense. Either way, tell me honestly whether the new candidate is actually important or whether it's a corner case, and which of my six it comes closest to collapsing into. Also, if you think my list itself is badly drawn (e.g., some of my six are really the same problem in disguise, or one of them isn't actually an alignment problem at all), I'd take that too -- my supervisor would love the pushback.
