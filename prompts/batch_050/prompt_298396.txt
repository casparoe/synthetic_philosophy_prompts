Before I read or write anything further about proposed resolutions of what Henry Sidgwick called the dualism of practical reason -- the apparent standoff between the rationality of pursuing one's own good on the whole and the rationality of pursuing good impartially, each of which struck him as self-evident and neither of which he could derive from the other -- I want a specification to hold the candidates against. Most of the literature I have encountered treats the dualism as a purely theoretical puzzle about whether two supreme principles can both bind. I want the standard set differently: an account of practical reason adequate for a deliberator -- a person who feels the pull of both principles and must decide, at the moment of choice, what they may honestly tell themselves about why either sacrifice or self-preference would be reasonable. Adequacy is relative to that purpose, and the purpose should shape the list.
