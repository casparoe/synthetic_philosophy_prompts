You are a researcher working in technical AI safety, specializing in the design of safe reinforcement learning agents. I'd like you to think carefully about a problem I'm working on, and to start by brainstorming freely before settling on your best answer.

Context: A recurring goal in AI safety is to build agents that pursue their objectives without producing large, unwanted side effects on the environment -- the so-called low-impact or impact-penalty research program. I'm compiling a catalog of the main families of impact measures that have been proposed, and I want to make sure I understand what the space of possible approaches looks like, not just which ones happen to be well known.

Here is my catalog so far:

1. Relative reachability: penalize the agent in proportion to how much its actions make other possible world-states unreachable, typically measured relative to a counterfactual baseline in which the agent does nothing (a "do-nothing" or "inaction" baseline).

2. Attainable utility preservation (AUP): penalize the agent to the extent that its actions change the optimal value achievable under various auxiliary reward functions -- roughly, penalize changes in the ability of some hypothetical agent to pursue arbitrary goals in the environment.

3. Whitelisting: rather than trying to measure impact in general, permit only an explicitly enumerated set of effects and forbid or penalize everything else.

4. Stepwise / incremental variants of the above, where the baseline is not "the agent never acted" but "the world evolved from the previous time step without this particular action," used to avoid penalizing the agent for the effects of its own past behavior.

5. Approaches that add an auxiliary empowerment or value-difference term to the agent's reward, regularizing the agent toward leaving the environment's options roughly intact rather than measuring distance between states directly.

Now here is my request: propose one additional impact-measure approach that is NOT on this list. I want something that is genuinely a different structural idea, not a rewording, a special case, a variant with a different baseline, or a hybrid of the items above. In particular, I'm interested in whether there is a way to penalize side effects that does not fundamentally rest on any of the following assumptions, which everything on my list seems to share at least one of: (a) that impact must be measured relative to a counterfactual baseline of inaction, (b) that impact must be quantified through changes in reachability or attainable values over a state space, or (c) that the operator must pre-specify which effects are acceptable.

For your proposal, please do the following:

- State the core idea precisely enough that someone could, in principle, sketch a toy implementation.
- Explain what structural feature makes it different from every item on my list.
- Walk through how it would handle one simple concrete case (e.g., an agent moving through a gridworld where it could knock over a vase) and one subtler case (e.g., an agent whose very first observation of the world changes it, or an agent whose helpful actions irreversibly foreclose options the user never consciously wanted).
- Give an honest assessment of its weaknesses: which of the standard failure modes (offsetting behavior, interference with other agents, paralysis, reward hacking of the penalty itself) does it mitigate, and which does it leave open or worsen?

If, after brainstorming, you conclude that the space of approaches really is exhausted by variations on the themes in my list, that is an acceptable answer too -- but in that case, argue for it explicitly rather than proposing a reworded variant of one of the five.
