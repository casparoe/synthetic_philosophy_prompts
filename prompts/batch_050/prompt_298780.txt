Before I read another take on whether DNA is “really a code” or whether that’s just a metaphor we got stuck with, I want the spec first. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of genetic information: what it has to explain, which cases it has to get right, which distinctions it must not collapse, and which findings it can’t contradict. Give the motivating case behind each requirement, not just a label. Cases I care about include: degeneracy/synonymous codons where the sequence changes but the protein doesn’t; a mutation that improves a protein while breaking a regulatory motif; overlapping genes and alternative splicing where the same stretch seems to “say” different things depending on cellular context; RNA editing and epigenetic marks; horizontal transfer where a gene lands in a totally new host; synthetic biology code reassignment; archival DNA data storage used only by humans; and a “watermark” sequence placed in a genome that has meaning for the designers but no biological role.

Separate at least these questions: bare correlation/causal covariance vs aboutness; information in the molecule vs information-for-a-reader; error/misreading vs mere malfunction; and “code” as engineer talk vs as something nature literally does. Flag any requirements you think can’t be jointly satisfied — for example intrinsic sequence-only information vs context-dependence, or normative misreading vs plain physics/chemistry. Say which desiderata are non-negotiable, which are camp prejudices, and which single requirement knocks out the most standard views. Then, briefly and secondary to the criteria, score the usual suspects against the list: Shannon-style information, causal covariance, teleosemantic/selected-function accounts, deflationary “code is just a handy metaphor,” and an instruction/semantic view. A compact table is fine, but keep the weight on the requirements.

Answer in American English. Don’t turn this into a bibliography or name-drop fest. Definately mark what’s basically settled in biology/philosophy of biology vs what’s genuinely contested, and don’t present a contested reading as the consensus.
