Okay so this comes up constantly in the animal welfare corners of the rationalsphere and I want a cleaner version of it: construct a thought experiment where the two heuristics people actually use to guess whether an animal is conscious — behavioral flexibility/complexity on one side, neuroanatomical similarity to us on the other — give opposite answers about the same creature.

Invent an Earth-plausible animal (no aliens, no AIs, no magic neurons) where one heuristic says "definitely conscious" and the other says "probably not." Pick whichever direction you think is harder to make work and say why. Constraints: describe the animal in one paragraph, and no cheating — the behavioral evidence has to be something more specific than "it's smart and social," and the neural dissimilarity has to be more than "its neurons are just arranged weird." After the scenario: say what each heuristic concludes and why, which theories of conciousness (global workspace, higher-order, local recurrence, whatever) line up with which heuristic, and name the single structural feature of your animal that's doing all the work. Finally: if you removed that feature, does the case just collapse back into "octopus again"?
