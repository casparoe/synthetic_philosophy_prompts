so i have no background in philosophy or comp sci or anything, im an electrician whos gotten way too into the AI safety corner of the internet over the last year. mostly youtube, some blogs, a couple podcasts. lately ive been reading about "debate" as a plan for keeping superhuman AI honest -- two AIs argue, a human judges who wins, and supposedly this lets us oversee systems smarter than us. ive sort of assembled my own position on it and i want to know if it actually hangs together before i go arguing with people about it.

heres roughly where ive landed:

1. debate genuinely can scale past human ability, because the honest debater has a structural advantage -- if the other guy is lying, the honest one can point at the exact step where the lie lives, and lies get harder to defend the more you zoom in. truth has home field advantage.

2. i dont think experiments todays researchers run on debate (with current models, or humans playing judge on hard questions) tell us much of anything, because the entire point is overseeing claims about stuff the judge could never verify. so im basically going on the theoretical argument.

3. the judge doesnt need to be an expert in anything. if an argument depends on something the judge cant follow, the debater can just break it down into smaller and smaller pieces until each individual step is simple enough for a normal person to check. recursion handles it.

4. an AI that can argue ANY side of an issue equally persuasively would be just about as useful for oversight as one that only argues for the truth. the judge hears both cases at full strength and picks the better one. thats the whole idea of an adversarial system, like courts.

5. when we train these things, the reward has to go to whichever debater convinces the judge, because thats the only feedback signal we actually have access to. you cant reward "telling the truth" directly since we cant check whats true.
