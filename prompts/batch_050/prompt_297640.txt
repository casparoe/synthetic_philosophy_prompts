In On Bullshit, Harry Frankfurt argues that bullshit is produced whenever a person's obligations or opportunities to speak on a topic exceed their knowledge of the relevant facts, and that what defines the bullshitter is not falsehood but a lack of concern with how things really are -- the liar still cares about the truth, since deceiving requires tracking it, while the bullshitter simply does not. I paraphrase rather than quote; the relevant discussion is in the essay's final sections.

I am a longtime reader of LessWrong and adjacent forums, and the vocabulary I think in most fluently is that of machine learning: training objectives, reward models, next-token prediction over a distribution, calibration, abstention, gradient pressure. My request is a translation exercise, not an explainer. Restate Frankfurt's account -- the lie/bullshit distinction, the diagnosis of why bullshit gets produced, and his claim that sincerity is no refuge from it -- in that vocabulary as precisely as you can. Then give me the residue report. Which parts translate cleanly? (I suspect the production diagnosis translates almost perfectly, since an autoregressive model is structurally a device whose obligation to produce text always exceeds its knowledge.) Which parts require distortion? And which have no counterpart at all -- in particular, what in the ML frame, if anything, corresponds to Frankfurt's notion of indifference, given that a model has objectives but arguably no attitudes?

Finally, take a stand on the question that motivates me: when an LLM hallucinates, the rationalist-adjacent debate usually stalls between calling it lying, calling it bullshit, and calling it mere error. Does the residue from your translation show that the ML vocabulary is missing something Frankfurt was tracking -- in which case "hallucination as bullshit" is a substantive claim -- or does it show that Frankfurt's account, keyed as it is to intentions and unconcern, simply has no application to artifacts, in which case the metaphor is doing harm and we should retire it?
