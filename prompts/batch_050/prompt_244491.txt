I draft the disciplinary code for a national amateur sports body -- county-level leagues, volunteer officials, disciplinary panels of three people with day jobs who meet maybe twice a month. Our lawyers keep pushing us to make more offences strict liability because proving intent is nearly impossible at our level, and the clubs keep writing in to say punishing people who did nothing wrong is unjust. I'm redrafting the code and want an actual procedure, not a lecture: for each candidate offence, the questions to ask in order to decide whether it needs a mens rea element, whether strict liability with a due-diligence defence will do, or whether it should be genuinely absolute. Ground it in what the philosophy of criminal law actually says about when punishing without fault is defensible, but translated into tests our panel members (non-lawyers) can apply on the night. Cover whether the answer should change with the severity of the sanction -- a two-match ban versus a lifetime ban -- and which defences I should write into the code explicitly rather than leave to panel discretion. Then run the procedure on two real offences from our current code: a prohibited substance showing up in a player's sample, and a club fielding an ineligible player because of an admin error. One of them should come out with an answer I'll find uncomfortable, and tell me which and why.
