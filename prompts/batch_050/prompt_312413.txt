For the better part of a decade I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums, where the claim that rational agents with common knowledge of each other's rationality cannot agree to disagree has been treated almost as a lifestyle directive: persistent disagreement between smart interlocutors is taken to indicate a failure of rationality somewhere, and various protocols for resolving disagreements are built on this idea. I have written a talk pushing a sharpened version of this: I argue that two humans who repeatedly exchange their honest posteriors about a proposition, over enough rounds, will almost always converge, and that when they do not converge, the explanation is rarely "no common knowledge of priors" but almost always a diagnosable defect -- motivated reasoning, self-deception about one's own update, or smuggling private evidence in as a prior. So my thesis is optimistic about convergence and pessimistic about the common-knowledge escape hatch: the standard reply that Aumann's theorem simply fails to apply to real people is, I claim, itself mostly a rationalization.
