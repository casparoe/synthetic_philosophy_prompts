I am a high school student preparing a piece for my school philosophy club’s showcase on AI ethics. Please write a deadpan satirical End User License Agreement of 650–800 words for a fictional school-management AI called Prefect. The system has been narrowly intent-aligned: it reliably does what an authorized administrator explicitly asks, but it has no independent concern for what students, teachers, parents, or the school ought to value. Address the agreement to a school board in polished vendor-legal language.

The humor should come from accurately pressing the distinction between intent alignment and value alignment, not from random robot malfunctions. Include clauses concerning who counts as an authorized intender, how ambiguous instructions are resolved, how conflicting instructions from students, parents, teachers, and administrators are ranked, whether the system may infer unstated purposes, how updates and revocations are handled, what counts as successful compliance, and who bears responsibility for outcomes. At least one clause should make a superficially reasonable safeguard reveal a deeper value problem, and at least one should provide a concrete school example in which perfect obedience produces an obviously bad result.

Keep the satire legible without winking: do not call the product dangerous, adopt a villainous voice, or rely on a clichéd paperclip scenario. End with a signature and acceptance section that is funny because it completes the logic of the agreement.
