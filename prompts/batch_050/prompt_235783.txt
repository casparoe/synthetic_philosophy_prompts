I'm putting together a statement of my views in formal epistemology for a reading group and want a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments: (1) Rational degrees of belief satisfy the probability axioms, and confirmation is just conditionalization: E confirms H for an agent when Pr(H|E) > Pr(H) on that agent's credence function. (2) The problem of old evidence is genuine: the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion really did confirm general relativity when Einstein put the theory forward, even though the data were decades old and already known with certainty, and any adequate account of confirmation must deliver this verdict. (3) The right solution is that what confirms H is not learning E but learning that H entails (or explains) E: confirmation flows from discovering a deductive or explanatory connection one had not previously recognized. (4) Evidential support is an ahistorical relation: whether E confirms H depends only on the contents of E and H and the agent's credence function, not on when, or in what order, the agent acquired the evidence. (5) Rational agents are not logically omniscient: they can rationally assign intermediate credence to claims that are in fact logical truths, such as entailments nobody has yet derived.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which apparent conflicts dissolve on inspection? Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give, and identify the single cheapest revision. I'm particularly unsure whether (3) can actually be implemented inside the framework of (1), but I may be wrong about where the deepest problem lies.
