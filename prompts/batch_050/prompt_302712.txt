On the alignment forums I have read for years, one analogy does more work than any other: natural selection optimized humans for inclusive genetic fitness, yet what humans actually came to want — tasty food, sex, status — merely correlated with fitness in the ancestral environment, and in the modern environment we cheerfully decouple the proxies from the objective altogether, contraception being the canonical exhibit. The moral drawn is that an optimization process can push hard on an objective while producing an agent that internalizes proxies which come apart from that objective off-distribution; inner misalignment, on this telling, is not a paranoid edge case but the default outcome of selection-like processes, and gradient descent is close enough to selection that we should expect trained models to pursue proxy goals rather than the reward signal's intent.

I want an audit of this analogy, not a defense and not a debunking. Concretely:

1. The correspondence table. What plays the role of the fitness function, the training distribution, mutation and recombination, the genome, the ancestral environment, deployment? Be explicit, and flag any slot where the mapping is strained or missing entirely — for instance, what corresponds to the population in ordinary single-model training, and does the analogy need one?

2. Which disanalogies matter for the conclusion, as opposed to merely existing. At minimum, address these: selection filters while gradient descent shapes — fitness touches a genome only through differential reproduction across generations, whereas the loss touches the weights on every step; evolution has no designer with an intended objective, so whether the source case even contains an outer objective to be misaligned from is doing questionable work; and human goals are acquired substantially within a lifetime via culture and learning, which has no clean analog in a single training run.

3. The standard sub-claim that capabilities generalize while goals misgeneralize, since evolution supposedly exhibits exactly this pattern in us. Does the analogy actually license that split, or is it borrowed from elsewhere?

4. The rival source: artificial selection produced dogs that are, by most lights, reasonably aligned with human purposes. Does the analogy's proponent have a principled reason to prefer wild-type evolution over domestication as the source case, or is the choice of source doing the argumentative lifting?

Then commit: does the conclusion that inner misalignment is the default survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it? Take a definite position with reasons, say how much weight analogy-arguments of this kind can bear at all in forecasting trained systems, and if you think a better analogy exists for the underlying phenomenon, name it and say what it buys.
