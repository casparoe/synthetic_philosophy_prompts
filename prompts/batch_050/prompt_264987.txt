Two coworkers of mine, both engineers, got into an argument at a team offsite that I've been turning over since, and I'd like a referee. The setup: Mara claimed that if we could compile, neuron by neuron, a complete rulebook that maps every possible conversational input to exactly what Einstein would say and do in response, then flipping through that book with a visitor would amount to conversing with a conscious Einstein, because consciousness lives in the functional pattern, not the meat. Dev said that's absurd: a book is inert paper, nothing is happening inside it, and at best the person flipping pages is doing whatever thinking occurs, and they have none of Einstein's experiences. Mara replied that the consciousness, if located anywhere, must then be in the whole system of book plus page-turner, and that if we replaced the human flipper with a simple machine, the machine-plus-book would plainly be having Einstein's experiences. Dev countered that a static lookup of precomputed answers has none of the causal structure that matters: every entry just sits there, correct for no reason, with no counterfactual machinery underneath, whereas a real brain computes its answers, and our intuitions are misled by the fact that the conversation looks the same from the outside. Mara then offered a revised version: suppose the book instead contains the full rules of the neural simulation and the page-turner works through the actual step-by-step computation with pencil and scratch paper, so the causal structure is preserved, only slowed down and implemented on paper. She said surely slowing a process down and writing it on paper cannot delete consciousness. Dev's last move was that if consciousness survives that transformation, then we seem forced to say that a mere recording of a past run of the simulation, or maybe even the unused book sitting on the shelf, contains experiences, which is reductio-level crazy, so one of Mara's earlier steps must be where it breaks. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: for each of Mara's and Dev's claims, say whether it is right, wrong, or half-right and why, then tell me who is more right overall and what, if anything, both of them are missing.
