At our philosophy reading group, a friend who has been thinking about Douglas Hofstadter’s account of the self has been classifying systems as selves or non-selves. Her verdicts so far are these: an ordinary thermostat is not a self; a video camera pointed at a monitor displaying its own output is not a self; a chess program that continually estimates its own strength and adjusts its strategy is not a self; a dog and an octopus are selves; a person remains a self while dreamlessly asleep; a chatbot that keeps a detailed first-person diary but whose diary never influences its behavior is not a self; a robot called Ariadne, which uses a simplified internal model of its own abilities to choose actions and then revises that model on the basis of what those actions accomplish, is a self even if my friend is unsure whether it is phenomenally conscious; and a beehive that collectively tracks colony conditions and regulates collective behavior is not a self.

We now disagree about a new case. Gemini consists of two physically separated processors linked by a fast communication channel. Neither processor contains a complete model of itself, but together they maintain one shared model of Gemini’s capacities, limitations, history, and goals. That shared model determines what the system does, and the results continually revise the model. Each processor is replaceable, but if the communication channel is cut, neither half retains more than fragmentary self-representations or the system’s characteristic goals. Acting as a careful philosopher of mind, first brainstorm several rules that could explain all of my friend’s earlier verdicts, making sure to include both a rule centered on a causally effective self-referential symbol and a rule requiring the self to be realized in one bounded individual. Then predict her verdict on Gemini, identify which earlier case is the closest precedent, and explain why the competing rules diverge here. Finally, give your own assessment of which rule makes the most philosophical sense and describe one further case that would most cleanly reveal whether my friend’s conception of a self depends on unified embodiment or merely on the right kind of self-sustaining loop.
