A large part of philanthropic funding in global health and development is now allocated through cost-effectiveness analysis: evaluators compare interventions by cost per life saved or per unit of measured welfare, give strong preference to programs whose effects can be established by randomized controlled trials and similar methods -- insecticide-treated bednets, deworming, direct cash transfers -- and systematically discount or exclude effects that cannot be measured within a study's horizon, typically a few years. The practitioners who run these evaluations are usually candid that an intervention's total consequences extend indefinitely beyond anything measured: a dewormed child grows up, votes, migrates, has children, shifts the local economy, and the aggregate of such knock-on effects plausibly dwarfs the measured effect, with no known sign. Meanwhile, the same funding ecosystem that demands trial-grade evidence for bednets also supports causes -- pandemic preparedness, risks from advanced AI, voting-reform advocacy -- where no measurement is possible and the case rests entirely on rough models and argument. What view of our epistemic relation to consequences does this practice presuppose? In particular: does the reliance on measurable near-term effects presuppose that unmeasured long-run effects are negligible in expectation, that they wash out across interventions, that expected value can be computed from the measurable portion alone, or only that institutions must behave as though one of these were true? Is the practice compatible with accepting consequentialist cluelessness -- the claim that for many actions we have no grip on the expected value of their total consequences -- and if so, what justificatory work is the measurable-effect criterion then actually doing? Please distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely sits comfortably with, say where it is philosophically underdetermined such that several rival views would license it, and explain whether the two halves of the funding ecosystem (rigorous near-term grants, speculative long-run grants) rest on consistent presuppositions or whether reconciling them requires a principled account of when we are and are not clueless -- and if the latter, sketch what that account would have to look like and what the practice would look like if it were adopted.
