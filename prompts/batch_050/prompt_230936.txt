So I volunteer at a small natural history museum, mostly dusting minerals and doing the weekend tours, and somehow I talked my way into giving a lunch talk at the geology department of the university in town. My talk is basically: the Anthropocene should be an actual, official geological epoch, starting around 1950. I got obsessed with this after reading that there was a whole proposal to make it official -- something about a lake in Canada where the mud layers have plutonium from the hydrogen bomb tests, and they wanted that layer to be the marker, the "golden spike" thing -- and then a commitee of geologists voted a couple years back against making it official, so right now the Anthropocene is still just a word people use, not a real unit on the chart. My talk argues the vote was wrong, or at least that it shouldn't settle the matter, because something genuinely new is happening to the planet and 1950 is as clean a line as geology ever gets.

The audience will be faculty and grad students in geology, including, I'm told, at least one actual stratigrapher, meaning someone who does this time-scale stuff for a living and probably has Opinions about how you get a new age or epoch approved. I am not a geologist and they will know that within thirty seconds. So I want to be prepped for the question period: what objections will these people actually raise, phrased the way they'd phrase them (I keep seeing words like "diachronous" and I suspect that word is coming for me), which objections are just guild gatekeeping about their approval process and which ones would genuinely sink my argument on the merits, and what I should concede cheerfully up front -- I assume the fact that humans were already reshaping things with farming and coal way before 1950 is going to be a problem for my clean start date, and I don't know how big a problem.

Also, what's the question I should pray nobody asks -- the one where my honest answer is "yeah, that's fair, my position might just be wrong" -- and if it comes anyway, what's the least-bad thing to say in the moment? And is there one point I can make in reply that, if it lands, lets me walk out with my dignity, like the idea that this is the first time a species can read its own marker in the rock and argue about it, or is that too cute for this crowd?
