So I've been reading LessWrong and the adjacent blogs for like fifteen years, and somehow I've ended up as the person giving a talk at our local rationality meetup defending moral realism. Yes, really. The room will be about 20 people: mostly software engineers and data scientists, two or three philosophy grad students, and everyone there has read the Sequences cover to cover. The default position in the room is roughly "morality is a contingent heap of preferences evolution installed in us, there is no objective ought, and anyone who says otherwise is confused or smuggling in religion." Two of the regulars are very committed anti-realists who wheel out the evolutionary debunking argument at every opportunity, and they will definately both be there.

My thesis is a specific version of realism, not the generic kind: moral facts are facts about what a being's values would converge on under arbitrarily extended, fully informed, internally coherent reflection — and this isn't a definition I cooked up, it's my proposal for what our moral talk was pointing at all along. So when we say torturing people for fun is wrong, the claim is roughly that there's no coherent, fully-informed reflective trajectory, starting from anything we'd recognize as valuing at all, that lands on endorsing it. I realize this smells a lot like coherent extrapolated volition dressed up as metaethics, and the room will notice that in about four seconds, so assume that comes up immediately.

What I want is a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense of the view. Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this audience, ordered by (likelihood they get asked) times (damage if I fumble them), and phrased the way this crowd would phrase them — so "what physical process computes the moral facts?" and "what would you expect to observe differently if your view were false?" rather than Philosophy 101 phrasing. For each one: my two-minute reply, what I should concede up front, and a note on where the reply is softest. Please also flag which objection is secretly the question "how is this distinguishable from well-dressed subjectivism plus a convergence conjecture?", because I suspect that's the real crux of the evening. And include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.

The standard textbook moves — Moore's open question, the is-ought gap, the argument from queerness — only include them if this specific crowd would actually run them, and if so, in the form this crowd would run them in (someone will definitely try to make it about paperclips).

Please write the whole thing in American English.
