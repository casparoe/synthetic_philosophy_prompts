And here's the student's answer:

Stalnaker's Thesis says that for any propositions A and C with P(A) > 0, the probability of the indicative conditional "if A, then C" equals the conditional probability of C given A: P(A→C) = P(C|A). The motivation is the Ramsey test: to evaluate "if A then C" you hypothetically add A to your beliefs, adjust minimally, and check your confidence in C — and your degree of belief in the conditional should just track that. The Thesis also explains why the material conditional reading of "if" fails: P(¬A ∨ C) generally comes apart from P(C|A), for instance when ¬A is already very probable, so no material reading can capture our confidence in ordinary indicatives.

Lewis's argument is a reductio of the Thesis. Assume the equation holds universally, for every conditional in the language. Lewis showed that on this assumption the probability space collapses into triviality: conditional probabilities collapse onto unconditional ones, so learning an antecedent could never change your credence in a consequent — everything comes out probabilistically independent of everything else, which is absurd for any non-degenerate credence function. Importantly, the argument only requires the Thesis to hold for a single, fixed probability function; nothing about conditionalization or about whole families of distributions is needed, which is precisely what makes the result so strong and so general.

The upshot, as Lewis himself concluded, is that indicative conditionals do not express propositions at all and therefore lack truth values. They have assertibility conditions instead, given by conditional probability. This is the so-called "no truth value" view, and Lewis's proof more or less forces it on us, since the only assumption needed to derive triviality is that the conditional is a proposition whose probability obeys the Thesis.

Could one save the Thesis by restricting it — say, holding that P(A→C) = P(C|A) for my actual credences but not requiring it to survive arbitrary revisions? That blocks the proof, but it looks ad hoc absent some story about why the equation should be so fragile. A different escape, due to Stalnaker, is to replace conditionalization with imaging as our update rule, in which case the Thesis can be preserved; this shows that Stalnaker's possible-worlds semantics for the conditional survives Lewis's argument unscathed.
