So I've been thinking about the doctrine of double effect -- the idea that it's sometimes ok to bring about a bad outcome you merely foresee, but not one you intend as a means or end. I've been collecting thought experiments that are supposed to test or break the doctrine, and my list so far is:

1. The classic terror bomber vs tactical bomber pair (both kill the same number of civilians, but only one aims at their deaths).
2. The trolley loop case, where redirecting the trolley only saves the five because the one person's body stops it -- so it seems like you need the death, not just foresee it.
3. The hysterectomy vs craniotomy pair (removing a pregnant uterus to save the mother vs crushing the baby's skull to get it out).
4. The "closing the cave door" style cases, where whether the agent counts as intending the harm seems to depend on how you describe what's in their head, which makes the doctrine look dangerously flexible.

Here's my problem: every new case I come up with feels like one of these with the props swapped out. Change the train to a trolley to a tram, change the bystander to a fat man, whatever -- it's the same skeleton. Can you give me a case that tests the intention/foresight distinction in a genuinely different way, not just a reskin? Bonus if it pressures the doctrine from a different angle than "you secretly needed the harm" or "intentions are too hard to pin down." Then tell me honestly whether you think the case actually works, or whether a defender of double effect has an easy out.
