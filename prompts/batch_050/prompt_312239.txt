So last week I was hanging out with two friends, Marcus and Dee, and they got into this argument about procrastination that I couldn't settle, so I'm bringing it here.

Marcus had just pulled his third all-nighter this month on a project he'd known about for weeks, and he called himself weak-willed. Dee said there's no such thing as weakness of will. At the moment he chose to watch videos instead of working, that was simply what he preferred. Her line was basically: you can't act against your own judgment, if you really judged working was best you'd have worked, so "weak will" is just a story you tell afterwards to make yourself sound better.

Marcus came back with: okay but look at the actual pattern. Every morning he says "tonight I'm working." Every evening he chooses not to. Every next morning he regrets it. That's not a stable preference, that's a preference that flips on a schedule, and he said he heard on a podcast that it's called hyperbolic discounting — stuff available right now gets massively overweighted compared to stuff later, so when the deadline is far away the plan wins, and once evening rolls around the videos win.

Dee said discounting the future is perfectly rational, a dollar today really is worth more than a dollar next year, so maybe evening-Marcus just has different but legit priorities and morning-Marcus is being a control freak about it. Marcus said the difference is that the normal, even kind of discounting doesn't cause flips — if you discount steadily, you'll still prefer the same plan tomorrow that you prefer today. The hyperbolic kind makes you reverse yourself, and the proof it's a genuine problem and not just "different priorities" is that he'll literally pay money to tie his own hands: he uses an app that locks his phone, and he once gave Dee $50 she could keep if he missed a deadline. Why would morning-him need to defend himself against evening-him with actual cash if evening-him's priorities were just as good?

Dee's last move was that regret doesn't prove anything — you can regret any choice, and the regret is just another mood, not a verdict from his "real self." There's no real self, there's just him at different times wanting different things, and calling the evening version "weak" is just taking sides. Marcus said if that's true then his blocking app is basically one self bullying another, and nobody would think of it as a helpful tool.

Neither of them budged and I genuinely can't tell who's winning. Can you go through it point by point — like, is Dee right that you can't literally act against your own judgment, and if she is, does that kill the idea of weakness of will or just force a better description of what's happening? Is Marcus right that the flipping pattern is what makes it irrational, as opposed to just having a strong taste for the present? And does the precommitment stuff actually count as evidence for his side, or does Dee's "no real self" picture explain it just as well? Also, what are they both missing?
