Hi, some background first: I'm a retired structural chemist, spent forty years squinting at protein folds, and since retiring I've fallen hard down the philosophy rabbit hole. My granddaughter set this one off. Her reading book prints its a's as double-story -- bowl underneath, little arch on top -- but at school she's taught to write a single-story one, just a circle with a stick. Nobody blinks; both are "the letter a." So what makes an a an a? I gather Hofstadter's group obsessed over this decades ago, but I don't want a book report on anyone's work. I want you to actually build me a case.

Here are two theories I've cooked up, and I want them pried apart:

1. The structure theory: a mark is an 'a' iff it realizes the right abstract schema -- the right parts (bowl, stem) in the right relations -- where the schema tolerates stylistic stretch but not the loss or addition of parts.
2. The readership theory: a mark is an 'a' iff it stands in the convention by which competent readers in the relevant community reliably take it as an 'a'. Recognition and use, not anatomy.

Construct a thought experiment where these come apart: one concrete scenario, set somewhere realistic (a type designer's studio, a handwriting-recognition startup, a sign-painter's workshop -- your pick), in which a particular mark gets "that's an a" from one theory and "that's not an a" from the other. Constraints: the mark has to be physically described precisely enough that I can picture it; no alien alphabets or sci-fi readers; the scenario itself one paragraph; and no cheating by stipulating the verdicts -- they have to fall out of the theories as I've stated them.

After the scenario: walk through each theory's verdict and why it lands there; tell me which single feature of the case is load-bearing and whether removing it collapses the thing back into boring agreement; and then give the best counter-move each theorist has -- e.g. can the structure theorist save the verdict by loosening the schema to allow "implied" parts, and can the readership theorist wiggle out by appealing to ideal rather than actual readers?

Full disclosure, my chemist's instinct says shape is everything, but the more I think about it the more I suspect the answer is alot slipperier. Have at it.
