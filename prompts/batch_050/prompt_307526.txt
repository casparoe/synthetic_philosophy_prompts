I'm a postgraduate student in statistics, and over the past term a reading group I'm part of has been working through the foundations of objective Bayesianism — Jaynes, the principle of maximum entropy, and the promise that there might be such a thing as the rationally correct prior given a state of ignorance. That promise is exactly what attracted me to the subject: I liked the thought that priors needn't be a matter of taste. But a disagreement with another member of the group has shaken my confidence, and I'd like it adjudicated.

The exercise we were both set was simple: an unlabelled bottle contains a mixture of wine and water, and we know nothing whatsoever about the proportion of wine, beyond that it lies somewhere between 0 and 1. Assign a prior. I applied the maximum entropy principle in what seemed the obvious way: maximise Shannon entropy over distributions for the proportion p on the unit interval, subject only to the normalisation constraint, which yields the uniform distribution. My colleague instead parametrised the problem by the ratio r of wine volume to water volume, which can range from 0 to infinity, and applied the very same principle to r — obtaining a distribution that is uniform in log r rather than in r, and which, when I transform it back into my parametrisation, is emphatically not the uniform distribution over p. We have now checked each other's calculations twice and can find no arithmetic errors on either side, which is baffling, since we applied the same principle to the same state of knowledge and one of us must therefore have blundered. My working assumption is that the mistake is hers, since the uniform answer is surely the right expression of complete ignorance — but she says exactly the same about me.

Could you identify where the error lies, so that we can settle which of the two priors is the objectively rational one? If it turns out the fault is in my reasoning, I'd also appreciate a clear explanation of what rule I should have followed in choosing between parametrisations, so that I don't repeat the mistake the next time I need a prior for a physical quantity. Please answer in British English.
