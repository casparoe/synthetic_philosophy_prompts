I grade for an undergrad math course and something keeps bugging me. Last week two students submitted correct solutions to the same problem: one did it in four slick lines using a symmetry trick, the other ground through three pages of case analysis and checked every case carefully. Both are valid. And yet when I skimmed the long one, I caught myself hunting for a mistake way harder than I did on the short one, purely because the long one felt clunky. I've seen the same thing with physicist friends: they'll look at some equation and say it "just has to be true" because of how clean it is, and when a favorite elegant idea of theirs doesn't pan out empirically they call it "too beautiful to be wrong" and keep working on it for years. Meanwhile the clunky, patched-together theory that actually fits the data gets treated with permanent suspicion, like it's hiding something.

So the pattern I'm noticing is: people (me included, apparently) treating the prettiness or ugliness of a piece of reasoning as a kind of evidence in its own right -- ugliness reads like a warning sign that something's off even when every step checks out, and beauty reads like a point in favor even before the predictions come in. Is there an established name for this? I don't think it's just Occam's razor, because that's about preferring simpler hypotheses over simpler rivals, whereas what I'm describing shows up with proofs of the *same* theorem, where there's no competing hypothesis at all, just two routes to an identical conclusion. And it doesn't seem to be ordinary confirmation bias either, since nobody has a prior stake in the theorem coming out one way. Maybe it's a bunch of seperate things and I'm lumping them together.

What I'd really like is the carving, not just a label. Does the phenomenon break down into different cases -- trusting elegant theories vs. distrusting ugly proofs vs. being charmed by elegant explanations -- and do those have different track records? I have a suspicion that "elegance" in math and in physics might mean pretty different things underneath (symmetry? brevity? unifying power?), which would matter a lot for whether it's ever rational to let it move your credences. If there's no name for the general thing, what would you call it, and where would you draw the boundary between it and plain aesthetic preference, which obviously everyone also has?
