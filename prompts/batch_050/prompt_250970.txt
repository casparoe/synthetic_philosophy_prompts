So I'm on an infra team and we're about to roll out a new caching layer, and I got into a dumb argument with a coworker about how to estimate the risk and now I'm genuinely stuck on who's right.

We have no idea how often the new cache corrupts data under some rare race condition. Like genuinely no idea, the load tests didn't catch anything but they never do. So for the launch review I said fine, let's just treat the corruption probability p as a uniform draw between 0 and 1, since we know nothing, and compute expected damage off that. That gives you expected corruption of 50% of writes in the affected window, which sounds insane but whatever, it's a worst-case-ish neutral assumption, and under that number we should NOT launch.

My coworker says my assumption isn't neutral at all. His version: the thing we actually know nothing about is how many writes happen between corruptions, call it N. If you know nothing about N, you should spread your belief evenly over N instead (say uniformly over some huge range), and if you do the math on that, the implied p is tiny almost all the time, and the launch totally passes review. And then a third person in the thread said why not just be uniform over the log of p, which gives yet another answer.

Here's the thing that's melting my brain: all three of us are using the exact same rule. "We know nothing, so spread our credence out evenly." We're just applying it to different ways of describing the same situation (p, 1/p, log p) and getting completely different launch decisions out of it. They can't all be the neutral choice, but I can't find a non-arbitrary reason to prefer one variable over the others. p feels natural because the damage model is written in terms of p, but that's just... how I happend to write the spreadsheet?

So my question: is there a principled answer to which parametrization is the "right" one to be uniform over, or is the whole "when you know nothing, be uniform" rule just broken and you can't actually express ignorance this way? And if it's broken, what should I actually put in the launch doc, because I have to put SOMETHING. Please don't just tell me to run more tests, assume the tests are done and the decision has to be made with what we have.
