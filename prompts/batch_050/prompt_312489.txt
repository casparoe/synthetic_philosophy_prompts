I'm a PhD student in evolutionary biology who keeps ending up in my department's phil science reading group, and there's a pattern I keep tripping over that I can't find a term for in anything I've read.

The pattern: two rival theories agree on every possible observation, and not because our instruments are too crude, but because the theory that posits the extra thing also says, through its own laws, that the extra thing can never show up in any measurement. The standard example is the old ether response to relativity -- keep a preferred rest frame, but build in contraction and clock effects so that no experiment can ever reveal whether you're moving relative to it. The frame exists in the theory's picture of the world, yet the theory itself guarentees its invisibility. Same shape with setups of classical mechanics that posit absolute positions versus ones that posit only relative distances: the laws conspire so that the absolute facts leave no trace whatsoever.

Contrast that with the case people usually discuss, where two theories fit all the current data but diverge on experiments nobody has run yet. There the tie is breakable in principle -- build the bigger collider. In my cases the tie is not breakable even in principle, and not because of anything about us; the theory forecloses it itself.

Everything I've found lumps both situations under empirical equivalence or underdetermination, which seems to flatten a real difference. So: does the stronger kind have a name of its own? I don't just want a label -- if nothing established fits, give me a proposed term and a definition that separates it from the ordinary breakable kind, a view on whether internally-equivalent "rivals" are really two theories or one (philosophers I ask say two, physicists say one, which makes me suspect the interesting issue is hiding in what counts as the same theory), and some thought about boundary cases, like when the undetectability holds only if the theory is exactly true, or only given some extra assumption about initial conditions.
