I am a PhD student in practical ethics preparing a seminar exercise on moral status. I would like a classification instrument, aimed at philosophically literate non-specialists, that sorts positions on whether entities can possess moral status by degrees, focusing on cases ranging from insects to thermostats.

Please construct a decision tree with eight to twelve diagnostic questions and no more than ten leaves. Begin with the broad question of whether an entity matters morally only insofar as there is something it is like to be that entity, then proceed through distinctions such as:

- whether sentience is binary or admits of degree;
- whether degree of moral status tracks only intensity and richness of experience, or also temporal continuity, agency, sociality, and cognitive integration;
- whether uncertainty about sentience should be treated as reducing moral status, generating probabilistic obligations, or triggering a precautionary response;
- whether morally relevant states must be phenomenally conscious rather than merely functionally represented;
- whether tiny amounts of welfare, aggregated across enormous numbers of entities, can outweigh much larger amounts of welfare in a few beings.

Use concrete cases at the branch points wherever possible: a fruit fly avoiding heat, a honeybee displaying apparent disappointment after a reward is withdrawn, a nematode with 302 neurons, a sea sponge with no neurons, a thermostat that switches state to avoid overheating, and a simple robot trained to recoil from stimuli labelled as damaging. Do not require respondents to know the scientific literature; each case should specify the behavioural and structural features relevant to the question.

The leaves should include suitably precise versions of at least the following positions, using either established labels or clearly stipulated ones:

1. strict threshold sentientism;
2. scalar or gradualist sentientism;
3. a multidimensional account on which sentience is necessary but cognitive integration can increase status;
4. a precautionary account under evidential uncertainty;
5. an expected-moral-status account;
6. a view assigning minimal status to any goal-directed or homeostatic system;
7. a view denying degrees of status while allowing degrees of welfare.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than simply listing labels. Note any plausible combinations of answers that remain unoccupied and briefly explain whether that is because the resulting view looks incoherent, unattractive, or merely undertheorised. Flag any leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate. Finally, include a short scoring key explaining how each answer pattern leads to its leaf, and identify the question most likely to produce the greatest disagreement among respondents.

Please write the answer in British English.
