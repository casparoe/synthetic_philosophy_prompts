A colleague who audited an ethics course gave me this summary of the situationist critique of virtue ethics: "Social psychologists have shown that character traits are largely an illusion. In experiments such as Milgram's obedience studies and the Good Samaritan study, whether people behaved decently tracked situational details -- an authority figure's presence, being in a hurry -- rather than any stable disposition to be honest or compassionate. So the virtues that virtue ethics takes to be the foundation of morality turn out not to exist, and the view is empirically refuted: rather than trying to cultivate character, we should focus on engineering situations that produce good behavior." I suspect this is stronger than what the situationists actually claimed, but I can't say precisely where it goes too far. Which parts would Doris or Harman accept, where does the summary slide from a claim about how well traits predict behavior into a claim that traits do not exist, and does the concluding prescription about moral education follow from what the experiments support even once the summary is corrected?
