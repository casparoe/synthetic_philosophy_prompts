I teach a combined law-and-philosophy seminar for second-year undergraduates, about 20 students, and I have one 90-minute session to introduce strict liability and mens rea. None of them will have read anything on the topic beforehand, and I don't want any lecture or legal vocabulary up front.

Design a simulation they play in small groups that makes them feel the force of punishing without fault from both sides before they have names for anything. Concretely, I want the game to have: (1) roles with a genuine choice about how much of a risky-but-beneficial activity to engage in, so the activity-level deterrence argument can emerge from their own behavior rather than from me; (2) chance events that can produce real harm to others even when a player took every precaution the rules made available; (3) an adjudication phase in which other students must decide punishment under conditions where fault is genuinely undeterminable, so they are forced to choose a liability rule and live with what it does to the person in front of them; (4) at least two rounds, the second introducing a due-diligence-style defense, so participants can feel how much of their recoil from round one survives and how much was really about the absence of any escape hatch; and (5) small but real stakes (I'll provide them; suggest options).

The design should keep two questions visibly separate that students will otherwise conflate: whether liability without fault could ever be justified, and whether fault can ever be reliably found -- so they can see that some strict liability rules are epistemic stopgaps rather than rejections of the fault principle.

Then give me the debrief: map specific moments of the game onto mens rea, the due diligence middle ground, and the deterrence-versus-desert tension; tell me what will predictably go wrong when I run it (players gaming the adjudication, the unlucky player becoming a martyr, groups converging too fast) and how to handle each; and state plainly which features of real strict liability the game cannot reproduce and what I must therefore supply in discussion afterwards.
