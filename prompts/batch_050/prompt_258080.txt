So there's a pattern I keep running into and I can't figure out if philosophers or economists already have a word for it. The pattern is: sometimes it pays to be the kind of person who would do the "wrong" thing. Like, my coworker genuinely flies off the handle if he finds out someone lowballed him in a split, and because everyone knows he's like that, nobody ever tries it -- his temper makes him money, even though in the moment blowing up the deal costs him too. Or the classic movie thing where the driver in a game of chicken yanks the steering wheel off and waves it out the window. Or imagine someone gets rescued from a breakdown in the middle of nowhere and promises the driver a thousand bucks when they get to town -- once they're safely in town, keeping the money is clearly the "smart" move, but if the rescuer had known the guy was the type to think that way, he'd have driven right past.

I don't think this is just weakness of will, because the person's not failing at anything -- the flakiness or hotheadedness is doing work for them. And it's not quite what game theory people mean by a credible threat, or at least I don't think so, because the coworker isn't strategizing at all, he really is just like that. Is there a name for this -- where being predictably irrational in some domain ends up rational overall? If there's a name, I want more than the name: how does the carving go, what counts and what doesn't, where are the edges. If there isn't one, what would you call it and what would the borderline cases be?
