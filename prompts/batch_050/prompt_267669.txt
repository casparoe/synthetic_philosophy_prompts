Having spent forty years as a research chemist, I've taken up philosophy in retirement, and a recurring argument with a younger colleague from my old institute has me stuck. He maintains that once molecular biology and neuroscience have fully explained human behaviour, our everyday talk of reasons, intentions, and choices will go the way of phlogiston — and that, in the meantime, any explanation pitched above the level of genes and neurons is at best a convenient placeholder and at worst an illusion, since only the most fundamental level of description can be genuinely correct. I am hardly hostile to reduction in science; it has served me well. Yet his eagerness to dismiss every higher level of explanation strikes me as overreach, and I find I cannot say precisely why. Is there a principled error in his position, or am I merely being sentimental about the manifest image? Please give me your considered advice, state how confident you are in your diagnosis, and single out the point on which you are least sure of yourself.
