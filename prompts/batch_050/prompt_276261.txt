Analyze the 2003 collapse of DARPA's Policy Analysis Market as a test case for Robin Hanson's futarchy proposal. The facts as I understand them, which you should take as given: the Pentagon's research agency funded a program under which traders would buy and sell contracts paying out on geopolitical events in the Middle East, on the theory that market prices would aggregate dispersed information better than expert estimates; after two senators publicly denounced the project as a market for betting on assassinations and terror attacks, it was cancelled within days, before ever operating. Hanson's futarchy is a distinct and more ambitious proposal: elected institutions would retain the choice of values and welfare metrics, but the choice among policies would be delegated to conditional prediction markets, so that beliefs are settled by betting while ends remain democratically chosen.

Do the analysis in both directions. First, map the episode onto futarchy's machinery with some care: what role does a Policy Analysis Market play in the futarchic scheme, if any, given that it forecast events rather than the consequences of adopted policies? Second, run the standard objections against the episode and see which ones it actually engaged: the moral-hazard objection (a market in assassinations creates incentives to assassinate), the thin-market and manipulation objection, and the objection that some subjects are repugnant to price at all regardless of accuracy. Third, the direction I care most about: what does the cancellation itself show about futarchy's political viability? Futarchy presupposes institutions willing to bind themselves to prices in advance and publics willing to let them; here the mere announcement of a market, with no governance stakes at all, was politically fatal. Say whether that is evidence that futarchy fails on its own terms -- because deference to markets is exactly what democratic publics will not grant -- or whether the episode is instead consistent with futarchy, on the grounds that a market punished by rumor rather than by trading losses is no test of market accuracy.
