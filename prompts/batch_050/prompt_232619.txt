I work on deployed probabilistic forecasting systems and recently encountered the epistemology debate between reductionism and anti-reductionism about testimony. As I understand it, reductionists think a hearer is justified in accepting testimony only insofar as they possess independent, non-testimonial evidence that the speaker or type of source is reliable; anti-reductionists think testimony can itself provide defeasible justification, without the hearer first having to assemble such a reliability case, provided there are no relevant defeaters. Restate the two positions as faithfully as possible in that vocabulary, and use cases such as a child accepting a parent’s report, a traveler asking a stranger for directions, a researcher relying on a laboratory’s measurements, and a forecaster aggregating conflicting expert predictions to show how each position would treat them.
