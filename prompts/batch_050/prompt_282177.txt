i'm TAing a philosophy of biology seminar and we're doing the species problem next month. i want a handout that's basically a "which species concept do you actually endorse?" quiz, 9 to 12 questions, that sorts students by how they react to cases rather than by which label they already think sounds smart. so no question may ask anything like "do you think interbreeding matters?". instead each question should be a concrete biological situation with two or three answer options: a ring species where the end populations can't interbreed but the chain in between can, a bacterial lineage that keeps swapping genes horizontally with distant relatives, an asexual dandelion or bdelloid rotifer lineage that's been stable for ages, a fossil sequence where the endpoints look like different species but every intermediate grade is present, a hybrid zone where two forms interbreed freely in one valley and not at all in the next. you can use those or better ones you come up with, but at least two questions have to involve asexual or microbial organisms, because every quiz i've seen just quietly ignores them.

the scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these leaves: the biological species concept, a phylogenetic/monophyly-based concept, an ecological niche concept, a phenetic or morphological concept, pluralism (different concepts for different groups or purposes), and outright eliminativism (there are no species, only populations and lineages, and the category is a bookkeeping convenience). build the key from the logical space first, not from the names: figure out which combinations of answers are even possible, and for any combination no real position occupies, add a one-sentence diagnosis of why -- is it incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. flag any two leaves your key can't reliably tell apart. also, somewhere in the key, note where the "species are individuals, not kinds" thesis lands, since it cuts across the usual concepts and i don't want students thinking the quiz settled it.

write the whole thing as continuous prose, like you're writing me a long email -- no headings, no bulleted or numbered lists, no bold, no flowchart diagram, and no table for the scoring key. the questions and the branching and the key all have to come through in sentences, e.g. "if you said the salamanders are one species because gene flow connects them, that answer counts toward..." you get the idea.
