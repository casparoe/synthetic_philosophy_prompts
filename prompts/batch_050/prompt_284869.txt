Question: "Maxwell's demon appears able to sort molecules by speed and thereby decrease entropy without doing work, seemingly violating the second law. Explain the setup, and assess whether Landauer's principle and the physics of information resolve the paradox."

Student's answer:

Maxwell's demon sits at a trapdoor between two gas chambers and lets fast molecules go one way and slow ones the other, creating a temperature gradient from which work could be extracted, with no work input. So either the second law fails or there's a hidden cost somewhere. Szilard simplified this to a single molecule in a box: the demon inserts a partition, learns which side the molecule is on, and extracts kT ln 2 of work from a one-molecule heat engine via isothermal expansion. Szilard showed that the act of measurement itself dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat, which is exactly where the second law is saved. Landauer later generalized this: erasing one bit of information costs kT ln 2 of heat dumped into the environment, and since the demon must erase its memory of each measurement to operate cyclically, this erasure cost precisely cancels the work gained. Since computation always involves erasure, this shows that information processing inevitably generates heat, which is why your laptop gets hot. The conclusion is that the demon fails not because of any limitation on measurement, but because the demon is a physical system with a memory, and thermodynamics applies to information itself. The second law survives intact once we count the full system including the demon's brain.
