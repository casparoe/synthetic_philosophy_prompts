I write a column for a student philosophy magazine, and I recently got into an argument with a friend about a view he encountered in a popular essay on epistemology. He summarized the view for me, from memory, as follows:

"There's a trendy position in epistemology called pragmatic encroachment. On this view, whether you know something isn't fixed by the traditional factors like truth, belief, and evidence, because knowledge isn't really about getting at the truth — it's about what's practically useful to you. So whether a belief counts as knowledge changes with your stakes: if nothing rides on it, you know the bank is open on Saturdays; if a missed mortgage payment would ruin you, you don't know it, even though nothing about the bank or your evidence has changed. Knowledge, on this picture, is created by your practical situation rather than discovered, so there are no facts about what anyone knows independent of anyone's interests. And that's absurd — a theory that makes knowledge turn on the size of your bank balance has lost the plot entirely, since knowledge is by definition a purely epistemic matter. So pragmatic encroachment is less a theory of knowledge than a reductio of the whole idea that epistemology can be interest-relative."

My sense is that my friend has garbled a more defensible position, but I can't yet say exactly where. I also suspect he has quietly run together two views that specialists keep apart, because I've heard the phrase "pragmatic encroachment" used both for a claim about knowledge itself and for a claim about when it's legitimate to *attribute* knowledge to someone, and I'm not sure which one his summary is about, or whether the distinction matters for his conclusion.

Say whether his refutation at the end survives once the summary is corrected: does the view really make knowledge depend on facts like the size of your bank balance, and even if it does in some sense, is that a reductio or just a surprising consequence that proponents are willing to defend with cases?
4. Clarify the knowledge/attribution ambiguity I flagged: state the two positions crisply, say which of them my friend's summary is actually criticizing, and whether his argument would fare better against the other one.
