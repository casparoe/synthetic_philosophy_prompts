Morgan's canon, articulated by C. Lloyd Morgan in his work on comparative psychology around the turn of the twentieth century, holds that an animal's behavior should not be attributed to a higher psychological faculty if it can be adequately explained by a lower one; the historical touchstone here is the Clever Hans affair, in which a horse's apparent arithmetic turned out to be responsiveness to subtle, unintentional cues from onlookers. Write a satirical piece of about 700 words in American English that turns this principle back on its human inventors. The piece should take the form of a mock peer-review report from an invented journal of comparative cognition, published in a world where the discipline is run by nonhuman animals — say, a board dominated by corvids, cetaceans, and great apes — and where a brave researcher has submitted a manuscript claiming to have discovered evidence of language, episodic memory, and theory of mind in Homo sapiens. The reviewers, scrupulous adherents of Morgan's canon, find the manuscript unparsimonious in the extreme: each reported phenomenon must be re-explained via lower faculties, so human conversation becomes conditioned vocal behavior maintained by intermittent reinforcement, planning becomes associative chaining, and the purported "theory of mind" results are dismissed with the Clever Hans precedent — the experimental subjects were surely responding to subtle cues from the experimenters. Keep the tone perfectly deadpan throughout, in the exact bureaucratic register of a real review: a recommendation line, numbered major and minor comments, a condescending Reviewer 2 who demands additional controls, and an editor's summary. The humor must arise from the genuine logic of the canon and from the real methodological debates in animal cognition research — anthropomorphism, parsimony, the double standard about which species gets the benefit of the doubt — rather than from generic jokes about academics or reviewers. A specialist in comparative psychology should recognize that the satire understands what Morgan's canon actually says and what it was reacting against, while a lay reader should still be able to follow the inversion and find it funny. Do not name or caricature any real researcher; all reviewers and the submitting author should be fictional.
