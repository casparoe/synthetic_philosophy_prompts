I'm a retired chemist, spent forty years doing risk assessments for industrial processes, and now in retirement I've fallen down the philosophy-of-rationality rabbit hole. So here's what's bugging me.

Standard expected utility theory says rationality means obeying some axioms, and one of them (Independence, or something like it) basically forces you to care only about the probability-weighted average of outcomes. But there's a family of views -- I think Buchak's is the famous one, though describe the idea however you like -- where you're allowed to weight outcomes by their *rank*: a globally risk-averse agent gives extra weight to the worst-case outcomes beyond what their probability alone would justify. So such an agent can prefer $1 million for sure over a gamble that's 89% $1M, 10% $5M, 1% nothing, even at the same time as preferring an 11% chance of $1M over a 10% chance of $5M, and this is supposed to be perfectly rational, not a lapse.

Give me your actual credence that this kind of pure risk-attitude-driven departure from expected utility theory is *rationally permissible* -- not just understandable or excusable, but no less rational than maximizing expected utility. I want a number, and I want the decomposition:

- How much of your answer rides on whether the usual arguments for the EU axioms (Dutch books, money pumps, dynamic inconsistency) actually work against rank-dependent agents, or whether those agents can escape them somehow
- How much rides on whether an attitude toward risk is more like a taste (not up for rational criticism) or more like a belief (up for criticism)
- How much rides on whether "rationally permissible" even has a determinate answer here, versus the question being terminological
- Whatever other terms you think I'm missing -- you probably see some I don't, I'm new at this

A rough probability and one or two lines of justification for each term, then how they combine into your final number. Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or thought experiment would move it most.

For context: my own gut says about 0.65 that it's permissible, but my gut also spent decades being paid to treat worst-case scenarios as special, so I don't trust it.
