Next month I’m doing a short talk at a local EA/rationalist-ish meetup defending a deliberately unsexy answer to equality-of-what: not welfare, not resources, not full-blown capabilities, but what I call a protected option floor. Rough idea: what matters for equality is that everyone has secure access to a small set of life-shaping options -- enough food/health/safety to think straight, some real say over where their life goes, no being permanently trapped by other people’s preferences -- while above that floor we mostly maximise expected welfare and stop fussing about comparative gaps. It’s meant to dodge utility monsters and wireheading without going full “list of ten central capabilities,” and it’s meant to be legible to people who like QALYs but hate tyranny-of-the-marginal. The floor is lexical-ish but not absolute: catastrophic tradeoffs can breach it if preventing something even worse requires it, which is definately the bit they’ll smell blood on.

The room: mostly longtermist-leaning utilitarians who’ve read the usual blogs, a couple of GiveWell-pilled global health people, one annoying but sharp deontologist, and an econ PhD who thinks anything non-measurable is astrology. Prepare me for the objections I’ll actually get from this audience, not from a political theory seminar. Order them by expected damage, phrase each as that person would phrase it over pizza, say what I should concede quickly, what I should hold, and give a two-minute reply for each. Flag the weakest point in the view that I should defend head-on rather than hope nobody notices, and include the question I’m praying nobody asks. Please answer in British English, and keep it practical rather than a bibliography.
