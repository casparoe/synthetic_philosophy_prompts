I’m a software engineer without formal philosophy training, designing an empirical testbed for AI safety via debate. Assume that two AI debaters optimize to persuade a judge and can exploit obfuscated arguments: shifting definitions, long proof chains, cherry-picked intermediate conclusions, and claims supported by computation the judge cannot independently rerun. Before building the benchmark, which open conceptual questions should most guide a one-year research agenda? Identify three to five questions, rank them, and defend the ranking by three criteria: how many other questions depend on them, how tractable they are for an engineering-led team, and how much their answers would change the debate protocol. For each question, specify what experiment or formal criterion could help settle it. In particular, assess whether the central problem is that judges cannot distinguish compression from understanding, that honest debaters lack a winning strategy against strategic complexity, or that debate lacks a stable success criterion once invalid arguments can be made compelling—or propose a better decomposition.
