In my ethics seminar I have to give a 20-minute presentation defending moral trade — roughly the idea that two people who disagree morally can sometimes both do better, by their own lights, by cooperating across the disagreement instead of each just pushing their own view. The case my paper is built around: two flatmates, one thinks eating meat is a serious moral wrong, the other thinks the serious wrong is spending money on luxuries instead of effective charities, and they strike a deal — the meat-eater goes vegetarian and the vegan donates a set amount to the other person's charity. My thesis is that trades like this aren't just permitted, they can actually be morally required, even when you think the other party's values are mistaken. The audience: the prof leans Kantian/contractualist, the rest are mostly second-years who've done trolley problems and not much else, plus one very committed effective altruist who will definetly have opinions. Write my Q&A prep sheet: the 6–8 objections I'll actually get from this crowd (not from philosophers who already work on this), ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would really phrase it, with a short reply and a note on where my reply is weakest. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
