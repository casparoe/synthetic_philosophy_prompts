So I'm in a philosophy of art and ethics seminar and last week we watched Amadeus. The film turns Salieri into this bitter mediocrity who sabotages Mozart and then confesses to basically killing him, which as far as I can tell is invented -- the real Salieri was a respected composer and teacher, and now he's permanently "the jealous hack" in everyone's head because of a great movie. Read the film through the question of what we owe the real dead when we dramatize them. Is there even such a thing as harming someone after death, and if the harm is to reputation rather than to the person, does that change the answer? Does it matter that Salieri has no descendants who care, that he was already semi-public property, or that the slander made a better story -- the director's defense was basically that it's a fiction, not a biopic? I want a reading that takes the film seriously as art, not just a hit piece about it, and I also want the place where the ethical reading strains: Amadeus is pretty openly Salieri's self-mythologizing confession, so is the film actually claiming any of this happened, and does that get it off the hook or make it worse? End with what, if anything, a case like this adds to the posthumous harm debate that the journal articles don't capture.
