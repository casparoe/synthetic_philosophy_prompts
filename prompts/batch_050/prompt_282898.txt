My book group is doing Arrival (the Villeneuve film) next week and I want to run a session reading it against Augustine on time in Confessions XI. His picture, roughly: only the present properly exists, but the mind stretches itself out into a threefold present — memory of what's gone, attention to what's here, expectation of what's coming — and his stock example is someone reciting a psalm they know by heart, with the lines already sung draining into memory and the lines to come still held in expectation, whereas God supposedly grasps the whole thing at once without any stretching at all. So here's what I want the group to chew on: is Louise, once she's learned heptapod B, just living inside Augustine's stretched threefold present with an unusually wide span, or is she actually living something like God's eternity — and does the film even let you hold that distinction? A wrinkle I find interesting: Augustine's example is spoken recitation, essentially sequential, but the heptapod logograms are written, circular, and grasped all in one go, which looks far closer to how Augustine says God knows a psalm than to how we recite one. And the film's big emotional beat — Louise choosing to have her daughter already knowing how it ends — seems to require her to act from inside time while seeing it from outside, which Augustine more or less says no creature can do. Where does the film vindicate him, where does it quietly help itself to what he denies, and does Louise's choice dramatise something his theory can't state? British English please, and don't just summarise the plot at us, we all saw it.
