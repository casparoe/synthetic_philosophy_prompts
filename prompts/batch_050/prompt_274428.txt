I'm presenting a paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on AI and moral status, and I'd like you to act as a sharp philosopher of mind who has sat through many such Q&As and build me a question-period prep sheet.

My thesis: the moral status of artificial systems should be assessed neither by substrate nor by behavior — least of all by self-reports, which carry essentially no evidential weight for systems trained on human text, since training can install any report without installing the underlying state. Instead, status comes in degrees and tracks specific functional markers — an integrated self-model, recurrent global information broadcast, and intrinsic (non-instrumental) goal representations with valence-like structure — on the grounds that our best theories of consciousness treat these as constitutive of experience rather than as report-linked surface features. My conclusion is that today's large language models plausibly have negligible moral status, but that near-future systems could cross the threshold without ever telling us.

The audience: roughly half machine-learning researchers with behaviorist instincts, allergic to talk of consciousness, and quick to note that any marker can be fine-tuned in or out of a system; plus a few philosophers (one illusionist who thinks phenomenal consciousness is a introspective illusion, one higher-order theorist, one sympathetic panpsychist) and one animal-welfare scientist who works on invertebrate sentience.

Before drafting, think briefly about which premise of my argument each audience member is most likely to attack, since they will not all go for the same joint. Then give me the eight objections I will actually get from this audience — not the ones a generic philosophy seminar would raise — ordered by expected damage, each phrased the way that particular person would put it, with a two-minute reply and a candid note on where the reply is weakest. Flag which objections I should partly concede rather than fight. End with the question I'm hoping nobody asks, and the best available answer to it.
