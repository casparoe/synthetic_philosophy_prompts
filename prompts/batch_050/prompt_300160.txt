I'm putting together a position statement for a grad seminar I'm teaching next term on the metaphysics of physics, and the deal is that the students get to spend a session tearing it apart. So before I hand it over, I'd like a dry run. Here's where I currently stand on the ontology of quantum particles, roughly in order of how confident I feel:

First, I'm a realist about the quantum state -- the wavefunction is part of the furniture, and I have no truck with hidden-variable programs that promise to restore a comfy classical picture underneath. Second, I take Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles seriously as a metaphysical truth, not just a methodological rule of thumb: if there's no qualitative difference between two things, there aren't two things. Third, I think physics genuinely counts particles -- the helium atom has two electrons, a Bose-Einstein condensate has however many thousand atoms, and that cardinality is a real fact about the world, not an artifact of bookkeeping. Fourth, I'm fully on board with permutation invariance: swapping two same-state bosons doesn't get you a different way the world could be, it gets you the very same state, full stop. Fifth, I want nothing to do with haecceities or primitive thisness -- there's no fact about which particle is which beyond their properties and relations.

So: does this set hang together? Go through it pair by pair where it matters and give me an audit, not a lecture -- which commitments actually pull against each other, which only look like they do but dissolve once you're careful, and for each real tension, how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be. I'd also like to know which single change would buy me the most consistency for the least philosophical cost. One constraint: I care a lot about the counting commitment (that's doing real work in how I teach this stuff), so if a fix requires giving that up, I want it flagged as expensive even if it's technically clean. And please be honest if you think two of my commitments are only compatible via some maneuver I havent mentioned -- I'd rather hear that now than from a sharp student in week three.
