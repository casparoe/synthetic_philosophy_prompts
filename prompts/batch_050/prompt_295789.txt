So I'm in my high school's debate/current events club and next week I'm presenting a proposal I've been working on for like a month. Background: last semester we had a whole mess where a guest speaker at a school event used his slot to argue that certain students (it was about immigration status, I won't get into it) shouldn't get the same participation rights as everyone else, and the club spent three meetings fighting about whether "letting all sides speak" means we have to platform people whose position is literally that other people shouldn't get a platform.

My proposal: the club adopts a rule where the only views that can get you excluded from speaking slots are views that, if they won, would end open debate itself -- like arguing some group of students shouldn't be allowed to speak or vote in club stuff. Merely offensive or unpopular opinions are still fine. My justification is basically a reciprocity thing: tolerance is a contract, not a suicide pact, you only get its protection if you'd extend it to others. I'm borrowing the general shape of this from a philosopher we read a short excerpt of (Popper, the paradox of tolerance), but the "exit-threatening vs. merely offensive" distinction is my own and I'm kind of proud of it.

The audience: about 20 club members, including two guys who are hardcore free-speech absolutists (one of them has read On Liberty and brings it up constantly), our faculty advisor who's a civics teacher and keeps saying school clubs aren't bound by the First Amendment but should act like they are, a few members who are conflict-averse and just want the drama to stop, and a couple kids from the group that got targeted last semester who think my rule doesn't go far enough.

Prep me for the Q&A. Give me like 8 objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of these specific people would actually phrase them (the Mill guy talks different than the civics teacher), how much damage each one does to the proposal, what I should concede up front vs hold firm on, and a short reply for each with a note on where the reply is weak. I especially need to know if my exit-threatening/offensive distinction actually survives scrutiny or if it collapses into "we ban views we really hate." Also tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks -- I suspect it has something to do with who gets to decide what counts as exit-threatening, since the answer is... me and the officers. Definately include that one if you think it's as bad as I fear.
