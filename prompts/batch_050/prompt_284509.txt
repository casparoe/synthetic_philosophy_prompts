I'm a psychology grad student and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for, wondering if its an established thing. Say a diagnosis gets defined based on how untreated people look. Once the label exists, people who get diagnosed also get treatment, coping strategies, accomodations, whatever -- and over time the diagnosed population stops looking like the original picture. Researchers then study the diagnosed population (it's who you can recruit), notice the condition "presents milder than thought," and the criteria get revised to match the managed version. End result: someone walking in today with the exact presentation the diagnosis was built on no longer fits the criteria, or gets shunted to some new label. The net effect is the diagnosis drifts toward describing the *treated* version of the condition and loses the untreated one.

I know this has to be related to Hacking's looping effect, where classifying people changes the classified, but from what I can tell the looping effect is usually told as the classification *creating* or amplifying the phenomenon, not as treatment-induced drift hollowing out the original category. And its not quite concept creep either, since the category isn't expanding on moral grounds, its sliding sideways.

Does this specific feedback loop have a name? If it's just a subtype of the looping effect, explain how it fits Hacking's account and what makes this direction distinctive. If nothing established covers it, coin a term, define it precisely, and give me a couple of boundary cases -- situations that look similar but shouldn't count.
