'Wittgenstein's private language argument goes like this. Imagine someone who decides to keep a diary of a particular inner sensation, writing "S" in it every time the sensation occurs. Because the sensation is accessible only to him, he has no way of checking that he is using "S" correctly: his memory might be playing tricks on him, and with no external standard available there is no difference between applying "S" rightly and merely seeming to himself to apply it rightly. So a language whose words refer to private sensations is impossible — meaning requires the possibility of public correction. It follows that sensation words get their whole meaning from their connections to observable behavior, and Wittgenstein turns out to be a kind of sophisticated behaviorist: pain-talk is really talk about pain-behavior and the dispositions surrounding it. A further consequence is that a genuinely solitary speaker — say, someone who grew up entirely outside any linguistic community — could not even give a name to their own itches.'
