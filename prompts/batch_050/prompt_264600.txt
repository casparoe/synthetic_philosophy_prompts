So here's something that's been bugging me. On the one hand you have the standard "deadliest events in human history" genre — wikipedia lists, atlases, those youtube videos — where atrocities get ranked purely by death toll. Taiping rebellion above the Holocaust, that kind of thing. Nobody bats an eye, it's presented like ranking rivers by length. On the other hand you have international criminal law, which does almost the opposite move: genocide is treated as the "crime of crimes," specially heinous in a way that larger killings often aren't, and to prove it you don't need a huge body count at all, you need a specific intent to destroy a group *as a group*. Killing two million people for territory can be legally and morally ranked *below* killing eight thousand for who they were.

So we seem to run two totally different ranking practices side by side and switch between them depending on context. What I want to know is: what theory of evil does each practice presuppose? The death-toll list seems to presuppose something like "evil is additive across victims and only the victims matter" — but does it really, or is it just agnostic about intent the way epidemiology is agnostic? And the genocide convention seems to presuppose that targeting a kind of person is worse than targeting the same number of persons — but what exactly makes that true? Is it something about the victims' identity being attacked, something about the perpetrator's state of mind, or something about the group as an entity in its own right that gets destroyed over and above its members?

Also: are the two practices actually inconsistent, or could one coherent theory of evil underwrite both — e.g. are they just measuring different things (harm vs. culpability, or harm vs. symbolic meaning) and the confusion only arises because we use the same word "worst" for both? And where are the practices philosophically underdetermined — which parts would look exactly the same under several rival theories? Finally, pick a side if you can: if you had to redesign one of the two practices to match the other one's implicit theory, which one should give way, and what would the redesigned version actually look like?
