So there's this long-running fight in the ethics of blame that I want to turn into an adversarial collaboration document. On one side, people who think standing to blame is a real thing: a hypocrite who condemns you for exactly what she does herself has lost the right to blame you, even if everything she says is true. On the other side, people who think the whole standing idea is confused or worse: if the blame is accurate, it's warranted, and worrying about who gets to deliver it is just etiquette dressed up as ethics, or worse, a way for wrongdoers to dodge criticism by attacking the critic.

Write the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a serious, good-faith adversarial collaboration. Specifically I want:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims -- things like: the content of blame can be true even when the blamer is compromised; that we can always at least ask about a blamer's motives; that 'you're one to talk' is sometimes a legitimate reply in ordinary conversation even if it doesn't refute anything. Push for agreements that aren't trivial, and flag any that one side only accepts in a weakened form.

2. The precise statements of disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. I suspect one real disagreement is about what blame even is -- whether it's essentially a communicative move within a relationship (where standing could matter the way standing matters for making demands or pressing charges) or whether it's more like a judgment with a feeling attached (where the truth of the judgment is all that could ever matter). If that's the load-bearing split, say so.

3. The cruxes, and for each one a case or test both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Some cases you might use or improve on: the reformed hypocrite (someone who used to do the thing, stopped, and now blames); the person who blames while secretly still doing it; the bystander with clean hands vs the fellow wrongdoer; and the institution case -- can a state that commits the very crime it's punishing still have standing to punish, and does whatever you say about individuals carry over or not.

4. A section on which parts of the dispute turn out to be verbal. For instance, both sides might actually agree that hypocritical blame is objectionable but disagree about whether the objection is a matter of the blame being unwarranted or just of the blamer being insufferable. Sort out how much of the debate survives once you stipulate that.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table (disagreement / position A / position B / why it's not merely verbal), then cruxes with predictions. Don't adjudicate at the end -- the document should read as co-authored, not as a referee's report. If it turns out the two camps can't even agree on what standing would be standing *to do*, that itself is a finding worth stating in the doc.
