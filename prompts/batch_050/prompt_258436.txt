I am on a small interdisciplinary committee (two philosophers, a behavioural economist, a machine-learning researcher) charged with writing the requirements document for what we are internally calling a 'preference idealisation engine' — a system intended to advise people on major life decisions (careers, medical treatment, relocation) not by doing what they currently ask, but by estimating what they would endorse under better conditions: more information, more reflection, fewer cognitive errors. The obvious worry, which came up in our first meeting, is that every existing family of proposals — full-information accounts, ideal-advisor accounts, coherence-or-laundering approaches, and 'extrapolated volition'-style aggregation schemes — seems to smuggle in a substantive view of the good while claiming merely to formalise the person's own will. Before we let anyone argue for their favourite proposal, I want the specification written down first.

Please write the desiderata that any adequate theory of idealised preference must meet, treating each desideratum as a requirement with the case or consideration that motivates it, not as a bullet point. At minimum, the list should reckon with the following tensions, and you should add any I have missed: the theory must do some work beyond reporting current desires, yet its authority must still be traceable to the person rather than to the theorist; it must handle adaptive preferences (the person who has learned not to want what they cannot have) without licensing wholesale disregard of what people say they want; it must say something sensible about people whose deepest commitments include not wanting to be idealised at all, or whose endorsement of the idealisation procedure itself is contingent and fragile; it must cope with the fact that extrapolation is underdetermined — many different 'better informed, better reasoned' versions of me are reachable, and they do not agree; and it must remain implementable enough that an engineered system could approximate it without the implementers' values doing the real work. For each desideratum, state whether you take it to be non-negotiable for any theory in this space or a parochial demand of one tradition, and give the reason.

The part I care most about: identify any subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain why the impossibility is structural rather than merely a matter of cleverness not yet applied. Then, briefly, indicate which single requirement does the most eliminative work against the four families mentioned above — the requirement that, if adopted, knocks out the largest share of the existing literature. Keep any scoring of the four families to a paragraph at most; the specification itself is the deliverable.
