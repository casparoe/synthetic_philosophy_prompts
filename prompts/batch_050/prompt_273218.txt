I'm in my final year of high school, but I've been programming since middle school, and the vocabulary of git — commits, content-addressed hashes, parent pointers, the commit DAG, branching, merging, rebasing, amending, cherry-picking — is honestly more natural to me than most of the vocabulary philosophers use. In an elective on metaphysics we recently covered origin essentialism, the thesis associated with Kripke that a person's origins are essential to their identity: I could not have come from a different sperm and egg, and any individual who had, however similar to me they ended up being, would simply be someone else. I want to know how much of this thesis and the standard arguments around it can be restated in the vocabulary I actually think in.

So: translate origin essentialism into git terms as faithfully as you can. Treat an individual as something like a commit, its qualitative character as the tree, its origins as the parent hashes, and a possible course of life as a chain of descendants, adjusting the mapping wherever you think my suggested correspondences are off. Use rebasing and amending to model claims of the form 'I could have had a different past than I do,' and cherry-picking to model 'same qualitative content, different origin.' Along the way, work through these test cases explicitly rather than in the abstract: (1) the case philosophers lead with — same egg, different sperm, or entirely different parents; (2) a numerically different but genetically identical sperm — as I understand content-addressing, git recognizes no distinction between two objects with identical content, so if this case cannot be expressed in the framework, say so directly instead of forcing it; (3) whether 'I could have had a very different life from the one I've had' survives as a claim about long descendant chains sharing a root; (4) identical twins, who share both parents.

Then give me the residue report: which parts of the thesis and the usual considerations for and against it translate cleanly, which you had to distort to fit, and which have no expression in git vocabulary at all. For everything in the last category, I want a verdict: is the gap a defect of my framework — say, that commit identity is a stipulated convention baked into a hash function, or that git has no way to represent numerically distinct but qualitatively identical objects — or is it evidence that the metaphysical claim contains less than it appears to, that once you have said what individuates the commit there is nothing left to say? One caveat: please keep the translation honest. Don't quietly build the thesis into the framework and then present it as a translation.

For context on where I'm coming from: my suspicion going in is that origin essentialism is ultimately just a decision about what we are willing to call the same individual, in the way that commit identity is a decision about what goes into the hash. Tell me whether the translation exercise supports that suspicion or undermines it.
