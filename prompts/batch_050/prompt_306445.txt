So I've been going down a rabbit hole lately about memes, the internet kind, and this idea people repeat that culture evolves the same way biology does. The standard story I keep hearing is: memes are like genes, they copy themselves from brain to brain (or screen to screen), the catchy ones spread and the boring ones die, and random mistakes in copying give the process new material to select from. Ok, fine.

But the more I think about it the more it seems like internet memes break exactly the rules that story needs. Two things bug me.

First: when a new version of a meme shows up, it's usually not a random copying error like a mutated gene is supposed to be. Somebody sat there and edited it. They took the drake meme or the distracted boyfriend or whatever and deliberately rewrote the caption because they thought the new version would land better. That's the opposite of random. It's aimed. I thought the whole point of Darwinian evolution is that variation is blind and selection does all the steering -- giraffes can't try to have longer-necked babies. So if people steer their memes on purpose, is meme evolution actually Darwinian at all, or is it that other thing, Lamarkian or however you spell it? Or is there some third option where it's "Darwinian enough" and the steering doesn't matter?

Second: a gene gets copied. A meme often just... doesn't. Half the time nobody sees the original, they see a screenshot of a screenshot with someone's comment layered on it, or they just hear the format described and remake it from scratch in their own words. There's no clean copying event, and no two instances are really identical the way I picture gene copies being identical. But I thought high-fidelity copying was load-bearing in this theory -- the thing has to be a "replicator" that actually replicates. If there's mostly reconstruction instead of copying, what's left of the replicator idea?

So here's what I actually want explained: can the standard Darwinian theory of culture handle memes as they really work -- deliberate editing, lousy copying -- or do you have to quietly swap in a different picture, one where a meme is less like a gene being copied and more like a recipe people keep re-cooking from memory? And if you make that swap, what does it cost? Is there anything the gene analogy explained that the recipe picture can't, or the other way around? Don't just tell me "the analogy is imperfect," I want to know which specific parts of it break, and whether the theory can be patched or has to be replaced.
