I am a retired experimental physicist who spent forty years in the laboratory and has only lately taken up philosophy in earnest, and I find myself stuck on the pessimistic meta-induction. My difficulty is not with the argument's history but with the state of play: every realist response I read seems to answer a slightly different question from the one the induction poses, and I cannot tell whether that is evasion or legitimate reframing.

The proximate cause of my asking is a chapter I have just finished. In "The Theories of Modern Physics," the tenth chapter of his Science and Hypothesis (1902), Henri Poincaré observes, in substance, that when Fresnel's optics was absorbed into Maxwell's electrodynamics, the luminiferous ether — the very stuff the theory was ostensibly about — was discarded, while Fresnel's differential equations and the relations among optical quantities they express were carried over intact. His suggestion is that what survives scientific revolutions is a theory's form, its mathematical structure, rather than the entities it posits. Before I decide whether this move, or any of its modern descendants, succeeds, I want to know what I should be demanding of it.

So write the desiderata: what any adequate realist response to the pessimistic meta-induction must deliver, with the consideration or historical case behind each requirement. At minimum, address which theories the response must treat as continuations rather than refutations (Fresnel to Maxwell, Newtonian gravitation to general relativity) and which as genuine ruptures (phlogiston to oxygen, caloric to kinetic theory), and what principled criterion draws that line; how it must account for the predictive success of theories we now take to be radically false without simply defining success as approximate truth and thereby begging the question; and how it must preserve the no-miracles intuition that made realism attractive in the first place. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable for anyone who wishes to remain a realist and which are the preferences of particular camps. If some subset of the requirements looks jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect the demand to explain the success of discarded theories sits poorly with the demand to retain truth as the explanation of success — say so plainly, since that is the part I most want to see.

Then, briefly and without a formal scorecard: how does the Poincaré-style proposal that only structure survives fare against the list, and does any rival (selective realism about well-deployed posits, or the anti-realist who simply declines one of the desiderata) do better by rejecting a requirement rather than meeting it? Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; no reading lists or citations, please. Kindly write in American English.
