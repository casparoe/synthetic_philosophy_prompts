I'm putting together the final exam for my upper-level seminar on self-knowledge and assertion. Write one essay question on Moore's paradox that students get 75 minutes for, which can't be answered by just reciting that "p and I don't believe that p" is weird. Specifically, I want the question to force them to locate where the absurdity lives: it should give them the plain fact that both conjuncts can be true at once (it can be raining while I don't believe it is), plus two variants to chew on -- the omissive form versus the commissive form ("p but I believe that not-p"), and the same sentence moved into the third person or past tense ("it was raining, but she didn't believe it"), which is perfectly fine -- and require them to explain why the paradox vanishes in those cases but not in the first-person present-tense one, and whether the problem is in what's said, in the saying of it, or in the believing itself. Then give a model answer (about 600 words) and a marking scheme with five must-hit points worth 4 marks each out of 20, with a one-line note per point on how students typically botch it -- e.g. treating the paradox as a plain contradiction, or claiming the commissive and omissive forms are equally bad without argument.
