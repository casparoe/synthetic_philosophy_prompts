Hey, can you read this mini essay idea and tear it apart a bit? I’m in high school and got obsessed with the nonidentity problem after our ethics club talked about climate stuff, but I wanted a case that isn’t just the usual have-kids-later or pollute-and-people-exist examples. My draft thesis is: sometimes an act is wrong even when every actual future person has a life worth living and would not have existed without the act, because the wrong is in how the act treats a possible lineage as a replaceable resource. The example I made up is a town that can switch from dirty generators to clean ones now, or wait twenty years for a cheaper fusion plant. Waiting creates a different set of future kids because parents time pregnancies around jobs, blackouts, asthma meds, etc., but waiting also makes the town richer later so the eventual kids have awesome hospitals and say they’re glad things happened exactly this way. I argue waiting can still be wrong if officials secretly chose it mainly because a model predicted the future population would be more genetically homogeneous and easier to govern, not because of the hospital benefits. My slogan is that the nonidentity block shows you cannot complain as the specific child, but it does not erase the standpoint of the public that had to decide which possible children got a ticket into existence.

The part I’m least sure about is my invented distinction between existence-gating reasons and existence-tinting reasons. Existence-gating reasons are about whether any people get to exist at all, like preventing a catastrophe. Existence-tinting reasons are about which roughly similar people get to exist, like nudging conception timing so the future population skews calmer, smarter, more loyal, or whatever. I claim existence-tinting reasons are especially creepy when hidden, because they bypass consent in a sneaky way: nobody is harmed relative to not existing, but the decision still expresses a ranking over possible persons, and later people may feel gratitude that makes the ranking look fine retroactively. A friend said this is just regular bad motivation plus maybe discrimination, not a new wrong, because if the fusion delay really is better for every actual future person and worse for none, then only the officials attitude is bad. I tried to reply that attitudes can change what an act is, like the same push can be rescue, shove, or bullying depending on aim, but I definately worry I’m smuggling in intent to dodge the nonidentity math.

Could you comment specifically on three things: whether my existence-gating vs existence-tinting distinction is real or just vibes, what the strongest counterexample would be where existence-tinting seems obviously fine or obviously required, and whether the essay accidentally assumes a too-simple picture where identity is fixed by exact conception timing. Please be picky about accuracy and flag any place where I’m basically saying a person is harmed by not being someone else, which sounds spooky. Also tell me if the public standpoint idea is interesting enough to keep or if I should cut it and make the essay about hidden motives, democratic accountability, and gratitude being manufactured by the very choice being judged.
