Our reading group has two people who've been arguing in circles for months: a Boethian and a defender of simple foreknowledge. The Boethian says God is outside time entirely, so what we call foreknowledge is really God timelessly taking in all of history at once -- the traditional image is watching the whole road from a hilltop instead of walking it. The simple-foreknowledge defender says God is in time and just straight-up knows what will happen, and that this was never a threat to freedom in the first place, since knowing something doesn't make it happen.

Write the joint statement these two could actually both sign. Specifically:

1. A numbered list of claims both accept -- about what divine knowledge of the future would have to involve, what the freedom problem is actually supposed to be, and which popular "solutions" both sides agree are dodges.
2. A disagreements table where each row is one precise point of disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of there own position.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would genuinely move at least one side. For each crux, give a case or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, with each side's verdict on the case written down in advance.

Some candidate cruxes, which you can use or push back on: whether a timeless being could even know tensed facts (like which day is today); whether the fixity worry is really about the pastness of God's belief or about the mere fact that God believes it; and whether timeless knowledge is actually *worse* for freedom than ordinary foreknowledge rather than better, as the Boethian assumes. For test cases, I'm curious whether something like a time traveller from the future who knows what you'll do tomorrow isolates anything the two sides disagree about -- if it doesn't, say so and propose a better case.

Two ground rules for the document. First, both parties stipulate that God knows everything and that humans are genuinely free, so neither is allowed to "solve" anything by giving up one of those -- if either side's view secretly requires doing so, the statement should flag it rather than hide it. Second, no appeals to scripture or named authorities, just arguments and cases both sides can evaluate directly. And if part of their dispute turns out to be verbal -- say, they mean different things by "the future" -- identify exactly which part and why.
