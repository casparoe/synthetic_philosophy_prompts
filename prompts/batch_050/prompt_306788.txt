I work as a software engineer, and a few colleagues and I run a small reading group on the side -- most recently we've been arguing about luck egalitarianism after someone raised it during a debate over how our company should think about compensating employees for circumstances outside their control (health shocks, layoffs, bad markets) versus outcomes they walked into with open eyes. I don't have any philosophy training, but I've picked up the rough shape of the view: the idea that inequalities traceable to brute luck -- things nobody chose -- are objectionable in a way that inequalities traceable to option luck -- deliberate gambles taken against known odds -- are not, and that a just distribution is one where no one is worse off than others through no fault or choice of their own.

For our last meeting I'd like to open with a villanelle that takes the view seriously rather than satirizing it. I'm asking for the specific form because I think the material fits it, and I'd like you to use the form structurally, not just decoratively:

- One refrain line should state the brute-luck side (the person born into the wrong body, the wrong country, the wrong decade, who did nothing to deserve their disadvantage), and the other refrain should state the option-luck side (the person who bet and lost, and whom the theory is tempted to leave where they fell).
- Because a villanelle's refrains keep returning, I'd like the repetitions to drift: by the final quatrain, the two refrains should be noticeably harder to tell apart than they were in the opening tercets. The point is to enact, in the form itself, the recurring criticism that the line between what we chose and what merely happened to us won't hold still -- that every gamble was entered from a starting position nobody chose, and every stroke of luck was met with some response that was partly ours.
- Somewhere in the middle stanzas, please give the harshness objection its due: the person lying in the wreck of their own freely chosen risk, whom a strict version of the view seems to abandon. Don't resolve it; the poem should end with the theory still intact but visibly under strain, not refuted and not vindicated.

Keep the diction concrete -- actual gambles, actual accidents, actual inheritances rather than abstractions like fortune and desert -- and make sure the meter and the ABA rhyme scheme are genuinely observed rather than approximated. I'd rather have one clean nineteen-line villanelle than anything looser or longer.
