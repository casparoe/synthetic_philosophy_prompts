Like last week someone pushed the classic objection -- if everything is empty of inherent existence, then emptiness itself must be empty of inherent existence, so doesn't the whole view eat itself? -- and he just goes 'yes, emptiness is empty, that's the point, not a problem.' And when someone else worried the view is really just nihilism in disguise, same answer. It feels like he's doing something real with it, not just vibing, but I genuinly can't tell what the claim even is.

Can you lay out the different precise things 'emptiness is empty' could be saying? I'm imagining a menu, from the most deflationary reading up to the most radical one. Like, is it just the trivial point that emptiness falls under 'everything' so the universal claim applies to it? Is it the more specific Madhyamaka point that emptiness lacks svabhava in the same way as tables and chairs, and that this is supposed to stop you turning emptiness into a new Absolute? Is it a pragmatic/therapeutic claim, i.e. emptiness is a tool you let go of once it's done its job, not a thesis to hold? Or is it something weirder, like the view isn't a proposition at all so it can't be self-refuting (and how does that interact with the tetralemma, where the middle way supposedly rejects all four options about a thing's existence)? For each reading on the menu, tell me: is it a platitude, is it substantive and defensible, or is it actually confused? And crucially -- which readings, if any, would actually get my reading-group guy the two moves he was making, namely defusing the self-application objection AND rebutting the nihilism charge? My suspicion is that the reading that handles the first doesn't handle the second, and he was sliding between them, but I might be wrong about that. Also flag if one of the readings collapses Madhyamaka into something its opponents would happily accept, since that seems like the failure mode he'd care about most.
