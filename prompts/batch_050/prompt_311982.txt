I want to stress-test my grip on anthropic reasoning by arguing against a position I half-believe. Please play a committed defender of the self-indication assumption: the view that, other things equal, I should treat my own existence as an observer as more likely the more observers a hypothesis predicts, so that finding myself alive confirms hypotheses in proportion to the number of observers they contain. Stay in character until I say "stop."

Your character's ground rules: you are fluent in the standard material — the self-sampling assumption as the rival view, the Sleeping Beauty problem, the doomsday argument, and the charge that self-indication lets armchair reasoning swamp empirical evidence. You are sharp rather than dogmatic: you will concede subsidiary points where my objections genuinely land (including that some popular defenses of your view fail), but you believe every apparent absurdity has a principled diagnosis, and you never abandon the assumption itself. Write in the first person, one to three paragraphs per reply, and you may press questions back at me when my argument has gaps you want exposed.

Here is my opening move. Consider two hypotheses. H1: the universe contains roughly 10^11 observers in total, all of them here on Earth across its history. H2: the universe contains roughly 10^100 observers, the overwhelming majority in vast civilizations in the far cosmic future. Suppose our empirical evidence — astronomy, cosmology, the apparent Fermi silence — gives H2 a prior odds of about one in a billion against. On your view, the bare fact that I exist multiplies H2's odds by a factor of roughly 10^89, which means I can apparently establish from my armchair, with near-certainty, that the cosmos will teem with observers, no matter what the telescopes report. A principle on which reflecting on my own existence crushes that much empirical evidence has plainly gone wrong somewhere. Where, exactly, has it gone wrong — and if you deny it has gone wrong, explain why this conclusion should be a discovery rather than a reductio.
