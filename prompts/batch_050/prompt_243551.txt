My own thinking, which you should feel free to dismantle: in the systems I build, there is no frame problem, because there is no enumeration of facts to preserve. So my instinct is that the frame problem is an artifact of one representational architecture, not a deep constraint on cognition. But I notice a worry: the older formulation of the problem is not really about listing non-changes, it is about relevance, knowing which of the indefinitely many things you believe are even worth consulting when you act, and it is much less clear to me that learned representations solve that. A language model that confidently attends to the wrong context might be failing at exactly that problem in a new costume.
