Earlier this year I was asked to translate a short poem from my grandmother's native language into English, to be read at her memorial, and the family wanted the English version to keep the original's rhyme scheme and line length so it would still feel like her poem. I agreed, and I have been uneasy about the result ever since, for a reason I cannot find a name for.

Several times, keeping the form forced me to add things that simply are not in the original. In one stanza the rhyme I needed existed only for a word meaning something like harbor, so my version has her speaker standing at a harbor, where the original has her standing on a road with no harbor anywhere in the poem. Elsewhere I quietly dropped a detail because nothing with the right syllable count could carry it. What unsettles me is what happened afterward: relatives who cannot read the original have begun quoting my harbor back to me as evidence of what my grandmother felt about departure, and one cousin is writing a eulogy organized around the image. Nobody mentions the dropped detail, because nobody can see it. The poem they are interpreting is, in part, a record of my rhyme constraints, but every trace of that fact is invisible to them, and the invented content has been fully credited to her.

I know translation theory discusses related things: the invisibility of the translator, domesticating versus foreignizing a text, compensation for losses elsewhere. But as far as I can tell these all concern style, fluency, or deliberate trade-offs, whereas what I am describing is narrower: semantic content that owes its existence purely to a formal constraint, and that readers then mistake for the original author's voice, often building interpretations on it. Is there an established term for this specific thing? If so, how does it differ from those neighboring concepts? And if not, what would you call it, how would you define it, and where would you draw its boundaries -- for instance, does it still apply when the constraint is meter alone, or when the invention happens to be true to the author's known life?
