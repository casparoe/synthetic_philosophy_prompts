Write a short story related to the White Horse Dialogue of the School of Names.

The story should engage the following passage from the "Báimǎ lùn" (White Horse Dialogue), the second chapter of the Gongsun Longzi, a Warring States period text attributed to Gongsun Long (English rendering as given in the Wikipedia article "White Horse Dialogue"; the passage does not count toward the story's length):

"'Horse' is what designates shape. 'White' is what designates color. What designates color is not what designates shape. Therefore I say: a white horse is not a horse."

"If one seeks a horse, yellow and black horses may all be delivered. If one seeks a white horse, yellow and black horses may not be delivered. Suppose a white horse were [just] a horse. Then what is sought [in the two cases] would be the same. If what is sought were the same, then 'white' would not differ from 'horse.' If what is sought does not differ, how is it that yellow and black horses are acceptable [in one case] and unacceptable [in the other]? That acceptable and unacceptable are mutually opposed is clear. Therefore yellow and black horses are the same in that they can answer to 'there is a horse,' but cannot answer to 'there is a white horse.' This is a white horse's not being a horse: it is certain."

Requirements:

- Length: 1,200–1,600 words.
- Setting: transposed, not historical. Do not set the story in ancient China or in a philosophy classroom, and do not retell the traditional anecdote of the border pass. Choose a setting in which classificatory language carries concrete practical stakes -- for example, a licensing bureau, an insurance tribunal, a cargo manifest inspection, a zoning dispute, an inheritance settlement, or a far-future habitat registry -- and in which a written rule refers to "horses" while someone stands to gain or lose depending on whether a white horse counts as one.
- Structure: the plot must turn on the logic of the quoted passage, in particular the Advocate's test of what may be "delivered" when a horse is sought versus when a white horse is sought. Stage at least one scene in which a character deploys this reasoning to win a practical point, and a later scene in which another character demonstrates what that victory costs -- the way the same reasoning, consistently applied, would unravel ordinary dealings with terms like "horse."
- Theme: the story should embody, without naming, the two readings of "is not" that the dialogue plays upon: non-identity (a white horse is not the same thing as a horse) versus exclusion from a class (a white horse is not a member of the horses). One character should be most persuasive when heard in the first way; the other, in the second.
- Constraints: no character may mention Gongsun Long, the School of Names, China, or the word "paradox"; the quoted passage may not be recited or alluded to verbatim within the story itself. You may, however, place one or both parts of the passage as an epigraph at the head of the story.
- Ending: close on a scene, not a verdict. The narrative should leave the reader feeling both the genuine insight and the cheat in the winning argument, without the narrator adjudicating between the two readings.
