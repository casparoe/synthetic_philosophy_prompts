The dispute between Humeans and anti-Humeans about laws of nature has been running for decades with essentially fixed positions: one side holds that the laws are nothing over and above the best systematization of the distribution of local qualities across spacetime, while the other insists that the laws must in some sense govern, necessitate, or be grounded in the dispositional essences of properties, on pain of failing to explain the very regularities the Humean takes them merely to summarize. My tentative diagnosis is that the standoff is not really about laws at all but about what metaphysical explanation is allowed to rest on: each side's key complaint (that the other leaves regularities mysterious, or posits idle necessary connections) presupposes the standards of explanation the other rejects. Is that right, or is there a substantive disagreement underneath — about modal facts, about what could count as evidence, about the direction of explanation between laws and the mosaic — that could in principle be settled? Please diagnose the structure of the disagreement rather than adjudicate it, engage with what each side actually argues, and say what, if anything, would count as progress: a conceptual distinction, an argument form neither side has tried, or a reason to think the impasse is permanent.
