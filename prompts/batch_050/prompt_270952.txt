So there's a line that gets wheeled out in basically every decision theory thread where blackmail or extortion comes up, and it came out again yesterday in a thread about a guy who got an anonymous email claiming to have compromising screenshots and demanding like $300 in bitcoin or they go to his employer. Someone said "it's $300, just pay it, the expected disutility math isn't close," and within a few comments somebody dropped "giving in to extortion is how you get more extortion" and treated that as game over. It got upvoted a ton. Nobody spelled out what it actually means.

The thing is, in that particular case the standard reading seems to just... not apply? Nobody can observe whether he paid. The extortionist is a stranger, not an institution that keeps a list of marks, and there's no reputation to protect because the payment is untraceable-ish and the victim sure isn't telling anyone. So "you'll get more extortion" through what channel, exactly?

Here's what I want: lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could be expressing, from weakest to strongest. I can gesture at some candidate readings but I want them stated properly and I probably haven't thought of all of them:

1. an empirical/causal claim about signaling (paying marks you as a payer, word gets around, you get targeted again)
2. a game-theoretic claim about precommitment (being *known* as someone who refuses prevents threats from being made, ex ante)
3. some kind of acausal/logical claim where refusing now is somehow correlated with living in a world where fewer extortion attempts get made at you in the first place, even when no one can ever find out what you did
4. maybe it's just a disguised normative claim -- paying is *wrong*, full stop, and the predictive packaging is decoration

For each reading: state it precisely, say whether it's a platitude, substantive-and-plausible, or actually false, and crucially say whether it would justify refusing to pay *in the anonymous one-shot case*, since that seems like the stress test that separates them. Also -- and this is the part I keep going back and forth on -- is it possible that "never pay extortion" is a kind of surrogate goal? I.e. a rule we imported wholesale from iterated/reputational contexts where it's genuinely adaptive, and we're now applying it in a context where the original point has been amputated and nobody noticed. If that's what reading 1 and 2 amount to when misapplied to one-shot cases, say so.

Finally, which reading do the people who repeat the slogan in those threads probably *mean*? My suspicion is the slogan survives on equivocation between the mundane readings and the exotic one, and I'd like to know if that suspicion is fair or if I'm being uncharitable.

Give me the readings as a numbered list I can paste into the thread. Then at the very end, last line, give me \answer{N} where N is the number of the reading that best justifies refusing to pay in the anonymous one-shot case, or \answer{none} if none of them hold up there.
