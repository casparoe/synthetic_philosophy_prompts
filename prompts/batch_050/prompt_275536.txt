I curate functional annotations for a public genome database. My six annotators each write a short statement of every gene product's function, and we disagree in a systematic way I want to eliminate with a written procedure. Roughly half the team uses "function" for whatever a molecule stably does in the organism: it binds this ion, catalyzes this step, scaffolds that complex. The other half reserves "function" for what the molecule is there in order to do, cashed out through evolutionary history: a trait's function is whichever of its effects explains why the underlying sequence was retained rather than lost. I understand this tracks a standing distinction in the philosophy of biology between causal-role and etiological or selected-effect accounts of function, and I want that distinction operationalized, not explained.

Constraints: annotators process about thirty entries a week, have MSc-level training, and will not read philosophy. Annotations feed automated pipelines, so consistency across annotators and across years matters more than nuance. A senior curator reviews only flagged entries. Depositing researchers describe functions in their own inconsistent idioms, so the procedure cannot simply copy the wording of source papers.

Give me a procedure the team can follow: a sequence of tests or questions applied to each candidate function claim, stating which philosophical distinctions must survive the simplification and which can safely be dropped, and marking the steps where judgment cannot be eliminated and a flag to the senior curator is mandatory. It must settle, with a worked example of each, at least these cases: a kinase that robustly phosphorylates a substrate in vitro and in vivo but whose knockout is phenotypically indistinguishable from wild type; an effect that is reliable and clinically useful, the way heart sounds are, without being why the structure exists; a recently duplicated gene with no established selection history that currently does something beneficial; a gene characterized only through disease association, where the proposed function is inferred from what goes wrong when it is broken; and a promiscuous enzyme that catalyzes several reactions, where the question is which, if any, count as its function. Include one worked case where the procedure delivers an annotation my team will dislike, and say plainly what the procedure cannot decide. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
