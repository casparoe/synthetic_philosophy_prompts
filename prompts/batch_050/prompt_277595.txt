I'm preparing a position statement for a graduate seminar on the ethics of deception and would like a consistency check before I commit to it. Here is what I currently hold:

(a) Lying is morally worse than merely misleading, even when the false belief produced in the hearer is exactly the same.
(b) What makes deception wrong, when it is wrong, is the harm it does to the deceived person -- the damage to their autonomy and to the trust their judgement rests on -- rather than anything about the speaker's choice of words.
(c) When a hearer draws a false inference from something literally true, the inference is the hearer's own doing, and so the responsibility for it falls on the hearer rather than the speaker.
(d) Faced with the murderer at the door, I would feel no guilt in answering with a carefully chosen literal truth designed to send him the wrong way.
(e) Breaking an explicit promise is far worse than making a misleading statement one never strictly committed oneself to.

Please audit this set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and in each case what would have to give and at what cost. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and say which single revision would restore consistency most cheaply. Do not assume I am attached to any particular commitment.

At the very end, give a last line beginning "Verdict:" stating whether the set is jointly tenable as it stands and, if not, the commitment to revise.
