So I fell down a rabbit hole about this question of whether I could have been born from a different sperm and egg. Like, obviously if my parents had conceived a month later it would've been a different sperm and a different egg, but would that kid still be ME, just with slightly different genes, or would it be a totally different person and I'd simply never have existed? My gut says the latter, and apparently Kripke argued something like this (origins are essential to who you are), but then I started reading summaries of the back-and-forth in the literature and I can't figure out why it's still going after fifty years.

Here's what I mean. As far as I can tell the two sides basically agree on all the ordinary facts: they agree a sperm and egg can fuse into an embryo, they agree you can tweak one gene and get a different-looking person, they agree twin cases and adoption cases work how they work. And then one side goes "so clearly you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg" and the other side goes "so clearly you could have, at least from a nearby one" and... that's it? Nobody has a new argument, they just keep re-stating the intuition with more elaborate thought experiments (slight variations on the gametes, gradual replacement of genetic material, that kind of thing).

My own hypothesis, which might be totally off, is that this isn't really a disagreement about people at all. I think it's a disagreement about what "could have" is even doing in these questions. Like, one side treats possibility as constrained by where a thing actually started, and the other side treats it as constrained by how similar a scenario is, and once you've picked your rule the answer about sperm and eggs just falls out for free. So the debate LOOKS like it's about origins but it's actually about which notion of possibility we should use, and there's no fact of the matter about that, so it can't end. Kind of like two people arguing about whether a tomato is a fruit where they actually disagree about whether we're doing botany or cooking.

But I'm not confident in that, because the people involved clearly think there's a real question here and they're smarter than me. So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a genuine metaphysical question with a right answer that we just can't settle from the armchair? A verbal dispute where both sides are using "could have been me" in different senses? Two different questions getting squashed under one word ("identity")? Or is there some hidden empirical hitch, like the vagueness about which cell counts as "the same egg" or where a person begins, that's doing all the work and nobody wants to admit it?

Please give me a diagnosis with actual reasons, not just a rundown of the positions, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- like, is there a distinction that dissolves it, an argument form that would settle it, or should we just accept that some modal questions have no answer and move on? And be honest if you think my tomato comparison is wrong and this is a real substantive question after all.

Answer in American English please.
