Construct a thought experiment in which a single act of clientelism counts as corrupt on an impartiality-based account of corruption — corruption as the violation of the impartiality norms that constitute public institutions — but does not count as corrupt on the standard public-office account — corruption as the abuse of entrusted power for private gain. The purpose of the case is to adjudicate between these two accounts as analyses of the ordinary concept of corruption, so it fails if the verdicts are merely asserted rather than shown to fall out of each account.

Constraints on the scenario: it should be one or two paragraphs, set in an ordinary functioning democracy, not a failed state or a caricature of machine politics; the act must be legal and must use discretion the official genuinely possesses; the official must gain nothing personally — no money, no office, no reputation — and you must stipulate this within the story rather than merely leaving it unstated; and the people who benefit from the act must be genuinely deserving by some defensible criterion, so the case cannot be brushed aside as an official simply indulging the undeserving.

After the scenario: (1) state the verdict each account delivers and derive it from the account; (2) identify the single feature of the case on which the divergence turns, and say whether the accounts converge again if a small private benefit is introduced — and if so, roughly how small; (3) give the strongest response available to a defender of the private-gain account who wants to deny that the case is a counterexample (for instance, that 'gain' should be read broadly enough to cover the case, or that the act is wrongful but misclassified when called corrupt); (4) reach a verdict on whether the case shows the impartiality account better captures the ordinary concept, or whether it instead shows the concept is disunified.

Say how confident you are in the verdicts you attribute to each account, and single out the point where you are least sure the case does what is claimed for it. Write in British English.
