So I'm in a reading group on criminal law theory and we spent the whole last session on blackmail, and afterwards I wrote down my positions to see if I actually have a coherent view or just a pile of intuitions. Could you audit them? Here's where I stand: (1) Blackmail statutes are justified. Demanding money in exchange for not revealing true information you legally came across should be a crime, and the famous puzzle about this -- that the law seems to criminalize conditionalizing two things that are each perfectly legal on their own, telling the truth and asking for money -- is a puzzle with a solution, not a proof that the law is incoherent. (2) The solution I find most convincing is the triangular or "leverage" story: what's wrong with blackmail is that the blackmailer is cashing in on leverage that morally belongs to someone else. The cheated-on spouse, the defrauded business partners, whoever it is the victim owed disclosure to -- the blackmailer is essentially selling something that isn't theirs to sell, and the deeper wrong runs to that third party, not just to the person being squeezed. (3) I don't think the identity of the threatener matters at all. Whether it's a stranger who stumbled onto the emails or the wronged spouse themselves demanding a payout in exchange for silence, it's the same act and the law should treat it the same. (4) Threatening to file a non-frivolous lawsuit unless the other side settles is legitimate hard bargaining, and it's correct that extortion law leaves it alone. (5) A tabloid that just publishes the same true story for profit does nothing legally wrong -- tasteless maybe, but fine.

My sparring partner in the group claims at least two of these can't be held together, but when he explains I keep thinking he's flagged things that are only apparently in conflict.
