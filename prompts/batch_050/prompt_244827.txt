I work as a software engineer and have no philosophy training, but a friend of mine with a philosophy degree and I have an ongoing argument about thought experiments. I tend to trust them as cheap test cases for concepts; she thinks most of the famous ones are rigged. Her track record so far:

- Trolley problems: legitimate. She says they cleanly isolate a real distinction between doing and allowing harm.
- The experience machine (would you plug into a machine that gives you a life of perfect simulated pleasure?): legitimate. She says it changed her mind about whether pleasant experience is all that matters.
- Gettier cases (lucky true beliefs): legitimate. She accepts them as showing knowledge isn't just true justified belief.
- Mary's room (a scientist who knows every physical fact about color but has never seen red): dismissed. She says it cheats by stipulating that someone "knows all the physical facts," a state she thinks we can't coherently specify.
- The Chinese room (a person following rules to manipulate Chinese symbols without understanding Chinese): dismissed. She says the intuition comes entirely from picturing one human slowly shuffling cards, not from what's actually stipulated.

Say which rule you think best fits and why. Second, note where the two rules diverge on this case.
