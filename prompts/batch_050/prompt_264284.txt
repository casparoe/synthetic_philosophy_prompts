I'm a retired organic chemist who's been falling down a philosophy rabbit hole ever since I hung up my lab coat, and the problem of the unity of the proposition has gotten under my skin. As I understand it: what glues the constituents of a proposition (say, Socrates and wisdom) together so the result is more than a mere list? If the glue is itself a relation among the constituents, then that relation seems to need gluing in turn, and off we go on Bradley's regress.

My chemist's instinct is that a proposition is like a molecule. A bond between two atoms is not a third atom sitting between them, so it doesn't need further bonds to hold it in place. Maybe the relating relation is "unsaturated" (I gather Frege said something along these lines) or maybe it just isn't a constituent at all, and the regress never gets going.

Here's what I want from you. Take the main live responses to Bradley's regress -- the copula/relation as a non-constituent that somehow unifies, facts as primitive unities whose constituents exist only by abstraction, Frege's unsaturatedness, and biting the bullet that the regress is non-vicious -- plus my molecule idea, and rank all of them, mine included, on a 0-10 scale for how much you'd actually trust each one to dissolve the problem. Give me a few sentences of reasoning per entry.
