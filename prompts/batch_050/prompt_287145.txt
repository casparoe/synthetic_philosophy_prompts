In my work as a journalist covering science and ideas, I recently spent months cultivating a potential source inside a large research institute — someone with documents bearing on a story of real public importance. She has now laid out her terms, and they are stark: she will hand over the material only if I promise, in advance, to protect her identity no matter what happens down the line, including if a court orders me to reveal it and I face personal consequences for refusing. Here's the snag that has been keeping me up at night. Right now, making the promise is easy and obviously worthwhile — without it, I get nothing. But she is shrewd enough to reason one step ahead: she knows that if that court order ever actually arrives, the calculus at that future moment will look very different. Refusing to comply will cost me dearly and gain me nothing — the documents will already be published, the story already told, and nothing I do then can retroactively change any of it. She therefore doubts my promise, and I can hardly blame her, because I doubt it myself.

What fascinates me is that this seems to put me in a strange double bind. If I am the sort of person who will coldly weigh costs and benefits at the moment of decision, my promise today is worthless, and I lose the story. But if I could somehow become — or convincingly appear to be — the sort of person who keeps such promises even when keeping them is, by that future moment's lights, pointlessly self-destructive, then her trust becomes rational and everything works out better for both of us. In other words, it seems as though it would be genuinely advantageous for me to be, in one crucial respect, the kind of person who acts against my own later interests. And yet deliberately cultivating such a disposition feels perverse, almost like engineering a flaw in my own reasoning.

So here is what I'd like you to argue, taking a definite position rather than surveying options: should I try to make myself into that kind of person, and would doing so be rational? Please address directly whether it can be rational to adopt a firm policy of honoring commitments even in cases where, when the moment comes, honoring them serves no purpose, and whether the future version of me facing the court order should reason from the standpoint of that moment or from the standpoint of the promise that got me the story. Ground your argument in the structure of the situation itself rather than in generic advice about journalistic ethics or professional codes, since I want to understand whether the puzzle here is really about morality at all, or about something odder in the nature of rational decision-making across time.
