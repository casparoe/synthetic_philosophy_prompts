So I'm putting together my "epistemic profile" for a discord reading group (mostly SSC/LW-adjacent people) and I want someone to check it for consistency before I post it, because I have a feeling something in here doesn't add up but I can't see where. Here's where I've landed on politics/polarization stuff:

(1) I think most political disagreement is asymmetric, i.e. it's not "both sides equally tribal," the other side is on average more tribal, more motivated-reasoning-y, and less truth-seeking than my side. (I know everyone thinks this, but I think it's actually true in my case.)

(2) I'm pro-steelmanning: when I engage with an opposing argument I should respond to the strongest version of it, even if nobody on the other side has actually stated it that well.

(3) I curate my information diet pretty aggressively. I mute/unfollow/block people who repeatedly make bad arguments, and I think this is epistemically healthy and not "building an echo chamber."

(4) Whether an argument should update me depends only on its content, not on who makes it, what side they're on, or how confidently they say it.

(5) Debate and disagreement-seeking are good; I think you find truth faster by exposing yourself to the smartest people who disagree with you, and I try to do this.

(6) My own positions mostly track the truth because I got them by updating carefully; if everyone updated the way I do, polarization would mostly dissolve.

Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say what the cheapest fix is (which commitment gets weakened and what it costs).
