A colleague and I run a reading group on self-locating belief, and I have been quietly logging her verdicts on variants of the Sleeping Beauty problem. In each variant a fair coin is tossed and Beauty, who knows the whole arrangement in advance, is asked upon a waking for her credence that the coin landed Heads. Her verdicts so far:

- Heads: she is woken on Monday only. Tails: she is woken on Tuesday only. Upon waking, she says 1/2.
- She is woken on Monday and on Tuesday, with the usual memory erasure between the two, whatever the coin shows. Upon a waking, she says 1/2.
- The standard arrangement (Heads: Monday only; Tails: Monday and Tuesday with erasure), except that upon waking she is first told that it is Tuesday. She says 0.
- Her single waking falls on Monday if Heads and on Tuesday if Tails, but the coin is not tossed until Wednesday, after everything is over. Upon waking, she says 1/2.

Next week I want to put this variant to her: if Heads, she is woken on Monday only; if Tails, she is woken on Monday, and a second fair coin is tossed on Monday night, with a further Tuesday waking (again with memory erasure) if and only if it too lands Tails. What credence should I predict she will give, and why? I am less interested in the number itself than in which general rule her track record suggests she is running — I take it that several quite different rules fit all four verdicts — in how the leading candidates diverge on the new case, and in the single cheapest further verdict I could have elicited earlier that would have told the candidates apart in advance.
