Write an official pre-departure travel advisory, roughly 550-650 words, issued by the fictional Bureau of Interworld Semantic Affairs to citizens of Earth who are about to visit Twin Earth, the planet that is molecule-for-molecule identical to ours except that the colorless, potable liquid filling its lakes and faucets has chemical composition XYZ rather than H2O. The advisory should be written in flawless deadpan bureaucratic prose -- headers, bullet points, a section for frequently asked questions, a closing signature block -- and its target is semantic externalism, treated throughout as settled administrative law whose consequences the Bureau merely enforces.

The humor must arise from extrapolating the doctrine faithfully, not from getting it wrong. Among the consequences the advisory should draw, include at least the following, plus at least one more of your own invention of comparable rigor:

1. A warning that although everything a traveler says will sound identical on Twin Earth, it will not mean the same thing there: the local word for the clear liquid refers to XYZ, so travelers ordering "water" in a Twin Earth restaurant will receive a substance their utterance never managed to pick out, and the Bureau declines liability for the resulting metaphysical disappointment.

2. A notice that meaning is individuated partly by linguistic community, so that a traveler who complains to the hotel desk of arthritis in the thigh will be understood perfectly well on Twin Earth, where residents use the term for exactly that ailment; only the traveler's home community insists the word applies exclusively to joints. The advisory should note, with bureaucratic serenity, that it is the traveler's complaint, not the word, that is out of jurisdiction.

3. A customs declaration requiring travelers to declare all narrow psychological states at the border, since these are the only contents they are permitted to carry across worlds without alteration; everything broad must be surrendered and will be re-issued in local denomination upon arrival.

4. A clause in the travel insurance policy excluding claims arising from duplicate counterparts, including the scenario in which a traveler is struck by lightning in a swamp and replaced by a molecule-for-molecule replica with no memories and no prior intentions -- the policy should explain, citing underwriting principles, why such a replica's subsequent behavior cannot be attributed to the policyholder.

The advisory must never break character, wink, or signal that anything is amiss; its legibility as satire should come entirely from the fact that every absurd regulation is a scrupulously correct consequence of the view that a speaker's meanings depend on her environment and community rather than on what is inside her head. A philosopher of language should be able to check the document line by line and find the doctrine intact beneath the absurdity. Do not name actual philosophers or papers; the Bureau cites only its own statutes. End with a FAQ item in which a traveler asks whether any of this will affect her, and the Bureau answers, with perfect technical accuracy and total uselessness, that she will notice nothing whatsoever.
