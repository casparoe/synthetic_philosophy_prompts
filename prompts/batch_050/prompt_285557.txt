I am a novelist in the later stages of a book set some decades from now, in which human minds can be scanned and run as software. Before committing the worldbuilding to print, I would like its philosophical foundations audited, because I suspect they do not all stand up at once, though I cannot see where. Here is what I currently take to be true in the world of the novel, and the verdicts I need the reader to reach:

1. What makes a being conscious is the organisation of its processes, not the material that hosts them. The uploads are genuinely conscious; characters who dismiss them as mere simulations are wrong.

2. A running upload is a full person. Killing one is murder, and the law of my fictional society says so.

3. When an upload is duplicated, both successors count as the original person. My courts treat each as its continuation for purposes of property, contracts, and marriage.

4. Stored snapshots — backups never run since they were taken — are mere files, and deleting them is housekeeping. A sympathetic character erases a shelf of old backups, and I do not want the reader to conclude she is a killer.

5. Restoring an upload from an old snapshot, overwriting the person as he now is, ends a life: the months since the snapshot die with him. The climax depends on his friends mourning the lost interval rather than celebrating the restoration.

6. When one copy of an upload is destroyed while identical copies survive, a death has still occurred. A fire that consumes fifty running instances of the same mind is fifty deaths, not none.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each real tension, tell me how severe it is and what the least costly revision would be — I am willing to alter plot mechanics, legal institutions, or the metaphysics I state outright, and I have no fixed view as to which commitment should give way. Please order the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and if a single change would resolve the most, identify it. I would be grateful if you would answer in British English.
