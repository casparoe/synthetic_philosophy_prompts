Is there a name for this? I'll describe the thing first and then ask. My buddy and I have this running argument habit, and I've noticed we both pull the same sneaky move, just in opposite directions depending on what we want to conclude.

Instance one: we're arguing about whether intelligent life is rare or common in the galaxy. He says -- forget telescopes, forget exoplanet surveys. We exist. Our existing is vastly more probable if the galaxy is stuffed with civilizations than if there's basically one per galaxy. So the sheer fact that we're here is already strong evidence for the crowded galaxy, and the crowded-galaxy skeptics are ignoring data they carry around with them. I told him that's absurdly presumptuous, you can't do astronomy from your armchair, and he said the math doesn't care where the chair is.

Instance two, a few weeks later, same guy, different argument: how long will humanity last. He says think of yourself as a random sample from all humans who ever live. It would be a freakish coincidence if you happened to land in the first tiny sliver of the entire sequence, so probably you're somewhere in the ordinary middle, so probably there are huge numbers of humans yet to come. I.e., assume you're nothing special.

But wait. Move one says: favor whatever theory makes observers like you plentiful, because that makes your existence likely. Move two says: distrust whatever theory makes you an unusual observer among all observers, because you're probably typical. And these two can point in opposite directions! If the crowded galaxy contains trillions of beings and almost none of them are in my exact position -- early, planet-bound, still wondering whether anyone else is out there -- then move two says I should be suspicious of the crowded galaxy, because I'm not a typical member of it. So move one inflates the crowded galaxy and move two deflates it, and I have caught both of us just grabbing whichever move supports the conclusion we already liked. He used move one on aliens and move two on doomsday and didn't notice the tension until I shoved it in his face.

So, questions. Do these two rules of thumb each have an established name somewhere? Is there a name for the inconsistency of switching between them as convenient? Is there an actual literature on which rule (if either) is legit, or does everybody just pick the one that flatters their thesis? I know there's a vague thing people call the anthropic principle, but every time I look it up it seems to mean like five different ideas in a trench coat, so that doesn't help me. If the two rules are named, I want to know how the people who named them handle cases like mine where the rules pull apart, and if nobody has bothered naming the switching-between-them thing, feel free to coin a term -- but define it properly and tell me what it doesn't cover, because a bare label won't satisfy me at this point.
