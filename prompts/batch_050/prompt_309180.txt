A colleague and I disagree about the Fermi paradox, and we have managed to localize the disagreement to a single hypothesis, which I will state as precisely as I can. Call a civilization "forest-rational" if it concludes, from the impossibility of establishing trust across interstellar distances and centuries-long communication delays, that any other civilization capable of detecting it poses an unacceptable existential risk, and if it acts on that conclusion by concealing itself and, where feasible, striking first against civilizations that reveal themselves. The hypothesis is then: conditional on technological civilizations arising often enough that, absent such incentives, at least a few would currently be detectable from Earth, the dark forest dynamic — most surviving civilizations becoming forest-rational, or being eliminated by those that are — is the principal reason we observe no unambiguous evidence of extraterrestrial intelligence. My credence in that conditional proposition is about 0.25; my colleague's is about 0.02.

Please give me your own credence, and derive it rather than assert it. At minimum, decompose the proposition along the following lines, assigning a rough probability and one or two sentences of justification to each term:

1. The base-rate term: the probability that technological civilizations are common enough in our galaxy that, absent concealment incentives, we should already have detected one, treating our roughly century of increasingly sensitive observation as the relevant window.
2. The incentive term: the probability that the strategic logic actually compels concealment-and-preemption — that the expected cost of being detected exceeds the cost of striking, given plausible constraints on interstellar weaponry, the difficulty of verifying that a target has been neutralized, and the possibility that revealed civilizations are decoys, probes, or already dead.
3. The discipline term: the probability that a civilization can sustain the policy internally — that no faction, hobbyist, breakaway colony, or automated probe betrays its position over geological timescales, given that concealment must be near-perfect and perpetual while defection need only happen once.
4. The explanatory-priority term: the probability that, even granting terms 2 and 3, this dynamic — rather than genuine rarity, short technological lifespans, or the unobservability of post-biological minds — accounts for the particular silence we in fact observe.

State how the terms combine (you may treat them as roughly independent, but flag it if you think they are not), and give your final number. Then do two further things. First, identify the single term to which your estimate is most sensitive, and say what would change it: a specific observation, calculation, or argument. Second, locate the disagreement: given my 0.25 and my colleague's 0.02, and assuming for the sake of argument that we agree on terms 1 and 4, which of terms 2 and 3 is the more plausible locus of the gap, and what consideration should each of us put to the other?

One constraint on the exercise: I am not asking whether the dark forest is a good story, a respectable member of the space of Fermi solutions, or a useful warning about signalling; I am asking for the probability that it is the true explanation, conditional as stated. If you believe the conditional itself conceals an ambiguity that changes the answer, identify it and resolve it before giving your number.
