i'm in a phil of science seminar and we're working through Kuhn's Structure of Scientific Revolutions. In ch. III he says:

"Few people who are not actually practitioners of a mature science realize how much mop-up work of this sort a paradigm leaves to be done or quite how fascinating such work can prove in the execution."

Right around there Kuhn frames normal-science research as puzzle-solving, like crosswords or jigsaw puzzles: a solution is guaranteed to exist, the rules are fixed in advance, and the reward is in the solving rather than in discovering anything fundamentally new. Our prof leaned on this pretty hard to argue that most science isn't really aiming at truth or novelty at all -- it's just a skilled game the paradigm licenses, and Kuhn's "mop-up work" line proves he knew it. That felt too quick to me but i struggled to say why in section. Audit the analogy for me: give the correspondence table (who or what is the puzzle-setter? what plays the role of the guaranteed solution, the rules printed on the box?), then where it breaks -- e.g. a jigsaw has a designer who knows the finished picture and nature presumably doesnt -- and which of those breaks actually matter for the prof's deflationary reading. Does the "science as puzzle-solving" framing really license the conclusion that normal science isn't truth-seeking, or was the analogy smuggling that conclusion in? If theres a better analogy for what Kuhn is describing in that passage, offer it and say why it fits better.
