Okay, so you're a philosopher who specializes in Buddhist metaphysics and analytic personal identity — like, you actually know the anatta literature and the Parfit-style stuff, not just the pop versions. Think carefully, and before you commit, briefly brainstorm two or three candidate setups and tell me why you rejected them. Then build me one thought experiment to these specs:

The case has to be about a single ordinary person changing over time — no teleporters, brain swaps, uploads, fission, or magic. Realistic setting: a monastery, a hospice, a courtroom, long-term illness, whatever. One paragraph for the scenario itself.

Engineer it so that all three of these verdicts come out at once:

1. An animalist (the person just is the human organism) says the person at the end is unproblematically the same person as at the start. No puzzle there.
2. A Buddhist no-self theorist and a psychological reductionist (Parfit-style) agree that there is no deep further fact of personal identity in play — nothing over and above the body, the mental events, and the causal relations between them.
3. But the Buddhist and the reductionist disagree sharply about what matters in this person's survival. The reductionist says Relation R — psychological connectedness and continuity, memory, intention, character — is fully intact, and since that's all that ever matters, nothing important has changed. The Buddhist says something crucial has changed that Relation R simply does not track, and it has to be a real, describable psychological/causal difference (my suggestion would be something about appropriation or taking-states-as-mine, but do what works), not just the slogan that everything is impermanent and there's no self anyway.

After the scenario: (a) walk through each of the three views and show the verdict actually falls out of the theory rather than being stipulated, (b) name the single design feature that makes the Buddhist and the reductionist peel apart while they stay in agreement about the metaphysics, (c) tell me what happens if you remove that feature — does the case collapse into the Buddhist and reductionist just shrugging in unison?, and (d) give the strongest reductionist comeback, i.e. that the Buddhist is quietly smuggling a self back in to be the thing that benefits from the change.

Also say upfront what the case is for, since that fixes what would count as cheating. And keep the scenario to one paragraph, seriously — I've seen these things balloon into novels.
