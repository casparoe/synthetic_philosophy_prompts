So my roommate and I got into a massive argument last night about what it means for a body part to have a function, and I built an argument against her view and now it feels like it proves too much and I cant find the hole.

Her view: what a trait is *for* is fixed by its history. The heart's function is pumping blood because pumping is what hearts did that got them kept around over generations. A thing that never went through selection has no function at all, even if it happens to do something useful.

My argument:
(1) A trait's function = whatever effect of the trait explains why creatures with it out-reproduced creatures without it (her view).
(2) So having a function requires an actual history of selection for that effect.
(3) Imagine a freak lightning strike in a swamp assembles, purely by chance, a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me, memories and all.
(4) This duplicate's heart has no history. None of its "ancestors" did anything, because it has no ancestors.
(5) So by (1) and (2), his heart has no function. It isn't *for* pumping blood, it can't malfunction, and if a valve sticks there's nothing *wrong* with it, no more than there's something wrong with a rock that stops rolling.
(6) But that's absurd. His heart is physically identical to mine; if his valve sticks, that's a heart defect same as mine.
(7) So (1) is false and history-based accounts of function are dead.

She refused to budge. First she said fine, swallow (5) and (6), the duplicate just *seems* defective to us because we can't help seeing him through our own categories. Then she said worse: if I give up history, then "function" just means "what it currently does," and now everything has every effect as a function -- the heart's functions include making thumping noises for stethoscopes and filling up chest cavities, and a pebble in my shoe has the function of hurting my foot. So my rescue of the duplicate destroys the whole concept anyway.

Where does my argument actually go wrong, if it does? Walk me through the options: which premise or step is cheapest to drop, what each drop costs, and whether she's right that the only alternatives to her view collapse into everything-has-every-function. For the record I'd rather save "the swamp duplicate can have a heart defect" than any particular theory of function, but tell me if that's the wrong priority.
