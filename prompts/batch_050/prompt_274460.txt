So last night I got into a stupidly long arguement with a friend (call him Dan) about whether the Renaissance actually happened, and I want to figure out what kind of fight we were even having, because over three hours we made literally zero progress — and I get the sense professional historians have been circling the same spot for over a century.

His side, roughly: the Renaissance is a 19th century invention. A Swiss historian, Burckhardt I think, basically manufactured the whole picture in the 1860s by projecting then-modern ideas about individualism back onto Italy. Worse, "the Middle Ages" was just a slur the humanists came up with for everything between them and antiquity — so of course the Renaissance looks like a rupture, the gap was defined as a gap by the people claiming to end it. That's circular. Medievalists keep finding earlier "renaissances" (Carolingian, 12th century), which shows the label is elastic enough to stick anywhere. And for ordinary people nothing much changed: the peasant of 1500 ate, worked, and died a lot like the peasant of 1200. So period labels are bookkeeping devices historians build afterward, not things that existed.

My side: people at the time said they were living through a rebirth. Petrarch is already complaining about the dark centuries separating him from Rome, Vasari flat out describes art as reborn. And it's not just vibes — within a generation you get mathematical perspective in painting, Greek studies genuinely taking off in Italy, printing, a changed relationship to ancient texts. Also, a period doesn't need peasants' lives to change to be real, the same way "the digital age" is real even though my grandmother's kitchen looks the same as in 1975.

His counter: self-description is the cheapest evidence there is. Literati in every century announce a new age; the humanists were a tiny self-promoting coterie, and we mainly remember the ones who fit the story we already want. Also my Greek point is half a myth — Greek was being taught in Florence decades before Constantinople fell, so 1453 didn't trigger anything. And "wow, one drawing technique" — meanwhile population, diet, life expectancy, the actual fabric of life...

My counter: fine, then what about the Scientific Revolution? That's also a period label, also coined late, and yet something obviously happened between Copernicus and Newton — unless he wants to say that's fake too. He said yes, actually: there's a famous history book literally called The Scientific Revolution that opens by telling you there was no such thing, that it's a patchwork of local changes retconned into an event. So by 1am we had the exact same fight running on two fronts.

Here's what I want from you, and it's not a verdict on the Renaissance — it's a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. The options I've been turning over: (a) it's purely factual, and one of us is simply wrong about the degree of discontinuity; (b) it's verbal — I'm using "real" to mean a self-conscious break in high culture, he's using it to mean visible in aggregate material life, and we're both right under our own definition; (c) it's a disguised methodological fight about whether periods are things historians discover or tools they build, which no amount of 15th-century data could settle either way; or (d) we're answering different questions entirely — me asking how elites understood themselves, him asking about structural change — with "did the Renaissance happen" as a bad container holding both.

My honest hunch is (b), but I notice (b) is suspiciously convenient for me, since it means nobody has to concede anything. So: based on what we each actually said, which diagnosis fits? What's the strongest version of his side that I'm probably not hearing? And what would progress even look like here — is there a distinction that dissolves this, a test both sides could agree on in advance, or is "was there a Renaissance" just the kind of question you should stop asking and replace with smaller ones? Bonus: is the academic standoff over periodization the same kind of disagreement as our pub version, or does the professional one have extra structure ours lacked?
