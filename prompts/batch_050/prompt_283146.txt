So my reading group just finished Why Nations Fail and we got into a huge fight afterwards (the good kind) about whether anyone actually knows why some countries are rich. My one friend is a full-on Jared Diamond person, another keeps saying "it's all culture," and I ended up staking out a position that felt very reasonable in the moment, but now that I've written it down I'm worried it doesn't add up. Since I've got to defend it at the next session, can you audit my commitments for me? Here they are:

1. The North/South Korea comparison is the single most informative piece of evidence in this whole debate. Same people, same peninsula, same geography, same culture in 1945, wildly different outcomes. I think it basically settles that institutions dominate both geography and culture.
2. I'm deeply skeptical of the cross-country regression literature with historical instruments -- the settler mortality stuff and its cousins. The instruments plausibly violate the exclusion restriction (colonizers brought a lot more than just institutions), and more fundamentally history only gives us one draw, so causal inference from it is limited in a way no clever econometrics can fix.
3. Geography matters, but only insofar as it shaped institutions -- e.g., disease environment shaped where Europeans could settle, which shaped what institutions they set up, and that's the channel through which geography still affects incomes today.
4. I'm quite confident that if a poor country genuinely adopted rule of law and secure property rights, it would get substantially richer within a generation or two. This is actionable knowledge, not a guess.
5. Culture-based explanations are post hoc and unfalsifiable. "Confucian values" got blamed for East Asia's poverty in the 60s and credited for its success in the 90s. Any theory that flexible explains nothing.
6. There's no automatic convergence. Poor countries can stay poor indefinately because extractive elites block reform -- poverty isn't just a starting condition that growth naturally erodes.

What I want is an audit, not a summary of the debate. Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict but actually don't, and for each real tension tell me how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be -- i.e., what I'd have to weaken and what it would cost me in the argument with my Diamond friend specifically. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly don't know where the problem is, so don't be gentle about it.
