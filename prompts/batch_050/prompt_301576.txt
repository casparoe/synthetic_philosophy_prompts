I'm a high school junior on my school's technology policy committee, and this semester we're drafting recommendations for how the AI tools students use — tutoring chatbots, writing assistants, study apps — should handle requests that are legal and harmless to others but arguably bad for the student making them. Think of a student asking a chatbot to just hand over the essay answer instead of walking them through it, or asking for help planning an all-nighter before an exam.

To organize our debate, I've been using a three-way distinction I came up with myself (so please take it as defined here, not as something from the literature): a friction is when the AI complies but adds a speed bump, like a warning, a delay, or a required reflection prompt; a redirection is when the AI delivers something other than what was asked, like explaining the concept instead of giving the answer; a refusal is when the AI declines outright. Any school policy will basically be a set of rules about which of these three responses applies to which kinds of requests.

Here's my problem: our committee meets only four more times this semester, so we can't investigate everything. Which open questions about this kind of everyday AI paternalism matter most for writing a good policy, and in what order? Some candidates I've considered: how anyone could tell whether paternalistic interventions actually improve students' outcomes rather than just feeling responsible; whether determined students will simply route around frictions and redirections, making them pointless; who, if anyone, has the legitimacy to define a student's own good — the school, the software company, parents, or the student's future self; whether being regularly protected from tempting choices stunts the ability to resist temptation; and whether there's a question I've missed entirely that matters more than all of these. Please give me your top five, ranked.

Defend the ordering rather than just listing it. State the criteria you're using — for instance, how much our actual policy text would change depending on the answer, whether the question is answerable at all within a semester by a student committee, and how many of the other questions depend on this one. Note any trade-offs between those criteria, and for each item say what kind of evidence or argument would move it up or down the ranking.
