Write a villanelle in the voice of a low-level functionary in some bureaucratic machine of wrongdoing -- a clerk who stamps permits, say, or an accountant who reconciles the ledgers -- who insists throughout that they caused nothing: the harm would have proceeded identically without them, someone else stood ready to take their desk, and their individual contribution made no difference to a single outcome. The two refrains should be, in effect, the agent's causal excuse ("nothing turned on me") and the accuser's reply ("yet it was also yours"), and the poem's structure should do philosophical work: since a villanelle's refrains must finally meet in the closing quatrain, let that forced convergence dramatise the tension between the counterfactual conception of responsibility (what difference did you make?) and the participation conception (what did you join?), so that the reader feels why complicity resists analysis in purely causal terms. Nineteen lines, no moralising narrator, and precise enough that someone who knows the literature on contributory and complicit responsibility recognises the structure of the problem rather than a slogan.
