Afterwards it felt cheap, like I'd won by picking the story rather than by having the better reasons. So I'm compiling a list of the ways intuition pumps rig the verdict, and I want one more for it. What I have so far: (1) loaded description -- the case is narrated using the very concept that's in dispute, so the conclusion is smuggled into the setup; (2) vivid irrelevant detail -- gore, names, faces, one sympathetic character doing the intuitive work the principle is supposed to do; (3) forced binarism -- the pump presents two options and the intuition collapses once a third is added; (4) unreliability of imagination -- the case is so far-fetched that our reaction tracks our inability to really picture it, not the principle; (5) framing and order effects -- the same case gets different verdicts depending on wording or on which cases came before it. Give me a sixth mechanism that isn't a rewording or special case of any of those, explain with a made-up example how it would work, and tell me whether it indicts thought experiments as such or only badly built ones -- and if only badly built ones, what the diagnostic sign is that a pump is clean.
