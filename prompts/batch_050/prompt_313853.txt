I teach a high school biology elective, and in preparing a unit on evolution I have kept stumbling over the same pattern, one I can describe but cannot name, and it is starting to feel like the pattern shows up well outside biology too. The standard explanations I give my students run like this: the heart beats in order to circulate blood; the honeybee's dance exists in order to tell hive-mates where nectar is; the gazelle's stiff-legged bounce in front of a lion exists in order to advertise that it is not worth chasing. In each case the "in order to" does real explanatory work, yet in none of these cases does any mind possess the reason. The bee does not know what its dance means; natural selection is not a someone. Then I noticed the same shape elsewhere. A friend who works in logistics tells me their pricing algorithm has learned to raise prices shortly before demand spikes, and nobody on the team can say why the pattern it found works, only that it does. A corporation can be said, truly, to be trying to exit a market even when no individual executive has yet formed that intention. Certain food taboos turned out to guard against parasites nobody who observed the taboo had ever heard of. In every instance there is a reason things are arranged as they are, the reason explains and even justifies the arrangement, and no reasoner anywhere holds it.

Is there an established term for this, for reasons or purposes that exist and do explanatory work without being anyone's reasons? I know some neighboring vocabulary, and I suspect a good answer will involve distinguishing my phenomenon from each neighbor: teleology and functional explanation generally, which seem broader than what I mean; emergence, which seems to be about properties of wholes rather than about purposes without holders; and whatever it is that is going on when we say the market "knows" something no trader knows, which feels like a cousin but perhaps not the same thing. If there is one well-carved concept here, I want to know what it is called and which of my examples it actually covers. If there are several, I want the map. If there is none, I would be interested in what you would call it and how you would define it.

What I would not find satisfying is a bare label, because what really puzzles me is the boundary. Does a thermostat's set point count, or is that too cheap, since the reason was designed in by an engineer who did hold it? Does the arrangement of a secondhand bookshop that has, over decades, drifted into a useful classification no bookseller planned count? And here is the case that most unsettles me: when psychoanalysis attributes to me a motive I sincerely do not recognize as mine, is that an instance of the same phenomenon, or does the fact that it is still, in some sense, my reason put it on the other side of the line? I would like to know whether the phenomenon comes in degrees, what test decides borderline cases, and whether anything important turns on whether the reason could in principle be recovered by someone, as opposed to being recoverable by no one at all.
