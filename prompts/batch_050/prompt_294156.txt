A colleague and I keep arguing about when, if ever, it is rational to commit now to whatever you will believe later. Her verdicts on my cases so far:

1. By Friday I will have run the full load-test suite and read two migration postmortems, and I trust Friday-me to weigh them honestly. Should I simply adopt now whatever confidence in the migration Friday-me ends up with? Her verdict: yes.
2. After three whiskeys at the team dinner I will be certain the ground-up rewrite is a brilliant idea. Should I treat that as a reason to start it now? Her verdict: no.
3. A year from now I will have forgotten the two incidents that made me distrust a vendor, and I will rate them neutrally. Should I rate them neutrally already? Her verdict: no.
4. Next month my feed will show me only articles favorable to a framework I am evaluating; future-me will read them in good faith and come away convinced it is excellent. Should I be convinced already? Her verdict: no.
5. Tomorrow I will read a pre-registered benchmark of our caching change run by people whose methods I trust. Should my confidence today simply be whatever I expect it to be after reading? Her verdict: yes.

Here is the next case. We have a race condition we have not fixed; from the logs, I know it fires roughly weekly under production load. For the next month the service will run in a demo environment whose load pattern is such that a still-live bug would produce a clean month about half the time. Future-me will watch the service run clean for the whole month, review the telemetry carefully, and reason flawlessly — but nobody will have told future-me that demo load is atypical, so future-me will conclude with high confidence that the bug is gone. I already know today that a clean month is what I would expect to see half the time even if nothing has been fixed. Should I adopt future-me's confidence now?

Predict her verdict on this case and justify the prediction: what rule best explains her first five verdicts taken together, what other rules fit those five just as well, which of those rules diverge on the new case, and what further case would best tell the remaining candidates apart? I gather this territory is near what philosophers call the Reflection principle, but I would rather you work from her verdicts than from the literature.
