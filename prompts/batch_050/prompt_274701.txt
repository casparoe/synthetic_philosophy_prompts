ok so i'm taking a seminar on the ethics of influence this semester (basically: when is it ok to try to change someone's mind or behavior, and when does it become manipulation) and i have to design a "what's your theory of manipulation?" quiz for a class activity. i want to make it actually good, so i need your help.

please write a quiz with 8-10 questions where each question is a concrete scenario and the reader picks one of 2-3 responses. the scenarios should be everyday-ish, stuff like: a health app that makes the "skip workout" button tiny and grey, a friend who sighs loudly until you offer to help them move, a doctor describing a treatment as having a "90% survival rate" instead of a "10% mortality rate", a political campaign buying psychographic ad targeting, a parent telling a kid santa only visits well-behaved children, a boss scheduling the all-hands meeting right before lunch so people agree faster, etc. feel free to use some of those or swap in better ones.

the point is that the reader should get sorted by their answers to the scenarios, not by which label sounds nice, so no question should ask anything like "do you think intent matters more than outcomes?". the responses to each scenario should be phrased as gut reactions ("totally fine", "manipulative but harmless", "genuinely wrong") plus maybe a short reason, so people can answer without knowing any of the theory.

then i need a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least these positions:

- the autonomy/agency view: manipulation is wrong when it bypasses or subverts someone's rational decision-making, regardless of whether it's good for them
- the intention/motive view: what makes something manipulation is that the influencer has a self-serving or covert motive, not the method itself
- the outcome/welfare view: influence is fine if it leaves the target better off by their own lights, so a well-designed nudge is ok and a harmful but honest argument is worse
- the contextual view: whether it's manipulation depends on the relationship and the norms of the setting (ads are one thing, your best friend or your doctor is another)
- a deflationary "there's no real line, it's all a continuum of pressure" option

some requirements, because i've seen bad versions of these quizzes:

1. at least two of the questions should specifically pull apart the autonomy view from the intention view, since those two agree on a lot of cases and i don't want them ending up indistinguishable.
2. if any pair of positions still can't be separated by your quiz, say so in the key instead of pretending the scoring works.
3. include at least one scenario where the deflationary view does noticeably better at capturing the natural reaction than all the others, and say which one it is.
4. each scenario should have at least one response option that no "right answer" corresponds to, so it doesn't feel like a test with an answer key.
