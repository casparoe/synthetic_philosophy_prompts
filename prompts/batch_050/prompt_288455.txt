I host a small philosophy of mind podcast, and next week I'm interviewing a cognitive scientist who defends massive modularity: the claim, against Fodor, that the mind is modular all the way up, including the central systems that handle belief formation and reasoning. She's slippery in one particular way -- whenever the strong claim gets pressed, she slides to a weak reading where "module" just means some functional specialization, which nobody denies. Write me a sequence of eight to ten questions. I want the early ones to pin down exactly what she takes a module to be (informational encapsulation? domain specificity? looser Carruthers-style criteria?), the middle ones to force her to answer Fodor's actual argument -- that central cognition is isotropic and Quinean and therefore can't be encapsulated -- plus at least one question each on the frame problem, on developmental plasticity (the Karmiloff-Smith style objection), and on how a swarm of specialist modules is supposed to produce flexible cross-domain stuff like planning or doing science. For every question give me a follow-up aimed at the dodge she's most likley to try, and close with a question about what empirical finding would actually make her abandon the view.
