Activists and politicians regularly reach for the same image when pressed to justify long-shot moral campaigns: history is a long arc that bends toward justice. The image does argumentative work in two directions that are rarely distinguished — sometimes it consoles (present defeats are temporary; the bend is on its way) and sometimes it mobilizes (the arc bends only because people bend it, so join in). Audit this analogy. Give the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the arc itself, of the bending force, of the fixed standard against which curvature is judged, and of the endpoint. Then identify where the mapping breaks and which breaks matter for which use — for instance, whether an arc whose curvature is fixed when it is cast imports determinism, whether a bent arc implies a bender and so quietly smuggles providence into secular rhetoric, and whether the consoling and mobilizing uses can be supported by one and the same picture or only by switching between incompatible ones. Conclude with whether either conclusion survives the audit, and whether a better metaphor — a tide, a ratchet, a climb — would carry it, saying what each candidate implies about the possibility of regression.
