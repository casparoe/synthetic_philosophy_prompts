ok so i fell down a youtube rabbit hole about consciousness a few weeks ago and ended up stuck on this one idea i can't shake. the idea goes: what if the feeling of things — the redness of red, the actual hurt of a stubbed toe — doesn't cause anything in the physical world. my brain does all the work of yanking my hand off the stove, and the pain is just along for the ride, like steam coming off a train. spooky, but fine, whatever. here's the part that actually broke me: if the feeling causes nothing, then it also can't cause me to SAY "i'm in pain" or even think "wow, i have experiences." those words come out of neurons firing, same as everything else. so this paragraph i'm writing about my inner life is not, strictly, being caused by my inner life. which feels insane to type. how am i supposed to trust any report about experience, including my own, if the reports are all downstream of the brain machinery and not the experience itself?

anyway, i want a poem about this. specifically a villanelle — the nineteen-line one where two lines keep coming back, like that dylan thomas poem. the two repeating lines should be the two horns of the thing: one asserting something like "i know what it's like to be me," the other asserting something like "nothing i feel decides what i say." whatever exact phrasing you want, but that contrast. and the form should do the philosophical work: the two refrains keep circling each other, never touching, until the last stanza forces them into the same closing couplet — and i want that moment to land as uncomfortable, not resolved. do not let the poem declare a winner, because as far as i can tell nobody's won this one.

the bit i care most about: somewhere in the middle the narrator has to notice that the poem itself is caught in the trap — that these lines about experience were not caused by the experience, they're just more brain-weather, and the poem saying "i feel" is exactly the kind of utterance the theory says to doubt. if the poem never notices that, it's just a sad poem about being a ghost, and thats not the thing keeping me up at night.

last thing — i'm not a philosophy person, i just read a lot of reddit threads, so if i've mangled the idea and the real version is slightly different, quietly write the poem about the real version instead of correcting me in verse. just keep the refrains doing the job i described.
