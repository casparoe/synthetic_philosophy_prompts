I'm in a small reading group on AI alignment (we're all amateurs, mostly CS people and one philosophy PhD student) and we're working through scalable oversight stuff this month. A friend of mine who runs a newsletter sent me his summary of iterated amplification, since he knew we were reading it, and I want to check it before I embarrass myself by repeating it. Here's what he wrote:

"Iterated amplification is a proposal for building aligned AI by bootstrapping from human judgment. The core move is that instead of asking one human to evaluate an AI's output directly -- which breaks down once the AI is doing things no human can check -- you give the human a team of AI assistants, and the human-plus-assistants system evaluates the output together by breaking hard questions into easier subquestions. Since each assistant is just a copy of the previous, already-aligned model, and the human is sitting on top checking everything, the combined system is at least as aligned as the human who started the chain, just a lot smarter. Then you 'distill' the combined system's behavior back into a single fast model by compressing it, so you don't have to pay the cost of running the whole committee every time, and you repeat the cycle with the new, better model. Because alignment is preserved at every step of the loop -- the human verifies each decomposition before it's used -- it follows by induction that the final system stays aligned no matter how many rounds you run, so the whole thing amounts to a constructive proof that aligned superintelligence is achievable in principle, with the only open questions being engineering ones. The main technical risk people raise is that the distilled model might be less capable than the committee it was compressed from, so you could lose a bit of performance each round."

My instinct is that this is mostly right but something is off about how breezy the induction step is, and I also have a vague memory that "distillation" in this context isn't literally compression. Could you go through his summary sentence by sentence and tell me which parts someone who actually works on this would accept, which parts are subtly wrong or overconfident, and which are flat-out mistakes? In particular: is the inductive argument for alignment preservation as solid as he makes it sound, or does it rest on assumptions he's glossing over? Is he right that the main worry about distillation is capability loss? And if I strip out whatever's wrong, does his big conclusion -- that this is close to an in-principle proof that aligned superintelligence is possible -- still follow from the corrected version, or was it leaning on the errors?
