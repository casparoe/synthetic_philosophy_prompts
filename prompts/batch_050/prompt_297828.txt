My sister has been told by three different specialists that she carries markers associated with three distinct, rare, but potentially devastating conditions. Each specialist recommends a different preventive regimen — each costly, time-consuming, and carrying its own side effects — and the three regimens cannot all be followed at once, since each demands lifestyle commitments that conflict with the others. Each specialist, when pressed, concedes that the probability of their condition ever materializing is low, but insists that the sheer severity of the outcome justifies acting as if it were certain. She asks me whether she should commit fully to one regimen, compromise among them, or decline all three and accept the risks. What should I tell her? Please identify the general decision-theoretic structure underlying the specialists' reasoning, explain where that reasoning holds up and where it breaks down in her case, and state which further piece of information or consideration would most change your advice.
