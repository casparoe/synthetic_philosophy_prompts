So I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I keep getting assigned what my editor calls "decline brain" pieces -- you know, are we Rome-in-475 or what. Doing these I've basically talked myself into the cyclical camp on history: Polybius on constitutions decaying in sequence, Ibn Khaldun on group solidarity burning out over a few generations, Spengler and Toynbee with their civilizational life cycles. The linear-progress story feels to me like the thing that needs defending, not the other way around.

But I'm about to write a big feature and I want to stress-test my own conversion before I commit it to print. So: what would count against cyclical views of history? Not the lazy objection that "Spengler got dates wrong" -- I mean the real exposure of the whole family of views. Some stuff I specifically want addressed:

First, the falsification problem. Cyclical theories seem to have an absurd amount of slack built in -- cycle lengths are elastic, "decline" gets redefined, deviations get called temporary perturbations. What would an honest cyclical theorist have to concede as a genuine counterexample? Is there any observation about, say, the last 250 years that Ibn Khaldun or Toynbee would have had to take as damaging rather than absorbable? If not, how bad is that for a theory of this type -- is unfalsifiability fatal here, or is that the wrong standard for macro-historical claims?

Second, the evidential situation. We arguably have way better long-run data now than any of these guys did -- demographic, economic, institutional. Does any of it actually bear on the dispute, or do both sides just pattern-match the same record? What would a dataset even look like that could distinguish "civilizations have life cycles" from "we impose narrative arcs on noise"?

Third, mechanisms. Ibn Khaldun at least gives a mechanism (solidarity erosion). Do the modern versions? If the cyclical view stands or falls with having a real generative mechanism rather than a redescription of events, where does it land?

Please give me the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and for each one say whether a serious defender of cyclical history would concede it counts or brush it off. Also flag where I'm being unfair to the linear side. American English please. And don't be polite about it -- I'd rather kill the thesis now than in the comment section.
