After a long argument with a friend about whether psychiatric diagnoses such as depression are real kinds in the way chemical elements are, she kept invoking the slogan science carves nature at its joints. She used it to end the discussion: lithium belongs to a genuine kind because atomic number settles membership, whereas diagnostic categories are merely institutional sorting devices because their boundaries change over time. I objected that biological species are also theory-laden and contested, yet still seem to track causal structure, and that an interest-relative classification can be non-arbitrary. What distinct claims could science carves nature at its joints be making? Distinguish at least metaphysical, causal-explanatory, predictive, methodological, and normative readings, saying which are trivial, substantive and defensible, or false. Then explain which readings would actually support my friend’s inference from shifting diagnostic boundaries to merely institutional, and how each would treat biological species, elementary particles, psychiatric syndromes, and social categories partly created by our classifications. Present the readings as a menu with explicit verdicts, and identify the argument or consideration that would most plausibly change your overall assessment.
