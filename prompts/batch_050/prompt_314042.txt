i'm putting together a seminar session on higher-order theories of consciousness (the family that says a mental state is conscious when the subject has a suitable higher-order representation of it, whether that's a thought, a perception-like state, or some kind of self-monitoring signal). before we argue about which version wins, i want the spec first: write the desiderata that any higher-order theory of consciousness must meet to count as an adequate theory, with the case or consideration motivating each one.

some things the list has to cover, though feel free to restructure:

- what to say about the empty higher-order representation case -- a system represents itself as seeing green when there's no first-order green state at all. does consciousness just pop into existence from the misrepresentation? a theory has to take a stand or explain why it doesn't have to
- animals and infants: if the higher-order representation requires fancy metacognitive machinery, the theory seems to deny consciousness to creatures most people think have it. is that a bullet the theory can bite, or a desideratum it fails?
- why conscious states feel immediate and unmediated if they only become conscious via a further representation (the theory seems to predict they should feel like being thought about)
- consistency with the actual cognitive neuroscience, eg what happens to conscious experience when the relevant frontal areas are damaged or taken offline
- cases like subliminal perception and blindsight, which the theory is supposed to explain -- it has to actually deliver the verdict that the stimulus is unconscious, without also ruling out too much

for each desideratum say why it's a genuine requirement vs a prejudice of one camp (eg is "must not multiply representations unnecessarily" a real constraint or just first-order fans complaining), and mark which are non-negotiable and which a reasonable theory could sacrifice. if some subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly -- i suspect something in the neighborhood of "explain the immediacy" + "handle the empty representation case" + "stay empirically lean" can't all hold at once, and i want to know if i'm right. also tell me which single desideratum does the most work in ruling out existing variants.

no need to namecheck every theorist in the literature, i care about the criteria not the scorecard. end with a last line of the form: Verdict: <the single most load-bearing desideratum, in a few words>
