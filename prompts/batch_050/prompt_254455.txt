The question was how you supervise an AI system that's better than you at the relevant task, and we each kept leaning on one analogy.

Her analogy: overseeing a strong AI is like a non-technical manager overseeing engineers, or a hospital administrator overseeing surgeons. The manager can't check the reasoning step by step, half of it they couldn't even read, so they judge by results: does the bridge stand, does the patient survive, do the tests pass. On her view outcome-based supervision (reward the model when the code runs, the proof verifies, the answer checks out) isn't some desperate fallback, it's how competent oversight of experts has always worked, and the "process-based" people who want to reward good-looking reasoning steps are basically asking for micromanagement by someone who doesn't understand the work, which in human organizations we correctly regard as dysfunction. My analogy: grading a math student. If you grade only the final answer you reward copying, lucky guesses, memorized solutions, and confident wrong-reasoning-that-happened-to-land-right, and the student learns to produce plausible answers instead of learning to solve things. Show-your-work grading exists precisely because correct answers are cheap evidence of nothing, and a model that can reason illegibly or make wrong answers look right is more like a student with the answer key than like a surgeon.
