I have no formal training in philosophy, but I've been reading on my own about the history of science, and an argument with a friend has left me stuck. Looking at a list of once-successful theories that were later discarded -- the phlogiston theory of combustion, the caloric theory of heat, the luminiferous ether, the geocentric model -- my friend concluded that since every past theory eventually went in the trash, our current theories are headed there too, and scientific belief is basically a fashion that expires. I pushed back with a distinction I came up with: some old theories seem to have been scrapped wholesale (nobody extracts anything from phlogiston), while others seem to have been absorbed rather than refuted -- Newtonian mechanics still works inside relativity as a special case, and engineers still build bridges with it. My friend replied that even the "absorbed" parts only survive by being reinterpreted beyond recognition. Neither of us could map out all the possible stances a person could take here.

So I'd like you to build me a decision tree for a hobbyist science-discussion group I run. The tree should start from a member who accepts one thing only -- that science has had genuinely successful theories that were later abandoned -- and lead them, through roughly seven to nine yes-or-no questions, to a named position on whether our current theories are on track toward the truth. Each question should be phrased so someone can answer it without knowing any philosophical vocabulary (ask about what they'd say in a concrete case, not "do you endorse realism"), and at least one branch should turn on my scrapped-versus-absorbed distinction. The leaves must include, at minimum: a position that the induction from past failures doesn't apply because the old successes weren't the right kind of successes; a position that we should believe only the mathematical or structural skeleton of our theories; a position that we should believe only in entities we can directly intervene on; a position that we should trust only the specific parts of a theory that earned their keep by generating surprising new predictions; and a full anti-realist position that science aims only at fitting observations, not at truth. If some combination of answers is logically possible but nobody defends it, mark the leaf "unnamed" and say in one sentence whether the gap looks like incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. My own hunch is that the mathematics of a theory survives even when its entities turn out to be fictions; please give that a leaf and tell me whether it coincides with any of the positions above or stands alone. Also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate with its questions.

Please state, at the end, how confident you are in the tree overall and single out the single branch or placement you are least sure of. Write the whole response in American English and keep it to a hard maximum of 750 words, tree and notes included.
