This came up again in an ACX-adjacent thread about whether psychopaths deserve blame and I think I've argued myself into a corner. Numbered for clarity:

1. It's only fair to blame someone for doing X if they were in a position, at the time, to know that X was wrong.
2. But "knowing X is wrong" can't just mean being able to say the words or pass a quiz about it. If someone says "sure, it's wrong" while feeling literally nothing and does it anyway without a flicker of inner conflict, they don't really know. Moral knowledge has to have some motivational grip, some aversion, however weak and easily overridden.
3. So anyone who does X with zero aversion and zero inner conflict didn't, at that moment, know X was wrong.
4. Psychopaths do awful things with exactly zero aversion -- that's basically the defining feature of the condition.
5. So psychopaths don't know their actions are wrong, and by (1) they aren't blameworthy. Honestly I'm fine biting that bullet; blame isn't the only tool we have for dealing with dangerous people.
6. But (3) doesn't stop at psychopaths. The mafia hitman, the fanatic who's certain he's doing God's work, the ordinary guy who's just really good at compartmentalizing -- anyone who does wrong without flinching fails the same test. The only people left blameworthy are the weak-willed, the ones who knew better, felt the pull, and did it anyway.
7. So blameworthiness ends up inversely proportional to how bad you are: the more settled and unconflicted your wrongdoing, the less you deserve blame. That's absurd.

This proves too much, but each step looked fine when I wrote it down. Which premise do I give up? If I drop (2) I owe a story about what moral knowledge even is if it can be totally inert, and if I drop (1) I'm saying you can fairly blame people for violations they couldn't have known about, which feels worse. What I'd most like to keep is ordinary blame for ordinary wrongdoers -- the psychopath case can go wherever it goes -- but I want the line between the cases to be principled, not just "psychopaths are a special exception because they're weird." Also, whatever step you pick: what does rejecting it actually cost downstream? What else am I now commited to?
