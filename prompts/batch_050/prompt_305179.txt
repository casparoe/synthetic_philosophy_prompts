I'm a software engineer working on a multi-agent system where autonomous agents can delegate in-game decisions to a shared mediator. While reading around the theory of this, I encountered the notion of a "safe Pareto improvement," and I want to pin down what an adequate formal account of it must do before I evaluate any specific definitions.

The setup as I understand it: players in a game face some default outcome (say, a non-cooperative equilibrium). A mediator can propose that some subset of players delegate their choice to it, and it then selects actions on their behalf. A Pareto improvement over the default is an arrangement that leaves every delegating player at least as well off and at least one strictly better off. The hard part is "safe": the players do not share common knowledge of each other's beliefs or payoffs, so the improvement has to be one each player can verify from their own private information alone, rather than one that merely holds from the mediator's or an outside analyst's vantage point.

Write the desiderata a theory of safe Pareto improvements must meet to count as adequate for guiding real delegation. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Among the things I want the list to engage with: which notion of improvement is the right baseline (guaranteed better in every possible state, better in expectation under each player's own beliefs, or better in the actual state); which distinctions the theory cannot collapse (weak versus strict dominance over the default, common knowledge versus mere mutual knowledge of the arrangement, the ex ante versus ex post evaluation of a randomized delegation); which edge cases it must handle gracefully (games where the default is already Pareto-optimal, more than two players with only partial delegation, delegation that itself becomes a move in a larger game); and what consistency constraints apply (it should reduce to ordinary Pareto comparison under full common knowledge, and it should be checkable given what players can plausibly be assumed to know).

Indicate which desiderata you take to be non-negotiable and which look like optional strengthenings. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, if demanding state-by-state safety makes improvements vanishingly rare — say so explicitly and show the tension. Finally, briefly score these three candidate criteria against your list: (A) the proposal must dominate the default in every state the delegating player considers possible; (B) it must dominate in expectation under each player's own beliefs; (C) it must dominate in expectation under the mediator's beliefs. Keep the scoring brief — the requirements list is the deliverable I care about.
