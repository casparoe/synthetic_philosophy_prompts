I teach a second-year undergraduate metaethics seminar, 14 students, 75 minutes, currently working through expressivism. Design an activity that makes them feel the force of the Frege--Geach problem before they ever hear its name. My rough idea: equip them with a communicative toolkit that is genuinely expressivist -- they may assert plain facts and may cheer or boo actions, but they may not treat moral sentences as true or false -- and then stage tasks that force them to negate moral claims, embed them in conditionals, and assess an argument whose premises mix moral and factual claims, so that they watch the toolkit that works smoothly for unembedded avowals break down under embedding. I need the concrete rules and materials, a minute-by-minute timeline, and the exact moment where the breakdown should become visible to them. Then give me a debrief that maps what happened onto the logical structure of the problem (why a conditional like "if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong" resists translation into booing), what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to recover, and an honest note on what the activity cannot reproduce -- I suspect it dramatizes communicative awkwardness rather than the semantic problem about content and inference, and I want the debrief to close that gap without turning into a lecture.
