Right, so I've just spent about three hours in the pub having a long arguement with my mate, who's a die-hard dispositional essentialist about laws of nature, and she kept insisting my views don't actually fit together. Annoyingly I suspect she might have half a point, so I'd like an audit before I see her again. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Humean supervenience is true: everything about the world supervenes on the distribution of local qualities across spacetime, and the laws are just the theorems of the best systematisation of that mosaic — the summary that best balances simplicity against informativeness.

2. Laws genuinely explain their instances. The reason the apple fell rather than hovering is that its falling was law-governed, and this explanatory role is exactly what marks laws off from mere accidents, like every coin in my pocket happening to be silver.

3. The best-system story applies everywhere: even in a world of total chaos there's still some best summary of what happens, so that world has laws too. Every possible world has laws.

4. Physical possibility is a real restriction on metaphysical possibility — there are metaphysically possible worlds that break the actual laws, and that's precisely what makes the laws contingent.

5. I'm a determinist about the actual world, so there are no non-trivial objective chances here; but I still think something like the Principal Principle is the right norm linking rational credence to chance, wherever chance does exist.

6. The generalisations of the special sciences — the stuff in ecology, economics, epidemiology — are genuine laws as well, not just handy approximations waiting around to be reduced.

Tell me whether this package hangs together. Which pairs are actually in tension, as opposed to just sounding like they are, and how bad is each one — rank them by severity rather than treating everything as equally damning. For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me. And be honest about which single commitment I'd get the most mileage out of dropping or weakening. I'm not precious about any of them — well, except maybe (1). Please answer in British English.
