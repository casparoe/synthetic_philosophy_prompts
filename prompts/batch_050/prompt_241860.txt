I'm writing a chapter on rational choice among equals and want a complete taxonomy of responses to Buridan's ass. The solutions I already have: (1) randomize -- flip a coin or use any arbitrary tiebreaker, since choosing between equals requires no reason and a random device supplies the needed trigger; (2) deny the premise -- perfect symmetry never actually obtains, so the paradox describes an impossible situation and needs no solution; (3) the picking/choosing distinction -- selecting without reasons (picking) is a distinct and perfectly rational mode of selection, so the ass errs only in insisting on choosing; (4) appeal to thresholds of discrimination -- options whose difference falls below what the agent can discriminate count as equal, and taking either is rationally permissible, so indecision is never required; (5) act on impulse rather than reason -- the paradox assumes action must be reason-governed, but whim, habit, and brute inclination are equally available motors of action. Give me one further solution that is not a rewording, special case, or combination of these five -- in particular, not just another tiebreaker mechanism or another way of denying the symmetry. Then say whether your solution actually dissolves the paradox or merely relocates the difficulty to a different point in the deliberative process.
