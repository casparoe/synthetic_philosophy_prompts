I'm the philosophy rep on my university's animal research ethics commitee (we review maybe 15 protocols a term, about an hour each, and the chair signs off on our votes). My problem: my own votes keep coming out Kantian while everyone else does welfare math. Kant's actual position on animals, as I understand it: we have no duties directly to animals (they aren't self-conscious rational agents, so they can legitimately be used as means), but cruelty toward them is still out because it deadens our sympathetic feelings and corrodes our own character, kindness is in because it cultivates dispositions duty needs, and a long-serving animal is owed something analogous to gratitude.

Turn that into a screening procedure I can actually run on a protocol in under an hour: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure just stops with a no, and where it forces a character judgment I can't dodge — and for those, give me non-arbitrary proxies, since "does this corrupt the researcher's character" is not directly checkable. Then run it on three made-up-but-typical protocols: (1) terminal surgical training on pigs for med students when decent simulators exist; (2) mild food deprivation in standard rat learning studies; (3) a lab that wants to euthanize its retired beagles rather than pay to rehome them. At least one of these should come out with a verdict I'll hate — I want to see where the view bites, not where it's comfortable. Also flag where the Kantian procedure diverges from the commitee's actual remit (animal welfare, full stop), since I have to live inside that tension.

Structure: The procedure (questions in order, ~600 words), Three test runs (~150 words each), Where it misfires (~200 words).
