For a graduate seminar on semantic externalism, I've been asked to write up my commitments so the group can stress-test them. Here is where I've landed:

(1) The Twin Earth intuition is correct. Oscar on Earth and his molecule-for-molecule duplicate on Twin Earth — where the clear, drinkable liquid in lakes and taps is XYZ rather than H₂O — mean different things by the word "water," and when each thinks the thought he would express as "water is wet," they are thinking different thoughts. Mental content is wide: it depends on the thinker's environment, not just on what is in the head.

(2) Self-knowledge is privileged. I can know what I am currently thinking — for instance, that I am thinking that water is wet — by reflection alone, without conducting any chemical or geographical investigation of my surroundings.

(3) Closure holds. If I know that p, and I competently deduce q from p, then I know, or am at least in a position to know, q.

(4) Justification is narrow. Oscar and Twin Oscar are exactly equally justified in their respective beliefs; nothing about justificational status turns on facts external to the thinker.

(5) Both Oscar and Twin Oscar know that water is wet (each using the word of his own community). Externalism about content is fully compatible with ordinary, widespread knowledge of the external world, and I reject any skeptical conclusion that would follow from denying this.

(6) Slow-switching cases are reflectively detectable. If I were unwittingly transported to Twin Earth and remained there long enough that my word "water" came to refer to XYZ, I could in principle come to recognize, by reflection on what I am now thinking, that my thought contents had shifted.

Are these jointly tenable? I want a genuine audit, not a list of everything that might be questioned. In particular: which commitments really pull against each other — I have a nagging sense that (1), (2), and (3) together generate trouble, but I cannot pin down exactly where — and which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection (my seminar partner insists that (1) and (4) are incompatible, and separately that (1) and (5) are, but in both cases I suspect the worry can be answered). For each real tension, tell me how severe it is and identify the least costly revision, being explicit about what that revision costs. Please rank the tensions rather than treating them all as equally bad, and conclude by naming the single revision that would clean up the most.
