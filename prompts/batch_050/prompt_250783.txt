I co-run a small reading group on formal epistemology, and we've spent the last three sessions going around in circles on a question I thought we'd settle quickly. The setup: standard conditionalization handles cases where you learn a proposition with certainty, and Jeffrey's rule handles cases where an experience shifts your credences across some partition without rendering any cell certain. The sticking point is learning that seems to arrive as a *constraint* rather than as an update to a partition — the classic shape being cases where what you learn is best reported as "my conditional credence in B given A has gone up," with no underlying proposition you can point to as the thing learned. Half the group insists this is just Jeffrey updating with a cleverly chosen partition, or else that the appearance of unpartitionable evidence dissolves once you're careful about what the experiential input actually was. The other half insists that no choice of partition recovers the intuitively right posterior, and that rational agents sometimes genuinely update on constraints, full stop. Both sides keep producing examples the other side calls misdescribed.

My hypothesis, which I'd like you to stress-test rather than accept, is that the debate has stalled because the two camps are actually disagreeing about what evidence *is* — propositions or distributions — and that every contested case is just this disagreement wearing a costume. Is that right, or is there a real formal question underneath: say, whether the constraint-based update and the best partition-based update provably diverge, and if so, which one satisfies the constraints that made conditionalization attractive in the first place (avoiding diachronic Dutch books, commutativity of successive updates, reflection-style principles)? I'd like a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is — verbal, normative, or a hidden theorem-shaped question — supported by what each side's best arguments actually presuppose, plus a verdict on what would count as progress: a proof, a principled criterion for when a partition *correctly* represents an experience, or an admission that the question needs to be reformulated. Please don't just survey positions; I want the structure of the impasse.
