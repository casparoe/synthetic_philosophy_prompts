Okay so I've been arguing with my brother about meritocracy and I want to get my own intutions straight before round two. Here's where I land on cases, tell me what principle (if any) I'm actually commited to:

- Two warehouse workers, same shift, same effort, but one is just naturally quicker and moves more boxes. Paying the quicker one more: fine.
- But saying the quicker one deserves more *praise* than the slower one: not fine. Praise should track how hard you try, not what comes out.
- A guy who coasted into a VP job at his dad's company and is genuinely good at it now: he can keep the job, whatever, but he doesn't *deserve* it the way someone who clawed their way up does.
- My friend who grinds insanely hard at coding but is honestly mediocre at it and keeps getting passed over: she deserves respect, maybe extra help, but not the same pay as the dev who's ten times better.
- Someone who started a company and got rich mostly because the market happened to explode that year: entitled to keep the money, but I wouldn't say they earned it the way the phrase "self-made" implies.
- Hiring: the most qualified candidate should get the job even if their qualifications come from fancy schooling they didn't earn. Anything else feels unfair to... someone, though I'm not sure who.

So my rough principle is: rewards track contribution, esteem tracks effort, and jobs track qualifications, and desert is basically about what you did with your cards, not the cards.

But here's the case that keeps bugging me: the only reason my hardworking friend *can* work hard is that her parents drilled discipline into her from age five, and mine never did, so I'm a slacker partly because of my upbringing too. If effort is also a card you were dealt, does my whole principle collapse, or is there a way to keep most of these verdicts? And which one of them would you bet I'd have to give up first?
