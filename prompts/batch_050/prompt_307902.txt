I teach a seminar on the metaphysics of mind and artificial systems, and two of my graduate students have spent the term at loggerheads. One holds that large language models are best understood as agents: systems with something like beliefs, goals, and dispositions, however alien their implementation. The other holds that they are simulators: engines that generate text by enacting characters or personas, such that any agency we detect belongs to the simulated persona rather than to the model itself, and that speaking of what the model "wants" is a category error. Draft the joint statement the two of them might produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign — concerning, for instance, the distinction between a base model and the assistant persona produced by fine-tuning, and what behavioral evidence does and does not establish; (2) a table of their remaining disagreements, each formulated so that the relevant student would accept the wording of their own position; (3) the cruxes — I suspect one is whether the model/persona distinction marks a real joint in nature or is merely a descriptive convenience, and another is what to make of cases where a single model enacts mutually inconsistent characters within one context — and for each crux, a concrete observation or experiment both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of their dispute is merely verbal, the statement should say so explicitly and explain why. The statement should not relitigate generic debates about machine consciousness; keep it focused on the agency-versus-simulation question as such.
