A philosophy podcast I listened to recently described the heterogeneity problem for hedonism as follows: "Hedonism holds that pleasure is the only thing good in itself. But pleasures are wildly diverse: the pleasure of a warm bath, of solving a proof, of hearing a joke land, of seeing an old friend. Introspection reveals no common sensation running through all of these. Since there is no single 'pleasure feeling' that all good experiences share, the hedonist's central posited entity turns out not to exist, and hedonism collapses. Some philosophers have tried to save it by saying that pleasure is just whatever we happen to desire, but that gets things backwards, since we desire things because they are pleasant rather than finding them pleasant because we desire them." Which parts of this summary would a defender of hedonism accept, and which misstate the dialectic? In particular: does the heterogeneity of pleasant experiences actually threaten hedonism as a theory of value, or only a particular account of what pleasure is? Is the "whatever we desire" response fairly characterized and fairly dismissed? And does the conclusion that hedonism collapses follow from anything accurate in the summary?
