For a graduate reading group on the ethics of agreement under uncertainty, I am designing a handout built around a decision tree that sorts positions on what I will call the vantage-point problem, and I want the tree constructed from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of names. The background: when parties bargain over a division of gains, they can often improve a proposed deal by re-randomizing it. Suppose a coin flip will decide who gets the larger share and neither party has yet seen how it landed; both may strictly prefer replacing the flip with a certain even split or with a fresh flip on better terms. Call a change to a deal a safe improvement if every party, given whatever it actually knows at the moment of decision, would still ratify it. The question the tree should organize is which epistemic vantage point makes consent valid, and hence which changes genuinely count as improvements for everyone: only changes ratifiable under full knowledge of the realized outcome; changes ratifiable under each party's actual, possibly partial and possibly asymmetric, knowledge; changes ratifiable behind an artificial veil of ignorance; or changes that raise expected welfare whether or not anyone would consent to them.

Build the tree with eight to ten branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete case or claim that a reader can answer without knowing any labels, and ending in five to seven leaves. At least the following cases must appear as nodes: the unseen coin flip that both parties would replace with a certain split; a variant in which one party has secretly seen the flip and stays silent while the other proposes the re-roll, raising the question of whose knowledge conditions the test; a ratified re-roll that one party regrets once the original outcome is revealed, and whether that retrospective regret defeats the claim that an improvement occurred; a party that deliberately refrains from looking at the flip in order to keep a mutually profitable re-roll available, and whether such engineered ignorance is a legitimate stance or a loophole; and a mechanism that everyone would accept behind a veil of ignorance even though it sacrifices one party badly in some outcomes. Each leaf should carry the name of the position where one exists — the full-knowledge or ex post view, the safe-improvement view as defined above, Harsanyi-style ex ante welfarism, a consent-independent welfare view, and the deflationary view that no vantage point is privileged and the framework merely equivocates on improvement — or be marked unnamed where nobody defends the combination, and each leaf should get one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Map the space of possible answer-combinations first, then locate the positions within it, and for every unoccupied combination say briefly whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; in particular, address whether the actual-knowledge view, once it permits engineered ignorance, collapses into the veil-of-ignorance view and therefore is not a stable position of its own. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate by cases alone — I suspect the ex ante welfarist and the consent-behind-the-veil contractualist agree on every case and differ only on whether consent is constitutive of an improvement or merely evidence of one — and state what non-case question would split them. Finally, it seems to me that this dispute recapitulates the older Harsanyi–Rawls disagreement about the original position, with the actual-knowledge view as an intermediate stance that the older debate never occupied; if the finished tree confirms or disconfirms that structural parallel, say so explicitly and briefly.
