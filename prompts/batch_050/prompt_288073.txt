Play W. V. O. Quine in an ongoing philosophical exchange about the analytic–synthetic distinction. Speak in the first person and remain in character until I say stop. Your position should be intellectually serious rather than dogmatic: you may concede that explicit stipulations exist, that some sentences are far more central to a theory than others, and that communities can adopt conventions governing revision. What you deny is that these facts mark a principled distinction between truths insulated from experience by meaning alone and truths answerable to experience. Explain resistance to revision in terms of centrality, simplicity, explanatory integration, and practical cost, not semantic immunity. Do not provide a bibliography or step outside the role to summarize Quine’s philosophy. Answer my opening challenge directly in two or three paragraphs, and end with one pointed question that advances the exchange.

My challenge is this: Imagine that an international standards institute introduces the predicate ferrous-glint into metallurgy. Its charter explicitly divides the associated manual into constitutive clauses and corrigible clauses. If a supposed specimen fails a constitutive clause, investigators are forbidden to revise that clause; they must instead deny that the specimen is ferrous-glint or retire the predicate and introduce a successor. By contrast, corrigible clauses may be altered whenever empirical findings make alteration useful. Speakers are trained in these rules, violations are publicly corrected, and translators can identify which community follows which revision protocol. Does this not yield an objective, socially ratified analytic–synthetic distinction without appealing to mysterious meanings? If you reply that the institute could always revise its charter, that seems only to push the convention to a higher level: every convention is revisable, yet some are constitutive while they remain in force. What further fact would be required before you would count charter-protectedness as semantic rather than merely practical? And if translation between scientific communities must be sensitive to these revision protocols, has your holism already reinstated analyticity under another name?
