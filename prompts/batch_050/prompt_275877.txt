I'm a junior in high school, and something from my physics class this semester has been nagging at me. Our teacher mentioned, almost in passing, that on several widely discussed cosmological models the universe is spatially infinite — which would mean infinitely many galaxies, plausibly infinitely many inhabited planets, and infinitely many sentient beings spread out past any horizon we could ever reach. I tried to turn my discomfort about this into a precise argument for our school's philosophy club, and I ended up with a conclusion I refuse to accept. The problem is that I can't find where the reasoning slips. Here is the argument as I wrote it up:

(1) One state of affairs is morally better than another only if it contains a greater total amount of welfare.
(2) If the universe contains infinitely many sentient beings, then whatever I do, the total amount of welfare in the universe is infinite.
(3) Adding any finite quantity to an infinite total does not make that total any larger: infinity plus a million is still just infinity.
(4) Therefore nothing I do — saving a life, sparing someone pain, anything within my power — makes the total amount of welfare in the universe any greater.
(5) Therefore, by (1), nothing I do makes any state of affairs morally better than another.
(6) But that cannot be right. I lifeguard at the community pool in the summers, and pulling a drowning child out of the water would obviously make things better than walking past.
(7) So at least one of (1) through (3) has to go.

A few things about where I stand. (6) is non-negotiable for me; I take it as fixed that some actions genuinely make things better. (3) feels like arithmetic rather than philosophy, so I'd be surprised if that's the culprit, though maybe my surprise is the point. (1) is the premise I'd most like to keep, because without some version of it I don't know what "better" could even mean. And I've noticed that just denying the universe is infinite feels like dodging — but I'm not sure.

Please tell me which step you reject and why it looked sound to me in the first place, and price the alternatives: what does each rejected premise cost, and is the cheapest option the one you'd actually take? One more thing: if the resolution turns on the universe being infinite, does the problem really go away if the universe is finite but merely enormous, or does some version of it come back? If it comes back, I'd like to know whether my original argument was ever really about infinity at all.
