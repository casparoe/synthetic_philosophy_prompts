I have no academic background in history or philosophy, but I have been turning over the old question of whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler, and I have come to think the question is usually asked too sloppily to be answerable. So let me try to fix that, and then I would like your credence, with the reasoning decomposed.

First, it seems to me there are at least two different counterfactuals being conflated: one where Hitler is never born, and one where he is removed later — say, killed in the failed putsch of 1923, by which point the movement exists but is small and has other figures in it. I suspect these give different answers, so please treat them separately.

Second, "a Nazi Germany" is doing a lot of work. I would break it into three graded outcomes, and I want a probability for each, under each of the two counterfactuals: (a) Germany ends up under an authoritarian, revanchist, antisemitic regime of some kind; (b) that regime launches a European war of conquest; (c) that regime carries out systematic, industrialised genocide of the Jews of Europe. My own intuition is that the probabilities drop sharply from (a) to (c) — that the structural conditions of the 1930s plausibly produce (a) without any particular man, but that (c) required something rarer. But I hold this loosely.

Please give me your six numbers with a line of justification each, then tell me: which of the three links in the chain your overall judgement is most sensitive to; what historical evidence, if you could have it, would shift that link most; and where you think the disagreement actually lives between a "Great Man" person who puts the full package at 0.1 and a structuralist who puts it at 0.8 — that is, which of the six terms they most likely differ on.

One further wrinkle I have been chewing on, which I have not seen discussed: perhaps the dispute confuses the replaceability of the role with the replaceability of the person. Structural arguments show that Weimar Germany had a vacancy — a part waiting to be cast — but a vacancy does not tell you how many actors could fill it. If the part demanded an unusual combination (genuine belief in the ideology, oratorical force, personal nerve, and a willingness to gamble everything early), then the structural argument might establish the vacancy while leaving Hitler nearly irreplaceable in it. Does this "casting problem" dissolve the Great Man versus structure dispute, or does it just relocate it — and does it change any of your numbers?
