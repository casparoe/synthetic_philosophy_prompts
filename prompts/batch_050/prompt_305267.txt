I’m a PhD student in bioethics preparing to defend a paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on dementia care. Act as a sharp but fair professional philosopher: brainstorm possible weak points first, then turn them into a practical Q&A prep sheet.

My thesis is that a clear advance directive refusing life-prolonging treatment during advanced dementia should normally prevail even if the later self appears content, because the earlier competent self has special authority over identity-constituting values. But that authority ends when the current self has strong, repeatable preferences going the other way and psychological continuity has become too thin for deference to count as respecting the same person. I call this a dual-authority threshold view.

The audience will include two dementia clinicians, a disability-studies scholar who is wary of treating cognitive change as a loss of personhood, a Kantian autonomy theorist, and someone working on elder law. Give me the eight objections they’re most likely to raise, phrased in their own vocabularies and ordered by likely damage. For each, say what I should concede, what I should hold, and give a two-minute reply. Also flag the objection I’m defintely hoping nobody asks. No bibliography or citations, please.
