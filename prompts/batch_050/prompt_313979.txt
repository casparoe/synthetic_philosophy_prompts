i'm running a small reading group on philosophy of mind and our last session of term is on hofstadter's strange loop idea — the brain builds a symbol for itself, the "I", and that symbol ends up sitting at the centre of the causal story it's meant to be merely describing, so you get this level-crossing loop where the self is both cause and effect of the goings-on below. i want to close the session by reading out a villanelle (19 lines, five tercets then a quatrain, two alternating refrains) and i want the form to do the philosophical work, not just decorate it: one refrain should belong to the "I" speaking from inside the loop, the other to the level beneath it, the neural churn that doesn't know it's an anyone, and across the poem the two refrains should drift towards each other so that when they come back together in the final quatrain they've become the same line, or very nearly, heard two ways. the poem should be spoken by the loop itself rather than about it, if that makes sense — first person, but a first person that keeps noticing it's made of something. no name-checks of hofstadter or gödel or escher; someone who knows the book should recognise the idea from how the poem behaves, someone who doesn't should still get a good unsettling poem out of it. don't resolve the eerie part at the end, i want them going home slightly bothered. british english spellings please, and keep it unsentimental — no dawn light, no whispers.
