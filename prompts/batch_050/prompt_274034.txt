I run a reading group on institutional design, and next month we're doing a session on multipolar traps and the Moloch dynamic -- the pattern, familiar from the widely circulated Slate Star Codex essay on the topic, in which individually rational competition forces every participant into behavior that leaves everyone worse off, and no one can unilaterally defect without being crushed. In preparation, I've been compiling a canonical list of examples to distribute beforehand, and I'd like your help finding one more. Please don't just explain the concept back to me; I know it well. What I want is a concrete new case.

Here is the list as it stands. First, military arms races between rival states, where each side's buildup is defensive from its own perspective and the equilibrium is expensive insecurity for all. Second, performance-enhancing drugs in elite sports, where a clean athlete either dopes or loses to those who do. Third, social media platforms and content creators competing for attention through ever more optimized outrage and engagement mechanics. Fourth, credential inflation in education and hiring, where everyone must acquire degrees and internships that mostly signal rather than train. Fifth, the overwork equilibrium in prestigious professions like law and finance, where visible hours function as a positional weapon. Sixth, regulatory races to the bottom, in which jurisdictions weaken labor, tax, or environmental standards to attract mobile capital. What I'm looking for is one additional trap that is not on this list -- and, importantly, not a re-skin of an item on it. A case that is just "competitors incur a costly signal to outrank each other in domain X" would, I suspect, collapse into items four or five, so I'd prefer something whose structure differs in an identifiable way: perhaps the losers and the decision-makers aren't the same people, or the trap operates within a single organization rather than between rivals, or the ratchet is driven by measurement rather than competition itself.

For whatever case you propose, please do three things. First, describe the trap concretely enough that I could check it against reality -- who the players are, what the per-step incentive is, and what the bad equilibrium looks like. Second, explain precisely why it isn't a variant of something on my list, and if you're honest with yourself and it partly is, say so and locate the overlap. Third, assess whether the standard escape hatches for multipolar traps -- enforceable coordination among the players, an outside authority changing the payoff matrix, or deliberately degrading the metric being competed over -- would actually work in your case, or whether the trap you've found is unusually resistant to them. If you conclude that your example isn't really a multipolar trap at all but a tragedy of the commons or a principal-agent failure wearing Moloch's clothes, that's a valuable answer too, provided you argue for it.
