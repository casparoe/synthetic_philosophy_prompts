A friend who runs a psychology reading group sent me her one-paragraph summary of higher-order theories of consciousness, which she adapted from a popular book:

"Higher-order theories hold that a mental state is conscious only when the subject has a thought about that state: you consciously feel pain only if you are also thinking that you are in pain. Consciousness, on this view, is the mind's internal self-monitoring — it scans its own states, and the states it manages to represent are the conscious ones. Two consequences follow. First, since having thoughts about one's own mental states requires possessing concepts of mental states, infants and most non-human animals are probably not conscious. Second, the theory explains why we can always report our conscious experiences: being conscious just is being available to thought and report. The standard objection — that we can have false thoughts about our own states, so the theory implies conscious experiences that don't exist — is generally taken to refute the view."
