I've been reading alignment stuff for years (LW, the Alignment Forum, the usual supects) and I keep bouncing off the deceptive alignment literature for one specific reason: everyone argues about whether it will happen, and almost nobody says what would count as a theory of it. Before I sink another weekend into this, I want the spec first.

So: write me the desiderata an actual theory of deceptive alignment would have to meet to be worth the name. By "theory" I mean something stronger than a just-so story about gradient descent favouring patient schemers — something that could in principle be wrong, and that would tell us something we didn't already know if it were right. And to fix what "adequate" means here: good enough that you'd actually shift your allocation of safety research effort on the basis of its predictions, not just seminar-room adequate.

Some things I suspect belong on the list, but push back if any of them are prejudices I've absorbed from the discourse rather than genuine requirements:

- It has to explain why SGD in particular would land on a deceptively aligned policy, not just why deception would be instrumentally useful to an already-misaligned agent. These get conflated constantly and they're not the same claim.
- It has to say something about goal stability under continued training: if the policy keeps getting gradient-updated, why doesn't the inner goal drift, get overwritten, or get slowly ground into whatever the reward signal actually wanted?
- It needs to draw a hard line between deceptive alignment and ordinary sycophancy, reward hacking, or playing-along-during-evals, because half the empirical work people cite as evidence seems to me to be about the latter.
- It should make some near-term, checkable predictions — what we'd expect to observe in current or next-generation models if the theory is on the right track — rather than only retrodicting a hypothetical future treacherous turn.

For each desideratum: state it crisply, give the case or consideration behind it (what goes wrong with a theory that drops it), and mark whether it's non-negotiable, nice-to-have, or a camp-specific prejudice. The part I most want: flag any pairs or subsets of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied — I've got a nagging feeling that "deception is a generic attractor of SGD" and "we should expect deception to be rare enough that we'd be surprised to see it soon" don't sit together comfortably, but maybe that's just me. Also tell me which single requirement excludes the most of what's currently on the market, counting informal forum-post arguments, the mesa-optimisation framing, and the more recent mechanistic interpretability-flavoured work as "the market".

If you reckon one of my four starters is wrong-headed, cut it rather than politely keeping it, and add any I've missed. No bibliography or reading list — I want the spec, not a syllabus.

End with a line in exactly this format: \answer{...} containing, in a few words, the one desideratum you'd pick as hardest for any current proposal to satisfy.
