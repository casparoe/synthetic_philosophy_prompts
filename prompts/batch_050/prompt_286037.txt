Consider the animal welfare law of a typical wealthy country and the curious shape of its concern. The same legal system that makes it an offence to cause unnecessary suffering to a pet mouse permits that very species, reclassified as vermin, to be poisoned or trapped in ways that would be prosecuted if anyone's pet endured them. It regulates the slaughter of cattle and chickens down to the approved methods of stunning, while commercially caught fish are killed in their millions with scarcely a welfare rule to their name. In at least some such countries it has lately been persuaded to extend formal recognition of sentience to octopuses, crabs and lobsters — a chef there may no longer boil a lobster alive — yet it leaves insects wholly outside its scope: no licence is needed for experiments on fruit flies that would require one for zebrafish, and a company opening an industrial cricket farm discovers that no welfare standard applies at any stage from rearing to killing. At the bottom of the scale, meanwhile, nobody at all proposes welfare standards for thermostats or for the robot vacuum that keeps bumping into the chair. What theory of moral status does this patchwork presuppose, if it presupposes one at all? Does the law treat sentience as a threshold a creature either clears or does not — and if so, why are its lines drawn where they are — or does it treat status as a matter of degree, in which case why is protection itself binary, in or out, rather than graded? Which rival foundations does the practice quietly rule out: a precautionary view that protects in proportion to the probability of suffering, a view on which status tracks the richness of possible experience, the old claim that our duties regarding animals are really duties about human character? Are the parts even consistent with one another, or does the asymmetry between pet and pest presuppose something the slaughter rules deny, and the solicitude for lobsters something the neglect of crickets denies? What would concretely have to change if the law were rebuilt on each rival foundation in turn? And where is the practice simply underdetermined — a sediment of public sympathy and industry pressure, compatible with several theories and committed to none — rather than genuinely presupposing anything?
