So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I keep falling down the Maxwell's demon rabbit hole via the whole "information is physical" thing. The position I want stress-tested is the standard Landauer/Bennett resolution of the demon: the demon can't beat the second law because eventually it has to reset its memory, and erasing one bit of information dumps at least kT ln 2 of heat into the environment, wich cancels out whatever entropy reduction the demon achieved by sorting fast and slow molecules. So on this view the demon is exorcised not by the measurement (which supposedly can be done reversibly and for free) but by the cleanup.

What would count against this position? I want a real map of its exposure, not a textbook recap of the demon story. Some specific things I'd like covered:

- The compression loophole, which bugs me the most as someone who thinks in bits all day. Erasure is only forced if the memory has to get back to a blank state. Why can't the demon just compress its records instead of erasing them? My vague understanding is that in the single-cycle case the measurement outcomes are effectively random and therefore incompressible on average, so compression gains you nothing. Is that actually a theorem, or is it quietly assumed? And what about a demon running many cycles that accumulates records and compresses their statistics — does the amortization change anything?
- The random tape case: if the demon's memory starts full of genuinely random bits instead of blank ones, it seems like it can use those as fuel and come out ahead. Does the resolution survive that, or does it just relocate the cost to wherever the random tape gets manufactured?
- What if the demon just never erases — infinite memory, or we simply let it end the process with a dirty memory? Is the position then committed to "the second law only applies to cyclic processes," and is that an honest concession or a dodge that makes the whole thing toothless?
- The status of Landauer's principle itself. I've seen claims (I think Norton argues something like this?) that the principle has never been derived from plain statistical mechanics without circularity — that the proofs smuggle in the link between logical irreversibility and thermodynamic irreversibility that they're supposed to establish. If that critique lands, does the whole exorcism collapse, or is there a fallback route (measurement costs, fluctuation-theorem stuff like the generalized second law people prove for feedback-controlled systems) that doesn't go through erasure at all?
- The experiments: people have built single-bit erasure setups with colloidal particles and measured dissipation near kT ln 2. How much does that actually confirm? Counterfactual check: if those experiments had come out the other way, would defenders have conceded defeat, or just said the setup wasn't a truly minimal erasure? I.e., is the position risking anything empirically, or is it insulated?
- What do defenders of the resolution themselves concede would count against it — as opposed to what the critics say? Please sort the defeaters by how decisive they'd be: clean refutation vs. merely awkward vs. "this just moves the bump in the carpet."

One ground rule: don't just tell me the demon is impossible because the second law is true — that's the thing in dispute. I want to know which step of the exorcism argument is load-bearing, and what the world would have to look like for that specific step to fail.
