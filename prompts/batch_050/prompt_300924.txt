I'm deciding whether to leave a stable engineering job to join a small startup working on a genuinely new kind of product with no close predecessors on the market. A friend who studied economics tells me I'm being irrational unless I assign the startup a probability of success and compare expected outcomes, and he's pressed me to just commit to a number -- say, 30 percent. I've tried, but I can't shake the feeling that any figure I write down here is a different kind of thing from the odds of a coin flip, or even from the odds that an established vendor ships a feature on time: those feel grounded in something, while mine feels like a guess dressed up in numerical clothing. My friend insists a probability is just a degree of belief, that the 30 percent is no worse than any other estimate, and that refusing to state a number is evasion rather than rigor. Who's right? If some uncertainties really can't be put on the same scale as coin flips, what follows for how I should actually make this decision -- and what, if anything, is lost or distorted when I force a precise number anyway?
