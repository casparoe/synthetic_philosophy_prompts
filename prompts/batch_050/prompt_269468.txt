I work in local government (permitting and infrastructure, nothing glamorous) and I keep running into the same puzzle wearing different costumes, and I want to know if philosophers already have a name for the specific version I keep seeing.

The general question — can an enormous number of tiny harms add up to outweigh one big harm — I know has been chewed over, there's that famous thought experiment with torture versus dust specks in people's eyes. But my cases have a wrinkle I never see addressed. The tiny harms aren't just small, they're each below the point where the person who suffers them would even call it a harm. Two real examples from the last couple years. One: a proposed bypass would add roughly ninety seconds to the daily commute of around 200,000 people. If you do the honest arithmetic on total lost time, it comes out to something like several entire human lifespans per year, forever. But no individual commuter would say they've been wronged, most wouldnt consciously notice, and if you polled them they'd shrug. Two: a factory whose low hum measurably degrades average sleep quality across a whole town on aggregate health data, but no resident can point to a single night and say the factory ruined it — the effect only exists in the statistics.

Here's what I can't sort out. Some of my colleagues say an unfelt harm isn't a harm, so a billion of them sum to zero, case closed. Others say the total is real even if every part is invisible, the lifespans don't stop being lost just because they're lost in ninety-second slices. And a third person on our board argues something slipperier — that each slice is a harm but the *adding* move itself breaks down, because the units stop meaning the same thing at that scale, like adding grams of weight to get a unit of loudness.

So: does this specific configuration — aggregation of sub-noticeable or complaint-proof harms, pitted against concentrated harms — have an established name or a literature of its own? How exactly does it differ from the standard dust specks setup, where the speck is at least felt as a speck? I don't just want a label, I want to know where the edges are. Does it matter whether the person *could* notice if they paid attention, versus couldn't notice no matter what? Does it matter whether the harm shows up only in population statistics and never in any biography? And if nobody has named it, what would be a decent name and definition that carves it off cleanly from both the original thought experiment and from plain old "many small costs versus one big cost" cost-benefit accounting?
