Alright, background first. I spent 35 years as a condensed matter physicist, retired last spring, and in a moment of weakness I picked up a metaphysics book at a library sale. Now I'm stuck on the identity of indiscernables — Leibniz's idea that two things exactly alike in every qualitative respect can't really be two things, there's just one. And then Max Black's counterexample with the two perfectly identical spheres floating in an otherwise empty universe, apparently meant to show you can have two things sharing every property. Back in the lab we dealt with "identical" electrons all the time and nobody lost sleep over it, but in philosophy this turns into a big fight about whether objects have some bare "thisness" beyond their properties, whether spatial position counts as a property, and Leibniz's business about God needing a sufficient reason for everything.

My reading group has a tradition where each month somebody presents the topic as a comedy piece, and my turn is up. So write me an internal audit memo from a fictional Office of Ontological Efficiency, a dreary bureaucratic body tasked with eliminating redundancy from the universe. The office has audited Black's two-sphere universe and flagged it as a violation: two objects, one property profile, pure waste of being. Deadpan corporate-memo register throughout — findings, property inventory, a compliance deadline, an appeals process, the works.

The humor should come from the actual philosophy, not generic silliness:

- The auditors constantly need to refer to the spheres individually but can't, because any distinguishing description would itself be a qualitative difference. So the memo ties itself in knots with devices like "the sphere (hereafter 'the sphere')", "Sphere 1 / Sphere 2 (labels provisional and themselves under audit)", cross-references to footnotes that resolve nothing.
- The sufficient-reason angle: there's no reason for either sphere to be where it is rather than where its twin is, and no reason for the pair to be arranged one way rather than its mirror image, so the corrective-action section follows Leibniz's logic all the way down and recommends deleting both spheres and reinstating the void, presented cheerfully as a 100% reduction in redundancy.
- An appendix on the appeal filed on behalf of the spheres, which pleads numerical distinctness via haecceity or thisness; the office denies the appeal on the grounds that thisness is not a recognized line item and cannot be entered into the property ledger.
- A footnote where the office considers counting spatiotemporal location as a property and regretfully rejects it, since in an otherwise empty universe the locations are defined only relative to the other sphere and so come out identical too.

Around 700 words, formatted like a real memo with headers and footnotes. The satire should land because the office takes Leibniz completely seriously and follows his princple to a conclusion he'd hate — a specialist should be able to tell the writer actually understands what PII, PSR, and haecceity are doing in the debate. Never break character, no winking, no "this is satire" disclaimer.
