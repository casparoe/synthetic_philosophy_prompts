I spend a fair amount of time on LessWrong and adjacent blogs, and one lesson I've absorbed from that world is that a lot of confused philosophy dissolves once you notice that two descriptions are just the same situation relabeled -- the map is not the territory, and sometimes two maps are one map. So I was interested to stumble onto a corner of physics where this move apparently doesn't go through cleanly, or at least where serious people seem to disagree about whether it goes through. In general relativity, as I understand it, you can take a solution to the field equations and produce a second solution by smoothly reshuffling which point gets which field values, but only inside some bounded region that is empty of matter, leaving everything outside the region untouched. Physicists seem to treat the two solutions as the same physical universe. But someone -- I think this traces back to Einstein himself, who was briefly troubled by it -- pointed out that if you instead insist the two are genuinely different situations, then the laws fail to fix what happens inside the region even given complete information about everything outside it, which looks like a catastrophic breakdown of determinism for no physical reason. So the options appear to be: accept that the relabeled universe is just the same universe, or accept a bizarre indeterminism.

What I want to know is whether this whole configuration has a name, and whether the underlying philosophical fork has named positions. I can see that it's in the neighborhood of several things I already have concepts for, but it doesn't seem to be any of them exactly. It's not merely the trivial point that physics is invariant under a change of coordinates, because the reshuffling here isn't a redescription in new coordinates -- both solutions are stated in the same coordinate system, and they genuinely assign different values at the same points. It's close to gauge redundancy in electromagnetism, where different vector potentials give the same observable fields, but there the two descriptions agree on everything measurable, whereas here the two solutions disagree point-by-point about the metric itself, which is normally the physical content of the theory. And it rhymes with Leibniz's old argument that shifting the whole universe three feet to the left changes nothing, but that argument is usually taken to be about uniform translations of everything, while this one is about a localized rearrangement that leaves the surroundings fixed. Am I right that this is a distinct thing with its own literature? If so, what is the standard taxonomy of responses -- is "the two solutions are one universe" itself a named thesis, and is "no, they're distinct and we must live with the consequences" a position anyone actually defends? And if I'm wrong and it really does collapse into one of the neighboring concepts, I'd like to know which one and why.
