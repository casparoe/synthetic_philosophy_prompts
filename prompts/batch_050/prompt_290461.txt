I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a long feature about artificial systems that check their own reasoning: verifiers, self-critique loops, models asked to certify their own outputs. Every technically minded researcher I interview eventually reaches for the same obstacle. Löb's theorem, and Gödel's second incompleteness theorem behind it, seem to show that any sufficiently strong formal reasoner cannot establish its own soundness from the inside; and the obvious patch, simply assuming that one's own reasoning is trustworthy, just relocates the problem one level up rather than solving it. Before I sit down with the people proposing workarounds, I want to know what I should actually be asking of them.

Write the desiderata a theory of self-trust for a reasoning agent must meet to count as an answer to this obstacle rather than a restatement of it. I want the specification, with the consideration behind each requirement: which phenomena the theory must explain (why naive self-verification collapses; why bounded agents like humans seem to get away with a kind of self-trust that formal systems provably cannot), which cases it must get right (an agent deferring to a future, slightly modified successor of itself; an agent trusting a simpler subroutine it can fully inspect; an agent that can only trust a strictly weaker system), and which distinctions it must respect (trusting a procedure versus trusting its outputs; consistency versus soundness; idealized provability versus computationally bounded reasoning). Say which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which look to you like the preferences of particular research camps.

The part I most want to see is whether some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given Löb's theorem itself. If an agent cannot simultaneously be a classical enough reasoner for the theorem to apply, fully trust its own inference rules, and remain consistent, then the desiderata list is itself the story and my interviews should be organized around which horn each researcher grabs. Say plainly if that's the situation.

Then, briefly, run the main families of workarounds against the list: infinite towers of systems in which each level trusts only the level below, theories with reflection principles added as axioms, probabilistic or quantitative self-models in place of proof-theoretic ones, and the pragmatic position that refuses to formalize trust at all. No bibliography and no citations; I'll chase sources myself. And note where a scientifically literate but non-specialist reader is most likely to be misled by each candidate, since that's the failure mode my editor will flag.
