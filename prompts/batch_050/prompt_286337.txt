I work in economic theory, mostly mechanism design and repeated games, and a colleague recently insisted that Kant's Perpetual Peace anticipates ideas I use every day. I can half-see it: the preliminary articles read like restrictions on the strategy space (no secret reservations appended to treaties, standing armies as a commitment problem, war financed by credit as a way of externalizing costs onto future players), and the definitive articles read like claims about which institutional arrangements are self-enforcing. But I keep sliding between a loose analogical reading and a strict formal one, and I'd like the translation done properly by someone who takes both sides seriously.

So: restate Kant's argument in Perpetual Peace -- the six preliminary articles, the three definitive articles (republican constitutions for every state, a federation of free states rather than a world state, cosmopolitan right limited to hospitality), and his claim that nature's mechanism pushes toward peace through self-interest even where moral motivation is absent, including his notorious remark that the problem is solvable even for a race of devils provided they are intelligent -- in the vocabulary I actually work in: stage games and repeated games, discount factors, incentive compatibility, credible versus incredible commitments, cheap talk versus costly signals, equilibrium selection, and enforcement. Be explicit about which Kantian article becomes which formal object, and flag every place where you have to fudge: where an article resists formalization, where Kant relies on something my vocabulary either treats as a primitive or simply ignores (the distinction between legality and morality, the requirement that maxims of action be compatible with publicity, the teleology of nature, the idea that hospitality is a right and not merely a cooperative outcome).

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. What survives in Kant's argument that has no counterpart in game-theoretic vocabulary, and what should I conclude from that? Two diagnoses interest me: that the residue is load-bearing content my framework cannot express (for example, that republican citizens act on reasons and not merely on incentives, or that the publicity condition is a constraint on justification rather than on information), or that the residue is vagueness in Kant that the translation has usefully exposed. Take a position -- don't just inventory the residue. And on the devils remark specifically: is Kant's claim, in my terms, just the folk theorem plus mechanism design, i.e., that cooperation is sustainable among patient rational egoists given the right institutions, or is there something in it that resists that reading once you look closely?
