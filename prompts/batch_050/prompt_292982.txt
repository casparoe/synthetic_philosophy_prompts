I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, and I keep ending up in this argument with my uncle (religious, I'm not) about whether religion actually makes people behave better. His line is basically: look around, the secular countries only work because they're coasting on leftover Christian norms, and once those wear off you get low trust and everyone defects on each other. My line was: Scandinavia seems fine? But honestly I was arguing vibes. I'm an engineer, I want to treat this like an estimation problem instead of a vibes fight.

So here's the proposition I want your credence on, stated as precisely as I can manage: if you took a currently secular, high-trust Western country and magically doubled its rate of sincere religious belief and practice (not just nominal affiliation), average prosocial behavior and generalized social trust would be meaningfully higher ten years later. Give me an actual number, not "it depends" — and then decompose it. I figure the credence has to ride on several sub-questions: whether religiosity even correlates with prosociality once you control for the obvious stuff, whether any of that correlation is causal (does belief change behavior, or do prosocial people join communities, religious or not), whether effects that show up at the individual level scale to societal trust, and the annoying definitional one — whether "more moral" means more generous in general or just nicer to the in-group, because I suspect my uncle and I are using different scoreboards. Put a rough probability on each sub-question, show how they combine into your final number, and tell me which sub-question the final answer is most sensitive to — i.e., which one, if the evidence landed the other way, would move your credence the most.

One more thing: please be honest about the state of the evidence. Where the research is reasonably settled (or at least where careful people mostly agree), say so, and where it's genuinely contested — replication worries, cross-cultural problems, WEIRD samples, whatever — flag it as contested rather than presenting one side as the consensus. I'd rather get a 0.45 with the weak joints labeled than a confident 0.8 built on studies that turned out to be shaky. My own credence going in is maybe 0.25, so if you land far from that, also tell me where you think my number is most likely wrong.
