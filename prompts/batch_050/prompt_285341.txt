I'm building a taxonomy for a grad seminar of the proposed answers to the democratic boundary problem -- the question of who should get a say in a collective decision, where you can't just point to a prior democratic vote to settle membership without circularity. Here's what I've got so far: (1) the all-affected-interests principle (everyone whose interests are touched by the decision gets a say, however far-flung they are); (2) the all-coerced principle (everyone subject to the coercive apparatus that will enforce the outcome); (3) stake-weighted versions of either, where your say scales with how much is at stake for you; (4) national self-determination answers, where the demos is whoever shares the relevant collective identity and democracy just presupposes that group rather than constituting it; (5) brute-force answers, where the boundaries are wherever power happened to draw them and any principled-sounding answer is rationalization after the fact. I want one more answer that isn't a restatement, special case, or hybrid of these five. Note that just splitting (1) from (2) decision-by-decision versus polity-wide doesn't count as new either. Then tell me honestly whether your new answer survives the first objection anyone would raise to it, whatever that turns out to be.
