I'm a machine learning researcher working on representation learning and interpretability, with a working vocabulary of learned representations, embeddings, linear and nonlinear probes, feature geometry, world models, and distributional semantics. I keep noticing a surface resemblance between Richard Rorty's antirepresentationalism -- his attack, developed across Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature and the later work on contingency, on the picture of mind and language as mirroring an intrinsically structured world, with truth understood as accuracy of that mirroring -- and live debates in my field about whether neural networks genuinely "represent" features of the world or merely encode statistical regularities that prove useful. I want to know whether that resemblance is deep or spurious.

Please restate Rorty's position in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. That includes at least: his claim that vocabularies are tools rather than attempts at correspondence to the world's own joints; his treatment of the contingency of language, self, and community; his Davidson-inspired insistence that the world does not itself privilege one description over another; and his replacement of epistemology-centered philosophy with a picture of coping and social practice. For each component, state it first in Rorty's own terms and then in the vocabulary of representation learning -- for instance, whether "the world does not privilege a description" becomes a claim about the underdetermination of feature geometry by training data, or about the absence of a canonical coordinate system in embedding space.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about. Which of Rorty's claims translate cleanly into claims about learned representations? Which translate only with distortion -- and be specific about what gets bent in the translation? Which have no expression in my vocabulary at all: I'm particularly suspicious that the normative and political dimensions of his view, and his anti-essentialism about the representational relation itself rather than about particular representations, may have no counterpart in a framework that takes "representation" as a technical primitive. Where translation fails, say whether you think my vocabulary is missing something Rorty saw, or whether the untranslatable remainder is a sign that part of his position is less substantive than its rhetoric suggests. Do not smuggle my framework's assumptions into the translation and call the result faithful -- if Rorty would reject the very setup in which, say, a probe is taken to discover what a model "really represents," flag that rather than smoothing it over.

Conclude with a verdict: is the resemblance I noticed a genuine structural parallel that cuts in one direction -- for example, evidence that interpretability research inherits a confused picture of representation -- or is it the kind of analogy that survives only until you look at the residue? Please write in American English.
