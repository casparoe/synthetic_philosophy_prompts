I'm a functional programmer who ended up in a philosophy reading group (don't ask, it started as a joke and now I'm stuck going every other week). Last session we did Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and I keep having the strong feeling that this whole problem just... doesn't arise in the vocab I think in. The puzzle, as I got it: take the proposition that Socrates is wise. It's apparently not enough to have the constituents Socrates and wisdom sitting next to each other like items in a list, because a list of constituents isn't a unified proposition that says anything, so you seem to need a further relation connecting them, and then something to connect that relation to the relata, and off you go on the regress. Frege's move with the "unsaturated" concept that completes itself, and Wittgenstein's thing about objects hanging together like links in a chain, were floated as attempted escapes.

Here's what I actually want: translate this debate, as faithfully as you can, into the vocabulary I live in -- typed lambda calculus, function application, currying, constructors, the distinction between an expression and its value, maybe dependent types if they earn their keep. In my world, application isn't itself a term you can pass around and re-apply; `f x` isn't `f` and `x` plus some third thing called application sitting in the expression tree, it's just... what the tree does. So my suspicion is the regress never gets started for us, because we never reify the gluing operation as one more thing that itself needs gluing. But I want the honest version, not the version that flatters me. For each piece of the philosophical problem -- the mere list objection, the regress step, the unsaturatedness move, the chain metaphor -- say whether it maps cleanly onto something in the type-theoretic picture, whether you had to fudge anything to force the fit, and what's left over with no counterpart at all. Then the part I care about most: does the residue mean my vocabulary silently presupposes the very unity the philosophers are trying to explain (i.e. the type checker inherits it from somewhere and I just never look), or does it mean the original problem was an artifact of treating relations like ordinary constituents, and type theory simply refuses that mistake by design? If you think it's some mix, say which parts go which way, and don't be afraid to tell me my instinct is wrong.
