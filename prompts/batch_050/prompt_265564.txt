The Fermi paradox — the tension between the apparent abundance of potentially life-bearing worlds and the total absence of observed extraterrestrial civilizations — has attracted many proposed resolutions. The ones I am already familiar with, and do not want repeated or merely rephrased, are these: the Rare Earth hypothesis, according to which complex life requires an improbable confluence of geological and astronomical conditions; the suggestion that some Great Filter lies ahead of us, so that technological civilizations reliably destroy themselves before becoming detectable or expansionist; the observation that a civilization's era of detectable radio leakage may be extremely brief, since efficient communication technologies quickly replace omnidirectional broadcast, making temporal overlap between detectable phases unlikely; the zoo hypothesis, under which advanced civilizations deliberately refrain from contact; the possibility that interstellar travel or colonization is simply never economical relative to any civilization's planning horizon, however long-lived; and the idea that we are listening incorrectly, because mature civilizations compress and encrypt their transmissions until they are indistinguishable from noise. Please propose one further resolution that differs in its underlying mechanism from all of the above — not a rewording, special case, or combination of them, and not the bare suggestion that aliens are hiding or that we have already been visited. Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: the first item should state the proposed resolution precisely; the second should explain why it is not a variant of anything on my list; the third should give the strongest objection to it; the fourth should give the best available reply to that objection; and the fifth should give your honest verdict on whether the resolution is actually any good.
