So I'm prepping a seminar session (or maybe a long essay, havent decided) on theories of punishment and I want to stress-test the whole retributivism vs consequentialism framing against a real case. The case: Norway after July 22, 2011. Anders Behring Breivik murdered 77 people, most of them teenagers at a political youth camp on Utøya. Norway's response: he was tried, found criminally responsible, and sentenced to 21 years of preventive detention, the maximum available, extendable in five-year increments for as long as he's deemed dangerous, so potentially forever. Meanwhile he lives in conditions that by international standards are bizarrely comfortable, he sued the state over his isolation and partly won at first instance before losing on appeal, and he gets parole hearings where he shows up doing nazi salutes and gets denied. And the public mood never really turned toward vengeance -- the political response was essentially a doubling down on openness and democracy rather than a crackdown.

Here's the passage I want the analysis to pivot on. Nietzsche, On the Genealogy of Morals, Second Essay, section 2, in the Horace Samuel translation (public domain), on how humans were made into responsible agents:

"man, with the help of the morality of customs and of social strait-waistcoats, was made genuinely calculable [...] then do we find as the ripest fruit on its tree the sovereign individual, that resembles only himself, that has got loose from the morality of custom, the autonomous "super-moral" individual (for "autonomous" and "moral" are mutually exclusive terms),—in short, the man of the personal, long, and independent will, competent to promise"

What I want, in order:

1. The standard mapping first. Run the Norwegian response through both theories. The consequentialist reading is easy (rehabilitation, incapacitation via the extendable detention, Norway's famously low recidivism). But where exactly does retributivism break on this case -- is 21-years-extendable a desert-based sentence wearing a consequentialist costume, or does retributivism just have nothing to say once a system caps punishment this low for this much harm? A Kant-style retributivist would say Norway failed the victims. Is that right, and should the total absence of a public revenge mood figure in that judgment at all?

2. The Nietzsche pivot. If the responsible, promise-competent agent is a manufactured product, the ripest fruit of a long and originally violent process of making humans calculable, what does that do to the debate in part 1? Retributivism seems to treat responsibility as a metaphysical given; Nietzsche treats it as a social achievement with a bloody backstory. Does that undercut desert, or just relocate it? And notice that the Norwegian prison system is explicitly in the business of producing calm, normality-adapted, calculable citizens, which makes it look uncannily like the morality-of-customs project continued by gentler means. Is rehabilitative punishment the social strait-waistcoat minus the cruelty? Would Nietzsche read Norway as moral progress or as the same old breeding program with better PR?

3. The awkward fit: Breivik himself. He styled himself as a sovereign actor answerable to nobody, a grotesque parody of the individual who has "got loose from the morality of custom." Does the case show that Nietzsche's category can't distinguish autonomy from monstrosity, or does it show the opposite, that Breivik is precisely what the sovereign individual is not?

4. Now run it backwards, case against theory. Over a decade on: no reoffending, no vigilante problem, no durable public appetite for harsher law, system intact. Is that a confirmation of consequentialism, an anomoly for retributivism (if punishment this lenient for crimes this monstrous didn't produce the outrage and instability desert-theorists sometimes predict, what does?), or a case neither theory anticipated because both assume punishment is society's answer to crime, whereas Norway arguably treated the punishment as the least important part of its answer?

Be disciplined about the mapping throughout, and where a theory just goes silent on some feature of the case, say so instead of forcing it to have an opinion.
