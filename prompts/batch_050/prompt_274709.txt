Write the FAQ page that the administrators of the prize in Kavka's toxin puzzle would publish. Recall the setup: an eccentric billionaire offers a large cash prize to anyone who, at midnight tonight, sincerely intends to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow; the toxin would make you miserably ill for a day but is not dangerous, the billionaire's intention-detector is infallible, and whether you actually drink the toxin is irrelevant — only the midnight intention counts. Format the page as roughly 600 words of chipper, reassuring customer-service prose ("Frequently Asked Questions," "We're here to help!"), fielding the questions the puzzle's readers actually ask: Can I win by forming the intention while quietly planning not to drink? What if, at midnight, I realize I already have the money coming and drinking would be irrational — does that realization disqualify me? Do you recommend hypnotists, hired enforcers, or signing contracts with penalties? A winner's testimonial section is optional but encouraged. The humor must come from the real structure of the puzzle: every answer should follow the foundation's official logic to a place the reader can see is absurd, without the foundation ever noticing. Stay deadpan — no winking — but a reader who knows the philosophical literature on intention, rationality, and precommitment should recognize that each cheerfully unhelpful answer corresponds to an actual move in the debate, and should be able to reconstruct the paradox from the FAQ alone.
