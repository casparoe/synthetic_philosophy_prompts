Ok so I've been reading about Hofstadter's superrationality stuff, the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery where like, everyone has to decide independently whether to enter, and the prize only pays out (or is worth the most) if very few people enter. And here's what I don't get. Hofstadter's whole point is that superrational players all reason the same way and so end up making the same choice, right? So in the Platonia case, since my reasoning is identical to everyone else's, whatever I decide, everyone else decides too. If I send in an entry, everyone sends one, nobody wins. If I don't send, nobody sends, nobody wins either. So the dilemma is actually rigged so that no one can ever win it, and the rational move is to just ignore it.

But then people say the "correct" superrational solution is to enter with probability 1/N or whatever, using dice. That makes no sense to me though? The whole premise is you can't correlate with the other players except through shared reasoning, and dice aren't reasoning, they're just noise. If I'm allowed to roll a die then the situation isn't symmetric anymore and the superrationality argument doesn't apply. So isn't the dice thing just smuggling in ordinary game theory while pretending it's still superrationality? Like, either you take the "everyone does what I do" idea seriously, in which case the lottery is hopeless, or you don't, in which case why call it superrational at all? Which is it?
