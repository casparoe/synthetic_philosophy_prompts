Write a villanelle in the voice of Maxwell's demon, stationed at its little door between the two chambers, in which the two refrains are the demon's boast -- that it sorts the fast from the slow and makes heat run uphill for free -- and the quiet counter-line it would rather not hear, about the cost of forgetting. The poem should let the demon gloat through the first five tercets as the temperature difference grows, but each return of the second refrain should edge closer to the truth: the demon must keep a record of which molecules it let through, that record is physical, and erasing it to make room for new measurements dissipates exactly the entropy the sorting seemed to save. In the final quatrain, where the refrains meet, the demon should understand at last that its memory, not its trapdoor, was where the second law was hiding. Someone who knows the Szilard engine and Landauer's principle should recognize each stage of the argument in the form itself; someone who doesn't should still read a good poem about a creature undone by its own bookkeeping.
