I've been reading Hofstadter's columns on superrationality and the Luring Lottery, and I don't get why people say the solution involves randomizing. The whole point of superrational reasoning is that everyone thinks alike and lands on the same answer, so the cooperative solution is obviously that everyone enters with certainty — a guaranteed share of the prize split among everyone beats the risk that everybody stays home and the million goes unclaimed, right? Randomizing seems like defecting with extra steps. So where does this supposed "correct" entry probability of around 1/N come from, and why wouldn't entering for sure be the dominant superrational strategy? Also does the same argument carry over to the Platonia dilemma, where it seems even more clearly better if everyone submits?
