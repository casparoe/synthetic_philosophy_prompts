A friend who writes an economics blog gave me this summary of Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility theory, and I want to know whether to trust it: "Standard expected utility theory forces all aversion to risk into the shape of the utility function, which conflates two separate things: how much you value the goods at stake and how you feel about taking chances. Buchak's fix is to introduce a 'risk function' that distorts the probability of each outcome in proportion to how bad that outcome would be for you -- pessimists inflate the probabilities of the bad outcomes, and so cautious agents end up preferring gambles whose worst case is tolerable. Because the risk function is a matter of personal temperament rather than belief or desire, two agents with identical credences and identical utilities can rationally choose different acts. The upshot is that the Allais preferences, long treated either as a paradox or as a refutation of expected utility theory, come out perfectly rational -- which shows that the independence axiom at the heart of orthodox theory was simply mistaken." The broad motivation sounds right to me, but the mechanics described in the middle sound off, and I'm unsure whether the closing verdict on the independence axiom follows from the rest. Which parts of this summary would a careful exponent of the view accept, which parts conflate distinct claims or overstate what the theory actually delivers, and does the summary's conclusion about Allais survive once those errors are corrected?
