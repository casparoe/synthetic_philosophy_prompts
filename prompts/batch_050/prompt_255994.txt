Is there an established name for the following kind of apology? I have been reading official state apologies for historical wrongs, and a particular structure keeps recurring that I cannot find a label for.

Two instances. First, consider apologies for the forced removal of indigenous children from their families, as issued by governments in Australia and Canada. The wording typically expresses profound sorrow and regret and acknowledges that great suffering occurred. But read closely, the suffering tends to appear as something that happened — children were removed, families were broken, harm was done — with the agent of the removal kept grammatically offstage, or with the policy described as misguided rather than wrong: a program whose effects were tragic, presented almost as if the tragedy were a terrible side effect rather than the point. Second, consider some of the official Japanese statements on wartime conduct: sorrow and remorse are expressed, yet subsequent gestures by officials — visits to controversial shrines, revisions to school textbooks — are taken by neighboring states as retracting the apology in practice, and the cycle of apology and grievance repeats.

What interests me is the specific gap between sorrow and agency. The apologizer grieves the outcome without owning the deed, or owns the deed while defending the intention behind it. This is not quite the classic non-apology apology of the "sorry you were offended" variety, since real harm is acknowledged and real sorrow expressed; nor is it simple insincerity, since the apologizing government may genuinely grieve. It seems closer to a missing component than to a counterfeit.

So: does the literature on political or official apologies have a term for an apology that acknowledges the harm but omits, displaces, or dilutes the apologizer's agency and wrongdoing? How does it relate to the standard accounts of what makes an apology valid or complete — criteria like naming the offense, accepting responsibility, expressing remorse, offering repair — and is the missing piece better described as failed acknowledgment, failed acceptance of responsibility, or something else? And a harder boundary case: if an apology names the act plainly but attributes it entirely to predecessor governments, casting the current state as an innocent heir regretting a stranger's crime, is that the same phenomenon or a different one? If nothing established fits, please propose a term, define it precisely, and mark its edges against the non-apology apology, the diplomatic expression of regret, and the merely insincere apology.
