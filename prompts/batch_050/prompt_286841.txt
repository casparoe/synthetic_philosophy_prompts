I'm on the grant committee for a small longtermist AI safety fund (yes, one of those). We're evaluating a proposal that wants money to scale up AI-assisted deliberation tools -- think debate, amplification-flavored setups where a human plus an aligned model try to match whatever an unaligned model could pull off. The PI's whole case rests on something like the strategy-stealing assumption: for basically any strategy the unaligned system might use to get ahead, the aligned assistant can adopt a similar-enough strategy at similar cost, so the human side doesn't lose ground. One of my fellow committee members wants to reject on the grounds that this assumption is doing all the work and is borderline unfalsifiable -- she says strategies that depend on being willing to lie, coerce, or take huge risks can't actually be "stolen" by an aligned system without it stopping being aligned, and that nobody's even defined the equivalence class of strategies precisely enough to test the claim. Another member thinks these are just the usual growing pains of a young research program and we should fund.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before our vote. It needs a concrete recommendation (fund, reject, or fund with conditions -- if conditions, say what they'd actually be), and it has to take seriously the strongest version of the objection rather than treating it as pedantry. I'm especially interested in whether there's a way to state the assumption so that it could turn out to be false, and what evidence or pilot results would bear on it, since that seems like the crux for whether this is fundable reserach or philosophy with a budget attached. Keep it to something committee members will actually read.
