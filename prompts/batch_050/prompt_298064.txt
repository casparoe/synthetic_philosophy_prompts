Ok so I've been sitting in on this recurring fight between my physics grad student friends and the philosophy postdocs in our department and honestly it's gone nowhere in like two years. The physics side keeps rolling out the greatest hits: Weinberg's chapter basically saying philosophy of science has been useless or worse for scientists, Hawking declaring philosophy dead, Feynman's various jabs. The philosophers respond that these guys are doing philosophy constantly, just badly — that "shut up and calculate" IS a philosophical position, that Einstein read Mach and Poincaré and that shaped relativity, that the Bohr-Einstein debates were philosophy that turned into physics, and that half of what philosophers of physics do (the hole argument, Bell's conceptual setup, making sense of QFT) is indistinguishable from theoretical physics anyway. The physicists then say fine, that stuff was just good physics done by people who happened to read philosophy books, and nothing a professional philosopher has written since 1920 has generated a single prediction or equation anyone uses. And then it loops.

What I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this actually a factual disagreement about the historical record (did philosophy really contribute to relativity/QM?), or is it a verbal disagreement about what counts as "philosophy" vs "physics" vs "useful"? Or is it something less respectable — a status fight where both sides are really arguing about who gets to be the grown-ups of inquiry? My own hunch is that the two sides have completely different standards for what would count as philosophy "helping" science, so they can each go through the same list of cases (Einstein, Bohr, Bell, decoherence) and both walk away thinking the case history vindicates them. But maybe that's too charitable and one side is just wrong about the facts.

Also: given your diagnosis, what would actually settle it? Like is there a specific kind of case that, if it happened, would force one side to concede — say a philosopher publishing something that a working physicist cites in a real result, or a historian showing the Einstein-Mach connection was overblown? Or is the question just badly posed and the honest answer is that nothing could resolve it?
