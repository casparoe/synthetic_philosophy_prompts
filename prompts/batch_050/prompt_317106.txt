For a small ethics reading group I've joined, each of us has been asked to circulate a short statement of our working views so the others can try to break them. Mine is about complicity and consumer ethics, since that's where I feel most confident, and I'd like an outside audit before I send it around. Here are my five commitments, roughly as I wrote them:

First, I hold a difference-making principle of responsibility: you can't be blameworthy for an outcome your action did nothing to bring about. I'm willing to count *expected* difference-making, since almost nothing in life is certain, but I won't count anything weaker — mere association, symbolic endorsement, or "being part of a culture that" does nothing for me. Second, I think buying sweatshop-produced fast fashion is wrong and that ordinary consumers genuinely share blame for sweatshop conditions, even though I'm aware any single purchase is causally negligible: factories don't retool because I bought one more shirt. Third, I think voting is a real obligation, and I ground that entirely in expected value — the chance of being pivotal is minuscule but the stakes are enormous, so the math works out. Fourth, I think purely symbolic gestures carry no moral weight whatsoever; divestment campaigns by universities, for instance, are self-congratulatory theater that changes nothing, and people who feel virtuous for participating are confusing expression with action. Fifth, I think someone who inherits money they know was originally extracted through exploitation — colonial profiteering, say, a few generations back — is permitted to keep it: they caused nothing, endorsed nothing, and the wrong was done before they existed.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of positions. Go through the set and tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, distinguishing the two cases carefully rather than flagging everything. For each genuine tension, rate its severity, state precisely what would have to give on each side, and note whether the standard repairs I might reach for are actually open to me given my other commitments — I have a suspicion some of the usual escape hatches are ones I've already welded shut. Where a tension depends on an empirical or interpretive question I haven't settled, say what it turns on. And please be committed rather than even-handed: rank the tensions, and then tell me which single revision you would actually make if these were your views, what it would cost me, and why it's cheaper than the alternatives.
