I am preparing a proposal for an eighteen-month postdoctoral fellowship in philosophy of science and need the document drafted. The area is Goodman's new riddle of induction. Here is my half-formed idea, which I want developed rather than replaced: the standard literature treats the riddle as a problem about which predicates are projectible, and the main responses try to supply a criterion for excluding grue-like predicates -- by appeal to entrenchment, to the simplicity or naturalness of properties, or to the priority of observational over dispositional language. My suspicion is that these responses all grant Goodman his framing, namely that confirmation is a relation between evidence statements and hypotheses stated in some language, and that the real mistake lies there. Concretely: I want to argue that grue-like hypotheses look confirmable only if we describe the evidence purely extensionally, and that once we require a hypothesis to specify how the observed instances were generated -- that is, to embed the predicate in a minimal causal or explanatory account of the sampling process -- the grue variant is no longer a rival hypothesis about the same evidence but a different and worse explanation of it, and that this dissolves the riddle without needing a privileged vocabulary or a simplicity metric. Whether this works, and whether it collapses back into a disguised appeal to natural properties, is exactly what I do not yet know.

The proposal should: state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the entrenchment, simplicity, and natural-properties responses leave it open, without attributing to any of them claims they do not make; set out the method, meaning what I would actually do across the eighteen months, including the phase where I try to construct the strongest possible extensional redescription of a causal hypothesis as a stress test of my own idea; identify the two or three hardest objections the project would face and what would count as the project failing, including the possibility that the causal reframing turns out to be question-begging; and close with an honest assessment of what a useful deliverable looks like even if the dissolution fails. Please do not include a bibliography or citations by name and year -- I will supply those myself -- but you may refer to the standard positions and arguments by description.
