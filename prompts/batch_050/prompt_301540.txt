I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm drafting a long essay that uses Borges's story "Funes the Memorious" as a way into the frame problem in artificial intelligence — the puzzle, posed classically by McCarthy and Hayes and later dramatized by Dennett in his parable of the robot that kept inferring side effects while a bomb ticked beside it, of how any intelligent system knows which of the indefinitely many consequences of an action are worth computing, and which facts about the world simply stay put. My editor insists that the piece take both halves seriously: Borges not as colorful decoration for a technical point, and the frame problem not as a loose metaphor pasted onto literature.

I'd like your reading as raw material. The natural parallel is easy to state: Funes perceives and remembers everything — every leaf on every tree on every day, the exact shape of every cloud — and is thereby incapable of thought, because thinking requires ignoring; he looks like the mirror image of Dennett's robot, a system with perfect registration of particulars and no grip on relevance. But press the parallel on both sides. Funes's deficit seems to be one of abstraction and categorization, whereas the frame problem is standardly posed as one of belief revision — knowing what persists, what changes, and what matters for the goal at hand. Are these the same problem in different dress, or two distinct failures my essay would be wrong to conflate? Does the story, read carefully, actually anticipate the frame problem, or a neighboring one instead — relevance realization, concept learning, the loss of generality? And what does Funes's own compensating device — his private system in which every number and every perception receives its own proper name — reveal about what he can and cannot do?

Finally, the hook for my editors: contemporary AI appears neither to have solved the frame problem in its classical form nor to suffer from it — nobody writes frame axioms anymore, and large language models generalize promiscuously rather than refusing to. Has deep learning dissolved the frame problem, relocated it, or merely hidden it? If Funes is a warning, what exactly is he a warning about now?

(~250 words, closing with a one-sentence bottom line I can use as the essay's thesis).
