So I've been in yet another thread about consent norms and its the usual mess — everyones using the phrase "valid consent" as though it picks out one thing, and half the disagreement is people running different implicit theories past each other. Before I wade back in I want to do the thing we always tell people to do and properly taboo the word.

Write me the desiderata: what any adequate account of what makes consent valid has to deliver. Which cases it must get right, which distinctions it must respect, which puzzles it must dissolve or at least explain away — with the case or consideration behind each desideratum, not just a bare list. Some cases I specifically want the list to cope with:

- The deception gradient. Lying about your job to get someone into bed; lying about wearing a condom; the classic identical-twin-impersonating-his-brother case. Most people judge these very differently, but they're all "sex procured by deception", so the account has to say what the morally relevant variable actually is — what the lie was about, what the other person would have cared about, something about the nature of the act itself — rather than gesturing at "deception bad".
- Threats versus very lopsided offers. "Sleep with me or you're fired" is obviously coercive; "I'll sell you the insulin you need, but only at a hundred times cost" is an offer, and offers are meant to leave you better off, yet it feels nearly as bad. The theory has to either draw a principle line here or bite the bullet and say the insulin case isn't coercive — and if it bites the bullet it owes an error theory for why it feels coercive.
- Competence as a dial, not a switch. Children, very drunk adults, early-stage dementia patients. A binary threshold gives you absurd cliff edges; a sliding scale needs to say what it slides with (stakes? reversibility of the decision?).
- Retroactive regret. Someone fully on board at the time who later wishes they hadn't been. The account has to say whether that's their problem or a defect in the original consent, and it had better not make every later regret retroactively invalidate the earlier agreement, because that breaks promising in general.

For each desideratum, say whether you reckon it's genuinely non-negotiable or just the hobbyhorse of one camp — the "consent is a mental state" people and the "consent is a public performative act" people seem to want quite different things, for a start. And the part I most want: flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. My prior going in is that "gets all the intuitive edge cases right" and "simple enough to function as an actual legal or social rule" are in tension, and likewise "filters out all manipulation" and "leaves ordinary humans capable of consenting to anything at all, given that advertising exists" — but tell me if I'm wrong.

Then, briefly, how the two obvious contender framings — consent as an internal mental state versus consent as a communicative act — stack up against the list. A few lines each is plenty; the criteria are the deliverable, not the scorecard.

Hard limit of 800 words, and if you need to trim, trim the scorecard, not the desiderata.
