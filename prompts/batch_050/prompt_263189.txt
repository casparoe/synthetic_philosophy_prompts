So I'm a philosophy PhD student (philosophy of language, awkwardly enough) and I'm the grad student rep on our faculty's academic integrity committee, which means I get to sit through cases like the one we're deciding next week. A master's student turned in a term paper with six references, four of which don't exist -- plausible-sounding articles, real journals, plausible page ranges, all fabricated. She admitted pretty quickly that she used an LLM to draft the lit review and pasted in whatever it gave her without checking. Her defense is basically that she never claimed to have read those papers, she just... didn't do anything to make sure they were real, and she honestly didn't know chatbots make things up. She seemed genuinely shocked, for whatever that's worth.

Here's the problem. Our integrity code has two relevant bins: "fabrication of sources," which everyone reads as requiring intent to deceive and which carries suspension, and "inadequate citation practices," which is a slap on the wrist plus resubmission. Half the committee says pasting in references you know you never verified fits fabrication fine, intent or no intent. The other half says you can't lie or fabricate about something you had no attitude toward at all -- she wasn't trying to deceive anyone about the contents of the literature, she just didn't care whether the citations were accurate, and punishing that as fabrication stretches the concept past breaking. (Someone brought up Frankfurt's bullshit essay at the last meeting and it actually clarified things for about five minutes before making everything worse, because now nobody agrees whether what she did even counts as bullshitting rather than a weird new category of outsourced negligence. There was also a side argument about whether the LLM itself lies, bullshits, or just errs, which I don't think is resolvable in a committee meeting but which keeps coming up.)

Draft the decision memo I'll circulate before the meeting: it should recommend which bin this case goes in (or propose a third category, if you think that's what the code needs), what the sanction should be, and -- this is the part the dean actually cares about -- a test the committee can apply to future cases to sort intentional deception from careless indifference from genuine mistake when an LLM is somewhere in the causal chain. Give the strongest version of the side you don't land on, because whichever way the memo goes, half the committee will be reading it looking for reasons it fails. Keep it under 900 words so people actually read it.
