So I host this little discussion night at my apartment, like eight people, mostly friends from work plus my brother, and we pick a theme each month and I do a 20 minute "explainer" at the start before we open it up. Next month's theme is societal collapse -- everyone immediately said Rome, Easter Island, the Maya, all the greatest hits. Here's my problem: halfway through prepping I realised I'm not sure collapse is even a real category. The Maya "collapsed" but there are millions of Maya people today speaking Mayan languages. The Bronze Age "collapsed" but Greece came back with writing again a few centuries later, arguably better. Easter Island supposedly collapsed but the newer-ish story is they were doing okay-ish until Europeans showed up. So the thing I actually want to teach my friends is this idea I keep circling: that whether something counts as a collapse depends on what you think the "thing" is that was supposed to survive -- the state, the people, the culture, the population, the technology level? Like a band: if all the original members leave one by one but the name and the songs continue, did the band break up or just change?

Plan my 20 minutes for me, specifically the structure of the explanation rather than the explanation itself. What concept do I need to get across first before the "collapse vs. transformation" question even makes sense to a room of smart non-academics? Which ONE case should I build the whole talk around (I was going to use all three of Maya/Easter Island/Bronze Age but I suspect that's a mistake -- tell me if so and which one survives the cut, and what that case needs to have and not have to carry the idea). Then give me the misconception map: what wrong ideas my friends will walk in with (I can already hear someone saying "well obviously population decline is collapse, case closed"), in the order they'll probably voice them, and where in my sequence each one should get headed off versus deliberately left alone until later. Also: what should I explicitly NOT try to cover in 20 minutes, and how do I check at the end whether they've actually grasped the "identity of the thing that survives" framing rather than just learned some fun facts about Rapa Nui? My brother will definitely try to derail this into "are WE collapsing," so if you have advice on whether to plan for that or ban it, I'll take that too.
