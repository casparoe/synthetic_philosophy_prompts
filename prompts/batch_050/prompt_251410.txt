So I fell down a rabbit hole about Moore's paradox (the "it's raining but I don't believe it's raining" thing) and my brother-in-law, who is annoyingly good at this stuff, used it to corner me into an argument whose conclusion is clearly nuts. He says it proves nobody can actually lie. That can't be right, people lie constantly, but I've gone over it like ten times and cant find where it breaks. Here's his argument, as best I remember it:

(1) There's something genuinely broken about saying "It's raining, but I don't believe it's raining." The sentence could even be true, but no sane person would ever actually say it.
(2) The brokenness isn't in the grammar or in what the words mean, since each half is fine by itself and they could both be true at once. What's broken is the act of saying it: the speaker is putting something forward as true while at the same time admitting they don't buy it.
(3) So the absurdity comes from the combination itself: asserting p while not believing p. Whenever those two things come together, you get the brokenness.
(4) But a lie is exactly that combination. The liar asserts p while believing not-p, and believing not-p is one way of not believing p.
(5) So lying has the same brokenness as the Moore sentence.
(6) And broken speech acts are ones you literally cannot perform, like you can't fire an employee if you don't work at the company, or "marry" two people as a joke at a party. The attempt just fizzles, it doesn't count.
(7) So attempts at lying fizzle too. Every apparent lie is really something else, maybe a mistake, a joke, an acting exercise, whatever.
(8) Therefore lying is impossible.

Now obviously the conclusion is false, so something has to give. I'm most confident in (1) and (2), since the Moore thing really does seem broken and his explanation of why sounds right to me. And honestly (7) and (8) follow pretty mechanically. So I suspect the slip is somewhere in (3) through (6) but when I push him on it he just restates the step slower, like I'm a child.

Which step should I actually reject? And here's the part I really want: explain why the bad step *sounded* fine to both of us, because it clearly did, and tell me what I'd have to give up if instead I rejected one of the other steps, like if I dug in on (3) and (4) and went after (6) instead, what would that cost me? I want ammunition for next Thanksgiving, not just a verdict.
