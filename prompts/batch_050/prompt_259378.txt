Every account of time travel to the past that I come across claims to dissolve the grandfather paradox through consistency: the traveler cannot kill his grandfather because he did not. But this strikes me as evasive, because it says nothing about what actually prevents the killing. Suppose the traveler is well prepared -- a reliable weapon, a clear opportunity, and a settled intention. Then the attempt must fail for some contingent reason: a misfire, a cramp, a bystander's intervention, a last-minute change of heart. And it must fail every time, on every attempt, no matter how carefully the traveler plans. That no longer looks like a resolution; it looks like a conspiracy, as if the universe had to keep arranging freak accidents to protect one particular man. So what is the mechanism of prevention? If there is none, in what sense was the traveler ever able to try, and doesn't the consistency answer collapse into fatalism? If there is one, doesn't the consistency answer owe us an account of it rather than simply citing logical impossibility after the fact?
