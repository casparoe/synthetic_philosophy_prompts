So me and my cousin have been arguing about this for like two years at every family dinner and we never get anywhere. He says religion makes people better — more generous, more honest, more likely to trust each other. I say look at Denmark or Japan, barely anyone goes to church and those places run fine, so clearly you don't need religion for a decent society. We've both just been lobbing the same examples back and forth and I'm sick of it.

Here's what I want: pretend we finally sat down and did one of those adversarial collaboration things, and write the document we'd come out with. So:

1. A numbered list of claims we could both actually sign. Like I'm pretty sure we'd both agree religious people give more to charity on some measures, and he'd agree that plenty of secular countries have low crime and high trust. Find more stuff like that where the facts point one way and neither of us has an excuse to deny it.

2. The disagreements, stated precisely enough that each of us would say "yes, that's my position." My suspicion is a lot of our fight is really about whether it's the *believing* that matters or the *showing up every week with the same people* — like, is church doing something a bowling league or a strong union could also do? Get at things like that.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where if one of us got a clear answer, we'd actually have to update. For each one, describe some evidence or some real-world comparison we'd both accept as relevant, and write down in advance what each of us predicts it would show.

One more thing that's important to me: when you're doing this, be clear about which claims are actually settled among people who study this stuff and which ones are still genuinely up for debate. I dont want a document that dresses up a contested interpretation as established fact just because it sounds authoritative — I'd rather have "researchers disagree about whether this is causation or selection" than a confident-sounding story either way.

Format it as the agreed-claims section first, then a table of the disagreements and cruxes.
