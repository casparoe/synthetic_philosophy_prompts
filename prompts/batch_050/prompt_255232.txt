I've been reading about the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and it seems to make fatalism a theorem of special relativity. Because simultaneity is relative, events that lie in my future are simultaneous with "now" in the frame of some suitably moving observer; so the future must be just as real as the present, and the universe is a four-dimensional block in which everything is already laid out. But if what will happen to me tomorrow already sits in some observer's present, then it cannot be otherwise than it is, and deliberation can only be the experience of discovering what was fixed all along. If that's right, why do physicists and philosophers continue to debate free will as though the question were open, instead of treating it as settled physics — and if it's wrong, where exactly does the inference go off the rails?
