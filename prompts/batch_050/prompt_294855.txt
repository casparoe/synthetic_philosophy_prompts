I run a reading group on Korean Neo-Confucian moral psychology, and we keep circling the same impasse about the Four–Seven debate, so I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. The record of the exchange is not in dispute. In a long series of letters in sixteenth-century Korea, Yi Hwang (Toegye) and Ki Daeseung (Gobong) argued over how the four beginnings — the sprouts of commiseration, of shame and dislike, of deference, and of approval and disapproval that Mencius treats as the seeds of the virtues — stand in relation to the seven feelings of joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate, and desire. Toegye's settled view was that the four are issuances of principle with material force following, whereas the seven are issuances of material force with principle riding along — a doctrine of two distinguishable issuances, which Gobong rejected on the ground that a single psychophysical event cannot be issued twice. Yi I (Yulgok) later defended the opposing line in his own exchange with Song Hon: only material force issues, principle merely rides, and the four are simply the seven at their most refined and morally attuned pitch, not a second kind of occurrence at all.

What strikes me is that nothing either side could observe or deduce seems able to settle it. Both accept the same metaphysics of principle and material force, the same Mencian texts, and roughly the same facts of moral experience; both agree the four are morally special. My hypothesis is that the issuance vocabulary is idle, and the real disagreement is about what must be metaphysically true of moral emotion for self-cultivation toward sagehood to be possible — but treat that as a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is: a substantive dispute about the metaphysics of principle and material force, a merely verbal one about what issuance means, a disagreement about what moral psychology must be like for cultivation to work, or a hermeneutic dispute about Mencius conducted in metaphysical dress. Support the diagnosis with what the parties actually say, and state what would have counted as progress — a sharper distinction, an appeal to experience, a reframed question — or argue that no resolution was possible and why. Begin with your diagnosis stated in a single sentence, and keep the whole reply under 800 words.
