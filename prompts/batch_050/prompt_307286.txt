So here's a slightly unhinged request, but bear with me. I teach a grad seminar on recognition (Hegel, Honneth, the usual), and every year I make the students write one satirical piece to prove they actually understood the lordship-and-bondage dialectic and didn't just nod along to the secondary lit. This year I want to show them a model example first, so I need you to write one.

The passage I want the piece to turn on is from Hegel's Phenomenology of Mind (Baillie's 1910 translation of the Phenomenology of Spirit), paragraph 182, right before the whole lord/bondsman fight gets going:

"The process then is absolutely the double process of both self-consciousnesses. Each sees the other do the same as itself; each itself does what it demands on the part of the other, and for that reason does what it does, only so far as the other does the same. Action from one side only would be useless, because what is to happen can only be brought about by means of both."

Write a memo, roughly 550 words (the quote above doesn't count toward that), from the office of the lord to the bondsman workforce, announcing a new Mutual Recognition Initiative. Deadpan corporate HR voice throughout: synergy, deliverables, quarterly recognition metrics, a recognition portal, whatever. The whole joke is that the memo is trying to achieve by unilateral managerial fiat exactly what the passage says is impossible. The lord has clearly read just enough Hegel to learn that he needs to be recognized, and has concluded that the efficient solution is to mandate it: bondsmen will submit recognition of management on a schedule, recognition will be tracked, low recognizers will be coached. What the memo cannot let itself notice -- and this is where the satire has to do the philosophical work, not just the comic work -- is twofold. First, that recognition collected by directive from someone you treat as a thing is worthless, because the lord's whole problem is that the bondsman doesn't count for him as an independent consciousness whose acknowledgment could confirm anything. Second, the passage's point that action from one side only is useless: the initiative demands from the bondsman precisely what management has no intention of giving back, so it structurally cannot succeed. If you can also smuggle in, completely straight-faced, the fact that the bondsman is meanwhile the one actually becoming somebody through the work the memo keeps praising as a development opportunity, even better -- but don't over-egg it, the reader should catch it, not be handed it.

Stay deadpan the whole way through, no winking, and it should be funny to someone who's never opened Hegel while a Hegel person sees that you've gotten the dialectic right. One more thing on form: I know real memos have headers and bullet points, but do NOT do that -- no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables, nothing. Continuous prose only, like a long email, and that goes for the memo itself too. I hate how chatbots reach for formatting the second you let them.

Then, after the memo, add a short note (same rules, continuous prose, no heading) telling me how confident you are in the reading of the passage your satire depends on, and single out the one interpretive move you're least sure of -- e.g. whether the bit about recognition from an unfree consciousness being worthless is actually there in Hegel's text or whether that's a later gloss we've all back-projected onto it. Be honest if it's the latter; I'd rather know.
