I have been reading about the ladder of creature-kinds sometimes associated with Daniel Dennett: creatures whose behavioral repertoire is fixed by natural selection alone; creatures that learn from their own trial and error; creatures that can test candidate actions in a kind of inner rehearsal, so that bad ideas are eliminated before anyone has to act on them; and creatures that accelerate the whole process by importing ready-made thinking tools from their culture. My question concerns a configuration this ladder does not seem to have a slot for, and I would like to know whether it has an established name.

Here are two instances. A chess amateur learned her opening principles from a coach and has never tested them against her own experience. When she mentally plays out a line and the position strikes her as wrong by the coach's lights, she discards the line, but she is in no position to tell whether the line was bad or whether an unexamined heuristic just condemned a good one. Or take a young songbird that memorizes an adult's song before it can sing, then spends months rehearsing in private, keeping the renditions that match the stored model and dropping the rest: the testing is the bird's own, but the standard it tests against was borrowed wholesale.

The pattern is this: a creature runs genuine inner trials, yet the criteria by which candidates pass or fail were absorbed from others and have never been independently re-examined, so when something gets rejected in rehearsal, the failure cannot be cleanly located in the idea or in the judge. Is there an established concept for this — for the inner-rehearsal kind of learner whose evaluating machinery is itself an unvetted import? If the nearest existing notions fit only partially, I want to know exactly where each one breaks down; and if nothing fits, I would rather have a newly coined term with a precise definition and explicit boundary cases (does it matter whether the borrowed standard is in fact reliable? does it still count if the creature could in principle re-derive the standard but never has?) than a bare label.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — named, partially named, or unnamed — then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
