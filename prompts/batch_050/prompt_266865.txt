I'm a novelist, mid-draft on a book about a woman who becomes convinced that her life couldn't have gone any other way, and someone at my writing group told me I should read Spinoza, specifically the necessitarianism stuff and the "intellectual love of God." I did, and I have this itch that something in it maps onto things I already think about constantly in craft terms -- the way a good ending feels inevitable in retrospect, like the story could never have ended differently even though I sweated over five alternatives; the way characters who are properly built just *do* what their nature demands and the plot follows; the way writers talk about a book "wanting" to be what it is, as if all our revision is just uncovering something fixed. So here's what I want: restate Spinoza's necessitarianism, and the intellectual love of God as the attitude that supposedly follows from understanding it, in the vocabulary of novel-writing -- drafts, outlines, revision, character consistency, plot necessity, the difference between a contrived twist and an earned one. Be as precise as you can about the translation, not just vibes.

Then give me the residue report, because that's the part I actually care about. Which of Spinoza's claims translate cleanly into craft-speak (my guess: "everything follows from the divine nature by necessity" is suspiciously close to "everything in the finished book follows from its premises and its people, or the book fails"), which ones you have to fudge, and which ones have no counterpart at all. I can already see trouble spots -- I revise and God doesn't, I could genuinely have written a different book and Spinoza seems to deny there are real alternatives anywhere, and my characters might be free in a way his universe isn't, or maybe it's the other way round. For each bit of residue, tell me whether it's a limitation of my vocabulary or a sign that Spinoza is saying something stranger and more radical than the novelist's version, because I definately don't want a translation that just flatters my existing intuitions.

One more thing, since it's for the novel: my protagonist's arc hinges on whether loving the necessity of her life is more like loving a finished novel -- appreciating how it all hangs together -- or loving the fact that it *couldn't* have been otherwise, which sounds colder and weirder. If the translation is any good it should be able to tell me which of those the intellectual love of God actually resembles, or whether it's a third thing neither of us has words for.
