"Deflationary theories of truth hold that 'true' is not a genuine property at all; sentences like 'It is true that snow is white' add nothing to 'snow is white,' as Ramsey showed with his redundancy (or prosentential) theory. The core of the view is the disquotational schema: 'p' is true if and only if p. Unlike correspondence theories, which analyze truth as a relation between sentences and facts that requires metaphysical explanation, deflationists take the schema to exhaust the concept: there is no substantive nature of truth left to discover. The predicate still earns its keep as a device of generalization -- it lets us endorse claims like 'Everything the witness said is true' without enumerating the witness's statements one by one. The liar paradox is often raised as an objection, but it is really a problem for any theory of truth, and especially for correspondence theories, which must identify the fact to which the liar sentence corresponds. Deflationists, by contrast, can simply accept all instances of the T-schema, since these are guaranteed by logic alone, and so no special problem arises for them."
