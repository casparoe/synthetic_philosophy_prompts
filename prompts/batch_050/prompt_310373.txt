I'm an undergraduate in a political philosophy course, currently in a unit on contested monuments, and I want to build a decision tree for a class presentation that sorts the main positions on what to do with statues and street names honoring figures now widely judged morally compromised. The tree should run from "leave everything exactly as it is" through adding contextual plaques, relocating to museums, outright removal, and replacement with counter-monuments.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of named positions: each node should be a question someone can answer without knowing the literature — for instance, whether a statue primarily honors its subject or merely records that history happened; whether a monument's meaning is fixed at its erection or shifts with what surrounds it and who walks past it; whether the commemorated person's achievements can be separated from their wrongdoing; and whose standing settles the question (current residents, descendants of those wronged, or the community that erected it). Each leaf should carry one policy position and one sentence on the case it handles worst. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, say so and commit to a diagnosis: is that position incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored? Where two rationales converge on the same policy and the tree cannot separate them, flag that rather than forcing a split. And if more than one question could plausibly sit at the root, choose the one you judge to do the most classificatory work and defend the choice — I would rather present a tree you stand behind than a menu of alternatives.
