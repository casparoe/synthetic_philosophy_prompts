Two members of my philosophy of religion reading group have argued all semester without progress: one defends the argument from divine hiddenness, roughly the claim that a perfectly loving God would ensure that anyone capable of a conscious relationship with him believes he exists unless they are resisting, so the evident existence of nonresistant nonbelievers shows there is no such God; the other is a theist who holds that a loving God might deliberately withhold unmistakable evidence of himself for the sake of goods like uncoerced faith, moral autonomy, or the value of genuinely seeking. They now want to do an adversarial collaboration and produce a joint statement both could actually sign. Draft it for them: the claims both accept, including about what perfect love does and does not obviously require of a God toward creatures; the remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would endorse rather than in the other side's caricature; the cruxes, where I suspect one is whether perfect love entails pursuing an explicitly conscious, reciprocal relationship with every person, and another is whether nonresistant nonbelief genuinely occurs or always involves some subtle resistance; and for each crux, a thought experiment or consideration both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. Since their debates keep sliding between mere belief that God exists and a trusting personal relationship with God, the statement must keep those two notions distinct throughout. Write the entire statement as continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables, with the agreed claims, disagreements, and recorded predictions all woven into sentences.
