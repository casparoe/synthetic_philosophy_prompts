Last night a friend and I spent three hours arguing about hypocrisy and blame, and we ended exactly where we started. His position was that tu quoque is a textbook fallacy: if your criticism of me is accurate, then your own record is beside the point, and a hypocrite's blame is every bit as good as a saint's. I kept insisting that there is something defective about a serial adulterer loudly berating a colleague for an affair even when every word he says is true, but I could not characterize the defect without seeming to concede my friend's point about fallacies. We traded cases in circles -- the reformed alcoholic warning his children off drink, the executive who pioneered an exploitative practice and now publicly condemns competitors for adopting it, the parent punishing a child for behavior the child plainly learned at home -- and I finally realized that what we needed was not another case but a specification.

Write the desiderata for a theory of standing to blame: what any adequate account of who is positioned to blame whom, and for what, must deliver. Give each requirement together with the case or consideration that motivates it. Among the questions I want the list to settle or at least organize: whether a theory must distinguish the truth or aptness of a blame's content from the blamer's authority to issue it; whether it must explain why "you do it too" functions as a genuine reply to blame without functioning as a rebuttal of anything the blamer said; whether it must avoid the implication that only the morally spotless may blame, which would empty the practice entirely; what it should say about the hypocrite's self-blame, about blame issued by someone guilty of something far worse, about blame from a person complicit in the very wrong at issue, and about the reformed wrongdoer; and whether standing is something one forfeits, something one merely diminishes, or something one never had. Mark which requirements are broadly accepted constraints that any theory in this area must meet and which are contested demands characteristic of particular approaches. Most importantly, flag any pair of independently plausible desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, since that would tell my friend and me that our real disagreement was over which requirement to surrender rather than over the cases.

Close with a brief note on which single requirement does the most work in separating the main families of views (those built on moral address, on fairness or clean hands, and on the blamer's own commitments), but keep that short -- the list itself is the deliverable. Throughout, separate what is settled among people who work on this topic from what is genuinely contested, and do not present any one camp's preferred constraint as though it were the consensus.
