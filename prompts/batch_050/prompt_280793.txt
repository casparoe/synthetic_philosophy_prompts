Okay so I've fallen down a rabbit hole on Nozick's experience machine — the thought experiment where a super-machine can give you any experiences you want for the rest of your life, and the question is whether you'd plug in. Nozick says most people wouldn't, and takes that to show we care about more than just how things feel from the inside. I have no philosophy training, I've just been arguing about this with my brother for like two weeks, and I want to stress-test the thing properly.

Here's every objection to the thought experiment I've managed to collect, from reddit threads and a couple of youtube videos and one very patient friend:

1. The status quo bias objection — of course people hesitate to leave their current life for an unknown machine, but that's just fear of change, and you could flip it (imagine you're IN the machine and get offered the chance to unplug into a worse "real" life) and people would refuse to unplug too, so the intuition proves nothing.
2. The "you wouldn't be you anymore" objection — plugging in erases your memories and personality and replaces them with a scripted life, so really it's closer to dying and being replaced, which nobody wants regardless of their views on pleasure.
3. The "bad enough life and everyone plugs in" objection — people in genuinely terrible situations WOULD plug in, so the machine doesn't show reality has independent value, just that it has some value that gets outweighed sometimes.
4. The "rigged from the outside" objection — you're being asked to decide from your actual life, where you already have real commitments and people who depend on you, so refusing says more about loyalty than about what ultimately matters.
5. The cheat where the machine can just simulate your current life, including the part where you refuse to plug in, so the thought experiment doesn't even give you a real choice between "experiences" and "reality."

What I want is one more objection that is NOT any of these and not a mash-up or rewording of them. Ideally something the internet arguments I've seen haven't surfaced.
