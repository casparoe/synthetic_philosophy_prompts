i recently got roped into sitting on my university's animal research ethics commitee (my roommate is the grad student rep and needed a warm body) and at the last meeting a funding decision basically came down to one member insisting the octopus cognition study needed vastly more welfare oversight than the fruit fly work because of, and i'm paraphrasing, the sheer number of neurons involved. when pressed, nobody could actually defend neuron count as the thing that matters, but nobody could fully let go of it either -- it was clearly doing *some* work in everyone's heads, mine included. it's been bugging me ever since, because it feels both obviously crude and weirdly hard to replace.

so: write me a deadpan official guidance document, around 600-700 words, issued by an imaginary government body -- something bland like the Office of Comparative Standing -- that has fully committed to neuron count as the measure of how much an animal morally matters. format it like real civil-service guidance: a schedule of animals with their official standings, worked examples applying the formula to awkward cases (is one elephant exactly equivalent to some number of mice, and how many? where does a crow land relative to a capybara? is an ant colony one very large patient or forty thousand very small ones, and does it matter which?), plus a section on appeals and maybe a footnote-laden FAQ. the satire should work by taking the neuron-count proxy completely seriously and following it where it leads, so that the real objections -- that neuron numbers are a rough proxy for the capacity to suffer at best, that the arithmetic quietly smuggles in the intuitions it claims to replace, that nobody actually accepts the verdicts at the extremes -- emerge from the document itself without the office ever noticing them. stay deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader, and the humor should come from getting the underlying debate right, so that someone who actually works on moral weights reads it and goes "oof, fair."
