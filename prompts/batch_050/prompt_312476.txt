Ok so I'm in a philosophy club at school and we got into this huge argument about whether you can be blamed for something if you couldn't have done otherwise. Like the classic "secret mind-control chip" scenario. 1. Jake cheats on the final exam, totally on his own, no chip, nothing. Definitely blameworthy.
2. Same thing, but unknown to Jake a chip was implanted that WOULD have forced him to cheat if he'd decided not to. He never hesitates so it does nothing. Still blameworthy, just as much as case 1.
3. The chip actually kicks in and forces him to cheat. Not blameworthy at all, it's basically not even his action.
4. The chip kicks in at the exact moment he was going to cheat anyway, so it sort of "helps along" something he was already doing. I keep flip-flopping on this one but I lean blameworthy.
5. The chip was installed but it was broken the whole time and could never have worked. Jake cheats on his own. Blameworthy, same as case 1... though idk why case 2 and 5 feel slightly different when neither chip did anything.
6. Jake knows about the working chip, believes it'll force him to cheat no matter what, and cheats anyway without it ever activating. I say blameworthy but my friend says knowing changes everything.
