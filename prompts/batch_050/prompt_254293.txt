In a research group I work with on AI alignment, we keep returning to an argument about the coherent extrapolated volition proposal — roughly, the idea that instead of specifying values for an AI system directly, one should aim it at what humanity would want if we knew more, thought faster, were more the people we wished we were, and had more time to consider, with the extrapolation run over some idealized dynamics and the outputs aggregated where they converge. The argument has been stuck for months, and I want a diagnosis rather than another position paper.

One camp holds that the entire disagreement reduces to an empirical conjecture: either reflective equilibrium across humans under idealization converges or it doesn't, and psychology, survey evidence about preference change under information, and formal models of deliberation could in principle settle it. The other camp replies that this is naive, because the output of any such procedure is fixed by the choice of idealization dynamics — how fast people "think," which cognitive limitations get removed, which get kept, how disagreement is aggregated — and since many admissible dynamics yield wildly different endpoints, calling the result "our extrapolated volition" just launders the designer's normative choices through technical-looking machinery. On their view the convergence question is a distraction; the real content of the proposal lives in the idealization parameters, and those parameters encode exactly the contested value judgments the proposal was supposed to outsource. The first camp answers that this objection proves too much, since it would dissolve every ideal-advisor-style theory in normative ethics, not just this one.

Neither side has moved. The empiricists keep proposing studies; the structuralists keep saying the studies can't touch the issue. My own suspicion is that the two camps are answering different questions under one heading — one asking whether a fixed procedure would yield a determinate output, the other asking whether any output of such a procedure would have normative authority over us — but I'm not confident, and it's also possible there is a hidden formal question (about underdetermination of idealizations) that both sides are gesturing at without stating.
