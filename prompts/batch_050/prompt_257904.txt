I got into it in the comments under a local news piece about my city rezoning a neighborhood near me to allow six-story apartment buildings where there's currently a parking lot and some duplexes. Half the thread was people saying longtime renters getting priced out is an injustice, and the other half kept shutting that down with the same one-liner: nobody has a right to live wherever they want. It got like forty upvotes each time and was clearly functioning as a conversation-ender. But it bugged me, partly because I suspect the same people would lose it if you told them they have no right to keep their neighborhood's zoning exactly as it is.

What are the different precise claims that slogan could actually be making? Lay them out as a menu I can refer back to: what kind of right is being denied (legal? moral? a claim against the government, against landlords, against other residents?), what "live wherever they want" has to mean for the claim to come out true, and for each reading whether it's trivial, substantive and plausible, or just false. And then the part I actually care about: which of those readings would do the work the commenters needed, wich is showing that being priced out isn't an injustice? I suspect the slogan only lands by sliding between a strong reading nobody believes and a weak reading that proves nothing, but I can't quite pin down the slide. Also tell me whether it cuts both ways -- if no one has a right to live where they want, does anyone have a right to a neighborhood staying the way they like it, or to the view from their porch? Because if the slogan defeats the renter's complaint it seems like it should defeat the homeowner's too, and nobody in that thread seemed to notice.
