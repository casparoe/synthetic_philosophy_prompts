"So basically, Norman Malcolm argued that dreams aren't experiences at all -- nothing actually happens while you're asleep, and the 'dream' is just a false memory your brain manufactures in the instant you wake up. Daniel Dennett took this further and showed, mostly on the basis of experiments where people got woken up during REM sleep, that dream narratives are fabricated at waking too, which proves that introspective reports about our own minds can't be trusted and that consciousness is more or less an illusion. Between the two of them they basically demolished the common sense idea that you really experience anything in your sleep, and REM research was the nail in the coffin."

Can you audit this for me? Which parts would someone who actually knows the Malcolm/Dennett literature sign off on, which parts are plausible-sounding distortions, and which are just wrong?
