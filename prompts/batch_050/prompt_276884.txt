Here's a thought experiement I've been chewing on. A.J. Ayer built his early reputation on the idea that statements which can't be verified even in principle are meaningless, and on that basis he treated talk of surviving bodily death as empty metaphysics. Late in life he famously had a near-death experience and wrote that it had weakened his confidence that death is simply the end, though he still didn't convert to belief in an afterlife.

Now fast forward: today there are "griefbots," chatbots trained on a dead person's texts, emails, and posts that let mourners keep having conversations with a simulation of them. Some people even describe this as a kind of continued existence. Say Ayer were alive to see all this. Would he say a good enough griefbot finally gives "survival after death" some verifiable content, or would he insist it changes nothing philosophically? And would his own near-death experience make him more sympathetic to people leaning on these bots, or more suspicious of them?
