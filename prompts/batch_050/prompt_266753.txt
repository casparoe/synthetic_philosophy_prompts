I'm pretty convinced by the straightforward answer to the Judy Benjamin problem: when Judy radios in and all she learns is a conditional (something like: given that she's in Blue territory, the odds she's at Blue HQ rather than with the second company are 3 to 1), this tells her nothing about the Red side, so her credence that she's at Red HQ should stay exactly where it was. People who say it should go up (or that there's a uniquely correct higher value) seem to me to be reading information into the message that isn't there.

What would count against this? Not just restatements of the competing answers, which I know. I want: what does the "stays the same" view actually commit you to about how updating on learned conditionals works in general, and are there nearby cases where that commitment gives clearly wrong results? Is there a formulation of what Judy learns (as an indicative conditional, as a constraint on her own future credences, whatever) where the no-change answer becomes untenable on its own terms? And which intuitions would I have to give up if I'm wrong -- e.g., would a defender of the rival answers concede that their answer looks wrong for a conditional learned with full certainty, and if so, where's the discontinuity? Rank the threats to my view from fatal to merely awkward, and lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble.
