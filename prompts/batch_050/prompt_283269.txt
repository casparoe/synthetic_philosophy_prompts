I'm a few weeks into a philosophy of physics reading group and we're stuck on the individuality-of-quantum-particles literature, and honestly I can't tell anymore what the fight is actually about. Everyone seems to agree on the physics: states of systems of fermions are antisymmetric, the formalism is permutation invariant, and any two electrons share all their intrinsic, state-independent properties. Then one side (following French and Redhead, I think) says this means identical fermions violate Leibniz's priniciple of the identity of indiscernibles, so quantum particles can't be individuals in the classical sense. The other side (Saunders and the weak-discernibility people) points out that particles in an entangled state stand in irreflexive relations to each other — being in opposite spin states, say — and that this is enough for a kind of discernibility, so PII survives and the particles can be individuals after all. There's also a quieter third camp that says the whole question is metaphysical window dressing and the formalism has already told us everything there is to know.

But here's what bugs me: nobody in this debate seems to disagree about a single experimental prediction or mathematical fact. The entire dispute looks like it's about what counts as "discernible" — whether relations count, whether irreflexive relations count, whether PII is a piece of metaphysics we bring to physics or a hypothesis the physics could in principle confirm or refute. My working hypothesis is that this is really a disagreement about the status of PII itself (necessary truth? empirical generalization? part of the definition of 'individual'?) dressed up as a disagreement about quantum mechanics, and that the physics was settled the whole time. But I could easily be missing something — maybe there's a genuine question about the structure of Hilbert space or about which metaphysical package does better explanatory work that I'm not seeing.

So: has this debate stalled because it's basically verbal (the sides mean different things by 'individual' and 'discernible'), because it rests on starting intuitions about PII that neither side can argue the other out of, or because there is a real question here that just hasn't been formulated sharply enough yet? And given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like — a sharper theorem about permutation invariance, a settled verdict on irreflexive relations, or everyone agreeing the question was ill-posed?
