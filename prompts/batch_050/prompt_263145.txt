Our current rule is just "no pseudohistory," which is useless because every poster thinks their theory is the legit one and the other guy's is the pseudohistory. I want to write an actual decision tree the mods can use to sort claims, and I figured the philosophy of science demarcation debate is where to start.

So: build me a decision tree that starts from "someone posts a historical claim that contradicts the scholarly consensus" and ends at one of these leaves: legitimate but contested revisionism, honest amateur error, pseudohistory, and myth/speculation that isn't really making a historical claim at all. Each branch node should be a yes/no question a mod could actually answer from the post and the poster's follow-up behavior -- things like whether the claim is even falsifiable, whether contrary evidence gets explained away ad hoc, whether the argument mostly runs on "mainstream historians can't explain X," whether the poster engages with criticism or just repeats the original claim. Phrase the questions so you don't need to know the answer's classification in advance.

Requirements: the tree should have somewhere between 8 and 12 nodes. Run it on test cases at the end -- Atlantis, ancient astronauts, Fomenko, and one genuinely hard case like the debate over when humans first arrived in the Americas -- and show which path each takes. Also flag any spot where the tree can't cleanly seperate two leaves, because I'd rather know where it breaks than pretend it doesn't.
