My two friends got into it at the pub last night over Heidegger, of all things, and now they've both texted me seperately demanding I declare a winner. Here's the fight as best I can reconstruct it.

Background: Maya's a carpenter, Leo's a philosophy grad student. Maya said that when she's planing wood she's not thinking about the plane at all -- her attention is entirely on the wood and the curve she's cutting, and the tool only "shows up" when the blade catches or the handle's loose. Leo said that's textbook readiness-to-hand: equipment is transparent in use, and it only becomes present-at-hand through breakdown, and he went off about conspicuousness, obtrusiveness, obstinacy, section 16 of Being and Time, blah blah.

Then they diverged. Maya said: here's the thing, when I'm doing really fine work I actually AM aware of the plane, not just of the wood. I feel the grain through the sole of the plane, I adjust the angle by feel, I can hear whether the cut is clean. Nothing's malfunctioning, but the tool is very much present to me. So she thinks the transparency picture is wrong, or at least wildly overstated: skilled use involves rich awareness OF the tool, not just awareness THROUGH it.

Leo pushed back: she's confusing two kinds of awareness. Readiness-to-hand doesn't mean you're unconscious of the tool; it means the tool isn't thematized as an object with properties sitting there. Her feel for the grain through the plane is exactly what absorbed coping is -- the awareness is in the doing. The moment she starts thinking "this is a heavy plane with a sharp blade," that's when it's become present-at-hand, and not before.

Maya: that makes readiness-to-hand unfalsifiable. Any case of tool-awareness gets relabeled "absorbed coping," any case of object-awareness "present-at-hand." What would even count as a counterexample?

Leo: breakdown is the counterexample. When the blade catches and suddenly she's just standing there staring at the plane as a thing with properties, that's the switch. And that's philosophically the interesting part anyway, because breakdown reveals the whole workshop as a web of assignments -- the plane for smoothing, the smoothing for the tabletop, etc. -- which you never see while absorbed.

Then Maya made what I think is her best move. She said: sometimes nothing breaks, nothing's missing, nothing's in the way. I finish a cut and I just hold the plane up and look at it, because I like it. I admire it. The tool becomes an object of attention purely because I feel like it, not because anything interrupted the task. So the breakdown taxonomy can't be the whole story of how tools come out of transparency. Either tools can go present-at-hand spontaneously, or plain contemplation is a third thing his scheme doesn't cover.

Leo said something like: that's just a deficient mode of concern, holding back from using stuff is still a way of being-in -- and Maya said that's suspiciously convenient, everything that doesn't fit gets filed under "deficient."

Then it got heated over whether the distinction is even about psychology at all. Leo insisted it's an ontological distinction about modes of Being, not about what's in your head, so asking "what are you aware of" misses the point entirely. Maya said if the distinction has zero implications for experience, it's not clear what it even amounts to.
