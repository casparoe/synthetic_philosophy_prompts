I hold a position about self-reference and control that I want stress-tested, not summarized. Drawing on the distinction between tangled hierarchies and inviolate levels -- the structure Escher's lithograph Drawing Hands depicts, in which each of two hands draws the other into existence and neither occupies a privileged position -- I have come to accept the following:

(1) Any actual system of rules that governs behavior must contain an inviolate level: some rules, or some agent applying the rules, that are not themselves governed by the system in the same sense. (2) Tangled hierarchies, where level A controls level B and level B equally controls level A, are therefore never genuinely instantiated as control systems; they are descriptive illusions produced by alternating which level one attends to, the way the viewer of the lithograph shifts attention from one hand to the other. (3) It follows that proposals to govern a powerful AI system through recursive self-oversight -- the system auditing its own reasoning, or a chain of systems each supervising the next, or two matched systems supervising each other -- must either bottom out in an unsupervised base level that carries all the risk, or collapse into a mutual-overwatch loop that no one actually controls.

I am aware of the obvious gestures against this: thermostatic feedback loops, checks and balances in constitutional design, quines and other self-reproducing programs, Godel-style arithmetization that lets a system speak about itself without a meta-level, and non-well-founded mathematics generally. What I want is a serious map of the position's exposure, not a list of buzzwords. For each candidate defeater, tell me whether it genuinely threatens the conjunction above or only appears to; whether a committed defender of the position would concede it as counting against them or dismiss it as trading on an equivocation (for instance, between causal feedback within one level and a true tangle of levels); and what the world would have to look like for it to be decisive. I would also like you to press on the distinction between the strong reading of (2) -- tangled control hierarchies are impossible -- and the weak reading -- they are possible but always decomposable into a single-level loop plus a description artifact -- and to say which reading the defeaters actually engage. Finally, consider the possibility that the position is unfalsifiable in practice: that every apparent counterexample can be absorbed by re-describing it at a different level, and tell me whether that flexibility is a virtue or a symptom.

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, being explicit about what each would need to show, and do not soften the ranking to seem balanced.
