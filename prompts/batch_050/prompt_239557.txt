For a magazine essay on the foundations of physics, I've been pressed by my editor to state my own view on what gauge theories say the world contains, rather than hiding behind the usual on-the-one-hand/on-the-other-hand. Before I commit this to print, I want to know whether the position I've assembled is actually coherent. Here it is:

(1) Determinism matters to me. A theory whose equations admit futures that differ physically while agreeing on the entire past is, to that extent, defective, and I take the apparent indeterminism of unconstrained gauge evolution seriously as a problem rather than waving it away as a mere artefact of description.

(2) I hold that gauge-related descriptions are redescribings of one and the same physical situation. If two configurations of potentials differ only by a gauge transformation, there is one possibility, not two — the symmetry is redundancy in our mathematics, not structure in the world.

(3) I hold that the Aharonov–Bohm effect settles that the electromagnetic potential is physically real, and not merely a bookkeeping device: charged particles acquire measurable phase shifts while travelling only through regions where the field strength vanishes, and reality must contain whatever produces that shift.

(4) I hold that nothing in nature corresponds to the absolute phase of a wavefunction at a point; only relative phases are observable, which is precisely why I take electromagnetism's gauge freedom to be surplus.

(5) I hold that the lesson of the hole argument in general relativity — diffeomorphism-related models represent a single possibility — should be applied uniformly, so that internal symmetries deserve exactly the same treatment as spacetime symmetries.

(6) And I find myself wanting to say that gluons, which carry colour charge themselves, are straightforwardly real physical things, on a par with electrons.

Some of these are clearly in at least superficial tension, but I can't tell which conflicts are fatal and which dissolve under a more careful formulation — for instance, whether (3) really needs the potential as opposed to something non-local built from it, and whether (6) sits comfortably with (2) and (4). Please audit the set: identify the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, explain for each what would have to give and at what cost, and flag any apparent tensions that evaporate on inspection. Then tell me which single revision buys the most consistency for the least theoretical damage.

Please answer in British English.
