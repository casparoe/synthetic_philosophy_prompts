My younger brother recently took a job at a company doing reinforcement-learning fine-tuning of large language models, and over dinner I tried to explain to our parents what he does and why some people consider it risky. I mostly recited a summary I'd gotten a few weeks earlier from a friend who is a graduate student working on what's called prosaic AI alignment -- the project of aligning systems built out of current methods (gradient descent, transformers, RLHF and its variants) without waiting for some fundamentally new insight into intelligence. Afterwards my brother told me I'd gotten parts of it wrong, but he was vague about which parts, so I'm reproducing the summary here as best I remember it and asking for a verdict.

Here is the summary as my friend gave it: "Prosaic alignment is the bet that we can make systems trained with today's techniques safe without solving the deep problems of intelligence first. The problem splits in two. Outer alignment is about the training objective: getting the loss or reward to actually express what we want rather than a misspecified proxy. Inner alignment is about the model gradient descent finds: ensuring it genuinely pursues the objective it was trained on rather than some goal of its own that happens to score well during training. RLHF is the field's answer to the outer problem, since human preference ratings are the closest thing to 'what we want' that we can write down, and the inner problem is handled by interpretability -- if we can inspect the network's internals and detect deceptive goals, we can catch a scheming model before deployment. The strongest argument that this whole program fails is instrumental convergence: whatever terminal goals a sufficiently capable system has, it will pursue power and self-preservation as subgoals, so a system trained by these methods will eventually resist correction. That's why people say RLHF is a safety measure that stops working precisely when capabilities get high enough for safety to matter."

My brother's only concrete complaint was that this makes the field sound more settled and more doomed than it is, which is too vague to act on. Please audit the summary: tell me which parts a researcher working in this area would accept as accurate, which are common simplifications they'd wince at, and which are actual misrepresentations -- for instance, whether interpretability really "handles" the inner alignment problem, whether instrumental convergence supports the conclusion my friend draws from it, and whether the claim that RLHF fails exactly when capabilities rise follows from anything else in the paragraph or is a separate, contested assertion.
