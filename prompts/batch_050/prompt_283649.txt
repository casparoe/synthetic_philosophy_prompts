Last week I had a long argument with a friend about whether I could still have existed if my parents had conceived a month later than they in fact did. I said the child they would then have had might perfectly well have been me; she said it would at most have been a sibling of mine. Midway through, she fetched a popular philosophy book she had been reading and read out its summary of the Kripkean position, which she took to close the matter in her favour. I wrote the passage down afterwards, as best I remember it:

'Kripke proved that a person's origins are essential to them. Anything originating from a different sperm or egg would by definition be a different individual, so it is simply impossible that you should have come from different gametes. It follows that every fact about your origin — parentage, time and place of conception included — is a necessary truth about you. It also follows that scientists' counterfactual talk is strictly confused: when a biologist says "had this embryo received the other sperm, a different person would have developed", she is describing an impossibility, since "this embryo" already picks out something defined by its actual origin. The same holds for artefacts: this very table could not have been made from a different block of wood.'

My instinct is that the summary is mostly on the right track but quietly overreaches in a couple of places — the word 'proved' and the phrase 'by definition' both set off alarm bells — yet I cannot say precisely where. Could you go through it claim by claim and tell me: which parts a Kripkean would happily endorse; which parts overstate a more modest thesis or trade on a conflation; whether the step from 'you could not have had different gametes' to 'every fact about your origin is necessary' is valid; and whether the concluding swipe at biologists' counterfactual talk follows from anything true in the rest of the passage? Finally, does the corrected version of the view actually settle our original dispute? Does origin essentialism, properly stated, rule it out that I could have been conceived a month later, or does it leave that question open — and if it leaves it open, what further premises would each of us need to make our positions stick? Please answer in British English.
