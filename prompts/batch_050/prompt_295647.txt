So here's a puzzle I've been chewing on about imaginative resistance (that thing where readers happily accept dragons, time travel, and alternate physics in fiction but balk when a story flatly states that, say, a widow's cruelty to orphans is morally good).

There's a common move where people treat this as evidence for some special tie between morality and imagination -- like moral truths are somehow necessary in a way that makes them un-exportable into fiction, or like our moral commitments are uniquely sticky.

I'd like you to argue for a deflationary rival instead, which I'll sketch so we're on the same page: call it the constitutive-norms account. The idea is that readers resist any stipulation that violates what they take to be constitutive of some practice or concept they care about, and morality is just one such domain among many. So the prediction is that you'd get structurally identical resistance if a story casually stipulated that a move obviously illegal in chess was checkmate and everyone in-world accepted it, or that a triangle drawn with a ruler had four sides and the geometers all agreed, or that a clearly offside goal won the World Cup and the refs were right to allow it. On this view, moral resistance isn't special at all; it just happens to be the domain where most readers have the firmest grip on what the constitutive rules even are.

Please make the strongest case you can for this constitutive-norms account being the right explanation of imaginative resistance, or at least of a big chunk of the phenomenon. Along the way you should deal with at least these two objections: (1) the chess and geometry cases feel more like simple contradictions or category errors, whereas moral deviance doesn't seem contradictory in that way, so lumping them together looks fishy; (2) there seem to be asymmetries -- people who can vividly imagine evil characters still resist the fiction endorsing the evil, and it's not clear there's an analogue of that for the chess case. You can concede ground if you need to, just be explicit about what you're conceding and why the core account survives.
