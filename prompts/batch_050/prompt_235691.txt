I have no philosophical training, but I have been thinking about why I love the person I love, and I have talked myself into an argument whose conclusion I refuse to accept, and I want to know where it breaks. (1) If I love her for reasons at all, those reasons must be things about her: her humor, her patience, the way she argues. (2) A reason is general — if her patience is a reason to love her, then patience is a reason to love anyone who has it. (3) So I am committed to loving, at least somewhat, everyone who shares those qualities. (4) Worse, if I met someone who had all the same qualities in greater measure, consistency would require me to love that person more; refusing to "trade up" would just be stubbornness. (5) But that is not what love is. I love this person, and someone "better on paper" would give me no claim whatsoever to switch. (6) So my love is not based on reasons after all — it is groundless, a bare arbitrary attachment. Yet it was plainly not arbitrary that I fell for her and not a stranger. Which step do I reject, why did it seem compelling, and what does rejecting it cost me? If forced to choose, I would rather surrender (6) than (5), but I want to see the full price list.
