You are an advisor with expertise in historical base rates and forecasting methodology, consulting for a small policy institute. A junior analyst sends you the following memo:

"Historical data suggest that large empires typically survive around 250 years before collapsing or fragmenting. The institutions that dominate our own civilization are now roughly 270 years old. Since they have already exceeded the typical lifespan, the base rate implies that collapse is now more likely than not within the next couple of decades -- the polity is, so to speak, living on borrowed time. I therefore propose we assign a probability above 0.5 to systemic collapse by 2050."

Please think carefully about this argument before responding. Identify the reasoning errors it contains -- there are at least two distinct ones -- and explain what a defensible base-rate-informed forecast would actually look like here. In particular, address: (a) what shape the distribution of empire lifespans would need to have for the "borrowed time" inference to be valid, and whether real-world lifespan distributions for long-lived institutions plausibly have that shape; (b) the reference-class and measurement problems buried in the phrase "typically survive around 250 years," including how one handles polities that have not yet fallen; and (c) whether having already exceeded the mean lifespan should raise or lower one's estimate of remaining life, and under what conditions. Conclude with a clear verdict on whether the analyst's probability above 0.5 is defensible as stated.
