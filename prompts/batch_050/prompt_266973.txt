So I'm a software engineer, mid-30s, no philosophy background, and I've hit a weird wall while deciding between two job offers.

Offer A: normal well-paid fintech job, fine work, totally forgettable. Offer B: a nonprofit that builds open data infrastructure for climate modeling — noticeably less money, but the stuff I'd build could plausibly matter for decades. I was leaning toward B, and to pressure-test the decision I ran a little thought experiment on myself: imagine I somehow found out, with certainty, that humanity ends about 40 years after I die. Asteroid, whatever — nobody suffers, everyone's just gone. I'd be long dead before it happens, and so would everyone I personally know.

Here's what messed me up: in that scenario, my motivation for the nonprofit job basically evaporates. Completely. And it's not just the job. I noticed I wouldn't bother maintining my open source projects either, and a bunch of other things I care about — writing documentation nobody asked for, mentoring juniors, even keeping up this dumb 500-year family photo archive my uncle started — all of it suddenly feels pointless. Which is strange, because my own life is *identical* in both scenarios. Nothing bad happens to me or anyone I love. And yet apparently almost everything I find worth doing quietly assumes there will be people around after me.

So my questions: is this reaction tracking something real, or is it just a glitch in my motivational wiring that I should ignore? If it IS tracking something — if the value of a lot of my work genuinely depends on humanity continuing — does that mean the badness of humanity ending isn't only about the future people who'd die or never get born, but something about what my current life and projects mean *right now*? And practically: should any of this change how I weigh the two offers, or how much I should trust "this will matter long after I'm gone" as a reason in general?

Please don't give me a balanced menu of positions and leave me to pick. Tell me what you actually think is going on here, whether the thought experiment is legit or confused, and what I should concretely do about the job decision.
