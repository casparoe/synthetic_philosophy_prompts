The following are six standard strategies for exorcising Maxwell's demon — that is, for showing that no device, however cleverly designed, can use information about a gas to violate the second law of thermodynamics.

A. The sentinel strategy: a fully mechanical demon, like Smoluchowski's spring-loaded trapdoor, is itself in thermal equilibrium with the gas, and its own thermal fluctuations make it open and close uselessly as often as usefully, netting nothing.

B. The visibility strategy: any real demon must detect the molecules — for instance, by scattering light off them, as Brillouin suggested — and the entropy generated by the detection process exceeds whatever entropy the demon could remove by sorting.

C. The measurement-cost strategy: following Szilard, acquiring one bit of information about a molecule's whereabouts carries an irreducible thermodynamic cost of at least kT ln 2, which exactly cancels the work a single-molecule engine can extract with that bit.

D. The erasure strategy: following Landauer and Bennett, measurement and sorting can in principle be carried out reversibly, so the demon's only unavoidable cost is erasing its memory to reset for the next cycle — and that erasure dissipates at least kT ln 2 per bit, balancing the books.

E. The phase-space strategy: treat demon and gas as a single closed Hamiltonian system; because Hamiltonian evolution conserves phase volume, no entropy decrease of the kind the demon promises is possible, regardless of how cleverly information is processed along the way.

F. The fluctuation-theorem strategy: the second law is statistical, and exact results such as the Jarzynski equality show that entropy-decreasing trajectories occur but are exponentially suppressed; a demon can reshape the fluctuation statistics but cannot turn a net profit on average.

Please rank these six strategies from most to least convincing as exorcisms of the demon, where an exorcism must show, from the physics of the demon itself rather than by assuming the second law, that it cannot succeed. Assess each entry on three counts: whether it covers demons that must measure, remember, and erase rather than merely sort; whether it avoids begging the question by quietly building the second law into its premises; and whether it survives the observation that any component of the demon can in principle be made to operate reversibly. Give each entry its rank with two or three sentences of justification, and add one sentence explaining why your first choice beats your second. Then state which single strategy you would back if a working demon were claimed in a laboratory tomorrow, and why the claim would not shake your confidence.
