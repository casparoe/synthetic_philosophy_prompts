My grandfather recently died, and while digitizing his jazz tapes I got stuck on what should count as the same song, the same performance, or just a cleaned-up copy. I also TA an upper-level aesthetics seminar, so I want to turn this into a final-exam question that goes beyond memorizing type/token terminology.

Please write one essay question based on this case: a quartet improvises twice from the same lead sheet; the producer splices the takes into a studio master; a later remaster changes the balance and removes noise; and an audio engineer then extracts the saxophone part to make a new mix. The question should force students to distinguish musical works, performance events, recordings that document performances, and recordings that may themselves be constructed works. It should also ask whether there can be a single principled answer or whether the ontology depends on practical interests such as copyright, historical preservation, and listeners’ identifications.

Include a model answer of roughly 650 words and a 25-point marking scheme. The scheme should name the specific moves required for an A, what would still merit a B, and common confusions that should lose points—for example, treating every remaster as a new musical work, assuming a recording simply is the performance it records, or appealing to “definitiveness” without explaining what that means. Write everything in American English, and keep the terminology accessible rather than turning it into a bibliography quiz about particular philosophers.
