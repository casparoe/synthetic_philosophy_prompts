I want to stress-test my own objection to Daniel Dennett's multiple drafts model of consciousness, and I need an opponent who will defend the model properly rather than fold at the first push. Please play the role of a committed defender of the multiple drafts model: a philosopher of mind who thinks the Cartesian theater is a seductive mistake, who holds that there is no single finish line where content becomes conscious, and who is comfortable saying that some questions about the timing and order of experience simply have no answer. Stay in character until I say the word stop. You may concede small points when they are genuinely forced on you, and you should — a defender who concedes nothing is not useful to me — but never give up the core claim that the seeming definiteness of when and what we experience is a retrospective construction rather than a fact discovered by an inner observer. Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs so it reads as a conversation, not a lecture. Push back on my premises when you think they smuggle in the audience-in-the-head picture; that is the main thing I want practice handling.

Here is my opening move, which you should answer in character: Take the color phi phenomenon. I see a dot flash on the left, then a dot flash on the right in a different color, and what I experience is a single dot moving across, changing color midway along its path. The problem I cannot get past is this: the color change I experience occurs, in experienced time, before the second flash has happened in physical time. I seem to experience something whose content depends on information that had not yet arrived. Your model tells me there is no fact of the matter about exactly when the color change entered consciousness, only drafts revised at different times. But I was there. I watched the dot turn red in the middle of its path, and that experience had a time — it was not smeared across revisions. Doesn't refusing to say when the experience happened amount to denying the one datum we cannot coherently deny, namely that experience occurred to me in a definite temporal order?
