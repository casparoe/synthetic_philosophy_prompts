I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so forgive me if this is a thing everyone already knows about. I keep noticing the same pattern when I build characters, and I want to know if it has a name and a literature.

Say I want a character to change. If I just give them a new opinion -- they start thinking capital punishment is wrong, say -- nothing much happens to them as a person, they just have one more view. But if I want a *deep* change, I have to go after one of the things they were never even considering. Like: the character has never once asked themselves whether their mother loves them, not because they're sure of the answer but because the question isn't one that occurs, it's just the ground everything else stands on. Their whole personality, their career, how they treat people, is built on top of it. Then something happens -- a letter surfaces, whatever -- and it's not that they now doubt it, it's that it turns out it was never true. And here's the thing I keep hitting as a writer: when that goes, the character doesn't re-examine each belief that rested on it one by one. They don't sit there thinking "well, if mother didn't love me, then I should reconsider my attitude to X, and also to Y." The whole person just... rearranges, all at once, and afterwards they can't even say exactly what changed, becuase the thing that changed was never a belief they held in the first place -- it was the stuff that made their beliefs intelligible.

Same pattern in reverse: some of my characters have things they treat as unquestionable that turn out to be load-bearing for their worldview but which, looked at coldly, are just contingent facts -- a family story, a version of an event from childhood. You can't argue these people out of it with evidence, because it never got in by being evidenced.

So: is there a name for these things -- the never-questioned, never-justified ground-floor commitments that you can lose without ever having doubted them? I know this is somewhere in the vicinity of skepticism stuff, and maybe Wittgenstein late in life was poking at it, but what I specifically care about is the *collapse-and-rearrangement* dynamic -- that losing one of these isn't belief revision, it's something else. Is that distinction drawn anywhere, and does the literature say what happens to everything that was built on top?
