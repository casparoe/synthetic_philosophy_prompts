I'm writing a post for a long-running rationalist forum, part of an informal sequence on population ethics and longtermism. My recurring frustration with these discussions is that the nonidentity problem gets collapsed too quickly into "person-affecting versus impersonal," which hides where the disagreement actually lives. I want to give readers a sorting instrument instead of a label to nod at.

Please write a decision tree that leads from the bare problem — an act determines both who will exist and how well their lives go, and the resulting people seem to have no complaint against it — to a named response. Leaves must include at least: the impersonal response on which the outcome is worse without being worse for anyone; comparative harm cashed out via counterpart relations across possible worlds; non-comparative harm, i.e., wronging someone by giving them a life below some decency threshold; narrow and wide person-affecting views as separate leaves, with the tree making clear which question splits them; a rights- or complaint-based view on which the child has a legitimate grievance despite having no better-off alternative; and the skeptical response that dissolves the problem by denying that existence and nonexistence can be compared for a person's sake at all.

Each branch node should be a concrete case, not an abstract principle, and must be phrased so a reader can answer without knowing which leaf it leads to. Use at least these three: a policy that depletes resources and predictably shifts which people are conceived, leaving later generations worse off than the different people who would otherwise have existed; a choice between two disease-screening programs, one acting before conception and one after, which avert the same number of disorders by causing different people to be born; and one case of my own — a couple can implant embryo A now, knowing a transient medication will give the resulting person a serious but not life-wrecking disability, or discard it and implant embryo B next month with no such risk.

Note any answer combinations nobody occupies and diagnose why (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot distinguish. Finally, for each leaf, add one sentence on the verdict it delivers in the depletion case that its defenders tend to mention least.
