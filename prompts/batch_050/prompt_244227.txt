I'm a third-year taking a philosophy of physics course and our prof lets students submit practice exam questions for the final (the best ones actually get used, so I want mine to be good). Write me one essay question on effective field theory and the autonomy of scales, answerable in about 60 minutes.

The thing is it shouldn't be answerable just by explaining what an EFT is or reciting the renormalization group machinery about relevant/irrelevant operators. I want the question to force students into the actual tension: EFTs supposedly show that low-energy physics is insensitive to UV details (universality, decoupling, all that), which people take to support the explanatory autonomy of the macro-level theory. But at the same time, an EFT is literally defined as the low-energy limit of some more fundamental theory, and its parameters are fixed from above — which looks like a point for the reductionists. The question should make them take and defend a position on which lesson the framework really teaches, and ideally make them say something precise about what "autonomy" would even have to mean for the EFT case to count as evidence for it.

Along with the question, give me a model answer of roughly 500 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 built around five must-hit points with the marks for each. For each point, add a one-line note on how students typically screw it up — e.g. sliding between "the low-energy theory is insensitive to UV details" and "the low-energy theory is independent of the UV theory," which I'm pretty sure are different claims but everyone conflates them.
