So I got into it with a buddy of mine over beers about the whole "would there have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler" thing, and we realized we were both just asserting stuff. He said Hitler was obviously replaceable -- Versailles, the Depression, German nationalism, antisemitism, all the structural ingredients were there, someone would have ridden that wave. I said the Nazi vote was collapsing by late 1932 and it took a very specific backroom deal (Papen convincing Hindenburg) to put a guy with Hitler's exact combination of talents and obsessions in the chancellery, and that a generic nationalist authoritarian -- a restored monarchy, a Schleicher military government -- is what the structure was actually pointing at. Neither of us could move the other, and I suspect that's because neither of us has a clear idea of what "structural explanation" and "Great Man explanation" even predict differently here.

So take the rise of the Third Reich, 1918 to 1939, and run it through the Great Man vs. structural explanation debate properly. First do the mapping: what does each side actually claim about this episode, which specific facts (the electoral trajectory of the NSDAP, the January 1933 appointment, the failed 1923 putsch, Hitler's rhetorical skill, the Enabling Act vote, the absence of any plausible successor figure inside the party) does each side treat as load-bearing, and which does each have to wave away? Then -- this is the part my friend and I totally missed -- split the counterfactual into three separate questions that might have different answers: no Hitler means no Nazi regime? no Hitler means no second European war? no Hitler means no Holocaust? A structurally-caused fascistoid Germany could plausibly give you war but a very different racial policy, or vice versa, so don't let one answer do the work of three. Then use the natural comparisons as controls: Mussolini's Italy is fascism without a Hitler (and had a notably different relationship to racial extermination until late), and maybe Spain or the USSR if they earn their place. Finally, tell me what the episode does to the theories themselves: is "Great Man theory" even falsifiable here, or does it just retro-fit charisma to whoever won? And does structural explanation actually explain why it was the Nazis specifically rather than some other vehicle, or does it go silent exactly at the interesting point?

Format-wise, please structure it as: "The mapping" (~250 words, what each theory predicts and which facts it leans on), "Three counterfactuals, three answers" (~300 words), "The control cases" (~150 words), "Where each theory goes quiet" (~150 words), and "Verdict" (~100 words, who wins my bar argument, or whether the argument was malformed from the start).
