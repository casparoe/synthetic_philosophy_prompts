The continuum hypothesis (CH) sits at an unusual crossroads: it was the first problem on Hilbert's famous list, it is known to be independent of the standard ZFC axioms (via Gödel's constructible universe and Cohen's forcing), and yet set theorists continue to disagree sharply about whether the independence result settles the matter or merely relocates it. Several distinct research programs and philosophical positions are live: the search for new axioms justified intrinsically (as extending the iterative conception of set) or extrinsically (by their fruitful consequences); forcing axioms such as Martin's Maximum, which decide the size of the continuum as aleph_2 and hence refute CH; Woodin's Ultimate L program, which aims at an inner model accommodating large cardinals in which CH holds; and pluralist or multiverse views, on which the independence phenomenon shows there is no single question left to answer, only different set-theoretic universes to study.

Suppose you are advising a small research group -- say, five people with three to five years of funding, competent in forcing and inner model theory but not at the frontier of large cardinal theory -- that wants to contribute to the resolution, or at least the clarification, of the status of CH. Which open questions should they prioritize, and in what order? I am less interested in a survey of the landscape than in a defended agenda. For each question you prioritize, explain:

1. Why it is upstream of other work -- that is, which other questions about CH, about the search for new axioms, or about the philosophy of set theory depend on its answer, such that settling it would change how the rest is pursued.
2. How tractable it is with existing tools, versus which questions you would deprioritize because current methods plausibly cannot touch them within the time frame.
3. Whether settling it would actually change mathematical practice or merely redescribe the disagreement -- and how you weigh that.

Please commit to a definite ranking rather than presenting the options and leaving the choice to me. State the criteria your ranking uses (dependency, tractability, and consequence for practice, or others you think better), acknowledge the trade-offs between them where they pull apart, and say what specific developments -- a technical breakthrough, a failed proof attempt, a widely accepted axiom candidate -- would cause you to reorder the list.
