Design the one bet that carries the whole session: say what it must have (a real edge, repeatability, a genuine chance of loss at reasonable stakes) and what it must not have. Map the misconceptions I should expect — I anticipate 'Kelly is log utility in disguise', 'it maximizes expected wealth', 'full Kelly is correct whenever your edge estimate is', and 'drawdowns are a psychology problem, not a mathematical one' — and say at exactly which point in the sequence each should be headed off and why that is the right point. Then give me one closing question that separates someone who understood the argument from someone who merely memorized f* = p − q/b, and tell me what I should openly decline to cover at this level (I'm assuming the Samuelson critique and the continuous-time version, but correct me).
