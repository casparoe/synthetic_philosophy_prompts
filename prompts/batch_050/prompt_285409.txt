ok so there's this thing about sphex wasps that philosophers bring up a lot (dennett talks about it iirc): the wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the entrance of its burrow, drops it, goes inside to check the burrow, comes back out, and drags the cricket in. but if an experimenter just moves the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp comes out, drags it back to the entrance, drops it, and goes in to check AGAIN. and again. as many times as you do it. it never notices the loop its in.

i want a villanelle about this. the idea is that the two refrains should be the two halves of the wasp's routine (drop it at the threshold / go in to check), and every time they come back around they should read *slightly* different -- same words, but the context of the surrounding lines should shift how they land, from "sensible procedure" to "oh no" to something sadder. the wasp shouldn't be mocked. the poem should make you uncomfortable about how much of your own day is threshold-and-checking (the re-read text thread, the fourth time opening the fridge, whatever) without ever saying that directly. no last-stanza resolution where the wasp or the speaker wakes up. end still in the loop.

19 lines, proper villanelle form, and please don't make it rhyme "wasp" with anything, that never works
