So I'm on a climate policy reading group that's been circling geoengineering for a month and I've been asked to write down where I actually stand before we do a session on it, so people can poke holes. Before I circulate it, can you audit my position for internal consistency? Here's roughly what I believe:

1. Research into stratospheric aerosol injection should continue, including small-scale outdoor experiments, because you can't govern a technology you don't understand.
2. Actual deployment would only be legitimate with broad international agreement -- something close to consent from the states that would be affected, which is basically everyone.
3. That said, if some low-lying island state facing literal existential threat from sea level rise got tired of waiting for the international community and deployed unilaterally, they'd be justified in doing so.
4. The moral hazard objection (that geoengineering research/deployment saps the will to cut emissions) is mostly empirically overblown -- people don't drive more recklessly because of seatbelts, countries don't slash mitigation budgets because of modelling studies.
5. Termination shock is the scariest thing about the whole idea: if you deploy and then stop abruptly, you get the warming all at once. So any deployment has to come with binding, century-scale international commitments to maintain the program and manage the ramp-down.

A few things I suspect but can't prove: (2) and (3) are probably fighting each other and I don't know which one I'd actually give up. (4) and (5) feel fine together but maybe I'm wrong. And I have a nagging feeling (5) causes problems somewhere else that I haven't spotted.

Give me the audit as a JSON object with these fields:
- "tensions": a list of objects, one per tension you find, ordered most to least severe, each with fields "commitments" (which of my numbered points are involved), "severity" (high/medium/low), "why_real" (what actually forces the conflict -- don't just gesture at vibes), and "cheapest_fix" (the minimal revision that resolves it and what I'd lose by making it)
- "apparent_tensions": a list of pairs that look like they conflict but dissolve on inspection, each with fields "commitments" and "why_fine"
- "one_thing_that_has_to_go": if exactly one of my five commitments had to be dropped or seriously weakened to make the rest cohere, which one, stated in a single sentence

Please actually rank things rather than declaring everything equally problematic -- a list where every tension is "serious" is useless to me.
