A coworker who follows AI safety summarized work on chain-of-thought faithfulness for me like this:

Chain-of-thought is supposed to make an LLM's reasoning visible. If the final answer follows from the displayed steps, we can treat the text as an explanation of why the model gave that answer. But experiments show that models can be influenced by hints, stereotypes, or a target answer without mentioning that influence, and can produce a polished rationale for a conclusion reached some other way. So CoT is fundamentally post-hoc confabulation: it may improve benchmark performance, but the words are not doing the reasoning and are worthless for auditing.

The last part feels too strong, but I cant say exactly why. How fair is this summary? Separate what researchers would broadly accept from what overstates the results. In particular, distinguish CoT as a place where computation happens, evidence about computation happening elsewhere, and a report of the model's internal state. Could a rationale be causally relevant but incomplete or misleading? Could editing the CoT change the answer without making the original CoT fully faithful? Finally, give me a practical rule for how much trust an engineer should put in model-generated reasoning logs.
