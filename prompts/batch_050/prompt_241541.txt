Every year at Thanksgiving my sister-in-law Dana and I get into it about charity. I volunteer at a no-kill animal shelter and every December I do a little fundraiser for it among family. Dana thinks this is basically a scam -- not that the money gets stolen, but that spending on pets while people are suffering is messed up. Meanwhile she gives a big chunk of money every year to her church, which among other things funds these two-week "mission trips" where volunteers fly abroad to help build stuff in poor villages. Everything I've read about those trips suggests they're mostly feel-good tourism and the locals often have to redo the work after. So we both think the other's donation is somewhere between useless and actively harmful.

So this year Dana proposes a deal: she'll give $300 to my shelter if I give $300 to her church's mission fund. Her pitch is that we each care way more about our own cause than we hate the other's, so we both come out ahead versus the status quo where we just donate to our own thing and glare at each other. And weirdly the math seems to check out even by my own lights? Like, I think $300 to the mission trip does a little bit of harm, but I think $300 to the shelter does way more good, so the package deal is a net win even for me, and presumably a net win for her by her own values too. So refusing feels like I'm just protecting my own sense of purity at the expense of actual animals. But it also feels gross, like I'd literally be funding something I think is bad and calling it strategy.

Two things are really bugging me though. First: Dana was probably going to donate to the church anyway this year, so am I actually getting anything, or am I just paying $300 for the warm feeling of a "deal"? Like does the trade even change what happens in the world? Second, and this one kept me up: the whole deal only works because Dana still thinks mission trips are great. If I actually convinced her they're harmful, she'd stop giving to them, which is what I want, but then she'd also have no reason to trade with me -- she'd just tell me to donate to the shelter myself. So the deal kind of depends on her staying wrong, and taking it feels like betting against my own arguments. Is this just smart cooperation between people who disagree, or is there something actually fishy about profiting off someone elses mistaken values? And should I take the deal?
