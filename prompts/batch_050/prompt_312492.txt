I host a small philosophy podcast and next week's guest is a philosopher defending a coherent-extrapolated-volition style view: roughly, what we ought to want is what we'd want if we were smarter, better informed, more reflective, and had "grown up" differently. She's sharp and tends to handle objections by saying the extrapolation "washes out" the problem cases. Draft eight or nine interview questions that press on the view's actual mechanics rather than letting her restate it, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she'll most likely give. In particular I want:

1. At least one question on the order-of-operations problem: if you idealize someone's factual beliefs first you get one volition, but if you idealize their values first and then inform them you can get a different one -- so which parameter gets adjusted first, and by what non-circular criterion? Follow-up for when she says the iterations converge.
2. At least one on adaptive preferences: the person beaten down by circumstance whose current desires are humble might, under extrapolation, end up endorsing exactly the humility the circumstances trained into them -- does the view have a way to tell genuine contentment from adaptation, or does it just launder whatever the counterfactual spits out?
3. At least one on why present-me should defer to extrapolated-me at all, given that extrapolated-me might be a stranger with different tastes who I wouldn't even like. Anticipate the "he's just you with better information" reply and press on how much revision is too much before the continuity claim collapses.
4. At least one on divergence between people: if extrapolated volitions don't converge across humanity, the view seems to either license majority volition over minorities or admit it's empty at the collective level. Push on which horn she takes.
5. One closing question on what could empirically or conceptually count against the view -- e.g., if actual deliberation experiments show people's preferences keep drifting under idealized conditions rather than settling, is that a refutation or just a modeling inconvenience?
