I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy, trained mainly in analytic normative ethics, with a working reading knowledge of Sanskrit, and I'm trying to settle a dissertation area. I've become interested in treating karma not primarily as a doctrine of rebirth or as a piece of soteriology, but as a theory of moral desert: roughly, the claim that there is an impersonal, non-agent-mediated connection between the moral quality of what people do and what they later undergo, such that they get what they deserve. Reading this against the contemporary desert literature — desert bases, proportionality, the distinction between desert and entitlement, the question of whether desert requires an institutional or personal dispenser — it seems to me that many of the standard puzzles reappear in sharpened form: desert without anyone doing the deserting, desert claims spanning multiple lives and hence multiple candidate subjects, apparently disproportionate outcomes explained by appeal to unobservable past action, and the uncomfortable implication that victims of misfortune deserve their suffering, which seems to license indifference toward present injustice.

Could you help me identify which open questions in this area matter most and which two or three I should prioritize for roughly three years of work? I'm not after a survey of the territory, and definitely not a bibliography — I want a defended ordering. For each question you put near the top, say what makes it worth the years: whether it is upstream of other questions in the area in the sense that their resolution depends on it, how tractable it is for someone whose method is philosophical argument rather than philology, how neglected it is relative to its importance, and what it would imply for adjacent debates (for instance, whether an impersonal karma-desert is even coherent, or collapses into a merely causal thesis, bears on whether Western desert theorists are wrong to assume a dispenser). Also tell me what would change the ranking: what kinds of objections, or what developments in the desert literature or in comparative work, would move a question up or down over the next few years, so that I can revisit the choice rather than marry it.

One more request. Some of these questions plainly hinge on contested interpretive claims about the source traditions — for example, how far karma in classical Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain texts functions as a desert claim at all, as opposed to a causal-soteriological mechanism to which desert vocabulary is only loosely attached, and whether the traditions themselves distinguish desert from mere consequence. Where a question on your list presupposes a contested reading, flag it explicitly as contested, indicate how the priority changes on the alternative readings, and don't present any one scholarly interpretation as the consensus where there isn't one.
