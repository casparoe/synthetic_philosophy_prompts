Is there a name for this? I got into an argument at an auction preview last month and I havn't been able to let it go. Here's the pattern. A painting sells for a fortune as a Vermeer. Then it's exposed as the work of a twentieth-century forger — the famous case everyone knows is van Meegeren, who even had one of his fakes end up in Göring's collection — and the value craters overnight, the museum takes it off the wall, it's a curiosity at best. Fine, that part nobody disputes. But then a few decades pass, the forger becomes famous in his own right, and the very same canvases start getting exhibited and auctioned again, now honestly labelled as his, and they pull real money. Not joke money, not irony money — people genuinely compete for them. Same object, same paint layer, not one molecule different, but it's gone from "a Vermeer" to "worthless fake" to "a genuine van Meegeren," and both the price and the entire way people stand in front of it flips at each step.

Second instance, less famous and I made up the details but it happens all the time: a sideboard long catalogued as the work of some celebrated furniture maker gets reattributed to his apprentice. Value drops. Twenty years later the apprentice has a monograph and a cult following, and the same sideboard is now proudly "an early So-and-so" and worth more than it was as the master's. Nobody was defrauded the second time around. The object's career is: misattributed, disgraced, reborn as itself.

So the configuration I want a word for is roughly: an object whose value lives in its authorship gets stripped of the identity that gave it value, and instead of staying at zero it settles into a new identity under which it is, weirdly, authentic again — it never was the thing it was sold as, but it now openly is the thing it actually is. Authentic as a former fake, I guess?

I know the adjacent territory exists: the whole philosophical debate about why originals beat perfect copies (the argument that even if you can't tell two pictures apart right now, the possibility that you could come to tell them apart already makes them different), provenance research, aura, all that. But everything I've found is about why the fake loses to the original. I can't find anything on the fake that gets promoted. Does this have a name? If it doesn't, coin one and define it tightly enough that I can separate it from: (a) plain celebrity value, where anything a famous person touched goes up; (b) kitsch or ironic collecting, where the pleasure is in the badness; and (c) ordinary scarcity stories, where something just gets rarer over time. And give me the boundary cases: does it count if the forger stayed anonymous and it's the legend of the fake itself that's collected? Does it count when the object was authorized all along, like bronzes cast after a sculptor's death from the original molds and sold as authentic — is that the same move or a different one? Where does the phenomenon stop?

And since I might be forcing a distinction that isn't really there: tell me what would change your mind. What example or consideration would show this is just celebrity provenance wearing a costume, and where is your own definition most likely to crack?
