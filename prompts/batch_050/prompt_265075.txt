I'm a software engineer with zero formal philosophy background, and I just finished I Am a Strange Loop (well, most of it) after my coworker would not shut up about it. The picture I walked away with: the self is like the pattern you get when you point a video camera at a monitor showing the camera's own feed. There's no extra ingredient in the loop — just camera, screen, and light going around — but a stable swirling pattern appears that you can point at, that reacts when you poke it, that seems to exist at a level above the hardware. My coworker's takeaway: the "I" is exactly that, a self-referential symbol the brain spins up by modeling itself, and therefore (his word) the self is basically a useful hallucination — nobody home, just the loop. He's now using this to claim that personal identity puzzles are all confused: teleportation dilemmas, fission cases, whether you'd survive uploading, all dissolve once you see the "you" you'd be trying to preserve was never their in the first place.

I'm suspicious the analogy is carrying that whole conclusion. So: how far does the video-feedback picture actually hold as a model of the self? Give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of the camera, the screen, the pixels, the lag, the stable pattern — and then where it breaks. Things nagging at me: the feedback pattern doesn't perceive anything, doesn't care about anything, doesn't model the world or itself the way the brain's self-symbol supposedly does (or does it? maybe I'm underestimating what the video loop is doing). Also, the pattern only exists for an outside viewer, whereas my self seems to exist for me — is that a fatal disanalogy or is that literally the point Hofstadter is making? And then the payoff question: does my coworker's conclusion — self is a hallucination, so identity questions are pseudo-questions — survive once the disanalogies are on the table? Or does "illusion" talk smuggle in a viewer (an illusion for whom?) and quietly reintroduce the very self it was meant to dissolve? And if the video loop is the wrong source domain, what's a better everyday analogy for a self-referential symbol? I keep reaching for quines, or a pointer in a program whose target is its own struct, but I suspect those break too, just in different places — tell me where.
