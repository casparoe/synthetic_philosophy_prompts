Two people in my group chat have been arguing about persuasive design for like six months and getting nowhere, so I want to force them into an adversarial collaboration. One of them (call her the manipulation camp) thinks stuff like infinite scroll, pull-to-refresh variable rewards, autoplay, and streak mechanics is a serious form of manipulation -- worse than ordinary advertising because it targets attention and habit rather than giving you reasons, and that the attention economy is basically an arms race to bypass people's autonomy. The other (the commerce camp) thinks this is moral panic: designers making products engaging is just what selling things has always looked like, users can leave whenever they want, and the manipulation framing disrespects people's agency and would condemn every enjoyable product ever made.

Draft the joint statement these two could actually both sign after a good-faith process. To make it concrete, have them hash out their positions on one specific hypothetical I've invented: an app called Skroll that runs what I'd call a "regret audit" -- every Sunday it shows you how many hours you spent in the app that week and asks you to rate, per session, whether that session felt worth it afterwards. Imagine Skroll's internal data shows that users rate about 60% of their own usage as not worth it in hindsight, and usage only drops 10% after the audits are introduced. Both camps have to take a position on what this shows and whether it changes anything.

Format it in exactly these sections, in this order:
1. "Claims we both sign" -- a numbered list of claims about attention, habit formation, autonomy, and what the regret-audit data does and doesn't establish. Keep each claim substantive, no vacuous both-sides-are-partly-right stuff. Around 300 words.
2. "Where we still disagree" -- a table with one row per disagreement. Column one is the disagreement in neutral wording, column two is the manipulation camp's position, column three is the commerce camp's position. Each side's position has to be phrased the way that side would phrase it, not a strawman. Around 250 words.
3. "Cruxes" -- the two or three sub-questions where settling them would actually move one or both of them, and for each crux a test, argument, or observation both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. The regret-audit data should show up here somewhere. Around 350 words.
4. "What turned out to be verbal" -- a short section (~100 words) on any part of their fight that you think dissolves once terms get pinned down, and which terms.

Don't adjudicate who's right overall, and don't be wishy-washy in the disagreements table -- if they genuinly can't agree, write that down plainly.
