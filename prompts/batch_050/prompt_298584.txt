I'm a grad student putting together a paper on the death passages in the Zhuangzi, and my advisor told me my chapter outline is "a tour of everything everyone already says." Fair. I want to push back on that, so here's where I'm stuck. The passage I keep circling is from chapter 6, the "Great and Most Honoured Master" chapter, in Legge's translation: "The True men of old knew nothing of the love of life or of the hatred of death. Entrance into life occasioned them no joy; the exit from it awakened no resistance. Composedly they went and came." My question is basically: what are the ways to read this, and am I missing one?

The readings I already have, which you don't need to re-explain to me: (1) the Stoic-adjacent reading where fearing death is just a cognitive mistake, since death is one more natural transformation and the wise person accepts the process like they accept the seasons -- this is the one that leans on the episode where Zhuangzi drums on a tub after his wife dies. (2) the skeptic/agnostic reading, where the point isn't that death is fine but that we have no idea whether it's bad, since for all we know the dead regret ever clinging to life, so the passage is deflating our confidence rather than recommending anything. (3) the anti-valuing reading, where the real target is the preference for life over death itself, because having fixed preferences between the two is exactly the kind of this-vs-that discrimination the text attacks elsewhere. (4) the playful/therapeutic reading, where the sage's equanimity isn't a doctrine at all but a performance meant to loosen our grip, and treating it as a thesis about death already misses the joke.

What I want is one more reading that isn't on this list and isn't a mashup or a special case of these. It should be something you could actually defend as an interpretation of that specific passage, not just of Zhuangzi's vibe in general -- so tell me how it handles the details, like why it says the true men "knew nothing" of loving life rather than that they mastered or suppressed it, and why the text describes them as composed in both directions, coming and going. And be honest with me about whether the new reading is genuinely better than my four or just different, because if I bring my advisor a fifth reading that's secretly reading (2) in a hat, I'll hear about it.
