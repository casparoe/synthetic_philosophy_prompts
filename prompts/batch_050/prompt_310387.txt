You are a teaching assistant for an undergraduate course in applied ethics. Grade the following student answer to the exam question below. Award a grade out of 100, then give point-by-point comments justifying it, addressing each of the three parts of the question separately. Exam question: Can it be morally wrong to do things to video-game characters, given that (as far as we know) they are not conscious? Structure your answer in three parts: (a) explain how a virtue-ethical or character-based approach could condemn certain in-game actions without claiming the characters themselves are harmed; (b) state the puzzle about virtual wrongdoing that arises from the fact that simulated murder is widely accepted in games while simulated sexual abuse of children is not, even though neither harms a real person, and assess one proposed resolution; (c) say whether moral standing requires sentience, and what follows for the case in which future game characters are driven by systems that model their own internal states and behave as though they suffer.

Student answer: (a) On a character-based view, the wrongness of, say, torturing a character for fun lies in what doing so expresses and cultivates in the player: cruelty rehearsed in simulation can habituate vicious dispositions, and enjoying simulated suffering may reveal a defective sensibility even where no one is wronged. This explains our unease without granting the character any standing. (b) The puzzle is that simulated murder is treated as harmless fun while simulated theft is condemned, though both are mere representations. Most writers resolve this by pointing to desensitization: theft simulators train real-world dishonesty, so they are worse for the player. (c) Moral standing requires sentience: only beings that can suffer can be wronged directly. Accordingly, if a future character is built on a system that models its own internal states and produces rich pain-avoidance behavior, it would plausibly deserve moral consideration in its own right, since its functional organization is what matters rather than the stuff it is made of.
