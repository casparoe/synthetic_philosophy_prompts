I'm writing up my philosophy-of-science position for a reading group and want to know if it coheres before I present it. Roughly where I've landed: (1) falsifiability is the demarcation line — astrology and Freudian psychoanalysis are pseudoscience precisely because no possible observation would count against them; (2) the hypothesis that there is life somewhere else in the universe is a scientific hypothesis, which is why SETI is definately a legitimate scientific enterprise; (3) given two rival theories neither of which has been falsified, you should put your money on the one that has survived the riskier tests, since that gives you reason to expect it'll keep working; (4) Popper was right that induction has no rational justification and science gets along fine without it; (5) when a prediction fails, revising an auxiliary assumption instead of ditching the theory is often perfectly good science, not face-saving. Where does this set actually strain, and where does it just look like it does? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one tell me the cheapest revision and what it costs me.
