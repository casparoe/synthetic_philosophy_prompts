At a philosophy of mind workshop I attended last week, a speaker defended the extended mind thesis almost entirely by leaning on an analogy. She said: Otto's notebook, in the well-known thought experiment, stands to Otto exactly as biological memory stands to Inga. Inga consults a memory stored in her brain to recall the museum's address; Otto consults an address stored in his notebook; in both cases, she argued, the information sits there dispositional before use, is trusted without re-verification, is retrieved fluently and automatically, and was endorsed by the agent when it was first recorded. So, she concluded, anyone who counts Inga's brain-stored information as a belief must, on pain of an arbitrary prejudice in favor of what happens inside the skull, count Otto's notebook entry as a belief too. The analogy was doing all the work: the whole argument was that the two cases match feature for feature.

A respondent pushed back with a counter-analogy of his own. He said the notebook is to Otto as a calculator is to an accountant -- a tool the agent deliberately picks up, operates, and puts down -- and that no one thinks the calculator's registers contain the accountant's beliefs, even if the accountant trusts it completely and uses it daily. He also claimed the speaker's analogy quietly smuggles in the very features under dispute: Inga's memories are integrated with the rest of her cognitive economy in ways that shape her reasoning even when she isn't consulting them, they are subject to reconstruction and distortion, they can't be left on a bus, and they don't survive their bearer being deceived about their provenance. The speaker replied that each of these is either an inessential difference or one that some biological memories also exhibit, so none of them breaks the analogy at the joint that matters.

Please audit both analogies. Give me the explicit correspondence mapping for each -- what plays the role of the belief, the storage medium, retrieval, trust, and endorsement in the Inga case, the Otto case, and the accountant case -- then identify which disanalogies the respondent raises actually threaten the speaker's intended conclusion and which are merely differences that any two storage systems would exhibit. In particular, tell me whether the parity-style reasoning (same functional role, therefore same status) needs the analogy to be near-perfect or only to hold at the right level of abstraction, and whether the tool analogy is a genuine rival description of Otto's situation or just the same facts re-narrated with skull-favoring vocabulary. Finally, say whether the extended-mind conclusion survives the audit, and if neither analogy can carry it, suggest what a more apt comparison would be. Please write your answer in American English.
