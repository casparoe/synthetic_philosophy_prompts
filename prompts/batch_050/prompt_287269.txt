I'm putting together a list of objections to etiological (selected-effects) theories of biological function for a paper. So far I have: (1) Boorse's first-instance case -- a brand-new advantageous mutation seems to have a function on its very first occurrence, before there's been any selection on it at all; (2) swampman cases -- a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of you popped into existence by accident seems to have a heart with the function of pumping blood despite having zero history; (3) vestigial traits -- things like the appendix seem to keep a function or lose one in ways that don't line up cleanly with their actual selection history; and (4) the epistemic objection -- we ascribe functions confidently all the time without knowing anything about selection history, which would be odd if that's literally what function is. Give me one more objection that is structurally different from all of these -- not just a new case of the same form, and not a combination of two of them -- and say what it puts pressure on that these four don't. Also tell me honestly whether you think the etiological theorist has a good reply to it.
