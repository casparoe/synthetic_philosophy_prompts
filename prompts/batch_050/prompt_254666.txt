At a philosophy reading group last week, a friend shut down a discussion of moral objectivity by insisting that God is good by definition — asking whether anything God wills could be wrong, he said, is like asking whether a bachelor is married. I suspect the sentence is doing more work than he realizes, but I can't articulate what. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses it might be expressing — a claim about the meaning of the word good, a claim that goodness is identical to God's nature, a claim that God's will creates the moral facts, or merely a claim about how we come to know moral truths — and for each say whether it is trivially true, substantive but defensible, or confused? Then tell me which readings would actually deliver his conclusion that no divine command could possibly be wrong, and what Euthyphro-style pressure each surviving reading still faces. I'd like the readings set out as a menu I can bring back to the group rather than a single favored interpretation.
