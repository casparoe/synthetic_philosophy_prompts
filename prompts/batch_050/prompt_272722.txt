I'm designing a one-semester grad seminar on thought experiments as a method -- not a survey of famous cases, but a course on how intuition pumps actually work on us and when they shouldn't be trusted. I have maybe 12 weeks and each week is one big question. Which open questions about intuition pumps should get those weeks, and in what order?

To make this concrete, here's the taxonomy I'm working with, though you can push back on it. I suspect thought experiments persuade through at least two pretty different mechanisms: compression, where the case works by stripping away detail so a general principle becomes vivid (the principle was doing the work all along), and staging, where the case forces you to actually perform a judgment inside a constructed scenario, and the punch comes from you catching yourself judging. If that's roughly right, different failure modes attach to each: compression pumps can hide the detail that would have changed your verdict, staging pumps can exploit quirks of how judgments get elicited rather than reveal anything about the subject matter. But maybe this distinction doesn't survive scrutiny, which is itself a candidate week.

Candidate questions I can think of: whether there's any general test for when an intuition elicited by a hypothetical deserves evidentiary weight; whether the order and framing effects found in experimental philosophy actually undermine the method or just its sloppy uses; why intuition pumps seem to do real work in ethics but mostly rhetorical work in, say, physics; whether a good pump can be defeated by a better-constructed counter-pump or only by theory; and whether training people on a pump's mechanism inoculates them or not.

Give me a defended ordering, not just a list: what makes a question worth a week of a smart student's time, which of these are upstream of the others (i.e. answering one changes how you'd approach the rest), which are tractable in a seminar versus which are basically empirical projects wearing a seminar costume, and what would make you reorder. Also tell me which question on my list you'd cut entirely as overrated, and what I'm not asking that I should be.
