Write a joke about the Euthyphro dilemma — Socrates' question of whether the pious is loved by the gods because it is pious, or pious because it is loved. The joke should land specifically for someone who grasps the dilemma's structure rather than merely recognizing the names: the humor should turn on the fact that one horn makes divine approval explanatorily redundant (the gods merely register an independent order of value they did not create) while the other makes value arbitrary (any content whatsoever could become good through sheer divine fiat). A punchline that just gestures at gods, togas, or ancient Athens without exploiting this fork misses the point; the setup and payoff should be such that the joke fails if you swap in a generic "authority makes the rules" structure in place of the actual dilemma. You may transplant the scenario to a modern setting if that helps, but the Euthyphroic fork must remain load-bearing. After the joke, include one section titled "Commentary" (roughly 80–120 words) explaining which horn the punchline exploits, how the joke handles the relation between the two horns, and where it takes liberties with the source material or leans on a contested construal — for instance, collapsing the polytheistic "the gods" into a single will, or conflating piety with moral goodness generally.
