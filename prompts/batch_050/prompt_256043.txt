I am a PhD student working in population ethics, and I am drafting an essay on the axiology of infinite populations. Below are my working notes -- a compressed statement of the puzzle at the heart of the essay and the resolution I tentatively favor. I would like substantive comments before I expand these notes into a full draft.

Working title: Enumeration Relativity in Infinite Axiology

The puzzle. Consider a universe containing infinitely many people, some with happy lives and some with unhappy lives, and suppose for simplicity that each happy life contributes +1 unit of welfare and each unhappy life −1, with infinitely many of each. If we try to evaluate this world by summing welfare in some order, the result depends entirely on the enumeration we choose. Enumerate one happy life, then one unhappy life, alternately, and the partial sums oscillate between 1 and 0 forever. Enumerate two happy lives for every unhappy life, and the partial sums march off to positive infinity. Reverse the ratio and they march off to negative infinity. Same world, same lives, same welfare facts: the "total value" is an artifact of the order in which we list the population. And there is no canonical enumeration -- the population of an infinite cosmos is not naturally given to us as a sequence.

Why I think this is not the standard problem. Much of the literature on infinite ethics I am familiar with concerns comparisons between infinite populations (how to rank worlds that all have infinite total value) or failures of dominance principles. My concern is more basic: for a single, fixed world, the notion of its total value is not even well-defined once both signs of welfare appear infinitely often. The problem arises before we get to comparing worlds, and it does not rely on any aggregative axiology beyond the idea that the value of a world is some function of a sum over lives.

Candidate resolutions I consider:

1. Give up on total value: adopt a purely comparative axiology (e.g., some limit-based or overtaking-style criterion for ranking worlds). My worry: most such criteria smuggle an enumeration back in through the sequence of finite truncations they take limits over, so the relativity resurfaces one level up.

2. Restrict attention to cases where the sum is absolutely convergent or one-signed, e.g., by imposing a boundedness condition on aggregate welfare per epoch or per region. My worry: this looks like changing the subject. The world might simply not cooperate with our convergence conditions, and an axiology that only evaluates convenient worlds is not an axiology.

3. My preferred proposal: let physics supply the missing order. Sum along a preferred foliation of spacetime -- expanding spatial slices -- and take the limit of partial sums along that foliation. Different foliations can yield different limits (for the same reason different enumerations do), so the value of an infinite world becomes foliation-relative, in the way that simultaneity is frame-relative. I argue this is a tolerable conclusion: we have independent reasons, from physics, to be suspicious of frame-independent global facts about infinite spacetimes, and axiological perspectivalism of this kind may be no worse than the perspectivalism about "the present" that many philosophers of time already accept.

Where I want comments:

- Is the puzzle correctly stated? In particular, is there some natural, enumeration-independent notion I am overlooking that assigns a definite value to mixed infinite populations (even a value like "indeterminate in a principled way")?
- Is my criticism of resolution (1) fair, or do limit-based comparative criteria genuinely avoid reintroducing an enumeration?
- Resolution (3) is where I expect the most resistance. Does foliation-relativity actually follow from the setup, or have I conflated a representational dependence (different descriptions, same fact) with a genuine metaphysical relativity? Is the analogy with simultaneity apt, or does it paper over a disanalogy -- e.g., that simultaneity is interdefinable across frames while divergent partial-sum limits are not?
- Is there a fourth option I have missed entirely?
