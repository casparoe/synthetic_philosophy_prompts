Here's where I've landed: (1) In a genuinely one-shot prisoner's dilemma between strangers, defection is the only rational move, full stop — it strictly dominates, and any argument for cooperating there has confused itself somehow. (2) Cooperation in nature and in human society is basically explained by iterated interaction: reciprocity strategies like tit-for-tat do well because the shadow of the future makes cooperation pay. (4) Nonetheless, in real-life repeated relationships with a clearly known endpoint — like a final business deal before one partner retires, or colleagues who know one of them is leaving the company next month — it can be perfectly rational for both sides to keep cooperating right up to (and including) the end.
