(1) The reflection test: hold the judgment in mind for weeks, expose yourself to the best opposing arguments, and see whether it survives; genuine preferences are supposed to outlast mere biases, which fade under scrutiny.
(2) The dumbfounding test: if I cannot articulate any reason for the judgment beyond restating it, that's evidence it came from my gut rather than from anything I actually value.
(3) The causal-origin test: trace where the attitude came from; if it arose from disgust conditioning, childhood punishment, or group conformity rather than from anything connected to the welfare of those affected, discount it.
(4) The ideal-advisor test: ask whether I'd still hold the judgment if I were fully informed about all non-moral facts and vividly aware of what it's like to be everyone affected.
