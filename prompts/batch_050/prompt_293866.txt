I am drafting the statement of commitments that will open my dissertation chapter on diachronic rationality, and before I lock it in I want an internal consistency audit — not a literature review, not a defense, just a check of whether the positions below can be jointly held.

Terminology. By the Reflection principle I mean van Fraassen's constraint that a rational agent's current credence in a proposition, conditional on their later credence in it being r, should itself be r. By conditionalization I mean the rule that upon acquiring new evidence E, and only then, one's new credences should equal one's prior credences conditional on E.

The case my chapter uses is a study I call the Rotation. On Sunday you are fully informed of the protocol and put to sleep. A coin biased two-to-one toward heads is flipped. You are awakened on Monday morning whatever the outcome; if and only if the coin lands tails, you are also awakened on Tuesday and on Wednesday, and before each of those later awakenings your memory of the preceding awakening is erased. On every awakening you remember the entire protocol but retain no memory of any earlier awakening, so no awakening is distinguishable from any other while it is happening. On Friday, after the study concludes, you are told how the coin landed.

My commitments:

1. Reflection holds without restriction: it constrains rational credence even when the agent knows in advance exactly how their future credences will be formed.

2. Conditionalization is the uniquely rational way for credences to change over time.

3. In the Rotation: on Sunday, my credence in heads should be 2/3, matching the known bias. On any awakening, my credence in heads should be 2/5 — a heads outcome produces one awakening and has probability 2/3, while a tails outcome produces three awakenings and has probability 1/3, so the awakening-weighted odds are 2/3 to 1, and an arbitrarily selected awakening is more likely to occur in a tails-run than a heads-run.

4. Forgetting is not the acquisition of evidence, so conditionalization imposes no constraint across an erasure; which credence is rational after forgetting is fixed entirely by the epistemic state one then occupies, not by one's earlier credences.

5. Knowing in advance that my credence will change gives me no reason to change it now: on Sunday I know that by Friday my credence in heads will be 0 or 1, and I take this to be entirely compatible with commitment 1.

What I want from the audit: for each pair of commitments, state whether there is a genuine tension; where there is, say how severe it is, what exactly would have to give, and what that revision costs. Where a pair only appears to conflict, explain why the appearance dissolves rather than counting it among the problems. Order the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. Show explicitly, with the arithmetic worked through, whether my Sunday credence and my awakening credence in the Rotation can be jointly held together with commitment 1. Finally, name the single revision that would restore consistency at the lowest overall cost and state plainly what I would be giving up. I have deliberately not indicated which commitment I am least attached to; recommend on the merits, not on a guess about my preferences.
