Here's the pattern in my own moral reactions that I want a name for, if there is one. I help allocate a public health budget, so these aren't totally hypothetical for me.

Case one: we can fund either a treatment that saves one kid from going blind, or a program that spares three million people one bad headache each, one afternoon only. My gut says fund the blindness treatment, and it keeps saying that no matter how many headaches you pile on. The headaches just don't add up into something that competes.

Case two: a single patient, choose for him between one day of severe agony, or a mild headache every afternoon for the rest of his (long) life, so thousands of headaches. Here my gut flips: at some point the accumulated headaches really do seem like the worse outcome for him. The adding-up feels completely legitimate.

So same headache, roughly the same arithmetic, but whether stacking is allowed seems to depend on whether the units share an owner. Inside one life, pile them up, fine. Spread across lives, the stacking feels like a trick -- the blind kid can say, none of those headaches is anything remotely like what I'd lose, so why does thier sheer number matter?
