I was arguing with my roommate about prison reform and brought up Halden (that Norwegian prison with the flatscreen TVs) and he shut me down with the line everyone repeats: in Norway, being sent to prison IS the punishment, the prison isn't there to punish you further. He used it to mean that even American-style supermax is obviously unjust since the sentence was already the loss of freedom. Ok, but that slogan feels like it's doing like five different jobs at once. Sometimes it sounds like a descriptive claim about how Norwegian law works, sometimes a normative claim about what a sentence morally licenses the state to do, sometimes it's just the empirical point that humane prisons have lower recidivism so coddling "works," and sometimes it seems to smuggle in something much stronger, like that suffering inside prison is never deserved no matter the crime. Can you separate out the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing, flag which are platitudes, which are substantive but defensible, and which are probably false (does even Norway actually treat liberty-loss as the whole punishment?), and then say which readings would actually get my roommate his conclusion about supermax? I suspect the strong reading he needs is the least plausible one but I can't quite pin down why.
