I just got out of an absurdly long argument with a friend about police abolition, and we definately talked past each other. Her refrain was: “The police are the state’s only social service that is open all night and can’t say no. That’s the whole problem.” She used this to argue that better training, civilian liaisons, and stronger oversight mostly reform the wrong thing, and that police departments should be shrunk until most current calls are handled by institutions without arrest powers. I said the same sentence could just mean police are overtasked and need better support, which sounds like an argument for reform rather than defunding or abolition.

Please unpack the different precise claims her slogan could be making. Don’t turn this into a generic pro-and-con list about policing. For each possible reading, state the exact thesis, what evidence would bear on it, whether it is a platitude, a plausible empirical claim, a contested normative claim, or probably false, and whether it actually supports reform, defunding, abolition, or none of those.

I especially want you to separate these ideas, which we kept mashing together: police as responders of last resort versus police as the institution with a monopoly on legitimate violence; the claim that police are bad at social work versus the claim that nobody with coercive powers should be doing social work; shifting particular calls to other responders versus reducing the number of situations in which any coercive response is needed; and replacing police with unarmed services versus quietly transferring coercion to psychiatric, child-welfare, immigration, or public-health institutions.

Then reconstruct the strongest version of my friend’s argument and identify the extra premise it needs to get from “police are the all-night residual agency” to “police should be defunded or abolished.” Also say whether my reformist reading could still be compatible with substantial defunding.
