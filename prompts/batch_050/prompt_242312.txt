I'm a computer science PhD student working on temporal and event-sourced data systems, and I've been sitting in on a metaphysics reading group where we've been covering the debate about persistence through time: endurantism, the view that ordinary objects are wholly present at each moment of their existence, versus perdurantism, the view that objects persist by having distinct temporal parts or stages located at different times. My strong instinct, which my reading group colleagues find suspicious, is that this dispute maps onto a distinction I work with every day: state-based designs, where the database stores the current state of an entity and overwrites it on update, versus event-sourced designs, where the "entity" is nothing over and above an append-only log of events and any current state is just a materialized view produced by replaying the log. I want to know whether my instinct is right, so I'd like you to restate the persistence debate in the vocabulary of my field as faithfully as you can, and then report what didn't survive the translation.

Concretely, the vocabulary you should translate into includes: snapshots versus event logs; replay and materialization; entity identity conferred by primary keys or event-stream IDs rather than by anything in the stored content itself; bitemporal schemas that distinguish valid time from transaction time; and the well-known fact that log and snapshot representations are interconvertible, with the choice between them driven by storage costs, replay latency, and query patterns rather than by anything deeper. Please restate each position in these terms, and also translate the central arguments on each side — at minimum the argument from temporary intrinsics (how can one and the same object be both bent and straight, if properties are had simpliciter?) and the perdurantist's invocation of temporal parts as the solution. For each argument, tell me what it becomes when expressed as a claim about logs, snapshots, and keys.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which claims translate cleanly, which have to be distorted to fit, and which have no expression at all in this vocabulary? Two candidate translation failures I want your verdict on: first, in my systems the choice between log and snapshot is a pragmatic one and both are views of the same underlying record, so if the whole metaphysical debate translates as a dispute over which representation to prefer, does that mean there is no fact of the matter about persistence, or is the interconvertibility itself the thing that fails to translate — is the metaphysician committed to there being a correct answer in a way that has no counterpart in schema design? Second, in my systems an entity's identity over time is conferred externally, by a keying decision the schema designer makes, whereas the metaphysicians seem to think persistence conditions are discovered rather than chosen. Is that a defect of my framework or evidence that the residue is less substantive than it looks? Please don't smuggle metaphysical assumptions into the translation and call them equivalents; I'd rather have an honest gap than a tidy mapping.
