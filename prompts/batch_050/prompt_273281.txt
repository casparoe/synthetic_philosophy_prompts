Objections to illusionism about phenomenal consciousness that I already know and do not need repeated:

1. The strong-illusion or regress objection: for things to seem a certain way to a subject, the seeming must itself be present to the subject, so the alleged illusion of phenomenality smuggles phenomenality back in rather than eliminating it.
2. The datum objection: illusionism denies the one claim a theory of consciousness is least permitted to deny, since that it is like something to be us is the most immediately given fact there is.
3. The zombie objection: the illusionist's explanation of our introspective judgments applies just as well to a phenomenal zombie, so it explains why we would say we are conscious without justifying our belief that we are.
4. The inconceivability objection: no one has ever genuinely conceived of phenomenality being an illusion, as opposed to mouthing the words, which suggests the position is not a real option.
5. The meta-problem objection: explaining why we judge ourselves phenomenally conscious requires explaining the specific structure of phenomenal judgments, which is at least as hard as the hard problem the view was meant to dissolve.
6. The appearance–reality collapse objection, which I take to be distinct from 2: for phenomenal states there is no gap between seeming and being -- if it seems to you that you are in pain, you are in pain -- so phenomenality is exactly the kind of thing that cannot be an illusion.

State it as precisely as you can, say which listed objection it comes closest to and how it genuinely differs, and indicate whether it attacks the coherence of illusionism or only its plausibility. Then tell me honestly whether you think it is any good -- in particular, whether a committed illusionist already has a ready reply, and if so what that reply is.
