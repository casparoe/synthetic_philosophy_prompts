After a long argument with a friend, I still cannot locate the flaw in her position on gauge freedom. She reasons that if gauge-related field configurations are different descriptions of one physical state, then that state is nothing over and above their equivalence class; consequently, an explanation assigning causal work to a particular potential configuration is not even approximately true, but merely a calculational convenience. I suspect this equivocates between saying that a representative is conventional and saying that the structure it helps express is unreal. Is that the correct diagnosis? Distinguish descriptive redundancy, surplus structure—mathematical structure varying within the equivalence class—and local physical explanation, and indicate whether boundary degrees of freedom or charged matter change the answer. Please avoid a survey of named interpretations.
