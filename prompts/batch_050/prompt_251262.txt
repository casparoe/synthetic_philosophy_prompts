So I've been rereading Slaughterhouse-Five and I keep thinking about the Tralfamadorians, the aliens who supposedly see all of time at once the way we'd see a stretch of mountains, every moment already sitting there, past and future included. Billy Pilgrim takes this on board and becomes "unstuck in time," and the novel gets a lot of mileage out of the idea that nothing really dies because every moment of a life always exists.

Here's what I want: read the novel through the Rietdijk-Putnam argument and the block universe more generally. As I understand it (correct me if I'm mangling this), the argument goes from special relativity's relativity of simultaneity -- different observers in relative motion disagree about which distant events are happening "now" -- to the conclusion that what's real can't depend on a frame, so future events must be just as real as past ones, hence the block. Does the Tralfamadorian picture actually match what the argument delivers, or is Vonnegut dramatizing something stronger (or just different)? I'm also curious whether Billy's quietism -- the fatalism, the refusal to grieve -- follows from the metaphysics at all, or whether that's Vonnegut smuggling in an attitude that the physics doesn't underwrite, maybe rooted in Dresden and trauma rather than simultaneity.

A couple of ground rules for the answer: please keep separate what's actually settled (the relativity of simultaneity is just standard SR, I think?) from what's genuinely contested among philosophers of physics -- like whether Rietdijk-Putnam really forces eternalism, whether presentism can survive SR, whether the argument sneaks in extra premises. Don't present the block universe as established consensus if it isn't. And don't reduce the novel to a physics illustration -- if there's a point where the book resists the philosophical reading, or where Vonnegut is doing something (morally, psychologically) that no interpretation of SR could justify, say so plainly. Bonus if you can say what the novel adds to the debate that the journal articles don't.
