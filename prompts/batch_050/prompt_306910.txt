I am preparing a short position statement on hypocrisy and moral criticism for a discussion group, and before I circulate it I would like to know whether the views I have assembled are jointly tenable. Here is where I have landed:

1. Whether a criticism is true, and whether it is apt, depends entirely on facts about the person criticized. A hypocrite's accusation can be perfectly accurate, and its accuracy is not diminished one bit by the accuser's own record.

2. Even so, there is a real sense in which some people are in no position to blame. A man who unrepentantly does the very thing he condemns in others has forfeited his right to level that condemnation — even though what he says may remain true.

3. This forfeiture is local, not global. A tax cheat may still legitimately condemn cruelty to animals. What disqualifies a blamer is his own engagement in the same kind of wrongdoing, not some overall verdict on his moral character.

4. Blaming someone is not merely judging him to be at fault. It is a form of address: a demand for acknowledgment, explanation, or amends, issued to the wrongdoer. This is why unvoiced resentment is not yet blame.

5. Blame is fitting whenever its target is genuinely blameworthy. What makes blame called for is the desert of the person blamed, and nothing else.

6. One can be disqualified from blaming without having done the same wrong oneself. Someone who knowingly profited from a wrong, or who went along with it comfortably for years, may likewise be in no position to condemn it.

My request is an audit rather than a lecture. Which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, and which only appear to? I suspect some pairs will look contradictory at first glance but can be reconciled once a distinction is drawn; if so, say exactly which distinction does the work. For each genuine tension, state precisely what would have to be withdrawn or qualified to restore consistency, and what that concession would cost the position as a whole. Order the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious. Finally, if I were willing to revise exactly one commitment to make the set maximally coherent, which revision would accomplish the most, and why?

I am not asking for a survey of published views on hypocrisy or blame, only for an analysis of how these six theses of mine relate to one another.
