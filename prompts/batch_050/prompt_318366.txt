So I'm writing a novel set maybe thirty years from now, and the premise is this: there's a medical treatment where they swap out your neurons for synthetic ones, one at a time, over the course of about two years. You're awake the whole time, you go to work, you argue with your spouse, and each week a few more million of your neurons are artificial. As a side effect of the procedure, the clinic also ends up with a complete scan of your brain state, which they keep on file "just in case."

Here's my problem: I've got like five characters who all have different opinions on whether the person who walks out of the clinic is the same person who walked in, and right now they're all secretly the same opinion wearing different hats. I need them to be genuinely, coherently different from each other, down in the details.

So: write me a taxonomy of the possible positions on this, structured as a tree where every branch is labeled with the question that splits it. The questions should be concrete enough that one of my characters could answer them without knowing any philosophy jargon -- stuff like whether it would matter if the whole swap happened overnight instead of over two years, whether it matters that the stored scan exists even if it's never turned on, whether it matters if the synthetic neurons are slightly *better* than the originals, that kind of thing. Not "do you believe in functionalism?"

A few requirements:

- Six to eight leaves. Each leaf gets a name for the position (or "unnamed" if nobody actually defends it) plus one sentence on the most awkward thing that position is committed to -- I want to know what each character would squirm about if pressed.
- If some combination of answers is logically available but nobody occupies it, mark it and tell me in a sentence whether that's because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or weirdly unexplored.
- If two of my characters' positions can't actually be separated by your tree, flag that instead of pretending they're distinct.
- One of the branch questions has to deal with the scan: what happens to your verdict if, ten years later, they boot it up.

At the end, tell me which branch question in your tree is doing the heaviest lifting -- i.e. where you'd most expect the whole structure to collapse if that question turns out to be a false fork or badly posed, and what consideration might make you rebuild the tree differently. I'd rather know where the map is unreliable than get a clean-looking map that quietly papers over the hard part.
