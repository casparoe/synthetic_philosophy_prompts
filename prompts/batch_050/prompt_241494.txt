Before a reading group on anthropic reasoning, I've written up where I've landed and I'd like a consistency check before I present it. My commitments:

(1) The Carter–Leslie doomsday argument is sound in form: learning that I am roughly the hundred-billionth human should shift my credence toward there being far fewer humans overall, other things equal.

(2) In the Sleeping Beauty problem I am a thirder, for the standard reason that Beauty acquires no new evidence on waking that would distinguish this awakening from any other.

(3) I reject presumptuous-philosopher reasoning: the mere fact that a cosmological theory predicts vastly more observers in my epistemic situation is not by itself strong evidence in that theory's favor.

(4) For doomsday purposes, the appropriate reference class is roughly human observers — not all sentient beings, and not all observers whatsoever.

(5) My actual credence in human extinction this century, set mainly by the empirical picture on AI, biological, and nuclear risk, is low — on the order of a few percent.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a lecture. Which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest way out would be. Rank the tensions rather than treating them as equally serious, and finish by identifying the single revision that would leave the overall package most coherent. I have not decided which commitment I would sacrifice, so please don't build that assumption in.
