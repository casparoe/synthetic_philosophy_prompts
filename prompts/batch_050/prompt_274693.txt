I work at a small AI lab on the evals team, and leadership keeps asking us whether we're seeing signs of a fast takeoff -- like the recursive self-improvement, capabilities-jump-out-of-nowhere scenario -- versus the slow takeoff picture where capabilities climb gradually and you get lots of warning. The problem is that debate is all framed at the civilization level and nobody's told me what it means for my job, which is running evals on each new model every couple months and filing a report that someone non-technical reads.

Can you turn the fast-vs-slow takeoff debate into an actual monitoring procedure I could run per model release? Like: what to measure, in what order, what results would count as evidence for discontinuity vs continuity, what to do about leading indicators like sudden emergence of a capability vs steady scaling vs the model getting better at helping us train the next model. It should say what to do when a measurement is ambiguous (which will be most of the time), and be honest about which steps are real signal vs which are basically vibes dressed up as a checklist.

Then run the procedure on two hypotheticals: (1) new model is 15% better across the board on our suite, nothing weird; (2) new model is roughly the same on most stuff but suddenly way better at writing exploit code, a capability we weren't even testing for until last cycle. Bonus points if at least one application gives an answer I'd find annoying, like the procedure saying "this tells you nothing" when it feels like it should tell you something.
