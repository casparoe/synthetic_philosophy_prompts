I am preparing the final examination for an upper-level seminar on the ethics of war and international order. Please draft one essay question on humanitarian intervention and regime change in Kosovo (1999), Iraq (2003), and Libya (2011).

The question should be answerable in 90 minutes without access to notes and should resist a mere recitation of sovereignty, just-war criteria, or the Responsibility to Protect. It should instead require students to compare three different authorization problems: NATO acting without Security Council authorization in Kosovo, the contested interpretation of Resolution 1441 in Iraq, and Resolution 1973’s authorization of civilian protection in Libya followed by an expansion toward regime change. The question should also require students to evaluate whether regime change can sometimes be an unavoidable means of protecting civilians, even when it is not an independently legitimate war aim.

The exam question should ask students to construct and defend their own distinction among an intervention’s legality, legitimacy, and prudence, then apply that distinction consistently to all three cases. It should make them address at least one genuine tension in each case rather than treating any intervention as simply legal, illegal, humanitarian, or imperial.

Please provide:

1. The examination question, including a time limit and a 40-mark total.
2. A model answer of approximately 750–900 words.
3. A marking scheme identifying six essential moves, with the marks assigned to each.
4. For each essential move, one sentence describing the characteristic mistake that should prevent full credit.
5. A short grade boundary explaining what distinguishes an A answer from a B answer and a B answer from a C answer.

Do not request or supply citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; the assessment should test conceptual judgment and knowledge reasonably expected of students who have studied these cases.
