I play viola in a community orchestra, and I keep noticing a split in my own responses that I have never seen treated as a single phenomenon. I would like to know whether it has an established name and an established literature, or whether I am on my own.

The split runs in both directions. In one direction: I will hear an impeccably played set of variations in a style that leaves me completely cold, and I catch myself conceding, with full conviction, that it was beautiful — while feeling no pleasure whatsoever, sometimes even mild irritation at having to sit through it. In the other direction: there is a ragged old recording I return to monthly, structurally a mess, played by people who could barely manage their instruments, and I love it, but I would never call it good. When a friend with more cultivated taste than mine asks what she should listen to, I hand over the first kind of thing and keep the second to myself, and this division of labor feels principled rather than embarrassed. The same thing happens with novels. There is a celebrated novel I admire, would defend as excellent in an argument, and could not finish; and there are thrillers I devour in a day while noticing on every page that the prose is slack.

The asymmetry that interests me most is this: the first kind of response seems capable of being wrong, and the second does not. After a year of working at a genre that used to leave me cold, I sometimes conclude that my earlier dismissal was a failure on my part, and saying "I was wrong about that" comes naturally. I have never once thought I was wrong to enjoy the ragged recording. Enjoyment drifts; the other response gets corrected.

I know the neighborhood. It is not, I think, Kant's distinction between the beautiful and the agreeable, because as I understand it that distinction concerns what a judgment claims — whether it demands everyone's agreement — whereas both of my responses are simply mine, and I am pressing no claim with either of them. It is adjacent to Hume's standard of taste, but Hume's machinery is about whose verdicts fix the standard, and I am asking about a fissure within one person's reactions. It is also not what people call a guilty pleasure, which covers only one direction and treats it as a confession, and not merely acquired taste, which covers only the correction part and only in one direction.

So: does this two-way dissociation — the gap between what pleases me and what I am prepared to call beautiful, where only the latter feels corrigible — have a name? If several concepts come close, I want to know how the thing I have described differs from each of them and which fits best, not just a list. If nothing fits, I would rather have a coined term with a precise definition and boundary cases — does the same split occur with humor, with food, with admiring a person one does not like, and does the corrigibility asymmetry hold there too? — than a bare label with no carving behind it.
