I'm trying to work out what I actually think about population size and whether more people is better, by going on my reactions to cases. A couple in a prosperous country having a third child: good, or at least fully fine. A world with a trillion people whose lives are barely worth living, over a world of ten billion flourishing people: the smaller, better-off world seems clearly preferable, even though it has less total wellbeing. Humanity gradually dying out because nobody has children: a real loss, over and above anything bad happening to the last generation. A policy that raises the wellbeing of existing people versus one that adds an equal amount of wellbeing by bringing new, happy people into existence: I find myself preferring the first, which I'm aware sits badly with my reaction to the extinction case. A couple conceiving a child they know will have a difficult but still worthwhile life, when they could have waited and conceived a different child with an easier life: choosing the first seems wrong, even though no one is made worse off than they otherwise would have been. Is there a principle, or a small family of principles, that captures most of these verdicts, and what does it say about the case I can't settle -- a government subsidizing births in a below-replacement-fertility country, justified partly by appeal to future generations who would otherwise not exist? Please say how confident you are in your proposed principle and single out the specific step in your reasoning you are least sure of.
