Write an essay on whether the "friendship model" of filial duty gets things right when applied to parents who did their duty but were never really friends with their kids. Jane English famously argued that adult children don't owe their parents gratitude-debts for being raised -- favors are asked for, sacrifices are gifts, and ongoing obligations between parents and grown kids should flow from whatever friendship exists between them, same as between any two friends. Fine. But consider this case: a parent who scrupulously provided everything -- food, tuition, stability, driving you to soccer practice for ten years straight -- but who was emotionally distant the whole time. No cruelty, no neglect, just... no friendship ever formed, on either side, and now the parent is 78, lonely, and wants weekly phone calls and help navigating their medical stuff.

If English is right and filial duties just are friendship duties, it seems like the adult child in this case owes roughly what they'd owe a former coworker they never clicked with -- which is basically nothing beyond general decency. Lots of people find that verdict chilling; others find it liberating and honest. In your essay, weigh whether the friendship model really does entail that verdict (maybe there are ways friendship-adjacent bonds form even in cold households, or maybe "being the person who shaped you" generates something friendship-like even without warmth), and consider the main rival accounts -- gratitude for past sacrifices, special obligations grounded in the parent-child relationship itself, and duties based on the parent's current vulnerability and dependence on you specifically. Which account handles the cold-but-dutiful parent best, and which handles the genuinely awful parent best (the one you cut off for good reason -- does a vulnerability-based account wrongly force you back in)?

Reach a conclusion and defend it. Do adult children in that scenario owe their parent the phone calls and the medical help, or not? A verdict like "yes, but for reason X rather than friendship" or "no, and the chillingness is something we should just accept" are both fine -- just dont sit on the fence.
