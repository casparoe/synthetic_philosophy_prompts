I work on netcode for competitive fighting games, so my native vocabulary for anything involving time and revision is the one from rollback architectures: input delay, frame buffers, the ring of confirmed game states, rollback and resimulation when late input arrives, the difference between what was rendered to the screen and what the authoritative log now says happened.

Please restate Dennett's Orwellian-versus-Stalinesque distinction about the timing of experience in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. As I understand the background: in the color phi phenomenon, two differently colored dots are flashed in sequence at different positions, and subjects report seeing a single dot move and change color midway along the path -- which is puzzling, because the second dot's color had not yet appeared when the midpoint of the trajectory would have been experienced. Dennett uses this to set up two hypotheses. On the Stalinesque one, the brain stages a show trial before publication: experience is briefly delayed while a fabricated version of the intervening event is composed, so what reaches consciousness was never actually the raw sequence. On the Orwellian one, the subject experiences the sequence as it arrives and then the record is rewritten afterward, like a newspaper retroactively altering history, so the misreport concerns memory rather than experience itself. Dennett's own conclusion, if I have it right, is that once the brain processes things in multiple parallel drafts at different times, there may be no fact of the matter about which hypothesis is correct.

Here is why I think the translation might be illuminating. Input delay and rollback are exactly two engineering strategies for the same problem: withhold the frame until the inputs are in and show a fabricated-but-consistent sequence, versus render now and silently resimulate the past when corrected information lands. From inside the engine these feel deeply different -- one costs latency, the other costs visible popping and computational overhead. But from the perspective of an observer who only sees the final recorded match replay, they can be indistinguishable, and I have caught myself thinking there is no fact of the matter about what the game "really showed" at a given tick.

So: give me the translation, piece by piece -- the staging delay, the retrospective rewrite, the multiple drafts, the claim of indeterminacy. Then, the part I care most about: a residue report. What in the philosophical debate survives cleanly into engine terms, what survives only with distortion (for instance, the engineer's popping artifacts seem like they should correspond to something, but I'm not sure to what), and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. My suspicion is that the notion of there being something it is like to see the frame -- as opposed to a frame being rendered, buffered, or logged -- is precisely the component with no counterpart in the pipeline, and I want to know whether you agree. Finally, tell me whether the residue indicates that my vocabulary is missing a concept (there really is an observer of the frames, and the engine just doesn't model one) or whether it indicates that the philosophers are arguing about a distinction the underlying mechanism does not support -- the way two of my implementations can differ internally while there is no question an external test could ask that they answer differently.
