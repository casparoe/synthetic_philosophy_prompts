I'm on a small ethics-and-policy team at an AI lab, and I've been given a weirdly specific job: put together a one-year research agenda on how our (future, much more capable) systems should talk to users, on the assumption that these systems will eventually be far more persuasive than any human -- better at modeling the person they're talking to, better at finding the argument or framing that will land, tireless, and able to A/B test their rhetoric across millions of conversations. My boss wants a defended prioritization, not a survey: which open conceptual questions we should spend our limited people-hours on first, and why those.

Here's the tension I keep circling. Some people on the team think the core problem is defining the line between legitimate persuasion and manipulation -- something like: does the system's influence survive the user's fully informed reflection, or does it exploit some quirk the user would disavow if they noticed it? Others think that's backwards, because before we can draw that line we need to figure out what the user's "real" values even are when the AI is the one helping them reflect -- like, if the system shapes the very deliberation we'd use to check whether it manipulated us, the reflective-endorsement test is circular. And a third faction says both of these are academic navel-gazing compared to practical questions: should there be disclosure norms (the AI announcing its persuasive intent), hard constraints (never optimize for attitude change at all, only for accuracy), or asymmetry rules (persuasion allowed toward some ends but not others)?

So: which of these open questions (or others I'm missing) is actually upstream of the others, and which should a small team with a philosophy-heavy skillset and no ability to run experiments on a superintelligent system tackle first? For each thing you put on the agenda, tell me what makes it tractable with armchair methods, what other questions it unblocks, and what would move it up or down the list -- e.g., findings about how humans respond to current chatbots, or arguments that one of these framings collapses into another. Also flag anything people in this space seem to treat as urgent that you think is actually overrated or a distraction, since I suspect at least one of my three factions is barking up the wrong tree and I'd love ammunition.
