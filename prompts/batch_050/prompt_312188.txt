I need you to ghostwrite the joint statement from an adversarial collaboration between two camps in statutory interpretation: a careful textualist and a careful purposivist. Not internet-tough-guy versions — each accepts that statutes constrain courts, that purpose has to be disciplined by enacted language, and that judges shouldnt just rewrite policy they dislike.

Set it in a fictional jurisdiction so nobody can lean on memorized cases. The statute is the Harborview Micromobility and Shopfront Act. It was passed after delivery robots, e-scooters, cargo bikes, and food carts started clogging downtown sidewalks. Section 1 defines vehicle as a device used to transport persons or goods and propelled wholly or partly by mechanical power, but also says it includes any bicycle and excludes devices moved solely by human power. Section 2 bans motor vehicles from pedestrian zones except authorized emergency and maintenance vehicles. Section 3 says pedestrian zones include sidewalks, plazas, and any path primarily used by foot traffic, and an old un-repealed ordinance defines path to include privately owned arcade walkways open at lunch. Section 4 adds bigger fines for repeat offenders but never says whether a parent company and its separately incorporated franchisee count as one offender. Section 5 lets the transit agency issue interpretive bulletins; one bulletin says delivery robots under 25 kg are basically fine, but it was never noticed-and-commented. Theres also an apparent typo: the Senate draft banned motor assisted devices, the enacted text says motor vehicles, and the conference report praises clarity while changing three words.

The deliverable is the document both could sign after a long weekend: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims that are neither vacuous nor slanted, including what counts as text, context, purpose, absurdity, drafting error, and legislative silence; (2) a disagreements table where each row states the residual dispute in wording each side would accept for its own position; (3) cruxes, meaning sub-questions where an answer would move at least one camp, such as whether any bicycle settles bikes by list or wrecks the mechanical-power definition, whether motor vehicles in section 2 inherits section 1 or is a narrower ordinary phrase, when a conference report is evidence of meaning versus just mood music, and whether the agency bulletin can fill gaps without becoming an end-run; (4) for each crux, a concrete hypothetical both agree bears on it, with each sides prediction recorded in advance. Use a few homemade tests but define them from scratch: a swap test where you substitute a horse-drawn cart, a cargo trike with pedals plus a weak motor, and a shop vacuum on wheels; a typo-triage rule for when text is broken enough to repair; a silence audit for whether not mentioning robots is a ban, permission, or no signal; and an accountability check asking who gets blamed politically if the reading is wrong.
