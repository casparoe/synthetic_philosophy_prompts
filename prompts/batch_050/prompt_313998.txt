Write a deadpan FAQ (roughly 450-550 words) published by a fictional regulatory agency — call it something like the Office of Fitting Attitudes — that answers questions submitted by demons, tyrants, and other entities who threaten people into holding attitudes toward them. The running joke is the wrong kind of reasons problem: petitioners keep writing in to argue that, since admiring them is the only way to avoid catastrophic punishment, everyone plainly has decisive reason to admire them, which settles it, they're admirable. The office replies in bored civil-servant prose that the threat supplies a reason to *have* the attitude (a state-given reason, grounded in the benefits of holding it) rather than a reason grounded in the properties of the object (the demon himself), and therefore does nothing whatsoever for admirability, no matter how severe the penalty schedule is. The humor has to come from getting this distinction right — a philosopher who works on this stuff should nod along, not wince. Include at least one application the office can't resolve and punts to an appeals board that's been deadlocked for years (e.g. the demon who torments anyone who fails to fear him — isn't he, in some annoying sense, genuinely fearsome?), and at least one jab at buck-passing accounts of value that treat being good as just shorthand for there being sufficient reason to favor something. Stay completely in character, no winking at the reader.
