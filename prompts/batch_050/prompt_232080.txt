Three months ago my older sister donated a kidney to a stranger she had matched with through the national registry, and next month the mayor's office is presenting her with a civic medal at a public dinner. I have been asked to give the speech honoring her, and I have a problem I did not have until last night, when my oldest friend argued me into a corner over a bottle of wine. The draft citation describes her act as 'above and beyond the call of duty,' and he insisted that phrase is either false or an insult: if the donation did vastly more good for the recipient than it cost my sister, then it was simply the right thing to do, and calling it more than her duty is a fiction the rest of us maintain so we can applaud her without feeling accused ourselves. I refused to concede, but I could not give him a clean account of what makes the act so admirable if it was not, in some sense, optional. Please write the speech. The room will contain my sister (who despises sanctimony and has threatened to walk out if she is canonized), several transplant recipients, registry volunteers, city officials, and my friend, who will be in the third row waiting to see whether I answered him. The speech must therefore hold three things at once: it must give her the full measure of praise without implying that everyone present who kept both kidneys is a moral failure; it must avoid the opposite deflation in which the donation was merely an admirable pastime, like running marathons; and it must leave honestly open what, if anything, the rest of us ought to do with her example, without turning the evening into a recruitment drive. Do not use the words duty, obligation, or hero: my friend will be listening for the first two, and my sister will flinch at the third. Deliver the speech as exactly five numbered parts of two to four sentences each, with no preamble or framing remarks and the toast itself contained within the fifth part; somewhere across the five parts, answer my friend's argument without naming him, in a way he will recognize as an answer.
