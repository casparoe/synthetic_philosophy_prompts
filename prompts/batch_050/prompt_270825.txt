I'm putting together a seminar unit on the ethics of dramatizing real people — biopics, historical dramas, roman-à-clef novels, the whole genre — and I've assembled what I take to be the complete list of standard considerations in the debate over what we owe the real dead when we put invented words in their mouths. Here is my list. First, the no-subject argument: the dead have no experiences, so nothing can be bad for them, and any duties in the vicinity are owed only to the living. Second, the posthumous-interests reply: some interests — in one's reputation, in how one's life's work is received, in not being remembered as a villain — can be set back after death even though the dead person never finds out, so harm without experience is possible. Third, collateral harm to the living: surviving children, spouses, and communities can be genuinely wounded by a distorted portrayal. Fourth, the public-memory argument: audiences treat dramatizations as history, so a slick fabrication doesn't just wrong the individual, it corrupts the collective record on which later judgments and identities depend. Fifth, the artistic-license defense: fiction has its own legitimate aims, and a strict duty of accuracy would destroy the genre, which often illuminates moral and political truths better than documentary can. Sixth, the consent asymmetry: public figures arguably accepted a degree of mythologizing as the price of prominence, while private individuals swept up in someone else's story accepted nothing.

I think this list covers the familiar moves, so I'm not looking for any of them repeated. What I want is one further consideration — a reason for or against a moral duty to the dramatized dead — that is genuinely different in structure from all six: not a rewording, not a special case, and not a combination of two items on the list. I'm open to considerations that aren't framed in terms of harm or rights at all, and to ones that cut in either direction (a new constraint on artists, or a new reason to think the whole anxiety is misplaced).
