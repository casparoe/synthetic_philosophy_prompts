Okay this might be a strange question but I keep running into a pattern and I want to know if philosophers have a name for it.

I help run a tutoring program, and I've noticed something wierd about predictions *about people*, specifically. Three cases from the last year:

1. One of our tutors told a student, kindly, "kids with your background usually burn out around midterm, so watch out for that." The student had been doing fine. She burned out around midterm. The tutor felt vindicated, but I couldn't shake the feeling that the prediction was somehow involved in its own coming true.

2. We use software that flags students as "likely to drop the course." One student saw his flag on his dashboard (a whole other mistake) and got mad and finished the course basically out of spite. So the prediction was accurate in the sense that the model was good, but saying it out loud broke it.

3. The one that really bugs me: a coworker claims she's great at predicting people. She told me "you're going to check your phone in the next ten minutes, you always do." And I realized that the moment she says it, she can't win -- if I hear the prediction, I can just not check, whatever my habits are. But if she'd kept the prediction to herself, she'd probably have been right. So her accuracy depends on her staying silent.

It's case 3 that I most want a name for: predictions about someone that are reliable only as long as the person doesn't hear them, where announcing them hands the person the power to make them false. Cases 1 and 2 seem like related but different animals. Is there an established concept for any of this? I have a vague sense there's some old story or parable with this structure, like a man trying to dodge a prediction and failing, but that's sort of the opposite, since in my case hearing it lets you succeed. If there are names, please explain how the three cases differ and which concept actually fits each -- I don't want just a label, I want to know where the edges of the concept are. And if case 3 specifically has no name, tell me that and maybe propose one with a definition and some borderline cases.
