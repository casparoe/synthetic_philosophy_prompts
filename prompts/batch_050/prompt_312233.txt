In my philosophy of physics reading group we recently discussed effective field theories and the so-called autonomy of scales, and one participant defended a picture I've also encountered in popular and semi-technical writing: physical theories at different scales are like maps at different resolutions. A world map, a national road atlas, and a hiking trail map each capture the same territory, none is a mere "approximation" of the others, each is fully adequate within its intended range of use, and asking which one is "the true map" is a mistake. She used this analogy to draw a strong anti-reductionist conclusion: since condensed matter physicists work with phonons and hydrodynamics without ever needing quarks, and since effective field theories come with their own degrees of freedom, symmetries, and perturbative expansions valid below a cutoff, the higher-level theories are autonomous in a deep sense — not stopgaps awaiting reduction but self-standing descriptions on a par with the more microscopic ones. A second participant pushed back that the analogy is doing illicit work here, but couldn't say exactly where.

I'd like you to act as a philosopher of physics with a firm grasp of the technical machinery (cutoffs, integrating out high-energy modes, the renormalization group, power counting, naturalness) and to audit this analogy carefully. Please proceed in stages: first, brainstorm freely about what the two relata share; then give an explicit correspondence table — what plays the role of map resolution, of the territory, of the mapmaker's purposes, of projection from a detailed map to a coarse one, of representational distortion? Next, identify where the mapping breaks. I'm especially interested in disanalogies that matter for the intended anti-reductionist use, separated from ones that are merely cosmetic. For instance: maps are deliberate omissions made by agents for pragmatic ends, whereas the relation between an effective theory and its ultraviolet completion is governed by principled structures like decoupling and controlled expansions; a higher-resolution map is straightforwardly "more accurate," whereas an effective theory at its own scale can be more predictive than the fundamental theory applied there; and the parameters of the low-energy theory are in principle fixed by the underlying theory, whereas no road atlas's symbols are derivable from a world map.

Finally, give me your verdict on how much of the conclusion survives the audit. Does the analogy support autonomy of predictive practice, metaphysical autonomy, both, or neither — and if the map analogy was carrying more weight than it can bear, what would be a better analogy for the autonomy of scales, or should the claim be argued for directly rather than analogically? Please reason carefully rather than listing considerations, and feel free to rate the strength of each disanalogy you identify.
