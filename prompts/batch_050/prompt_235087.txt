I'm writing up my position for a seminar on Quinn's self-torturer puzzle (the device with many settings, each imperceptibly more painful than the last, paying a fixed sum per notch upward) and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I've landed:

(a) Each single step upward is rational for the self-torturer: no perceptible difference in pain, a real gain in money, so refusing a step is leaving value on the table.

(d) There is no sharp cutoff between the tolerable and the intolerable settings; "unbearable pain" is a vague predicate, and any principled stopping rule must respect that.

(e) Pain and money are genuinely commensurable in small increments — that's what makes each trade a real trade rather than a category mistake.

Do these hang together?
