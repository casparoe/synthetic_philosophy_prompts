I'm in a phil of physics seminar this term and for the final project we got assigned an "adversarial collaboration" instead of a normal debate. My partner is a committed substantivalist about spacetime, I'm the relationist, and after a semester of arguing about Newton's bucket we mostly just talk past eachother. So draft the joint statement we should actually be able to produce by the end of term — the document both of us could put our names to.

Format:

1. Agreed claims — a numbered list of 6-10 claims both sides can sign. Make them non-trivial: stuff like what the bucket argument actually does and doesn't show, what the two-globes case establishes, where general relativity leaves the dispute, what frame-dragging measurements settle vs don't settle. Nothing vacuous like "spacetime is real in some sense."

2. Disagreements — for each remaining disagreement, phrase it twice: once in wording the substantivalist would accept as a fair statement of her own view, once in wording the relationist would accept for his. Plus a neutral one-line framing of what the dispute is actually about.

3. Cruxes — identify 2 or 3 sub-questions where settling them would genuinely move at least one side, and for each one record in advance what each side predicts/expects. My guess is one crux is whether the hole argument forces everyone into some "sophisticated" position where the substantivalist/relationist labels stop doing work, and another is whether GR's frame dragging vindicates Machian intuitions or is just beside the point. But check these — if you think a proposed crux wouldn't actually move anyone, say so rather than forcing it.

4. Verbal dispute audit — flag any part of the classic debate you think is really about words rather than facts, and justify the call.

Don't adjudicate a winner at the end. The whole point is the residual disagreement, made as exact as possible, in language neither of us would want to red-pen.
