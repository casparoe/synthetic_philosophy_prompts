I'm preparing a seminar unit on whether karma can be defended as a genuine theory of moral desert rather than merely a moralized cosmology, and I want a thought experiment built to order. Construct a single realistic scenario — one paragraph, ordinary life, no gods dispensing rewards and no narrator announcing anyone's karmic balance — in which two characters perform outwardly identical actions whose results diverge through sheer luck (say, one fatally strikes a pedestrian who steps into the road while the other's drive ends uneventfully), with the characters matched in intention, negligence, effort, and foresight. The scenario must be engineered so that an intention-centered karmic account, on which karmic weight tracks the agent's intention rather than the outcome, and an outcome-sensitive desert account, on which what one deserves tracks what one actually brought about, deliver opposite verdicts about whether the two characters' deserts differ. Crucially, the verdicts must fall out of the theories, not be stipulated in the telling.

After the scenario, do the following: (1) state each account's verdict and its reasoning; (2) identify the single structural feature that makes the two accounts peel apart, and say whether removing that feature collapses the case into agreement; (3) given the control condition — that people deserve things only for what was up to them — argue which of the two accounts can still plausibly call itself a theory of desert, since one appears to desert-punish luck while the other risks detaching desert from anything the agent actually did; and (4) give the strongest available reply from the side your analysis treats worse. Close with an honest note on where the scenario leans on a contested reading of karma, for instance where treating karmic weight as intention-only flattens traditions in which results, ripening conditions, or the agent's broader mental cultivation also figure in.

If any part of this specification is unsatisfiable as posed — for example, if you think no recognizable version of karma can be made outcome-sensitive, or that the control condition settles the case in advance and drains it of interest — say so plainly and explain where the request goes wrong rather than forcing the construction.
