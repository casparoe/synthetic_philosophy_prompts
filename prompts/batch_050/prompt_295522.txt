I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on indicative conditionals and I want a decision tree that sorts responses to Lewis's triviality result -- the 1976 proof that if indicative conditionals have truth conditions whose probability always equals the conditional probability of consequent on antecedent (the Adams-Stalnaker Equation), and you grant a few innocuous assumptions about how those probabilities behave under supposition, your whole probability function collapses into something trivial.

Start the tree at the root commitment: "I take the Ramsey test seriously -- to evaluate 'if A, then B' I hypothetically add A to my beliefs and see how confident I end up in B." Every branch node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing the literature, and each one should be a genuine fork where positions come apart. Forks I especially want to see somewhere in the tree:

- whether 'if A, B' even has a truth value, or whether 'if' just marks a conditional speech act (or restricts some nearby modal) rather than expressing a two-place connective
- whether you're willing to give up on conditionals embedding -- negated conditionals, conditionals inside conditionals, conditionals under quantifiers -- since several popular escape routes just can't say what those mean
- whether the conditional's probability is allowed to shift with context rather than being a fixed function of the agent's credences
- whether the Equation itself is the thing to drop, and if so whether you explain away its intuitive pull pragmatically or just bite the bullet

Leaves should include: Adams-style assertability/expressivism, the restrictor view (if-clauses restrict modals, no conditional proposition exists), contextualism about which proposition a conditional expresses, Stalnaker-style selection semantics with its tradeoffs, Grice/Jackson-style material implication plus pragmatics, and at least one route that keeps truth values but weakens the probability assumptions (imprecise credences or similar). Mark any combination of answers the proof itself makes incoherent as unoccupied for that reason, and flag any pair of leaves your tree cant actually separate. End each leaf with one sentence on the most embarrassing thing that view has to swallow -- e.g. what happens to modus ponens, or to 'its not the case that if A then B'.
