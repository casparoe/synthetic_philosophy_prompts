So my friend who reads a lot of decision theory blogs tried to explain counterfactual mugging to me at a party and I'm like 60% sure he garbled it. His version went something like this:

'Omega flips a fair coin. If it comes up heads, Omega appears and asks you for $100, explaining that had the coin come up tails, it would have given you $10,000 -- but only in the case where you would have paid up in the heads world. If it comes up tails, Omega just hands you the $10,000 on the spot, since it already knows you would've paid. So obviously you should pay the $100 when asked, because before the flip you'd have wanted to commit to paying, and that's basically what updateless decision theory formalizes. It also shows causal decision theory falls apart here, because even a CDT agent should pay -- paying is what causes Omega to give you the ten grand. And it's the same trick as XOR blackmail, where Omega sends you a letter demanding money exactly when an asteroid really is about to hit Earth, because it predicted you'd pay to avert it.'

A few things nag at me. First, I thought the whole point of the counterfactual mugging is that you never actually get the $10,000 in any branch -- it's purely hypothetical, which is why paying feels so weird. His telling makes it sound like a straightforward trade where you get rewarded in the tails world, and then of course paying looks reasonable. Second, the claim that CDT pays because paying 'causes' the reward -- the coin has already landed when you're asked, so what could paying possibly cause at that point? I suspect he's sneaking in the very thing CDT denies. Third, I'm fairly sure the XOR blackmail letter arrives when things are fine, not when the asteroid is coming, but I can't reconstruct why.
