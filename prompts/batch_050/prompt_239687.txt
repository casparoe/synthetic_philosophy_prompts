I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background who has gotten absorbed in Douglas Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment, and I want help turning it into a live workshop activity. As I understand the setup: imagine a closed system whose substrate is an enormous number of tiny marbles (simmballs) knocking into one another according to simple mechanical rules. Out of these collisions emerge large, slow, stable patterns (simms) whose interactions can be described in an entirely different vocabulary — at that higher level, events look like a game with its own rules, and describing the system in pattern-talk is the only way to make it intelligible or predictable. Hofstadter's question, as I take it, is who shoves whom around inside such a system. Every event is fully accounted for at the marble level, so the patterns seem to cause nothing; yet the marble-level story is hopelessly vast, and everything we care about — explanation, prediction, and presumably meaning — lives at the pattern level.

I run a monthly hands-on evening at a software meetup: about 16 participants, all engineers, none assumed to know any philosophy, in a plain meeting room with movable chairs. I have 90 minutes. Design a runnable activity — roles, rules, and some small real stakes (points, a prize, bragging rights, whatever works) — that makes participants *feel* both pulls of this question in play, before anyone names it. Concretely, I want a structure in which participants first experience the pattern level as genuinely explanatory (the only usable handle on what the system is doing), then experience the substrate answer as inescapable (every local move was mechanically forced, so pattern-talk starts to look like a mere summary), and finally land in a situation where a pattern-level decision or intervention seems to do real causal work on the substrate, so that neither flat answer sits comfortably.

Constraints: ordinary physical materials only (index cards, floor tape, tokens, blindfolds or screens are fine — no computers, deliberately); the facilitator must not use the words careenium, emergence, symbol, or causation until the debrief; the tension must arise from what the rules and incentives make happen, not from anything the facilitator explains. Include: the rules and roles; a minute-by-minute timeline that fits 90 minutes; a debrief plan that maps specific moments of play onto the two levels and the who-shoves-whom question, and connects it to something engineers recognize (nobody explains an outage at the level of electrons, yet nobody thinks the load balancer literally pushes electrons around); the ways this will predictably go wrong when actually run (participants cheating across levels, rounds dragging, one sharp participant spoiling the reveal early) and how to handle each; and an honest note on which feature of the thought experiment the activity cannot reproduce — I suspect it's that the patterns in any enactment won't actually *mean* anything the way symbols in a mind supposedly do — and what the facilitator must supply verbally to compensate.

After the prose design, give me a structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "title" (string); "one_line_premise" (string); "materials" (array of strings); "roles" (array of objects with "name", "count", "can_see_or_do", "cannot_see_or_do"); "timeline" (array of objects with "phase", "minutes", "rules_in_effect", "tension_this_phase_should_produce"); "stakes" (string); "debrief" (array of objects with "moment_in_play", "maps_to_in_careenium", "question_to_ask"); "predicted_failure_modes" (array of objects with "failure" and "mitigation"); "irreproducible_feature" (object with "what" and "facilitator_workaround").
