I'm a physics PhD student who's been sitting in on our department's phil-physics reading group, and this semester we've been circling Maxwell's demon in its modern form and honestly it's getting exasperating. The two camps, roughly: the Bennett/Landauer side, which says the demon is exorcised because measurement can in principle be done reversibly but erasing the record in the demon's memory costs at least kT ln 2 of heat per bit, exactly canceling what the Szilard engine extracts; and the Earman–Norton side, which says this rescue is circular, because the standard derivations of the Landauer bound themselves assume the second law, so you can't then wheel Landauer in to *save* the second law from the demon. What's striking to me is that nobody disputes any of the actual results. Everyone accepts the Szilard arithmetic, everyone accepts the fluctuation theorems, everyone accepts that the colloidal-particle-in-an-optical-trap experiments measured erasure heat right around the Landauer bound. And yet the two sides have barely moved in decades, and each thinks the other is making an elementary mistake.

My working hypothesis is that the disagreement isn't about statistical mechanics at all but about what a "principle" in thermodynamics is allowed to be and what kind of justification it can have — i.e., the Bennett side is fine treating the second law as an empirical regularity that information-theoretic bookkeeping helps explain, while the Norton side wants proofs that don't presuppose what they're supposed to ground, and neither side will state this openly. Is that right, or is there a real substantive fault line I'm missing — say, a hidden disagreement about what a memory *is* physically, or about whether the demon was ever a threat to the second law as opposed to a puzzle about its scope, or whether both sides are actually answering different questions under one heading? Give me a diagnosis with reasons, grounded in what each side actually argues rather than a survey, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a cleaner theorem, a new experiment, a conceptual distinction, or an argument that this is a case where no resolution should be expected and the impasse is itself the answer.
