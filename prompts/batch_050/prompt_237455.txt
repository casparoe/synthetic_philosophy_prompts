I teach a first-year intro course in Chinese philosophy (about 24 students, one 75-minute session) and next week we're on the Mohists. I want a classroom activity that makes students feel the pull of both Mozi's impartial care and the Confucian idea of graded love before either gets named. The session should turn on this passage from Mozi, "Universal Love I" (trans. W. P. Mei):

"As he loves only his own family and not other families, the thief steals from other families to profit his own family. As he loves only his own person and not others, the robber does violence to others to profit himself. And the reason for all this is want of love."

Mozi's move here is striking: concern for your own isn't innocent affection, it's the same logic as theft, just scaled down. The Confucian pushback (which I want them to articulate themselves) is that graded love is natural, morally good, and the root from which wider benevolence grows, not the root of disorder.

So: design me an in-class game or structured deliberation where students first get to act on graded concern and discover where it leads (without being told beforehand what they're enacting), and then have to run the group again under Mozi's prescription and feel where it chafes. Give me the rules, any materials, a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the passage above and onto the Confucian reply. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of the disagreement the game can't actually capture and I'll have to supply in lecture.
