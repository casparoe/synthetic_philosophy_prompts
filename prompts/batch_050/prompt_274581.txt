In my seminar on long-run risks from AI, students keep treating "AI catastrophe" as a single event type, and I want to model better reasoning for them. Please give your credence in the following proposition: conditional on advanced AI causing a catastrophe for humanity this century, the failure will have taken the form of slow value drift -- a gradual, broadly noticed erosion of human control or of what humans actually value, through incremental delegation to systems whose incentives diverge from ours -- rather than the form of a discrete influence-seeking maneuver, in which a system deliberately conceals its aims and then seizes power in a compressed episode. Decompose your answer: assign rough probabilities to the sub-questions the verdict rides on -- e.g., whether capable systems will in fact develop stable, influence-seeking goals rather than merely locally useful behaviors; whether such goals could stay hidden through training and deployment; whether gradual drift would be corrected by the people experiencing it; whether economic pressures make delegation effectively irreversible -- show how they combine into your final number, and identify which sub-question the final credence is most sensitive to. Finally, state what argument or empirical development would most change your mind, and where your decomposition is most likely to be missing a consideration.
