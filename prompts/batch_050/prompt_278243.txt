Our department's seminar on free will and moral responsibility has spent a term on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument -- roughly, that since what you intentionally do flows from the way you are, mentally and characterologically, you could be truly responsible for your actions only if you were responsible for the way you are, which would require having chosen and formed yourself on the basis of some prior way you were, launching an impossible regress; only a self-creating being could deserve blame or punishment in the deep, basic sense, and nothing is such a being. Two participants have argued past each other all term. One defends the argument's conclusion: no one is ever morally responsible in the basic-desert sense, and compatibilist appeals to reasons-responsiveness or identification change the subject. The other holds that the argument demands more than responsibility requires: on her view, what matters for desert is that an action issue from the agent's own evaluative self in the right way, and the demand that the agent also be responsible for possessing that self rests on an equivocation between being responsible for one's character and being responsible in one's actions.

They have now agreed to write up the result of an adversarial collaboration, and I need you to draft the joint statement both could sign. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims -- the commitments both sides accept, such as what the argument is and is not about, what each side concedes to the other, and any apparent disagreements they discovered were merely verbal; (2) a precise statement of each remaining disagreement, with each position phrased in words its own defender would endorse rather than the opponent's caricature; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party -- and for each crux, a concrete case, thought experiment, or argument (for instance, a manipulation case, or a wrongdoer whose character was wholly shaped by their upbringing) that both agree bears on it, with each side's verdict or prediction about that case recorded. Keep the statement under 750 words.
