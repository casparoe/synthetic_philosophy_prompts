So I've been chewing on the Scheffler "afterlife conjecture" thing (the idea that if we all knew humanity would end shortly after our own deaths, most of what we care about would lose its point). It came up in an xrisk-adjacent thread and I realised I don't actually know what my own theory is. Here are my gut verdicts, roughly in order of confidence:

- Asteroid wipes out humanity 200 years after I die, unrelated to anything I did: this is bad in a way that goes beyond the badness of the deaths themselves, and knowing about it now would cast a shadow over things I currently do.
- My research project gets finished by strangers after I die: genuinely glad about this.
- Same, but someone else gets all the credit and I'm forgotten: still glad, but noticeably less glad, and the "less" doesn't feel like it's about my experiences, since I'd be dead.
- Humanity survives but gradually abandons everything I care about (truth-seeking, curiosity, whatever) and just wireheads forever: bad, and I can't seem to reduce the badness to anyone's suffering, because they're all blissed out.
- Conscious AI descendants replace us but carry on science and keep asking real questions: I'm mostly fine with this, which might be inconsistent with the previous one? Or maybe the principle is different from what I think it is.
- Here's the one that trips me up: an alien civilisation on the far side of the universe, psychologically like us, doing all the things I'd praise in humanity, gets wiped out. Intellectually I want to say this is just as bad as the asteroid case, but my gut cares a lot less. I can't tell if my gut is being parochial or whether it's tracking something real about "ours" vs "theirs".

Is there a single principle that captures most of these, or a small set of candidates? Please state it precisely enough that I can see which clause does which work, and tell me which of my verdicts each candidate forces me to abandon. In particular I'm torn on whether the credit one (case 3) and the alien one are the same kind of failure or different kinds.

And what does your best principle say about the case I keep going back and forth on: the last generation knows they're the last, nobody suffers, everyone just... carries on with their projects anyway, apparently undisturbed? Does that refute the conjecture, or does it just show those people have a different principle from mine?
