Hey, so I'm a third-year and this semester I'm one of the undergrad TAs for our normative ethics course. The prof asked me to draft one essay question for the final on the inefficacy objection -- you know, the "my one purchase / one vote / one flight makes no difference" thing about collective harms -- and I want to bring something decent to our meeting on Thursday so I don't look like I phoned it in.

The problem with every question I've seen on this is that students can answer it by reciting the two sides: "every little bit helps" vs "the market is too big for one person to matter," maybe drop the harmless torturers case, done. I want a question where that gets you a C at best.

So: please write the question, built around a concrete made-up case, plus a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme. Some things I want the question to force students to deal with:

1. The case should have a threshold structure, e.g. some company that restocks or reorders in fixed batches so that most individual purchases genuinely change nothing but occasionally one purchase tips the threshold and causes a whole batch's worth of harm. Students should have to say what changes morally when you know the threshold vs when you only know it exists somewhere but not where.
2. It should make them distinguish "my action makes no actual difference" from "my action makes no expected difference" -- and notice these come apart in threshold cases.
3. It should push them to say something about what happens when everyone reasons this way, without letting them just gesture at "what if everyone did that" (the prof hates that move, she says it smuggles in Kant through the back door).
4. There should be a twist in the case where the "ineffective" option isn't abstaining but switching to a slightly-less-bad alternative, so students have to think about whether the objection is really about causation or about complicity/participation, and whether non-consequentialist responses actually answer the math or just change the subject.

For the marking scheme: out of 20 marks, built around 5 must-hit points with what each is worth, and for each point a one-line note on how students typically botch it (so the other graders and I are on the same page). Also tell me roughly what seperates an A answer from a B answer -- like what specific move does the A answer make that the B answer misses.

The exam is 3 hours total and they'd have about 50 minutes for this essay, if that matters for calibrating difficulty. It's a 200-level course, so most of them have only done intro ethics before this. Thanks!!
