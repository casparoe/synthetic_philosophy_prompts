I am drafting the decision-theory chapter of a textbook and, before committing my positions to print, I want a consistency audit of the views I find myself holding about rational choice under risk. They are these:

(a) Transitivity of preference is a genuine requirement of rationality, and the money-pump argument is what justifies it: an agent with cyclic preferences is exploitable in a way that suffices to condemn the preferences themselves.

(b) The Allais pattern is rationally permissible. An agent may prefer a sure $1 million to a gamble offering $5 million with probability 0.10, $1 million with probability 0.89, and nothing with probability 0.01, while also preferring a 0.10 chance of $5 million to a 0.11 chance of $1 million. The difference between certainty and near-certainty can legitimately matter to her, and this need not reduce to the shape of her utility function over money.

(c) Rational preference is consequentialist in the following sense: preferences between lotteries may depend only on the final probability distributions over outcomes they offer — not on which alternatives were available but forgone, not on how the choice situation was described or framed, and not on what would have happened under contingencies that did not obtain.

(d) A rational agent is dynamically coherent: she does not adopt plans she can foresee herself abandoning, and a predictable pattern of reversal across sequential choices counts against the preferences generating it.

(e) Ordinary risk aversion — declining actuarially fair bets, purchasing insurance at a modest premium — is rationally permissible and adequately explained by the diminishing marginal utility of money.

Please tell me: which of these commitments stand in genuine tension with one another, and which pairs only appear to? Rank any genuine tensions by severity; for each, state what would have to be given up to resolve it and what that concession would cost, and note whether the tension is strictly pairwise or only emerges among three or more commitments taken together. Where an apparent tension dissolves on inspection, show why rather than merely asserting that it does. Finally, if a single revision would restore consistency at the lowest overall cost, identify it — allowing for the possibility that the repair is a refinement of one commitment rather than its wholesale abandonment.
