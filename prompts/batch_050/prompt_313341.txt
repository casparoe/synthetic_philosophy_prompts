So I'm a retired cell biologist, and over the past year or so I've fallen down the philosophy of mind rabbit hole, mostly via a reading group some folks from my old department put together. We just finished the chapter on the color phi phenomenon in a popular book, and afterwards my friend Dave (retired engineer, very confident) sent around an email summarizing Dennett's argument, which the book draws on heavily. His summary went like this: 'In the color phi experiment, two dots of different colors flash briefly in different spots on a screen, and what people report is a single dot moving across and changing color partway. Dennett says there are two candidate explanations. On the Stalinesque one, the brain stages a kind of show trial before anything reaches consciousness: the second dot has already registered, so the brain fabricates the moving, color-changing dot in advance and only that fabrication is ever experienced. On the Orwellian one, you genuinely did experience the first dot as it was, but your memory gets rewritten immediately after, so you sincerely misremember having seen the motion and the change. Dennett's point is that no experiment, even with far better brain scanners than we have, could ever settle which of these is what really happened, so he offers his multiple drafts model as a workaround: since the question is unanswerable, we should treat consciousness as a kind of fiction the brain tells and give up asking when an experience actually occurred. Which just goes to show that our inner life is mostly confabulation anyway.'

Now, a lot of this sounds plausible to me, and honestly it sounds like the sort of hard-nosed materialism I'm sympathetic to after forty years in a lab. But two things make me itchy. First, as a scientist I get suspicious whenever someone declares a question unanswerable in principle rather than merely unanswered -- in my experience that move has a poor track record. Second, the conclusion that our inner life is 'mostly confabulation' feels like a much bigger claim than one odd little illusion about dots could possibly support. But Dave is no fool and he swears he's being faithful to Dennett, and I havent read the primary source myself.

So: how fair is his summary? Please go through it piece by piece and tell me what's accurate, what's a defensible simplification, and what misrepresents Dennett's actual position -- in particular whether Dennett really thinks the Orwellian-vs-Stalinesque question is empirically open but technologically out of reach, and whether 'consciousness is a fiction' follows from anything he says or is Dave's own garnish. Also tell me whether the big confabulation conclusion survives once the summary is corrected.
