(1) Jaynes's invariance strategy: require that the answer be unchanged under the symmetries of the problem -- translation, rotation, and scale for the chord case -- and show that only one distribution survives, so the paradox arises only because the problem was posed without those constraints. (2) The partition-relativity diagnosis: indifference is only ever applicable relative to a way of carving up the possibilities, the three textbook answers correspond to three carvings, and the lesson is that there is no carving-free indifference, so the principle must be restricted or rejected outright. (4) The under-specification response: probabilities attach to a physical randomizing procedure, not to a geometric description, so the problem as stated simply has no answer until the mechanism for drawing the chord is fixed.

Is there a fifth strategy, genuinely different from these four -- not a combination of two of them, and not one of them dressed in new notation?
