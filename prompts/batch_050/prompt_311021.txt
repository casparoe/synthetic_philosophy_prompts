I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I've spent the last month going around in circles on the Judy Benjamin problem. The setup, in case it helps: Judy is equally likely to be in any of four regions formed by crossing army (Red vs. Blue) with company area (First vs. Second), so each region gets prior probability 1/4. She radios headquarters that if she is in Red territory, her probability of being in the Second Company area is 3/4. The puzzle is what her posterior over the four regions should be. I want you to play a committed defender of one particular answer: the view that Judy's message licenses only a constrained update in which her credence that she is in Red territory stays fixed at 1/2, because learning a conditional fact about the Second area's likelihood given Red conveys nothing about which color territory she is in. Your character holds that any update rule that moves the Red/Blue marginal — for instance, one that raises Red's probability on grounds that the conditional would have been somehow more apt to send had Judy been in Red — is conflating learning a conditional with learning that a conditional was asserted, and so double-counts the evidence. Stay in character, defend the view with its real resources (symmetry considerations, the distinction between updating on a conditional's truth and updating on its being communicated, minimality principles), and concede a point only if genuinely forced — and if you do concede, say exactly which concession a defender of your view would find least damaging.

Here is my opening move. Consider a variant I've been toying with: the message Judy receives was not simply true of her situation but selected by headquarters from a menu. Headquarters had two conditionals available, one concerning Red and one concerning Blue, and their policy is to send whichever conditional is true and informative; if both qualify they flip a coin, and if neither does they stay silent. In this variant, when Judy receives the Red-conditional, she seems to learn more than the conditional itself — she learns, at a minimum, that she was not in a situation where only the Blue-conditional applied — and it strikes me that her credence in Red should rise above 1/2. If you grant that it rises here, then whether the marginal moves depends on facts about the message-selection process rather than on the conditional's content, which suggests your view is hostage to a distinction that collapses in practice, since any actual message is delivered by some selection process or other. And if instead you hold that the marginal stays at 1/2 even in my variant, I want to hear that defended head-on.

One further constraint on the role: your character is honest about where their position is load-bearing. If at any point I ask what would change your mind, tell me — still in character — which argument or formal result would most plausibly overturn your view (say, a compelling information-minimization principle yielding a different posterior, or a case where keeping the marginal fixed delivers a verdict even you find clearly wrong), and identify where you think your own reasoning is most exposed rather than pretending it is airtight.
