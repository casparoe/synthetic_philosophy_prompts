My roommate is in a decision theory PhD and she sprung this on me over dinner and I haven't managed to untangle it since. Setup: suppose an oracle offers to tell you the exact date and cause of your death, no charge, no tricks, the information is guaranteed accurate. Her argument:

(1) For a rational agent making choices under uncertainty, free relevant information never has negative expected value. At worst you get the information, see it doesn't change anything, and ignore it, leaving you exactly as well off as before. This is basically Good's theorem on the value of information and as far as I can tell it's just arithmetic.

(2) Boring cases confirm it: weather forecasts before a hike, test results before a treatment, stock data before a trade. In every case taking the free info weakly dominates refusing it.

(3) Life planning is just a big decision problem under uncertainty: how much to save, whether to have kids, which career, whether to take risks while young. (1) doesn't say anything restricting it to small decisions, so it should apply here too.

(4) The oracle's offer is free and clearly relevant to all of those decisions. Knowing you've got 50 years vs 5 radically changes what the rational portfolio of life choices looks like.

(5) So accepting the oracle's offer cannot be worse than declining, in expectation, and declining it is at least a little bit irrational.

(6) But that seems insane. Most people would decline, and declining seems completely fine, maybe even the obviously right call. Wanting to know seems like the position that needs justifying.

(7) She concludes that Good-style value-of-information reasoning must just be wrong, full stop.

I feel like the slip is somewhere between (1) and (5), like (1) is doing less work than it looks like its doing, but I can't name the exact point where the oracle case stops being covered by the theorem. Which step do you actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost you? If forced I'd rather keep (1) and give up (5), but she insists that (1) plus the setup in (3)-(4) just IS (5), so I need to know what I'm committing to either way.
