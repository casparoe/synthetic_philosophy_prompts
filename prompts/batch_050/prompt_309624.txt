I've been running a small wine-tasting group for about a decade, and a disagreement has come up among us often enough that I've started to wonder whether philosophers have a name for it. The pattern is this. One member says that a wine she has drunk for years no longer tastes the way it used to: the flavor itself, she insists, has gone flat for her, and she experiences it differently than she once did. Another member, describing the very same trajectory of early enthusiasm and later indifference, puts it entirely differently: the wine tastes exactly as it always did, he says, and what has changed is only his reaction to it — the flavor is stable, but he has lost his appetite for it. What strikes me is that when we press them, both concede that nothing could settle the dispute. They would behave identically, report the same preferences, pass the same blind tests, and presumably show the same brain scans. Yet each insists that the other is misdescribing what actually happened, as though there were a fact being fought over that neither evidence nor introspection could reach.

I've since noticed the same shape of disagreement elsewhere. A friend who learned to love very spicy food cannot say whether the burning sensation has faded or whether the identical burn has simply become pleasant. A colleague who quit smoking says cigarettes now smell different to her, while her brother, also quit, says they smell exactly the same and only now repulse him. In each case there seem to be two rival accounts of a single transformation — the inner quality of the experience changed, versus only the person's stance toward an unchanged inner quality changed — and no conceivable observation, whether first-person or third-person, appears capable of deciding between them.

So my questions: does this predicament have an established name, and does the literature treat it as a genuine question with a correct answer that we merely cannot access, or as a sign that the two descriptions were never really distinct to begin with? If there is no fact of the matter here, does that tell us something general about whether there is any such thing as the raw feel of a sensation, over and above our reactions, judgments, and dispositions toward it? And if no existing term quite fits what I've described, please propose one, with a definition and some indication of which cases would fall outside it, since a bare label without the conceptual carving won't help me settle anything at our next tasting.
