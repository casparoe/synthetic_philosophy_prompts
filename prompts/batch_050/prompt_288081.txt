I work in organizational theory and am fluent in the vocabulary of principal-agent relationships, delegation chains, bureaucratic roles, procedural legitimacy, accountability mechanisms, and institutional drift. In Henry David Thoreau’s essay Civil Disobedience, originally published as Resistance to Civil Government, he writes:

“The mass of men serve the State thus, not as men mainly, but as machines, with their bodies. They are the standing army, and the militia, jailers, constables, posse comitatus, &c. In most cases there is no free exercise whatever of the judgment or of the moral sense; but they put themselves on a level with wood and earth and stones; and wooden men can perhaps be manufactured that will serve the purpose as well.”

In 1,200–1,500 words, reconstruct the position expressed in this passage using the vocabulary of organizational theory. Translate as systematically as possible: (1) Thoreau’s distinction between serving the state as a person and serving it as an instrument; (2) his account of how institutions turn ordinary occupants of roles into executors of injustice; and (3) what the passage implies about when role-based duties cease to bind. Distinguish claims directly licensed by the passage from plausible extrapolations from the essay as a whole.

Then provide a residue report. Identify which elements translate cleanly, which can be translated only by distorting Thoreau’s meaning, and which have no adequate expression in organizational-theoretic vocabulary. In particular, assess whether his appeal to the moral sense should be understood as an additional accountability mechanism, an external normative premise, or a challenge to the framework’s very way of individuating agents and assigning responsibility. Conclude by judging whether the residue exposes a limitation of institutional analysis or instead shows that Thoreau has blurred distinct questions about legitimacy, responsibility, and resistance. Write in American English, avoid reducing the argument to a generic case for or against civil disobedience, and do not include citations or a bibliography.
