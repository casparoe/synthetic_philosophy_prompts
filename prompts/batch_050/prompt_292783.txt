I teach a bioethics module and, before finalizing the syllabus, I want to check whether the position I've drifted into over the years is actually coherent. My commitments, as best I can state them, are the following. (a) The therapy/enhancement distinction marks a genuine moral line: bringing someone up to normal functioning is a matter of justice and can ground claims on shared resources, whereas improving someone beyond normal functioning is at most permissible and never owed. (b) "Health" and "disease" should be defined naturalistically, in terms of departures from species-typical functioning, and not by appeal to what a given society happens to value -- otherwise the concept of disease becomes whatever the majority disapproves of, and the history of psychiatry shows where that leads. (c) There is no intrinsic moral difference between producing a trait in a child through a pill and producing the same trait through environment or education; if the outcome is the same, the means are irrelevant to the verdict. (d) Routine vaccination is clearly therapy rather than enhancement, and funding it publicly is justified on exactly the same grounds as funding treatment. (e) Ultimately, what should drive our judgments about any intervention is its effect on the recipient's wellbeing, not its relation to the statistical norm of the species.

Tell me whether this set hangs together. I have a nagging worry that (b) and (d) don't sit well together, given what vaccination actually does to an already healthy immune system, and a vaguer worry that (e) might quietly do more damage to (a) than I've admitted to myself -- but I may be wrong on both counts, and (c) may interact with the others in ways I haven't traced. For each pair of commitments that is genuinely in tension, say what exactly forces the conflict and what the least costly revision would be; for pairs that only look in tension but aren't, say why the appearance is misleading. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and don't just tell me the whole framework is contested -- I know that. Keep the whole reply under 700 words.
