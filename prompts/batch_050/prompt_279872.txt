write me a parody HR policy document, about 650 words, from a company (something bland, like a mid-size logistics firm) whose board has decided that since the staff can't agree on which moral theory is right, all company decisions will now be made by maximizing "expected moral value" -- each plausible ethical theory gets assigned a credence, outcomes get scored under each theory, and the weighted total picks the policy. The document should be deadpan corporate: numbered sections, an appendix, a FAQ for employees, a revision history noting this is version 3.1 because earlier versions caused "stakeholder incidents."

The humor has to come from the real problems this framework actually has, exaggerated but not invented. So somewhere in there: an appendix on "intertheoretic exchange rates" that just flatly stipulates how many units of utility one instance of lying or one violated right is worth, presented as settled; a standing rule that any theory assigning infinite disvalue to anything is capped at 0.01% credence "for operational stability," with no justification given except that the alternative broke the spreadsheet; a Moral Parliament subcommittee where each theory's delegate gets votes proportional to how much is at stake according to that theory, so the utilitarian delegate somehow always controls procedure; a non-cognitivist employee complaint that gets filed and then closed on the grounds that his objection "does not express a proposition." Also at least one spot where the policy hits a genuine problem it can't solve and just papers over it in committee-speak.

Don't state any of the objections outright -- no narrator voice saying "of course this is absurd because." A reader who knows the moral uncertainty literature should recognize each joke as a real fault line and feel the satire is basically fair; a reader who doesn't should just slowly realize the document is describing something unhinged. End the document normally, like a real policy would, not with a punchline.
