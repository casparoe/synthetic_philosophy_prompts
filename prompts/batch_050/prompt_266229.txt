Here's roughly where I've landed on whether decomposition can preserve both competence and alignment:

1. I'm broadly on board with iterated distillation and amplification as a scheme: a human overseer with access to lots of weak copies of a model can produce answers better than either the human or the model could alone, you train the next model to imitate those answers, and you iterate.

2. Distillation is close enough to lossless for the scheme to work: whatever the distilled model fails to pick up will be recoverable in the next round of amplification anyway, so small imitation errors wash out rather than compound across iterations.

3. Decomposition preserves alignment: provided the overseer is aligned and each subquestion gets answered faithfully, the recombined answer serves the overseer's intent.

4. Debate gets you the fidelity checking more or less for free: two copies of the model arguing in front of the overseer make honesty the winning strategy, so the overseer doesn't need to be able to verify the reasoning itself.

5. Alignment and capability are separable axes -- a system can be very capable and misaligned, or weak and aligned -- so we can push capability up through iteration while auditing alignment seperately with red-teaming.
