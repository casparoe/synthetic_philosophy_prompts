So I repair commercial espresso machines for a living, cafes, restaurants, that kind of thing, and a big part of the job is diagnosis -- figuring out WHY a machine is down from a pile of symptoms. Over the years I developed my own informal system that I call duct-tape points: every time a theory about whats wrong needs a little patch to survive the evidence (like "ok the pressure is fine BUT maybe the gauge is lying" or "yes that part is new BUT maybe it was defective out of the box"), thats one duct-tape point. My rule of thumb is that any theory needing more than two points is probably wrong, even if it technically fits.

Recently I got obsessed with youtube videos about Kolmogorov complexity and Solomonoff induction, and it struck me that my duct-tape system is maybe a crude garage version of the same idea -- penalizing theories by how much extra stuff you have to say to keep them alive.
