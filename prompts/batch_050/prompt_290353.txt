I’m commissioning a taxonomy for a magazine issue on artificial life and the old DNA–Gödel analogy, and I want it built around Hofstadter-style Typogenetics rather than around slogans. For readers who have not memorized the literature: imagine a toy world with strands made of A, C, G and T; enzymes are themselves encoded on strands, yet also act on strands by copying, cutting, joining, inserting or rewriting letters according to local rules. The tempting claim is that this captures the same knot as molecular genetics and Gödelian self-reference: symbols can be both instructions and material, both text and thing. Please design a decision tree, eight to eleven branch nodes, that sorts my scientifically literate readers into named positions on how far that isomorphism really goes. Start from the commitment that a strand can sometimes specify operations that are later applied to strands, possibly including its own description, and make each branch a concrete diagnostic claim a reader can accept or reject without knowing the label: whether self-reference requires an external interpreter; whether copying fidelity matters more than semantic aboutness; whether a quine-like printer that cannot reproduce the active machinery still counts as self-replicating; whether an enzyme that edits the strand encoding it is closer to mutation, suicide, or learning; whether punctuation errors and amino-acid-like substitutions are failures of the same kind; whether selection and error catastrophe are essential rather than incidental; whether information is physically causal or merely a descriptive gloss; and whether an arbitrarily assigned coding convention still preserves an isomorphism.

Build the tree from the logical space first: give the combinations of answers, then place leaves such as strong formal isomorphism, weak pedagogical analogy, category-error eliminativism, construction-before-reference, genome-as-program-versus-data, observer-dependent semantics, origin-of-life adequacy, and any better-named position you need. For every unoccupied combination, say briefly whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any leaves your instrument cannot reliably separate rather than forcing a verdict. Each leaf should get one sentence naming its most awkward commitment for a general audience, plus one example from biology, computation, or logic that would embarrass it. No bibliography, no citations, and no list of further reading: the value should be in the cuts your tree makes and in whether it can tell apart a real theory of self-replication from a seductive formal rhyme.
