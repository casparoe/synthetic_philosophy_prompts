I have written literary fiction for twenty years, and my working method embodies a standing tension: I outline the whole book in advance, yet I compose each scene from inside a character who knows only what that character could know at that particular moment. I am currently drafting a novel whose protagonist suffers recurrent blackouts — she keeps arriving at situations she cannot tell apart and cannot remember how she handled the last time — and a friend tells me I have reinvented the absent-minded driver problem: the driver who must leave the highway at the second of two exits, who cannot tell, on reaching an exit, whether it is the first or the second, and who cannot recall what he did at any previous one. I would like that puzzle restated, as faithfully as you can manage, in the vocabulary I actually think in: the outline as a plan made before any scene exists, the individual scene as a local decision point, focalization and the strict limits of a character's knowledge, the character for whom two scenes are indistinguishable, the reader's dramatic irony, and the familiar craft conflict between what the plot requires and what the character would plausibly do from where she stands. Then give me the residue report: which elements of the puzzle — the plan chosen in advance, the indistinguishable decision points, the failure of memory, the driver's deliberate randomization between exiting and continuing, the numerical payoffs, and the fact that planner and decider are a single mind stretched across time — translate cleanly into craft terms, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all in the vocabulary of fiction. Close with a verdict on the residue: does what fails to translate mark a limitation of my craft vocabulary, or does it reveal that the puzzle essentially depends on something fiction necessarily splits in two — one agent who both makes the plan and must endorse or abandon it from inside the moment? Please give your response as a numbered list of exactly seven items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.
