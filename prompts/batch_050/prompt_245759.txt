I'm a high school student putting together a presentation for our philosophy club on whether doing nothing can genuinely cause something to happen, and I need a case built to order rather than a stock example.

Construct a single, realistic scenario — one paragraph, an everyday setting such as an apartment building, a workplace, or a campsite — in which two different people each fail to act, such that (a) had either one of them acted, the bad outcome would have been prevented, so the outcome depends counterfactually on each omission equally, yet (b) common sense clearly treats only one of the two omissions as a cause of the outcome. Two constraints: neither person may hold a special professional role (no lifeguards, doctors, or security guards), since that would settle the verdict too cheaply through job duties, and the case must actually generate the two intuitions rather than stipulate them.

After the scenario, do the accounting: state what verdict each of the following gives and why — a pure counterfactual account of causation along Lewisian lines, an account on which an omission causes only when it departs from what is normal or to be expected, and a contrastive account on which causes are identified against a specific alternative outcome. Then identify which single feature of your scenario is doing the work of separating the two omissions, say what happens to the verdicts if you remove that feature, and close with the best reply a defender of the pure counterfactual view could give to stop the case from counting against them.
