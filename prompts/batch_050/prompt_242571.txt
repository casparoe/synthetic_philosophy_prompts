As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I’m used to separating forecasts from hopes, tendencies from guarantees, and moral claims from claims about optimization processes. In a recent discussion about institutional decay, someone dismissed worries about long-term political regression by saying that the arc of history bends toward justice.

Treat that slogan as ambiguous rather than as a platitude. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making: for example, a statistical trend claim, a claim about long-run causation, a claim about equilibrium or attractor states, a providential or teleological claim, a definitional claim about what counts as justice, or an exhortation disguised as a prediction. For each interpretation, explain what evidence would count for or against it, over what timescale it should be evaluated, and whether it is compatible with severe local reversals, moral uncertainty, path dependence, or irreversible catastrophes.

Then assess which readings are trivial, which are substantive but unsupported, which might be defensible under careful qualification, and which are likely doing rhetorical work rather than making a forecast. In particular, distinguish the claim that moral improvement is probable from the claim that it is inevitable, and both from the claim that acting as if it is inevitable will help bring it about. Finally, say whether any plausible version of the slogan justifies dismissing concerns about institutional decay, or whether it is better understood as a call to identify the mechanisms that might make moral progress more likely.
