At a dinner party, two friends got into an argument about whether the simulation argument should temper enthusiasm for prioritizing the far future. (1) Maya argued that longtermism derives its force from a multiplier -- the sheer number of future lives that could exist -- and that the simulation hypothesis casts doubt on precisely that multiplier: a civilization simulating us plausibly runs on finite computational resources, might terminate or downgrade the simulation, and has no particular reason to compute a galaxy-spanning future for us, so the astronomical populations doing all the work in the calculus are exactly the ones whose existence is least secure. (2) Daniel replied that the argument is perfectly general and therefore inert: if being simulated discounts future people, it discounts present people too, and a consideration that deflates everything equally cannot change anyone's priorities -- besides, the hypothesis includes simulators with vast resources running complete cosmologies, not just stingy ones. (3) Maya answered that the cases are not symmetric: our duties to the present rest on people who demonstrably exist and suffer now, whatever their ultimate metaphysical status, whereas the case for the far future rests on a speculative extrapolation that the hypothesis specifically targets; suffering is real to its bearer even in a simulation, but trillions of future sims that the simulators may never bother to instantiate are real to no one. (4) Daniel countered that this proves too little: the worry that the future may be cut short exists in an unsimulated universe too -- existential catastrophe, false vacuum decay -- so the simulation argument adds nothing to ordinary uncertainty, and Maya's appeal to simulators' motives and compute budgets is speculation stacked on speculation. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: say who is right about which specific point, identify anything both parties are missing, and give an overall verdict on whether the simulation argument bears on far-future fanaticism at all.
