Is there a name for this? Ive noticed that people (me included) will keep paying for something they never use as long as canceling requires doing something, but if the same thing lapses on its own -- card expires, free trial ends -- they almost never bother to restart it. Like my gym membership: hadn't gone in eight months, wouldn't cancel because "I might get back into it," then my card got replaced, the payments failed, and I just never updated it. Honestly felt relieved. The end state is identical either way (no gym, no payments), the only difference is which of the two outcomes requires me to lift a finger. I dont think it's sunk cost, since I wasn't trying to justify money already spent, I knew that was gone. And it doesn't feel like textbook loss aversion either, because the thing I'd supposedly be losing is something I demonstrably don't use -- what's being protected seems to be a possibility, not a possession. Is there an established concept for this asymmetry between actively ending something and passively not resuming it? Where exactly does it sit relative to status quo bias and plain procrastination? And if it doesn't have its own name, what would you call it and where would you draw its edges?
