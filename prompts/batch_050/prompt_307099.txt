I work at a mid-sized AI lab and a lot of what we do is reinforcement learning -- agents trained with reward and penalty signals, sometimes millions of episodes. Lately I keep getting stuck on a worry I can't shake or dismiss: if an agent is shaped by negative rewards the way an animal is shaped by pain, at what point (if ever) should I start treating the penalty signal as something the agent *dislikes* rather than just a number that updates weights? I don't want to be the person who panics over a thermostat, but I also don't want to be the person who shrugs at something genuinely aversive because it happens to be implemented in Python.

Give me a checklist I can actually run before we greenlight a training run -- something I can do in maybe 30 minutes per run, that a tech lead with no philosophy background can sign off on. It should tell me what features of a setup to look at (things like: does the agent build persistent internal representations of penalty sources, does it develop flexible avoidance behavior that generalizes beyond training, is the negative signal doing long-run "teaching" or moment-to-moment control), what counts as evidence for vs. against the signal functioning like something bad, and where I should just flag it as "we don't know and here's what that means for how cautious to be." Be upfront about which steps are real tests and which are judgment calls I'm pretending are tests.

Then walk me through applying it to three cases we're actually deciding between this quarter:
1. A warehouse-navigation agent that gets a large negative reward for collisions and has learned to freeze near certain shelf layouts -- it will sit motionless for the whole episode rather than risk a route that sometimes clips a corner.
2. A game-playing agent that gets penalized for losing pieces but can "resign" bad positions, which in training just means resetting the episode early.
3. A language model fine-tuned with a reward model that punishes certain outputs, where the fine-tuning makes it produce long apologetic hedges in exactly the contexts that used to get penalized.

At least one of these should be a case where the procedure spits out an answer I won't enjoy acting on, and say what the checklist just can't settle no matter how carefully I run it.
