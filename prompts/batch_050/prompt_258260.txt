So I host a kind of philosophy-ish game night at our community centre, its just a bunch of regular people, no students or anything, maybe 10-14 folks on a good night, and we get the room for about 75 minutes. I read somewhere about this thing where people would rather bet on a jar where they know exactly whats in it than a jar where they dont, even when the odds work out the same, and I want to build a whole evening around making people FEEL that, not just hear about it.

Design me an actual game we can run. Heres what I need:

- The setup should involve real choices with some kind of stakes (not money, maybe tokens that convert into who has to bring snacks next time or something). Two containers, one where the contents are fully known and one where theyre hidden. People pick which to bet on, repeatedly, and ideally they start noticing that they keep paying a little extra to avoid the mysterious one even when they cant explain why.
- The idea has to land through the playing, not through me explaining. So no mini-lecture at the start. They should catch themselves acting weird first, then we name it.
- Heres a twist I want built in: in a later round, one player gets to be the "house" and secretly decides the contents of the mystery jar AFTER seeing which side everyone bet on. I want to know whether peoples fear of the unknown jar gets worse when the unknown is controlled by someone who profits from their loss, or whether its the same. Build that comparison into the design and tell me what the two cases might reveal if they come apart.
- Timeline for the 75 minutes, exact rules, what materials I need to buy or make, how the scoring works.
- A debrief plan: what questions do I ask, in what order, so the group reconstructs the principle themselves (something like: a bet can look bad not because the odds are bad but because you cant pin down the odds at all). Also how to handle the one smartass who says "well its basically 50/50 anyway, why did I hesitate."
- Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time -- people gaming the tokens, someone peeking, the house player being too nice, whatever -- and how to patch it.
- And be honest with me about what the game cant capture. I have a feeling sitting at a table with friends is different from, like, deciding on a medical treatment with unknown risks, so tell me which part of the real phenomenon stays outside the room and that I'd have to just say out loud.
