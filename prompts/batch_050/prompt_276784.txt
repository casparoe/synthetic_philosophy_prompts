I run a small animal welfare charity and lately I've been reading about this position called lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, where the idea is roughly that some suffering is so bad that no amount of pleasure can make up for it, but below that threshold tradeoffs between pleasure and pain might still be fine. I'm trying to figure out what this would actually imply for how we spend our budget, since we currently fund both things that reduce suffering (shelter vet care, spay/neuter stuff) and things that add positive experiences (enrichment programs, a dog park we maintain, etc). Please describe the most important implications of this view for our funding decisions, including the cases where the implications get weird or counterintuitive. Also, the whole thing seems to hinge on whether pleasure and pain are even commensurable in the first place — like whether there's really a single scale where units of one can be traded against units of the other. If they're NOT commensurable in that way, what happens to the lexical threshold view? Does it collapse, get stronger, or turn into something else? I'd especially like to know where the view forces conclusions I'd probably hate (e.g. about the dog park money) and whether those are genuine bullet-biting moments or signs the view needs tweaking.
