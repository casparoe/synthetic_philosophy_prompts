I've recently started tutoring an undergraduate logic course, and our unit on modus ponens sent me back to Lewis Carroll's "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles," where the Tortoise announces: "Well, now, I want you to consider me as a reader of the second kind, and to force me, logically, to accept Z as true." Over coffee afterward, two colleagues disagreed sharply about what that demand actually shows, and I couldn't settle it.

Elena argued that the passage exposes a genuine justificatory regress: the Tortoise accepts the premises but withholds the inference, and since every attempt to secure the inference only adds another premise the Tortoise can decline, no argument can ever compel acceptance of a conclusion — deduction, on her reading, is ultimately underwritten by nothing more than habit. Daniel objected that this misreads the passage entirely. On his view, the Tortoise's trick is to treat a rule of inference as if it were a further premise; rules are used, not stated, so converting modus ponens into premise C changes nothing, because the original rule still does the work, and the regress is an artifact of that category error rather than a problem for deduction. Elena replied that the premise/rule distinction merely relabels the difficulty: saying a rule is "used" rather than "stated" doesn't explain what makes using it correct, and if nothing does, her conclusion stands. Daniel countered that demanding a justification for basic inference rules is incoherent, since any justification offered would itself employ inference, and a practice that constitutes justification cannot sensibly be asked to justify itself. Elena's last word was that this concedes her point: if the rule is just an unjustifiable practice we happen to follow, then logical compulsion collapses into psychological compulsion, which is precisely what the Tortoise is mocking.

Could you adjudicate this exchange move by move? For each of the five moves, say whether it succeeds, partly succeeds, or fails, and why. Then tell me who is more right overall about what the Tortoise's demand shows, and whether both sides are missing something about what it would mean to force someone logically to accept Z.
