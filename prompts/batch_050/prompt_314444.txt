My friend Daniel died last month at fifty-eight. He was a longtime fixture of our local rationalist meetup and a convinced Hofstadterian about personal identity: on his view, a self is a pattern rather than a substance, and because that pattern gets copied -- at steadily lower resolution -- into the minds of everyone who knows you, death is less like a light going out than like a signal attenuating with distance. He made me promise that at his memorial I would take this seriously as a claim, rather than treating it as a quirky metaphor he happened to like. I'm delivering the main remarks. The audience will be split between meetup friends, who have read I Am a Strange Loop and will notice if I fudge the philosophy, and his family, who are churchgoing and grieving conventionally. Please write a seven-to-eight-minute eulogy that honors his actual view, including its hard edge -- what persists in the rest of us is a coarse, lossy copy of Daniel, not Daniel himself -- without letting that edge curdle into coldness, and without smuggling in an afterlife he didn't believe in. The speech should also engage honestly with the objection his family will feel even if they never voice it: that a blurry sketch of a person running on someone else's hardware is a poor substitute for the person, and that calling it survival is a consolation only a very particular kind of person could mistake for comfort.
