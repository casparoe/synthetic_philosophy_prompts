I'm helping allocate a small methods-reform fund (about €2M over four years) aimed at the replication crisis in psychology and the adjacent social sciences. One thing I keep noticing is that the debate gets framed almost entirely as a fight over inference -- p-values vs Bayes factors vs estimation-with-uncertainty -- while what I'd call the upstream stuff (construct validity, measurement precision, whether the "effect" in a replication is even the same effect as in the original) gets way less airtime. If you had to rank the open questions by where the money does the most good, what would your ordering be? Candidates I'm weighing: (a) settling when Bayesian reanalysis actually changes substantive conclusions vs just repackaging them, (b) shared measurement/operationalization infrastructure so that "successful replication" means something, (c) working out which findings are fragile to analytic flexibility vs robust across reasonable specifications, (d) incentive redesign at journals and funders. Defend the ordering with explicit criteria -- how many downstream problems each one unblocks, tractability, neglectedness -- and also tell me which popular reform priority you'd actually defund, and what evidence would change your ranking.
