I work in the governance office of a mid-sized city, and ever since two citizens' assemblies we ran got good press, every department head now wants one for their pet issue. I need a reusable decision procedure for when sortition -- a randomly selected, demographically stratified panel of residents -- is actually the right tool, versus a normal council vote, a public consultation, or just deferring to staff experts. Write it as an ordered checklist I can run in a 30-minute meeting: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a passing answer to each, the points where the procedure returns a hard no (think: decisions that need to be reversible fast, or where someone identifiable should lose their job if it goes wrong), and the points where it has to hand off to a person because the question is values not facts.

Take definite positions. Don't hand me a menu of considerations -- pick thresholds and defend them, including on the contested stuff: whether panels should ever get binding power instead of advisory power, and whether a permanent standing body chosen by lot (one councilor keeps pitching this, lottocracy-style, to replace our planning committee) is a different animal from a one-off assembly or just a bigger version of the same idea.

Then run the procedure on three cases actually in our queue: (1) redesigning the downtown street layout, (2) setting the police department's use-of-force policy, (3) allocating a one-time $4M budget surplus. At least one of these should come out no even though the optics would be great, and tell me which. Finish with what the procedure can't do.
