so i run a little humor studies reading group (mostly grad students, a couple of standup comics who sit in) and i want to make a "which theory of humor do you actually believe?" quiz for our last session. the four theories weve covered are superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation, and the whole point of the quiz is that nobody gets to just pick the label they like -- they have to get sorted by what they say about actual cases.

so: 9 or 10 questions, and each one should be a concrete funny thing (a specific kind of joke, pratfall, meme format, awkward moment at a funeral, a kid saying something brutal, laughing when youre tickled, whatever you think sorts well) followed by 2-4 answer options about *what's doing the work in making it funny* or *why it stops being funny in some variation*. the options have to be phrased so someone can answer without knowing the theory names -- no "do you think laughter is an expression of superiority" nonsense. like, a question about why a joke lands worse when the target is clearly weaker than the audience vs. clearly more powerful is a good sorter; a question that just describes a joke and asks "is this funny" is not.

then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the four theories. a few things i want handled in the key:

- some questions should deliberately split benign violation from plain incongruity, since those two are the easiest to blur -- e.g. cases where something is surprising/incongruent but has no violation at all, or has a violation that isnt made benign
- include at least one case each theory famously struggles with (tickling, puns/wordplay, laughing from nervousness or relief after a near-miss) and let the key notice when someone's answers are all over the place
- if a pattern doesnt land cleanly on one theory, give a mixed outcome with a name (like "superiority with incongruity leanings") rather than forcing it -- but also tell me which two theories your quiz just *cant* separate with these questions, and why, instead of pretending it can

flag at the end which single question does the most sorting work and which one you'd cut first. no citations needed, i already have the readings, i just need the instrument.
