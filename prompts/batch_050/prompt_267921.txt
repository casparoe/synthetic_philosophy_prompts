I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've been having the same argument with a close friend, a physicist, for over a year. It keeps resurfacing in slightly different forms, and neither of us has conceded a single point. The question at the center of it is: why is there something rather than nothing?

His position is that modern physics has essentially answered this question, or at least shown how it will be answered. On his telling, empty space was never really empty: the quantum vacuum is a structured, law-governed state with fluctuations, and physicists have models in which a universe like ours can emerge from such a state with no external cause. He thinks the old philosophical puzzle is a leftover from a time when nobody understood this, and that demanding a further explanation for why the laws and fields themselves exist is like asking what a rock is for: grammatically a question, but not one reality owes us an answer to.

My position is that he has changed the subject. What he's explained is how one something (a vacuum, fields, laws, mathematical structure) gives rise to another something (particles, atoms, galaxies). But "nothing," as the question intends it, means no fields, no laws, no structure, no space, no time. Any physical explanation, it seems to me, has to start with something already on stage, so the question of why there's anything at all lies permanently outside its reach. As an engineer, I'd put it this way: he's explained how the system produces its output, but not why there's a system at all.

What's striking to me is that we agree on every relevant fact. He doesn't dispute that his accounts presuppose laws and fields; I don't dispute the physics. Yet the impasse is total.

My working hypothesis is that this is a purely verbal dispute about the word "nothing" that would dissolve if we just defined our terms. But I suspect it might instead be a genuine disagreement about what counts as an explanation, or about whether "why is there anything?" is a question that can, or must, have an answer at all.

So here's what I'm asking for. First, diagnose the disagreement: is it verbal, is it about standards of explanation, is it a clash of starting intuitions neither side can argue for, or are we answering two different questions under one heading? Engage with the two positions as I've stated them rather than surveying the history of the debate. Second, given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a sharper distinction, a formal result, a new kind of argument, a reframing of the question? Or is this the kind of dispute where no progress should be expected, and if so, why?
