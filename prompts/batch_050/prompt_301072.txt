So I just spent like three hours arguing with my buddy Marcus about free will (it started over wings and got way out of hand) and I'm stuck on something about the argument itself, not about who's right. Here's where we landed: Marcus is an incompatibilist, basically the hard determinist kind — he says if determinism is true then nobody ever really could've done otherwise, so nobody truly deserves blame or credit, and all the compatibilist stuff about acting on your own reasons and not being coerced is just, his words, changing the subject. I kept pushing the compatibilist line — the abilities that matter are things like "would have done otherwise if she'd decided to," being responsive to reasons, the action coming from you and not a brain tumor or a guy with a gun. The weird part: we never disagreed about a single fact. Not one. He grants everything I say about how deliberation and self-control work, I grant him that given the exact past and the laws, only one future was possible, and we still walked away disagreeing about whether a guy who robbed a liquor store "could have done otherwise."

So my question for you: what kind of disagreement is this actually? I've got three candidate diagnoses and I want you to evaluate them and pick one (or reject all three with something better), with actual reasons:

1. It's purely verbal — we both just mean different things by "could have done otherwise" (a conditional ability vs. some categorical alternative-possibilities thing) and we're talking past each other, and the whole compatibilism/incompatibilism fight in philosophy is this scaled up.
2. It's substantive but not about freedom at all — the real engine is a disagreement about what blame and desert are *for*, i.e. Marcus thinks blame requires that the person deserves to suffer in some basic sense, and I think blame is a forward-looking social practice, and "could have done otherwise" is just where that fight happens to be visible.
3. It's a bedrock clash of unarguable intuitions — he has the intuition that tracing any action back through causes outside the agent makes the agent disappear, I have the intuition that a normal adult making a decision in full possession of their faculties is the paradigmatic case of freedom, and neither of these can be argued for, only felt.

A couple constraints: diagnose the structure of the debate, don't adjudicate it — I'm not asking you to tell me whether compatibilism is true, I'm asking what Marcus and I were even doing for three hours. Use what each side actually says (manipulation arguments where a secret neurosurgeon designs someone to commit the crime, the consequence argument, the conditional analysis of ability, sourcehood vs. leeway, all that) as evidence for your diagnosis, not as things to refute. And then the payoff: given your diagnosis, tell me what would actually move an argument like ours. If it's verbal, would stipulating terms fix it or would the disagreement just migrate somewhere else? If it's about desert, is there some further question we should've been arguing about instead? If it's bedrock intuitions, is there literally anything — a thought experiment, an experimental philosophy result about what ordinary people actually intuit, a conceptual distinction — that would count as progress, or should Marcus and I just agree to never do this again?

Also be honest about the meta-level: is the fact that this debate has run for centuries with super smart people on both sides itself evidence for one of these diagnoses?
