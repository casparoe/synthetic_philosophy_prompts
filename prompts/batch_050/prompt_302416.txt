Write a short story related to whether we owe more to people from our own country than to strangers far away.

Here's the setup I'd like you to run with, though feel free to change details. The main character, call her whatever, works for a charity and her job is literally to decide where a big donation goes. Option one: fix the contaminated water supply in a poor neighborhood in her own city, people she could drive and visit, who wave at her. Option two: the same money could save maybe ten times as many lives through malaria nets overseas, but she'll never meet any of those people and can't picture them. She's not a bad person, she just keeps noticing that the local family FEELS real to her and the faraway ones feel like a statistic, and she's not sure if that's a moral insight or just a bug in her brain.

The thing I want the story to actually wrestle with, not just mention: is her pull toward the locals like the pull a parent feels toward their own kid (which seems fine, maybe even good), or is it more like a hiring manager who favors people from his old fraternity (which seems clearly bad)? I honestly can't decide if national borders are morally more like families or more like fraternities, and I want the story to make me feel the weight of both answers.

Don't give it a tidy ending where she figures out the obviously right answer. Maybe let her make a choice and then have to live with the part of it that still feels wrong. Somewhere in the 800-1200 word range is good.
