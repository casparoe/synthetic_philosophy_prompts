ok so i'm in a metaethics seminar this semester and we just did the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism, and i realized i can recite the standard example (the "if tormenting the cat is wrong..." one everyone uses) but i'm not sure i actually *understand* the problem well enough to generate a new case of it. so here's what i want you to do.

Construct an original short dialogue — like 6-10 lines, everyday setting, two roommates or coworkers or whatever, no seminar room, no one saying "the moral proposition that p" — that generates a fresh Frege–Geach-style embedding problem, under these constraints:

1. The normative/moral language has to show up in at least two different unasserted contexts, and one of them can't be a plain conditional antecedent. Use something like a question, a "suppose that..." construction, an imperative, or "it's unlikely that..." — pick whichever, but not just the standard if-then setup.
2. The dialogue has to contain an inference that both speakers obviously treat as valid, so there's a genuine logical problem to solve and not just people expressing attitudes at each other.
3. Include at least one line where a naive reader would be tempted to take the normative talk as plain description (e.g. someone cites a moral claim as evidence, or says "that's true"), so the expressivist has to actually explain that line instead of ignoring it.

Then, after the dialogue:

(a) Go line by line and show where the crude emotivist or prescriptivist reading breaks — like, concretely, which utterance it can't parse and why the inference goes invalid on that reading.

(b) Take one modern expressivist framework (Schroeder's "being for," Gibbard's plan-laden states, hybrid expressivism, whichever you think fits your dialogue best) and show how it handles the embedding *in your specific case* — not a general sketch of the view, but applied to your actual lines.

(c) Tell me whether your choice of unasserted context makes the problem harder or easier than the textbook conditional version, and why.

One more thing: be upfront about what's settled vs. genuinely contested here. As far as i can tell, everyone grants that the embedding problem is a real constraint any expressivist semantics has to meet, but whether the contemporary solutions actually work is very much disputed — so don't present one of them as the accepted fix. And flag anywhere your dialogue might be quietly building in an assumption an expressivist would just deny (like assuming validity has to be cashed out in truth-conditional terms), since that would be begging the question rather than testing the view.
