So in my philosphy elective we got into Kavka’s toxin puzzle, the one where a billionaire pays you if tonight you sincerely intend to drink a gross toxin tomorrow, even though tomorrow you’ll have no reason to actually drink it. I kept arguing with my friend Mara because I think a rational person is basically locked out of winning, and she thinks that makes rationality sound magical. Here’s a tweaked version I care more about: imagine a sleep lab has a headset that can tell at 11:59 pm whether you have a real, whole-hearted intention to press a button at noon tomorrow that releases a harmless but awful-tasting sludge into your mouth. If the headset detects genuine intention, $10,000 is wired to your family at midnight and cannot be clawed back. By noon, everyone knows the money is already safe, the sludge only makes you nauseous for an hour, and pressing is strictly worse than not pressing.

Give me your actual credence that an ideally rational person, knowing all this in advance, could win the money without lying to themselves, hypnotizing themselves, becoming irrational, or using some loophole like getting kidnapped. I want a number, not just “depends what rational means.” Decompose it like: probability that intention can be causally separated from later action enough to fool the headset; probability that rationality is mainly time-slice so tomorrow-me not pressing is fine; probability that rationality is diachronic so making a plan you’ll later abandon counts against you; probability that genuine intention requires some real motivational grip rather than just saying the words inside; plus any term I missed. Then combine them roughly and say which term your final number is most sensitive to.

Also, Mara says the lab case is different from the original because the headset is an external judge, so intending is more like making a credible commitment to a machine, and machines can be gamed. Do you think that helps her side, hurts it, or is it a distraction? If your credence is above 0.5, what kind of psychology would the winner need? If it’s below 0.5, is the puzzle showing rationality is self-defeating, or just that some prizes are designed so only the wrong kind of mind can grab them?
