Topic: commitment races. By that I mean situations where two parties each gain an advantage by being the first to visibly bind themselves to a course of action — Schelling-style, burn the boats, throw the steering wheel out the window — because once one side is credibly locked in, the other side is forced to accommodate it; but if both sides lock in to incompatible positions they crash into each other, so the whole thing has a chicken-game structure with a speed contest layered on top. My hunch: the standard way this gets modeled treats commitment as instant, fully credible, and immediately visible to everyone, which makes the analysis collapse into "move first, win." But real commitments are leaky. They take time to build (you can't deploy a weapons system or a reputation overnight), they can be walked back at some cost, and the other side learns about them with a lag. I suspect that once you build in construction time, partial revocability, and detection lags, you get qualitatively different equilibria — maybe ones where both sides rationally stay slow, or even deliberately avoid learning about the other's progress, since ignorance is what lets you keep your own hands free. Basically I want to know under what conditions the race incentive actually survives, because "everyone should commit as fast as possible" seems like a conclusion we stumbled into via an idealization rather than a robust finding.

What I need in the proposal: a precise statement of the question (narrow enough to actually answer in a year); why the existing machinery — first-mover advantage in bargaining models, brinkmanship, the program-equilibrium literature where agents can inspect each other's code — doesn't already settle it; the method, which I want to be actually building and solving a small formal model with a timing structure for commitments, then pressure-testing the results against one real-ish case (nuclear posture, or automated agents negotiating with each other); what a positive result looks like vs. what counts as failure — and here be honest, because if the race incentive turns out to be robust to delay and revocability that's a result but a boring one, so tell me how to frame the project so that outcome is still publishable; and a realistic month-by-month-ish scope for one person, twelve months, who is okay-but-not-great at game theory.
