For my senior philosophy elective, write a 24-line poem on Augustine’s account of time in Confessions XI, aimed at classmates who keep saying that only the present is real. Use three eight-line stanzas for memory, attention, and expectation, but make the middle stanza’s lines noticeably shorter than the other two so the page itself performs the squeeze of the present. Let the first and last stanzas each repeat one exact line from the middle stanza, altered by only one word, so recollection and anticipation appear as the present stretched in opposite directions rather than as two extra rooms behind and ahead. The poem should take seriously Augustine’s claim that what we call past and future exist as present memory, present sight, and present expectation, and that time is measured as a distension of the mind, but it should not end by simply announcing that everything is now; close with an image in which attention gathers the stretch without erasing it. Keep the diction plain enough for a high school seminar, avoid quotation or pastiche, and make the argument recognizable to someone who knows the distinction between distentio and intentio.
