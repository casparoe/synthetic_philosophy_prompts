The theorem, as I understand it, ties continuous symmetries of a system's dynamics to conserved quantities — time-shift symmetry to energy, spatial translation to momentum, rotation to angular momentum. But then the argument starts: some people say this shows conservation laws aren't brute facts but follow from deep structural features of the world; others insist the theorem is just a formal correspondence between two mathematical objects and explains nothing on its own; and there's a further scrap about which direction the explanation is supposed to run, and what to make of gauge symmetries that don't yield any conserved quantity at all but still seem to do explanatory work. Everyone in this debate seems extremely smart and no one seems to move.
