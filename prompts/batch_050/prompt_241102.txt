I'm a novelist, currently stuck on my third book, a time travel story. A friend who studied philosophy told me that most philosophers of time these days are eternalists — the past, present and future all equally exist, like every frame of a filmstrip is already sitting there at once. Honestly this has been bugging me alot as a writer. If 2145 already exists, then my protagonist's choices there are already made, so nothing she does can actually change anything and the stakes just evaporate. So is eternalism basically just fatalism with fancier packaging? Should I build the book's world on presentism instead, so the future is genuinely open and I can have real suspense and real moral responsibility? Or is there some other theory of time that lets me keep free will intact for the plot?
