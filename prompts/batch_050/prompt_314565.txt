I host a small podcast whose audience is drawn mostly from readers of the rationalist blogs and forums, and next month I am interviewing a cognitive scientist whose new book defends the Hofstadterian thesis that the self is a strange loop: a level-crossing structure in which a mind's highest-level self-symbol reaches back down and steers the lower levels that implement it. On her view, this also explains the inviolate level — there is a level of description (beliefs, goals, the self-model) at which a person can be meaningfully intervened upon without anyone touching the neural machinery underneath, and the autonomy of that level from what lies below it is partly what selfhood consists in.

She comes with practiced answers. Thermostats and economies, she says, are mere feedback rather than strange loops, because they lack a causally active self-symbol. Quines and self-printing programs have self-reference without tangling, because nothing at their top level reaches back down to reconstitute the bottom. And when someone raises Escher's Drawing Hands — each hand drawing the other, while Escher's own hand sits outside the loop doing the real work — she replies that the third hand is only the artist's conceit, whereas minds genuinely bootstrap.

Write me ten to twelve interview questions that do not let those answers stand. For each question, state the reply she is most likely to give and the follow-up that forecloses it. The set should, among other things: demand an operational test for when a purported self-symbol is doing causal work rather than being projected onto the system by the interpreter; press on whether a model that can inspect its own weights, or that is trained on data containing its own previous outputs, realizes a genuine strange loop or merely couples two systems that an outside level still coordinates; ask whether prompt injection — attacker text that seizes control of a model's behavior through its input channel — shows that the inviolate level was never inviolate, or instead shows only that the level is porous in a way her account can absorb; and close with a question that forces her to choose between her two commitments, the inviolate level and the level-crossing tangle, in a case where they come apart. No warm-up questions and nothing biographical; every question should turn on the actual content of the view.
