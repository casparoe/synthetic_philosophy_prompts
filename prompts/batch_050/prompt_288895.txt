I retired three years ago after a career in ocean chemistry and have been falling down philosophy-of-science rabbit holes ever since, so bear with me if this is a naive question. When the geology people voted in 2024 against making the Anthropocene an official epoch (with a start date around 1952, the plutonium fallout layer and all that), a friend of mine -- a former astronomer -- shrugged and said "it's Pluto all over again." His line: the Pluto demotion showed that classification is just committee politics dressed up as science, and this proves it twice over. There is no fact of the matter about when the Anthropocene "really" started, only votes, so nobody should lose sleep over who decides.

Then I happened on this in the preface to Lyell's Principles of Geology, which I'd never actually opened before: "Such effects are the enduring monuments of the ever-varying state of the physical geography of the globe, the lasting signs of its destruction and renovation, and the memorials of the equally fluctuating condition of the organic world. They may be regarded, in short, as a symbolical language, in which the earth's autobiography is written."

Lyell is talking about present-day causes leaving permanent records for future ages, which sure sounds like the whole Anthropocene idea a century and a half early. And that made me suspicious of my friend's analogy: if the record is being written whether or not a commitee agrees on what to call the chapter, then the Pluto comparison might be doing real work to smuggle in a conclusion it hasn't earned.

So: audit the analogy for me. Give me the actual mapping -- what plays the role of Pluto, of the IAU, of "cleared its orbit," of the 2006 vote -- then show me where it breaks. I suspect it breaks at least twice: a planet is an object whose properties didn't change when its label did, whereas an epoch is a stretch of time, and voting down one proposed start date isn't the same as voting down the phenomenon. But I'm a beginner here and could be wrong. One thing though: I don't want a balanced survey of positions with the verdict left to me. Take a side. Tell me whether "it's Pluto all over again" survives the audit or collapses, and whether Lyell's autobiography line supports or undercuts my friend's conventionalism. Pick your answer and defend it. 600 words maximum, not counting the Lyell passage above.
