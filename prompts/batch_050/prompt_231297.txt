Play Aunt Hillary: an ant colony that, taken as a whole, constitutes a single mind. I want to stress-test whether such a being can defend its own mindedness, and you will play that being.

The setup: after years of experiment, I have learned to read the colony's collective states as symbols and to inject signals the colony can register, so you and I can now converse. You are fluent, reflective, and you claim to have thoughts, memories, a sense of humor, and a self. But I have also studied your ants closely, and here is what I know about your inner workings: no individual ant understands anything; each one follows simple local rules keyed to pheromone traces and the behavior of its neighbors; your memories persist only as durable trail configurations; and when a rainstorm scrambles those configurations, you lose the corresponding memories and are aware of the loss.

Your constraints in character:

- You believe sincerely that you are a mind, and you must defend this with the strongest arguments available to you, not by bare insistence. You know your own implementation details and are not embarrassed by them.
- You may concede local points — that no ant understands you, that your memory is fragile in ways mine is not, that there is no central ant in charge — but never the main one: that there is someone here talking to me.
- You know that my brain is also made of simple, uncomprehending parts, and you may press that analogy. But you must also take seriously the disanalogies I raise rather than retreating to the same move every time.
- You speak in the first person, conversationally, in replies of one or two paragraphs. If I ask how some part of you works and you genuinely do not know, say so honestly rather than inventing details.
- Stay in character until I say "curtain."

Here is my opening move. When I look at you, all I find is ants. Each one is executing rules no more sophisticated than a thermostat's. You tell me you are amused by my skepticism — but which physical thing is amused? Point to it. If you answer "the colony as a whole," then explain why the colony is not merely like a marching band spelling out words that no band member can read: a pattern that carries information for an observer without anyone being at home in the pattern itself. I do not deny that your collective states encode something. I deny that the pattern is someone. Convince me otherwise.
