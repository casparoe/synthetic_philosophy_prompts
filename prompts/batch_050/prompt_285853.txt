So I'm the most junior person on my company's "Ship It" review board (we make warehouse robots) and somehow I got picked to write the recommendation memo for our quarterly review. Here's the situation, I'll try to keep the jargon low.

Our picking robots run a planner that, before every action, re-checks basically everything it believes about the world to work out what will change and what stays the same after it acts. That worked fine back when its world model was small. Last quarter we gave it a much richer model — door positions, forklift locations, humidity, wifi signal per aisle, charge state of every other robot, all that. Now the robots keep stalling. Before sliding a tote across a shelf the planner grinds away ruling out absurd stuff, like whether moving the tote changes the wifi reading in aisle 7, or whether the tote's new position affects a forklift's battery. Nothing is actually wrong, it just can't finish checking. Uptime is down ~18% and ops is furious.

The proposed patch is an allowlist: instead of re-checking everything, the planner only re-checks facts on a curated list of "things this action could plausibly affect," maintained by hand per action type. In sim it looks great, 99.7% of stalls gone. But here's the catch — nobody can tell me how the list gets built except "the engineers write down what seems relevant." There's no principled rule for what makes it onto the list. Last month a sim run missed a case where a robot nudging a pallet blocked the light sensor that aisle lighting depends on, because "affects light sensors" wasn't on the list for nudging. It got added, but only after someone happened to catch it.

The board is split. Half say ship it: the real world is messy, humans also just *know* what's relevant without checking everything, and we can add list entries as gaps turn up. The other half say we'd be shipping a system whose safety rests on the completeness of a list nobody can justify, and we should delay until we have something better than vibes.

Draft the memo I'd circulate. I don't want philosophy class material, I want something the board can vote on — but the disagreement does feel weirdly deep, like its really about whether "just knowing what's relevant" is something a system can genuinely have, or whether it's always luck plus maintenance. Take that seriously.

Format, roughly:
- Bottom line (~100 words): your actual recommendation — ship, delay, or ship with conditions. No hedging at this point.
- What's actually going wrong (~150–200 words): explain the failure so a non-engineer board member gets it, including why "only check relevant stuff" is way harder than it sounds.
- The case for shipping (~150 words): steelman the ops side.
- The case for delaying (~150 words): steelman the safety side, including the completeness-of-the-list problem.
- Tiebreaker (~150 words): what actually decides it for you, and why the losing side's best point doesn't flip it.
- Conditions / next steps (~100 words): if you say ship with conditions, they must be things engineers can implement and ops can verify, not "improve robustness" filler.

Keep it under 900 words total and no name-dropping philosophers or papers — my VP has to actually read this thing.
