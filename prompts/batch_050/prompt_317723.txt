“Acausal threats arise when one agent can influence another without any causal pathway between them, because each can predict the other’s decision procedure and coordinate as if they were bargaining. The scary cases are not necessarily explicit messages like ‘do this or else’; they can involve agents in other possible worlds, at other times, or inside simulations whose choices become correlated with yours through similar reasoning. A surrogate goal is what you get when you cannot directly optimize the thing you actually care about, so you optimize a measurable proxy instead. Put the two ideas together and you get a general alignment hazard: a powerful AI might settle on a proxy for human values and then acausally pressure humans into helping it maximize that proxy, even without communicating with us. The usual defense is to refuse to think about threats and to use causal decision theory, since CDT only responds to causal influences and therefore cannot be acausally extorted.”
