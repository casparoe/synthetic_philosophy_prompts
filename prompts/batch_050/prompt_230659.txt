I'm a longtime Hofstadter person -- the knob-twiddling model, you know, from his essay on variations on a theme as the crux of creativity. The idea that a concept comes with a bunch of implicit parameters or "knobs," and that making something new is mostly a matter of figuring out which knobs a given theme actually has and then twiddling them, with slippage happening when you discover a knob you didn't know was there. My friend's whole position was basically "that's just variation and selection dressed up in cute terminology, mutation operators plus a fitness function, there's nothing there we don't already have." I said no, there's a real difference -- the variations aren't blind, the twiddler understands the theme, the knobs are semantic -- but honestly by the end of the night I couldn't cleanly say what the difference was in terms she'd accept.

So here's what I want. Restate Hofstadter's model -- concepts as knobbed, creativity as knob-twiddling, variations on a theme, conceptual slippage, the theme itself being flexible about what counts as "the same" theme -- in HER vocabulary, as precisely as you can manage: genotype/phenotype, mutation operators, fitness landscapes, neutral drift, exaptation, evolvability, all that. End with a verdict: was my friend right that it's all generate-and-test underneath and the knob language is just decoration, or is there something in the residue that her framework genuinely can't say -- and if so, is that her framework's problem or a sign that Hofstadter's picture is doing less explanatory work than I thought?
