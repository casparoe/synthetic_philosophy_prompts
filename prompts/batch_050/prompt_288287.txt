I am a third-year undergraduate taking a philosophy of mind module, and this term we have been studying the debate between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett over whether dreams are experiences had during sleep. For a long-form essay I want to push the debate past the point where the published exchange left it, and I would like your help thinking through a scenario that neither author properly confronted.

As I understand the positions: Malcolm argued that the concept of a dream is essentially tied to the waking report, that there is and could be no independent criterion for what happened during sleep, that a sleeping person is not in a position to make genuine judgements or hold beliefs, and hence that it is confused to think of a dream as a sequence of conscious experiences occurring while we sleep. Dennett rejected dreams-as-experiences too, but on quite different grounds: he held that dream content may be composed or 'loaded' at or around the moment of waking (his cassette-style account), so that the experienced narrative need not have unfolded in real time during sleep at all.

Now consider two kinds of experimental work that postdate this debate. First, studies of lucid dreamers in which subjects agree before sleeping to move their eyes in a pre-arranged left-right pattern once they become lucid; the characteristic eye movements are then recorded during REM sleep, and the subject's subsequent report matches the timing of the signal. Second, more recent experiments in which researchers present questions or stimuli to sleeping subjects — spoken words, simple arithmetic, flashes of light — and the subjects answer during sleep with coded eye or facial movements, again matching their later reports. Some subjects even report incorporating the presented stimuli into their ongoing dream.

Please write an essay of roughly 800–1,000 words addressing the following:

1. How would Malcolm respond to these experiments? In particular, does real-time signalling from a sleeping subject supply the independent criterion he claimed was impossible, or can he consistently deny that the eye movements are genuine judgements? Identify the strongest version of his reply, not merely the most obvious one.
2. How would Dennett respond? Does two-way communication during sleep undermine the idea that dream content is assembled retrospectively at waking, or can a composition-at-waking theorist absorb real-time signalling? Consider whether his position can handle cases where the dream reportedly incorporates an externally presented stimulus.
3. Whose framework survives the experimental challenge better, and why? If you conclude that both can absorb it, explain whether that shows the original dispute was not really empirical after all.

Please avoid simply restating the two positions; I already know them and will lose marks for summary. The essay should be written in British English. Conclude with a final line of the form \answer{Malcolm}, \answer{Dennett}, or \answer{Neither}, indicating whose position you judge to accommodate the lucid-dreaming evidence better, followed by one sentence stating the decisive consideration.
