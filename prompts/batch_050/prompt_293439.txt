Is there a name for this? In the preface to Ecce Homo Nietzsche writes: "I am a disciple of the philosopher Dionysus, and I would prefer to be even a satyr than a saint." Then later in that same book he spends a whole chapter insisting that questions of nutrition, climate, digestion and recreation matter more for philosophy than everything people had previously treated as important -- he even partly blames German intellectual heaviness on German cooking.

I keep noticing the general move here and I don't know what to call it. Some instances: a friend insists his pessimistic phase last year was really just chronic bad sleep; a historian on a podcast explained the spread of ascetic ideals in late antiquity as mass malnutrition doing the causal work; Nietzsche treating his own book-writing as basically a function of dry air and giving up coffee. In each case something we'd normally treat as spiritual, intellectual or moral gets redescribed as the output of a bodily condition.

It's clearly not plain materialism -- nobody involved is denying that ideas exist or making a metaphysical claim about what the world is made of. And I don't think it's the genetic fallacy either, because Nietzsche at least doesn't think that showing the bodily origin refutes the ideas; he seems to think knowing the origin is part of understanding them properly. So: does this explanatory move have an established name? If so, how does it differ from reductive explanation in general and from debunking genealogy? And if it doesn't have one, coin a term and give me the edges: does it count if the bodily cause is offered as only a partial explanation? Does it matter whether its a temporary state (bad sleep) or a fixed constitution? Does self-diagnosis count, or only diagnosing other people and whole eras? I'm not after a bare label, I want the carving.
