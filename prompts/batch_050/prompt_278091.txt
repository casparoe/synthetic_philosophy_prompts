In my philosophy of cosmology seminar I defend the view that the Boltzmann brain problem is a legitimate selection principle: any cosmological model according to which observers with experiences like ours are overwhelmingly likely to be isolated thermal fluctuations in a near-equilibrium universe, rather than evolved embodied creatures in a young low-entropy one, should on that ground alone be rejected. My students, reasonably enough, want to know what the view is actually risking, and I would like you to construct the case against my position as honestly as you can. What would have to be true for the selection principle to be toothless, misguided, or self-undermining? Please distinguish at least the following kinds of defeater and rank them from fatal to merely awkward. First, results within cosmology itself: would it suffice to show that in eternally inflating or de Sitter spacetimes the production rate of ordinary observers outruns fluctuated brains indefinitely, or that vacuum decay cuts off the equilibrium regime before fluctuations dominate? How much of the problem evaporates if such results hold only for some measures? Second, conceptual pressure on the argument's machinery: the selection principle leans on a typicality assumption and on a way of counting observers, both of which are contested. If the measure problem is genuinely unsolved, is the principle a real constraint or just a preference dressed up as one? Third, the internal-stability worry in the other direction: does the argument that believing oneself possibly a fluctuation undermines one's evidence for an old universe actually cut against invoking Boltzmann brains as a constraint, and could that line of reasoning be turned against my position? For each defeater, say whether a committed proponent of the selection principle would concede it as damaging or dismiss it as beside the point, and close with a verdict on whether the principle is well insulated against refutation and, if so, whether that insulation is a virtue or a defect in a constraint of this kind.
