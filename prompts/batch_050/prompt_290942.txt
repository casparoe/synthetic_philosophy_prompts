So I'm a journalist covering science and tech ideas, and I'm working on a humor column for our magazine's summer issue. I want to do a piece riffing on the simulation argument, but done properly, not the usual "whoa what if none of this is real man" stuff you see everywhere.

Quick setup in case you're rusty on it: the philosopher Nick Bostrom's famous argument basically says at least one of three things has to be true -- (1) civilizations almost always go extinct before they can run huge numbers of detailed simulations of their ancestors, (2) they reach that capability but choose not to run such simulations (maybe for ethical reasons), or (3) we're almost certainly living inside one, because there'd be vastly more simulated observers than flesh-and-blood ones. There's also related machinery: substrate independence (minds don't need carbon, just the right computation), the distinction between the simulation argument (a trilemma) and the simulation hypothesis (the claim that we're in one), the question of whether the simulators would bother to simulate all the suffering in history, the computing-budget problem, and the fact that "base reality" might itself be simulated, turtles all the way down.

Here's what I want you to write: a parody of an internal corporate memo, maybe 600-700 words, from the engineering team that runs the simulation, responding to an uptick in "user reports" from the inhabitants. Some humans have started to suspect they're simulated, and middle management is annoyed. So the memo addresses stuff like: a FAQ response template for when a simulee notices a glitch, whether they should just quietly nerf the physicists' particle accelerators to save on rendering costs, a cost-benefit analysis of letting one species figure it out versus just rebooting, a reminder that HR said they can't call the inhabitants "NPCs" anymore, and a section on whether publishing the simulation argument inside the simulation counts as a security breach or as free advertising.

The jokes should come from actually getting the philosophy right. E.g., a good bit would play on the fact that running ancestor simulations implies running all the wars and plagues too, and the legal team has questions. Or that the "it's probably base reality" department is one guy. A specialist in this stuff should read it and go "ok they understood the argument," not wince.

A few instructions: you're a professional satirist who moonlights as a philosophy lecturer, so write like it. Think carefully before you start, and give me a short brainstorm of 5-6 joke angles first, pick the strongest ones, and then write the memo. Keep it deadpan -- corporate memo voice throughout, no winking at the reader. No real people as targets, invented middle managers only. And keep the format recognizably memo-ish: subject line, numbered sections, maybe an action items list at the end.
