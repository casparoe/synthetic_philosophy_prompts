I am a novelist, and I have been drafting a scene in which a character begins writing the very novel she inhabits, and I keep catching myself treating her as genuinely reaching my level rather than merely being written doing so. I gather that Hofstadter's notion of tangled hierarchies, and in particular his idea of the inviolate level — illustrated by Escher's Drawing Hands, where each hand draws and is drawn by the other yet a real hand outside the picture holds the pencil — is the cleanest articulation of what I'm stumbling over. Please restate the tangled hierarchy and the inviolate level in the vocabulary I actually work in: diegetic levels, narrators and implied authors, frame-breaking and metalepsis, characters' knowledge versus their maker's knowledge. Then give me the residue report: which parts of Hofstadter's picture translate cleanly into narrative terms, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression in the vocabulary of fiction at all. Finally, tell me whether the residue shows that my craft vocabulary is missing something, or whether fiction's apparent level-violations are merely theatrical performances of a hierarchy that is never actually crossed — and what that verdict should mean for how I write the scene.
