I recently joined the AI deployment review board of a regional hospital network, and at next week's meeting I have to cast a vote on a proposal I keep changing my mind about. The board has no written charter; its only norm is consistency with its own prior decisions. I want to predict what consistency actually requires of us before the meeting, and I'd like your help. I use "goal-directed" for an agent that optimizes an explicit objective and "approval-directed" for one that takes the actions a designated human overseer would approve of. Here are the four decisions the board has issued in the past two years:

1. Approved a goal-directed agent that schedules sterilization runs for surgical instruments, minimizing autoclave idle time subject to hard safety constraints. The objective is read directly off equipment logs, and any schedule can be overridden the same day.
2. Rejected a goal-directed agent that would have drafted patient-portal messages so as to maximize measured patient response rates, on the grounds that the response rate was a proxy the agent could inflate in ways patients would eventually experience as pressure and manipulation.
3. Approved an approval-directed agent that drafts discharge summaries, each of which must be read and signed by the discharging physician before it enters the record. The physicians in question genuinely evaluate what they sign.
4. Rejected an approval-directed agent that would have proposed nursing-roster changes for a shift manager's sign-off, after the pilot showed managers approving proposals within seconds without examining the fairness trade-offs buried in them. The oversight was nominal, not real.

The new proposal: an approval-directed agent for the emergency department that recommends which patients with head injuries need a CT scan. An attending physician must approve or reject each recommendation, and — I have checked this — the attendings on this service review each case substantively rather than rubber-stamping; they are competent to judge whether a given scan is indicated. But a wrongly skipped scan can mean a missed bleed: the errors, when oversight fails, are severe and irreversible.

Please answer the following, and don't restate the general trade-offs between approval- and goal-directed agency except where they bear directly on these cases:

- What verdict is consistent with the board's precedents, and which prior decision is the closest analog to the new case? Say what makes it closer than the runners-up.
- At least two rules fit the four precedents. Rule A: approve when the agent's behavior is genuinely evaluable by whoever is positioned to stop harm — a checkable objective for a goal-directed system, substantive oversight for an approval-directed one. Rule B: approve when errors are cheap to detect and reverse, whatever the architecture. These diverge on the new case. Identify at least one further rule that also fits all four precedents and say what it predicts here.
- Give your best prediction of the board's verdict, state which rule you take the board to be implicitly running, and point to what in the record supports that reading over the alternatives.
- Describe one further hypothetical proposal, realistic for a hospital, whose verdict would discriminate between the two leading rules.
- Finally, tell me what would change your prediction: which consideration, if I brought it to you, would flip the verdict you attribute to the board, and where in your analysis the reasoning is most likely to break down.
