I’m sketching a nine-month postdoc proposal in philosophy of technology and need help turning a half-formed idea into something fundable. The usual debate about technological determinism and whether artefacts have politcs seems to focus on design and adoption, but my hunch is that maintenance and repair are philosophically more revealing. I want to distinguish four possible “locations” for an artefact’s politics: what designers intend, what an institution selects through procurement, what defaults do in ordinary use, and what later repairs or workarounds change.

Please write the proposal for reviewers who know the area. State a precise central question: is an artefact’s politics a property of the object, of the artefact-plus-institution, or of a temporally extended pattern of use and maintenance? Explain why the familiar determinist, social-shaping, and artefact-politics positions do not already settle that question. Set out a conceptual method using three brief, invented test cases, such as a school attendance app, shared cargo bikes, and library self-checkout machines. Explain why this is better than attempting a historical survey or a general normative theory of technology. Say what a successful result would look like, what would count as the proposed framework failing, and how much can realistically be done in nine months.

Keep it in British English and don’t provide a bibliography or invented citations; I’ll add references myself. Also, be willing to push back: if my four-way distinction collapses, assumes that artefacts can literally possess political properties, or makes the project impossible to answer, say that plainly rather than politely building the proposal around a bad premise.
