So I'm a novelist, mostly literary fiction, but the book I'm drafting now has a speculative spine to it and I've run into a conceptual problem I can't find a word for. The setup: a character has his brain cells swapped out one at a time for synthetic substitutes over the course of a few years, each replacement doing exactly the job the old cell did, and he never notices anything -- keeps talking through the whole procedure, same memories, same bad jokes. Fine. But then I added a wrinkle that's made the book much stranger: the clinic doesn't destroy the removed cells, it keeps them alive in a tank, and at first they're still wired back to his body through a signal link, which then gets throttled down over months until it's nothing. What I've noticed in writing this, and in how early readers react, is something I find genuinely weird: when the tank gets destroyed in a fire early in the draft, everyone feels he survived the procedure just fine; when the tank lives on, the same readers get uneasy and say he "died somewhere in there" -- even though nothing whatsoever happened differently to the man walking around. So the phenomenon is that whether a person counts as having survived a gradual process seems to hinge entirely on the fate of the parts that were taken out, not on anything happening in the continuing person himself. Does this have a name in philosophy? I know the ship of Theseus business but that's about planks and spare parts and feels like a cousin at most. If what I'm describing fits some existing concept only roughly, tell me the nearest ones, how my case differs from each, and which fits best; if nothing fits, coin a term, define it, and give me some boundary cases so I know where the edges are. Don't just hand me a label though, I want the carving. And write it as continuous prose please -- no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just like an email from a friend.
