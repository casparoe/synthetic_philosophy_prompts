But when you split patients into mild injuries and severe injuries, Clinic B has a higher recovery rate in *both* groups -- it just treats way more severe cases, which drags its overall number down.

Marta says: obviously go to B, it beats A for every kind of patient, the overall number is just an artifact of who walks in the door. Dave says: hold on, that only follows if the mild/severe split is real and measured the same way at both clinics. If Clinic B just calls borderline patients "severe" more often, its within-group rates get inflated and the reversal is fake. Marta says fine, but I can get my own injury graded independently, so that doesn't apply to me -- once I know which group I'm actually in, B's number is the relevant one, full stop. Dave says she's still missing something: the overall rate is the only number that describes what actually happens to patients as a whole, and if disaggregating always trumped aggregating you could keep slicing by more variables (age, weight, whatever) until the comparison says anything you want, so at some point you have to defend *why* severity is the right variable to condition on rather than just the one that flips the answer.
