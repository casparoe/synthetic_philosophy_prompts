I recently began keeping a small apiary, and it has pushed me into reading about insect minds. A popular science book I picked up glosses what it calls the scale argument as follows: Even if the chance that any individual insect is sentient is small — one percent, say — insects are so numerous that the expected number of sentient insects vastly exceeds the entire vertebrate population. Anyone who takes animal suffering seriously is therefore committed to treating insect welfare as one of the most urgent moral problems, and those who resist this conclusion are effectively betting that insects are certainly not sentient. The book is by a reputable science writer, but that last inference sits oddly with me: it seems one could grant insects a real chance of sentience and still resist the urgency claim. Is this a fair summary of the argument? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what the argument's own proponents would object to — and say whether the priority conclusion survives once the summary is corrected.
