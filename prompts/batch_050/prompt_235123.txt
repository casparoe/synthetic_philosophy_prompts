I'm a retired condensed matter physicist and since hanging up my boots I've gotten into philosophy of mind in a big way. I just worked my way through Hofstadter, mainly the stange loop book plus bits of the earlier stuff. As I read him, the self isn't a thing sitting in the skull, it's a pattern, a self-referential loop, and because patterns can in principle be instantiated at some fidelity in more than one substrate, a person who dies carries on in a diluted way in the brains of the people who knew them well. He seems to mean this as literal survival, not as a nice metaphor to wheel out at funerals.

Here's what I want you to do. Restate this whole position in the vocabulary I actually think in: information theory and statistical mechanics. Lossy compression, mutual information between two systems, coarse-graining, channel capacity, fidelity of a model to the thing it models. I spent thirty years in that world, so don't dumb it down. Concretely: what exactly is being claimed when we say the 'pattern' of person A is instantiated in brain B? Is it a claim about mutual information between A's brain at some time and B's brain now? What fidelity threshold, if any, makes it count as A surviving rather than just a good description of A? Translate the claim as charitably and precisely as the vocabulary permits.

Then the part I care about most: the residue report. Which pieces of the position translate cleanly -- my hunch is that the 'distributed across brains' bit survives fine as a statement about information -- which pieces you had to bend to make fit, and which pieces have no expression at all in the physics vocabulary. Then tell me what to make of that residue. Does it show my framework is missing something, e.g. that whatever makes it *my* loop rather than a copy of my loop has no third-person expression, full stop? Or does it show that the untranslatable parts of Hofstadter's view were doing emotional work rather than philosophical work all along?

One constraint: keep the translation honest. If 'being the same pattern' can't be cashed out in bits and nats, say so plainly -- don't quietly swap in some vaguer notion of pattern and then call the result a translation.
