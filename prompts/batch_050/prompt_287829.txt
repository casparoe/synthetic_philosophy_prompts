Write a short story based on this thing I've been mulling over for weeks and can't stop thinking about. I don't have any philosophy background, I just noticed something weird about how groups make decisions and I want to see it play out with actual people.

Here's the setup that bugs me: say a committee has to decide something that depends on a few separate questions. Like a town council deciding whether to build a bridge. It only makes sense to build it if (a) the river crossing is actually needed, (b) the town can afford it, and (c) if both of those hold, THEN build it. Now imagine every single council member is totally reasonable and consistent in their own head — some think it's needed but unaffordable, some think it's affordable but not needed, some think both. A majority says yes to (a), a majority says yes to (b)... but a majority also says NO to building the bridge, because nobody specific believes all the pieces at once, or only a minority does. So the group "believes" all the premises but rejects the conclusion. The group as a whole is irrational even though nobody in it is.

Write a short story where this actually happens to people and matters. Some things I'd love to see:

- Characters who realize mid-meeting that the outcome depends on HOW you vote — like voting on each question separately vs. voting on the final decision directly gives different answers — and one of them figures out they can rig things just by choosing the procedure, which is honestly kind of sinister
- The emotional side of it. Everyone did their homework, everyone voted honestly, and the result still feels like a betrayal. Someone should feel cheated without being able to point at a cheater.
- Don't explain the puzzle in essay-speak. Show it through the meeting going wrong. I want to feel the moment where the room realizes the group has a "mind" of its own and that mind is broken.

Maybe set it somewhere the stakes are personal — a housing co-op board, a jury-ish panel, a family business, whatever works. And if you can, end on the uncomfortable thought that there might be no fair way to fix it, that every voting method just moves the weirdness somewhere else. But if the story pulls you somewhere more hopeful, thats fine too.
