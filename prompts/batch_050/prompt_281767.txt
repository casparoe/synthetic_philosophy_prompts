So I have zero philosophy training, I got into this through a podcast and then fell down a rabbit hole. As I understand it, Hume's point was that we have no non-circular reason to think the future will resemble the past -- you can't prove it deductively, and proving it inductively is beging the question. Fine. Then I read about the replies: some people say demanding a justification of induction itself is confused because reasoning inductively is partly constitutive of what "rational" even means, some (Reichenbach I think?) say look, if ANY method works then induction will, so it's our best bet even if unproven, and the Popper crowd just bites the bullet and says science never used induction anyway. And then Goodman shows up and says everyone was worrying about the wrong thing, the real problem is saying which generalizations are legit (green emeralds vs grue ones), which as far as I can tell is ALSO still unresolved.

So both riddles are decades or centuries old, everyone knows every move, and nothing has budged an inch. My hunch from the cheap seats is that the Hume debate stalled because it's secretly a disagreement about what a justification even is -- one side won't accept anything circular, the other thinks circularity at bedrock is unavoidable and not even bad, and neither can defend their standard without assuming it. And here's my maybe-dumb observation about the Goodman one: when actual scientists argue about predictions, nobody ever brings up Hume, but they constantly fight about grue-shaped things -- which features of the data are the real pattern and which are artifacts. I work adjacent to some ML people and this is literally the overfitting problem. So maybe Goodman's riddle is actually an empirical/engineering question wearing an a priori costume, and philosophers are stuck because they're using the wrong tools on it?

Am I on to something or way off base? What kind of disagreement is each of these really, and what would count as progress on either -- a theorem, a distinction, an experiment -- or should I just accept that some questions are permanent? I don't want a rundown of all the proposed solutions, I can read wikipedia for that. I want the diagnosis.
