Construct a thought experiment that isolates Keynes's notion of the weight of argument from probability itself: a single decision problem in which two options carry the same best-estimate probability of success, a rational agent should on Keynes's view strictly prefer one of them, and yet the difference between the options cannot be redescribed as a difference in the probabilities, as ordinary risk aversion, or as a difference in stakes. Constraints: the setting must be realistic (hiring, investing, or clinical, for example) rather than an urn with an unknown composition or any Ellsberg-style setup; the scenario itself should be one short paragraph; and the two probability estimates must rest on the same kind of evidence-based judgment, so the contrast is not simply a statistic versus a hunch. After the scenario, do the analysis: first, state what a precise Bayesian says the agent should do and why the case leaves the Bayesian indifferent; second, state Keynes's verdict and identify the feature of the case that grounds it; third, explain the Bayesian's strongest reply -- that weight collapses into higher-order uncertainty or into the value of gathering further evidence -- and say whether that reply genuinely dissolves the case or quietly concedes Keynes's point. Begin with the scenario itself, then the analysis, with no preface and no restating of these instructions.
