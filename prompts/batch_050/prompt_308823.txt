I'm a retired chemist and over the last year or so I've been reading my way into philosophy of religion, mostly because an old grad school friend keeps dragging me to his reading group. Anyway, I've recently talked myself into accepting Molinism -- the idea that God has "middle knowledge," meaning he knows, prior to creating anything, what every possible free creature would freely do in every possible circumstance, and that this is how you square divine foreknowledge and providence with genuine libertarian freedom. It appeals to me because it feels like the only option that doesn't either flatten human freedom into determinism or make God into someone who just reacts to us like the rest of us do.

But having spent forty years in science, I'm suspiscious of any view I can't imagine being wrong. So here's my question: what would actually count against middle knowledge? I know the standard grounding objection -- that counterfactuals of creaturely freedom have nothing to make them true before the creatures exist -- but I'd like more than the greatest hits. Which specific intuitions, thought experiments, or commitments elsewhere in metaphysics (say, about counterfactuals generally, or about truthmakers, or about how knowledge works) does Molinism stand or fall with? Is there a possible result in the free will debate itself -- like libertarian freedom turning out to be incoherent, or compatibilism winning decisively -- that would make middle knowledge pointless or false rather than just unnecessary? And I'd be interested in whether the view has been hedged into something unfalsifiable: Molinists seem to have a response to everything, and I can't tell if that's because the view is right or because it's built to absorb any blow.

Please distinguish what committed Molinists themselves would concede as counting against their view from what only its critics would treat as a defeater, and rank the threats from genuinely fatal down to merely awkward. If your honest verdict is that middle knowledge risks nothing and can never be dislodged, say so and tell me whether that's a problem for a view of this kind or just par for the course in metaphysics. One request on format: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables -- just continuous prose, the way you'd write a long email to a friend. I read these things on my phone and all that structure drives me nuts.
