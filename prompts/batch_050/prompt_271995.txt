I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and last month I sat in on a workshop on formal methods for AI safety, where two researchers I was profiling got into a long argument over dinner that never got resolved. I'm writing it up and want a neutral referee before I put anything in the piece. Here's the exchange as best I reconstructed it from my notes. Round 1: Maya said the Löbian obstacle shows, basically, that a fully formal agent can never rigorously justify trusting a successor built on the same logical foundations -- because for the parent to trust the child it would need to prove that the child's proof system is sound, and Löb's theorem plus Gôdel's second tell you a sufficiently strong consistent system can't establish that kind of thing about itself. So any self-improving system has to either weaken itself each generation or trust its successor on non-deductive grounds. Round 2: Dan said she's proving too much -- humans trust their own future reasoning all the time without anything like a soundness proof, and no mathematician refuses to use PA because PA can't prove Con(PA). If a limit applies to every possible reasoner and everyone just lives with it, calling it an "obstacle" rather than a fact of life seems like a framing choice. Round 3: Maya said living with it is exactly what you can't do if your whole safety case rests on a proof that the successor preserves the original goals -- the one place you actually need the proof is the one place it's blocked, and "everyone lives with it" just means everyone's safety argument has a hole at the self-reference step. Round 4: Dan brought up the trick where each generation reasons in a slightly weaker theory than the one before, so trust goes down a descending chain, and said this shows the obstacle is escapable in practice. Maya said a chain of ever-weaker successors means the thing you're building in the limit can't do the reasoning you started with, so you've solved the problem by giving up on genuine self-trust.

Can you adjudicate this move by move -- say who's right about what at each of the four rounds, whether any of the apparent disagreement is really just about what standard of justification counts (proof vs. something weaker), and whether Dan's "fact of life" framing actually answers Maya's point about safety cases or just changes the subject? Also tell me honestly if I've misstated the Löbian obstacle itself anywhere in my reconstruction, since I'm a generalist and this is near the edge of what I can check.
