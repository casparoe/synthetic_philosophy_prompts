I co-host a philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is an epistemologist whose new book revives Nozick's tracking account of knowledge. She defends the sensitivity condition — roughly, you know that p only if, had p been false, you wouldn't have believed p — and she embraces its famous consequence: you can know you have hands without knowing you're not a handless brain in a vat. Draft eight to ten interview questions that push well past a restatement of the view. I want at least one question each on: the charge that rejecting closure licenses embarrassing combinations of claims (knowing you have hands, knowing that having hands entails not being a handless brain in a vat, yet not knowing the latter); how she individuates the "method" by which a belief is formed, given that sensitivity is method-relative and a coarse enough individuation can rescue almost any belief; the grandmother case — a grandmother who seems to know her grandson is well from seeing him, even though if he were seriously ill the family would conceal it and she would believe him well anyway; and what the sensitivity condition does with knowledge of necessary truths, where there is no closest world in which the proposition is false. For each question, sketch the reply she is most likely to give and supply a follow-up that makes that reply harder to sustain. End the sequence with a question about what result would make her abandon the account.
