I am preparing a one-page handout for an advanced undergraduate criminal-law seminar on responsibility without fault. Please construct a decision tree, rather than an essay, that sorts the main philosophical positions on when strict criminal liability can be justified.

For this purpose, strict liability means that the prosecution need not prove culpable awareness for at least one material element of the offense. Build the tree from the underlying logical space rather than merely listing familiar labels. Begin with the broadest issue that divides the positions—such as whether criminal blame ever requires personal fault—and proceed through no more than ten diagnostic questions. Each question should be a concrete case or a proposition that a reader could assess without already knowing the terminology. Do not ask questions such as whether one is a retributivist or consequentialist. Label every branch with the distinction that generates it, and distinguish strictness about circumstances from strictness about results.

Use at least the following cases somewhere in the tree: a careful driver whose regularly inspected car kills a pedestrian because of an undetectable manufacturing defect; a pharmacist who follows every accepted protocol but dispenses medicine secretly mislabeled by the manufacturer; a shopkeeper who sells a restricted product after checking a nearly perfect forged identity document; and a ship captain who reasonably follows an established route without knowing that an uncommunicated safety rule has just changed.

The leaves must include, under descriptive names: the view that culpability is required for every material element; the view that reasonable-care negligence supplies sufficient fault; a role-based view under which voluntary entrants into hazardous regulated activities may bear strict liability for resulting harm; an outcome-responsibility view under which culpable risk-taking makes one answerable for chance outcomes; a view permitting strict fines but not imprisonment or criminal stigma; and a hybrid view allowing strict liability only when the defendant may avoid conviction by proving due care. Add other leaves if the tree reveals an important position omitted from this list.

At each leaf, state which of the four cases produce liability and whether any resulting sanction may include imprisonment. Conclude with a short logical-space audit: identify answer combinations that lead to no recognizable position and briefly diagnose whether each is incoherent, morally unattractive, or simply underexplored. Flag any required leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate rather than forcing an artificial distinction.
