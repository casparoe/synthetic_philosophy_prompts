I work on model evaluations at an AI lab, so my whole world is capabilities elicitation, held-out test sets, adversarial inputs, distribution shift, deployment vs. sandbox, that kind of thing. I've been reading about the philosophy of dispositions -- fragility, solubility, and the mess around "finks" and "masks" -- and I have a hunch it's secretly about my job, but I want that checked rather than assumed.

So: restate the dispositions debate in the vocabulary of model evaluations, as faithfully as you can. The setup as I understand it: a glass is fragile even when never struck; the standard analysis says fragility is roughly "would break if struck"; then come the counterexamples. Finks: the electro-fink case, where a wire is live but connected to a device that would instantly cut the current the moment the wire were touched, so the conditional "if touched, current flows" comes out false even though the wire is genuinely live. Masks: the fragile glass wrapped in bubble wrap doesn't break when struck, but nobody wants to say it stopped being fragile. Also mimics, where something lacks the disposition but fakes having it under the stimulus conditions.

Translate all of this: the simple conditional analysis of dispositions, the fink counterexample, the mask counterexample, the mimic, and the moves philosophers make in response (intrinsic basis of the disposition, "under normal conditions" clauses, giving up on the conditional analysis entirely) -- into eval-speak. Test conditions, scaffolding, elicitation techniques, sandbagging, capability vs. propensity, monitors that change behavior when active. Be precise about what the mapping is: e.g. is a masked disposition like a capable model behind a crippled scaffold, or is that already a bad translation?

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which pieces map cleanly? Which did you have to fudge -- and where exactly does the fudge happen? And is there anything in the philosophers' debate that has NO counterpart in eval vocabulary? Tell me which residue items point to a hole in my framework and which point to a hole in theirs.
