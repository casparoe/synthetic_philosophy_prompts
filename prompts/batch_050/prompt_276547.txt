So I don't have any philosophy training or anything, but over the past year I've gotten weirdly obsessed with the question of what all pleasures actually have in common, and I've ended up with a little pile of opinions that I suspect don't all fit together. Before I go inflicting these on my brother-in-law at christmas, can you audit them for me?

Here's the pile:

1. Pleasure is the only thing that's good in itself. Everything else (money, knowledge, health, whatever) is good because it leads to pleasure or removes pain.

2. Pleasures don't share any common feeling. A hot bath, laughing at a dumb joke, finally understanding something, the taste of coffee -- if I introspect honestly, there's no single "pleasure glow" running through all of these. They're just totally different experiences.

3. What makes them all *pleasures* anyway is that they share an attitude instead of a feeling: with each of them, while it's happening, I want it to keep going, and afterwards I'd seek it out again. That's the thread, not some sensation.

4. Mill was right that some pleasures are just better than others *as pleasures*, not because of their consequences. The bit that convinced me is from chapter 2 of Utilitarianism, where he writes: "Human beings have faculties more elevated than the animal appetites, and when once made conscious of them, do not regard anything as happiness which does not include their gratification." I take him to be saying that once you've experienced the so-called higher stuff you won't trade it away, and that settles it.

5. Even so, how good a pleasure is comes down to how *much* pleasure it is -- intensity times duration, roughly. In principle you could measure it and make trade-offs, like deciding a shorter intense pleasure beats a longer mild one.

Some of these feel fine together and some feel like they're quietly fighting, but I honestly can't tell which is which, and I'm not attached to any particular one surviving. A few things that bug me: does #4 even make sense given #5? Does Mill's reason in that quote (it's about *faculties*, not about the pleasure itself) actually fit with #1? And does #3 secretly cause problems somewhere I haven't noticed?
