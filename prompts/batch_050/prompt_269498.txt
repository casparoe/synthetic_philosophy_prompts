I'm presenting at a reading group on standing to blame next week and before I embarrass myself I wanna check that my position actually hangs together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Standing is real and forfeitable: "who are you to blame me?" is sometimes a legitimate, blame-deflating response and not just a rhetorical dodge, and hypocrisy is the paradigm way to loose it.

(2) What does the work isn't the hypocrite's failure itself but their attitude toward the norm: someone who sincerely accepts the norm, applies it to themselves, and just keeps failing through weakness still has standing. Acceptance matters, compliance doesn't.

(3) Whether the blame is fitting and whether the blamer has standing are separate questions: a hypocrite's blame can be dead accurate — the blamee really did wrong and really deserves resentment — even if the hypocrite isn't the one licenced to deliver it.

(4) A chain-smoking parent who sincerely thinks smoking is terrible, hates their own habit, and applies the norm to themselves can properly blame their teenager for smoking. "You smoke yourself!" doesn't land against them the way it would against a parent who thinks the rule is just for kids.

(5) Standing is something you earn through how you've comported yourself: the ex-smoker of ten years can blame a current smoker without pushback precisely because their record secured the position, and that's what makes the weak-willed blamer so galling — they hold others to a standard they haven't earned the right to enforce.

Which of these genuinely conflict and which only look like they do? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say which commitment would have to give and what that costs. If more than one fix works, tell me which single revision cleans up the most.
