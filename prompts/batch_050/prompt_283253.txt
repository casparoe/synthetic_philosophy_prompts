For a few years now, without any formal training, I have held the view that whole-brain death is real death: when a person's brain has irreversibly stopped functioning, the person is gone, even if a ventilator keeps the heart beating and the tissues warm for weeks. I got here by trying to give one principled answer to two cases rather than two ad hoc ones: the ventilated body in intensive care, and the body whose heart has stopped for good. My own working formulation is that a human being dies when the body stops doing anything as a whole -- when whatever is left is a collection of living parts rather than an organism doing something. On that test, the brain-dead body on a ventilator fails: the heartbeat, the wound healing, the temperature regulation are the parts working, not a someone. But I know there are famous cases of bodies declared brain dead that were maintained for years, going through puberty, gestating pregnancies, and those cases sit badly with me in a way I have not resolved.

So tell me what would count against my position. Which observations, cases, or arguments should actually move me, and which are merely uncomfortable? Be specific about the pressure points: the long-maintained bodies, the fact that hearts stopped for half an hour are sometimes restarted (which seems to make circulatory death a judgment about what won't resume rather than what can't -- and to invite the same move for brains we cannot yet restore), and the possibility that my "organism as a whole" test quietly does the moral work while pretending to be biology. I also want to know what defenders of the neurological criterion themselves concede counts against it, as opposed to what only its critics say. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and end with an honest verdict on whether my position genuinely risks anything, or whether it has been insulated by redefinition -- and if the latter, whether that is a defect in a criterion that is supposed to track a fact about the world rather than settle a legal formality.
