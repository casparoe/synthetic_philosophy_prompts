I'm a backend eng, no phil training, trying to pin down when my present self should defer to future me's confidence, basically that Reflection idea: if now I'm sure tomorrow I'll be at credence r in P, should I be at r now? My gut verdicts are all over the place and I want the princple behind them.

Cases and guts:
- Canary deploy: before metrics I'm 60% the hotfix works. I know at 5 either green dashboard -> I'll be 90%, red -> I'll be 20%, and my current P(green) makes the weighted average 60. Defer: yes, obvious, anything else feels like bookable against myself.
- Same setup except an oracle tells me only: tomorrow at 5 you will be 80% the hotfix works, not which dashboard. I trust tomorrow-me has seen the real metrics. Defer: yes, jump to 80 now, even though it feels weird to update on a number with no evidence attached.
- Incident review bias: after a painful outage, future me always overestimates that the last change caused it, because blame gets sticky in retros. I know tomorrow I'll be 90% change X caused it, partly from evidence, partly from blame inertia. Defer: no, present me should not pre-import a known bias, even if future me is sincere and maybe has more facts.
- Selective screenshot: my manager will show me only passing test runs tomorrow, and I know this. After that I'll be 95% the flaky suite is fixed. Defer: no. But is this just the oracle case with a corrupt process, or is the difference that here I know the selection rule?
- Memory wipe: tonight a script will erase whether I ran the migration and leave a note saying I definitely ran it. Tomorrow I'll be near certain I ran it. Right now I know the note is unreliable. Defer: no. Weird one: future me has more apparent certainty but less total information? Or different information?
- Self-sealing belief: if I now adopt 70% that users will love the redesign, then tomorrow, because I've publicly committed and sunk a sprint into it, I'll be 90% regardless of analytics. If I now adopt 30%, tomorrow I'll be 40%. Defer: no to both, I think? But also current credence seems to causally set future credence, which breaks the usual setup.
- Prediction market clone: I can spin up a sim of me that sees extra noisy logs; in 99 of 100 runs its posterior in P lands in [0.6,0.7], average .65, and I can't inspect which run I'm in. Defer: mostly yes, set now to .65, even though no single future me is guaranteed .65. This feels like expected future credence matters, not certainty about one future credence.
- Adversarial future evidence: tomorrow I'll receive evidence E that is genuinely accurate but was chosen by an opponent to push me below .2 if P is true and above .8 if P is false, so my posterior will track truth anyway? I think defer still yes, which clashes with selective screenshot in my head and I can't say why.

What single principle, or least ugly cluster of clauses, gets these? Say which clause each case motivates, what it predicts for cases I didn't list like known upcoming forgetting of the evidence for P but not the conclusion, a future me that is smarter but morally motivated, and a coin flip deciding which of two differently biased future mes gets awakened. And be honest about which gut verdict you'd sacrifice if the simple version can't keep both the canary/oracle deference and the screenshot/memory-wipe refusal.
