I am preparing a one-semester graduate research project on the dispute between liberalism and communitarianism, and I need a polished two-page research proposal of roughly 1,000 words. The project should begin from the following passage in Aristotle’s Politics, Book I, Part II, which does not count toward the length:

“When several villages are united in a single complete community, large enough to be nearly or quite self-sufficing, the state comes into existence, originating in the bare needs of life, and continuing in existence for the sake of a good life. And therefore, if the earlier forms of society are natural, so is the state, for it is the end of them, and the nature of a thing is its end. For what each thing is when fully developed, we call its nature, whether we are speaking of a man, a horse, or a family. Besides, the final cause and end of a thing is the best, and to be self-sufficing is the end and the best.”

My half-formed idea is that liberals and communitarians often treat Aristotle’s “good life” as though it must mean a comprehensive conception of human flourishing. I want to develop and test an alternative I call threshold perfectionism: the view that a state may deliberately cultivate a limited set of civic capacities—such as political judgment, mutual trust, practical deliberation, and the ability to understand citizens with different conceptions of the good—because these capacities are partly constitutive of membership in a political community, without thereby promoting one controversial ideal of life as a whole.

The proposal should ask whether threshold perfectionism is a coherent middle position between liberal neutrality and communitarian perfectionism, or whether it collapses into one of them. It should explain why Aristotle’s distinction between originating in “the bare needs of life” and continuing “for the sake of a good life” makes the question difficult: the passage may suggest that survival explains why states arise but not what justifies their continued authority. It should also distinguish Aristotle’s teleological claim about the purpose of the state from the modern communitarian claim that individual identity is socially constituted; the proposal should not assume that accepting one commits us to the other.

Include:

1. A precise statement of the central question, formulated so that the project can reach a determinate answer rather than merely survey the liberal-communitarian debate.
2. An explanation of why the usual liberal and communitarian positions leave the question unresolved, including at least one serious objection to each.
3. A more exact definition of threshold perfectionism, together with criteria for distinguishing a civic capacity from a controversial conception of the good life. The definition should be tested against difficult cases such as compulsory civic education, public funding for the arts, religious exemptions, and state promotion of national historical memory.
4. An account of the method: close analysis of the quoted passage, conceptual distinction-making, and evaluation of the proposed position through objections and borderline cases. Explain why this method is preferable to a broad historical survey of liberal and communitarian thought.
5. A plausible working hypothesis, but also the principal alternative conclusions the project might reach and the considerations that would decide among them.
6. A realistic semester plan, identifying the main stages of the work.
7. A statement of what would count as failure—for example, discovering that the supposedly limited civic capacities cannot be specified without presupposing a comprehensive conception of flourishing.
8. A brief explanation of the project’s significance for contemporary political disagreements, especially disputes over whether public institutions may cultivate civic virtues without privileging particular cultures or ways of life.

Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or formal citations. The proposal may refer to liberal neutralism, communitarianism, and civic republicanism in general terms; I will add any scholarly references myself.
