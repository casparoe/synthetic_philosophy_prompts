I'm putting together a short paper for my epistemology class on testimony and want a consistency check before I commit to anything. My anchor is Hume -- in the Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding (Section X, "Of Miracles") he says that "our assurance in any argument of this kind is derived from no other principle than our observation of the veracity of human testimony, and of the usual conformity of facts to the reports of witnesses." I basically endorse that. Here's my full list:

(a) Hume is right about testimony: my justification for believing what people tell me bottoms out in induction from observed conformity between reports and facts. Testimony isn't a basic source of justification on a par with perception.

(b) Little kids, say 3 to 5 years old, genuinely know tons of stuff through testimony -- that the stove is hot, what their grandparents are called, where they live -- even though they haven't yet collected anything like a track record of testimony's reliability. They're justified in these beliefs, not just lucky.

(c) I'm an internalist about justification: whether my belief is justified depends on the reasons and evidence available to me, not on whether the source happens to be reliable out in the world.

(d) I know a lot of history (Rome fell, there was a First World War) entirely through testimony, and I've never personaly done the checking Hume describes -- most of what I take to be my evidence for testimony's general reliability is itself stuff I was told by teachers and books. My historical beliefs are justified anyway.

(e) Memory preserves justification rather than creating it: if I was justified in believing p when I learned it, I stay justified for as long as I remember it, without needing to reproduce the original evidence.

So: does this package hang together? Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. And if there's one single revision that cleans up the most, tell me which it is.
