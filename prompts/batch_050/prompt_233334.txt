For my final-year extended essay I'm writing about strong longtermism — the thesis that, because the far future may contain vastly more people than the present, the most important feature of most of our actions is their expected effect on the long-run trajectory of humanity. My essay can't cover every objection, so my question is really one of prioritization: which open questions about strong longtermism deserve the most space, and in what order should I take them up? The candidates I know of include the problem of cluelessness (can we even estimate the long-run effects of our actions, given that they seem to wash out or flip sign over time), the small-probability-huge-stakes worry (does the view collapse into fanaticism about astronomical stakes), the population-ethics dependence (does the thesis stand or fall with total utilitarianism), and the tractability question (even granting the stakes, can anyone actually steer the far future, or only pretend to). For each, tell me whether it threatens the thesis itself or only particular arguments for it, how much of the other objections it sits upstream of, and whether a student-level treatment can say anything non-superficial about it. I'd rather end up with a defended ranking — including what evidence or arguments would move an item up or down the list — than a survey of all four.
