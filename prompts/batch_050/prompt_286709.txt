had a long argument last night with a friend who's gotten deep into cliodynamics (Turchin, secular cycles, that whole thing). his entire defense of the field rests on one analogy: history is like the weather. nobody faults meteorologists for failing to say where an individual storm will hit six months from now, yet climate models still reliably capture long-run statistical patterns -- so likewise a secular-cycle model can't tell you which leader cracks or which year it blows up, but it can still give real forecasts about instability, elite overproduction, state breakdown, decades out. anyone demanding precise predictions, he says, is making the same mistake as a climate skeptic demanding a forecast for next tuesday.

how far does this actually hold? give me the correspondence table -- what plays the role of the laws of fluid dynamics, of the atmospheric state, of the observation network, of the forecast horizon -- then where the mapping breaks, and which breaks matter for the conclusion. my two suspicions: weather models are fitted against billions of hourly measurements from satellites and stations, while cliodynamics has maybe a few dozen past cycles reconstructed from tax rolls and chronicles; and the atmosphere doesn't read the forecast and change its behavior. does the "it's climate, not weather" defense survive, or was the analogy carrying his whole position? if it collapses, is there a better analogy for what cliodynamics can legitimately claim?
