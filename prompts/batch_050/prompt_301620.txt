A friend hit me with this over beers and I've been turning it over for days because the conclusion is clearly nuts but I can't find the crack. It's about Hofstadter's whole strange-loop picture of the self, roughly as follows:

(1) On the strange-loop view, a self just is a self-referential symbol -- a pattern in a brain that, among other things, represents the very system it is part of, so that the "I" is the loop's way of talking about itself.

(2) A symbol is not like a chair; it isn't a physical object sitting somewhere. It's a pattern of activity, and a pattern of activity exists only while it is actually running. The loop exists only while the self-referential processing that constitutes it is occuring.

(3) In dreamless sleep (or under general anesthesia, or in a coma), there is no self-referential processing going on. The loop is not running.

(4) So during dreamless sleep, your self does not exist. It isn't hidden or suppressed; it's gone, the way a whirlpool is gone when the water stops moving.

(5) Nothing that has literally ceased to exist can come back into existence. What you get instead is a new thing, however similar. (If you freeze a whirlpool and thaw the basin later, you have a new whirlpool, not the old one back.)

(6) So the person who wakes up in your bed tomorrow morning is a numerically different self from the one reading this sentence. "You" die every night and a successor wakes up with your memories.

(7) But that's absurd -- tomorrow-morning guy is you, you have his debts, his plans, his marriage. So something in the setup is wrong, and since (3) is just a fact about brains, presumably the strange-loop theory of selfhood in (1) is what has to go.

Now, I really don't want to give up the strange-loop picture, it's the only account of selfhood that's ever felt to me like it gets at the actual weirdness of being someone rather than just pushing the mystery around. And (5) feels like bedrock metaphysics to me. But something between (2) and (4) smells. Which step do I reject, why did it look so plausible, and what does rejecting it actually commit me to? If the fix is something like "patterns can exist as dispositions even when not running," I want to know whether that move is available to Hofstadter specifically or whether it smuggles back the kind of static soul-substance the whole loop picture was designed to avoid. Also: does the same step do the work in the teleportation case (Star Trek transporter, scan-and-destroy), or does that case break somewhere else? If my friend is right that loop-theorists have to accept nightly death, I'd rather know now.
