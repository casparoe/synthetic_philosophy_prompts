So last night I got into this huge two-hour argument at the bar with my buddy Tyler about sweatshops and neither of us budged, though he definately thinks he won. His take: sweatshops are exploitation, the wages are an insult, end of story. Mine (started as devil's advocate, got increasingly committed): if workers are voluntarily lining up for these jobs because every alternative is worse -- subsistence farming, picking through the dump, an even worse factory down the road -- then both sides benefit, and the activists who get factories boycotted or shut down are the ones actually making workers' lives worse. Tyler kept saying it's still taking unfair advantage even when both sides benefit, and I kept asking "unfair compared to WHAT, exactly?" and honestly he never gave me a real answer.

So here's what I want: an op-ed, 550-650 words, in the voice of a fictional garment-factory owner in a poor country who is genuinely bewildered and a little wounded that anti-sweatshop activists keep calling him the villain. He takes the mutually-beneficial-exploitation argument completely seriously and would like his credit, please: he keeps demanding to know what the activists' counterfactual is, he notes with pride that his wages beat every local alternative, he's indignant at the suggestion that HE should pay some hypothetical "fair wage" when he didn't create the background conditions that make his offer someone's best option, and he draws a very firm moral line between himself and the guy one town over who pays even less.

Ground rules:
- Fully deadpan. He never winks and never admits he's the bad guy, because as far as he can tell, he isn't one.
- The jokes have to come from him applying the actual arguments flawlessly, not from mustache-twirling. When he mentions the dump-pickers he means it as a factual point about the local labor market.
- Don't strawman him either. Give him the strongest version of the case, so that by the end the reader is at least 10% unsure he's wrong. The discomfort should come from where the argument quietly strains -- what "voluntary" means when your other option is starving, who owes what for the background conditions, whether beating the alternatives settles anything -- not from him being dumb.
- Invented name, invented or vague country, no real brands or real people, and don't cite or quote anyone.

Give it a headline and a byline. Mostly I want to see whether Tyler's "it's still exploitation" survives contact with a guy who takes my side as seriously as I did.
