I'm in a reading group on semantic anti-realism and we just spent a whole session on Fitch's knowability paradox, and honestly I left more confused than I arrived. My understanding going in: you start from the seemingly modest principle that every truth is in principle knowable (if p then it's possible that someone knows p), you add some tame-looking assumptions about knowledge (it's factive, it distributes over conjunction), you substitute a clever candidate proposition -- something of the form "p and nobody knows p" -- and out pops the absurd conclusion that every truth is actually known, full stop. Everyone in the group agreed the conclusion has to be blocked, but people were throwing out fixes at random: restrict the principle to truths of certain shapes, switch to intuitionistic logic, say the substitution itself is the problem, say the possibility operator is doing the damage. Nobody could say what would even count as a good fix.

So before we reconvene, I want the specification, not the fixes. Write the desiderata an adequate response to the paradox has to meet to count as solving it rather than dodging it: what it must explain (including, I'd think, why the knowability principle seemed so attractive to verificationists in the first place -- a fix that just abandons it wholesale has to earn that), which parts of the derivation it has to locate the fault in and what it owes us by way of saying that fault isn't ad hoc, what it must do about the particular substituted proposition rather than just banning it by decree, how it should handle iterated or strengthened versions of the trick, and which distinctions it can't collapse (my guess: "knowable" vs "will in fact be known", and "unrestrictedly true" vs "the kind of truth verificationism ever cared about"). For each requirement give the consideration behind it, i.e. what goes wrong for a response that ignores it.

Then the part I care most about: tell me which of these desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, if any, and which single requirement knocks out the most of the standard responses on the market. Take a stand -- I don't want a menu of options ranked as "each has strengths and weaknesses". Commit to which desiderata are genuine and which are just one camp's hobbyhorse, and say which response you'd bet on surviving your own list and why. Table format for the list itself is fine.
