I work as a software engineer and have no formal training in philosophy, but for the past year a friend of mine -- a history PhD student -- has been running what she calls the Preface test on every social phenomenon we argue about, named after the 1859 Preface to Marx's A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy, which she keeps pinned above her desk. The sentence she quotes most is not the famous one about consciousness but this one: "The changes in the economic foundation lead sooner or later to the transformation of the whole immense superstructure." Her method, as I understand it, is to ask of any legal, political, or cultural fact whether the economic base explains it or whether it has to be explained some other way. I have been quietly keeping score, and I would like to figure out what rule she is actually running.

Her verdicts so far: the repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846 -- explained by the base, since industrial capital had outgrown the landed interest; the introduction of compulsory mass schooling in nineteenth-century Prussia and Massachusetts -- explained, because modern industry and armies needed literate, punctual workers; the particular articles of the Code Napoleon -- not explained, because the base explains why France needed a uniform modern property law but not why it got these provisions rather than others; the timing of women's suffrage in Britain and the United States right after the First World War -- explained, because women's mass entry into wage labor had made their exclusion from the franchise untenable; and the rise of Romanticism in European art and poetry after 1800 -- explained, as a consciousness-response to the dislocations of early industrialization. So she is willing to find base-explanations even in high culture, but she does sometimes say no.

Here is the new case I want to spring on her at dinner. The collaboration technology for fully remote software work -- video calls, cloud infrastructure, version control, asynchronous messaging -- has been mature for years, yet since 2023 many large tech companies have imposed return-to-office mandates, and the nine-to-five, five-days-a-week office rhythm has proven remarkably sticky even where it is demonstrably less productive. Please do four things. First, predict her verdict: explained by the base, or not? Second, set out at least two distinct rules that are each consistent with all five verdicts above but diverge on this case -- I can dimly see, for instance, that she might be a technological determinist about the productive forces, or a functionalist about what serves the dominant class, or someone who thinks the base only fixes the broad outlines and long-run direction of the superstructure, per her quoted sentence -- and state what each rule predicts here. Third, give your best guess as to which rule she is running and why, and say whether the quoted passage itself, especially the hedge sooner or later, actually discriminates between the rules or whether her text is less determinate than her track record. Fourth, describe the further case, historical or hypothetical, that would settle which rule she holds.
