I've been reading rationalist blogs and the EA Forum for years, and the unilateralist's curse is one of those ideas I keep reaching for. Here's my situation: I'm part of a donor-and-builder circle of about forty people -- each of us can unilaterally fund or launch projects (research prizes, new orgs, field-building initiatives) without anyone else's sign-off. After a couple of embarrassing launches that looked great to their champion and obviously bad to everyone else in retrospect, I proposed a structural fix, and two friends pushed back hard without quite being able to articulate why. My proposal was to *expand* the circle and to encourage members to trust their own judgment and act on it more readily. My reasoning: the curse, as I understand it, is driven by noisy private estimates, and the canonical remedy for noisy individual estimates is aggregation -- the wisdom of crowds, prediction tournaments, averaging independent guesses at the ox's weight. Errors in estimating a project's value should be roughly symmetric and independent across people, so adding more independent evaluators, each empowered to act when their own assessment clears the bar, ought to wash out the noise and leave the genuinely good projects standing. For the sake of argument, assume everyone involved is sincere, identically motivated, and not launching things out of ego or status-seeking; I want the question engaged at the level of the estimation mechanism, not incentives.

If my proposal is wrong, identify exactly where the inference from "aggregation fixes noisy estimates" to "more unilateral actors fixes the unilateralist's curse" breaks down, and spell out the asymmetry between pooling many estimates into one decision and multiplying the number of people holding unilateral action rights. Then steelman the two hardest follow-ups for your correction: first, whether a fully rational, curse-aware agent could simply shrink their own estimate downward in proportion to the size of the pool, and whether that restores the case for expanding the circle; second, whether there's a number of actors small enough that my aggregation intuition is actually fine. Finally, tell me what remedies would target the actual mechanism (e.g., mandatory pre-launch disclosure, comment periods, betting markets on outcomes, veto structures) and which of them merely re-describe the problem. Please end your response with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing a short label for whether my proposed remedy helps, harms, or is neutral with respect to the curse.
