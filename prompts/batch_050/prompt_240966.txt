I am setting the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate epistemology module and need one 90-minute essay question worth 20 marks. The question must be built around the following case, which students will not have seen: Dr Okafor, a barn inspector with twenty years' experience, drives through a district where, because of a film shoot she knows nothing about, every barn-shaped structure except one is a hollow façade. She happens to stop at the one real barn, gives it her usual expert scrutiny, and forms the belief that it is a barn. The façades would fool a tourist, but she would have identified each one as fake had she examined it. I want the question to be unanswerable by reciting the tripartite analysis and Gettier's original cases: students should have to work out whether this is a Gettier case at all, given that no false lemma or intervening accident features in it, and to test both a safety condition and a sensitivity condition against it, noting that both appear to verdict it as knowledge while the environmental-luck intuition resists. Please provide: (1) the question as it would appear on the paper; (2) a model answer of around 600 words; (3) a marking scheme identifying the five moves a first-class answer must make, the marks each is worth, a one-line note on how candidates typically get each one wrong, and an explicit statement of what separates a first from an upper second. If the case as I have stipulated it does not in fact deliver the verdicts I claim — for instance, if safety fails here once the relevant nearby worlds are properly specified — say so plainly and repair the case rather than writing around the problem. Use British English throughout.
