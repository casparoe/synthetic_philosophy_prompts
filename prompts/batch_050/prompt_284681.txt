Write the FAQ page for Uji, a fictional calendar and scheduling app whose development team has rebuilt the product around Dōgen's doctrine of being-time (uji). About 600 words, deadpan help-center prose, eight to ten questions with answers. The doctrine, as the developers take it to be: time is not a container through which beings pass; each being is itself time. Firewood does not become ash, and yet firewood and ash each have their own before and after. The present time-being is complete and fills all of time, and yet time is also said to flow, from today to tomorrow. Spring does not pass through winter on the way to summer but is itself a time-being, with the whole array of things contained within it. And the self, setting itself out in array, is the whole world. Each answer should take a mundane calendar feature — recurring events, reminders, the snooze button, deadlines, time zones, daylight saving, syncing across devices, deleting an event, the message saying no time slot could be found for a meeting — and resolve the user's question by applying the doctrine literally and consistently, so that the humor comes from the philosophy being taken seriously rather than from Zen-flavored vagueness. A reader who knows the Shōbōgenzō essay should recognize which doctrinal move each answer is making; a reader who doesn't should still find it funny as documentation for an unusable app. No koan-style mysticism, no winking at the reader.
