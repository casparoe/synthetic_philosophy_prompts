I'm taking a course on classical Chinese philosophy this semester, and for the creative assignment I want to write a satirical piece on wu-wei. Act as an experienced satirist who is also genuinely versed in early Daoist thought — someone who finds the paradox of trying not to try intrinsically comic rather than a cheap gotcha. Before writing, briefly brainstorm (in a few sentences) which features of the concept you intend to exaggerate and why those are the honest pressure points; then write the piece itself.

The piece: a glossy, deadpan marketing brochure of about 600 words for a week-long corporate retreat in which a consultancy sells wu-wei to executives as a peak-performance technique. Structure it like a real retreat brochure — welcome note from the facilitator, daily schedule, FAQ, one glowing testimonial, pricing tiers. The humor must arise entirely from the fact that the product defeats itself: scheduled spontaneity blocks, drills for practicing effortlessness, KPIs measuring how naturally attendees act, a certification exam in non-striving, a money-back guarantee voided if the attendee tried too hard to stop trying. Include at least one moment where the brochure's own instructions are impossible to follow by following them, stated with full corporate confidence. A reader who knows the material should recognize the real tensions being touched — cultivation versus forgetting, whether wu-wei can be taught at all, the suspicion that consciously valuing it already corrupts it — without the brochure ever naming them, while a reader who knows nothing of Daoism should still laugh at it as a send-up of the wellness industry. The target is the packaging, not wu-wei itself: the consultants are the fools, not the Daoists. Stay deadpan throughout — no winking, no narrator stepping in to explain the joke, and no concluding paragraph that winks at the reader about what it all means.
