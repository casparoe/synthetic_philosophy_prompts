So my officemate Lena teaches the causation seminar and has this running thing where students pitch her weird cases and she rules, instantly and with total confidence, on whether X "caused" Y. We've been logging her verdicts all semester and now we want to reverse-engineer whatever rule she's actually running. Verdicts so far:

1. A warehouse burns down because two unrelated wiring faults in different walls spark at basically the same moment. Investigators agree either spark alone would have started the fire. Lena: BOTH faults caused the fire. Not "overdetermined, so neither" — both.

2. A regional blackout: the eastern grid operator trips the main line at 9:00 for an emergency repair, cutting power to the whole area. Unbeknownst to him, the western operator had a scheduled shutdown at 9:05 that would have blacked out the exact same area if the line hadn't already been tripped. Lena: the eastern operator caused the blackout; the western operator's planned shutdown did not.

3. A landlord contracts with an irrigation company to winterize the sprinkler lines; the tech skips the visit, the pipes freeze and burst. Lena: the skipped visit caused the damage. Asked about a neighbor who happened to know how to winterize pipes and also didn't do anything, she says the neighbor's inaction caused nothing whatsoever.

4. Back to the warehouse: had the warehouse never been built, no fire, so building it was a but-for condition of the fire. Lena: still not a cause — that's background, not a cause. (She got visibly annoyed at this one.)

New case: a small chemical plant has a reactor cooled by a primary pump that regulations require and the plant was legally obligated to maintain. Years ago the plant also voluntarily installed a backup pump, way beyond what code requires — nobody was under any obligation to have it or to keep it running. Last week both pumps happened to be down at the same time: the primary from neglected maintenance, the backup because of a cracked fitting nobody had looked at in years. Either pump working alone would have prevented it, but with both down the reactor overheated and there was a release. Was the backup pump's failure a cause of the release?

What do you think Lena says, and why?
