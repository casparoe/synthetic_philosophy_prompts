For my comprehensive exams I have to defend a coherent package in epistemology, and before I commit it to paper I want it stress-tested. Here is where I've landed:

(1) Pragmatic encroachment about knowledge: two subjects with exactly the same evidence for p can differ in whether they know p solely because more rides on p for one of them. Accordingly, I take the standard verdicts in the low-stakes and high-stakes bank cases to be correct as stated.

(2) Purism about justification: stakes never change how justified a subject is in believing p. What varies with stakes is only how much justification knowledge demands.

(3) Contextualism about "knows" as the right response to skepticism: ordinary knowledge ascriptions come out true in ordinary contexts, and the skeptic's denials come out true in the skeptic's high-standards context; neither party is simply mistaken.

(4) The knowledge norm of practical reasoning: a subject is positioned to treat p as a premise in deliberation if and only if she knows p.

(5) These four divide the labor rather than compete: encroachment handles stakes cases, contextualism handles skeptical arguments, and the knowledge-action norm is what makes encroachment more than a curiosity.

Tell me whether this package hangs together. For each pair or triple of commitments in genuine tension, state precisely what the tension is, how severe it is, and what exactly would have to give; rank the tensions from most to least serious rather than treating them as equal. Where two commitments only appear to conflict, say so and show why the appearance is misleading; where two of them in fact reinforce each other, I want to know that too, since it affects which revision is cheapest. Conclude with the single change that would do the most to stabilize the package and what it would cost me in lost explanatory power.

Two further requests. First, if the package presupposes a distinction I haven't drawn — for instance, if one of my commitments is ambiguous between a semantic thesis about "knows" and a metaphysical thesis about knowledge itself — flag the ambiguity and assess the package under both disambiguations instead of silently choosing one for me. Second, if you think the audit as I've framed it rests on a confused premise, or that (5) stacks the deck in a way that conceals the real problem, say so plainly. I would rather have the whole project criticized than be politely assured it is coherent.
