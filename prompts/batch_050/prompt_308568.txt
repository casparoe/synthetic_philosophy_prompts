Got into a proper argument with a friend down the pub last night about whether video games count as art, and we got nowhere, so I want to try a different angle on it.

Suppose Kant somehow turned up today and you sat him down with a modern video game. Not a twitchy arcade thing, but one of those story-driven games where the player's choices genuinely shape what happens -- say a game where you're forced to make morally grim decisions yourself and the game withholds any comfortable "the character did it, not me" excuse. My friend reckons Kant would dismiss the lot as mere entertainment, on the grounds that he seemed to separate proper art from stuff that's just agreeable, and games apparently fell on the agreeable side for him.

But I'm not so sure, and here's where I'd like your help. A few specific questions:

1. Kant has that idea about the free play of the imagination and the understanding in aesthetic judgement. Does a game -- where the player is literally playing, making choices within rules -- fit that idea better than a film or a novel does, or does the fact that you're doing something rather than contemplating something rule it out from the start? I feel like his framework cuts both ways here and I can't settle which.

2. Kant seems to want aesthetic judgement to be disinterested. But in a game you're invested: you can win, lose, fail, feel guilty about what you chose. Is that automatically a deal-breaker for him, or is there a way to be the agent of the action and still relate to it aesthetically on his view?

3. The bit I really want answered: my friend said that for Kant, art is something made by a genius for spectators, so a medium where part of the work only comes into existence through what the audience does can't even be a candidate. Is that actually forced by Kant's position, or is it just an accident of what art looked like in the 1790s? If the latter, what would Kant have to say about a work whose artistic content partly consists in the player's own choices?

Please don't just tell me what Kant said about art in general -- I can look that up myself. Reason it through from his actual commitments and tell me where you think he'd land, and where you think he'd have to give something up either way.
