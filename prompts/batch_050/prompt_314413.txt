Construct a thought experiment that pits two of the standard criteria for life against each other: the metabolic criterion (something is alive if it maintains itself as a bounded, far-from-equilibrium system by taking in energy and matter and expelling waste) and the Darwinian criterion (something is alive if it belongs to a lineage that undergoes evolution by natural selection, with heritable variation in fitness). The experiment should feature two entities in one shared setting such that the first entity clearly satisfies the metabolic criterion while clearly failing the Darwinian one, and the second clearly satisfies the Darwinian criterion while clearly failing the metabolic one. Constraints: the setting must be scientifically plausible -- a plausible natural environment or a near-future laboratory, no science-fictional physics; the whole scenario should fit in two paragraphs; and you may not reach for any of the standard stock examples for this purpose (no viruses, no mules or sterile worker organisms, no fire, no growing crystals, no prions, and no generic "self-replicating robots"), since the point is to engineer fresh instances rather than recite the familiar borderline cases. The verdicts must fall out of the described features of the entities, not be stipulated.

After presenting the scenario, do the analytical work: (1) state precisely which features of each entity deliver its verdict under each criterion, and confirm that both criteria would classify each entity the same way if the relevant feature were absent; (2) identify the single load-bearing feature in each entity whose removal would collapse the divergence -- that is, make both criteria agree again; and (3) for whichever criterion's verdict you find less intuitive, give the strongest reply a defender of that criterion could make to deny that the case is a genuine counterexample (for instance, by insisting the entity is not a genuine "self-maintaining system" or not a genuine member of an evolving "lineage"), and assess whether that reply is a principled refinement or an ad hoc retreat. Conclude by saying which criterion, if either, the experiment ends up supporting.
