Write me a parody job advertisement, the kind a university HR office would actually post, recruiting a Counterfactual Intervener to support the philosophy department's responsibility experiments. The successful candidate will oversee a device implanted in experimental subjects' brains that monitors their deliberations and steps in to force the "right" decision should the subject show any prior sign of deciding otherwise. The whole selling point of the role is that the device never actually has to fire, so the job mostly consists of standing by, attentively, while remaining completely causally inert. Around 500-600 words, deadpan corporate HR voice throughout: an About the Role section, Key Responsibilities, Essential Criteria, salary band, an equal-opportunities statement, the works.

The humor has to come from actually getting Frankfurt cases right, not from generic mad-scientist stuff. So: the prior sign the intervener watches for, the way the intervener's mere presence is meant to eliminate the subject's alternatives while leaving what actually happens completely untouched, the awkward question of what counts as a reliable enough sign to trigger on (a flicker of hesitation? a twitch?), the fact that the intervener can never once take credit for an outcome, the performance-review problem of evaluating an employee whose success condition is never doing anything. Someone who knows the Frankfurt literature should nod along; someone who's never heard of it should still find it funny as an absurd job posting.

And commit to something: don't just riff neutrally. By the end of the ad the reader should feel a definite suspicion about whether Frankfurt-style setups really deliver what they promise — pick whichever side of that you actually find more defensible and let the satire carry it, rather than hedging both ways.

Structure it like this, in order: first the ad itself with its HR headings, then one final titled section, "HR Notes", roughly 150 words, where you say which specific features of Frankfurt cases each joke leans on and flag anywhere you stretched or flattened the literature for the sake of the bit.
