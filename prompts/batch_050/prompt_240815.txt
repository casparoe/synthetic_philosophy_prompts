I'm teaching a course on classical Indian epistemology and before we get to the usual "list the pramāṇas and who accepted how many" stuff, I want my students to think about what a theory of knowledge-sources even owes us. So: write the desiderata for a theory of pramāṇas -- what any adequate account of the instruments of knowledge has to deliver if its job is to tell us which sources of knowledge are genuinely independent and which are reducible to others.

Give me the requirements with the case or consideration motivating each. Things I'd want covered, though feel free to restructure: it has to handle error cases (the classic seeing-a-rope-as-a-snake situation) without making ordinary perceptual knowledge collapse into mere belief; it has to give a principled verdict on whether testimony is its own source or just inference about the speaker's reliability wearing a trench coat; it has to say something about how we know absences (how do I know there's no pot on the table right now?); it has to take a stand on whether knowing that a cognition is valid is built into the cognition itself or comes from outside confirmation; and it should explain what "reduction" even amounts to when someone claims one source is really just another.

Flag which requirements you think are genuinely non-negotiable vs which are really just the priors of one school dressed up as neutral criteria (the Cārvāka insistence that everything reduce to perception is the obvious candidate, but there are subtler ones). And if any subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable -- like wanting both a single unified definition covering all knowledge-sources AND genuine irreducible plurality among them -- say so explicitly, because that's the part I most want to see argued.

Then briefly, and I mean briefly, score the main contenders against your list: the Nyāya fourfold scheme, the Cārvāka perception-only view, and one of the Mīmāṃsā positions that admits postulation and non-apprehension as extra sources. No bibliography needed, I know the literature, just don't invent specific quotes from the sutras.
