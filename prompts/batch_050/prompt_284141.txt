I write a small newsletter on political language (very small, like 400 subscribers, so don't imagine I'm anyone important) and I've noticed a pattern I keep wanting a word for. A term starts out with a fairly precise, almost technical meaning -- think of how "gaslighting" originally referred to a specific pattern of making someone doubt their own perceptions, or how "neoliberal" once picked out an actual policy package, or how "fascist" picked out an actual ideology. Then the term catches on, and people start reaching for it in looser and looser situations, because it carries this punch that a plain word like "bad" or "dishonest" doesn't. Within a few years it's being applied to things that share almost nothing with the original referent -- gaslighting now just means lying, apparently -- until eventually the word means roughly "thing I strongly disapprove of" and nothing more. And here's the part I find interesting: the cycle doesn't stop there. People who still need the old, precise concept just coin a new term, and then that term gets sucked into the same machine. So it's not just that individual words decay, it's that there's a standing process generating precise terms and then hollowing them out.

I know this is adjacent to several things I have half-encountered. There's the euphemism treadmill, but as I understand it that's about neutral or polite terms becoming contaminated by what they refer to, which is kind of the opposite direction -- my thing is about strong terms going vague, not vague terms going sour. There's also whatever linguists call it when a word loses force through overuse, like how "awesome" stopped meaning awe-inspiring, but my cases seem different because the political charge is the whole reason the word gets overused, and because the stakes are different -- nobody is misled by "awesome," whereas calling a landlord dispute fascism does real argumentative work while meaning almost nothing. I also have a vague memory that Orwell complained about exactly this with political words of his day, that they'd been stretched until they signaled disapproval and nothing else, but I don't know if he named the phenomenon or just griped about instances of it.

So: does this cycle -- precise term, adoption as all-purpose condemnation, total drainage of content, replacement by a fresh precise term -- have an established name, in linguistics or political theory or anywhere? If it sits between a few existing concepts, tell me which ones and where it diverges from each, because I'd rather understand the carving than just get a label. And if nothing quite covers it, propose a name with a definition and some boundary cases (e.g., is "literally" an instance or a different process?). Also, please be upfront about what's actually documented here versus what's contested or just anecdotal -- I get the impression that some of these semantic-change stories are well-established regularities and others are mostly pundit lore, and I don't want a contested story presented to me as textbook fact.
