I'm prepping a handout on the dogmatism paradox for a reading group. Quick version of the puzzle: if I know that p, then I seem to know in advance that any evidence I get against p must be misleading (since p is true), so knowledge apparantly licenses me to just dismiss all future counterevidence -- which seems crazy. The handout already covers what I'd call the standard menu: (a) reject the inference, i.e. knowing p doesn't automatically give you knowledge that the counterevidence is misleading (the misleading-evidence point from Harman and Kripke), (b) Sorensen's 'junk knowledge' response, where the knowledge you gain is real but unusable, (c) the concessive response that dismissing higher-order evidence can be rational but tends to destroy the very knowledge it was based on, and (d) level-splitting, where your first-order belief and your belief about what you should believe come apart. Give me one more response that isn't on this list and isn't just a rewording, special case, or mashup of a-d -- I want something with a genuinely different structure -- and say which of the four it pushes back against hardest, and whether it actually dissolves the paradox or just moves the bump under the rug.
