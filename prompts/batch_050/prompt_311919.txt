I was discussing Warren and Brandeis with a friend from law school, and their reading-group handout summarizes their 1890 article The Right to Privacy like this: “Warren and Brandeis wanted courts to recognize a general right not to have one’s private life publicized, even when the report is true. They reached that right by extending older common-law protections for property, reputation, and bodily security. Privacy matters because unwanted publicity causes mental pain and threatens the solitude people need, so the core of the right is personal control: each person should be able to decide what information about her circulates. But the right fades once information is already public, when the person is a public figure, or when newspapers judge a subject to be newsworthy.” The handout treats this last bit as evidence that Warren and Brandeis’s supposed right to privacy is really just a property-like right in information that has not yet entered public circulation.

The handout also quotes this passage from the article: “Thoughts, emotions, and sensations demanded legal recognition, and the beautiful capacity for growth which characterizes the common law enabled the judges to afford the requisite protection, without the interposition of the legislature.” It says this proves that Warren and Brandeis thought privacy was merely an accidental extension of existing legal categories, not a distinct moral interest. Is the handout’s overall summary fair? Please separate what is accurate, what is a defensible simplification, and what Warren and Brandeis explicitly resisted or would plausibly object to. I’m especially interested in the difference between privacy as control over information, privacy as protection from publicity, and privacy as a legal interest analogous to property. Also say whether the handout’s property-based conclusion still follows once its mistakes are corrected. Please give your verdict with some indication of confidence, identify the part of your interpretation most likely to be wrong, and say what kind of textual evidence would change your mind.
