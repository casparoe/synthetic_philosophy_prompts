So I got into an argument with my brother-in-law over thanksgiving. He did some econ classes in college and kept saying economists don't care what you say you want, only what you do -- "actions speak louder than words" was his exact line, like that settles it. It came up because I said I want to eat healthier but I keep ordering pizza, and he said no, by definition you want pizza, your ordering is the proof, wanting is just what choosing looks like.

That felt wrong to me but I couldn't put my finger on it. Because ok, there's SOME truth in it, like if my friend says she values fitness but hasn't been to the gym in three years, I learn something. But there's also a difference between "I do X" and "I want X" that seems pretty basic -- like, I drive to work every day and I hate driving to work, there's just no train. And the pizza thing feels more like I lose to myself than like pizza is what I really want.

So here's what I'd like: take the slogan "actions speak louder than words" (or his version, "you reveal what you really want by what you choose") and break it into the different precise things it could actually be claiming. I suspect there are a few distinct ideas jumbled together, like maybe (a) choices are good evidence about what you want, (b) choices are the ONLY legitimate evidence, so asking people is pointless, (c) there's nothing to wanting beyond choosing at all, the want just IS the pattern of choices, and maybe others I haven't thought of. For each one: is it a boring truism, a real substantive claim that's plausible, or just false? And which versions would actually win my brother-in-law his argument vs. which ones would still let me say "I want to eat healthier" while ordering pizza without contradicting myself?

Also one more thing he said that I want checked: he claimed that if someone's choices are inconsistent (like picking A over B on monday and B over A on friday) then they just don't count as having real preferences at all. Is that part of the same slogan or a seperate claim, and does it survive the fact that literally everyone is inconsistent sometimes?
