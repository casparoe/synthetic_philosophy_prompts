My metaphysics professor has been issuing verdicts on statue-and-clay cases all term. Her rulings so far: (1) A lump of clay is made on Monday and shaped into a statue on Tuesday; on Wednesday the statue is squashed flat. Verdict: the statue ceased to exist on Wednesday, while the lump has existed since Monday and exists still. (2) The statue loses a finger, which is ground to powder. Verdict: the same statue persists, minus a finger. (3) Over many years the statue is repaired flake by flake with fresh clay, the old flakes discarded and destroyed, until none of the original clay remains. Verdict: the same statue persists throughout, and the same lump does too. (4) The statue is ground to dust; the dust sits in a jar for a decade and is then mixed with binder and sculpted into a statue qualitatively identical to the original. Verdict: this is a new statue, the original having been destroyed when ground down, and the original lump is likewise gone.

Next week's case: the statue is repaired flake by flake exactly as in (3), except that the old flakes are carefully preserved rather than destroyed; once the repairs are finished, the preserved flakes — the statue's entire original clay — are reassembled into a statue qualitatively identical to the repaired one, and the two are placed side by side. Please predict her verdict on which of the two, if either, is the original statue, and say what consistency requires her to say about the original lump of clay, noting that her treatment of statues and lumps has so far come apart only in case (1). Identify which principle she is implicitly running — spatiotemporal and causal continuity of form, same-matter tracking tempered by tolerance of gradual turnover, a closest-continuer rule, or something else — and show that at least two such principles fit all four verdicts while diverging on the new case. Finally, name the further case that would distinguish the two most plausible principles, and flag any point where her existing record simply underdetermines the answer.
