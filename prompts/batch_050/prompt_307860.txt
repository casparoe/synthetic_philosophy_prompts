Act as a professional philosopher specialising in formal epistemology and probability. Think carefully, and do a quick private brainstorm before drafting.

Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the Principal Principle. One side accepts strong deference to objective chance: if an agent knows that the present objective chance of A is x, and has no relevant extra information, their rational credence in A should be x. The other side accepts only evidential deference: information about chances is often excellent evidence, but it is not epistemically sovereign, because background frequencies, causal knowledge, model error, or worries about what “objective chance” really amounts to can sometimes justify departing from it.

The statement should include: (1) claims both sides can sign; (2) their residual disagreements, each phrased neutrally enough that both camps would accept the wording; (3) the main cruxes; and (4) for each crux, a case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side’s predicted verdict recorded. Include at least one ordinary case, such as a physical coin, and one harder case involving a fallible forecasting model. Avoid merely making the strong side win by defining all contrary evidence as “inadmissible”; say where admissibility itself is the crux.

Use British English, maximum 800 words, with numbered agreed claims followed by a disagreements table. At the end, say how confident you are in your reconstruction and identify the single point you are least sure either side would sign.
