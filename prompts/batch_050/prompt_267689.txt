Rewatching Infinity War it struck me that Thanos is basically a Malthusian: populations grow, resources don't, so unless something checks population you get catastrophe anyway -- he just does the checking himself, at random, "fair to rich and poor alike." Read the film through Malthus. Where does Thanos's argument actually match the Malthusian logic (geometric growth vs arithmetic resources, preventive vs positive checks) and where does he botch it -- e.g. does halving a population once even work on Malthus's own terms, since growth would presumably resume? And then the bigger thing: the film treats the question as settled that the crisis is coming, no matter how long it's been predicted and hasn't arrived. Is that a fair picture of Malthusianism? Can a theory like Malthus's be "wrong for two centuries but right eventually," or at some point does endless deferral of the prediction just mean the theory got rescued into unfalsifiability? I'd like the reading to take both the film and the philosophy of science question seriously -- including where the movie's framing (a villain who's weirdly reasonable) does philosophical work that a straightforward argument wouldn't.
