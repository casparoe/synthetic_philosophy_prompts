I am trying to formulate a Peircean position for a philosophy seminar and would like a consistency audit before committing to it. My commitments are as follows:

1. I accept a situated version of the pragmatic maxim: two hypotheses differ in meaning only if they could make some difference to the conditional expectations, preparations, or conduct of embodied inquirers. If no conceivable evidence, decision, or experience available to such inquirers would distinguish them, then they do not express genuinely different concepts.

2. I regard belief primarily as a habit of action rather than as an inner feeling of assent. A belief may therefore consist in a rarely activated conditional disposition, such as being prepared to revise one’s plans were a remote cosmological or mathematical proposition to become practically relevant.

3. I hold that inquiry begins with genuine doubt: a disruption caused by recalcitrant experience, practical failure, or conflict among established habits. Merely announcing that one can doubt everything at once is not enough to initiate inquiry.

4. I am a thoroughgoing fallibilist. Every belief, including logical and methodological commitments, is revisable in principle, and none should be treated as immune from reconsideration merely because it is currently indispensable.

5. I want to understand truth in terms of ideal convergence: a proposition is true when a sufficiently prolonged, self-correcting community of inquiry would settle upon it and would not subsequently be driven away from it. By ideal, I mean unlimited time and improved institutions, but not supernatural access to evidence that has ceased to exist.

6. I am also inclined towards traceless-fact realism. For example, the precise microscopic state of a region yesterday may have had a perfectly determinate value even if thermal mixing has destroyed every recoverable trace, so that no possible future inquiry could establish it.

7. I accept that inquiry is path-dependent. Experiments can destroy evidence, early theoretical choices can determine which measurements later become available, and communities can become locked into technologies or classifications that make some questions permanently inaccessible.

8. I regard scientific inquiry as privileged not because its practitioners are incorrigible or because its present conclusions are final, but because its public practices make correction by later experience comparatively difficult to suppress. No individual scientist, institution, or interim consensus is authoritative in itself.

Please assess whether these commitments hang together. Identify which pairs or larger subsets are genuinely in tension and which apparent tensions dissolve under a more careful interpretation. Rank any genuine tensions by severity, explain exactly what each commitment forces me to give up, and identify the least costly single revision that would restore the greatest overall coherence. Please distinguish revisions to my account of meaning from revisions to my account of truth, evidence, or inquiry. I am not asking for a general exposition of Peirce, for bibliographical references, or for citations; I want an audit of this particular package. Please write the answer in American English, even though this question is phrased in British English.
