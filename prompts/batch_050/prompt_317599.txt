Two animal-welfare researchers have agreed to an adversarial collaboration, and I want you to draft the joint statement they publish at its end. Researcher A defends small-animal replacement: a cow yields on the order of a hundred times the edible calories of a chicken while requiring only one death, and well-managed pasture-raised cattle plausibly live lives worth living, so a consumer who wants to reduce animal suffering should swap chicken and eggs for grass-fed beef rather than reduce meat intake overall. Researcher B defends the full-ledger view: any such recommendation is premature, because crop cultivation for every diet kills large numbers of wild animals through machinery, pesticides, and habitat loss; grazing uses far more land per calorie and so imposes its own wild-animal costs; and whether any farmed animal's life is net positive is too uncertain to license an endorsement of eating more of anything. Draft the statement both could sign. Structure it as: Agreed Claims, a numbered list of propositions both accept, including points each side originally resisted (roughly 200 words); Disagreements, a table in which each remaining dispute is stated as two precise formulations, each worded so that its own side would accept it as accurate (roughly 200 words); Cruxes, the two or three sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party, and for each a study, calculation, or observation both agree would bear on it, with each researcher's prediction recorded in advance (roughly 250 words). Where a disagreement turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, say so in the Agreed Claims section rather than the table.
