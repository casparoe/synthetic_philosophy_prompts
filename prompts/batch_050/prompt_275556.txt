I'm a Grade 12 student and I've volunteered to lead a session of our school's philosophy club — twenty-five minutes, a whiteboard, classmates who are sharp but have never read philosophy of mind. My topic is gradual-replacement mind uploading: the scenario in which biological neurons are swapped, one at a time, for silicon components that replicate their input-output behavior exactly, until the entire brain is artificial. I don't want an explanation of the debate; I want a plan for my explanation. Tell me the order in which the pieces have to go in and what each depends on — in particular, whether I should open with a teletransportation case to get survival intuitions on the table or whether that poisons the well by making everyone think in terms of copies before they have the concept of functional equivalence; when to introduce the distinction between whether anyone is conscious in there afterward and whether it is still me; and where, if at all, the fading-qualia point belongs (the observation that if consciousness dimmed as neurons were replaced, the subject would presumably notice the change and report it, which contradicts the setup's stipulation that behavior stays identical throughout). Choose the one scenario that will carry the whole session — I've been torn between a single overnight procedure and a replacement spread over years — and say what it has to contain (the subject noticing nothing, no interruption of experience) and what it must leave out. Then the misconceptions I should expect and exactly where in the sequence to intercept each: the "our cells turn over anyway, so obviously you survive" shortcut, which I suspect begs the question; its mirror image, "once the last original neuron is gone it's just a copy, so obviously you die"; the thought that consciousness requires unbroken continuity, which invites the sleep objection; the slide into talk of souls; and the assumption that making the change gradual settles everything automatically. Finally, one closing question that would show me the idea has landed rather than been memorized, and what I should openly decline to get into at this level.
