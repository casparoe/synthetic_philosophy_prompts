I've been thinking again about the meme's-eye view of culture — the Dawkins/Dennett program, which I've followed for years in discussions on rationalist blogs and adjacent forums, of treating ideas, tunes, and practices as selfish replicators whose spread is explained by their own replication success rather than by their usefulness to their hosts. I have collected what I take to be the five standard objections to this program:

1. The particulate-unit objection. Genes are discrete, but "memes" have no stable boundaries: cultural items merge, blend, and shade into one another, so there are no clean replicator units for selection to act on.
2. The fidelity objection. Replication requires high-fidelity copying, but human transmission is reconstruction: people infer, misremember, and rebuild ideas from cues rather than copying them, so what travels is a lineage of transformations, not copies of an original.
3. The no-genotype objection. Biology separates replicator from vehicle — genotype from phenotype, with a developmental system mediating between them. Memetics offers no analogue; the meme is supposed to be simultaneously the information, its expression, and its means of propagation.
4. The agency objection. Natural selection is blind, but minds actively filter, interpret, and redesign what they receive. The causal work is done by host psychology, so talk of the meme's own interests inverts the real direction of explanation.
5. The sterility objection. Decades in, the program has produced no laws, no formal models comparable to population genetics, and no novel predictions — only a rebranding of what diffusion-of-innovation research already said in plainer terms.

What is the strongest sixth objection I am missing? It should be genuinely distinct: not a special case, a restatement, or a conjunction of the five above. Please state the objection, say which of the five it comes closest to and how it nonetheless differs, and assess whether it is fatal to the Darwinian theory of culture or answerable from within it. Keep the whole reply under 300 words.
