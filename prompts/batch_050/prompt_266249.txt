I'm giving a talk at a philosophy dept colloqium next month defending infinitism about epistemic justification — the Klein-ish view that a belief is justified only if the subject has an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons available to them. Full disclosure: I've been reading rationalist blogs for like fifteen years and this whole project started as me taking the "but why do you believe THAT?" regress seriously instead of treating foundations as an emergency brake you pull when the chain gets annoying. The audience is mostly analytic epistemologists: a couple of reliabilists, one very committed coherentist who I know for a fact will be there, some internalist sympathizers, and nobody who actually works on infinitism. Write my Q&A prep sheet: the seven or so objections this specific crowd will actually raise (not the ones a reddit thread would raise), ranked by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that particular person would phrase it, with a two-minute reply for each and a note on where the reply is weakest. Also include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. One constraint: my talk bites the bullet that finite minds can't consciously entertain infinite chains and leans hard on a dispositional "available on reflection" account of having reasons, so most of the objections should be aimed at that move.
