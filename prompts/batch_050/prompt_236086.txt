Two logicians in my department have agreed to an adversarial collaboration, and I need the joint statement they would produce. One holds that Curry's paradox -- where a sentence that says, roughly, "if this very sentence is true, then the moon is made of cheese" lets you prove anything using only apparently innocent reasoning -- shows that classical logic itself must be revised, and she favors restricting a structural rule such as contraction. The other insists that the whole family of semantic paradoxes can be handled while keeping classical logic fully intact, by weakening the naive principles governing truth instead (for instance by denying that every sentence gets to be true-or-false in the way the paradoxical reasoning assumes). Their dispute also covers Yablo's paradox, the infinite list of sentences where each one says only that all the later ones are false: the first logician claims this shows circularity was never the real problem, while the second claims the list smuggles in a kind of self-reference through the single uniform predicate used to define all the sentences at once.

Write their joint statement. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, including what the paradoxes demonstrably rule out, what minimal ingredients a derivation of Curry's paradox actually needs, and which purported solutions both agree fail; (2) the exact remaining disagreements, each phrased in words that side would accept for its own view -- I expect these to include whether contraction is the culprit or a bystander, whether Yablo's construction is genuinely self-reference-free, and whether any consistent theory expressive enough to state its own truth predicate invites "revenge" paradoxes no matter what you give up; and (3) for each disagreement, the crux question whose resolution would move at least one of them, plus a formal result, derivation, or case they both agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. Where part of their dispute turns out to be verbal -- say, over what counts as "revising logic" versus "revising the theory of truth" -- the statement should say so explicitly and explain why. Use American English.
