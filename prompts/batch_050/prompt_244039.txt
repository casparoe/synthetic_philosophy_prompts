I'm a TA for an undergrad course on philosophy of language and technology, and I keep having the same hallway conversation with students. Everyone has latched onto the line that when a chatbot makes stuff up it isn't lying and it isn't even making a mistake -- it's bullshitting, in Harry Frankfurt's technical sense: the bullshitter, unlike the liar, isn't trying to hide the truth from you, he just doesn't care whether what he says is true or false. And the students find this really satisfying because the model, they say, quite literally has no relationship to truth at all, it just produces plausible-sounding text, so hallucination isn't error or deception, it's bullshit all the way down. One of them even built her whole term paper around the claim that Frankfurt's essay basically predicts LLMs.

Here's what I want worked out. Treat "the model is a bullshitter in Frankfurt's sense" as an analogy and audit it. Give me the explicit mapping: what in the model's situation corresponds to the bullshitter's indifference to truth, to the liar's need to know the truth in order to conceal it, to the honest person's truth-tracking, to the audience being deceived or not. Then tell me where it breaks. The one that bugs me: Frankfurt's bullshitter has motives (impressing the dinner party, selling something) that explain WHY he's indifferent to truth, and I can't figure out what plays that role for a next-token predictor -- or whether the account even needs motives to apply. Relatedly, does calling something bullshit require a mind that could in principle have cared, and if so does the analogy quietly smuggle in intentionality the model lacks?

Finally, the payoff question my student never gets to: if the disanalogies are real, does anything in the vicinity still do the work everyone wants, e.g. is hallucination better thought of as confabulation (like a patient with Korsakoff's sincerely filling gaps) or as bluffing, or is plain old "error" fine and the Frankfurt framing is just a metaphor that flatters our intuitions? And what would the audit imply for her thesis claim -- can Frankfurt's account predict anything about LLMs, or only describe them loosely?
