Novelist here, plotting a chapter where a character somehow resurrects John Stuart Mill (don't ask) and puts a modern case to him. Suppose Mill is shown a platform where users can generate a convincing sexual video of any real person from public photos. The maker labels it synthetic, so technically nobody is deceived, and the platform doesn't create the clips itself; it merely recommends them, sells ads around them, and makes reporting slow. The targets are overwhelmingly female journalists, politicians, and ordinary women who rejected someone's advances. Many stop speaking publicly or disappear from the internet altogether. Defenders say no contact occurred, viewers were warned, banning pixels is censorship, and if women withdraw from public debate more readily than men, that may simply reflect a natural difference in temperament.

What do you think Mill would want permitted, prohibited, or merely discouraged—and by whom? Please make the prediction do real work rather than just repeat harm equals bad. Would he understand the injury as defamation, fraud, privacy invasion, incitement, or something else? How would the gendered pattern matter given his criticism of appeals to women's nature? Would he distinguish privately making the video, sending it to the target, posting it, algorithmically amplifying it, and profiting from it? And where would he draw the boundaries between action by the state, the platform, advertisers, employers, and ordinary public opinion, given his worries about social tyranny? If Mill's principles genuinely pull in different directions here, say where instead of smoothing the conflict over.
