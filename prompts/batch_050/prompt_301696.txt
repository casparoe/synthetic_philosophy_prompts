I am a professor of philosophy whose research sits in the philosophy of action — I am fully at home with the standard vocabulary there: intention and intentional action, practical deliberation, causal versus teleological accounts of agency, guidance control and regulative control, reasons-responsiveness, acting for a reason, omissions, and the problem of responsibility attribution in cases of shared or mediated agency. I am much less fluent in the policy and legal literature on lethal autonomous weapons systems, where the organizing concept is meaningful human control.

Here is what I would like you to do. Restate the case for meaningful human control — the claim that some class of decisions by autonomous weapons, paradigmatically the selection and engagement of targets, must remain under a kind of human control that is not merely nominal — as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary of the philosophy of action that I just listed. In particular: reconstruct what the demand for meaningful human control amounts to if we translate it into claims about who or what must act for a reason, what kind of control (guidance or regulative) is being demanded, where in the deliberative and causal chain between a human operator's intention and the kinetic outcome the demand is supposed to bite, and how the responsibility-gap arguments in this debate map onto familiar puzzles about responsibility for omissions, for mediated outcomes, and for the behavior of agents one has set in motion.

Then, and this is the part I care most about, give me a residue report: an inventory of what does not survive the translation. I want you to distinguish three categories explicitly. First, claims that translate cleanly — where the policy debate is, at bottom, redeploying distinctions action theorists already have. Second, claims that translate only with distortion — where you have to smuggle in assumptions the action-theoretic vocabulary does not naturally carry, and where you should tell me exactly what you smuggled in and why. Third, claims that cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all, and here I want your diagnosis in each case: is the residue a defect of the philosophy of action — a phenomenon (say, control exercised at a temporal remove, over a system whose behavior is partly opaque and partly stochastic) that our concepts were simply not built for — or is it a sign that the corresponding piece of the policy discourse is less clear, or less substantive, than its proponents take it to be? Please do not adjudicate diplomatically; I want a verdict on the residue, claim by claim, with reasons.

A few constraints. Do not assume that meaningful human control has one canonical meaning in the policy literature — where the debate is genuinely ambiguous between rival readings, translate the readings separately and note whether the ambiguity looks like the policy community rediscovering, without knowing it, a distinction action theorists already draw. And keep the translation honest: do not quietly redefine terms like intention or guidance control to make the fit look better than it is.
