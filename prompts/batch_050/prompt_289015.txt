So I've been reading LessWrong and the adjacent blogs since basically forever, and our little reading group keeps hitting the same wall: someone says "but how do you know THAT?", the other person gives a reason, someone asks how they know that, and eventually everyone just sort of trails off or says "well, it's just obvious." I want to actually sort us by what we think is going on when a chain of justification has to deal with the regress problem.

Write me a decision tree / flowchart I can turn into a handout. It should take someone from "I agree that 'it's justified because it's justified' is cheating and 'it just is, stop asking' feels suspicious" to a named position on the structure of epistemic justification. The leaves must include at least: classical foundationalism, a modest/fallibilist foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism, and a skeptic leaf for people who bite the bullet that none of the options work. Feel free to add leaves if the logical space demands them.

Please build the tree from the logical space first, not from a list of names: figure out the questions on which these views actually come apart (does justification have to terminate at all? if it terminates, what kind of thing can sit at the bottom without itself needing support? can mutual support among beliefs ever generate justification, or is that just a longer circle? does a finite mind's inability to actually run an infinite chain disqualify infinitism, and does the infinitist even need the chain to be run?), and only then locate the positions in that space. Phrase every node as a concrete question or mini-scenario a smart non-philosopher could answer without knowing the labels -- e.g. stuff like a detective whose evidence all hangs together beautifully but none of it touches anything checkable, or being asked to justify "there's a screen in front of me" to someone who genuinely won't let it go. No node should just ask "do you think some beliefs are basic?"

Also: note any combinations of answers that no named position occupies, and give a one-line diagnosis for each (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). And flag any pair of leaves your tree can't cleanly seperate -- I suspect modest foundationalism and coherentism get blurry when the "basic" beliefs are only defeasible and coherence does a lot of the work, but I could be wrong about that.

Aim for maybe 5-7 branch nodes. Each leaf gets the position's name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Keep the tone light, this is for people who argue about calibration over dinner, and please write everything in American English.
