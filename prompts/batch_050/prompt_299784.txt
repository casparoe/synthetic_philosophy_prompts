Construct a thought experiment that prises collective intentionality apart from mere coordination. I want two versions of the same realistic scenario. In version A, two people have the same concrete goal, each knows the other has it, their behaviour meshes successfully, and each would adjust if the other faltered; nevertheless, it should still be plausible that they are not acting jointly. In version B, change exactly one feature so that they clearly are acting jointly.

Constraints: keep the whole thing under 300 words; no carrying sofas, cooking together, painting houses, dancing, or pushing broken-down cars; no deception, coercion, employment hierarchy, secret motives, or one person simply using another as a tool. The difference cannot just be that nobody privately thinks we are doing this. Make it behavioural or normative rather than a bit of hidden inner commentary. Also, don’t have the characters discuss philosophy or announce the relevant distinction.

After the scenarios, analyse them under three approaches: a Bratman-style account based on interlocking intentions and common knowledge, a Gilbert-style account based on joint commitment, and team reasoning. For each approach, say whether it counts A, B, or both as joint action, and why. Then identify the single variable doing the work, explain what happens if it is replaced by an explicit promise, and give the strongest objection from someone who insists that A was already a case of joint action. Finish by saying what, if anything, your case shows joint action adds beyond coordination: authority to object or correct, divided responsibility, persistence in the face of temptations, or something else entirely.
