I've been arguing with my brother about this for months, ever since he went vegetarian and I didn't, and I finally wrote down his best argument in a form he agrees is fair to him. Now I'm worried, because every premise seems right to me and the conclusion seems crazy. (1) An action is wrong only if things go worse because you did it than they would have gone otherwise; an act that leaves the world exactly as it would have been anyway may be odd or distasteful, but it harms no one. (2) Whether or not I buy the chicken at the supermarket, the same number of chickens get raised and slaughtered: the store orders in bulk based on aggregate sales, the slaughterhouse schedules in shifts, and no single purchase ever crosses whatever threshold would change a single order. (3) So my buying the chicken leaves the world exactly as it would have been, and by (1) it isn't wrong. (4) The same reasoning covers my flight's emissions, my vote in a national election, and my fast-fashion purchases, since none of them ever tips any threshold either. (5) Therefore no individual act of consumption or participation is ever wrong merely because it contributes to a collective harm; morality simply doesn't reach my single steak, my single ballot, or my single tank of gas. (6) But that can't be right: it would excuse every member of a firing squad, since the prisoner dies from the volley regardless of any one bullet, and it would imply that if everyone reasoned this way no one would be doing anything wrong even as the outcomes everyone deplores arrived exactly on schedule.

The step I most want to keep is (1), because tying wrongness to making a difference seems like the only way to keep morality honest and avoid empty gestures, and I'd also rather keep (6), since the firing-squad case feels decisive. What I can't tell is whether the argument sneaks something past me between (2) and (3) -- it feels like "this purchase changes nothing" might be doing different work in different premises -- or whether the argument is valid and I just have to pick which plausible premise is cheapest to surrender. If the answer involves expected effects rather than actual ones, or some notion of participating in a harm rather than causing it, I want to know exactly what those moves cost me elsewhere.
