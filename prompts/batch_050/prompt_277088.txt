I am a high school student preparing a discussion activity for a history elective on individual agency versus structural explanation. Our class became stuck on the question of whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler because people used that counterfactual to mean several different things: that Hitler alone caused Nazism, that Germany was bound to become some kind of dictatorship, that another extremist would have taken Hitler’s place, or that democracy might have survived without him. Please create a decision tree called Which explanation of Nazi Germany do you actually accept? Use the fixed counterfactual that Hitler permanently disappears from politics after 1923 and never returns, while events such as the Versailles settlement, the Depression, political violence, and elite opposition to Weimar democracy still occur unless the respondent concludes that they would have unfolded differently for independent reasons.

Before drawing the tree, map the relevant logical possibilities rather than simply listing famous historians. Then provide eight to ten diagnostic questions, each expressed as a concrete historical judgment rather than a request for a preferred label. The questions should distinguish, among other things: whether Weimar democracy was already likely to collapse before Hitler became chancellor; whether economic crisis, nationalism, antisemitism, and hostility to Versailles made some authoritarian nationalist government probable; whether the Nazi Party could have become a mass movement without Hitler’s particular talents; whether another plausible leader could have combined its antisemitic, racial, expansionist, and anti-democratic commitments; whether dictatorship, war, and genocide were separable possibilities; and whether the timing and sequence of events made the outcome path-dependent. Use answers such as very likely, plausible but uncertain, or very unlikely where appropriate. The leaves should include concise descriptive positions such as Hitler as indispensable exceptional leader, Hitler as catalyst or amplifier, authoritarian Germany without Nazism, a Nazi-like regime under different leadership, democratic survival, and an interactive path-dependent explanation. If two of these cannot genuinely be separated by the tree, combine or explicitly flag them rather than pretending that a question can distinguish them.

For each leaf, explain in two or three sentences what Germany would probably have looked like by the late 1930s, what causal role it assigns to Hitler, and one awkward implication or difficult piece of evidence for that position. Also identify at least two logically possible answer combinations that do not correspond to a recognizable position, saying whether they seem incoherent, historically implausible, or merely underexplored. End with an epistemic note that clearly separates established background facts, interpretations that knowledgeable historians broadly share, and genuinely speculative counterfactual judgments. Do not present either a Great Man interpretation or a structural interpretation as the consensus, and do not suggest that denying Hitler’s causal indispensability would reduce his moral responsibility. No bibliography or citations are needed.
