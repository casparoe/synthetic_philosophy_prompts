Okay so this is a weird one but bear with me. I'm a novelist (two published books, lit-fic with a speculative streak) and I'm applying for a six-month research fellowship that lets non-academics poke around in a university philosophy department. I need a proposal, roughly two pages, and my idea is still mush.

The mush: I've become convinced that philosophical thought experiments are basically flash fiction, and that a lot of their persuasive force comes from craft choices rather than logic. Like, the runaway trolley isn't just a dilemma, it's a scene with a camera angle -- you're up on the footbridge, the five workers are faceless, the fat man is right there next to you. Thomson's violinist works because she makes you wake up in a hospital bed instead of stating the abstract principle. Nozick's experience machine has to be a *machine*, humming in a lab, not just an offer of guaranteed happiness. When I change these stories the way I'd revise a draft -- switch the POV, give the worker a name and a kid, slow the pacing down, show the aftermath -- my own intuitions start sliding around even though the "relevant structure" is supposedly unchanged.

So the project would be: take a handful of famous intuition pumps, do close readings of them the way I'd do a fiction workshop (whose perspective, what's shown vs. told, what happens offstage, where the story cuts to black), and then write deliberate counter-versions that keep the abstract structure fixed but flip the narrative framing, to see whether the intuition follows the framing. The philosophical claim I'd be testing is something like: some thought experiments persuade by smuggling in what fiction writers call sympathy management, and once you isolate that variable the pump stops pumping. Or maybe it doesn't, which would be interesting too.

Turn this into a proper proposal. It needs: a precise statement of the question (one that could actually be answered, not "are thought experiments literary, discuss"), a bit on why philosophers' existing grumbling about intuition pumps hasn't already settled this (my sense is they argue about whether intuitions are reliable in general, not about the specific narrative mechanisms doing the work, but correct me if I'm wrong), the method broken down by month including how I'd actually test whether counter-versions shift intuitions without turning this into an experimental psych study I'm not qualified to run, what the deliverable is, and what would count as the project failing. Also an honest paragraph on the obvious objection that I'm just doing literary criticism of texts nobody reads for the prose.

Don't include a bibliography or any citations, I'll sort that out with the department librarian later.
