I'm putting together a position paper for a reading group on classical pragmatism (mostly Peirce, the two famous 1870s papers) and before I start drafting I want a consistency check, because I've got a nagging feeling that I've been nodding along to things that cancel each other out. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm on board with the pragmatic maxim as the test of meaningfulness: the entire content of a concept consists in the conceivable practical and experiential consequences its object would have.
(2) I buy Peirce's line against Descartes that inquiry can only begin from real, living doubt, the kind that actually irritates you into doing something, and that the universal doubt you perform in an armchair is fake and leads nowhere.
(3) I'm a thoroughgoing fallibilist: any belief I hold, including my best-confirmed scientific ones, might turn out to be wrong, and no belief is in principle immune to revision.
(4) I think truth just is whatever opinion inquiry would settle on if it were pushed far enough, in the long run.
(5) I'm a realist in the boring sense: there's a world whose character doesn't depend on what anyone thinks about it, and that's precisely why inquiry gets pushed around by something.
(6) On cases: I take it to be meaningful, and in fact true, to say of a diamond that nobody will ever test that it is hard, meaning it *would* resist scratching if anyone tried. The untested counterfactual is part of the furniture of the world, not a bookkeeping convention.

Each of these seems standard and defensible on its own, and (2)-(5) at least are all recognizably Peircean, so you'd think they'd hang together by default. But the more I stare at them the less sure I am. Which of these are in genuine tension, which pairs only look like they are, and in each case what would actually have to give? I'd like the tensions ranked by severity rather than a flat list where everything looks equally bad, plus the single least costly revision that restores the package and a honest statement of what it costs. One more request: I don't know where the problem is, so please don't just defend the whole set as-is or junk it wholesale — I want to know which committments are doing damage to which others, and through what mechanism.
