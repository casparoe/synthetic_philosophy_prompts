so i've been reading around in philosophy of physics and i've basically talked myself into the following view: effective field theory shows that reductionism is just wrong as a picture of how physics actually works. the rough argument: because of decoupling, heavy degrees of freedom only show up at low energies suppressed by powers of E/M, so the physics at any scale is autonomous -- you can do particle physics without knowing quantum gravity, do chemistry without knowing QCD, etc, and the higher-level theory isn't some approximate shadow of the "real" fundamental theory, it's just its own self-standing thing with its own entities. renormalization is what makes this possible, washing out the UV details into a few parameters you measure. so the "fundamental" level doesn't deserve its special status, and the hierarchy of sciences is vindicated by the actual structure of our best physics, not just by practical convenience.

what i want from you: what would count AGAINST this view? not just the generic "but surely there's a final theory" grumbling, but specific features of physics that sit badly with the autonomy-of-scales reading. i'm vaguely aware that there are awkward cases -- e.g. situations where the decoupling argument has singular limits, like near critical points where suddenly infinitely many degrees of freedom at all scales matter and the UV details do leak in; anomalies, where a symmetry of the classical theory can't survive quantization and the low-energy theory knows something about its UV completion; and the whole naturalness/fine-tuning business where the Higgs mass seems strangely sensitive to high-scale physics even though decoupling says it shouldn't care. which of these actually threaten the anti-reductionist conclusion and which can the EFT partisan absorb by saying "sure, that's still autonomous, you just parameterize it"? also: is there a difference between the view failing as a claim about actual physics vs failing as a claim about what physics COULD look like (i.e. could a world run on pure EFT decoupling with no fundamental level at all, turtles all the way down)?

rank the defeaters from genuinely fatal to merely awkward, and for each one say whether a committed autonomy-of-scales person would concede it as damage or dismiss it as missing the point. i'd especially like to know if the view has quietly become unfalsifiable -- i.e. whether "it's all EFTs" is the kind of thing that survives every possible discovery, and if so whether that's fine for a metaphysical lesson drawn from physics or a sign i've overextended a methodological point into an ontology.
