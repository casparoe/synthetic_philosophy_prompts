Ok so my husband and I have been arguing for like two weeks about whether to let our 9 year old bike to her friend's house across town (about 15 min, one busy intersection) and it's spiralled into this bigger fight we can't resolve, so I want outside input.

His position: the world is objectively way safer than when we were kids. He pulls up numbers on his phone -- homicide rates are half what they were in the early 90s, stranger abductions are incredibly rare, our parents let us roam til dark and the risk was HIGHER then, so keeping her home is just irrational anxiety fed by the news. Case closed, let her ride.

My position, or I guess my jumble of positions: (1) When I look at the news it feels like awful stuff happens constantly -- shootings, wars, people getting attacked on transit -- and sure, maybe the RATE per person is down, but there are also way more people than in 1950, so isn't the total amount of violence in the world actually up? Shouldn't the total count matter, not just the per-person slice? (2) A lot of the nasty stuff that happens to kids now doesn't show up in "violent crime" stats at all -- online harrassment, kids getting blackmailed over photos, bullying that follows you home and lives on the internet forever. If the stats only count old-fashioned physical assault, of course they show things improving; maybe the violence just changed shape. (3) Where do things like suicide and overdoses fit? If those count, I feel like the "everything's getting better" story falls apart, but my husband says you can't count harm people do to themselves as violence.

So two questions, I guess. Practically: who's being more reasonable about the bike ride, and is there a way to settle it that isn't just him googling stats that agree with him? And philosophically, because this is what's really bugging me -- is "the world is getting less violent" even a factual claim that can be true or false, or does it just come out true or false depending on which things you decide to count and whether you divide by population? If it's the second one, I want to understand what the argument is even about when smart people disagree about it. Is one of them just making a mistake, or are they talking past each other, and is there a right way to count?
