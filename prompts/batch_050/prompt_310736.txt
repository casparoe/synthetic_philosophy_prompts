I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about why human extinction would be bad, beyond just the deaths it involves. I came across Scheffler's afterlife conjecture stuff (roughly: we all assume humanity will outlive us, and a lot of what we value would lose its point if it didn't) and I've been testing my gut on cases. here are my verdicts, I want to know what principle, if any, captures them:

1. The infertility scenario: tomorrow everyone becomes sterile. Nobody dies early, everyone lives out a normal lifespan in reasonable comfort, but no new people are ever born. Even bracketing the practical chaos, I think this is a catastrophe far beyond the sum of individual disappointments. I'd even say it's worse than a plague that instantly kills half of everyone, even though the plague involves vastly more death.

2. The last two people: after some disaster, two survivors know for certain nobody else is left and nobody is coming (no aliens listening in either). They can spend their final decade comfortably, or in miserable toil preserving a library and a seed vault that will never, ever be used. My gut says the toil is pointless, maybe even a bit sad. At most it's worth something as a coping mechanism.

3. Individual choice: any given person deciding not to have kids becuase they just don't want them -- completely fine, wrongs no one. But universalized, that choice is literally case 1.

4. The handoff: extinction in 100 years is bad, but suppose before the end humanity hands off everything -- science, art, institutions, the running of the world -- to a genuinely thriving alien or machine civilization that keeps it all going. That removes a real chunk of the badness. Not all of it though: if the aliens keep the projects going but erase every record that we ever existed, that's better than nothing but still feels like a big loss. So it's not merely "someone keeps valuing things" that matters, it somehow matters that it's *us*, or that the chain runs through us.

5. Stagnation vs. sunset: humanity A coasts forever in stagnant comfort, endless people, nothing new ever again. Humanity B flourishes brilliantly for another 200 years and then ends painlessly. I honestly cannot decide which is worse and I keep flip flopping.

What I want: is there a single principle that delivers verdicts 1 through 4? The obvious candidate (what's bad is breaking the chain of valuing, and our projects get their point partly from expecting continuation) seems to handle 1 and 2 fine, but I don't see how it explains why 3 is fine while 1 is catastrophic, and 4 suggests something extra about *whose* continuation matters. If no principle gets all of them, tell me which verdict you'd throw out and why. And whatever you land on, apply it to case 5, since that's the one I'm stuck on -- don't leave it as a coin toss. Also don't just survey positions at me; I want an actual proposed principle, stated tightly, with the clause that handles each case.
