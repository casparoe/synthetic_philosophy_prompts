For a graduate seminar on rationality and time, I assembled the following argument from premises my classmates and I each found plausible, but it lands somewhere none of us can accept, and we cannot locate the misstep.

(1) A unit of well-being matters equally wherever it occurs; reasons to promote well-being do not vary in strength with arbitrary features of its location. (2) Temporal location is an arbitrary feature in this sense: an equal pleasure is not less of a pleasure for being later, so pure time preference is irrational. (3) Personal location is arbitrary in the same sense: my relation to my distant future self is one of fading psychological continuity, and by the end of a long life my future self may be less continuous with me now than my siblings are now. (4) So if temporal location cannot ground differential concern, neither can personal location: what does the work in (2) is that location is metaphysically superficial, and personal location is superficial in just the same way. (5) Therefore pure egoism — discounting a benefit merely because it is not mine — is irrational for exactly the reason pure time preference is. (6) But it is plainly rationally permissible to care more about my own projects and my children's welfare than about strangers'. (7) So something in (1)–(4) must go.

I would like to keep (2); I find pure time preference genuinely indefensible. And (6) seems non-negotiable. But every attempt to reject (4) has sounded to me like special pleading: whatever defect we name in the move from times to persons seems equally to afflict the move that condemns time preference. Diagnose the argument. Identify which premise or step a defender of (2) and (6) should reject, explain why that step looked irresistible, and say what rejecting it costs — in particular, whether the diagnosis leaves the standard case against discounting the future intact, or whether keeping (6) forces a more defensible restatement of (2). If the argument is in fact valid, say which premise is cheapest to surrender and what follows for prudential rationality.
