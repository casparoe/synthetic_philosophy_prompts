I am a novelist working on a political thriller set between Copenhagen and Washington, and the book's plausibility depends on my getting my own views about trust in government straight. Roughly where I've landed: (1) Scandinavian confidence in the state is mostly a consequence, not a cause, of decades of competent and relatively honest administration — institutions earn trust by delivering, rather than being propped up by some antecedent civic virtue; (2) American distrust of the federal government is, on balance, a rational response to that government's actual record, not a cultural pathology to be explained away; (3) nonetheless, a high-trust society is morally better to live in — defaulting to trust in strangers and officials is simply a more decent way to be, and this value does not depend on whether the trust in question turns out to be well placed; (4) for the plot to work, my Danish protagonist's serene confidence in the police and the ministry must come across to the reader as a naivety that the events of the book chasten and correct. Do these commitments hang together? I'd like an audit, not a lecture: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, order the real tensions by severity, and for each say what I would have to give up to restore consistency — bearing in mind that I am least willing to sacrifice (4).
