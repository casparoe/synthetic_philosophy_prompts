I don't want to just lecture, because the whole point of the paradox of trying not to try is that its something you feel happening to you, not something you get explained. So design me an in-class activity or game where the group has to actually attempt some task "effortlessly" and discovers that trying to be effortless is exactly what wrecks it — the paradox should bite them during the activity, before anyone names it. Constraints: no lecture up front, nobody hears the term wu-wei until the debrief, and the task has to be one where deliberate conscious effort genuinely degrades performance (not just one where people *think* it does). I want the design to cover the task itself and why it punishes trying, the rules and any stakes or scoring (no real money), how to structure a few rounds so people can watch their own attempts backfire, a debrief that maps what happened onto the trying-not-to-try problem and onto the two different routes the texts seem to offer (the spontaneous skill thing like the butcher and the cook vs. the gradual training thing), what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it, and an honest note on which feature of wu-wei the activity can't reproduce in 50 minutes — like skill built over years. Format: exactly six numbered items, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion.
