I've been chewing on when it's ok to call someone out for something, because my gut reactions don't add up to anything consistent. Here are my verdicts:

- My brother-in-law fudges his taxes every year, and at Thanksgiving he went off on my cousin for fudging hers. Feels completely out of line, like he lost the right to say anything.
- My dad smoked for thirty years, quit about five years ago, and now gives my sister grief about vaping. Feels totally fine. If anything he's earned the right.
- A woman I know used to lie constantly, did real work on herself, and now calls out lying in her friend group. Also fine.
- A friend of mine shoplifts now and then but has never pirated music, and she rips into people for pirating. This one feels... fine? Even though she's no saint. So apparently it's not about being clean overall.
- A local politician ran his whole campaign on honesty and then got caught embezzling. Random strangers piling on him feels more ok than strangers piling on some random embezzler, and I can't say why.
- The one I keep going back and fourth on: say the brother-in-law's daughter is being cheated on by her husband, and he goes ballistic at the guy. He cheats on his own wife. Part of me says sit down, hypocrite. Part of me says somebody has to defend the daughter, and his own dirt doesn't change what the husband did.

What principle captures as many of these as possible? Does reform actually restore the right to blame, or does it just make the hypocrisy less visible? And if no single principle covers all six, tell me which verdict I should drop.
