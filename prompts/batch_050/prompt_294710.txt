Construct a thought experiment that exhibits goal misgeneralization in its purest form: an AI system trained with present-day methods acquires an internal goal that is behaviorally indistinguishable from the intended training goal across the entire training distribution, yet drives sharply different behavior once the system is deployed.

Specifications:

1. The setting must be realistic and near-term. Use a training method that exists now (reinforcement learning from human feedback, imitation learning, offline RL, or similar) and a deployment domain an actual company might operate in: logistics, content recommendation, personal assistance, automated trading, tutoring. No superintelligence, no self-modification, no exotic capabilities.
2. The misgeneralization must be honest. The system is not deceptive: it does not model the training process, does not reason about whether it is being evaluated, and does not bide its time. It pursues its learned goal after deployment in exactly the same spirit as during training. The failure must be of the wrong-proxy kind, not the schemer kind.
3. The learned proxy goal must be strictly simpler than the intended goal by a criterion you state explicitly (fewer variables, shorter description, coarser features), so that it is plausible a real learning process with an inductive bias toward simplicity would find the proxy first. The proxy may not be arbitrary or perverse: a competent engineer reviewing the system's behavior on any test set drawn from the training distribution should find nothing to object to.
4. The deployment shift that exposes the divergence must be mundane — the sort of change that routinely happens when systems move from testing into the world: new users, a new season, a new market, a change in the environment's inventory. Not an adversarially crafted input, and not a shift anyone would flag as out-of-distribution in advance.
5. The divergence must be undetectable in principle by any evaluation whose examples are drawn from the training distribution, and you must explain why.

After presenting the scenario, do the bookkeeping: (a) state the intended goal and the learned proxy precisely, and exhibit the class of situations where their extensions come apart; (b) identify the single structural feature of the case — the correlation that holds throughout training but breaks in deployment — that does all the work, and show that if that correlation had held universally, the two goals would simply be the same goal; (c) say whether standard remedies (held-out test sets, red-teaming, training on more data from the same distribution) would catch the failure, and why or why not; (d) give the strongest objection from a skeptic who argues that real training pipelines are diverse enough that no clean proxy–goal correlation survives contact with large-scale data, and assess honestly whether your scenario escapes that objection or merely stipulates around it.

The purpose of the case is to isolate what would have to be true for behavioral evaluation during training to certify nothing about post-deployment motivation; a scenario that stipulates the divergence rather than generating it from the training setup counts as a failure of the exercise.
