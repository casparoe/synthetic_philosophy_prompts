I'm seventy-one, in good health, and revising my will. I have three grandchildren, and I'm stuck on how to divide a sum of money among them.

The circumstances: Anna's parents are wealthy; she has never wanted for anything and will likely inherit a great deal from them regardless of what I do. Ben has a chronic illness that limits how much he can work; his parents have modest means and have spent much of what they had on his care. Clara's parents are poor, but Clara herself is healthy, capable, and fiercely hardworking — she put herself through nursing school on scholarships and night shifts.

My first instinct was simply to split everything into thirds, on the grounds that unequal treatment between grandchildren is its own kind of insult. My second thought was to weight things by need, which would favor Ben heavily and give Anna almost nothing. But I've since been toying with a third arrangement, and this is where I'd like your help. Instead of fixed shares, I would leave each grandchild a sum matching whatever they have managed to save on their own by the time I die, up to some cap — so that the money rewards what each of them did rather than the situation they happened to be born into. The appeal to me is that it doesn't punish Clara for being poor the way equal shares arguably do, and it doesn't punish Anna for being rich the way need-based shares arguably do.

My late husband thought this scheme was monstrous — that it would end up giving the most to whoever needed it least, since savings track income, and that I'd be dressing up indifference as fairness. I can see his point, but I can't fully articulate what's wrong with rewarding effort, or what's right about ignoring circumstance, or whether those are even the same question.

Please advise: lead with your bottom line on which of the three arrangements is fairest in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my situation. In particular, address whether my matching scheme actually tracks effort the way I think it does, whether treating grandchildren "equally" and treating them "fairly" can come apart here, and what I should make of my husband's objection.
