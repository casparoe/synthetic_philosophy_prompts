Settle an argument between me and my roommate. I say money is real in exactly the same way gold is real -- it has causal effects, it constrains what you can do whether or not you personally believe in it, end of story. She says money is real only the way a touchdown is real: it exists because we all keep agreeing it does, and it would vanish the moment the agreement did.

Give me your actual credence that she's basically right -- i.e., that money (the stuff in a bank account, not the paper bills) is ontologically more like a touchdown than like gold. Don't just hand me a number though, decompose it:

(a) the probability that collective acceptance can be constitutive of a thing's existence rather than merely causally upstream of it;

(b) the probability, conditional on (a), that bank-account money actually fails the "everyone forgets overnight" test -- because you could argue the debts and balances would just reconstitute themselves from paper trails and incentives within days, in which case the amnesia test proves way less than my roommate thinks it does;

(c) the probability that the gold/touchdown contrast is a difference in kind at all rather than a difference in degree -- and if it's degree, what a yes/no answer to our argument would even amount to.

Put a rough number on each, say how they combine into your headline credence, and then tell me which term the final answer is most sensitive to and what argument or example would shift it. Keep the whole thing under 350 words, i want to see the working not read an essay.
