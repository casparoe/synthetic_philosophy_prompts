I've been circling around Rorty for a couple years now (mostly Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity, plus the Mirror of Nature stuff a while back) and I want to nail down what I actually beleive before I embarass myself in front of my reading group. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm an antirepresentationalist. Sentences don't picture the world, beliefs don't correspond to reality, and the whole idea that some of our talk gets the world right while other talk merely helps us cope is a metaphor we should drop. All beliefs are tools for coping, physics included.

2. Truth is not a goal of inquiry. You can't aim at truth because you could never know when you'd hit it; the only thing you can aim at is more and better justification, and justification is always justification to some audience.

3. At the same time, I think the predictive success of physics would be a flat-out miracle if our best theories weren't latching onto how things really are. That's basically why I trust engineers and not astrologers -- the planes stay in the air.

4. I'm an ethnocentrist about justification. I can't climb out of my late-modern liberal vocabulary onto some neutral perch, and neither can anyone else, so my liberalism has no foundations and doesn't need any. The torturer isn't irrational, he's just not one of us.

5. But I don't think the wrongness of cruelty is on a par with my taste in coffee. If my whole culture slid into fascism tomorrow, the sadists still wouldn't merely be different from us -- they'd be wrong, even if there was nobody left to say so.

6. I want to keep the ordinary gap between justified and true. A belief can be justified by everyone's best lights and still turn out false; that's just the cautionary use of true, and I'm not giving it up.

So does this package hang together? Audit it. I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which pairs only look tense but survive scrutiny, what the cheapest fix for each real tension would be and what it costs me, and which single commitment I should drop if I want the most coherence for the least loss. Reply as a numbered list of exactly five items, a sentence or two each, worst problems first -- and if you run out of genuine tensions before five, use the leftover slots for pairs that look like problems but aren't. No intro, no summary.
