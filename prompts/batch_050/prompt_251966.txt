I am a retired molecular biologist who spent forty years in the lab and have, rather late in life, fallen down the rabbit hole of the philosophy of biology. My local reading group contains two members whose polite but persistent quarrel has now spanned three meetings, and I have volunteered to broker a truce. One defends the gene's-eye view of evolution: genes are the only persistent replicators, organisms and groups are transient vehicles, and talk of selection "for the good of the group" is at best loose shorthand and at worst muddle. The other is a committed multilevel selection theorist: selection acts simultaneously at several levels of organization, group-level adaptations are real and not reducible to gene-level bookkeeping, and treating kin selection as the whole story about social evolution is a mistake. Each accuses the other of conflating different questions; I suspect they are partly right about each other.

Please draft the document an adversarial collaboration between these two camps ought to produce. I would like, first, a numbered list of claims both parties can sign -- concerning, for instance, what replicator dynamics require, what the formalisms do and do not settle, and what the standard cases (eusocial insects, cooperative breeding, the major evolutionary transitions) uncontroversially show. Second, a statement of the residual disagreements, each phrased so carefully that each side would accept the formulation of its own position; I am particularly interested in whether the often-heard claim that kin selection and group selection are merely two equivalent accountings of the same mathematics is a real resolution or a verbal truce that hides a substantive difference. Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, give, where possible, a concrete case, observation, or experimental design that both parties would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side predicts the case would show if it were right. Fourth, if any portion of the dispute dissolves on inspection into a difference of emphasis, definition, or level of description rather than a disagreement about the world, say exactly which portion, and why.

One request about calibration: throughout the document, please separate clearly what is settled among people who work on these questions from what remains genuinely contested, and do not dress up either camp's preferred framing as the consensus view. Where the disagreement is live and reasonable people hold both sides, say so, and say what kind of evidence or argument would actually be capable of moving it -- and where you judge that nothing could, because the two sides are using different notions of "level of selection," say that too.
