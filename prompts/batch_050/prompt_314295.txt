I just got home from a three-hour arguement with my friend Sam about Hume on causation, and we got nowhere. Sam is a die-hard regularity person: causation is just constant conjunction plus habit, and talk of “necessary connection” in objects is exactly the kind of metaphysical residue Hume was trying to get rid of. I keep having New Hume-ish sympathies, but Sam hit me with this sentence from Hume’s Treatise of Human Nature:

“Nothing is more usual and more natural for those, who pretend to discover anything new to the world in philosophy and the sciences, than to insinuate the praises of their own systems, by decrying all those, which have been advanced before them.”

Sam’s claim was basically: the whole New Hume debate is an example of this — people manufacturing a “new” Hume by dunking on the boring old regularity Hume. I think that’s too quick, but I also can’t fully shake it.

So here’s the actual question. Suppose our little philosophy group gets to run one term of work on this — say 8 meetings and one final essay, no archives, no rare manuscripts, just the Treatise, the Enquiry, and normal philosophical reasoning. Which open questions in the regularity / necessary connection / New Hume cluster should we prioritize, and in what order? I don’t want a survey of the debate. I want a defended agenda.

Some candidate questions, but feel free to replace them: whether Hume’s two definitions of cause can be made consistent; whether “necessary connection” is only something the mind adds or whether Hume allows real powers we just can’t know; whether the Treatise and Enquiry actually disagree here; and whether the New Hume reading changes anything downstream for induction, laws of nature, or explanation. For whatever ranking you give, explain the criteria — textual fit, payoff for the wider metaphysics of causation, tractability for people who aren’t Hume specialists, and how many other questions depend on getting this one right first. Also say which allegedly big question you think is overrated or mostly a turf war, and what would change your ranking. And yes, please actually use the quoted sentence: does it support Sam’s suspicion, or is he stretching a general jab at novelty into a bad objection?
