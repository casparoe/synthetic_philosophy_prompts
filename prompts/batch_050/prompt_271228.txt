im putting together the final for my upper-level decision theory seminar and i need one essay question that cant be answered by just defining CDT/EDT/FDT and gesturing at Newcomb. the topic is counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail, and what i really want to test is whether students can figure out if these two problems put pressure on causal decision theory in the *same* way or in two different ways that happen to look similar.

heres the setup i want the question built around (tighten the wording as needed but keep the three-part structure):

Case 1 (the mugging): Omega, a perfect predictor, flipped a fair coin yesterday. If heads, Omega would give you $10,000 iff it predicted that, in the tails branch, youd pay $100 when asked. The coin came up tails. Omega now asks you for the $100. Standard.

Case 2 (the letter): Omega sends you a letter (it sends one either way) saying it has released termites into your house iff it predicted you would refuse to pay $500 upon reading the letter. Termite extermination costs way more than $500. Standard XOR.

Case 3 (my hybrid, the part that should make the question hard): Omega flips a fair coin. Heads: nothing happens to your house, but Omega simulates you facing Case 2's letter, and if the simulation pays, Omega deposits $10,000 in your account tomorrow. Tails: you face Case 2 for real, termites and all. Omega does not tell you the coin result. In the heads branch you recieve a dummy letter with the same wording but no termites and no prediction about the real you, so the letter itself gives you no evidence about the coin. You get a letter and cant tell which branch youre in.

ask students to: (a) state what CDT, EDT, and updateless/FDT-style reasoning each recommend in Cases 1 and 2 and why, (b) analyze the hybrid from each theory's perspective, and in particular say whether the justification for paying there is the mugging-justification (subjunctive dependence between your decision and a simulation of you) or the letter-justification (evidential reasoning about termites), or somehow both, and (c) take a definite position: is there ONE principle on which a rational agent pays in all three cases, or does the hybrid show that "just pay the mugger/blackmailer" lumps together two genuinely different kinds of reason? also have them redo the verdict for Case 2 assuming Omega is only 90% accurate — i want them to actually set up the expected value comparison between the $500 and the extermination cost, not wave at it.

deliverables: the question as it would appear on the exam (90 minutes, calculators fine, no notes), a model answer of about 600 words that commits to a stance on (c) rather than surveying both sides, and a marking scheme out of 20 built on five must-hit points with the marks each is worth, plus a one-line note per point on how students typically botch it (e.g. thinking EDT pays in the mugging, or treating the hybrid as identical to Case 2 without ever mentioning the simulation link).

at the very end, after everything else, put the single must-hit point you think best discriminates an A answer from a B answer in \answer{...} so i can quickly check whether your scheme matches my sense of the question.
