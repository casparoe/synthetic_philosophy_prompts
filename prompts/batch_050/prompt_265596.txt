I was arguing with a mate about lethal autonomous weapons last week, and I got stuck on the phrase "meaningful human control", which campaigners and diplomats keep insisting on as the requirement but nobody can quite define. So I want a satirical piece that pokes at exactly that gap.

Write me a job advertisement, around 500-600 words, for the role of "Meaningful Human Controller" at an invented defence contractor (make up a suitably bland corporate name). The company makes fully autonomous targeting systems, and the successful candidate's job is to be the legally required human in the loop. Write it in completely straight-faced corporate recruitment-speak: responsibilities, essential criteria, desirable criteria, salary band, perks, the bit at the end about being an equal opportunities employer. The comedy should come entirely from the real problems with the "human in the loop" idea rather than from anything invented: the controller reviews dozens of machine-generated strike packages an hour, the interface defaults to approve and requires several clicks to reject, no-one is told what happens to controllers with high rejection rates, the job description accidentally reveals that the human's main function is to absorb legal responsibility, that kind of thing. A defence-ethics specialist should read it and recognise every objection to token human oversight -- automation bias, rubber-stamping, the vagueness of "meaningful", the human as accountability sponge -- without a single one of them ever being stated outright. Stay deadpan the whole way through: no winking at the reader, no overtly dystopian flourishes, just plausible HR prose that gets quietly worse the longer you look at it.

Please write it in British English, and keep the company and all staff fictional -- the target is the practice and the rhetoric around it, not any real firm or person.
