I'm the student representative on my university's Program Review Council, and I'm also in a history seminar on societal collapse, where we've spent weeks on whether 'collapse' is even the right category — whether Late Bronze Age palace societies, the Classic Maya, or Rapa Nui actually 'failed,' or whether what look like endings are transformations in which people, practices, and self-understandings persisted under new institutional forms. That debate is now uncomfortably relevant. The Provost has proposed dissolving the Classics Department (four tenured faculty, shrinking majors) and folding its faculty and courses into a new Ancient and Mediterranean Studies unit, ending the standalone Latin MA. The Provost's framing is 'managed adaptation'; the Classics faculty's framing is that the university would be killing a three-hundred-year-old intellectual community, whatever the new unit's nameplate says. In seminar we've been testing three candidate criteria for whether a society (or department) has survived a change: demographic continuity (do the same people keep doing the work?), practical continuity (do the distinctive practices and skills persist?), and self-ascriptive continuity (does the community still recognize itself in its successor?). Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before our vote: recommend approving the merger, approving it with conditions, or rejecting it; say which of the three continuity criteria should actually carry weight for a university and why; and give an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side rather than dismissing it. Lead with a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "recommendation" (one of the three options), "conditions" (a list of strings, empty if none), "criteria_ranked" (the three criteria in order of weight, with a one-sentence justification for each), "strongest_losing_argument" (a sentence), and "reply_to_losing_argument" (two or three sentences). Follow the JSON with the full opinion of roughly 600 words.
