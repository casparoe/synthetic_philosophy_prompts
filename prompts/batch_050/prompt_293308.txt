I don't want to do a survey of the whole debate; I want to teach one thing properly: the gradual replacement argument, i.e. the idea that swapping your neurons for functionally identical silicon components one at a time might preserve you, and the way this thought experiment is supposed to pressure people into or out of functionalism. What I'm asking for isn't an explanation of the argument for me -- I've read the standard stuff -- but a plan for how I should teach it in about 40 minutes.

Specifically: what's the dependency structure? Which ideas have to be in place before the neuron-swapping scenario does any work for this audience -- I'm guessing they need some minimal grip on functionalism as a live position (not just the word), and maybe the difference between numerical and qualitative identity, but tell me what actually comes first and why, and what I can skip without the argument collapsing. Second: the carrying example. And is there anything I should openly refuse to get into at this level -- I'm tempted to say the fading-qualia objection and questions about what the silicon is made of, but maybe one of those is actually load-bearing and I'd be gutting the argument by skipping it.
