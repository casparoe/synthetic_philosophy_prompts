I'm giving a talk at a department colloquium next month defending a stripped-down version of the conceivability argument. Instead of full philosophical zombies, I argue that "partial zombies" are ideally conceivable: molecule-for-molecule duplicates of us that behave and reason exactly like us but are missing just one kind of experience -- say, they taste nothing when drinking coffee, while still saying all the right things about its bitterness. My claim is that if even this minimal subtraction is coherent, phenomenal properties dont supervene on the physical, and property dualism follows without needing the whole zombie world. The audience is mostly physicalists: two people who work on the phenomenal concept strategy, an a posteriori physicalist who will definately reach for the water/H2O analogy, and one eliminativist who thinks the intuition of missing experience is itself an illusion. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the objections this crowd will actually raise, roughly in order of how much damage they'd do, phrased the way they'd phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is softest. And tell me which objection I'm hoping nobody asks.
