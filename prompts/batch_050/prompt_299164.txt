I'm putting together a handout for an undergrad phil-tech reading group and I want a decision tree that sorts people into positions on the reality of virtual stuff and virtual harms. Not a prose overview -- an actual flowchart they can walk through and land on a leaf.

Setup: start the tree at "somebody did something to someone in a virtual world, and it mattered. What exactly happened, and was any of it real?" From there each branch node should be one yes/no (or three-way) question, and each leaf a named position. Leaves I definitely want included:

- virtual realism (Chalmers-style: virtual objects are real digital objects, and at least some virtual harms are real harms)
- fictionalism (virtual objects are props in make-believe, events in-world are fictional truths)
- eliminativism / "it's just pixels" (there are no virtual objects at all, just screens, code, and people pretending)
- a deflationary reductionist leaf (virtual objects exist but are nothing over and above data structures plus our conventions)
- a social-constructionist leaf (virtual things are real the way money is real -- because a community treats them as real)
- whatever positions on the *harm* side you think need their own leaves (e.g., harms only count when they leak out into non-virtual consequences like money loss or trauma, vs. harms that can be fully contained in-world and still be genuine)

A few constraints:

- Build it from the logical space, not from the names. Figure out which questions generate the space of options, then place the actual positions in it. If some combination of answers is unoccupied, note it and say briefly why (incoherent? just unpopular? unexplored?).
- Every node question must be phrased so someone can answer it without knowing which position it leads to. No "do you think virtual objects are real?" -- instead use concrete cases they react to, like: a player's sword they've had for years gets deleted by the devs; someone sinks real savings into in-game currency that then crashes; two people get "married" in an MMO and one treats it as a joke; an avatar gets groped in VR; a guild leader spends 40 hours a week running their guild and the server shuts down.
- Around 6-8 branch nodes, 5-7 leaves. If two leaves can't actually be separated by any question on the tree (I'm suspicious realism and reductionism might collapse into each other on close inspection), flag that instead of papering over it with a fake distinguishing question.
- End with a one-paragraph "how to use this in a session" note.

Bonus points if the tree can also sort the annoying friend who says "it's all real, but none of it matters."
