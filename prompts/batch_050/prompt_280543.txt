Please compare the symbol grounding problem as it was classically posed for artificial cognitive systems -- roughly, the question of how the symbols inside a formal, computational system can come to mean anything on their own rather than merely being defined in terms of further ungrounded symbols, as a person stranded in a country whose language they do not speak might endlessly look up words in a monolingual dictionary of that same language -- with the form the problem takes for contemporary large language models trained exclusively on text. Begin by stating clearly what the original problem was meant to show and why it was taken to afflict purely symbolic AI in a way it arguably does not afflict human cognition, where at least some concepts are anchored through perception and action rather than through other words. Then address the contested middle ground. On one side stands the claim that a text-only model simply rides a larger and more impressive merry-go-round of intralinguistic definitions, so that whatever competence it displays is parasitic on the grounded meanings its training data had for the humans who produced it; on the other side stands the claim that next-token prediction at sufficient scale forces the model to internalize something like the structure of the world the texts describe, so that the resulting representations are grounded in a functional, if not sensorimotor, sense. For each side, give the strongest version of the argument and the strongest reply available to the other. Along the way, consider whether grounding admits of degrees or kinds -- for instance, whether being able to answer counterfactual questions about how the world would look if certain claims were false, or being able to interface with robotic perception and action after training, changes the verdict. Finally, say where the comparison genuinely breaks down: in which respects is the case of a large language model a genuinely new philosophical configuration rather than the original problem replayed at greater scale, and what, if anything, would count as a test -- conceptual or empirical -- capable of settling whether its representations are grounded?
