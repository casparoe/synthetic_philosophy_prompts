**Exam question:** Explain the role of the impartial spectator in Adam Smith's account of moral judgment in *The Theory of Moral Sentiments*. In particular: (a) how does the impartial spectator's judgment differ from the actual opinion of the people around us, and (b) what, according to Smith, happens when the two conflict?

**Student's answer:**

For Smith, all moral judgment begins in sympathy. When we observe another person's emotional state, we imagine ourselves in their situation, and to the extent that we come to feel what they feel, we approve of their emotion. Sympathy and approval are thus the same operation: to sympathize with someone's grief is precisely to judge that grief appropriate, and to fail to sympathize is to condemn it. Because no actual observer can fully inhabit another's position, we appeal to the impartial spectator, an idealized disinterested third party who observes the situation from outside and whose judgment supplies the standard of propriety. Since this spectator is an outside observer, the morality it grounds is in the first instance external to the agent: we judge our own conduct by asking how it would appear to onlookers, and moral evaluation is at bottom a matter of anticipating the judgment of others.

It follows that the impartial spectator's verdict and the verdict of actual public opinion will in general coincide, since the spectator is modeled on real observers. Where the two diverge — for example, where a person is widely blamed for an action that was in fact blameless — Smith holds that the agent should defer to the opinion of her community, because the spectator is constructed out of the judgments of real people and so cannot authorize a standard contrary to theirs. The desire for the approval of others is the foundation of conscience, and the agent who disregards actual opinion acts against the very mechanism that generates moral judgment in the first place. Smith's theory is thus in this respect a form of social relativism: propriety is constituted by what an agent's fellows can be brought to feel.
