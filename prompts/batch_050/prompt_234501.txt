After a long argument with a friend about moral responsibility and manipulation, I want to anticipate where she will land next. Her verdicts so far: an agent in a fully deterministic world who acts from her own deliberated values is responsible for what she does; someone whose urge was implanted overnight in a way that bypassed his deliberative capacities entirely is not responsible; an agent who would have been forced to act by a counterfactual intervener, but who in fact acted wholly on her own, is responsible; and a person whose entire psychological constitution was designed at conception by a manipulator who arranged that ordinary, reasons-responsive deliberation would lead him, decades later, to a particular deed is not responsible for that deed. Our new case: a government runs a social lottery that randomly assigns children to upbringing regimes deliberately engineered to instill broad dispositions such as risk-tolerance; no official intends anything for any particular child, but one man, so assigned and now fully capable of rational deliberation, embezzles from his employer. Predict my friend's verdict; identify which of the four earlier cases is the closest analog; set out the two or three principles that each fit her entire record and state where each lands here and which principle she would be adopting with each possible verdict; give your best guess with reasons; and describe the further case that would finally tell those principles apart.
