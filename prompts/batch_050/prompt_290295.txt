I've been reading LessWrong and the adjacent forums since roughly 2013, and I've noticed that the pause debate has a failure mode I find tedious: two people will argue for hours about "should we pause AI" when one is picturing an unenforceable global treaty and the other is picturing a domestic licensing regime, and one is assuming a five-year halt while the other is assuming six months. So let me pose the question the way I'd actually want it answered, with the object-level proposal specified.

The proposal I'll call the FLOP ceiling: a binding international agreement among the United States, the EU, the UK, and China under which no actor may run a training run exceeding 10^27 FLOP for five years, verified by a combination of on-chip attestation in newly fabbed accelerators and export controls on advanced lithography, with a standing joint inspection body and automatic sanctions for violators. Frontier algorithmic research continues; deployment of already-trained models continues; only new frontier training runs halt.

Give me your credence that adopting the FLOP ceiling in 2026 would reduce cumulative existential risk from AI over the period 2026–2040 by at least 20% relative to the no-agreement baseline. I want a number, and I want the decomposition. At minimum I'd expect terms for: the probability that the verification regime actually binds the major labs rather than being circumvented via shell entities, jurisdictional arbitrage, or covert state programs; the probability that frontier scaling beyond 10^27 FLOP is where the relevant danger actually lives, as opposed to algorithmic progress at fixed compute or elicitation of capabilities already present in sub-ceiling models; the probability that the pause does not backfire through compute overhang and hardware progress accumulating during the halt and being dumped into one giant run at expiry; and whatever fourth term you think I've missed that carries real weight.

Assign a rough probability to each term with a one-line justification, show how you combine them into the final number, and flag which single term your final credence is most sensitive to. Then tell me what observable evidence in the next eighteen months — not a thought experiment, something we could actually see — would move that term most. My own number is around 0.45, and a friend whose judgment I respect is at 0.85; if you can also diagnose which term most likely explains a gap that large between two people who have read the same arguments, that would be the most useful part of your answer.
