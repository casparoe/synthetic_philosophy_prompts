So I need help writing something and it's kind of out of my wheelhouse. I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training, but I somehow ended up on the exhibit committee of our local history museum (long story, I volunteered to fix their ticketing database and got recruited). We're doing a big WWII exhibit next year and the committee is deadlocked over the framing, and since I was the only one without strong feelings they made me draft the decision memo for next month's meeting. Fun.

The fight is basically this. One faction wants the exhibit built around Hitler as a person -- his biography, his speeches, his decision-making, the whole "how did this one man do it" arc, with a centerpiece being a listening room of his recorded rallies. Their argument is that visitors come in wanting to understand the man, attendance depends on it, and anyway without him specifically there's no war in 1939 and no Holocaust in that form; Germany had plenty of antisemitic nationalists but none who could actually do it. The other faction wants the exhibit to barely mention him by name -- they want it structured around the Versailles settlement, the Depression, the collapse of democratic norms, conservative elites thinking they could use him, the civil service and army going along, ordinary bureaucrats processing deportation orders. Their worry is that the Hitler-centric version teaches visitors a fairy tale where history is made by singular villains, which makes people feel safe ("we'd spot the next one") instead of looking at the boring institutional failures that are actually repeatable. They also point out our visitor survey from the WWI exhibit showed most people left remembering the Kaiser and basically nothing else.

Can you draft the memo? It needs an actual recommendation -- either pick one framing, or if you think some hybrid is defensible then specify concretely what that hybrid looks like, because "do both" is what everyone says when they don't want to decide and the exhibit space and budget only stretch one way. It also has to honestly present the strongest version of the losing side's case, in their own terms, since half the committee will read it hostile. One more wrinkle: the listening room recordings exist and a donor already paid for the acoustics, so a recommendation that kills the room entirely has to say what we do with that money. Around 800-1000 words, formal enough to circulate but not lawyer-speak.
