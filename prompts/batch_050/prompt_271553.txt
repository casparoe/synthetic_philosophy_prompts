I'm a novelist, and my current book is basically one long chain of people calling each other out, so I need to know if my gut about who gets to blame whom tracks anything real. My verdicts, as they come to me: the town gossip who's cheated on her husband twice has no business lecturing her sister for flirting with a married man, even if every word is true. A reformed embezzler, ten years out and completely open about his past, seems not just permitted but almost well-placed to tell his nephew to give the money back. A stranger who barges into a family fight to denounce the father feels out of line even when he's right on the facts. A judge who sentences a thief while dodging his own taxes seems compromised in a way a private citizen doing the same thing somehow isn't. But here's the pair I can't square: a guy who quit the scam years ago but never admitted it or made amends still strikes me as having no standing to condemn his old partner, which suggests it's not the past act but the refusal to disown it that disqualifies -- except the gossip genuinely repented in therapy and her moralizing still grates on me. Is there one principle here (repudiation? involvement? role?) or do I have to surrender a verdict? And tell me what it predicts for a scene I haven't written yet: a crew member who walked out mid-job and tipped off the police, now blaming the ones who stayed.
