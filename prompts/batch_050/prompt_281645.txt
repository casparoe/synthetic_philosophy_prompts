say Nietzsche gets dropped into the present and is asked to consult for a startup called Bloodline. The company combines consumer DNA tests, digitized church and court records, and an LLM trained on old newspapers to give customers a moral ancestry report. It estimates how many of your ancestors were conquerors, slaves, creditors, criminals, heretics, and so on, then gives you a nobility score and a guilt score. The founders make two conflicting pitches: to investors, they say the scores are objective measures of inherited rank and responsibility; to customers, they say learning that every lineage contains both victims and perpetrators will free people from Christian guilt and modern resentment.

What would Nietzsche most plausibly think of all this? Dont just say he would hate the technology or love the disruption. Reconstruct the answer from his genealogical method: the difference between tracing how moral concepts grew out of power relations and treating biological descent as a source of moral authority; why an origin-story can debunk a value without proving its opposite; and what he would make of the nobility and guilt scores. Say which parts of the product he might find genuinely useful, which parts he would see as herd morality in a scientific costume, and whether the victims-and-perpetrators-in-every-family finding would dissolve ressentiment or merely give it a new archive to work through. Also address whether he would object more to the company ranking customers or to the company promising them moral innocence. Where different strands of his thought point toward different verdicts, say so instead of forcing a neat prediction.
