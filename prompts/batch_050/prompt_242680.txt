I'm designing a seminar on whether explanations in terms of shared values can do genuine causal work in social science, in the spirit of Weber's Protestant ethic thesis, and I need a case that doesn't beg the question. Construct a thought experiment — a fictional but realistic natural experiment is welcome — that isolates culture as a cause: two populations whose institutions, capital access, legal incentives, and levels of education are held fixed or shown to be equal, differing only in the values they were raised with, such that a values-based explanation and a materialist explanation of their subsequent economic conduct make diverging predictions rather than redescribing the same outcome. Then do the checks: state what observation would vindicate the cultural explanation and what would vindicate the materialist one; identify which feature of the design prevents the materialist from absorbing the values as mere rational adaptation to incentives; say whether the case would collapse if that feature were removed; and sketch the strongest materialist countermove, e.g., that the differing values are downstream of some earlier material condition the design failed to control for.
