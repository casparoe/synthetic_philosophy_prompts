I'm a grad student helping my advisor put together a reading week for our AI safety seminar, and the topic that's landed on my plate is low-impact AI -- the whole idea that maybe we can get useful behavior out of powerful systems by penalizing "side effects" instead of fully specifying what we want. The more I read, the more I suspect the literature keeps talking past itself, because everyone has a different gut sense of what counts as an impact and nobody writes down what a formalization is even supposed to accomplish. Like, obviously a robot that avoids breaking vases by freezing all the humans so nothing ever changes has failed at the spirit of the thing, but it might be doing great by the letter of some penalty term.

So before we pick which papers to assign, I want you to write the desiderata: what any adequate formal criterion of "low impact" would have to deliver to actually do the job people want it to do. For each requirement, give the case or intuition pump behind it -- e.g. it has to not penalize the agent for effects it couldn't have foreseen (butterfly-effect stuff), it has to handle the fact that "do nothing" isn't a neutral baseline since the world changes on its own, it has to say something about effects routed through other agents, it has to not reward offsetting bad effects with elaborate compensating actions, whatever you think belongs on the list. Be opinionated about which requirements are genuine and which are just artifacts of one particular formal approach, and flag any pairs of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- if the list itself is incoherent that's a really useful finding for us.

Then, briefly, score the main families of approaches against your list -- penalizing divergence from some baseline state, penalizing changes in what other agents are able to do, penalizing shifts in the agent's own future options -- but keep that part short. The thing I actually need for the seminar is the criteria and the motivating cases, since I want the students arguing about what "impact" should mean, not just about whose formula wins. Oh and one thing I keep going back and forth on with my roomate: can any of this work without smuggling in a decent chunk of human values through the back door, or is "avoid side effects" secretly just "be good" in a trenchcoat? Work your answer to that into the desiderata somehow.
