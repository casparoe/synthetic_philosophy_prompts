I've been a longtime reader of LW-style rationality and econ blogs, and I recently got into a confusing argument about whether Kelly betting is really the same thing as what people call geometric rationality. The standard Kelly part seems clear enough: with a fixed bankroll, repeated opportunities, known odds, and no relevant constraints outside the bet, maximize expected log wealth rather than expected wealth. But my friend uses geometric rationality more broadly: choose the action with the best typical multiplicative trajectory for your whole future capacity to act, where your bankroll, health, reputation, information, social connections, and available opportunities all interact. Under that broader idea, it could make sense to pass up a positive-expectation bet if losing would knock you out of a valuable future game, or to accept a locally growth-negative action if it buys information, preserves optionality, or prevents a catastrophic absorbing state.

Please compare Kelly betting with this broader notion of geometric rationality. Is the latter a genuine generalization of Kelly, a different decision rule altogether, or just Kelly plus a lot of hidden state variables? I'm especially interested in the assumptions under which they coincide, and in where they diverge: finite versus indefinite repetition, changing opportunity sets, correlated risks, indivisible investments like education or a startup, necessary living expenses, soft versus hard ruin, and actions whose payoff is mainly information about future bets. If possible, state both decision rules precisely enough that one could tell them apart in a concrete case, then explain whether the broader version risks becoming an unfalsifiable slogan that can rationalize any choice after the fact. I suspect part of our disagreement is that one of us is treating log wealth as the fundamental objective while the other is treating it as a useful proxy under special conditions, but I can't quite pin down which assumptions make those equivalent.
