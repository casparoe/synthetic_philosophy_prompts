Write me a villanelle about Aristotle's sea battle argument from De Interpretatione ch. 9. The two refrains should basically be "either there will be a sea battle tomorrow or there won't" and "but neither disjunct is true yet" — the idea being that the form itself dramatizes the tension, since the first line keeps reasserting bivalence and dragging the second one back with it every time it returns. In the closing quatrain, resolve it (or refuse to) by pulling apart the necessity of the whole disjunction from any necessity attaching to either side. It should be accurate enough that someone who's actually worked on future contingents wouldn't wince, but still a decent poem in its own right. American English pls.
