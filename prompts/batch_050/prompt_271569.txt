so there's a debate in aesthetics that seems to me permanently stuck: the paradox of tragedy, i.e. why people voluntarily watch King Lear or Grave of the Fireflies or whatever and apparently enjoy being put through grief, pity and dread. hume's answer in Of Tragedy was roughly that the eloquence and artistry of the piece converts the painful passions into pleasure. aristotle had catharsis. since then it's been a conveyor belt of solutions: the pain is compensated by a larger pleasure that outweighs it; the pleasure is a second-order reaction to discovering in ourselves the capacity to sympathize (feagin I think is the name attached to that one); we can enjoy it because the response is under our control in a way real-life grief isn't; or the bold move that we never really feel negative emotion toward fiction at all, just convincing simulacra. every decade someone publishes a new solution as if the prevous ones didn't exist, the counterexamples are all well rehearsed, and meanwhile the psych studies keep finding that sadness ratings and enjoyment ratings for sad films correlate positively, which somehow everyone reads as confirming their own theory.

my working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: this isn't one disagreement. some authors are explaining the hedonic profile of the experience (how the net can be positive), some are explaining the choice behavior (why anyone picks the sad film over the comedy in the first place), and some are really asking a normative question about whether it's good or healthy to seek this stuff out, and they keep citing each other as if they were rival answers to a single question. the fallback hypothesis is that it's verbal, that 'enjoy' and 'pleasure' do different work on different pages.

so diagnose it. is the paradox-of-tragedy debate stalled because it's three debates wearing one coat, or is there a genuinely substantive disagreement underneath, say about the phenomenology of negative emotion or about whether taking pleasure in one's own pain is even coherent? show your working from what the main moves (conversion, compensation, meta-response, control, no-real-emotion) actually claim rather than summarizing them, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction between explananda, an experiment that would settle something, a stipulation — or argue honestly that nothing would, e.g. because the whole thing runs on a starting intuition (that no one could seek out sadness for its own sake) that neither side can argue for.

lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning. no preamble, and don't restate the question back at me.
