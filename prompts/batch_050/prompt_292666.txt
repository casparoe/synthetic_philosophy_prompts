I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind writing a dissertation on persistence through radical neural intervention, and I co-organize our department's graduate workshop on personal identity. For a year, two of our members have been relitigating Dennett's Where Am I? -- the essay in which a brain (Yorick) is placed in a vat and controls its body (Hamlet) by radio link, with a duplicate brain (Fortinbras) later introduced and the link switched between them. One member is a committed animalist in the Olson mold: she holds that you are a human animal, that you go wherever that organism goes, and that the story's first-person narration seduces readers into tracking the wrong thing. The other is a Lockean psychological-continuity theorist: he holds that you go where your memories, intentions, and character go, and that the case shows embodiment is contingent for persons. Last semester their sessions degenerated into trading intuitions about which name the narrator should use, so I have persuaded them to try an adversarial collaboration, and I need the document it should produce.

Please draft the joint statement they could both sign. It should begin with a numbered section of agreed claims: stipulations about the story both accept (including what the case as described does and does not establish), points about the limits of intuition in cases with no precedent, and any commitments about survival, death, or first-person perspective that both camps genuinely share. Follow this with a disagreements table in which each dispute is stated in wording that each side would accept as a fair statement of its own position -- no formulations that quietly presuppose the animalist's ontology or the Lockean's criterion. Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. I suspect two of these are whether first-person perspective is evidence for anything beyond the location of a point of view, and whether the story's later phase, in which the link flickers between two functional brains, forces a choice between counting one of them as the person or accepting that identity is not what matters -- but please correct me if the real cruxes lie elsewhere.

For each crux, specify a case, argument, or finding both parties would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. In addition to any standard cases you wish to include, both parties have agreed in my presence to rule on two bespoke variants of the story that I designed to force their hands, and the document must include these: first, the latency variant, in which the radio link between vat-brain and body is progressively slowed from milliseconds to a full day, with behavioral control maintained by predictive modeling on the body's end, so that at some point Yorick's decisions are being anticipated rather than executed; second, the branching variant, in which the signal is split so that Yorick simultaneously controls two healthy bodies, each receiving an identical sensory feed, until the feeds are deliberately desynchronized. For each variant, the statement should record whether the animalist and the Lockean diverge in their verdicts about where the person is and whether anyone has died, and whether either concedes the variant exposes a cost in their view. Finally, if your drafting reveals that some portion of their dispute is verbal -- disagreement about the words rather than about any possible arrangement of matter -- identify which portion and explain why both parties should be able to see it.
