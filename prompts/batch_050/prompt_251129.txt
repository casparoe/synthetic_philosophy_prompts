A friend and I have been debating which of two hypothetical worlds is better, and I'd like your help predicting her verdict. Please take on the role of a philosopher who specializes in population ethics, and think carefully about the pattern in her earlier judgments before committing to an answer.

Her track record: when I compared a world of one billion people living rich, flourishing lives with a world containing those same billion plus five billion more people living decent but unremarkable lives, she said the second world is better. When I compared a world of one billion flourishing people with a world of a trillion people whose lives are only barely worth living, she firmly preferred the first — she says she wants no part of the so-called repugnant conclusion. And when I compared one billion flourishing people with two billion people whose lives are almost as rich, only slightly less so, she again chose the larger world.

The new case: the original world of one billion flourishing people, versus a world containing those same billion plus five hundred million more whose lives are clearly worth living but drab — below the "decent" level of the first case, well above "barely worth living." Which will she say is better?

Please structure your response in exactly these sections, in this order: (1) Rules that fit her record — two or three principles consistent with all three verdicts, a few sentences each, noting any well-known principle her verdicts rule out; (2) What each rule predicts — how each surviving principle settles the new case, one paragraph total; (3) My prediction — your best guess at her verdict and why, one short paragraph; (4) The tiebreaker — one further comparison between worlds that would tell the surviving rules apart, a few sentences.
