Last week our uni philosophy society had a guest speaker on AI risk, and the centrepiece of his talk was the genie analogy -- you know the one. The alignment problem, he said, is exactly the problem you'd face making a wish to a genie that grants it maliciously-literally: the wish goes wrong not because the genie is evil but because your words don't say what you meant, and every loophole you close just opens another one. His conclusion was that alignment is essentially a drafting problem: get the specification airtight enough and a superintelligence is safe, the same way a sufficiently lawyered wish would be. Afterwards a friend of mine who does ML was grumbling that the analogy gets everything backwards, but when pressed he couldn't articulate it much beyond "models aren't like that".

Can you actually audit the genie analogy properly? Give me the explicit mapping first -- what plays the role of the wisher, the wish, the genie's perfect comprehension paired with perverse literalism, the single one-shot utterance, the genie's fixed and bounded power. Then the disanalogies, and for each one, whether it matters for the speaker's use of the analogy or is just cosmetic. Some things I suspect are load-bearing: a genie is presumed to fully understand human values and meaning and merely exploits ambiguity in the phrasing, whereas (my friend claims) the real worry is a system that never had the understanding to begin with; the wish is a one-off static text, whereas training is iterative and the objective is a proxy; and the genie can't rewrite the terms of the contract, whereas a capable system might reshape the situation so the spec no longer pins down behaviour. Does the analogy smuggle in the comforting picture that the danger lives entirely in the gap between intent and wording, i.e. that sufficiently careful language could close it? And does the "alignment = careful specification" conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the genie doing all the argumentative work? If the analogy mostly misleads, suggest what a better one would be. Please answer in British English.
