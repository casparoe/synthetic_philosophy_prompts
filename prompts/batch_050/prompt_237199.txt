So I went down a rabbit hole lately reading about the idea that consciousness might be a kind of "user illusion" -- the desktop-icons picture, where what we experience isn't the underlying machinery but a simplified interface the brain renders for its own use, and I started wondering whether my own gut intuitions about which things are conscious are really just tracking how good a system's interface is, not whether there's actually anything going on inside. So here are my verdicts on some cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, is behind them. One: an ordinary thermostat, definitely nothing it's like to be it, easy. Two: a self-driving car that maintains a detailed model of its own position, its tyre wear, its intended route and so on -- I still say nothing it's like, but I notice the self-model makes me hesitate a beat. Three: a blindsight patient who insists they see nothing in part of their visual field but guesses right well above chance -- I think there IS something it's like in that region, they've just lost access to the icon for it, so to speak. Four: a split-brain patient -- I'm fairly convinced there are two subjects in one skull, at least some of the time. Five: my dog -- obviously something it's like to be her, even though I doubt she has much of a self-model at all, no language, nothing. Six: a chatbot that gives far more articulate reports of its inner states than my dog ever could -- I say nothing it's like, full stop.

Here's the thing though: cases five and six look like they should fall together under almost any principle I can think of, and they don't, in my gut. And cases two and five both involve a self-model-ish system, yet get opposite verdicts. So: what principle (or small set of principles, stated precisely) captures all six of my verdicts, or as many as possible? Which verdict does the cleanest version of the principle force me to give up, and is that a price worth paying? And the question I actually care about -- does the principle that best fits my intuitions turn out to be one where my own introspective sense of what consciousness is (rich, qualitative, obviously present) is itself just the interface, so that my confidence in cases like these is exactly what the user-illusion picture predicts I'd feel? Please point out where my verdicts are doing that, if they are. Also tell me what the resulting principle predicts for a case I havent listed, like an octopus or a brain organoid. Answer in British English please.
