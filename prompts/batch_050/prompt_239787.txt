I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar in philosophy of biology and would like a decision tree that sorts the positions on what counts as a biological individual. The seminar's motivating question is whether entities like holobionts, clonal colonies, and insect colonies are organisms in the same sense as a paradigmatic animal, so the tree should take a reader from "a multicellular animal such as a mouse is clearly one organism" to a named position on where the boundaries of organismhood fall.

Please construct the tree from the logical space of possible answers rather than from a list of known theories, and only afterward locate the actual positions within it. No more than eight branching questions, and each branch node must be phrased as a question a reader can answer from a concrete case without knowing which position the answer leads to. The cases the questions must be built on are: a honeybee colony, a Portuguese man o' war (and siphonophores generally), a cellular slime mold that aggregates from free-living amoebae, a lichen, a human together with their gut microbiome, a quaking aspen clone connected by a shared root system, and a chimeric laboratory mouse grown from two fused embryos. A reader who has never heard of immunological or evolutionary accounts of individuality should still be able to answer every question just by deciding what they want to say about these cases.

Among the leaves, the following kinds of position must appear, though you may subdivide or add others if the questions force them: a view on which only entities with tight physiological or metabolic integration count, so none of the borderline cases qualify; a view on which anything functioning as a unit of selection counts, so insect colonies and perhaps aspen clones are organisms; a view centered on policing mechanisms that suppress conflict among the parts, which treats the honeybee colony and the slime mold very differently; a view centered on the immune system's discrimination of self from non-self, which has distinctive consequences for the human-with-microbiome case; a holobiont-friendly view on which host plus symbionts form a single individual; and a deflationary or pluralist view on which "organism" tracks different things in different explanatory projects and the boundary question has no unique answer.

For any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, say so explicitly and explain what further question would be needed. For each coherent combination of answers that no one appears to occupy, mark it as unoccupied and diagnose briefly whether the gap exists because the position is unstable, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

Format the result as the handout itself: begin directly with the tree (indented text or a numbered flowchart is fine), with no preface, no framing remarks, and no commentary before or after.
