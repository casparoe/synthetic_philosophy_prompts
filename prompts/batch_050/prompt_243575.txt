I help run a small irrigation district — roughly sixty farms drawing from one river, governed by an elected board, funded by annual assessments, and operating a ditch system older than anyone alive. I have no academic background, but I've been reading on my own about proposals to cool the planet by releasing reflective particles into the upper atmosphere, and I keep reaching for my own working vocabulary to think about it. I'd like you to do that translation carefully and systematically.

Take the three issues the commentary keeps circling — moral hazard (the cheap fix lets everyone postpone the hard one), termination shock (stop the intervention abruptly and the warming arrives all at once), and the question of who gets to decide — and restate each one in the vocabulary I actually use: water shares, headgates, ditch riders, the annual meeting, assessments, senior and junior rights, downstream users, drought years, the aquifer we all quietly overdraw. For context: some years back our district bought emergency water from the city during a drought, and for a decade afterward nobody could be persuaded to vote for relining the leaking main canal; when the contract lapsed, we were worse off than before. I suspect that single episode already contains two of the three issues, and I'd like you to say whether I'm right.

Then give me a residue report: which parts of the geoengineering debate have no counterpart in district governance? I can already see candidates — there is no selling your shares and leaving the atmosphere; everyone downstream is a member whether they consented or not; some of the affected parties haven't been born; and no board exists with the authority our board takes for granted. For each piece of residue, tell me plainly whether my vocabulary is missing something important, or whether the debate itself is less clear than it looks.
