I've been teaching moral luck in my undergrad ethics seminar again this semester, and every year I run into the same annoyance. The standard taxonomy (I'm thinking of Nagel's four-way division) gives you resultant luck — you're more blameworthy if your drunk driving actually kills someone than if you make it home fine, even though the difference is pure luck; circumstantial luck — you never faced the temptation or the hard case that would have revealed you as a coward or a collaborator; constitutive luck — you didn't choose your temperament, your impulsiveness, your capacity for empathy; and causal luck — the whole your-actions-are-upstream-products-of-stuff-you-didn't-control problem, which bleeds into free will. Fine. But my students keep generating cases that don't slot neatly into any of the four, and I've gotten tired of saying "well, it's kind of a mix of circumstantial and resultant" when it doesn't feel like that's what's going on.

So here's my request: give me a candidate fifth kind of moral luck — a way that luck can bear on moral assessment that is genuinely not a special case, combination, or rewording of the four above. I'm not asking for a fifth case study that fits one of the boxes; I'm asking for a box. To give you a sense of what I mean by "genuinely different": a student last week pointed out that whether we blame someone often depends on what the social norms around them later become — the mid-century executive who did nothing unusual for his era but gets retroactively condemned once norms shift. Is that just circumstantial luck about when you were born, or is the retrospective element doing something the four categories don't capture? That's the kind of move I'm looking for, though feel free to argue that one fails and go somewhere else entirely.

Also, once you've proposed your fifth kind, please stress-test it: try to collapse it back into the four, and tell me honestly whether it survives. And if you think Nagel's taxonomy is actually exhaustive (or that the right response is to subdivide one of the existing categories rather than add a new one), I'd rather hear that argued than get a forced novelty.
