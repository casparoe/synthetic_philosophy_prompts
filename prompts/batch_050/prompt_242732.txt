So I'm a backend engineer, zero formal philosophy background, but I've been totally sucked into this rabbit hole after a friend made me read about Hofstadter's superrationality stuff -- the Platonia dilemma (20 people, each told that if exactly one of them volunteers they all get rewarded, no communication allowed) and the Luring Lottery he ran through his magazine column back in the day, where the prize gets divided by the number of entries so the "smart" move seems to be not entering even though entering is free. What hooked me is that this maps weirdly well onto stuff I actually think about at work, like: if I know the other process is running literally the same decision algorithm as me, does that change what I should compute? Apparently people have been arguing about this since the 80s and it seems completly unresolved.

From what I can tell there are basically two camps. One camp says: in a symmetric one-shot game where you *know* everyone reasons the same way you do, you should cooperate / stay out / not enter, because whatever conclusion you reach, everyone else reaches it too, so you should pick the conclusion you'd want everyone to reach. The other camp says that's just confusing correlation with causation -- your entering the lottery doesn't *make* anyone else enter, so entering strictly dominates, and the symmetry is only evidence about what others will do, not a lever on it. My working theory is that this whole thing is secretly just the evidential vs causal decision theory fight wearing a funny hat, and the "superrationality" label is doing no real work. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a genuine third position here about what rationality even means in symmetric situations.

Act as a decision theorist who's spent years thinking about Newcomb-style problems and symmetric games. Think carefully and actually take both sides seriously before diagnosing -- don't just declare a winner. What I want is a diagnosis of why this debate has stalled, not a summary of the positions: is it a substantive disagreement about what reasons are, a verbal dispute about the word "rational", the EDT/CDT fight in disguise, or two camps answering different questions under one name? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress -- a formal result, a cleaner distinction, an experiment, or is this one of those things that just can't be settled?
