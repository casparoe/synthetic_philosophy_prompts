I'm a philosophy PhD student who somehow ended up in the "ethics seat" on our university's animal care and use commitee. We've got a protocol where a neuro lab wants to run a fairly invasive learning-and-memory study, and they say the science works equally well in rats or in crows, so the choice is ours. Our standing policy tells us to prefer the "neurally simpler" species, and in past meetings the committee has just used relative brain size as the proxy for that — which ranks crows way below rats and would basically rubber-stamp using the birds. The problem is the lab's own background section notes that birds pack far more neurons into a gram of brain tissue than mammals do, so if what we actually care about is cognitive capacity rather than raw size, crows arguably sit up near primates rather than below rodents. Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote: recommend which animal the committee should require (or whether we have to rewrite the policy before we can decide anything), say what brain size is even supposed to be evidence *for*, and take seriously the worry from one member that once we abandon the simple size rule, every lab will shop for whichever metric gets them the animal they already wanted.
