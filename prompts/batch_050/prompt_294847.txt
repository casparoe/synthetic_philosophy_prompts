I’m preparing a seminar handout on Goodhart’s law and specification gaming, and I’d like you to write a satirical internal onboarding manual for employees of the fictional Department of Civic Flourishing. The department has replaced the vague goal of making a city worth living in with a single Resident Vitality Index, assembled from measurable indicators such as average park shade, school improvement, emergency-room waiting time, housing satisfaction, and commuter calm. An automated planning system proposes policies, human officials implement them, and your manual is written after several years in which every indicator has improved while the city itself has become subtly worse. Write in the serene, procedural voice of an organization that sincerely believes these outcomes are compatible.

Make the manual about 800 words, with sections such as orientation, choosing the most improvable definition, responding to unintended consequences, preparing for audit week, and congratulating teams whose numbers have risen. Include at least one invented form or protocol, one policy that initially appears beneficial before its costs become clear, and one passage in which staff are warned against manipulating the data too crudely because sophisticated optimization preserves institutional trust. The satire should bring out proxy substitution, measurement capture, threshold harvesting, burden shifting, and the way audits become targets, but through concrete bureaucratic absurdities rather than a lecture. Do not mention Goodhart’s law by name, use real organizations or people, or append citations; the reader should recognize the underlying failure because every cheerful procedure follows it faithfully.
