My roommate and I have been having this running argument all semester about exploitation -- it started with sweatshops and kind of metastasized into everything. She's read a bit of the ethics literature (Wertheimer and that crowd) and she has very confident verdicts on individual cases but won't commit to a general principle, which drives me nuts. Her track record so far: a sweatshop paying the best wage available in the region, but way less than the value of what the workers produce, with the owner knowing the workers have no better option -- exploitative. A payday lender charging huge interest to people who can't get credit anywhere else -- exploitative. A rich tech guy paying his housecleaner the standard local rate, where she has plenty of other clients and sets her own hours -- not exploitative. A hardware store tripling the price of bottled water after a hurricane when there's no other source in town -- exploitative. A collector buying a broke grad student's rare record collection for a third of what it's worth because he knows she needs rent money this week -- exploitative. And refusing to deal with someone at all (like a company that just never sets up in a poor region) -- not exploitation, since you can't exploit someone by ignoring them.

Here's the new case I want to spring on her, and I want you to predict how she'll rule before I do: a private mountain rescue outfit gets a call from a stranded climber whose only real options are pay up or spend a night in genuinely dangerous conditions. The outfit charges him exactly their standard commercial rate -- the same price they charge everyone, enough to cover costs plus a normal profit margin, no gouging markup. Does she say this is exploitation or not? I'm less interested in the verdict itself than in the reasoning: tell me which rule she's most plausibly been running all along (unfair division of the gains from the transaction? profiting off someone's desperate situation regardless of whether the terms are fair? something else?), show me that at least two plausible rules fit all six of her past verdicts and then diverge on the rescue case, and give me your best guess about which one is actually hers and why. Also: what's the one follow-up case I could put to her that would settle which rule she's running -- i.e., a case where the candidate rules clearly predict opposite verdicts?
