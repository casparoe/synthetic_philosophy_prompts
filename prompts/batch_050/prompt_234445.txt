I am recording a long-form comparative-philosophy podcast with the author of a forthcoming book on Nāgārjuna and Madhyamaka. Her central claim is deflationary: the tetralemma is not a menu of four metaphysical positions, with Nāgārjuna rejecting all four in favor of some inexpressible fifth position. Rather, each limb fails because it treats a dependently designated thing as though it were an independently identifiable subject possessing intrinsic properties. On her reading, emptiness is a discipline for recognizing and withdrawing that assumption, while ordinary causal, ethical, and epistemic claims remain usable at the conventional level. She repeatedly insists that this is neither nihilism nor a doctrine according to which ultimate reality lies beyond all language.

Draft a sequence of ten to twelve interview questions for a sixty-minute conversation. The sequence should begin by forcing her to distinguish her position from nearby misreadings, then move into progressively more difficult philosophical territory. The questions should collectively address:

- the difference between denying a proposition, treating its presuppositions as failed, and refusing the conceptual framework in which it is posed;
- why the rejection of both and neither in the tetralemma does not commit her to dialetheism, classical bivalence, or mystical ineffabilism;
- whether the distinction between conventional and ultimate truth amounts to two kinds of truth, two standards of assertion, or merely two vocabularies;
- how emptiness can apply to itself without either reintroducing an absolute or making the entire account self-undermining;
- what dependent origination explains if neither causes nor effects possess independent natures;
- a concrete case involving the persistence of a person through time, where her account must say more than that the person is conventionally real;
- whether her therapeutic interpretation leaves any substantive disagreement between Madhyamaka and rival Buddhist schools;
- how much weight philosophical argument can bear if emptiness is ultimately supposed to be realized through meditative practice;
- why relinquishing intrinsic existence should produce compassion rather than ethical detachment or practical indifference;
- what textual or philosophical evidence would count most strongly against her interpretation.

For each question, include a brief note identifying the pressure it is meant to create, the answer she is most likely to give, and a follow-up that prevents her from escaping through a stock distinction. In particular, do not let her repeatedly invoke conventional truth, dependent designation, or soteriological purpose without explaining the relevant mechanism. End with a question inviting her to identify the strongest unresolved objection to her own view.

Phrase the questions as they could actually be asked aloud: precise but not needlessly jargon-laden, with Sanskrit terms glossed where used. Avoid biographical questions, requests for bibliographies, and debates about the views of particular modern commentators.
