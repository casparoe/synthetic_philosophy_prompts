I am a retired chemist who has lately been reading macrohistory, including Peter Turchin's work on so-called secular cycles, and I keep running into the old proverb 'history repeats itself.' It was last invoked at my reading group to defend the claim that cliodynamics -- extracting regularities from historical data and using them to forecast political instability -- is genuine science rather than numerology dressed up with statistics. I suspect the proverb is being made to carry more weight than it can bear, but I cannot yet say exactly why. Could you lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise theses that 'history repeats itself' might be expressing -- for instance, strict cyclical regularity, recurrence of structural conditions, mere statistical similarity across cases, or something weaker still about human nature -- and for each say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and what kind of evidence could in principle test it? Then tell me which of these readings cliodynamics actually needs for its forecasting claims to hold up, and which reading my reading-group friend was probably relying on. Finally, please state how confident you are in your verdicts and single out the one point in your answer you are least sure of.
