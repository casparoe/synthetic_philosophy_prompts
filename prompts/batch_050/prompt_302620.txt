I’m a final-year undergrad taking a course on value theory and practical reason, and I’m trying to write an essay on hard choices without just rehearsing the usual examples. Before I compare any positions, I want a proper specification of what an adequate theory of hard choices should actually be able to do.

Treat the purpose as helping agents choose rationally between options involving values that may be incommensurable, not merely as explaining why the choices feel difficult. Give me a numbered list of desiderata, with the philosophical motivation behind each one. Among other things, please consider whether a good theory must:

- distinguish genuine value conflict from uncertainty, incomplete information, vagueness and conflicting reasons;
- explain how two options can be hard to compare without it following that nothing at all can be said for or against either;
- give an account of permissibility and of when choosing arbitrarily is acceptable;
- make sense of phenomena such as parity, rough equality, moral residue, regret, partiality and long-term commitment;
- handle both personal choices and public-policy choices without assuming that one person’s values can always be traded against another’s;
- respect plausible constraints on “better than”, “equally good” and “at least as good as”, including what happens when options are added or removed;
- explain the phenomenology of hard choices without over-privileging what the choice feels like from the inside;
- avoid implying that all plural values are incomparable, or conversely that some hidden common measure must exist.

For each desideratum, explain which intuitions or cases it is meant to protect, and classify it as either non-negotiable, strongly desirable but revisable, or mainly a prejudice of one theoretical camp. If you think some of the desiderata cannot all be satisfied together, say exactly where the pressure is. I’m especially interested in whether a theory can preserve both meaningful incomparability and rational guidance, and whether regret and commitment can be accommodated without turning the theory into a psychology of whichever chooser is most sensitive.

At the end, briefly test the list against three broad approaches: one treating some options as simply incomparable, one using rough equality or parity, and one allowing plural values but letting certain values trump others. Keep this scoring brief; the desiderata are the main thing. No bibliography or citations, please, since I just want the conceptual map.

Be explicit about how confident you are in the overall specification, and single out the one criterion or conclusion you are least sure of.
