write me a villanelle about the money-pump argument for transitivity, from the point of view of the agent with cyclic preferences -- she prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A. one refrain should be her insisting, each time, that the trade on offer is an improvement by her own lights, and the other should be the pump operator calmly noting that she keeps ending up exactly where she started, only poorer. the form should do real work: the refrains coming back every few stanzas *is* the cycle, and in the closing quatrain, where the two refrains finally stand side by side, she should feel the contradiction land -- that every step was rational and that she was guaranteed to lose. someone who knows the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization should be able to tell that transitivity is the requirement on trial here (not independence or continuity or anything fancier), and that the argument is diachronic -- the pump only gets you one small trade at a time, never all at once. just the poem itself, no title, and no commentary or explanation after it.
