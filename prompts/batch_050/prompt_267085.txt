Is there an established name for this pattern? I keep tripping over it and I've only ever seen it discussed as a side-effect of other puzzles, never as its own thing.

Instance one, the classic setup: charity gala, two envelopes, one contains twice as much money as the other. You pick one, open it, find $200, and the organizer lets you swap. The other envelope has $100 or $400 with equal chance, so swapping gains you $100 on average. Fine so far. But notice: whatever amount you'd found, that same calculation says swap. You could run the reasoning before opening the envelope at all, and it says swap blind. And once you've swapped blind, the same reasoning says swap back. People argue endlessly about where the swapping argument goes wrong, but what interests me is the weird property underneath that lets it get started: for every possible content of your envelope, switching looks good, while "always switch" is obviously pointless because the envelopes are symmetric.

Instance two, different flavor, same smell: a friend says, suppose nature picks a positive whole number "at random" (never mind how), and you get to bet on whether it's bigger than a trillion. Before anything is drawn you should be near-certain it is, because for any particular number nature might pick, almost all numbers are bigger. Same for a quadrillion. Yet you're also dead sure the number drawn will be some specific writable-down finite thing. Each of those judgements looks correct on its own and the package is absurd.

So the pattern I want a name for: a verdict that holds in every individual case you might find yourself in, while the overall verdict you'd give in advance goes the other way, or the whole set of judgements is incoherent. It's not just "counterintuitive probability" like the Monty Hall thing, because there the right answer is merely surprising, not self-defeating. And it's not exactly the two-envelope paradox itself, which is about diagnosing the swapping argument, whereas I'm asking about the deeper structural feature.

Does this have a name in probability theory or decision theory? If it's only ever treated as a footnote to the envelope puzzle, tell me that honestly. Also: where are the edges? Does the phenomenon essentially require infinity or unbounded payoffs, or can you build a strictly finite example? And if there's genuinely no established term, propose one -- with a definition tight enough that I can test candidate cases against it, plus one or two boundary cases where you'd say it stops applying.
