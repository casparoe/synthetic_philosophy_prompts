So I'm a software engineer (backend stuff, mostly distributed systems) with basically zero formal philosophy training, and I've been chewing on Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment for a few weeks now and it won't leave me alone. For anyone who doesn't know it: picture a huge box of marbles rattling around, but the marbles come in clusters that form symbols, and when you watch at the zoomed-out level the symbols seem to do things to each other — one symbol's configuration triggers another, which triggers another — even though, zoomed in, it's just marbles bouncing off marbles following plain old mechanics. Hofstadter calls the symbol-level entities simmballs or something like that. The bit that gets me: in what sense do the symbols push the substrate around, if every marble's trajectory is fully fixed by physics at the marble level?

This bugs me professionally too. When I say the race condition in the payment service caused the outage, nobody thinks there are little "race condition forces" acting on the electrons in the server rack. But also the explanation genuinely lives at the architecture level — you can't find it by staring at packet captures. So something is going on with levels of causation and I don't know what.
