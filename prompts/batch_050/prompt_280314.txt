So I've somehow ended up volunteering to run a session for my local library's philosophy night (long story, I lost a bet) on "coherent extrapolated volition" — the idea that instead of asking what people want right now, an AI should aim at what we'd want if we were smarter, better informed, more the people we wish we were. Audience is maybe fifteen people, mixed retirees and book-club types, no philosophy background, forty minutes, and half of them will arrive assuming this is about the machines taking over. Don't give me the explanation itself — plan how I should give it. What has to be in place before the idea even parses for them (my worry: if I start with the AI stuff, the volition part never lands), the one everyday example to carry the whole thing — I was thinking about something like what you'd want your future self to choose about smoking, but tell me if that's wrong and what would be better — the misconceptions they'll form and in what order (I'm guessing: "so it's just a fancy poll," "whoever programs it picks the idealization," and "extrapolated me is a stranger, why should I care what he wants"), where exactly in the sequence to head each off, and one question I can ask at the end that would tell me whether they got the distinction between what we want and what we want to want, rather than just nodding along. Also what I should flat-out refuse to get into at this level and say so out loud.
