So I've been playing around with a little taxonomy of conversational moves and I want a second opinion on it. I've invented five moves below, each with a made-up example. Using Lewis's scorekeeping picture of conversation and the Stalnakerian idea of common ground (plus accomodation), rank the five moves from MOST dependent on the hearer silently accommodating a presupposition to LEAST dependent, and give one or two sentences per move explaining why it lands where it does. Then, at the end, tell me which move you think most threatens to break the whole ranking — i.e. where the "how much accomodation is needed" scale gets mushy or starts conflating two different things.

The moves:

1. Piggyback: slipping a loaded presupposition inside a subordinate clause while the main assertion is about something else. E.g.: "After Dave stopped cheating on his taxes, he finally got that promotion."

2. Overt bid: explicitly flagging that you're asking the common ground to be updated before proceeding. E.g.: "Okay, just assume for a minute that ghosts are real — what would that mean for house prices?"

3. Reclaim: using a presupposition you know the hearer accepted at some earlier point in the conversation but has since backed away from. E.g., an hour after they admitted the plan was doomed: "When the plan fails, who's gonna take the fall?"

4. Park: openly acknowledging a potential presupposition while refusing to let it on the record, then continuing anyway. E.g.: "Whether or not your brother is actually a doctor — and I'm not conceding he is — he still shouldn't be handing out medical advice at parties."

5. Smuggle-and-anchor: asserting p outright, then immediately saying something that only makes sense if p had already been common ground all along. E.g.: "The offsite got moved to Lisbon. Anyway, as we've always agreed, nobody flies business class."

One extra wrinkle I'd like you to address: does your ranking change if the hearer is cooperative vs. hostile? If yes, give me both rankings; if you think it shouldn't change, say why not.
