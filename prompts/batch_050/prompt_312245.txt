I'm a retired marine biologist who wandered into a philosophy reading group in my sixties, so excuse me if I misuse the jargon. Last week one member announced that 'survival of the fittest' is an empty tautology: fitness is just defined by who survives, so the slogan says nothing more than that the survivors survive, and natural selection explains nothing. I tried to push back that in my old field we could often estimate which organisms were fitter before seeing who actually made it -- faster swimmers, better camouflage, more offspring per brood -- but I couldn't articulate the difference in a way he accepted, and I have to admit his version did sound a bit like what some textbooks say. Could you seperate out the different precise claims that slogan might be making -- a bare definition, a disguised tautology, an empirical prediction about who survives and why, maybe something about tendencies or probabilities rather than guarantees -- and say for each whether it's trivial, substantive, or plain false? And then tell me which reading the tautology objector needs the slogan to have for his objection to bite, and whether that's the reading working biologists actually rely on. No headings, bullet points, or tables please -- just continuous prose, the way you'd write a letter.
