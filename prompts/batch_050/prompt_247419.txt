Here is the student's answer, reproduced verbatim: "Egalitarians hold that it is bad in itself if some people are worse off than others. Parfit calls this telic egalitarianism, since it treats inequality as making the outcome itself worse, unlike deontic egalitarianism, which objects instead to unjust inequality in how people come to be treated. The levelling-down objection then runs as follows. Suppose one group is better off than another through no fault or merit of anyone. If equality has intrinsic value, we could improve things by levelling down: making the better-off worse off until everyone is equal, even though nobody benefits in the process. Since egalitarianism is committed to regarding this levelled-down state as straightforwardly better, the view has an absurd implication and must be rejected. Parfit's Priority View avoids this result. On that view, benefiting people matters more the worse off those people are relative to others: a unit of benefit counts for more when it goes to someone near the bottom of the distribution than when it goes to someone near the top. Levelling down benefits no one, and since all the weights are positive, dragging the better-off down to the lower level makes the outcome worse overall rather than better. The Priority View thus escapes the objection while retaining the egalitarian intuition that we ought to care especially about the worse off, and Parfit accordingly presented it as a more defensible form of egalitarianism — one that captures what egalitarians really care about without the absurd commitment to levelling down."
