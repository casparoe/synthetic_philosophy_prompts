I'm a novelist, and the book I'm working on has gotten itself tangled up in an anthropic reasoning problem, of all things. The setup: my main character is a statistician who's part of a small group of people facing some uncertainty about how many observers there are in their situation, and the plot hinge is that the rational move for her depends on whether she reasons with something like the self-sampling assumption (reason as if you're a random sample from the actual observers) or the self-indication assumption (weight toward worlds with more observers like you). I had a long argument about this with a friend who does philosophy of cosmology and she kept saying the SIA side gets accused of being "presumptuous," which is exactly the kind of accusation I want another character to throw at my protagonist at a dinner party. So I need a case to build the novel's central scene around.

Here's what I'm after: construct me a thought experiment where SSA and SIA give clearly different credences, and where that difference actually changes what a character should do -- a bet she takes, a risk she walks into, something with stakes, not just a number on a page. Constraints: it has to be dramatizable in a realist novel, so no perfect duplicates, no amnesia serums, no sleeping-beauty memory erasure (that one's been done to death anyway), no brains in vats or universe simulations. Mild idealization is fine -- coin flips, a drug trial, a lottery, a weird bureaucratic procedure -- stuff a reader could believe happening in a slightly heightened version of the real world. Keep the scenario itself to a paragraph or so.

Then do the work of showing it works: state what credence SSA gives and why, what credence SIA gives and why, and trace how each one pushes my protagonist toward a different choice. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing all the work, and what happens to the divergence if I cut that feature (useful to know, since my editor will want things simplified). And finally, since the dinner party scene needs both sides to sound smart: give me the strongest way for the SSA character to accuse the SIA character of being presumptuous, and the best comeback the SIA character has. I want the reader to leave the scene genuinely unsure who won.
