I'm a high school junior and I run our school's philosophy club: about a dozen students, and we meet for one 45-minute lunch period each week. Next month I want to lead a session on the idea that competence can arise without comprehension — the sense in which Darwin showed that natural selection produces brilliantly functional design with no designer behind it, and Turing showed that mechanical rule-following can produce what looks like intelligent behavior, each overturning the traditional assumption that understanding must come first and skill second. Rather than lecture on this, I'd like an activity or game where club members experience the inversion themselves: for instance, by following purely mechanical rules that let them produce output that seems to require understanding, or by building up something sophisticated from mindless local steps. Please give me concrete rules, any materials I need to prepare, and a way to divide the 45 minutes. Include a debrief plan that maps specific moments in the activity onto the Darwin and Turing cases, warn me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (especially with teenagers who may figure out the trick early or refuse to take the rules literally), and tell me honestly which part of the idea the activity can't capture and I'll have to supply in discussion.
