so i write the science-and-ideas column for a mid-size magazine and my editors want something funnier than usual for the winter issue. write me an Onion-style news report, maybe 600-700 words, deadpan wire-service voice, about a research team at an invented university announcing they've confirmed the hypothesis "all ravens are black" without once looking at a raven -- they spent three years and a decent-sized grant cataloguing non-black non-ravens instead (green apples, white sneakers, a yellow school bus, one very cooperative flamingo).

the catch: every joke has to come from the actual logic of Hempel's raven paradox, not from a cartoon version of it. the universal claim and its contrapositive ("all non-black things are non-ravens") are logically equivalent, so evidence for one is evidence for the other -- and the team's methodology has to be airtight *by the paradox's own lights*. the comedy should be that it is. a specialist reading this should think ok, the reporter definately did their homework.

a few beats i want in there:

- a quote from the lead researcher explaining, totally straight, that each pink flamingo they log raises the hypothesis's credibility
- one exhausted grad student who keeps pointing out they could just... go outside and find an actual raven, and keeps getting overruled on methodological grounds
- a statistician (outside expert, quoted for balance) who concedes the evidence does technically confirm the hypothesis, but by such a minuscule amount that the team would need to catalogue roughly every non-black object in the universe to get anywhere -- and the team treating this as a funding pitch rather than an objection
- the piece should end by landing on the real puzzle -- if the logic checks out, why does the whole project feel insane -- without the narrator ever breaking the deadpan or editorializing

don't let the article wink at the reader, and don't misstate the paradox to make it punchier. if the funny version and the correct version conflict, correct wins.
