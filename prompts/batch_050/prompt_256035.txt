My dad is convinced a massive stock market crash is coming, and whenever I push back he just says "history repeats itself" and points at 1929 or 2008 like that settles it. This has stopped being an abstract debate because he's now pressuring me to pull my savings out of index funds and buy gold instead, and honestly the phrase is doing ALL the work in his argument. He never actually argues for anything, he just says it slower.

The problem is I can't figure out what "history repeats itself" even claims. Taken literally it's obviously false — 2008 wasnt 1929, nothing ever happens twice in exactly the same way. So there must be a bunch of weaker and stronger things the slogan could mean, and my suspicion is that the versions that are true are too weak to support his conclusion, and the versions strong enough to support his conclusion aren't true. But I can't prove that to him (or to myself, fully).

So here's what I want: lay out the different precise claims hiding in that one sentence, from the most trivial to the most ambitious. I have a vague sense that the weak end is something like "people keep making the same kinds of mistakes" and the strong end is something like "there are actual laws of history that make certain outcomes inevitable" (I think I've heard that Karl Popper attacked that kind of idea somewhere, but I don't know the details). I want the full menu with everything in between. For each reading, tell me: is it a platitude, is it a substantive claim that might well be true, or is it something we have reason to think is false? And then the key part — which of these readings, if any, would actually license my dad's move from "history repeats itself" to a concrete, timed prediction about my portfolio?

Bonus question: he also loves saying that "this time it's different" is the most dangerous sentence in investing. That seems like the same slogan wearing a fake mustache, but doesn't combining the two make his position completely unfalsifiable? Heads the past repeats, tails this time is dangerously different.

Format it as a list I can actually bring to Sunday dinner.
