I am trying to codify when resentment is the right response rather than when punishment is merely useful, and I want you to extract the principle from my verdicts rather than survey theories. Treat this as Strawsonian in spirit but do not cite texts or give a bibliography. My cases: an adult colleague knowingly takes credit for my work to win a bonus; I resent him and think an apology plus restitution is owed. A sleepwalking neighbor damages my car; I want compensation but resentment would be confused. A person with a credible gun to their head helps launder money; I do not resent them, though I still think they may be barred from handling funds. A psychopathic fraudster understands every rule, predicts consequences, feels no guilt, and ruins people calmly; I find myself unable to demand remorse from him, yet I still want him excluded from trust and I resist saying he is simply like the sleepwalker. A teenager raised by a cult to despise outsiders commits one cruel act after leaving; I feel some blame, but less than toward the colleague, and I think rehabilitation matters more than condemnation. A corporation’s pricing algorithm foreseeably blacklists poor applicants while no individual intends it; I want sanctions, audits, and changes, but calling the firm resentable in the same way as the colleague seems category confusion. A depressed friend cruelly insults me during a crash and later recognizes it; I excuse the insult but not a pattern of refusing treatment that reliably produces such episodes. Finally, an ordinary adult who is merely exhausted snaps once; I think the outburst is blameworthy but weakly, because the capacity to do better remained available.

The pair I cannot reconcile is the coerced money launderer and the psychopathic fraudster: I withhold resentment from both, but for what seem like opposite reasons, one because normal choice was overwhelmed and one because the normal address of conscience finds no receiver. I also tense at saying the corporation is only a target for management while the cult-raised teenager is partly a participant in moral address. Give me one committed principle, stated with explicit clauses, that sorts exemption, excuse, mitigation, and mere management; attach each clause to the case that forces it; say where it distinguishes fitting resentment from permissible sanction, backward-looking desert from forward-looking protection, and impaired capacity from corrupted values. Then name which of my verdicts you would overturn if the principle is to stay simple, defend that cost, and state the verdicts your principle gives for two unlisted cases: a well-informed adult whose selfishness was deliberately engineered by a manipulative partner over years, and an autonomous system that can revise its goals in response to criticism but has no affective life. Take a definite position; do not leave me with a menu.
