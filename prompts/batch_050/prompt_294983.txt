Ok so I got into this genuinely heated argument with my friend Marcus last weekend about Fear and Trembling (we're both TAing an upper-level seminar on 19th century continental thought this semester, and yes we are insufferable at parties). His take was that Johannes de silentio's whole "teleological suspension of the ethical" thing basically boils down to "if God tells you to do it, normal morality is off," full stop. I kept saying that's way too flat -- that the suspension has to be a suspension of the ethical understood specifically as the universal, and that Abraham has to be distinguishable from both the tragic hero AND from some guy who just thinks God told him to do something terrible, otherwise the book is just an apologia for fanatics. Marcus said the distinguishing-from-fanatics problem is unanswerable and Kierkegaard knows it. We did not resolve it lol.

So I want to turn this into an exam question for the final. Here's what I'm after: write one essay question on the teleological suspension of the ethical that students could answer in about 75 minutes, designed so they CAN'T answer it just by reciting the three problemata or defining "knight of faith" vs "tragic hero." Specifically, the question should force them to (a) explain what the ethical actually is for Johannes -- i.e. why "the ethical as the universal" matters and why the suspension isn't just moral relativism with extra steps, and (b) grapple with the epistemic problem Marcus raised: Johannes seems to concede that from the outside there's no mark that distinguishes Abraham from a delusional murderer, since faith is by definition paradoxical and can't be made intelligible in universal terms. Is that a fatal objection to the whole framework, or is the point that the question itself is badly posed because it tries to judge faith from within the universal?

Then give me a model answer of roughly 600 words and a marking scheme worth 20 marks total, built around five must-hit points. For each point, give the marks it's worth and a one-line note on how students typically fumble it (e.g. treating the tragic hero as just "a guy who also kills someone," or saying the suspension means ethics is abolished rather than suspended). Oh and flag somewhere what seperates an A answer from a B answer in terms of specific moves, not just vibes. American English throughout please.
