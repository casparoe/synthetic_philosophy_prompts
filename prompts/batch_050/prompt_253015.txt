My brother did a law conversion course and cornered me at Christmas with an arguement I can't find the exit of. (1) Evidence can only justify finding against someone if it would not have existed regardless of whether he did it -- it has to discriminate between his guilt and his innocence, not just reflect how things were anyway. (2) Take the Blue Bus case: a bus forces a car off the road, the company runs 80% of the buses on that route, but that 80% figure would hold whether or not this particular bus was one of theirs, so it can't justify a verdict against them. Same with the gatecrasher sued because 499 of the 1000 people at the rodeo didn't pay. (3) So naked statistical evidence can't ground liability, and everyone sensible accepts that much. But then: (4) a DNA match is also just a statistic -- it tells you the profile is shared by roughly one person in a billion, which is a fact about a population, not a fact about him, and the population figure would hold whether or not he left the sample. (5) So DNA evidence on its own can't ground a conviction either. (6) That's absurd. (7) Therefore (1) is false, and verdicts can legitimately rest on evidence that doesn't discriminate in the way (1) demands. Every step looks fine to me in isolation, but I'd rather not give up either the Blue Bus verdict or DNA convictions, and I can't afford both. Which step do I attack, what did it have going for it, and what's the going rate for abandoning it? Please answer in British English.
