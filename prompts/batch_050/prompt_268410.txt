Write a villanelle about the Gettier problem for a student who has just met it and suspects it is a trick question. Use the two refrains to do the argument's real work: one should carry the tripartite analysis (belief, truth, justification) returning intact after every attempt to fix it, and the other should carry the epistemic luck that keeps slipping in anyway, so that the villanelle's compulsive repetitions themselves enact the way each proposed fourth condition generates a fresh counterexample. Somewhere in the five tercets, render at least one classic case accurately enough to be recognized -- a stopped clock that happens to be right, a deduced disjunction true for the wrong reason, an object seen where none is -- and let the closing quatrain land the genuine unsettling point: that the subject in such a case is not careless or irrational, that their justification can be impeccable, and that knowledge is still missing. It should satisfy a philosopher who works on the post-Gettier literature, but it should also, first, be a good villanelle.
