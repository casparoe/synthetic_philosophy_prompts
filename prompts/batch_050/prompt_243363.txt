I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm writing a long explainer on the Anthropocene debate. I want to close the piece with a decision tree that walks readers from their own starting assumptions to a named position on when the Anthropocene began and who has the authority to say so. Please take the following as stipulated background, whatever you may know or not know independently: after roughly fifteen years of work, the Anthropocene Working Group proposed a formal epoch beginning in the mid-twentieth century — the Great Acceleration, with plutonium from hydrogen-bomb tests as the primary marker and the annually layered sediments of Crawford Lake in Ontario as the candidate golden-spike site — and in 2024 the relevant stratigraphic bodies voted the proposal down, leaving Anthropocene as an informal term. Rival start dates remain in play: around 1784 with the steam engine and the industrial carbon pulse; 1610, when colonization of the Americas caused population collapse, forest regrowth, and a measurable dip in atmospheric carbon dioxide; and the early Anthropocene hypothesis, which pushes the onset back to the spread of farming thousands of years ago.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the list of dates: first the questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then the actual positions located in that space. Each internal node should be a single question a scientifically literate reader could answer without knowing which camp it leads to — for instance, whether a geological time unit must be anchored in a globally synchronous stratigraphic signal; whether the boundary should track a shift in the state of the Earth system even if the rock record registers it patchily and at different times in different places; whether events of human historical significance are legitimate grounds for drawing a geological boundary at all; and whether the decision properly belongs to stratigraphers, to Earth system scientists, or to a wider constituency that includes historians and the publics the label describes. The leaves must include the mid-twentieth-century Great Acceleration position, the 1610 proposal, the industrial-era proposal, the early-agricultural proposal, the view that the Anthropocene is better understood as a diachronous geological event than as a formal epoch, and the position that the concept should be renamed or abandoned because of what it implies about which humans are responsible — Anthropos in general rather than particular economies and empires. Aim for eight to ten leaves. Note any combination of answers your tree permits that nobody defends, with one line diagnosing why (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored), and flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot genuinely separate rather than forcing a distinction.
