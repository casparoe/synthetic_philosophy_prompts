I moderate a neighborhood forum and over the last year or so I've noticed something about my own reactions in political arguments (mostly online, sometimes at family dinners) that I can't find a word for, and it's starting to bug me. Here's the pattern. When someone I broadly agree with says something that sounds terrible, my brain immediately goes to work repairing it: I figure out what they probably meant, I supply the reasonable version, I assume they misspoke or got quoted out of context. When someone on the other side says something that sounds terrible, I don't do any of that. But here's the part I think is interesting -- it's not just that I read them uncharitably. It's that I stop treating their words as expressing a belief at all. I read the statement the way you'd read a chess move: not "what do they think is true?" but "what is this move trying to accomplish, who is it aimed at, what does it let them do next?" If an opponent says something moderate-sounding, I catch myself thinking the moderation is the tactic. If they say something extreme, I think the extremity is the tactic. Either way the actual content almost doesn't register as something anyone believes -- it's just maneuvering. And then when their side does the same thing to us (I assume they do), I find it infuriating and bad-faith on their part.

I know this is somewhere in the vicinity of "assuming the other side argues in bad faith," and I've seen people talk about steelmanning, the outgroup homogeneity thing, and something called naive realism which I think is about believing you see the world objectively while others are biased. But none of those quite nail the specific thing I'm describing, which is the asymmetry in what kind of object the speech is taken to be: on my side, words are evidence about beliefs (possibly garbled ones); on theirs, words are pure strategy with no belief underneath worth reconstructing. The double-standard part is probably old news in psychology, but the specific flip from "interpret this as communication" to "interpret this as a move in a game" -- is that an established concept with a name, in philosophy of language or political psych or anywhere else? If it's just a subspecies of something bigger, tell me which one and how it differs.

And if there's genuinely no name for it, I'd like you to coin one and actually do the carving, not just hand me a label: give a definition, say what the boundary cases are (e.g., does it count if the strategic reading happens to be accurate? -- because sometimes people really are arguing in bad faith, and I don't want a concept that forbids ever noticing that), and say how it relates to plain hypocrisy detection and to the general habit of assuming bad motives. Basically: what exactly is this thing I'm doing, what is it not, and when, if ever, is it the right thing to do? A bare name with no analysis won't help me.
