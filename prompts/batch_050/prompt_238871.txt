I'm putting together the syllabus for a graduate seminar on vagueness, and I want a unit in which students try to develop their own solution to the sorites paradox. To keep them from reinventing the wheel, I've drawn up a list of the standard families of solutions that they should treat as off-limits. I'd like to check whether my list is complete by testing it against you: if you can produce a solution that genuinely escapes every family on it, I'll know the list has a hole.

Here is the list. (1) Epistemicism: vague predicates have sharp boundaries that we are constitutively unable to know. (2) Supervaluationism and related gap theories: vagueness is semantic indecision, and truths are what holds on all admissible precisifications. (3) Degree theories, including fuzzy and three-valued logics: predications of vague terms come in intermediate truth values, and the sorites inference fails or loses a little validity at each step. (4) Contextualism: the extension of a vague predicate shifts with conversational context, so no single context contains a violating instance of the induction step. (5) Nihilism or strict finitism about vague predicates: deny that the predicate applies to anything, or deny that there are heaps at all, taking the paradox to show the predicate is incoherent. (6) Paraconsistent and dialetheist treatments: some borderline cases both are and are not heaps, and the inference to absurdity is blocked by rejecting explosion. (7) Denying the validity of the induction step or of the inference rules that carry it (e.g., restricting modus ponens or treating the argument as a fallacy of equivocation across steps).

Your task: propose one further solution to the sorites that is not a restatement, special case, or hybrid of the seven families above. State the core idea in a single sentence first, then sketch the view in enough detail that I can see which premise or structural feature of the paradox it targets. Then do two things: explain precisely why it does not collapse into one of the listed families (say which one it comes closest to and where it diverges), and give one honest assessment of the strongest objection it faces and whether it survives. Keep the whole answer under 300 words.
