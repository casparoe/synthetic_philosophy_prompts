I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing a long-form interview for my magazine with a historian whose new book argues that pathogens, climatic shifts, and volcanic eruptions should be treated as historical actors in their own right -- on par with generals, merchants, and states -- rather than as mere background conditions against which human history unfolds. Her central cases include the volcanic dust veil of the mid-sixth century and the subsequent plague outbreaks, the Little Ice Age and the political turmoil of the seventeenth century, and the role of Old World diseases in the European conquest of the Americas. She is careful, and I expect her to be evasive on precisely the points where her framework is most vulnerable. Draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions, each paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The sequence should press her on at least the following: first, whether calling a bacillus or a volcanic eruption an actor is anything more than a provocative metaphor, and what, if anything, would make the claim literally true; second, how her approach escapes the charge, often leveled at environmentally minded histories, of sliding into determinism -- of making geography and germs do all the explanatory work while human deliberation becomes epiphenomenal; third, how she individuates causal contributions, that is, what licenses saying the eruption caused the famine rather than the grain policies that left the population vulnerable to it; fourth, whether her framework can distinguish between events like the Columbian exchange, where non-human factors seem decisive, and events where they seem marginal, without collapsing into the truism that everything has environmental preconditions; and fifth, what a piece of historical evidence would have to look like for her to conclude that, in some particular case, the non-human actor framing obscures more than it reveals. Avoid pleasantries and biographical questions entirely; every question should engage the substance of the position. Please write your reply as continuous prose, as one would in an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
