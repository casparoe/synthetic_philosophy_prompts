Last night he did BOTH in the same conversation, and when I pointed out that these two lessons seem to contradict each other (one says act early, one says stay out), he got annoyed, said "history doesn't repeat itself, but it rhymes," and left it there like that settled it.

Like, at one extreme it might just mean "no two events are exactly alike but some are similar," which is a platitude nobody would dispute and which supports nothing. At the other extreme it might be a claim about recurring causal structures in history, where similar setups produce similar outcomes often enough that a past episode is genuine evidence about what will happen now. I want the full menu in between, not just the endpoints -- maybe something about human psychology being stable, something about institutions, something about selection effects in which episodes we bother remembering.

For each reading, tell me: is it a platitude, is it substantive and defensible, or is it actually false? And then the part I really care about: which reading(s) would Marcus need to be right about for his Munich reflex to be legitimate? My instinct is that the slogan is doing a sneaky two-step, where the weak reading makes it unfalsifiable and the strong reading is what the argument actually needs but nobody defends it. Tell me if I'm wrong about that.

Also, one more thing: on the strongest defensible reading, does the slogan even give you a way to pick BETWEEN analogies? Because it seems like Munich and Vietnam "rhyme" with almost any crisis in opposite ways, and if the theory of rhyming can't tell you which rhyme fits, it's useless as a guide even if its true. Marcus would say you just have to look at the specifics, but then it feels like the specifics are doing all the work and the analogy is just decoration.
