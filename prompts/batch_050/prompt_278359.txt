Last weekend a friend and I fell into a four-hour argument about the long-term future of humanity, and we are now in a stalemate over two competing analogies, which is where you come in. His side ran on the standard urn presentation of the doomsday argument. Suppose, he said, that an urn contains numbered balls, either ten of them or ten thousand, and you draw out ball number 7. You should strongly conclude the urn held ten. Now, roughly a hundred-odd billion humans have lived so far, so my birth rank is about a hundred billion; if our species continues for hundreds of thousands of years at anything like the current population, the total number of humans will be in the trillions or beyond, and my position would then be absurdly early -- like drawing ball 7 from the enormous urn. He concluded that my optimistic talk about a long future is statistically reckless, since I am effectively betting that I happen to be among the earliest fraction of a percent of all humans who will ever exist.

My counter was a different analogy. In a fair raffle with a thousand tickets, someone must draw ticket number 1, and the person who does has no grounds whatsoever for inferring the raffle was small; their low number is not a surprising datum but a guaranteed consequence of someone having to occupy that position. Likewise, I said, someone had to live in the early centuries of the species, and the fact that I find myself among them is fixed by the trivial fact that I exist now rather than later -- it cannot carry evidential weight about how many people come after me. His urn, I argued, smuggles in a genuine random draw from a fixed population, whereas my birth rank is nothing like a draw at all; it is more like the raffle winner's number, guaranteed to be low for whoever holds the early tickets. He replied that this proves too much, since it would make my position evidentially inert even if humanity ended next year, at which point we agreed to outsource the dispute.

Please audit both analogies as arguments rather than as illustrations. Organize the response under the following headings, in this order: "Correspondence Tables" (roughly 250 words; for each analogy, state explicitly what plays the role of the urn, the balls, the draw, the prior over urn sizes, and the drawn number -- and for mine, the raffle, the tickets, and the guaranteed holder of ticket 1), "Where the Urn Breaks" (roughly 250 words; which features of a genuine lottery my friend's case imports that the human situation lacks, and which of those features are doing the evidential work), "Where the Raffle Breaks" (roughly 250 words; be honest about my side, including whether my analogy dissolves the argument or merely relocates the disagreement into the choice of reference class), "What Each Analogy Quietly Assumes" (roughly 150 words), and "Verdict" (roughly 100 words; whose conclusion, if either, survives the audit, and whether the whole dispute is really about the analogies or about a prior neither of us stated).
