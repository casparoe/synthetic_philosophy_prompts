So I just started working on a forecasting team at a mid-size company (we do demand projections for retail clients) and they have this policy I've never seen anywhere else. When two analysts give different estimates for the same quantity, they're not allowed to just file both and move on. They have to sit down together, walk through each other's reasoning, and keep going until they either converge on one number or one of them formally concedes. If they genuinely can't converge after that, they have to each put $50 from their own paycheck into a little escrow pot on the outcome, and whoever was closer collects. My manager explained it as "disagreement is a bug, not a feature" and said if you really believed your number you'd be happy to bet on it.

Here's what bugs me. Before this job I always thought "agree to disagree" was the mature, reasonable way to handle persistant disagreement between smart people who've heard each other out. My old grad school friends and I did it constantly. But this company basically treats that as impossible-or-dishonest: either one of you is missing information the other has (so talk more), or someone isn't being honest with themselves about their confidence (so bet and find out).

So here's my question: what theory of rational disagreement does this workplace policy presuppose? Like, what does it assume about whether two fully informed, fully rational people can knowingly hold different beliefs about the same fact? Does it presuppose that disagreement always traces back to private information that pooling would fix, and is that actually true? Does the betting rule presuppose something extra -- like that willingness to bet is a reliable test of sincerity -- and does that part stand up even if the convergence part does? And what would the practice have to look like if instead we took the view that reasonable people with the same evidence can justifiably land in different places? I'm trying to figure out whether the policy rests on a coherent philosophy or just on management folklore, so please be concrete about which assumptions do the real work.

Please answer in American English, and don't worry about giving me citations or a bibliography, I just want the reasoning laid out clearly.
