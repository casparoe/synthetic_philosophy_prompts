So I rewatched Dr. Strangelove over the weekend and it occured to me that the doomsday machine is basically a technological patch for Kavka's toxin puzzle. Read the film through that lens for me. In the puzzle, the money rides on merely forming the intention to drink the toxin, but once you've formed it you have no reason to actually drink, and knowing this in advance seems to make genuinely intending it impossible. The Soviets' machine solves the deterrence version of this by taking the "drinking" out of human hands entirely -- nobody has to intend an insane act, the machine just does it. But then the film's whole catastrophe is that they kept it secret before it was operational, so the intention existed but wasn't known. Where does the mapping hold and where does it break? Does the film suggest the real crux was never the intention but its detection and communication (which is exactly what the billionaire's brain-scanner is doing in the puzzle)? And is the machine a cheat -- Kavka's point is about what a rational agent can't bring themself to intend, so a device that removes choice might dissolve the puzzle rather than solve it. Which way do you come down, and do any of the humans (Ripper, Turgidson, Mandrake) end up dramatizing the parts of the puzzle the machine can't automate?
