The gavagai setup is familiar to everyone by now: the field linguist, the rabbit, the native informant, and the claim that the evidence could never settle between "rabbit," "undetached rabbit part," "rabbit-stage," and so on. Where we keep getting stuck is what the thesis actually amounts to. Half the group insists Quine is making a radical metaphysical claim -- that there simply is no fact of the matter about what anyone means, even in one's own language, full stop. The other half says he's just belaboring a mundane point about underdetermination of theory by evidence, and that once you allow the linguist more data (behavioral dispositions, neural facts, whatever a future linguistics turns up), the indeterminacy evaporates, and Quine only gets his spooky conclusion by arbitrarily restricting the evidence base to observable behavior. Both sides can quote chapter and verse of Word and Object at each other and neither has budged since October, which is starting to feel diagnostic in itself.
