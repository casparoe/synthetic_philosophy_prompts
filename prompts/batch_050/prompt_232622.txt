Here are the objections to higher-order theories that I ALREADY know, so please don't just re-serve me one of these in new clothes:

1. Targetless / misrepresenting HOTs: you can apparently have a higher-order thought that you're in a first-order state that doesn't exist, or that misdescribes it — so what fixes the phenomenal character in those cases?
2. The rock problem: having a thought about a rock doesn't make the rock conscious, so why should having a thought about a mental state make the state conscious?
3. Animals and infants: they plausibly have conscious experiences without teh conceptual sophistication needed for higher-order thoughts.

5. Extrinsicness: intuitively, whether an experience is conscious is intrinsic to the experience, but HOT makes it depend on a relation to a separate state that could easily have been absent.
