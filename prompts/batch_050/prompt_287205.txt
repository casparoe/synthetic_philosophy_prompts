I help run a civics night at our local library -- mostly regulars from the neighborhood plus some high schoolers who need service hours, usually 18-24 people, and we get the back room for about 90 minutes. We've been arguing for weeks about term limits and whether we'd do better just picking our town council by lottery like jury duty, and the conversation keeps going in circles because everyone just asserts stuff like "ordinary people would obviously be better" or "obviously it'd be chaos" and nobody budges. Someone in the group (not an academic, none of us are) mentioned that ancient Athens did a bunch of things by lot, which got people even more dug in. So I want to stop debating and instead run some kind of game or simulation where people actually feel the differences between the two systems instead of just reciting opinions.

Design the activity for me. What I want participants to experience firsthand, before anyone lectures them: (1) that electing people repeatedly seems to select for being good at getting elected rather than good at the job, and that once someone has held the role a while they start playing to keep it; (2) the weird thing that supposedly happens with term limits where a rep in their final term has no reason to please anyone anymore -- for better or worse; and (3) whatever it actually feels like to be randomly handed power you didn't seek, next to people you'd never have voted for. I need concrete rules, roles, some kind of stakes that don't involve real money (we've used chocolate and library fine coupons before), a timeline that fits 90 minutes including setup, and a debrief that connects specific moments in the game to the real-world arguments about sortition and career politicians. Also tell me what's most likely to go wrong the first time we run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of the real debate the game can't capture -- like, a game night probably can't reproduce a 20-year political career, so tell me what I'll have to just say out loud.

One more thing: since you're designing this, tell me where your own design is most likely to be teaching the wrong lesson. If the game ends up making lottery selection look great (or terrible), is that because of the idea or because of some artifact of your rules? What would I have to see happen during the activity -- what kind of outcome or participant reaction -- that should make me think the design is broken rather than the idea being confirmed?
