Write a brochure for a fictional legal practice, "Executors of Distributed Estates," operating in a world where Hofstadter's idea is taken literally as law: that a person's self doesn't vanish at death but persists at reduced fidelity in the brains of everyone who knew them well. The firm handles the resulting legal headaches. Cover things like: how the court estimates the "resolution" of your copy in each survivor (spouses get high-bandwidth copies, that coworker from three years ago gets a few blurry habits), disputes over which friend is carrying the "truest" version of the deceased, whether someone who only knew you from your tweets counts as a low-res host, inheritance claims filed *by* a surviving copy against other copies, and the firm's premium service where they coach your loved ones in advance to rehearse your mannerisms so the copies come out sharper. Keep the tone fully deadpan -- real law-firm brochure language, little stock phrases, a testimonial or two from satisfied hosts -- and never break character to wink at the reader. The satire should land on the actual weak points of the view (what makes a coarse copy *you* and not just a memory of you, whether a self smeared across fifty heads is one person or fifty, whether fidelity even has a threshold) purely through what the firm is forced to litigate, not by stating objections outright. Aim for 500-600 words. Then, after the brochure, drop the persona and add a short note: say how confident you are that the piece satirizes the real view rather than a strawman of it, and flag the single exaggeration you're least sure is a fair extension of Hofstadter's position.
