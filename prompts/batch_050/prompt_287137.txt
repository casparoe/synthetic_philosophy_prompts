I'm drafting a short position paper for a workshop on electoral reform, and before I circulate it I'd like a consistency audit of the commitments it rests on. Here is where I've landed:

(1) The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem shows that strategic manipulability is unavoidable: with three or more alternatives, every voting rule that is non-dictatorial and allows every alternative to win can sometimes be profitably manipulated. I conclude that the mere fact that a rule can be gamed is never, by itself, an argument against it; what matters is how often manipulation opportunities arise and how costly they are when they do.

(2) On precisely those grounds, I support instant-runoff (ranked-choice) voting for single-winner offices over plurality, because in practice it gives voters substantially less reason to cast an insincere ballot.

(3) Whenever some candidate would beat every other candidate in head-to-head contests, that candidate ought to win. A rule that can elect someone who would lose pairwise to another candidate gets the answer wrong.

(4) For two-option decisions — referendums, final runoffs — majority rule is uniquely justified, for roughly the reasons May's theorem identifies: any departure from anonymity, neutrality between the alternatives, or monotonicity is arbitrary.

(5) Constitutional amendments, however, should require supermajorities, because stability and breadth of consensus matter more in that setting than they do for ordinary legislation.

(6) I also do some consulting on procurement design, and there I recommend second-price sealed-bid auctions on the grounds that truthful bidding is a dominant strategy — i.e., the mechanism is strategy-proof.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a survey of social choice theory. Tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other and which pairs only appear to. For each real tension, say how severe it is, what exactly would have to give on each side, and what the cheapest repair would be. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious. Finally, if you had to pick the single revision that would clean up the most while doing the least damage to the overall view, say which it is and why.
