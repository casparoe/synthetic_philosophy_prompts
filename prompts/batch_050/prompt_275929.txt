Last week in my game theory reading group we did the absent-minded driver case. The version we used: a driver will pass exactly two exits, but the exits look identical and, whenever she reaches one, she cannot remember whether she has already passed the other. Exiting at the first is worth 0, exiting at the second is worth 4, and continuing past both is worth 1. She knows all this before setting out, but she has no clock, notes, or commitment device. She also cannot give different instructions for the first and second exits, since she won’t know which one she is at.

I said the puzzle was about the possible gap between what seems best when planning and what seems best when actually sitting at an exit. Someone else waved the whole thing off with: “Easy — just follow the plan you would choose in advance.”

That sounds sensible, but I think it might hide the entire problem under a slogan. Don’t just explain the driver paradox in general. Give me a menu of the different precise principles that sentence could be expressing. For each principle, say:

1. exactly what it tells the driver to do when she finds herself at an indistinguishable exit;
2. what it assumes about memory, self-location, beliefs about which exit this is, and the ability to bind her later self;
3. whether it is a platitude, a substantive rationality principle, or something arguably false in this setup; and
4. whether it actually dissolves the puzzle or just moves the puzzle somewhere else.

Include at least one reading on which the earlier self’s plan fixes the same behavior at both exits, and one on which each encounter with an exit is treated as a separate agent choosing locally, but don’t let those be the only two. I’m especially interested in the mixed-strategy case, where the advance plan says to exit with some probablity rather than giving a simple yes-or-no answer.

At the end, identify the reading my friend would need for “Easy” to be justified, and say honestly whether that reading does justify it. If the slogan has no single charitable precisification, say that too.
