I got into an argument with some friends after reading about acausal trade, roughly the idea that agents who can never causally affect each other might still cooperate by reasoning about the similarity of their decision procedures. One friend thinks this is a deep generalization of trade; another thinks it is just ordinary coordination with the causal part quietly swept under the rug; a third says both are being too hasty because logical correlation can do real decision-theoretic work even when nothing travels between the parties. We could not agree on whether the weirdness is the absence of communication, the reliance on mutual predictability, or the way incentives supposedly arise without enforcement, retaliation, or even shared evidence.

Write a satirical bureaucratic handbook, around 750 words, called The Civil Service Guide to Trading With Inaccessible Counterparties. It should be deadpan and formatted as numbered policies plus a short FAQ for confused staff. The jokes should come from getting the concept right: agents in causally isolated worlds, cooperation based on reasoning about counterpart algorithms, the temptation to cheat when no punishment can arrive, and the danger of treating every imagined symmetry as a binding contract. Include policies on drafting agreements nobody signs, verifying compliance without receiving reports, handling counterparties who may not exist, and distinguishing genuine acausal coordination from simply guessing that aliens share your preferences. Make it funny to someone who knows the decision-theory debates but still readable to an intelligent newcomer; do not explain the jokes with a little lecture afterward.

After the handbook, add a brief author’s note of 100–150 words saying where the satire deliberately exaggerates or depends on a contested interpretation of acausal trade. Be self-critical there: identify which philosophical or decision-theoretic argument would most undermine the central jokes, and say how you would revise the handbook if that argument turned out to be right.
