So I recently got into calibration training -- you know, the thing where you practice putting probabilities on stuff and then score yourself. It works fine for trivia and for guessing whether my friend will flake on dinner. But a few weeks ago I hit a wall and I can't stop thinking about it.

I was deciding whether to quit my job and go freelance. Classic big decision, made pro/con lists, talked to everyone, the whole deal. And as a calibration exercise I thought, ok, let me do this properly: what's my probability that I quit within the next month? And the question just... broke? Like, not that it was hard to estimate. It felt category-wrong. If I said 60%, that felt less like a forecast and more like I'd already mostly decided and was reporting on it. If I said 50/50 it felt like I was lying, because deliberation isn't a coin sitting there to be observed, it's the thing that's supposed to *produce* the answer. The probability question seemed to want me to be done deliberating before I was done.

Second instance, wierd in a different way: I predicted 20% that I'd keep doing my morning runs through the winter. And then the prediction itself became a player in the decision. Some mornings I'd skip and think "well, I only gave myself 20%, this is consistent with my model, good calibration actually" -- which is insane, the forecast was supposed to describe me, not give me permission. Other mornings I'd run out of spite at my own number. Either way the prediction wasn't passive, it kept getting into the machinery it was trying to measure. This doesn't happen when I predict my friend's flaking. His behavior doesn't read my forecast. Mine apparently does.

So, two questions. Is there an established name or literature for this -- specifically for the case where the thing being predicted is your own choice, and the prediction either can't get off the ground (case one) or contaminates the outcome (case two)? I know self-fulfilling prophecy is a thing but that's usually about other people's expectations, or about events rather than choices you're still making, so it doesn't feel like quite the right box. And second: if a rational agent genuinely can't put honest probabilities on her own open decisions, what does that do to all the advice about thinking in probabilities? Is the calibration project just inapplicable to the choices that matter most, or is there some workaround -- like predicting from a third-person stance, or only scoring yourself after deliberation closes?

If there's no clean term for the whole tangle, feel free to name the pieces separately, but I definately want the carving, not just a label -- where exactly does this stop being ordinary prediction and start being something else.
