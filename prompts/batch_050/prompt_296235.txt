ok so I just got back from the pub where I spent like three hours arguing with my friend Dana about those recidivism risk scores (you know, the whole fight from a few years back where journalists said the tool was racist because it overflagged Black defendants as high risk, and the company replied no actually our scores are calibrated, a 7 means a 7 for everyone). I'm a data scientist, she's a defence lawyer, we do this a lot, but this time I couldn't tell whether she'd actually cornered me or whether we were just talking past each other. So here's roughly where I landed and I want a consistency check before I see her again:

1. A fair tool has to have equal false positive rates across groups. If innocent-or-wouldn't-reoffend people in one group get wrongly labelled high risk way more often than in another, that's unfair on its face, whatever else is true.
2. A fair tool also has to be calibrated within each group: among everyone (in any group) who gets a score of 70%, about 70% should actually go on to reoffend. Otherwise the number is lying to judges.
3. The base rate differences in the training data (one group showing higher recorded rearrest rates) are substantially artefacts of who gets policed, arrested and charged, not clean measurements of underlying risk. So I don't want to just take the labels as ground truth about who is actually dangerous.
4. None of this is worth much if the tool has to be kneecapped to get it. A fair tool that's barely better than chance isn't a serious proposal; whatever fairness fix we adopt has to leave most of the predictive accuracy intact.
5. Just stripping race out of the input features doesn't make the tool fair, because everything leaks through proxies (postcode, employment history, whatever). Dana said I can't have it both ways — I want the model blind to group membership at prediction time but I also want to audit error rates by group — but that feels fine to me, and I said so.
6. Individual fairness matters too: two people who are similar in the relevant respects should get similar scores, whatever group they're in.

Which of these are actually in tension and which only look like it?
