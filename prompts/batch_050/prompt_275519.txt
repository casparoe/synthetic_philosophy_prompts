ok so i'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and we keep circling around the theater metaphor for consciousness. Global workspace people (Baars and friends) describe the brain as a theater: there's a stage, the spotlight of attention moves around it, whatever's in the spotlight gets broadcast to the whole audience of unconscious processes, and being conscious just IS being in the spotlight. Our group leader uses this picture constantly, most recently to argue that access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness can't come apart, because "if it's not on the stage being watched, it's not conscious at all" -- i think he was targeting Block's overflow stuff, the idea that experience is richer than what you can report.

But then someone else in the group pointed out that Dennett spent half his career attacking the "Cartesian theater" as exactly the confused picture that leads to homunculus regress -- a little person in the audience watching the show. And now i'm stuck: is global workspace theory just the Cartesian theater in a lab coat, or is the theater analogy doing something respectable here that Dennett's target wasn't?

Please act as a professional philosopher of mind who's sympathetic to both Dennett and the workspace folks, and think carefully about this. Maybe do a short brainstorm of possible mappings and objections first, then give me the full analysis:

1. Lay out the correspondence table explicitly -- what plays the role of the stage, the spotlight, the actor, the audience, the play itself -- in the actual theory, not the cartoon version.

3. What does the theater picture do to the access/phenomenal distinction?
