I work in causal inference, the Pearl-flavored kind -- DAGs, do-operator, counterfactuals via truncated factorization, all that. I use it at work for marketing attribution and I've got a solid grip on the formalism, but basically zero philosophy background. Lately I've been reading philosophy of biology for fun and I keep hitting the "new mechanist" people (Machamer, Darden, Craver and everyone after them) who insist that a mechanism is more than a causal model: entities and activities organized so as to be productive of a phenomenon, with levels, constitutive relevance, the works.

Here's what I want. Translate their account into my vocabulary as honestly as you can. What is a mechanism, stated in the language of causal graphs? Are entities just variables/nodes? Are activities just edges? Is the do-operator their test for constitutive relevance? What would "levels of mechanisms" even be in graph terms -- some kind of variable hierarchy or abstraction map? Give me the cleanest translation possible, and be explicit about each correspondence you set up.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which pieces of the mechanists' picture survive the translation cleanly, which pieces did you have to fudge, and is there anything in their account that simply has no counterpart in interventionist causal modeling at all? I suspect the spatial/organizational stuff and the whole "the parts are components of the mechanism, and the mechanism as a whole does the explaining" interlevel relation, since DAGs are flat and acyclic. But tell me where I'm wrong.

And then the verdict: is the residue a defect of the causal-graph vocabulary -- i.e., it's missing something real about how biologists actually explain things -- or does the translation expose that the mechanists' extra notions (activities, production, levels) are loose talk that a properly built causal model already captures? My prior going in is that the DAG people win and "productive activities" is just vibes, but I've been going back and forth on it and I'd rather be talked out of that than have it confirmed. If some of the residue is genuine and important, tell me which bit and why a causal inference person should actually care.
