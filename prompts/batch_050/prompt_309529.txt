I'm on a city task force designing a restorative justice pilot and we keep going in circles about what the program is even supposed to make offenders feel. Half the group wants public acknowledgment ceremonies (which smell like shame rituals to me), the other half wants private victim-offender mediation aimed at guilt and amends, and one guy keeps saying the whole thing is manipulation either way. Write me a taxonomy of the positions on which moral emotion, if any, institutions should try to cultivate, delivered as exactly five numbered items with no intro and no wrap-up, each item two sentences max. Every item needs: the position's name (or "unnamed" if nobody holds it), the claim that splits it off from its neighbors -- I'm thinking of axes like wether the emotion targets the act or the self, whether an audience is constitutive of the emotion or just an accident, and whether a culture can choose its moral emotions at all or they're path-dependent and policy can only work with the grain -- plus its most awkward commitment for someone running an actual program rather than writing a paper. The five leaves have to include the guilt-supremacist view, the shame-rehabilitation view, and the view that moral emotions are just control technology we should engineer out of justice entirely, and at least one leaf should be an unoccupied position with a one-clause diagnosis of why nobody's there (incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored). If two of the named views collapse onto the same leaf once the branching questions do their work, flag that instead of inventing a difference.
