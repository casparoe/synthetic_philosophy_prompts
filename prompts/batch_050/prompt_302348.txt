Having spent years reading rationalist blogs, I've noticed that critiques of intuition pumps always recycle the same half-dozen classics, and I want a purpose-built specimen instead. Construct a thought experiment engineered to make a reader feel the force of the conclusion that well-off people are morally required to give a substantial share of their income to effective charities, without actually arguing for it — an intuition pump that persuades by misleading. Constraints: (1) realistic and set in ordinary life, with no trolleys and no drowning children; (2) nothing in the scenario may assert the conclusion or stipulate what the reader feels — the intuition has to be generated by the scenario's structure; (3) there must be a single identifiable feature doing the persuasive work, such that neutralizing it deflates the intuition while the literal facts of the case stay fixed; and (4) the pump must still superficially resemble a legitimate argument, so it could get past an attentive reader. Commit to one design rather than offering alternatives, and answer in American English as exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, with no introduction or conclusion: 1) the scenario itself; 2) the intuition it produces and the mechanism by which it produces it; 3) the feature doing the work and why it is illegitimate; 4) what remains of the intuition once that feature is stripped out; 5) the strongest defense that the pump genuinely supports the conclusion, and your verdict on that defense.
