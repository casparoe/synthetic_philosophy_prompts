Next week I present a paper at a metaphysics colloquium defending the claim that so-called masked dispositions are not dispositions at all: a wine glass packed in foam that survives being dropped is simply not fragile while so packed, and masking cases should be reclassified as cases where the environment has temporarily changed the object's intrinsic dispositional profile rather than suppressed its manifestation. My core argument: finks and masks are standardly treated as two separate counterexamples to the conditional analysis, each with its own patch, but both show the same thing -- that any stimulus-manifestation conditional is hostage to indefinitely many background conditions, which can only be specified by re-invoking the disposition itself, so the whole conditional-analysis project is circular and my reclassification is the honest response. The audience: one dispositional essentialist who thinks fragility is an irreducible, intrinsically modal property that can't be "lost" to packing foam; one committed Lewisian who believes the reformed conditional analysis already handles finks and masks and will resist multiplying metaphysical machinery; a philosopher of science who thinks dispositions are placeholder talk awaiting microphysical reduction; and some graduate students.
