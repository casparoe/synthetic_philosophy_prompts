write me the autumn newsletter of the town council of a small municipality (you invent the name) that, after a referendum two years ago, adopted lexical threshold negative utilitarianism as its official charter axiology. around 500 words, deadpan municipal-bureaucrat voice, with the usual newsletter furniture: a note from the mayor, some agenda items from the last council meeting, a short budget summary, and a residents' Q&A.

the jokes have to come from the view being applied *correctly*, not from the council being stupid. so: under the charter, suffering at or above a certain intensity can't be compensated by any amount of pleasure, which means the summer festival got cancelled over one resident's kidney stones, and no quantity of oompah music changes that. meanwhile below the threshold everything still trades, so the council can approve a bake sale expected to cause three minor burns as long as the cake is good enough -- and the cliff between those two cases is where the comedy lives. in the Q&A, let a resident ask where exactly the threshold sits and give them an answer that is pure committee. include the council's awkward but consistent policy on new residents, whether moving in or being born (every new person is a new possible sufferer, but blocking everyone has its own costs -- or does it, the council keeps asking). and let one agenda item be the obvious one, tabled for the seventh consecutive quarter, that nobody wants to minute.

someone who actually holds the view should read it and think 'ok, fair, that IS the view' rather than 'that's a strawman'. stay deadpan the whole way through, no winking at the reader.
