So I'm putting together a reading group on explanation in the life sciences and before we get into the lit I want the spec first: what does a theory of mechanistic explanation (the MDC / new mechanism tradition) actually have to deliver to count as adequate? Not "what do these guys say" -- I know roughly what they say. I mean the requirements list, each one with the case or phenomenon behind it, and which ones are load-bearing vs. which are really just one camp's taste.

Some constraints I think any list needs to engage with, but tell me if I've got these wrong or am missing bigger ones:

1. It has to handle the Hodgkin-Huxley situation: a purely mathematical model of the action potential that fit the data beautifully for decades and nobody quite agreed on whether it explained anything before the ion channel story was filled in. Does your theory say it was explanatory all along, became explanatory retroactively, or was never explanatory -- and can it say one of those without looking ad hoc either way?

2. It has to say what makes something a *component* of a mechanism rather than a background condition or a cause of the whole thing, and ideally cash that out in a way that matches how biologists actually run interlevel experiments (stimulate the part, look for changes in the whole; lesion the whole, look for changes in the part). If the account of constitutive relevance turns out circular, say so.

3. It has to make sense of mechanism *sketches* -- the black-box diagrams that litter actual papers -- without either calling them non-explanations (false to practice) or calling them full explanations (then nothing distinguishes them from the finished product).

4. It has to draw whatever line it draws between how-possibly, how-plausibly, and how-actually stories somewhere principled, because evaluation of mechanisms in the wild seems to live entirely in that middle zone.

5. It has to survive the fact that biology allegedly has no laws -- if mechanisms are doing the explanatory work laws did in the covering-law picture, what makes a mechanism *regular* enough to explain without smuggling laws back in?

Then: which requirement do you think knocks out the most going accounts (I'm suspicious it's the completeness/sketch one), are any of these desiderata actually in tension with each other, and what would I have to give up if I also wanted the account to cover etiological explanations (why the mechanism is there at all) rather than just constitutive ones? Ranking the requirements by how much damage dropping each does would be great.
