I'm a journalist working on a feature about physicists' enduring faith in mathematical elegance as a guide to truth, and I want a short poem to run as a sidebar. Please write a sonnet — fourteen lines, rhyme scheme of your choosing, with a genuine volta — that dramatizes the epistemology rather than merely celebrating beauty. Use the octave to build the strongest case for elegance as evidence: Maxwell adding a term to his equations for the sake of symmetry and being vindicated, Dirac trusting the beauty of his equation over awkward experimental results and being vindicated too. The volta should be the moment the reader remembers that the luminiferous ether was elegant as well, and that the most beautiful theories of the past few decades have spent years returning null results at the colliders. The closing couplet should name the real problem as precisely as two lines allow: that elegance tracks what successful theories have looked like so far, not what nature is obliged to do next. It should be accurate enough that a philosopher of physics wouldn't wince, and good enough as verse that a general reader wouldn't notice the philosophy working.
