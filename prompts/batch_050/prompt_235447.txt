My flatmate and I got into an argument in the pub last night about Pascal's mugging, and I'm trying to figure out what principle she's actually running, because her judgements seem all over the place to me. So she isn't a naive expected-value maximiser, or she'd pay the mugger, and she isn't someone who simply refuses to reason about small probabilities, or she'd skip the lottery.

The case where we got stuck: suppose a sealed, tamper-proof, externally audited machine -- about as trustworthy as the charity lottery, no shady human promising anything -- offers her the mugger's deal in structural terms. Pay £1,000 for a guaranteed 1-in-10^15 chance of a payoff worth £10^25, with the odds and the payout mechanism verifiable by anyone who cares to check. The expected value dwarfs the ticket price, the source is credible, but the probability is minuscule. Predict her verdict, and more importantly tell me which rule best explains her overall pattern: straight expected-value maximisation with credibility discounted, EV with a probability floor below which chances get rounded to zero, or EV with a cap on how much utility any claimed payoff can actually deliver. Answer in British English, please, and don't be afraid to say her judgements are just inconsistent if that's where the argument leads.
