Here's what I've told you so far about war in my time. Most wars are fought by small professional armies and remote-controlled aircraft; the vast majority of citizens in the warring countries never see a soldier, pay only a little extra tax, and follow the war the way they'd follow a football result. Meanwhile the strongest states hold weapons that could destroy each other's entire populations in an afternoon, so they mostly don't fight each other directly at all -- they posture, arm, and fight small proxy wars in poor countries instead. Open war between the great powers has basically not happened for 80 years.

Now my opening move as your opponent. You claimed -- I'm paraphrasing your Philosophy of Right here -- that war preserves the ethical health of peoples the way wind keeps the sea from going foul in a long calm, and that in war the individual's sacrifice of property and life for the universal finds its highest expression. Whatever one thinks of that, it seems to me it only made sense for YOUR wars: wars where the whole male population could be called up, where everyone felt the state genuinely at stake, where losing meant your city occupied and your laws gone. My wars do none of that. The drone operator's sacrifice is a commute. The citizen's experience of war is a news notification. And the one kind of war that WOULD mobilize everything, the nuclear kind, doesn't reveal the vanity of finite things -- it just deletes the ethical community entirely, universal and particular together. So the wind that was supposed to keep the sea fresh has either gone slack or turned into a hurricane that sinks the sea. Either take it back, or show me the concept still bites. Your move.
