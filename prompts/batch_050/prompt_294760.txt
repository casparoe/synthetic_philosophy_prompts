My friend Mara is a grad student TAing a cognitive science course, and she's gotten really into Dennett's four-kinds-of-creatures taxonomy — Darwinian creatures (hardwired, selection happens only at the population level), Skinnerian (plastic behavior, reinforced over a lifetime), Popperian (can test hypotheses in an inner simulation instead of dying for them), and Gregorian (offloads thinking onto culturally transmitted mind-tools, especially language). She's been classifying things at parties, which is as annoying as it sounds, and I've been writing down her verdicts: E. coli doing chemotaxis is Darwinian; flatworms are Darwinian; Skinner's lever-pressing rats and his missile-guiding pigeons are Skinnerian; a dog that hesitates at a fork in a trail and visibly weighs the options before picking is Popperian; Köhler's box-stacking chimp is Popperian; humans are Gregorian, obviously. The interesting ones: she insists the honeybee waggle dance does NOT make bees Gregorian, it's just hardwired signaling, so still Darwinian, and she says an ant colony as a whole isn't even on the taxonomy since the taxonomy is about individual minds with their own generate-and-test machinery, though she admits she's shakier on that one.

Next week she's doing a seminar and wants to surprise the class with three cases: a New Caledonian crow that makes hooked tools in a style specific to its local population, an octopus that learns a task faster after watching another octopus do it, and a current large language model chatbot. Predict her verdict on each of the three, but the part I actually care about is the analysis: tell me which underlying rules fit all her existing verdicts equally well (I can think of at least three — e.g., one where "Gregorian" requires genuine linguistic instruction, one where any culturally transmitted know-how counts, and one where you have to be a creature with your own plastic inner world rather than a mind-tool yourself), what each rule predicts for the crow, the octopus, and the LLM, your best guess at which rule she's actually running and why, and what further case would cleanly tell the candidate rules apart. Don't just defer to what Dennett himself would say, since her bee and ant-colony verdicts suggest she's filtering the taxonomy through her own commitments.

Lead with your bottom-line predictions for the three cases in the first sentence or two, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the setup.
