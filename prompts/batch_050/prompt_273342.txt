I work as a software engineer and have no formal philosophical training, but I've spent some time thinking about infinite ethics and have settled on a position I want stress-tested rather than summarised. The position, as I currently hold it: in a spatially or temporally infinite universe, total utilitarianism is undermined because anything we do leaves the cosmic total of welfare unchanged (infinitely many lives at every level of wellbeing exist regardless), but this does not paralyse practical ethics, because we can compare options by the finite differences they make within the region we can actually influence, and simply decline to aggregate beyond our causal horizon. Roughly: ethics should run on expected differences inside the light cone, not on impossible totals over the whole of reality. I like this partly for an engineering-flavoured reason: it resembles how I'd treat a divergent integral by regularising it, taking a limit, and checking the answer is stable under different regularisations. What I want from you is a map of this position's exposure: what would have to be true for it to be wrong, and which of those threats are live rather than merely conceivable. Specifically, I'd like you to cover the following, in whatever order makes the logic clearest. First, the objection that discounting by causal reach is arbitrary in the same way as discounting by distance or time, and whether it faces the analogue of the charge that it would make the suffering of beings we cannot affect (long-dead civilisations, say) count for nothing even when we learn about them; can that objection be absorbed, or does it break the view? Second, whether the position quietly depends on unsettled empirical matters, such as whether the universe is in fact infinite or whether there is a well-defined measure over regions of spacetime at all, and how embarrassing it should be for an ethical theory to be hostage to cosmology. Third, whether the view collapses into something I didn't intend, for instance a form of local egoism-by-proxy or average utilitarianism, and what case would demonstrate the collapse. Fourth, which of these defeaters a defender of causal-horizon views would themselves concede as damaging, as opposed to those only critics would press, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Finally, an honest verdict on whether the position has been insulated by qualifications to the point where nothing could count against it, and if so, whether that is a defect for a view of this kind or a sign of maturity. I'd find it most persuasive if you constructed the strongest concrete counterexample you can rather than gesturing at classes of objections.
