I'm drafting a position statement on sweatshop labor for a political philosophy workshop and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments are these: (1) Sweatshop employment is typically wrongful exploitation: even though employer and worker both benefit relative to no deal, the employer captures an unfair share of the surplus precisely by leveraging the workers' desperation. (2) Because these arrangements are consensual and mutually beneficial, third parties act wrongly when they forcibly prevent them; workers' choices among the options actually available to them ought to be respected. (3) Consumer boycotts and public-shaming campaigns against firms that use sweatshop labor are legitimate, and often obligatory, forms of moral pressure. (4) Whether a worker is made better or worse off by some change must be measured against her actual alternatives, not against a hypothetical just wage. (5) An outcome in which a firm automates, dismisses half its workforce, and pays the remaining workers a genuinely generous wage is morally preferable to the sweatshop status quo. (6) The wrongness of the exploitation does not depend on the employer having caused the workers' desperate circumstances. Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. Rank any genuine tensions by severity, and for each one state what would have to give and what that revision costs. Finally, tell me which single revision would restore the most coherence to the set overall.
