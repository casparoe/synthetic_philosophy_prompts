Is there a name for this? My running club elects its commitee by ranked ballot: everyone lists the candidates in order, whoever has the fewest first choices gets knocked out and their votes pass to the next name on each ballot, and so on until someone has a majority. After last year's vote a friend showed me the tally sheets and, as far as I can tell, she's right that if a handful of members who ranked her candidate second had ranked her first instead, the extra support would have got her eliminated a round earlier and she'd have lost. So more first-place support makes a candidate do worse. Now, I've heard there's a famous theorem saying roughly that every voting system can be gamed by voters misrepresenting their preferences, and people keep telling me this is just an instance of that. But nobody misrepresented anything here; the weirdness seems to live in the counting rule itself, not in voter strategy. Does this phenomenon have its own name, and how exactly does it relate to that theorem -- is it a consequence of it, a separate defect, or what? Also, could a system have this quirk while being immune to strategic voting, or the other way round? Please answer in British English, say how confident you are in each part of the answer, and single out the claim you're least sure of.
