I want to work out what I actually think about the supererogatory — acts that are good to do but not required — and I would like a principle extracted from my verdicts on cases, if there is one to be had.

My verdicts are these. Jumping on a grenade to save one's comrades is supererogatory, and failing to do it warrants no blame. Donating a kidney to a stranger is supererogatory as well. Donating a kidney to one's own child, by contrast, strikes me as required: a parent who refused would be blameworthy, even though the cost to the agent is the same as in the stranger case. Leaving a generous tip is nice but optional; leaving no tip at all after good service is blameworthy, even though nobody would call tipping obligatory in the way promise-keeping is. Volunteering one evening a month at a shelter is good and optional; volunteering every evening, in a way that wrecks one's health and one's family life, stops being praiseworthy at some point and becomes something else — though I cannot say what. Telling a small lie to spare a stranger's feelings is permissible but not praiseworthy. And a judge who shows mercy where the law leaves discretion: I find myself unsure whether to call that supererogatory at all, or whether the category simply does not apply to decisions made in an official capacity.

Is there a principled account — in terms of cost to the agent, the strength of the reasons in favour of acting, the praiseworthiness of performance together with the non-blameworthiness of omission, or some combination — that captures all of these, or near enough? State the principle precisely enough that I can see which clause each case motivates, and say what it predicts for cases I have not listed: for instance, a bystander who rescues a drowning child at serious risk to herself, where many would say she did what anyone should do rather than something beyond duty. If the simplest coherent account requires me to drop or revise one of my verdicts, tell me which one and why, and whether the kidney pair can be reconciled without appeal to anything as blunt as the bare fact of parenthood. Please answer in British English.
