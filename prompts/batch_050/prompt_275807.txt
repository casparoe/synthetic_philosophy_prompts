I've recently taken over our second-year philosophy of mind course from a colleague on leave, which means I'm now writing the final exam on short notice. The course spent two weeks on Dennett: the critique of the Cartesian theater, the multiple drafts model, and the Orwellian-versus-Stalinesque distinction about how misperceptions get into consciousness. In seminars, my students were uniformly good at summarizing the multiple drafts picture and uniformly bad at explaining what work it actually does -- most of them could name the two revision mechanisms but none could say why Dennett thinks choosing between them is a mistake rather than an open empirical question. I want one essay question, answerable in 75 minutes, that forces that crux. Specifically, the question should present a concrete misperception case (I'm thinking something along the lines of the color-phi phenomenon, where a briefly flashed red dot appears to move and turn green en route to a second flash) and ask students to construct the best possible Orwellian and Stalinesque explanations of it, then adjudicate Dennett's claim that beyond some fine grain of temporal precision there is no fact of the matter about which explanation is correct. The question should make clear that merely reciting the labels won't pass: students must explain why the indeterminacy, if real, is itself an argument against the Cartesian theater, and at least gesture at whether an opponent could resist by denying that the grain of precision matters.

Along with the question, please supply a model answer of roughly 600 words written at the level of a genuinely strong second-year student (not a professor showing off), and a marking scheme out of 20 built around five must-hit points, each with its mark value and a one-line note on the specific way weaker candidates typically fumble that point -- for instance, treating the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction as two rival empirical hypotheses awaiting evidence, or conceding the indeterminacy but failing to connect it to the theater critique. Finally, add a short paragraph stating what separates an A answer from a B answer in terms of the moves made, not the prose quality.
