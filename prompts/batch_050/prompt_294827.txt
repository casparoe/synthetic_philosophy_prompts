So I've got this long-running fight with my brother-in-law, who does astrology and crystal stuff, and his whole defense is basically "your science is just one theory among many, mine fits my experience fine." Last week I tried to steelman him so I could take the argument apart properly, and now I've scared myself, because I can't find where it breaks:

(1) Any finite body of observations is logically compatible with infinitely many mutually incompatible theories.
(2) If two theories are both compatible with all the evidence we have, then that evidence gives us no reason to prefer one over the other.
(3) All the evidence anyone ever has is finite (and always about the past, never the future).
(4) So nobody ever has evidential reason to prefer any theory over any rival -- including "the sun comes up tomorrow" over "tomorrow it doesn't."
(5) But that's absurd. I have excellent reasons to trust the weather forecast over his horoscope.
(6) So at least one of (1)-(3) has to go.

Each premise looked totally airtight when I wrote it down, and yet the conclusion vindicates him completely, which I refuse to accept. Which step do I reject, and why did it look so solid? And please actually commit -- pick the one step you'd reject yourself, tell me what rejecting it costs, and don't just survey the standard moves and leave me to choose. If it matters, I'd much rather give up (2) than admit that "fits all the evidence so far" puts his charts on par with physics, but I want to know what I'm paying for that.
