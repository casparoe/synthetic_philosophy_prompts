Write a dialogue between two AI safety researchers arguing about the orthogonality thesis. One thinks it's basically correct: intelligence and final goals really can vary independently, and there's no capability level at which a paperclip maximizer stops being coherent. The other thinks it's misleading as stated: once a system is smart enough to do flexible long-horizon planning, it has to be able to represent, weigh, and possibly revise its own goals, and that puts real pressure on the idea that any goal can ride along with any intelligence.

Anchor the debate in one concrete case they keep returning to: two systems with identical world-modeling and planning abilities, one maximizing paperclips, the other trying to be a genuinely helpful assistant. Is that pairing actually coherent, or does something break? Don't strawman either side -- the orthogonality defender should freely grant instrumental convergence, and the skeptic should grant that a dumb, rigid utility function is perfectly buildable. They should engage each other's actual arguments, not talk past each other, and its fine if neither fully wins.
