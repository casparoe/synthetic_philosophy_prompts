So I was arguing with a friend about whether "closer to the truth" is a notion that can actually be made to work. Popper wanted verisimilitude to do the job -- a false theory can still be more truthlike than another false theory, and science progresses by getting closer to the truth. Then Miller's result (building on Tichý) showed that on Popper's own definition, no false theory is closer to the truth than any other, and worse, the whole ordering seems to depend on which language you describe the theories in. My friend says the concept is basically dead and realists should just drop it; I think something in the neighborhood obviously works, since "the earth is a sphere" is plainly closer to the truth than "the earth is flat" and any framework that can't say that much is broken.

Give me your credence, as a number, that a workable formal account of verisimilitude exists -- where "workable" means: it ranks intuitively clear cases the right way (sphere beats flat, relativity beats Newtonian mechanics on at least some respects), it's not trivially re-orderable by relabeling predicates, and it doesn't require God-like access to the true theory to apply in principle. Then decompose that number for me: roughly,

- the probability that the language-dependence objection is fatal in principle vs. just a technical annoyance that a privileged-vocabulary move (structural, natural-kind, or similarity-based) can answer;
- the probability that the apparent successes of the concept (we do in fact sensibly rank false theories) can be explained away as just tracking predictive accuracy or approximate truth of particular claims, without needing a global verisimilitude ordering at all;
- the probability that the realist project that needs verisimilitude can survive without it (i.e., even if the concept fails, that doesn't sink convergence-to-truth realism),
- plus any other sub-question you think the final number actually turns on.

Give a rough probability and a line of justification for each, say how they combine into your final credence (it doesn't have to be a clean product -- just show your work), and then tell me which term you're least sure of and what result or argument would move it most. Also: my friend is at maybe 0.05 on the original proposition and I'm at maybe 0.85 -- based on your decomposition, which sub-question do you think our disagreement most likely lives in?
