I'm making a zine for my college radio station and want a flowchart on the back page that sorts readers into positions on the ontology of musical works — the "what is a song, actually?" question. The diagnostic questions have to be concrete cases, never labels or jargon. Draw from cases like these (keep whichever actually seperate positions): a cover that's note-for-note identical but played on synths instead of guitar; a remix that pitches the original up 10% and lays a breakbeat under it; a track that exists only as a studio master (reversed cymbals, spliced tape) and could never be played live; the same MIDI file rendered by two different virtual orchestras; two composers 150 years apart who write note-identical pieces; a performance with exactly one wrong note.

The leaves have to include at least: the work is an eternal abstract sound-structure (discovered, not made); the work is an abstract type someone created by composing, so it has a birthdate and a spot in music history; the work is nothing over and above its performances; and for some music the work is the recording itself. If two of these can't be told apart by any case, flag them instead of inventing a fake question. And if some combination of answers lands nowhere, note it and guess whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Format: JSON, roughly 6-10 nodes. {"nodes": [{"id": int, "case": string, "question": string, "branches": [{"answer": string, "next": int, where next is a node or leaf id}]}], "leaves": [{"id": int, "position": string, "awkward_commitment": string}], "unsplittable_pairs": [[position, position, why_no_case_splits_them]], "empty_combos": [{"answers": string, "diagnosis": string}]}
