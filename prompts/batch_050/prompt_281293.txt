I am a PhD student in philosophy of science, and for the past two years I have been embedded in a machine learning research group for my dissertation on whether classical methodologies of science can describe what such groups actually do. I am now fluent in the lab's working vocabulary: train/validation/test splits, inductive bias, regularization and hyperparameter tuning, ablation studies, scaling behaviour, benchmark leaderboards, and the distinction between interpolation and genuine out-of-distribution generalization. What I am asking for is a systematic translation, not a loose analogy.

Take Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes -- the hard core shielded from falsification by methodological decision, the protective belt of auxiliary assumptions that absorbs recalcitrant results, the negative and positive heuristics, and above all the distinction between progressive and degenerating problemshifts, where a modification counts as progressive only if it predicts some novel fact rather than merely accommodating the anomaly that prompted it -- and restate the whole framework in the vocabulary I have listed, as precisely as you can. Then give me the residue report. First, which components translate cleanly: I assume the hard core maps to the fixed architectural family and the belt to the surrounding recipe (data augmentation, optimizer choices, normalization schemes), but tell me where even those mappings strain. Second, which components translate only with distortion: I suspect Lakatos's notion of novel prediction is close to but not identical with held-out test performance, since a test set is still drawn from a known distribution -- so say whether predicting a genuinely new phenomenon, as opposed to a withheld instance of a familiar one, has any real counterpart in this vocabulary, or whether the framework must simply drop the distinction Lakatos cared most about. Third, which claims cannot be expressed at all: my working hypothesis is that Lakatos's insistence that the unit of appraisal is a series of theories rather than a single theory has no natural home in a discipline whose unit of appraisal is a single checkpoint on a leaderboard, and I want that confirmed or corrected.

Then the payoff. Treat the transformer lineage as the test case -- a fixed architectural hard core, a decade of belt modifications (positional encoding schemes, normalization variants, attention refinements), and a scaling-driven positive heuristic -- and deliver a verdict on whether, in Lakatosian terms, it is currently a progressive or a degenerating programme. At each step where the verdict turns on a contested point of translation rather than on the evidence itself, flag it explicitly rather than resolving it silently.

Finally, assess the residue itself. Is what fails to translate a defect of the machine learning vocabulary -- things it simply cannot yet say -- or is it evidence that Lakatos's framework survives only by trading on notions that a more operational discipline forces us either to make precise or to abandon? I want a defended position on this, not a diplomatic both-sides answer.
