Last night I spent three hours arguing with a friend about Hannah Arendt's 1967 essay Truth and Politics, and we ended at an impasse I want to resolve properly. The position I found myself defending is Arendt's: that factual truth is structurally disadvantaged in politics in a way that philosophical or logical truth is not. Since I don't have the essay in front of me, here is her argument as I remember it, paraphrased in my own words rather than quoted. Facts are contingent: everything that actually happened could have happened otherwise, so a falsehood that presents the world as different never advertises itself as impossible. The liar therefore operates like an actor, remaking the shared world in miniature, while the truth-teller merely reports it and enjoys no such freedom. Moreover, once a fact is established it confronts us with the same coercive force as a theorem, which means factual truth is in a sense anti-political: it cannot be negotiated, only accepted. Arendt draws the conclusion that organized lying, backed by power or by modern image-making, is not just one political tactic among others but a standing threat to factual truth itself, and that the danger culminates in the liar who comes to believe his own lies, losing the very capacity to orient himself in the real world.

My friend thinks this is overwrought: lying is parasitic on truth and collapses of its own weight; propaganda regimes failed; facts have a stubborn way of resurfacing; and calling lies action flatters them.

Treat Arendt's position, as I've reconstructed it, as the object of scrutiny. What would count against it? Which historical episodes or observations, if accurate, would be decisive, and which would merely be awkward? Which parts of the argument — contingency of facts, the coercive character of established truth, the equation of lying with action, the self-deception thesis — are load-bearing, and which could a defender surrender without collapse? What would Arendt's own defenders concede as genuine defeaters, as opposed to what her critics take to be defeaters? Finally, an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified into something unfalsifiable: she restricts the claim to factual truth and to the political realm, and I want to know whether those restrictions are principled or protective. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely uncomfortable, and say plainly if you think the position survives all of them.
