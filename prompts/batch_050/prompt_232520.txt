Exam question:

State the Sleeping Beauty problem clearly. Explain the "halfer" and "thirder" answers to the question "Upon awakening, what should Beauty's credence be that the coin landed Heads?" Present the strongest argument for each answer, and then defend one of the two answers against the strongest objection to it.

Student's answer:

Beauty agrees to the following procedure. On Sunday a fair coin is flipped, out of her sight. She is put to sleep and awakened on Monday. If the coin lands Heads, that is her only awakening. If it lands Tails, she is awakened again on Tuesday. A drug ensures that on each awakening she cannot tell whether she has been awake before, so Monday and Tuesday awakenings are subjectively indistinguishable. The question: on awakening, what should her credence be that the coin landed Heads?

The halfer answer is 1/2. On Sunday, Beauty's credence in Heads is 1/2, and awakening gives her no new information: she knew in advance that she would be awakened whatever the coin showed. Since she learns nothing, her credence should not change.

The thirder answer is 1/3. When Beauty awakens, there are three possibilities for her current situation: Heads-and-Monday, Tails-and-Monday, and Tails-and-Tuesday. These are subjectively indistinguishable, so by indifference she should assign each probability 1/3, and hence 1/3 to Heads.

The strongest argument for the halfer view is the no-new-information argument just given. The strongest argument for the thirder view is that in a long run of repetitions of the experiment, only one third of Beauty's awakenings would be Heads awakenings, and her credence should match this frequency.

I will defend the thirder view. Notice first that the thirder answer is not confined to the awakened Beauty. On Sunday, knowing the protocol, she can already see that any future awakening will be one of the three candidates listed above, so she assigns 1/3 to Heads on Sunday night as well; the awakening merely confirms what she could work out in advance. This shows that the dispute is not really about what Beauty learns but about how to distribute credence over indistinguishable situations.

The halfer's best objection is that the thirder has Beauty's credence shift from 1/2 to 1/3 without any new evidence, in violation of conditionalization. But this objection backfires. Suppose that after awakening, Beauty is told that it is Monday. Heads guarantees a Monday awakening, whereas Tails produces a Monday awakening only half the time. On learning that it is Monday, the halfer must therefore remain at 1/2, since she has received information only about the day and none about the coin. But surely learning that it is Monday should raise her confidence in Heads: had the coin landed Tails, she might well have been in the Tuesday awakening instead, and learning that she is not is evidence for Heads. The halfer's refusal to update here is unmotivated, while the thirder handles the case smoothly: P(Heads | Monday) = (1/3) / (1/3 + 1/3) = 1/2, which is the intuitively correct answer.
