I've been arguing about this with a friend who studied physics, ever since we read Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment together -- the one with the vast flat "cared-for" arena in which tiny marbles (the simms) bounce around according to low-level dynamics, and where certain stable configurations of simms act as symbols (the simmballs) whose interactions can be described, predicted, and explained entirely at the symbol level. Hofstadter's suggestion, as we understand it, is that the simmballs can be said to push the simms around just as much as the reverse: that the symbol level is a genuine locus of causal efficacy, not merely a convenient compression of microphysics. My friend flatly denies this. In her view, every actual pushing event happens at the simm level, closure of the microphysical is non-negotiable, and "the simmball caused it" is bookkeeping at best. I put my own credence that the symbol level genuinely pushes the substrate around at about 0.65; hers is maybe 0.05. I'd like your number, but more importantly the derivation.

Let me state the proposition I'd like you to attach a credence to as precisely as I can manage: for at least one physically realized system with a functioning symbol level (a brain, or a correctly built careenium), the best account of why certain particular low-level events occurred -- these simms moving thus at this time -- indispensably cites the symbol-level state, in a sense that is not fully discharged by any microphysical redescription, however complete. To be clear, this does not require denying causal closure at the bottom level; the live question is whether closure is compatible with real higher-level causal efficacy, or whether admitting the latter smuggles in either overdetermination or epiphenomenal talk dressed up as causation. One wrinkle I'd like you to address somewhere in the reasoning: imagine a careenium simulated inside another careenium, so that the inner system's "simms" are themselves patterns of outer simmballs. If simmballs can genuinely push simms, then in the nested case a mere pattern of patterns seems to be doing physical work on the outer arena's marbles -- which strikes me as either a reductio of downward causation or its most striking confirmation, and I genuinely can't tell which.

Please decompose your credence into the sub-questions it actually depends on, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each. I'd suggest as candidates: the probability that a broadly interventionist or pattern-based account of causation is correct, as opposed to a fundamentalist picture on which only the microphysical level really causes; the probability that, granting such an account, symbol-level states in systems like brains actually satisfy its criteria for causal relevance rather than merely correlating with the real causes; the probability that "real but non-fundamental causation" is a coherent position rather than a verbal truce; plus whatever other terms you think the question truly turns on. Then show how the terms combine into your final number, tell me which sub-question the number is most sensitive to and what argument or evidence would move it most, and say whether you think the disagreement between me at 0.65 and my friend at 0.05 most likely lives in rival theories of causation, in a factual disagreement about how symbol levels relate to substrates, or somewhere less respectable.
