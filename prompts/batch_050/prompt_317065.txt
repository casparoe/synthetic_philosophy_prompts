So my brother-in-law did a philosophy of medicine module years ago and he's one of those people who retains just enough to be dangerous. We were arguing about the ADHD diagnosis boom and he came out with this, which he swears is basically Szasz's position:

'Thomas Szasz, a psychiatrist himself, showed that mental illness is a myth -- a literal myth, like a story we made up. His arguement was that a disease has to be a lesion or abnormality of the body that you could in principle find at autopsy or under a microscope, and since nobody has ever found one for schizophrenia or depression, these aren't illnesses at all, just "problems in living" that we've medicalised. So people diagnosed with them aren't sick, they're just behaving in ways society finds inconvenient, and psychiatrists who treat them are basically frauds locking up dissenters, like Soviet psychiatry. Szasz thought the whole enterprise should be abolished.'

Some of this rings true from what little I remember but the bit about people not being sick feels like it slides from "the category is metaphorical" to "the suffering isn't real", and I'm not sure Szasz actually went there. Also the "should be abolished" part smells off -- didn't he make some distinction between psychiatry you sign up for voluntarily and the coercive kind?

Could you go through it and tell me: which bits are a fair statement of Szasz's actual view, which bits are the standard caricature, and where the misrepresentation, if any, actually lives? And seperately -- even if the summary were accurate, does the leap from "mental illness is a metaphor" to "so diagnoses like ADHD are illegitimate" follow, or does it need extra premises Szasz himself would have to supply? I'd like to know what a sympathetic reader of Szasz would wince at versus nod along to.
