I'm a novelist working on a book where the narrator gradually figures out he's a character and starts rewriting the chapters he's stuck in. At my writing group people keep saying it's like Escher's Drawing Hands -- the hand drawing the hand that's drawing it -- and they've used this to claim my ending, where the narrator "escapes" his plot and addresses me directly, genuinely dissolves the author/character hierarchy and grants him something like real agency. I want to push back but can't quite articulate why. Audit the analogy for me: the correspondence table first (what's the left hand, what's the right hand, what's the paper, who's Escher), then where it breaks. Specifically: is there anything inside my novel that plays the role of the actual M.C. Escher standing outside the lithograph, untouched by the loop? Is my narrator's rebellion anything more than me writing a rebellion? Does the conclusion my group wants -- that the hierarchy collapses and the character achieves real freedom -- survive once the disanalogies are laid out, or was the Escher picture smuggling it in the whole time? And if there's a better image for what I'm actually doing, I'd definately take it.
