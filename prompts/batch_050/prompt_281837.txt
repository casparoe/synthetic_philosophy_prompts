A friend and I have been arguing about peer disagreement all semester — she's a conciliationist (when an epistemic peer disagrees with you, split the difference, at least partly) and I hold some version of the steadfast view (if your reasoning was good, discovering that a peer disagreed isn't itself much of a reason to change your mind). We've gone in circles, so we want to do this properly: draft the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between us would actually produce. It should have (1) the claims we'd both sign, like what higher-order evidence even is and that it at least sometimes should move you; (2) the exact residual disagreements, worded so each of us would accept our own side's formulation — I suspect the real ones are whether evidence of disagreement gets pooled symmetrically with first-order evidence, whether you can bracket your own disputed reasoning without also bracketing the evidence that made you trust your peer's judgment in the first place, and whether conciliationism self-defeats given that conciliationism itself is peer-disputed; and (3) for each disagreement, a concrete case both of us agree bears on it (e.g. two equally careful logicians check the same proof and only one finds a flaw) with each side's prediction of what the rational agent should do. If part of our fight is just verbal — like over what 'equal weight' means — flag that as verbal rather than pretending it's a crux. Start directly with the joint statement itself, no preface or framing.
