So I'm in a grad seminar on conceptual ethics this term and we just wrapped up a unit on ameliorative analysis — the idea, roughly associated with Sally Haslanger's work, that instead of asking what concepts like race, gender, or knowledge actually mean, philosophers should ask what we want those concepts to do for us, and then engineer them to serve those purposes. I found it exciting at first, but the more I think about it the more suspicious I get, and here's why. A friend of mine works in hospital administration, and her ethics committee is genuinely trying to re-engineer their working concept of "informed consent" so that it covers things like AI-assisted diagnosis, where the patient can't really understand the reasoning even in principle. Her problem, and the thing that keeps bugging me, is what you might call the interface issue: even if her committee perfects the concept internally, patients, insurers, courts, and other hospitals will keep operating with the old one, so the engineered concept might function beautifully inside the institution while generating confusion or even injustice at every boundary where it meets the un-engineered world.

Write an essay on this controversy: when a concept is causing practical or political trouble, should we try to ameliorate/re-engineer it to serve our purposes, or stick to carefully analysing the concept as it actually functions in ordinary thought and language? Use the hospital consent case and the interface issue as your central example, but feel free to bring in other cases (the ameliorative projects around race or gender are the obvious ones). Weigh the strongest considerations on both sides — for instance, the charge that amelioration smuggles in the theorist's own political goals under the guise of analysis, versus the charge that pure descriptive analysis just enshrines the status quo. Don't end on a wishy-washy "both approaches have merit" note; take a side and defend it, even if your verdict is conditional (e.g., ameliorate only under certain circumstances, with certain safeguards against the interface problem). Roughly 700-900 words is fine.
