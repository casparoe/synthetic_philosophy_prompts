I am a PhD student in philosophy of mathematics, and I keep encountering the line that 'Gödel showed mathematics cannot prove its own consistency' — in seminars, in online discussions, and most recently from a colleague who used it to dismiss my suggestion that Gentzen's consistency proof for Peano arithmetic gives us genuine reason to believe PA consistent. His reply was essentially that Gödel settled the matter: any such proof must be circular or must assume stronger principles than the ones it certifies. Could you disambiguate the precise theses this slogan might be expressing — which systems it ranges over, what counts as a system proving its own consistency, what class of methods a consistency proof may use, and whether the claim is a theorem, an epistemological gloss on a theorem, or something stronger — and for each reading say whether it is trivial, proven, contentious, or false? In particular, I would like to know which readings, if any, actually support my colleague's dismissal of Gentzen's proof, and what the strongest defensible reading implies about how seriously we should take the possibility that PA is in fact inconsistent. Please lay the readings out as a menu with verdicts rather than settling on one interpretation, and flag which reading you suspect people usually intend when they invoke the slogan.
