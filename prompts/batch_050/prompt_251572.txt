So I've been lurking on voting theory threads for years (the LessWrong election-method discourses, the occasional ACX comments section fight, etc.) and I realized I've never actually stress-tested my own position, which is basically a grab-bag of things I've absorbed. Help me audit it. Here's what I believe, roughly:

(1) Strategic voting is a defect in a voting system, not in voters. If a system regularly rewards people for voting against their honest preferences, that's the system failing at its one job, and a well-designed system should make honesty a good strategy for a normal voter. (2) Relatedly, I think the right fix for plurality voting in the US is some form of ranked ballot -- IRV is the realistic one, even if condorcet methods are prettier in theory. If a majority of voters prefer candidate A to candidate B, B just shouldn't be able to win, and ranked ballots get us closer to that. (3) Arrow's theorem shows there's no perfect voting system, so the whole search for the "correct" method is misguided and we should just pick something reasonable and move on. (4) At the same time I obviously think some systems are clearly better than others -- plurality is genuinely bad and this isn't just a matter of taste. (5) There's a theorem I've seen people cite, something like Gibbard-Satterthwaite, that says every nondictatorial system with three or more options is manipulable. My read has always been that this is one of those results that's technically true but practically overrated, since it assumes voters have full information about everyone else's ballots and can coordinate, which real electorates basically never do. So I don't think it should carry much weight in actual reform debates.

Is this a coherent package? I have a vague feeling that (1) and (5) are doing something funny together, and maybe (2) and (3) as well, but I can't pin down where the actual collisions are versus where things only look contradictory. Tell me which pairs genuinely conflict, how bad each conflict is, and what the cheapest fix is for each -- like, what's the minimal edit to my position that restores consistency, and what does it cost me. If one single revision cleans up most of the mess, flag that one. And if one of these "tensions" actually dissolves once you state it carefully, say that too, because I'd rather know.
