I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and part of my job is fact-checking a colleague's long essay on the meaning of freedom before it goes to press. The essay builds its argument through a series of small cases, and my colleague — who has strong, stable intuitions about which of her characters count as free — has ruled on each so far. Her verdicts: a prisoner kept in a locked cell under a warden's arbitrary rule is unfree; a prisoner whose door stands unlocked and unguarded, and who stays voluntarily, is free; a prisoner who longs to leave but never tests his door, which has been locked without his knowledge, is unfree; an employee whose capricious boss constantly meddles in her work is unfree; and a hermit who has withdrawn to the mountains and wants nothing but his solitude is free. The final vignette, on which the essay's conclusion is supposed to turn, describes a contented slave whose master is unfailingly benevolent and has never once issued an order or interfered with her in any way, though he retains the legal power to do so at any moment, and she knows it.

Before I query her, I'd like your prediction of her verdict on that last case — and, more importantly, your account of the rule she appears to be running. As far as I can tell, her verdicts so far are jointly consistent with more than one principle: something like a pure interference view (unfree wherever another's conduct blocks your options, regardless of anyone's further power over you), a domination view (unfree wherever you live at another's mercy, even if that mercy is never withdrawn), and a desire-satisfaction view (unfree wherever you cannot do what you in fact want). If I've misread the space of possibilities, say so and propose better candidates. Then tell me what each candidate rule predicts about the contented slave, which prediction you would actually bet on given her track record and why, which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog to the new case, and what further vignette — one I might suggest she add to the essay — would most cleanly distinguish the rules her record so far does not separate.
