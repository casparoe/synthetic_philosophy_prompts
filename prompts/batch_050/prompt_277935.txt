Suppose you are advising the director of a small research institute that has just received funding to support six researchers for five years on problems of radical interpretation: situations in which meaning must be assigned to a signal with no shared language, no interaction with informants, and no pre-existing translation manual. The institute has shortlisted three application areas:

(i) assessing whether large language models genuinely deploy our concepts or merely produce output that a charitable interpreter can read those concepts into;
(ii) preparing methods for the decipherment of a hypothetical extraterrestrial transmission, should one ever arrive;
(iii) building translation procedures for pairs of human languages with no living bilinguals and no parallel texts.

Quine's thesis of the indeterminacy of translation — the gavagai problem from Word and Object and the debates it spawned about reference, charity, and whether there is any fact of the matter about what a term picks out — clearly bears on all three areas, but not in the same way. A complication: the institute's principal funder is sympathetic to the deflationary view that indeterminacy is a pseudo-problem that dissolves once you take seriously the constraints of successful communication, and needs persuading that any of this deserves money at all.

Which open questions arising from the indeterminacy thesis should the institute prioritise, and how should it divide its six positions across the three application areas? I am less interested in the list itself than in the reasoning. For each question you select, explain why it is upstream of other work (or downstream and therefore deferrable), how tractable it is for philosophers as opposed to engineers, and what would count as progress on it within five years. Please also say which alleged implications of indeterminacy you consider overrated — for instance, does the thesis, if true, genuinely undermine machine-translation benchmarks or interpretability work on language models, or is it practically idle there — and address the funder's deflationary worry directly. Finally, indicate what evidence or results over the funding period would move a question up or down your ranking. The deliverable should be a defended agenda rather than a survey of the literature. Please write your answer in British English.
