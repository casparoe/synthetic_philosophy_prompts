I manage claims operations at a mid-sized insurance company, and I need to decide in the next month which of two machine-learning systems to deploy for triaging incoming claims before they reach our human adjusters. Both systems read the claim file and produce an approve/deny/escalate recommendation, along with a short written rationale that walks through the policy provisions it applied.

Model One was evaluated and refined purely on its final recommendations: over six months of back-testing, it agreed with our senior adjusters' decisions 99% of the time. Model Two was developed differently — adjusters reviewed its written rationales step by step and corrected the reasoning, not just the conclusions — but its final agreement rate is slightly lower, around 97%.

Last week, one of our engineers read a few hundred of Model One's rationales out of curiosity. In a small but real fraction of cases where the final recommendation was correct, the rationale got the policy wrong: it cited a provision that doesn't apply, or misstated a coverage limit, yet landed on the right answer anyway. Since nobody ever checked the rationales during training, we have no idea how often this happens, and there's no way to detect it from the outcomes alone.

My VP's position is simple: deploy Model One. It produces the right answers, and regulators and customers will judge us on the decisions we make, not on the essays we write while making them. I'm uneasy, but I'm struggling to articulate why. If the conclusions are verifiably right, what exactly is the harm in the reasoning being wrong?

And I have a second worry cutting the other way. Once Model Two's developers started grading its rationales, wasn't the system really just learning to produce rationales that adjusters like to read? Maybe its visible reasoning is now polished theater, while whatever actually drives its conclusions happens somewhere no one is looking. If so, Model Two's extra supervision bought us the appearance of oversight rather than the reality.

Am I right to hesitate about Model One, or is my VP? And between my two worries — that Model One is right for the wrong reasons, and that Model Two only looks well-reasoned — which should weigh more heavily in the decision?
