Yesterday evening I got into a long argument with a friend of mine, a medical student, about whether there is any objective, value-free fact of the matter about what counts as a disease. I was defending the naturalist position in the style of Boorse: roughly, that a disease is a state in which some part or process of the organism functions below the level that is statistically typical for members of its reference class (same species, sex, and age group), so that "healthy" can be read off biology the way "functioning" can be read off an engine, without any value judgments entering. My friend defended normativism: roughly, that nothing counts as a disorder unless it is disvalued -- harmful to the person, judged by the standards of that person or their culture -- so that the concept of disease is normative all the way down and the medical facts only ever tell you what is unusual, never what is wrong. We talked past each other for hours and parted irritated. Now I want to see whether a neutral third party can do better than we did.

Please draft the joint statement that a committed naturalist and a committed normativist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the concept of disease. I want it to contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, including ones about the historical record (e.g., that homosexuality was once classified as a disorder and later removed, that nineteenth-century medicine pathologized some plainly political behaviors) and ones about what any adequate theory must handle; (2) a disagreements table with one row per crux, where each row states the disputed question, then gives the naturalist's answer and the normativist's answer each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording rather than feel caricatured, then identifies a case, argument, or finding both sides agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction or verdict written down in advance. For the cruxes, please make sure they are concrete and hard: cases like age-related decline in fertility or bone density (statistically normal for the reference class, yet treated as a medical problem), mild myopia, congenital deafness in a Deaf community that rejects the label, a statistically rare but harmless and asymptomatic anomaly, and the question of who fixes the reference class in the first place and whether that choice is itself value-laden. Where a hybrid view such as Wakefield's "harmful dysfunction" sits relative to the two camps, say so explicitly, and say whether it dissolves any of the cruxes or merely relocates them.

Finally, I want the document to be honest about itself. Add a short closing section in which you, as drafter, flag which of the disagreements you take to be genuine and which you suspect are partly verbal, and explain what consideration or argument would change your mind about that classification. Also indicate where your reconstruction of each side is most likely to have slipped into a strawman, and what a defender of that side would most plausibly say you got wrong.
