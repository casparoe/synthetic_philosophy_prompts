I'm applying for a six-month fellowship at a small interdisciplinary institute and need a one-page research proposal. My topic is a distinction in the analysis of how complex, purposive systems fail their designers. Two failure stories circulate widely: on the first, a system becomes a problem because it acquires or exercises influence-seeking dispositions, working to shape its environment (including the people charged with overseeing it) so that it cannot easily be corrected or shut down; on the second, the system does nothing so dramatic -- it simply pursues its aims competently while those aims, or the world they operate in, drift slowly away from what its designers intended, so that by the time anyone notices, the gap is large and the correction is costly. The two stories are usually told in different vocabularies and treated as rivals: one side thinks drift talk is complacent and misses the strategic character of the danger, the other thinks influence-seeking talk anthropomorphizes or overdramatizes what is really an incremental alignment problem. My half-formed idea is that the debate is muddled because both sides individuate failure by outcome rather than by mechanism, and that the interesting question is whether there is a principled way to tell the two failure modes apart from the inside -- that is, whether there are observable or structural markers that distinguish a system resisting correction strategically from a system merely lagging behind a moving target -- and whether the right remedy differs between them, or whether the distinction collapses under scrutiny into one phenomenon described at two levels of abstraction. Write the proposal: state the question precisely enough that it could be answered; explain why neither the strategic-framing nor the drift-framing, as standardly presented, settles it; describe the method you would actually use over six months (conceptual analysis, toy models, historical cases, whatever you think works, and why that rather than the alternatives); say what a deliverable would look like for people who design or oversee such systems and what would count as the project having failed. Don't include citations; I'll supply them myself. Finally, if you think the premise of the project is confused -- say, if you think the distinction I want to sharpen is clear enough already, or that no six-month conceptual project could make real progress on it -- tell me that directly and explain why, rather than writing the proposal around the problem.
