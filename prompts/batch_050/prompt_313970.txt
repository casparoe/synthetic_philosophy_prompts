So I'm a PhD student in phil of physics, and in our seminar this semester something keeps bugging me. Whenever the status of thought experiments comes up -- Galileo tying the heavy and light bodies together, Einstein imagining chasing a light beam as a teenager, that kind of thing -- people reach for the same move: a thought experiment is just a real experiment without the equipment. You set up initial conditions, you let the world "run" in your head, and you read off the result. And then this analogy gets leaned on hard to settle the actual philosophical dispute: the conclusion is supposed to be that thought experiments deliver genuine empirical knowledge about the physical world, not just cleaned-up introspection or disguised logical analysis. My supervisor even used it to dismiss the Norton-style view that thought experiments are just arguments in fancy dress -- as in, no, they're experiments, and experiments aren't arguments.

Can you audit this analogy for me? I want the full treatment:

First, the correspondence table. What plays the role of the apparatus, the controls, the measurement, the replication, the error bars, the null result? Like, is "imagine the setup clearly" supposed to be calibration? Is consulting my physical intuitions supposed to be taking a reading? Spell out the mapping as charitably as you can.

Second, where it breaks, and specifically which breaks matter for the conclusion it's being used to support. The one that seems fatal to me: real experiments can surprise you because nature pushes back, but nothing in the armchair case is pushing back except my own priors -- so how does new information get in? Is the analogy smuggling in an oracle? But maybe there are subtler disanalogies too -- e.g., real experiments are replicable by others with different biases, while my "lab" runs on my intuitions specifically; or the role of idealization, where "stripping away friction" is treated like cleaning the apparatus but maybe it's actually changing what's being measured.

Third: which disanalogies are fatal to the "genuine empirical knowledge" conclusion and which are just noise. And if the conclusion doesn't survive, is there a better analogy on offer -- like, are thought experiments more like debugging a theory, or consistency checks, or proofs by contradiction? Or does something survive of the experimental picture after all?

Bonus if you can say whether the Galileo case and the Einstein case even fit the analogy equally well, since one of them seems to produce a contradiction and the other seems to produce... a hunch that turned out right.
