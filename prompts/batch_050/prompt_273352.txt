I'm a novelist, and the vocabulary I actually think in is craft vocabulary: the author's godlike relation to her characters, frame narratives and stories-within-stories, characters who suspect they're in a book, unfinished manuscripts abandoned in drawers, the question of whether a character can be said to suffer or only to be written as suffering. Restate the simulation argument in those terms, as precisely as the material allows: the trilemma that either civilizations like ours almost never reach the stage of running detailed ancestor-simulations, or they reach it and almost never bother, or we should assign substantial credence to living inside such a simulation ourselves. I want each load-bearing piece mapped onto something from fiction -- the simulator as author, simulated minds as characters, the indifference hypothesis as the drawer full of abandoned drafts, nested simulations as frames within frames. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the argument survive translation cleanly?
