Separately I've been messing around building a toy proof checker as a hobby project, and I noticed that the inference rules are just hardcoded in, not proved -- which now feels related, but I can't quite say how.

Please describe the most important implications of the tortoise regress, ranked by how much they'd actually change what I do, for things like: whether deduction can be justified to a genuine sceptic at all or whether justification always has to stop somewhere; whether logical validity is the kind of thing that can be taught vs. the kind of thing a student has to already "get"; what I should actually say to my student that isn't either circular or dismissive ("that's just what valid means" feels like a cop-out); and what, if anything, the regress implies for formal systems like my proof checker, where the rules are implemented rather than derived -- does the tortoise show there's something fundamentally different between using a rule and stating it, and does that matter for whether a machine can really "reason"?
