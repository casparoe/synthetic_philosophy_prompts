So my friend works at a small startup that builds "memory companions" — chatbots trained on a dead person's texts, emails, voicemails, that kind of thing — and she's been bugging me to help her think through the ethics side because apparently nobody at the company has read any philosophy since high school. I read some of the griefbots literature a while back and honestly found it a bit scattered: some people talk about consent of the deceased, some about whether grieving people get stuck and can't move on, some about the bots saying things the real person never would have, some about ownership of the data. But I can't tell which of these are the load-bearing questions and which are side quests.

Here's what I actually want: imagine you're helping me put together a short research agenda for someone (maybe me, maybe a grad student I know) who has a year or so to work on the ethics of simulating the dead, and who wants whatever they produce to actually matter for how these products get built and regulated in the next few years. Which open questions in this area are most worth settling first, and in what order? I'm not asking for a survey of everything anyone has worried about — I want a defended ranking. For each question you include: why it's upstream of other debates (or isn't), how tractable it is for a philosopher working alone rather than, say, an empirical psychologist who could run studies on grieving users, and what would change its place in the ranking.

A couple of framings I've been toying with, in case they help or in case you want to push back on them: one, I suspect there's an important difference between a griefbot that functions like a really good memorial (something you visit, like a grave) versus one that functions like a continued relationship (something that messages you first, remembers your birthday, develops), and that a lot of the ethical debates conflate these. Two, I keep wondering whether the interesting question isn't "should we build these" but "who gets a say" — the dead person's past self, their estate, the grieving user, the other people whose messages are in the training data — and whether existing legal categories like inheritance and likeness rights are even the right shape for this. Tell me whether those are the right things to be obsessed with or whether I'm missing something more fundamental. Also, please no reading lists — I want the reasoning, not homework.
