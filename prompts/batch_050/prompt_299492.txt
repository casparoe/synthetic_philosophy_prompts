I'm writing a term paper for a graduate seminar on contractualism, and the topic I've landed on is the aggregation problem: the charge that Scanlon's theory, because it insists on assessing principles through the complaints of individuals one at a time, can't handle cases where the numbers clearly seem to matter. Before I read any more of the proliferating literature proposing "fixes" -- partially aggregative contractualism, claims-weighting schemes, relevance filters, and so on -- I want to write down, independently, what any adequate fix would actually have to accomplish. Write the desiderata: a requirements list for a contractualist treatment of numbers, with the case or consideration motivating each requirement, and a judgment about which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp (the aggregators' insistence that numbers always count, the purists' insistence that trivial claims never combine, and so on). If some subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable -- say, never letting one million headaches outweigh one death while still letting five deaths outweigh one death -- that is precisely the result I want surfaced, not smoothed over.

To anchor the list, here are the cases I think any candidate theory must get right, though you should challenge my list if I've misclassified one: a choice between saving one person from death and relieving a million people of moderate headaches; a standard rescue case where you can save either one stranger or five strangers, all with equally strong claims (a like-for-like case where most people think you must save the five); Scanlon's transmitter-style case, where averting a serious injury to one worker means disappointing a vast audience's mild enjoyment; a policy case imposing tiny risks of serious harm on very large populations; and a mixed case where claims of different strengths compete and numbers differ on both sides. For each desideratum, tell me which of these cases it exists to handle. I also want you to identify which single requirement knocks out the most existing proposals, and to keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring Scanlon's own proposal, Kamm's, Voorhoeve's, or the partially aggregative views -- a brief note on how the main contenders fare against the final list is enough.

On calibration: please be explicit about your own uncertainty. For whichever desideratum you are least confident belongs on the list as non-negotiable, say what argument, thought experiment, or intuition-pump would change your mind and lead you to demote it to a camp-specific preference (or promote it to a genuine requirement), and flag where your overall judgment is most hostage to contested intuitions about rescue cases. Format: a numbered requirements list, each item tagged negotiable or non-negotiable with its motivating case, followed by the brief contender check, and ending with a one-sentence summary of the deepest tension in the list as its own final line.
