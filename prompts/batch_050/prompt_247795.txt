I do hand-lettering as a hobby — no formal training in design, philosophy, or anything else relevant — and I keep running into something I cannot find a word for. When I change how I draw one letter in an alphabet I'm developing — say I start giving the lowercase 'r' a rounded shoulder instead of an angular one — the change refuses to stay inside the 'r'. Within a line or two, the 'n', the 'm', and the 'h' begin to look like intruders until I adjust them as well, even though none of them contains the part I altered. Stranger still: shown only the 'h', 'e', and 'a' of an unfamiliar typeface, I can usually sketch a fair guess at its 'g' and 'k', and other hobbyists' guesses largely agree with mine. So there seems to be some quality that no single letter contains, that exists only across the set, that permits extrapolation to letters never seen, and that is violated the moment one letter departs from it. Calling it 'style' feels too broad, since that word also covers paintings, prose, and clothing. Is there an established name for this ensemble-level quality specifically, or for the way a local alteration propagates across a category's members? If it sits near existing notions — gestalt, family resemblance, and so on — I would like to know precisely where it diverges from each of them. And if nothing quite fits, what would you call it, and where are its boundaries: does an individual's handwriting have it, does a composer's body of work, does an architectural tradition?
