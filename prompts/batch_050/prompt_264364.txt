So I'm working through something that's bugging me and I want a proper philosophical reconstruction, not a policy wonk take.

The contrast is roughly this. In most of western Europe -- think Germany, France, Italy -- commercial surrogacy is flatly banned. Not just unregulated: in Germany its actually a criminal offense for a doctor to do the embryo transfer if they know the woman intends to hand the child over, and the contracts are void, and the intended parents sometimes have to formally adopt their own genetic kid afterwards. Egg donation is similarly restricted to "altruistic" donation with expense reimbursement only. Meanwhile in parts of the US (California especially) you have a full-blown market: surrogacy agencies, enforceable contracts, pre-birth parentage orders, surrogates paid tens of thousands of dollars, and egg donor listings where the compensation openly scales with the donor's SAT scores, height, and whether she went to an Ivy.

Here's what I find philosophically juicy, and where I want you to dig:

1. Neither regime bans the *activity*. Europe is fine with a friend carrying a baby for free, and fine with unpaid egg donation. So in both systems the money is doing all the normative work -- the exact same physical acts flip from permitted to criminal when a price tag is attached. What theory of exchange does that presuppose? That payment corrupts an otherwise fine act, or that payment reveals coercion that was there all along, or what?

2. The Americans have their own weird hangup: nobody in that industry will call the money a *price*. Its always "compensation for time and effort," the surrogate is never "selling a baby," and I think in most jurisdictions you technically can't contract for the child, only for the gestational services plus a surrender of parental rights. What does this euphemism layer presuppose? Is it a fig leaf over the same thing Europe bans, or does the services/body distinction actually do real work?

3. Both regimes are totally fine with paid sperm donation. So whatever each system presupposes about the body, markets, and exploitation apparently doesn't apply symmetrically across genders. Is there a coherent implicit principle there (degree of bodily invasion? bonding? something worse?) or is this just where each regime's theory breaks down?
