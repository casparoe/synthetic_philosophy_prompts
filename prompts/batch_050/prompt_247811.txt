I've spent years reading the alignment-adjacent corners of the rationalist blogosphere, and I've gradually converged on a personal position about how ethical reasoning ought to be implemented in machines. Before I commit it to a longer essay, I'd like an audit of whether the package is internally coherent. Here are my commitments:

(1) The right architecture is consequentialist at its core: a learned or aggregated utility function over outcomes, maximized in expectation. Explicit rule-based systems strike me as unmaintainable -- rules conflict, exceptions multiply, and the patching process just reintroduces the designer's implicit judgment at a less transparent level. Something in the family of inverse reinforcement learning or cooperative value learning is the only approach I can see scaling.

(2) The system should remain uncertain between moral theories rather than being fitted to one. It should carry a distribution over candidate theories -- consequentialist, deontological, contractualist, and so on -- and act so as to maximize expected choiceworthiness across that distribution, rather than collapsing the uncertainty prematurely into a single point estimate of the true ethics.

(3) The system must be corrigible: it should not merely permit but actively prefer human oversight, including being shut down, modified, or having its objectives rewritten, and it should not instrumentally resist any of these.

(4) I'm comfortable deploying such systems in domains where humans cannot independently verify the quality of individual outputs -- large-scale logistics, triage, resource allocation -- provided the system reports high expected value for its decisions and is corrigible in the sense of (3). The oversight requirement is satisfied by the system's willingness to be inspected and overridden, even where the overseers can't themselves check the reasoning.

(5) I don't think questions of machine consciousness or moral patienthood need to be settled before this research proceeds; they're a separate problem, and premature either way.

I hold all five and none strikes me as exotic -- each is recognizably held by serious people in this space. But I've never stress-tested them as a set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and how severe is each tension? For each one, what is the cheapest revision that resolves it, and what does that revision cost? If you think some apparent tension here dissolves on inspection rather than requiring any revision, say so explicitly and show why -- I'd rather be told that two of my commitments merely look incompatible than be handed a fix for a problem I don't have. Order the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as a uniform list, and if one single revision would clean up the most, identify it. I'm not attached to any particular commitment surviving; I want to know what the set actually commits me to.
