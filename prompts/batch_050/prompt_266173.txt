I've been thinking about Wittgenstein's On Certainty on my own, without formal training, and I keep running into two readings of his "hinge" commitments -- those background certainties (like that the world existed long before I was born) that all our doubting and checking silently relies on. One camp treats hinges as belief-like: they can be true or false, they stand in logical relations to our other beliefs, and the interesting question is whether we can know them or are justified in them. The other camp treats hinges as something else entirely: ungrounded ways of acting, more like the rules that make the game of justifying possible than moves within it, so that calling them true, known, or justified is a category mistake. Write the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want: a numbered list of claims both sides accept (e.g., about why ordinary doubt must stop somewhere, and what Moore-style attempts to prove such certainties get wrong); a table of their residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would endorse the wording; the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether a hinge can intelligibly be called true, and another is what happens when two people hold genuinely different hinges -- and for each crux, a concrete case both sides agree bears on it (say, an isolated community with radically different bedrock certainties, or a person who loses one of their hinges), with each side's prediction about that case written down in advance.
