So I'm trying to pin down what seperates manipulation from legitimate persuasion, and I've collected the standard tests people usually give for telling them apart:

1. The bypass test: persuasion addresses your capacity for reasons, manipulation routes around it — subliminal ads, guilt trips, playing on insecurities. Problem: plenty of manipulation works out in the open on fully conscious deliberation, and plenty of perfectly benign influence (a friend's encouraging tone of voice) bypasses reason too.

2. The transparency test: influence is manipulative if it wouldn't survive the target finding out exactly how it works on them. Problem: people often cheerfully accept being nudged even after it's explained, and some fully disclosed, out-in-the-open pressure still feels manipulative.

3. The interest test: manipulation serves the influencer's interests at your expense; persuasion aims at your good or at least at shared ground. Problem: paternalistic cases — tricking your chronically ill friend into finally seeing a doctor — still seem manipulative even though they serve her interests, not yours.

4. The autonomy test: manipulation treats you as a tool rather than respecting you as a self-governing agent. Problem: this mostly relocates the question into "what counts as respecting agency," and half of ordinary social life uses people as means in ways nobody would call manipulation.

5. The deception test: manipulation involves lying, misleading, or strategically hiding information; persuasion plays it straight. Problem: flattery, charm, strategic sincerity — you can manipulate someone without uttering a single falsehood.

I have the feeling there's a better way of carving this up that isn't on my list. Give me one more test that is genuinely different from these five — not a rewording, not a special case of one of them, and not just two of them stapled together. Then run your test on these three cases:

(a) a political campaign that microtargets each voter with truthful messages individually calibrated to their psychological profile, built from data the voters don't know was collected;
(b) a teacher who deliberately structures her syllabus so students are maximally likely to fall in love with the subject;
(c) a friend who knows you can never back down from a dare and dares you to quit smoking.

Then give me your answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

{
  "test_name": "a short label for your test",
  "the_test": "state the criterion in 3-4 sentences, precise enough to actually apply",
  "why_its_new": "explain why it isn't a rewording, special case, or combination of tests 1-5",
  "case_a_verdict": "manipulation or legitimate persuasion, plus one sentence of justification",
  "case_b_verdict": "same format",
  "case_c_verdict": "same format",
  "strongest_objection": "the best counterexample to your own test you can come up with",
  "does_it_survive": "yes or no, and why"
}

Feel free to think out loud a bit before the JSON, but the JSON is what I actually care about, so make sure it's valid and every field is there.
