As a longtime reader of LessWrong and adjacent rationalist forums, I have seen philosophical-zombie discussions repeatedly cycle through the same moves: one side treats the conceivability of a physical duplicate without experience as decisive, while the other treats the intuition of an explanatory residue as a predictable cognitive illusion. I want something more constructive than another debate transcript.

Draft the joint statement that could result from a six-month adversarial collaboration between these two participants:

- A modal rationalist property dualist, who holds that a philosophical zombie is ideally conceivable, that ideal conceivability is strong evidence of metaphysical possibility, and that such a possibility shows phenomenal consciousness is not entailed by the complete physical facts.
- An illusionist functionalist, who holds that consciousness consists in the relevant forms of access, self-modeling, integration, and reportability, and that the apparent conceivability of a zombie arises from the peculiar structure of introspective concepts rather than from any further nonphysical property.

The statement should be something both could sign without feeling that their position has been trivialized, presupposed away, or described only in the other side’s vocabulary. Do not adjudicate the debate, provide a history of the literature, or give citations. The task is to draft the collaborative document itself.

Use this format:

1. Agreed claims. Give ten to fifteen numbered claims that are substantive rather than diplomatically empty. These should cover, at minimum, the distinctions among logical, nomological, and metaphysical possibility; the intended definition of a philosophical zombie; the validity of the conceivability argument conditional on its premises; the fact that no behavioral or third-person physical test could distinguish the stipulated pair; and the difference between explaining reports of experience and explaining experience itself.

2. Genuine disagreements. Provide a table with columns for the issue, the property dualist’s formulation, the illusionist’s formulation, and the kind of disagreement involved: metaphysical, epistemological, semantic, methodological, or some combination. Phrase each position so its own advocate would accept the wording. Include disagreements about the evidential force of conceivability, the relation between phenomenal concepts and their referents, the causal efficacy of consciousness, whether explanatory closure supports ontological identity, and whether introspection reveals its own metaphysical nature.

3. Verbal or derivative disagreements. Identify at least three apparent disagreements that both parties now recognize as terminological, dependent on a deeper crux, or otherwise not independently substantive. Explain what each side had previously been claiming and what both can now agree the dispute was actually about.

4. Cruxes. State four to six cruxes in the double-crux sense: sub-questions such that a sufficiently clear answer would oblige at least one side to revise its position. For each crux, specify an argument, thought experiment, conceptual test, or possible scientific result that both parties agree would bear on it. Then record in advance what each side predicts that consideration will show. Do not treat every intuition pump as a crux merely because it makes one side’s view feel obvious.

5. The Introspective Reporter case. Both parties must analyze this stipulated case: a complete physical simulation of a human philosopher contains a detailed recursive self-model and, solely through ordinary causal processes, sincerely reports that it is directly acquainted with a private quality not captured by any physical or functional description. A stipulated zombie counterpart has the same self-model, produces the same reports, and would give the same explanations under every physical intervention. Every change to either system’s self-model produces the same change in its claims about experience. Ask what, if anything, the physical description omits in each case; why both systems describe their alleged acquaintance in the same way; and whether the complete causal explanation of the report strengthens, weakens, or leaves untouched the conceivability argument. The two participants should identify precisely where their analyses diverge.

6. Jointly signed conclusion. End with a short section stating what both parties now think the zombie argument establishes, what it does not establish, and what kind of future argument or discovery would most likely change one of their minds.

Where the two sides cannot agree on a proposition, prefer two exactly matched formulations over a vague compromise. Where a familiar slogan hides several claims, decompose it. Where one side’s standard reply depends on a contested theory of concepts, causation, explanation, or introspection, make that dependence explicit.
