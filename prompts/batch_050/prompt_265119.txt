I help run grants for a midsize conservation nonprofit, and we keep getting pulled into fights about whether to intervene in wild animal suffering: mange in urban coyotes, birds hitting windows during migration, drought years where herbivores starve slowly, a proposal to suppress a predator that is causing awful deaths but also keeping another population from exploding. The board wants something more principled than whoever has the cutest video wins. Act like a philosophically literate conservation advisor and think carefully, maybe brainstorm the failure modes before settling the final format. I want a reusable decision procedure we can run in under two hours with a program officer, one ecologist, and me, for grants between 5k and 250k, where the default is no and the ED has to sign off on yes. The seed principle is roughly: expected severe suffering reduced per dollar matters, but so do sentience uncertainty, reversibility, scale, ecosystem knock-ons, whether the harm is human-caused, whether intervention creates dependency or incentives for worse outcomes later, and whether we are confusing helping visible individuals with making the larger system worse. Turn that into an ordered checklist or flowchart in words: exact questions, what evidence is enough at each step, where uncertainty gets a default rather than a shrug, which thresholds are explicitly values choices and must be kicked upstairs, what monitoring/abandonment rules attach to any yes, and what the procedure cannot decide. Please flag which steps are real procedure and which are judgment calls in a lab coat.

Then apply the procedure to three made-up-but-realistic proposals: treating mange in a coyote population that people love seeing in parks; emergency feeding of deer during a once-in-decades drought when models say a crash is likely either way; and contraception for an overabundant grazers whose numbers are degrading habitat for many smaller species. Make at least one application come out with an answer I probably will not like emotionally, and say so. Also say how confident you are in the overall design and name the single point you are least sure about. No bibliography or citations; use common philosophy and ecology at the level a smart generalist can check. There is defintely no budget for a scholar, so it has to be usable.
