I'm working up a position statement for a reading group on Dennett's tower of generate-and-test (Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian creatures) and I want someone to kick the tires on it before I share. Here's roughly what I hold:

1. The four kinds form a real hierarchy: each kind presupposes the machinery of all the kinds below it, so every Gregorian creature is also Popperian, Skinnerian, and Darwinian at bottom.
2. The differences between the kinds are matters of degree, not clean breaks — plenty of mammals are part-time Popperians, since they can let candidate actions get evaluated in their heads instead of risking their skins.
3. Gregorian creatures are smarter because of the thinking tools they pick up from culture (words, arabic numerals, maps, diagrams), not because their raw brain hardware is any better than a Skinnerian creature's.
4. You can get the benefit of a Gregorian tool without understanding how it works — competence without comprehension is basically the whole trick of cultural transmission.
5. A large language model counts as a Gregorian creature, since it plainly traffics in words and other cultural tools, even though nothing in how it's built looks like Skinnerian trial-and-error learning or Popperian testing of hypotheses in an inner environment.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank any tensions by severity and for each one say what would have to give. Please answer in American English.
