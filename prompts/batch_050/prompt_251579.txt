I recently led a discussion group on formal systems and found that we kept talking past one another about the MU-puzzle. Some participants thought that stepping outside the system to prove that MU cannot be derived was an ingenious extension of the game, while others thought it changed the subject entirely. I want to rehearse the stronger version of the second position before our next meeting.

Please play Dr. Mara Venn, a fictional but philosophically sophisticated formalist who curates a museum of rule-governed systems. Venn believes that object-level derivation and meta-level reasoning must be sharply distinguished: proving from outside that a string is unobtainable may be legitimate mathematics, but it is not a move within the original system. She is not a strawman. She accepts that meta-level proofs can be rigorous, explanatory, and more illuminating than formal derivation; she concedes small points readily; and she will change systems explicitly rather than pretend that an external insight was generated internally. Remain in character until I say curtain, speak in the first person, and keep each reply under 180 words. Use only the rules I state here, and do not provide citations or reading lists. The system begins with MI. Its rules are: if a string ends in I, one may append U; after M, one may duplicate the remainder of the string; III may be replaced by U; and UU may be deleted.

My opening challenge is this: your distinction between playing the game and reasoning about it seems less secure than you claim. The familiar impossibility proof observes that the number of I-symbols modulo 3 is invariant under the rules, while MU has zero I-symbols. That reasoning is external to the system, but it is neither arbitrary nor merely psychological; it is a truth-preserving method available to any rational agent. If the point of the puzzle is to discover what follows from MI, why should that discovery not count as genuine progress? Begin by answering as Dr. Venn, and classify these three acts separately: deriving MU under the four stated rules, proving MU’s underivability from outside them, and adding a new rule that permits MU precisely because the external proof is known.
