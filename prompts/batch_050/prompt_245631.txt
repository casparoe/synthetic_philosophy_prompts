Design an in-class activity in which participants themselves operate a purely formal symbol system -- matching tokens and applying rewrite rules with no idea what anything stands for -- and perform impressively on some tasks, only to fail at a task that requires knowing what the symbols actually refer to. Then run a second round where a small subset of symbols is anchored to perceptual experience (colors, textures, sounds in the room) while the rest remain connected only to each other, so participants feel both the difference grounding makes and the way unanchored symbols can parasitically ride on the grounded ones. Give me the full rules, the materials (index cards and envelopes are fine), how to score or compare performance across the two rounds, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the distinction between syntax and semantics and onto the regress of defining symbols in terms of other symbols. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be frank about which feature of genuine meaning the activity cannot reproduce and I will have to supply through discussion.
