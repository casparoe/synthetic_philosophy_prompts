So I need a sanity check on an argument that keeps happening at my house. We live in a country that's supposed to be one of the most gender-equal places on earth (paternity leave, women everywhere in politics, the works), and my 16-year-old daughter just told us she wants to go into nursing. My wife is happy for her. I'm... not against it exactly, but I mentioned at dinner that it's a shame more girls don't go into engineering, and now somehow I'm the bad guy in a fight I didn't mean to start. Then my brother-in-law sent me this statistic that broke my brain a little: apparently the countries that have done the most for gender equality actually have *bigger* gender gaps in careers, with fewer women in tech and science than in countries that are way less equal and way less free. His takeaway was basically "see, when women are actually free to choose, they choose differently, so companies should stop bending over backwards with diversity stuff."

Here's what's eating me. If the freest countries show the biggest gaps, that could mean two totally opposite things, and I can't tell which: either those differences are just what men and women naturally prefer once you remove the pressure (in which case my brother-in-law is right and my vague guilt about my daughter's choice is misguided), or the pressure is still there even in equal countries, just hidden better (in which case maybe my daughter's "choice" isn't as much hers as it feels, and my wife's hands-off approach is naive). Both conclusions feel like they're skipping something, and I notice people pick whichever one they already believed.

So what I actually want help with, before I say anything else dumb at a family dinner: what does a finding like that actually tell you about nature vs. culture, and what *doesn't* it tell you? Is there even a way to tell when a preference is really someone's own versus a script they've absorbed? And separately — because this is the part with real stakes for me — if you can't tell, what's the fair thing to do? Does respecting my daughter's choice mean taking it at face value, or could nudging her toward engineering be the thing that actually respects her? Please don't just give me generic "support your kid" parenting advice; I want to understand what's genuinely at stake here, because I think my wife and my brother-in-law are *both* being too confident and I can't articulate why.
