So my uncle was a state trial judge for like twenty years and at family dinners he always ends up talking about the cases where people got punished without really meaning to do anything wrong. I've kept rough notes because it interests me, and now I want to test whether his "pattern" is actually a pattern. Here are the rulings he describes as his, with his outcomes:

- A pharmacist filled prescriptions with pills from a bottle whose supplier had mislabeled the dosage. Pharmacist did everything right by the book, genuinely had no idea. Uncle upheld the conviction: misdemeanor fine. His comment: when you run a pharmacy you accept that the public doesn't carry your mistakes.
- A guy shot what turned out to be a protected bird, honestly convinced it was a common species people hunt all the time, in a place where that mistake is easy to make. Uncle acquitted, said the state has to prove he at least knew what he was shooting at.
- A trucking company owner got convicted when runoff from his yard contaminated a creek, even though he'd spent real money on precautions and the contamination came from a supplier's faulty containers. Uncle upheld it: fine plus cleanup costs. Said regulated industries buy into this when they open their doors.
- A bartender sold a beer to a 19-year-old with a genuinely flawless fake ID, the kind that fools police. Uncle threw the charge out. Said convicting her would just be punishing her for being deceived.
- A landlord got convicted under a housing code for a dangerous wiring condition inside a wall that predated his ownership, no way he could have known, penalty was a small fine and a repair order. Uncle upheld it.

Now there's a new case coming onto his docket and we've got a bet going. A retired schoolteacher, not in any business, rented out her late husband's small storage unit as-is to make ends meet. Unknown to her, years earlier the husband had stashed a drum of some now-illegal chemical there, which leaked. She's charged under an environmental statute that on its face doesn't require knowledge -- but here's the kicker, this version of the charge carries up to 18 months in jail, not just a fine, and she never knowingly participated in any regulated industry at all.

Predict how my uncle rules and why. Which of his earlier rulings is the closest analog? If you think his track record is genuinely consistent with more than one principle that diverges here, lay out the candidate principles, say which one best explains all five rulings, and tell me what principle he'd effectively be adopting whichever way he goes. And give me the follow-up case that would settle which principle he's really been applying.
