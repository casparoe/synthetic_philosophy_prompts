I write about science for a general audience, and I'm drafting a piece on whether evolutionary theory is actually "science" in the strict sense. I want to argue a slightly unfashionable line: that Popper was basically right when he said Darwinism isn't a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research program -- and that he shouldn't have backed off from this later in his career. Here's the tension I need help working through: selection seems to explain everything, which by Popper's own lights is exactly what's suspicious about a theory. A trait persists because it aided survival, and if it hadn't survived we'd have a story for that too. But then people like Popper's critics point to concrete, risky predictions evolution makes -- transitional fossils, genetic common ancestry, the whole nested hierarchy business -- and say look, it's as falsifiable as anything in physics. Please argue my case for me: that Popper's original diagnosis was right and his recantation was a mistake. The tricky part is you have to do this without conceding that evolution is false or unscientific in the everyday sense (I'm not writing a creationist hit piece, obviously), and you have to deal honestly with the strongest falsifiability claims people make on Darwinism's behalf, not the weak ones.
