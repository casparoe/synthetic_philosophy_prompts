Longtime LW/rationalist-blog reader here, and illusionism is the one debate where I reliably end up on the anti side in the comment threads, so let me stress-test that. Context: I was arguing with a friend last weekend about Frankish-style illusionism (roughly: phenomenal conciousness is an introspective illusion, a story the brain tells itself) and realized my whole case rests on objections I've just absorbed from the discourse over the years. So, objections I already know and don't need repeated: (1) the direct-acquaintance / incredulous-stare move — phenomenal redness is presented to me, not merely represented, so you can't dissolve it into a misrepresentation; (2) the regress — explaining the seeming of phenomenal properties seems to require phenomenal properties to do the seeming; (3) the pain point — an illusion of pain still hurts, so the illusion already contains the thing it was meant to eliminate; (4) illusions are only identifiable against a background of veridical perception (we know the Müller-Lyer lines are really equal because measurement corrects appearance), but there's no veridical phenomenal case to calibrate against, so calling consciousness an "illusion" misuses the concept; and (5) the self-stultification worry — if introspection is unreliable enough to fabricate an entire inner life, it's too unreliable to support the illusionist's own story about self-representations.

Also, one I think I came up with myself in the shower, tell me whether it's any good or secretly just a variant of something above: representations have to be *of* something in order to misrepresent it — a map of a nonexistent city still has a city-shaped target it fails to match. So what's the target of the brain's self-representation of phenomenal redness? If no phenomenal property exists, not even as a failed posit, what makes it a representation of redness rather than of nothing? Anyway, the actual request: give me one objection to illusionism that is NOT on this list and not a rewording, special case, or combination of items on it. State the objection, say how it relates to the ones listed, and give your honest verdict on whether it actually lands. 250 words max.
