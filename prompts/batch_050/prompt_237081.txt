I serve on the grants panel of a private science foundation. Two proposals of roughly equal technical merit are competing for a single award, and only one can be funded. Proposal A is a conventional survey for biosignatures on nearby exoplanets using existing telescope time. Proposal B is a more ambitious survey whose principal investigator argues it is far more likely to succeed than the raw astrophysical uncertainties suggest, on explicitly anthropic grounds: since we necessarily find ourselves as observers, and since we are a priori more likely to exist in a cosmos containing vastly more observers, we should assign high prior credence to there being a very large number of technological civilizations, and hence a good chance of detection. A dissenting panel member calls this reasoning "presumptuous": on his view, conditionalizing on our own existence should merely rule out theories inconsistent with our being here, never boost a theory simply because it populates the cosmos more generously — otherwise armchair reasoning of this form could have told us the universe was teeming before anyone looked through a telescope.

Draft a memo for the panel recommending how much weight, if any, Proposal B's anthropic argument should carry in the funding decision. The memo should state precisely where self-sampling and self-indication styles of reasoning diverge as applied to this case; make a definite recommendation about which proposal to fund; and present the strongest version of the dissenting member's objection before either answering it or, if you judge it decisive, conceding it and recommending accordingly. Commit to a position rather than laying out options for the panel to choose between.
