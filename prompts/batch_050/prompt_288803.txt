I help organize the reading series at my local rationalist meetup -- most of us came up through LessWrong and the adjacent blogs in the late 2000s and 2010s, and our usual fare is decision theory, forecasting, and the cognitive-bias literature. This year I talked the group into a "dead authors" track, and we just spent two months on Plutarch's Lives and a sampling of Christian hagiography. Next month I'm presenting my own synthesis, and my thesis is roughly this: a Plutarchan practice of reading lives -- comparatively, in his paired manner, with attention to the small revealing gesture rather than the famous battle (Plutarch himself says, in effect, that he writes lives rather than histories because character shows in trifles where great events show nothing) -- is a genuine instrument of moral learning, one that transmits dispositional, know-how-like moral knowledge that explicit argument, and especially explicit consequentialist argument, cannot transmit. My further claim is that this practice survives the bias literature rather than being refuted by it: yes, narrative distorts, but a curated diet of lives, read against each other with the formal comparisons Plutarch appends, is a debiasing technology and not merely a bias delivery system.

The audience will be about thirty people: mostly software engineers and data scientists, a strong effective-altruist contingent that thinks in expected value, two or three people who can recite the heuristics-and-biases canon from memory, and one very sharp member whose entire frame is selection effects and who thinks most of what people call wisdom literature is survivorship bias with footnotes. I anticipate the obvious openings -- halo effects, hindsight bias, the unreliability of Plutarch's sources, moral luck in whom posterity chose to remember, and the community's own long-running ambivalence about whether fiction and narrative can be evidence at all -- but I also expect the sharpest attacks to be less obvious: that exemplar-based moral education is structurally the epistemology of personality cults, that I'm laundering entertainment into education, and that any knowledge that can't be stated propositionally can't be checked and therefore can't be knowledge.
