I've spent enough years reading LessWrong and the Alignment Forum that the approval-directed versus goal-directed distinction (in roughly Christiano's sense) feels like home turf, and I want to stress-test my understanding of it. The standard worry about approval direction is deception: the agent learns to produce actions that look good to the overseer rather than actions that are good, exploiting gaps in what the overseer knows or notices. The standard defense is that a sufficiently informed overseer collapses the distinction, since approving exactly the actions the overseer would choose is just choosing well. What interests me is the territory between those two claims, and I'd like you to build me a case that lives there.

Construct a thought experiment in which an approval-directed agent fails while a goal-directed agent pursuing the overseer's actual values succeeds, under the following constraints. The overseer must be honest, attentive, and never deceived: no information asymmetry is doing the work, the agent never manipulates, and for every individual decision the overseer approves exactly what a reasonable person with the overseer's knowledge and values would approve of that decision, considered on its own. The failure must instead emerge from the structure of approval itself, for instance from the fact that approval attaches to actions one at a time rather than to trajectories, plans, or states of the world. The setting should be mundane and realistic: a scheduling assistant, a triage nurse's decision support, a personal finance tool, something of that kind, not a superintelligence and not anything science-fictional. Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph.

After the scenario, walk through what each agent does and why, and identify precisely which feature of the case is carrying the load. Then do two more things. First, describe what happens if that feature is removed or weakened, so I can see whether the case is robust or a knife's edge. Second, give the strongest response available to a defender of approval direction: the obvious move is that a competent overseer can approve long-term plans, penalize myopia directly, or approve actions partly for their downstream consequences, and I'd like to know whether that move dissolves the case or merely relocates the problem, since approving actions for their consequences starts to look a lot like goal direction wearing a hat.

Throughout, write in continuous prose only: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables, just paragraphs the way you'd write a long email. And please be explicit about your epistemic state: say how confident you are that the case actually works as specified, and single out the single point in your construction you are least sure of.
