So I run a little philosophy reading group at work (tech company, mostly engineers who think utilitarianism is obviously correct and one Kant guy who won't let anything go) and we recently got into a heated argument about whether it could ever be better for millions of people to each get a dust speck in their eye than for one person to be tortured. The argument kept going in circles because people were actually disagreeing about different things and talking past each other -- some were really debating whether tiny harms even count, others were debating whether numbers can ever outweigh intensity, and one guy kept insisting it was an empirical question about how bad dust specks actually are, which seemed to miss the point entirely.

I want to build a decision tree or flowchart we can all walk through that sorts us into the actual positions on the aggregation of tiny harms, rather than the positions we think we hold. Each node should be a question or concrete scenario you can answer without knowing any jargon (no "do you accept diminishing marginal disvalue?"), and each split should correspond to a real point where positions genuinely come apart. Some splits I'd want included somewhere: whether there's a sharp threshold where harm quality stops mattering vs. a smooth tradeoff; whether the "separateness of persons" worry actually blocks aggregation or only blocks it across certain kinds of harms; whether tiny harms could aggregate against a big harm to one person but not against certain *kinds* of serious harms (like, is there a lexical tier for torture but not for broken legs?); and whether it matters if the tiny harms are experienced as harms at all versus being things like slightly shortened lifespan or imperceptible health risks.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than starting from named positions -- figure out what the live combinations of answers are, then label the leaves with the standard view that occupies each spot (threshold deontology, lexical views, unrestricted aggregation, discontinuity views, etc.), and flag any combination that's empty with a sentence on whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any pair of positions your tree can't separate. Aim for maybe 6-9 diagnostic questions, and each scenario should be fresh -- not the literal dust specks case, since we've all already anchored hard on that one. Things like airplane noise over a huge city, drip pricing, slightly rude cashiers, microplastics, that kind of texture.
