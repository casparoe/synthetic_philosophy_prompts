I don't have any philosophy background but I've got into a habit of listening to philosophy podcasts whilst walking the dog, and lately I've been chewing over this question of whether dreams are actually experiences. I mentioned it to a mate of mine who did a year of philosophy at uni before switching to economics, and he gave me a rundown of Dennett's position that I found myself nodding along to at the time, but the more I've thought about it the more I suspect he was giving me the cartoon version. I'd like to know what to keep and what to bin.

His summary went roughly like this: "Dennett's line is that dreams aren't experiences at all. Nothing is actually going on in your head while you're asleep, and the 'dream' is a false memory your brain cobbles together in the split second of waking up, like a cassette tape that gets slotted in and played. So when you tell someone you had a long vivid dream, you're simply mistaken -- there was never any film playing in your head, just a memory of a film that never screened. Malcolm pushed the same idea even harder and basically proved that dreams can't be experiences, because being asleep rules out consciousness by definition: if you can think or judge anything, you're awake, so whatever a dream is it can't be something you lived through. Between the two of them the whole idea of dreaming as we ordinarily picture it is more or less demolished."

Now, my friend is clever but he's also the sort who enjoys making views sound more extreme than they are, and something about this smells off. For one thing, surely Dennett doesn't think *nothing* happens during sleep -- your brain is clearly busy doing something, and people get woken mid-dream in sleep labs all the time, which you'd think would be awkward for the cassette story. And did Malcolm really *prove* anything, or is that just a very confident argument from definitions? Could you go through his summary and tell me which bits are a fair statement of what Dennett and Malcolm actually held, which bits are the standard overstatement everyone makes, and which bits are just wrong? Also, if the dodgy parts get fixed, does the overall moral -- that my ordinary picture of dreaming-as-watching-a-film is demolished -- still stand, or was he smuggling that in? I'm inclined to think my common-sense view survives, but I can't tell if that's insight or just stubbornness.
