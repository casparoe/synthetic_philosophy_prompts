I am a third-year philosophy PhD student applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship, and I need a two-page research proposal. Some context, because it is what made the topic urgent for me: over the past eighteen months my father, who has lived alone since my mother died, has come to treat an AI companion chatbot as a genuine conversational partner. He is not confused about what it is; he simply finds that the distinction stops mattering after an hour of good conversation. Watching this, I became convinced that the question of whether he is making a mistake is not primarily an empirical one, and it pushed me toward the philosophical literature on behavioral criteria for mindedness.

My half-formed idea is this. The debate around the Turing test tends to fixate on whether passing an imitation game would constitute sufficient evidence of thought. But Turing's setup builds in a feature that nearly all real cases of mind-attribution lack: an interrogator whose explicit task is to unmask the interlocutor. Ordinary mind-attribution, my father's included, happens in cooperative interaction, where the human is not probing but conversing, and where the system's outputs may be optimized precisely to sustain that cooperation. I suspect that behavior produced under adversarial scrutiny and behavior produced in trusting interaction carry systematically different evidential weight, and that conflating the two explains why the literature talks past itself: critics who find the test trivially easy to pass are picturing naive interaction, while defenders are picturing rigorous interrogation.

Turn this into a two-page proposal. It needs: a precise statement of the question; an account of why the standard positions leave it open, referring to the usual moves (the Chinese Room, the objection that the test measures the judge rather than the machine, the charge of behaviorism) without citations, which I will supply myself; a month-by-month plan of what I would actually do with a year; a description of what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a closing paragraph on why this is a philosophical rather than an empirical undertaking. One constraint I want the proposal to honor: I intend to use the distinction between a test's sensitivity and its specificity as a central analytical tool — a behavioral criterion can be an excellent screen against false positives while being poor at recognizing genuine minds, and vice versa — so the proposal should be explicit about what work that analogy can do and where it breaks down, since a behavioral criterion is not a diagnostic instrument in any straightforward sense.
