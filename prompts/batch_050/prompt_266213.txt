I’m trying to write down my view about biological functions, and I suspect it is mostly sensible but not completely consistent. Here are my commitments:

1. Proper functions are etiological: roughly, an effect of ancestral trait-tokens becomes the trait type’s proper function because that effect causally explains selection for the trait and the continued presence of later tokens.

2. Past selection alone should not ground a function forever. If a trait has persisted for 100,000 years only because of drift or a developmental constraint, I don’t want to say that its long-obsolete selected effect is still its current proper function.

3. On the other hand, selection does not have to be continuous. A function should not disappear merely because selection pressure lapses for a while, otherwise functions would pop in and out of existence every few generations.

4. Causal-role function attributions are perfectly legitimate in physiology. They identify what a component currently contributes to some capacity of a containing system, and they can be relative to how the system and capacity are described. But these are not the same thing as proper functions.

5. One trait can have several proper functions at once. It can retain an old function while acquiring a new one, provided both effects help explain why the trait is maintained.

6. Trait identity is fixed by things like homology and developmental organization, not by function. So the same trait can continue to exist after losing its old function, and it can change functions without becoming a different trait.

7. Malfunction is genuinely normative but not the same as being harmful. A component can malfunction while producing a fitness benefit, and it can perform its proper function while harming the organism.

8. Biology should be able to justify proper-function attributions using present-day organization, experiments, and comparisons even when exact evolutionary history is unavailable. I also don’t like the idea that a biological function fact could depend solely on an empirically inaccessible bit of history.

9. Here’s a made-up case I have a strong verdict about. A freshwater snail’s shell ridge was selected because it resisted shell-crushing fish. Those fish vanished 80,000 years ago, and since then the ridge has persisted only because it is developmentally linked to another selected shell feature; crushing resistance has contributed nothing to its maintenance. I still want to say the ridge’s proper function is resisting crushing, because that is what it was built for.

10. Second case: a bacterial pump was selected for exporting a natural toxin. In one mutant, the pump is much worse at exporting that toxin but happens to export a new antibiotic, saving the cell. I want to call it a beneficial malfunction rather than say its function has immediately changed.
