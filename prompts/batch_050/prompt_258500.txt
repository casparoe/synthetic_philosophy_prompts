In debates about repugnant markets, two objections to commodification are often run together: the corruption objection (putting a good up for sale degrades or changes its meaning, as when paid apologies or purchased friendship cease to be the thing they were) and the unfairness objection (market exchange of the good is objectionable because background inequality makes the seller's consent coerced or the bargain exploitative, as when the destitute sell kidneys). Construct two short scenarios, one for each objection, engineered so that the objection in question applies cleanly while the other is fully absent. Constraints: each scenario one paragraph; realistic settings (schools, workplaces, medicine, civic life); the corruption case must involve parties who are equally wealthy, well-informed, and under no pressure, so that no exploitation is available; the unfairness case must involve a good whose value and meaning survive sale completely intact, so that nothing is corrupted. For each scenario, then show the work: state the verdict of the objection it is built for, explain why the other objection gets no grip, and identify the single feature doing the load-bearing work. Finally, say what happens to each case if that feature is removed, and give the best reply available to someone who thinks one of the two objections ultimately reduces to the other.
