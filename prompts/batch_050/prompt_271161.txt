Write a mock official notice of roughly 650 words, formatted as a bureaucratic letter — reference number, date line, addressee line ("To All Mental Properties and Events"), an opening summary, numbered provisions, and a closing section on appeals — issued by a fictional Office of Causal Sufficiency. The notice informs its addressees (pains, beliefs, desires, intentions, and the like) that, following a routine audit of the causal order, their causal efficacy has been determined to be redundant and will be discontinued at the end of the fiscal year. The stated grounds should restate, in deadpan regulatory language, the actual premises of the causal exclusion argument: every physical effect already has a sufficient physical cause, so no causal work remains for anything distinct from the physical; and appeal to both causes operating at once would constitute systematic overdetermination, which the Office does not license.

The humor should come entirely from taking the metaphysics seriously as an administrative matter. The piece should include the following:

- A provision noting that the Office's jurisdiction extends only to physical effects, so any mental property claiming to cause other mental states will still need to explain how the chain bottoms out in bodily behavior.
- An appeals procedure in which different recipients lodge objections that mirror real positions in the philosophy of mind, each rejected or accepted on procedural grounds: an identity theorist applies for exemption on the grounds of simply being a physical cause and is asked to produce documentation of the identity; a nonreductive physicalist claims that mental properties are realized by, but not identical to, physical ones, and is informed that the Office has a standing form for this case and that no one has ever completed it to the review board's satisfaction; an epiphenomenalist writes in to say it accepts the notice without complaint and is thanked for its cooperation; a defender of overdetermination cites the firing-squad precedent and is told that one redundant firing squad is not a precedent but an anomaly; a theorist of counterfactual dependence submits evidence that the behavior would not have occurred had the pain not been felt, and the Office's response should leave genuinely open whether this appeal will succeed.
- At least one passing joke about the Office's strained relationship with the neural correlates, who do all the actual work but receive none of the correspondence.

The tone must remain perfectly deadpan throughout — no winking, no scare quotes, no narrator tipping the reader off. A specialist in philosophy of mind should be able to map every provision onto the real dialectic (causal closure, the exclusion principle, the overdetermination worry, the identity strategy, the functionalist's level-of-relevance reply, epiphenomenalism, and interventionist or counterfactual accounts of causation) and judge the satire fair; a lay reader should still find it funny as a piece of bureaucracy satire. Do not name any philosophers, real or invented; all appellants should be identified only by their positions.
