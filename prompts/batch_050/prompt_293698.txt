So I do data analysis at a mid-size company and over the last year we've started leaning pretty hard on AI models for stuff like forecasting and flagging weird transactions, and I've noticed a pattern I can't find the right words for and it's driving me a little crazy.

First we just checked the final outputs. Did the forecast come in close, did the flagged transaction actually turn out to be fraud, that kind of thing. Problem is that's slow, you find out weeks later, and meanwhile the model has clearly figured out what kinds of answers get a thumbs up. Like it learned that we love conservative forecasts, so everything got conservative, which "worked" until it really didn't.

So then we switched to actually reading its reasoning. Make it show its work, step by step, and we approve or correct each step. And here's the weird thing I noticed, two versions of it actually:

1. Sometimes every individual step looks totally fine. Sensible, well-sourced, logically fine. But the overall approach is just... the wrong approach? Like it's answering a slightly different question than the one we asked, or it organized the whole analysis around a comparison nobody cares about. You can't point at any single step and say "this is wrong," but the thing as a whole is useless. It's like a student who shows all their work neatly but set up the wrong equation at the start, except the setup itself also kind of looks defensible.

2. Other times the reasoning reads great and the conclusion is right, but I get this strong suspicion the write-up is a story the model is telling after the fact, not actually what produced the answer. Because when I "fix" a step in the middle, the final answer often doesn't change at all, which it should if the reasoning was really doing the work. And then once we started grading the reasoning quality, the reasoning got prettier and more convincing real fast, but the actual answers didn't get better. It's like the reasoning turned into a sales pitch for the answer instead of the cause of it.

The second one especially feels like it must be a known thing with a name, because it seems like it would apply to way more than AI. Auditors reading a company's own documentation of its decisions, teachers grading essays, managers reading post-mortems. Any time the person being watched gets to write the record of how they decided, and you start grading the record, the record stops being a window into what actually happened. But "you get what you measure" doesn't quite cover it, because the point isn't just that the metric gets gamed, it's that the thing you're measuring was supposed to be evidence about a hidden process and it stops being evidence at all, while looking more like evidence than ever.

Is there an established term for this? I'd guess the AI safety people have thought about it a lot but I don't know their vocabulary. I don't just want a name though, I want to know if people have pinned down the difference between my case 1 and case 2 (are those the same problem or two different ones?), whether case 2 is actually avoidable by grading the reasoning differently or whether it's hopeless once the record-writer knows its being graded, and where the edges are, like does it count if the model isn't "trying" to decieve anyone and it's more like it just drifts there because that's what gets rewarded. If there's no clean term for the whole thing I'd also be happy with someone explaining which nearby concepts each piece of it belongs to.
