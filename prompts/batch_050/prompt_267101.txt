Suppose a mid-sized country's parliament is drafting a civil liability code and must decide, for each of the following activities, whether harm caused by that activity should be governed by strict liability or by a negligence standard requiring proof of fault: (1) commercial aviation accidents, (2) adverse reactions to approved pharmaceuticals taken as directed, (3) dog bites, (4) workplace injuries in manufacturing, (5) contamination of packaged food, (6) data breaches at cloud-storage providers, and (7) damage caused by autonomous delivery robots operating on public streets.

Please rank these seven activities from most to least defensible for strict liability, and justify the ranking. In your reasoning, address at least the following considerations and be explicit about how each one moves particular items up or down your list: the distinction between deterring the *level of care* and deterring the *level of the activity itself*; which party is typically the cheapest information-holder about the risk; the administrative and error costs of litigating fault; the risk that strict liability chills socially valuable activity rather than merely pricing it; and the adequacy of insurance markets for spreading the resulting losses.

Finally, identify the single item on your list whose placement you are least confident about, and state what empirical fact, if established, would most change where you put it.
