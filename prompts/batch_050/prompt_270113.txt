"Popper's paradox of tolerance, from The Open Society and Its Enemies, is that if a society is tolerant without limit, its tolerance will eventually be seized on and destroyed by the intolerant, who will use the freedoms of an open society to take power and abolish those freedoms. So the paradox is a genuine logical contradiction in the concept of tolerance: the principle 'tolerate everything' contradicts itself, the same way 'this sentence is false' does.

Popper's solution was that tolerance therefore has to have limits, and that a tolerant society should suppress intolerant speech and movements whenever they appear, since otherwise the tolerant are signing their own death warrant. Rawls disagreed. Because justice as fairness makes equal liberty the first principle, Rawls argued that restricting anyone's liberty, including the intolerant person's, is always unjust, so a well-ordered society has to tolerate even intolerant groups and can only ever respond to them with argument, never with restriction.

My own view: I think Popper is basically right that unlimited tolerance is self-defeating, and since tolerating Nazis in Weimar Germany led to Hitler, history shows that restricting intolerant speech is always justified once a group becomes unambiguously intolerant."
