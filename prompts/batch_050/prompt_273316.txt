So my dog tore her ACL last month and while she was recovering I ended up in this long argument with a friend of a friend at a dinner party. I was saying the way she guarded the injured leg, whimpered when it got bumped, and avoided putting weight on it was obviously her feeling pain, and this guy goes "well, Descartes thought animals were just machines, and he was right that there's no way to know what they're feeling." Whatever. But it got me reading a bit, and now I'm genuinely unsure how the machine analogy was even supposed to work for Descartes.

So: audit it for me. Lay out what actually maps onto what -- what plays the role of the gears and springs, who's the watchmaker (this seems important? Descartes seemed to think God's machines were wildly more intricate than anything humans build, which cuts against the "so animals are dumb" takeaway), and what the analogy is doing for the bigger res extensa / res cogitans picture. Then where does it break down? Specifically I want to know:

1. Does the machine picture even license the conclusion my dinner-party guy drew, that animals don't really feel pain? I've seen people claim Descartes allowed animals some kind of sensation without thought and people claim he denied it outright -- does the analogy itself settle that, or is it neutral on the question and people are just loading different things onto "feeling"?

2. We now know animal bodies run on the same pain physiology ours do, nerves and opioids and all that. Does that fit inside Descartes' framework fine (the machine has those parts, sure), or does it pressure the framework itself by blurring wherever the line was supposed to be?

3. Is there a better analogy available for his actual view -- something like a very sophisticated thermostat or an LLM that talks about pain without feeling it -- and would he have found that more convincing than clocks and the hydraulic garden statues he apparently was impressed by?

And bottom line: does the "no way to know what they feel" conclusion survive, or was it riding on the analogy rather than the argument?
