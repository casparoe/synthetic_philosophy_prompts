You know how in big open-world games -- Skyrim, GTA, Red Dead Redemption, that whole genre -- you can mow down adult civilians by the dozen and the game barely blinks. Maybe you get a bounty, maybe the guards come after you, and then the world resets or just shrugs and the shopkeeper is back behind the counter tomorrow. But children are different. Most of these games either don't put kids in the world at all, or they put them in but make them literally unkillable -- flagged as "essential" so your sword swings right through them. And when players make mods that strip out the invunerability, the community treats that as genuinely creepy: the mods get pulled from hosting sites, people in forums say it crosses a line. A line that apparently isn't crossed by methodically massacring every adult in town.

Here's what I want to understand: what does this design convention -- which is basically universal across the industry and its playerbase -- presuppose about the moral status of video game characters? Because it seems like it can't presuppose that the characters themselves matter, or the adult-killing would be the scandal and the child thing would just be a worse degree of it. So what's the implicit theory? That NPCs are moral nullities and the kid rule is really about the player (what it says about you that you'd *want* to), or about real children somehow, or about the social meaning of depicting certain acts? Answer as a philosopher who works on ethics and isn't squeamish about weird conclusions.

A few things I specifically want: think through the candidate readings carefully first (feel free to brainstorm them), and for each one say whether the practice strictly *requires* it or merely sits comfortably with it -- I suspect the convention is compatible with several theories and I'd like to know exactly where it's underdetermined. Then tell me concretely what would have to change in how these games are built and rated if we took seriously the view that a sufficiently detailed simulation of a person has *some* moral standing of its own.

But don't just hand me the menu and leave me to pick. Commit: which single presupposition actually best explains the whole pattern (adults fair game, kids untouchable, modders ostracized)? And give me a straight verdict on whether the convention is coherent on that explanation, or whether it's just squeamishness dressed up as a principle.
