Me and a friend run a discussion night at our local community centre, mostly adults between 25 and 70, nobody with any philosophy training including us. It started because my brother in law kept saying at family dinners that privacy only matters if youve got something to hide, and I could never explain why that felt wrong even though I knew it was. Weve got our next session on privacy coming up and I dont want to just argue about it again, I want people to feel it.

Heres what I want you to design for me. There will be around 14 people, we have the room for about 75 minutes, and weve got index cards, pens, sticky notes, that kind of thing, no budget to speak of. I want an activity or game that makes people run into this idea themselves before anyone names it: that the problem with giving up privacy isnt that youve done something wrong, its that the same harmless fact about you means completely different things to different people, and that being watched changes how you act even when youre doing nothing bad. Like, my mum knows things about me my boss doesnt, and vice versa, and thats not me being fake, thats just what being a person is. But I dont want to open by saying any of that.

So the structure matters to me. People should share or use information that feels totally innocent at first, and then the rules of the game should make that information travel somewhere it doesnt belong, or put them under observation in a way that changes their choices, and they should feel a bit of genuine discomfort or at least surprise before we ever talk about why. Real small stakes if possible, not embarassing ones, just enough that its not hypothetical. I need the actual rules, any roles people should have, roughly how long each part takes, and then a debrief plan that connects specific moments in the game to the idea, so if someone says but I still have nothing to hide we can point at what they themselves did twenty minutes earlier.
