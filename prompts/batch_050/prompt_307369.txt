so i should say upfront i have zero academic background, im a plumber who fell down a chinese philosophy rabbit hole about a year ago (youtube video, then an audiobook, now i read the Mencius in the van between jobs and my colleagues think ive lost it). and the thing i keep chewing on is the Mencius vs Xunzi fight about whether human nature is good or bad, and specifically why it never seems to go anywhere.

heres what puzzles me. Mencius has the child about to fall into a well, how anyone would feel a flash of alarm before any calculation kicks in, and the idea that goodness is like sprouts that just need tending. Xunzi has us born greedy and envious, with goodness being pure artifice, like steaming a warped board straight or a potter forcing clay into shape. fine, clear disagreement. but then look where they both end up: both think people as they actually are are mostly a mess, both think it takes years of deliberate effort and ritual to become decent, both think pretty much anyone can get there. they agree on the diagnosis, the treatment and the prognosis, and then the two camps spend two thousand years fighting about what the patient was like as a newborn. and as far as i can tell nobody has ever switched sides.

so what kind of disagreement IS this? i have a half baked theory i want stress tested: i dont think either of them is really making a factual claim about babies at all. i think both moves are motivational technologies. Mencius tells the king he already has the sprout of goodness in him (that bit where he catches the king sparing an ox and goes, see, youre already compassionate, now act like it) so the king will be ashamed not to live up to it. Xunzi tells people every impulse they have is suspect so theyll submit to discipline instead of trusting their gut. on my theory both of them picked whichever story about human nature would produce better behaviour if believed, and then presented it as fact because the trick only works if its believed as fact. so the whole debate is two teaching strategies wearing the costume of a biology dispute.

am i onto something or is that too cynical? if it IS a substantive disagreement, tell me what its substantively about -- what fact, turning out one way or the other, would settle it? could developmental psychology do it (those infant puppet show studies get cited constantly) or is "nature" just not the kind of thing a study could measure either way? and whatever your diagnosis: what would progress look like here -- a distinction, an experiment, a better question? or should i expect this to still be raging in another two thousand years, and if so is that a failure or just what this kind of question is like?
