So I'm a PhD student (political theory, but this isn't really a dissertation question) and I've been offered one of a few spots to winter over at a research station in Antarctica — about 14 months, fully isolated, and for most of it there's literally no way to leave even in an emergency, you sign something acknowledging that. Everyone I know who's done a winter says it changed them permanently. One guy came back and left academia entirely, another ended his marriage, and most of them say things like it was the best thing they ever did but also that they can't explain why to anyone who hasn't been. Here's what's actually bugging me. The person making this decision is me, with my current values — I care about finishing the degree, my relationship, my career. But apparently the person who comes back has different values, and I can't find out what they are without already going. And polling the returnees feels rigged somehow: even the ones who had a miserable time say they'd do it again, which weirdly makes me trust their testimony less, not more. My partner says I'm overthinking it and should just list pros and cons like any other big decision. Am I overthinking it? Is there a way for a choice like this to be rational at all, or is "rational" just the wrong standard here? And if I go and come back as someone who doesn't care about the things I care about now — did future-me get what they wanted, or did present-me just get overwritten?
