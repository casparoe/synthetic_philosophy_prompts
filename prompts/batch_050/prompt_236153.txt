The debate over whether gradual, neuron-by-neuron replacement of a biological brain with functionally identical synthetic components preserves the person has been running for decades, and from where I sit it appears completely frozen. The continuity side argues that at every individual step the change is smaller than the brain's ordinary microscopic turnover, that nothing behavioral or phenomenological is lost at any point, and that any alleged death would have to be located at some arbitrary neuron. The duplication side argues that the end product is at best a perfect copy, that the gradualness merely conceals the killing, and that their strongest card is the branch-line question: if the extracted biological neurons were kept viable and reassembled into a functioning brain, which of the two resulting people would be the original? Both arguments have been on the table for a long time, and nobody moves. A recent wrinkle has made me suspect the impasse is structural: in a reversible version of the procedure, where each replaced neuron is banked and the swap can be undone at any point, some continuity theorists concede that identity would shuttle back and forth with each reversal, which they treat as a reductio of the case rather than of their view, while some duplication theorists say the reversible case is genuine survival precisely because the original can always be reconstituted. Each camp's verdict seems to stop tracking the very feature it claims to care about. My hypothesis, offered only as a hypothesis, is that the two sides are answering different questions under one label: one asks what grounds rational self-concern going forward, the other asks what strict numerical identity requires, and each side's thought experiments are built to be decisive for its own question only. Please diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, engaging what each side actually argues, and tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a conceivable empirical result, a concession about which cases are even data, or an argument that the question is empty and the stall permanent.
