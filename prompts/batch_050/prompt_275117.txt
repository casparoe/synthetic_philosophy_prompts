write me a deadpan parody, around 650 words, of the methods and results sections of a cognitive-science paper by an invented research group called the Institute for Broadcast Phenomenology. Their “Global Workspace Meter” claims to tell whether a stimulus was consciously experienced by checking whether it was reported, remembered, or used to guide later behaviour. The joke should be that they have invented an excellent instrument for measuring access consciousness and then announced, with total confidence, that it measures phenomenal consciousness.

Make the satire technically informed rather than just calling the researchers stupid. The paper should include things like a visual masking task, confidence ratings, a no-report control that the authors somehow turn back into a report measure, and a triumphant result showing that information unavailable to the “workspace” leaves no phenomenology behind. Let the institutional, grant-friendly prose do some of the comic work. For example, they might describe failures of report as “phenomenal packet loss,” but don’t overdo the terminology.

The target is the slide from consciousness as globally broadcast information to consciousness as whatever can be globally broadcast, especially the temptation to treat the limitations of our measurement methods as discoveries about experience itself. Stay fair enough that a sophisticated global-workspace theorist could complain that the theory has been caricatured while still recognizing a real methodological danger. Also include one genuinely awkward objection inside the paper—maybe the no-report condition produces evidence of residual experience—and have the authors neutralize it with absurdly circular reasoning. No bibliography or citations.
