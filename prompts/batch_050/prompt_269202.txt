i'm taking an applied ethics seminar this semester and we just finished the unit on family obligations, including the Jane English paper where she argues that what grown kids owe their parents works like friendship rather than like paying back a debt. for our final short paper we have to lay out our own position and defend it, and i want to check mine actually hangs together before i commit to it, because i suspect something in here clashes but i can't see where. my current view:

1. english's friendship model is basically right. kids never asked to be born and most of what parents do is just their job anyway, so there's no debt to repay — what you owe your parents as an adult is set by the relationship you actually have with them now, same structure as friendship.

2. having said that, i owe my parents a LOT. they immigrated here, worked insane hours, paid my tuition, and the reason i owe them so much (calls, visits, real financial and practical support when they're old) is exactly that — everything they sacrificed for me.

3. obligations between friends don't evaporate just because the friendship goes quiet. if an old friend i haven't spoken to in three years has a genuine crisis, i still have a real reason to show up. it isn't optional the way helping a stranger is.

4. someone whose parents gave them the bare minimum, coldly, and who has no relationship with them as an adult owes them basically nothing beyond what you'd owe any stranger.

5. between siblings, the eldercare load should be split roughly equally — it shouldn't land hardest on whichever kid happened to stay closest to the parents. my cousin's family is dealing with exactly this right now and it seems obviously unfair.

6. parents' duties to their young kids are unconditional and have nothing to do with how good the relationship is — you can't tell your eight year old that you've drifted apart.

so which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do? go through them ordered from most serious to least, and for each real tension say what has to give and what the cheapest way of giving it up would cost me. don't just flag everything — a couple of these are definately only apparent problems. and if there's one single edit that makes the rest of the set consistent, name it.
