write me a deadpan parody of a corporate risk-management handbook issued by a fictional consultancy, “Keynes, Knight & Spook,” which has decided that ordinary probability dashboards are cowardice because the future is not a repeated urn-draw. Keep it under 650 words, formatted as numbered rules from a fake employee manual, with one short memo at the end from Legal begging people to please stop citing the handbook during fires.

The satire should land because it gets the ideas right, not because it turns Keynes into a horoscope goblin: risk vs uncertainty, non-numerical or only partly comparable probabilities, the “weight” of evidence behind a judgment, reliance on convention when there is no stable basis for calculation, animal spirits doing real work, and the way long-run averages dont comfort someone facing a one-off, world-changing choice. Invent one fresh device, like an internal “Uncertainty Budget” that departments must spend before they are allowed to say a number out loud, or a rule that any probability estimate must be accompanied by a description of what evidence would make the team embarrassed later. Make the jokes exaggerate actual tensions: when does refusing false precision become an excuse to never decide, and when does pretending to know become fraud with nicer fonts?

Target the style of consultancy certainty and risk-dashboard theater, not any real person. Stay deadpan, no winking, no bibliography, no quotations from Keynes or anyone else. A reader who knows the territory should laugh and also feel slightly accused; a reader who doesnt should still get why the office is doomed.
