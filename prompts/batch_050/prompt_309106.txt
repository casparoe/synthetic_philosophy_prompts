I convene the epistemology seminar in my department and for the last couple of years we keep circling the same fight about On Certainty without anyone conceding anything. Roughly: one faction (the Moyal-Sharrock-ish tendency) insists that hinges are not the kind of thing that can be known, believed, doubted or even properly said to be true -- they're enacted, animal, showing up in what we do rather than standing as propositions we hold. The other faction (Pritchard, Wright and co.) wants hinges to be propositionally contentful commitments which can after all have some epistemic standing, rationally held even if not known in the ordinary way. Both sides can quote the river-bed passage, the door-hinge image, the stuff about Moore's hands, and both sides claim Wittgenstein is obviously on their team.

Here's my working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the dispute isn't really about exegesis or even about hinges. It's a disguised disagreement about what epistemic appraisal is for. If you think the point of epistemology is to carve out the largest defensible notion of rationality (so we can call our inherited certainties rational rather than merely brute), you end up on the Pritchard side; if you think the point is to mark the limit where reasons run out and evaluation simply stops applying, you end up on the Moyal-Sharrock side. The word 'knowledge' is doing no real work; the word 'rational' is doing all of it, and nobody says so.

So my questions. One: is that diagnosis right, or is there a genuine substantive disagreement underneath that I'm missing -- about the metaphysics of belief, say, or about whether certainties have propositional content at all? Two: if I'm roughly right, is the dispute then merely verbal, or is the underlying question about the point of epistemic appraisal itself a real and arguable one? Three: what would actually count as progress here? A distinction (I've toyed with splitting hinges into 'stated framework propositions' versus 'embodied certainties' but I'm not convinced it survives contact with the text), a concession about what Moorean anti-scepticism can and can't deliver, or is this one of those debates where the honest answer is that both readings are available and the text underdetermines the matter and we should just say so and move on? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not another tour of the literature, and I'd like you to be blunt about whether my pet distinction is worth anything or whether I should bin it before I embarass myself in front of the postgrads.
