In my reading group on twentieth-century history, we've spent three sessions on the question of responsibility for the Cold War: the older line that Soviet expansion made confrontation unavoidable, the revisionist rejoinder that American economic and atomic policy forced Stalin's hand, and the various later syntheses that spread the blame around or decline to assign it at all. Last week, one member shut the whole discussion down with a single sentence: the Cold War was inevitable, so asking who started it is a category error — like asking who started the weather. Several people nodded along, and we moved on.

Something about that move bothers me, but I can't yet say exactly what. I'd like you to take the slogan "the Cold War was inevitable" and disassemble it into the distinct precise claims it could be making. For instance: what is the alleged source of the necessity — the structural predicament of two surviving superpowers in an anarchic system, ideological incompatibility, the atomic bomb, the personalities of the particular leaders, or the economic needs of each bloc? Inevitable as of when — 1945, 1941, 1917 — and inevitable given what held fixed? And what is the modal force: genuinely overdetermined such that removing any single cause leaves the outcome standing, merely very probable, or only unsurprising in hindsight (a verdict we deliver retroactively about whatever happens to occur)?

For each reading you identify, tell me whether it is trivially true (as a determinist gloss on any past event would be), substantive and defensible, or actually false, with reasons. Then — and this is what I most care about — tell me which readings, if any, would actually license the inference my reading-group member drew, namely that the blame question is a category error. Does inevitability dissolve responsibility, or only relocate it (from individuals to states, from states to structures)? Can a question be a category error about an overdetermined event, or does overdetermination rather multiply the true answers to "who started it"? And are there respectable non-accusatory readings of "who started it" — counterfactual contribution, difference-making, abnormal versus background conditions — that survive even if the strongest inevitability claim is true?

Please don't give me a neutral menu and leave me to choose. Commit yourself: state which precisification of the slogan you take to be the one worth defending, whether any defensible reading supports dismissing the blame question, and whether I should let the reading group off the hook or press the point next week. Give me your reasons in each case.
