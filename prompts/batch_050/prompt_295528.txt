I am writing a report on long-range governance scenarios and I need a number, not a survey. The proposition: by the year 2200, Earth-originating civilization will be governed by a singleton, where a singleton means a single decision-making agency at the highest level, capable of resolving any major conflict of goals within its domain according to its own decision procedures, without effective opposition from inside or outside. For the proposition to come out true, this condition has to hold for at least fifty consecutive years within the period, so a decade of hegemony or a fleeting unipolar moment does not count. Please give me your credence in that proposition and then show me the derivation. Specifically, decompose it into at least the following sub-questions, with a rough probability and one or two lines of justification for each: first, the probability that some actor (a state, a corporation, an AI system, or a coalition) acquires a genuinely decisive strategic advantage over the rest of the world at some point in the next two centuries, where decisive means the advantage cannot be eroded by ordinary competition, leakage, or catch-up; second, the conditional probability, given such an advantage, that the actor actually consolidates it into a stable singleton rather than losing it to internal fragmentation, principal-agent drift, or deliberate restraint; third, the probability that a singleton forms without any decisive advantage, through gradual coordination, institutional convergence, or treaty-like mechanisms, which seems to me the least discussed pathway and the one I most expect you to fudge. Then tell me how these combine into your final number, being explicit about the dependence structure rather than just multiplying, and handle two definitional edge cases as you go: a world federation whose constituent regions retain meaningful autonomy (does it count?), and a single AI system whose internal components pursue partially conflicting objectives (does that count, and what does your answer do to the number?). Finally: my own credence is around 0.35; a colleague whose judgment I respect puts it at 0.05. Given your decomposition, tell me which sub-question our disagreement most likely lives in, and name the single observation or development in the next twenty years that would move your own number the most, in either direction.
