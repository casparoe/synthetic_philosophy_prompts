Write me a specular poem about the thermodynamic arrow of time — that's the form where the second half repeats the first half's lines in exact reverse order, pivoting on one middle line. Here's what makes it hard: the microscopic laws of physics are basically time-symmetric, so the reversal should *almost* work but not quite. When the lines run backwards let them come out slightly wrong — the grammar straining, the meaning gone a bit stale — the way reversed footage of a glass unshattering looks off even though nothing in the dynamics forbids it. The one place where the mirror genuinely breaks should be the middle line, the moment entropy increases. A physicist reading it should be able to tell that the poem's asymmetry encodes the low-entropy past as a boundary condition on the universe, not some little nudge hiding in the laws themselves. No title, no explanation, just the poem.
