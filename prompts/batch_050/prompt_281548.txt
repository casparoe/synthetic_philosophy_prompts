Here's one I've been chewing on. Putnam died in 2016, so he never saw LLMs, the chatbot-companion boom, any of it. But he spent his career on exactly the two things that collide there: philosophy of mind (he invented functionalism and then famously abandoned it) and, late in life, religion -- his Jewish practice, his reading of Wittgenstein on religious language, the idea that religious talk isn't failed science but a different mode of life.

So: what do you think Putnam would have made of people using AI companions the way some people use religion -- as confessor, source of comfort, someone to pray alongside or ask for moral guidance? I don't want vibes, I want the reconstruction to actually engage with:

- His early functionalism seems committed to saying that if the chatbot plays the right functional role, the comfort and companionship are as real as anything. Would he have bitten that bullet? And does the later Putnam, the one who repudiated functionalism, take it back -- on what specific grounds?
- His Wittgensteinian line on religious language: if a lonely person says the chatbot understands them, or that praying with it genuinely helps, is that a factual error about the machine, or a move in a language-game that a machine can't actually join no matter how fluent it is? Would he say religious practice essentially requires embodied humans in a community with shared forms of life, and if so, what's doing the work -- the embodiment or the community?
- He hammered the fact/value entanglement and the idea that ethics needs moral perception, not rule-following. Does a chatbot that perfectly simulates empathy show that simulation was enough all along, or is it the cleanest possible demonstration that it isn't? I honestly can't tell which way he'd go here and that's the part I most want argued.
- Externalism: his whole slogan-era point was that meaning is fixed by causal-social chains, not by what's in the head. The chatbot's words are derived from human text. Do its utterances mean anything at all, or do they only borrow meaning from the chain back to actual speakers -- and does that even matter to the person being comforted?

Give me your best reconstruction of his actual view, including the tensions, since early-Putnam and late-Putnam plainly disagree and you should say so rather than harmonizing him. Flag where you're extrapolating past anything he wrote. And please don't hand me a generic philosophy-of-AI essay with Putnam's name stapled to it.
