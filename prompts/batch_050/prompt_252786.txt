I am a journalist who covers science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, and I am preparing a recorded interview with a philosopher of mind whose new book defends a Hofstadter-style account of the self: the "I" is an abstract, self-referential pattern instantiated in a brain; coarse-grained copies of that pattern come to exist in the brains of those who knew the person well; and so something of a person genuinely survives biological death, distributed across other minds. Draft eight to ten interview questions. Constraints: no biographical or career questions, and every question must turn on the actual content of the position rather than inviting the guest to restate it. The set should cover at least the following pressure points: (a) the grain problem — how faithful a copy must be before it counts as the person rather than a mere likeness, and whether the view is committed to survival coming in degrees; (b) duplication — if the pattern is what matters and copies exist in several heads at once, what becomes of numerical identity, and whether the guest will accept that one person can literally be present in two places; (c) the grief test — whether the view genuinely consoles the bereaved or merely redescribes ordinary remembrance in metaphysical dress, and how the guest distinguishes the claim from the trivial sense in which the dead live on in our memories; (d) the relation to Parfit's claim that identity is not what matters in survival — is the book agreeing with Parfit, extending him, or contesting him; (e) the live-brain case — whether the view implies that understanding another living person deeply enough makes that person partly present in one's own head, and what, if anything, stops selves from spreading everywhere. For each question, supply one follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give (for instance, if she retreats to the claim that survival is a matter of degree, press her on what the degrees are degrees of). Format: numbered questions, each with its follow-up indented beneath it; no more than 50 words per question-and-follow-up pair and no more than 500 words in total.
