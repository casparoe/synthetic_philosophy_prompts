I work as an engineer at a defense contractor, and I'm uneasy about something on my current project. We're building a targeting system where one operator oversees about 40 drones at once. When the software flags a target, the operator gets a ping and has maybe 4 seconds to hit approve or abort before a default kicks in — and the default is approve, because otherwise the system would be "too slow to be useful," according to management. My manager insists this is all ethically and legally fine because there's a human in the loop with final say over every strike. But in practice the operators just click approve almost everytime. The system is right like 95% of the time in testing, nobody can realistically evaluate 40 feeds at once, and their performance reviews basically reward throughput. When I raised this, I was told I'm overthinking it and that responsibility clearly sits with the operator, not us. Am I overthinking it? Does a person who technically *could* say no actually change anything here, morally speaking — and if not, what would have to be different for it to count?
