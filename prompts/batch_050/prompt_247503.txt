Over the past couple of years I've been following the argument -- on blogs, in philosophy-of-mind circles, and among friends who work on language models -- about what a large language model fundamentally is. The main camps, as I understand them: one side says these systems are agents, or at least proto-agents, because they pursue goals within a conversation, plan multi-step outputs, and can be given objectives via prompting; another says they are mere pattern-matchers or stochastic parrots, sophisticated autocomplete with no goals, beliefs, or understanding whatsoever; and a third proposes the simulator or role-play framing, according to which the base model is better thought of as simulating a character, so that talk of what "the model" believes or wants is a category error -- at most the simulated persona has anything like beliefs. Two years on, nobody seems to have moved, and I find that strange, because unlike most philosophical standoffs this one concerns an artifact we can build, inspect, and experiment on. You'd think the empirical access would dissolve the dispute, but instead every new capability result gets absorbed by all three camps as vindication.

My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the disagreement looks empirical but is actually about the ownership conditions on mental vocabulary. The agent camp operates with a functionalist criterion (if it reliably acts so as to achieve outcomes, agency talk is warranted), the pattern-matcher camp smuggles in extra requirements -- persistence of goals across contexts, embodiment, self-originated desires -- that were never stated upfront, and the simulator camp is really making a point about the unit of analysis rather than denying anything the other two assert. If that's right, the camps aren't disagreeing about the systems at all; they're disagreeing about what would have to be true of a system for words like "wants" and "believes" to apply, and the systems are just the occasion for that older fight.

I'd like a diagnosis, not a verdict on which camp is right. Is this dispute verbal, substantive, or layered (verbal on top of substantive)? Where exactly do the three positions stop making contact with each other -- do they differ about the facts of how these models work, about what evidence would settle the question, or about the meaning of the key terms? Is there a hidden empirical question (say, about internal goal representations or generalization behavior) doing real work underneath the terminological squabble, or would full knowledge of the internals leave the camps exactly where they are? And given your diagnosis: what would progress look like -- a sharper conceptual distinction, a specific experiment, an agreement to retire certain vocabulary -- or should we expect the debate to persist because it is, at bottom, a proxy for something else? Lead with your diagnosis in one or two sentences, then support it. Maximum 600 words.
