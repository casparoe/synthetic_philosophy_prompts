Okay so here's the deal. I'm a retired entomologist, spent four decades counting beetles, and about two years ago I fell down the philosophy rabbit hole via our library reading group. Last month we got into animal consciousness and the whole thing fell apart over whether my old study subjects can feel anything. Someone's aunt was horrified that I used to pin specimens, someone else said a beetle is basically a wind-up toy, and we went in circles for an hour.

So I want to build a sorting quiz for the group: "How far down does moral status go?" Not a personality quiz, a real diagnostic. Here's what I need:

- 8 to 10 questions. Every question must be a concrete case, never an abstraction. So not "do you think simple organisms can suffer?" but "a crab on a hot grill keeps trying to climb out even after its claws are removed; it's also true that a decapitated chicken runs around. Does the climbing-out tell you anything the running-around doesn't?" Something in that spirit, with two to four answer options each that sound equally reasonable.
- Cases that must show up somewhere: the nematode worm whose entire 302-neuron nervous system scientists have mapped completely (and it still avoids hot spots); bumblebees apparently playing with wooden balls for no reward; a fish that will pay a cost to get painkillers in its water; the bog-standard thermostat, since some folks apparently think there's no principled line between it and a worm; and at least one case involving a creature whose behaviors are fully explained by individual reflexes we can name one by one.
- The scoring key has to sort people into real positions, and here are the ones I want as endpoints: (a) the "you need the right kind of brain" threshold view, vertebrates in, insects out; (b) the view that flexible, learned behavior is itself the evidence, so bees and octopuses make the cut; (c) the strict "reflexes prove nothing" view that demands evidence of something like felt aversion -- motivational trade-offs, self-directed wound tending, that sort of thing; (d) the no-sharp-line view where sentience comes in degrees all the way down and the thermostat gets a microscopic sliver; (e) the hedging view where you multiply probability of suffering by its badness and act on the expected value, so a 15% chance the shrimp suffers counts as 15%; and (f) the view that what matters is having ongoing interests or preferences, so a creature with only fleeting moment-to-moment states has much less at stake even if it does feel.
- The key should work off answer patterns, not which label sounds nice. And please flag honestly any two of those positions the quiz can't actually separate, plus any answer combination that lands nowhere (incoherent? just unpopular? tell me which).
- End each result with one practical takeaway, like "you may keep using fly spray but should skip the crab boil," since half the group only cares about that part.

Casual tone throughout, it's for retirees and a couple of grandkids. Don't mention the position names anywhere in the questions themselves -- that's the whole point.
