I am putting together a reading week for a graduate seminar on the ground of promissory obligation, organized around William Godwin's attack on promises in An Enquiry Concerning Political Justice. Godwin's argument, roughly, is a dilemma: if the promised act was already something morality required, the promise adds nothing and is superfluous; if it was not, no utterance can make it obligatory, and treating it as binding is pernicious. Either way the institution of promising does no genuine normative work. I do not want the seminar to debate Godwin himself so much as use the dilemma as a test that any adequate theory should pass.

The accounts I already have on the syllabus, each of which claims to escape the dilemma: (1) Hume's conventionalist account -- promises bind by invoking a socially established practice whose general utility grounds an obligation that does not reduce to the independent rightness of the promised act; (2) Scanlon's expectation account -- the promisor has, with adequate warning, voluntarily led the promisee to expect performance, and it would be wrong to disappoint an expectation created under those conditions; (3) Raz's normative-power account -- promising is the deliberate exercise of a power to change one's own normative situation, and its value lies precisely in our being able to bind ourselves at will; (4) the rights-transfer account -- a promise does not create a reason ex nihilo but hands the promisee a claim-right over the promisor's conduct, on analogy with consent. I need one more account of why promises bind that is not on this list and not a restatement, special case, or hybrid of anything on it. Say how the new account answers Godwin's dilemma as I have stated it -- or, if you think it dissolves the dilemma rather than answering it, say where my statement of the dilemma goes wrong. Finally, be honest about epistemic status: separate what is broadly agreed among philosophers who work on promising from what is genuinely contested, and do not present any one account, including your new one, as the settled consensus.
