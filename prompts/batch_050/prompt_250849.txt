I keep running into the same kind of situation and I want to know if there's a real term for it, or if I'm just conflating a bunch of different things.

Here's the pattern. Someone gives something of value to a person in a position of power, and later that person does them a favor with their official position. But it's never bribery in the classic sense: there's no deal, no "you scratch my back I scratch yours," no moment where the terms get stated, and often the two events are months or years apart and routed through social stuff that would exist anyway. Concretely: the local building contractor who sponsors the mayor's kid's soccer team, picks up the tab at every fundraiser, and is just *around* being generous for years, and then somehow keeps winning municipal contracts without anything rigged on paper. Or the minister who gives his wife's nephew a government job, not because anyone paid him, but because in his community refusing family is what would be shameful, and taking care of your own is literally what makes you a decent person. Or the businesswoman who brings an honestly expensive gift to a official's daughter's wedding because in her culture showing up empty-handed would be the insult, and she'd do the same for anyone at her own status level.

What interests me is that from the outside, an auditor or a Western NGO would file all of these under corruption. From the inside, the participants would be genuinly offended by that, and not in a self-serving way -- they'd say the gift and the favor are part of one ongoing relationship, not two halves of a transaction, and that the Western demand to keep gifts and decisions in sealed separate boxes is itself kind of weird and cold, maybe even its own kind of corruption (like, what does it say about you that you'd only help someone for money or not at all?). Meanwhile the system-level effects -- insiders get contracts, outsiders never break in, loyalty trumps merit -- look identical either way.

So: does this whole configuration have a name? I know bits of it overlap with stuff like patronage, clientelism, gift economies, cronyism, "conflict of interest," but none of those seems to quite be the thing I'm pointing at, which is specifically about the mismatch between how the act is classified from inside vs. outside the relationship, plus the fact that nobody involved ever has to form a corrupt intention for the corrupt-looking outcome to happen. If there's an established concept or literature, tell me what it is, but don't just hand me a label -- I want to know how the thing I described differs from plain bribery, from legal lobbying and campaign donations (which to me look uncomfortably similar), and from genuinely innocent hospitality. Where are the edges? What would a case have to look like to NOT count? And is there any account of why one and the same practice reads as generosity in one frame and corruption in another, or who if anyone gets to arbitrate that?

Please answer in American English, and feel free to coin a term if the real ones don't fit.
