My uncle cornered me at Thanksgiving this year with a theory he clearly picked up from some youtube video: great powers last about 250 years on average, America is 249 years old if you count from 1776, so we're basically at the buzzer. I've been chewing on this for two weeks because I think he's doing something epistemically sloppy but I can't fully articulate what, and its bugging me. I tried the obvious move — "the average is meaningless, the variance is enormous" — and he just shrugged and said an average with big error bars still beats pure vibes, which, annoyingly, is not nothing.

So here's the question I actually want your credence on, decomposed. Take this proposition: a base-rate calculation over historical empires and great powers (the ones anyone could list off the top of their head — Rome, the Ottomans, the Chinese dynasties, the British empire, etc.) gives you genuinely decision-relevant information about the probability that the United States undergoes a fundamental regime break within the next 50 years, where "regime break" means constitutional collapse, breakup, conquest, or revolution — something a neutral historian in 2200 would classify as a discontinuity rather than a continuation. I want your number, but mostly the decomposition. As I see it the credence rides on at least: (1) whether "empire/great power" is a real reference class at all or just a storytelling category where start and end dates get picked to fit a narrative; (2) whether the sample is survivorship-poisoned, since we only have completed lifespans for powers that already ended, and anything still running (or that transmuted slowly instead of dying) gets excluded or miscounted; (3) whether modern conditions — nuclear deterrence, instant communication, no more subsistence crises, mass democracy — break the analogy enough that the old base rates should barely move you; and (4) whether there's even a crisp event to predict, given that Rome "fell" over centuries and plenty of supposed ends were actually continuities wearing a new name. Give each sub-question a rough probability, show how they combine into your final credence, and flag which term the answer is most sensitive to.

Then tell me where my uncle's mistake actually lives, if he has one. My suspicion is that the reference class problem does all the damage — he can get anything from 20 years to 2000 by choosing what counts as the class — but I worry that dismissing base rates entirely is its own failure mode, the classic inside-view move of assuming this time is different. So if you think the outside view still earns some weight here, tell me what reference class you'd actually use for a modern industrial democracy, since "empire" seems too dramatic and "countries" seems too coarse. If you think it earns none, tell me what would count as a legitimate use of historical base rates for anything, so you aren't commited to never learning from the past at all.
