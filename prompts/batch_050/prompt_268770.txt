Before I read any further into the philosophy and psychology of humour, I want a specification rather than another theory. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of humour must meet to count as a theory of humour at all, as opposed to a theory of laughter, of comedy, or of some privileged subclass of jokes. The list should cover, at minimum: the phenomena the theory must explain (why incongruity alone is neither necessary nor sufficient — an anachronism in a film is incongruous without being funny, and a well-told punning insult can be funny without much incongruity; why tickling produces laughter without humour; why the same joke fails on third hearing; why dark humour succeeds for some audiences and merely offends others; why puns draw groans alongside laughs); the distinctions it must respect (humour versus laughter versus amusement versus wit; finding something funny versus judging it to be a good joke; the difference between a joke that fails by being too benign and one that fails by being too transgressive); and the findings it cannot contradict (laughter in nervous, social, and non-humorous contexts; humour's apparent universality across cultures alongside its strong local variation; the existence of teasing and play signals in non-human animals).

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as a commitment of a particular research tradition, since I suspect several items people routinely demand of humour theories are really the superiority theorist's, the incongruity theorist's, or the relief theorist's own predictions dressed up as data. The part I care most about: say whether any subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, whether a single theory can both explain why humour requires a violation to register as a violation and explain why humour is so often a bonding and status-management device, without collapsing one function into the other. Identify which single requirement, taken seriously, eliminates the most existing contenders.

Then, briefly, score the four standard accounts — superiority (Hobbes's lineage), incongruity (the Kant–Schopenhauer lineage), relief (the Spencer–Freud lineage), and benign violation theory (McGraw and Warren) — against the list, noting where each passes, where each fails, and where a failure is a genuine empirical miss versus a failure of scope that the theory never claimed.

Open with your bottom line in the first sentence — which requirement does the most eliminative work — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question; a table is fine for the scoring. State how confident you are in the overall verdict, and single out the one point in the whole answer you are least sure of.
