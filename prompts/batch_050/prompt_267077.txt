In our undergraduate ethics society's debate on legalizing kidney sales, the affirmative side leaned on an analogy I have since heard elsewhere: selling a kidney is no different in principle from what commercial divers, coal miners, and professional footballers already do, since they too sell the use and gradual degradation of their bodies for wages, and the kidney seller merely compresses the same transaction into a single afternoon. The negative replied that an organ is not a shift of labor: what is sold cannot be rested, retrained, or recovered, and the circumstances of a typical seller make the supposed bargain unlike any employment contract. Please audit the analogy rather than re-litigate the policy from scratch: tell me what it establishes if it holds, and what is left of the affirmative's case once it is qualified.
