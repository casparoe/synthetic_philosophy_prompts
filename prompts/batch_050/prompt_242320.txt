I got cornered after reading too much suffering-focused ethics at 1am and now my friend’s extinction argument seems to prove way too much, but I can’t find the exact crack. Call the background view the analgesic ledger: pain, misery, frustration, terror and so on are the only things with final moral weight, while pleasure is like pain relief - welcome when it removes badness, not a seperate positive currency that can push an outcome above neutral. His numbered version: (1) Outcome A is morally better than outcome B iff A contains less total suffering, weighted by intensity, duration and number of beings. (2) Pleasure never adds positive value; at most it prevents, relieves or distracts from suffering. (3) Any future with sentient life has nonzero expected suffering, even in very lucky lives, because accidents, grief, illness and boredom are never fully excludable. (4) A blank outcome with no sentience has no suffering, and nobody is deprived of pleasure because there is no subject to miss it. (5) So every possible sentient future scores below the blank, or at best ties it if the maths gets weird. (6) If a better available outcome violates no one’s claim, we ought to bring it about. (7) No uncreated person has a claim to exist, and existing people could in principle be ended painlessly, without fear or warning, so no claim is violated there either. (8) Therefore the ideal is an instantaneous painless shutdown of all sentience, and any refusal has secretly smuggled positive value back in. I want to keep (1) and (4) if possible, mostly because I don’t want goods to be easily trumps for torture, but (8) is obviously barmy.
