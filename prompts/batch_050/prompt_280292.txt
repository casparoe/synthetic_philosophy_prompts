I work as a software engineer and am trying to understand what conception of freedom is built into the governance of mainstream mobile computing platforms. I have no formal philosophy background, but I have encountered the rough distinctions between negative liberty as freedom from interference, positive liberty as having the effective capability to act, and republican liberty as freedom from arbitrary power.

Consider this familiar arrangement. A platform provides SDKs, documentation, emulators and sample code so that almost anyone can learn to build an application. A developer may experiment privately with relatively little oversight. Commercial distribution, however, normally requires a paid developer account, platform-controlled code signing and approval through a central application store. The platform can reject or remove applications under broad policies concerning malware, privacy, payments and objectionable content, sometimes without a detailed individual explanation. The operating system also makes installation outside the approved store difficult, ostensibly to protect users from security threats. The platform describes the overall arrangement as supporting developer freedom, user choice and a secure ecosystem.

What view or views of liberty does this practice presuppose? In particular:

- Is the free availability of development tools being treated as sufficient for meaningful developer freedom, even when distribution depends on a continuing and revocable permission?
- Is the restriction on installing software outside the store understood as a reduction in user and developer liberty, as a condition for preserving their liberty against malware and fraud, or as a justified trade-off between the liberties of different people?
- Does predictable enforcement of published rules remove the republican objection to platform control, or does broad discretion to reject, delist or change policies constitute domination even if it is rarely exercised?
- Does the arrangement rule out a purely negative conception of liberty, or can that conception explain why the central review is objectionable while ordinary security protections may not be?
- Which features merely fit comfortably with a conception of liberty without strictly presupposing it, and where is the practice philosophically underdetermined?

Please reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as possible, identify any inconsistencies between the platform’s treatment of developers, users and security, and then describe how the governance model would have to change if it were redesigned around, respectively, negative liberty, positive liberty and republican liberty. I am interested in the best reconstruction rather than a verdict on whether any real company is morally good or bad. Please answer in British English.
