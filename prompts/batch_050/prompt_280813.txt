Clinical medicine uses two different methods for measuring pain, depending on the patient. Patients who can speak are asked to rate their pain on a numeric scale from 0 to 10, and standard clinical guidance treats the answer as settling the matter: the patient's report, not the clinician's observation, is the measure of the pain. Patients who cannot speak -- infants, sedated or demented patients -- are instead assessed on behavioral scales that score grimacing, limb movement, crying, and consolability, and the resulting number is treated as a measurement of the same quantity. In sections 244 to 270 of the Philosophical Investigations, Wittgenstein argues against the idea that a sensation word gets its meaning by private ostensive definition -- by a person's inwardly pointing to a sensation only they can access. In section 270 he considers someone who keeps a diary in which a private sign is recorded whenever a certain sensation occurs, and who notices that the sensation correlates with a rise in blood pressure on a manometer; Wittgenstein argues that once the sign has this public use, it no longer matters whether the person has correctly re-identified the sensation, and the very idea of a private checking procedure becomes an idle wheel. Reconstruct what the two clinical practices presuppose about the nature of pain and pain language. Does the authority granted to the verbal self-report presuppose the picture Wittgenstein is attacking -- pain as a private object that only the sufferer can inspect and name -- or can the 0-to-10 scale be understood in the way his manometer case suggests, as a public instrument that needs no inner act of recognition behind it? Do the behavioral scales presuppose the opposite extreme, that pain simply is its behavioral expression, or is that a misreading? State where the two practices are philosophically underdetermined -- compatible with several views of sensation -- and where, if anywhere, they commit to something Wittgenstein's argument rules out. Finally, say what would have to change if his argument is sound: for instance, whether the incorrigibility of the self-report should be retained, reinterpreted, or abandoned in favor of treating it as one fallible reading among others.
