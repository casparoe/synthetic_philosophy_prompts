I am a novelist, and I have noticed that my characters — and, worse, my narrator — traffic constantly in generic sentences: "waiters remember faces," "small towns forgive nothing," "a mother knows." Some of these survive their exceptions; some are exactly as false as their exceptions make them, and the difference matters to me on the page. Before I wade into the semantics literature on generics, I want to know what I should be asking of it. Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of generic sentences ("ducks lay eggs," "mosquitoes carry malaria") must deliver — the cases it must get right, the contrasts it must not collapse, the judgments it must predict — with the consideration or example behind each requirement.

I want the list to wrestle, specifically, with the following puzzles, and to say what counts as resolving each: the fact that "ducks lay eggs" is true although the males never do, while "ducks are female" is false although roughly half are; the fact that "mosquitoes carry malaria" and "sharks attack swimmers" seem true on the strength of a tiny minority, while "books are paperbacks" seems false on the strength of a clear majority; the difference between kind-level generics ("the dodo is extinct") and property generics, and whether one account can serve both; and the difference between descriptive generics and normative ones ("boys don't cry"). For each desideratum, tell me whether you take it to be non-negotiable for any theory or a preference of a particular camp, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — that tension, if it exists, is the part I most want understood.

Finally, and briefly: how do the main families of proposals — modal or "normalcy" accounts, statistical or prevalence-based accounts, kind-based accounts — fare against the list, and which single desideratum does the most to exclude candidates? Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than the scoring. My purpose, to fix what "adequate" means here: an account subtle enough to tell me which generic sentences of mine will still be true in a world where the exceptions pile up, and which were never doing more than sounding true.
