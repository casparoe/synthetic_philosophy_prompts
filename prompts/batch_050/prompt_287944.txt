My father-in-law ambushed me at Sunday dinner with what he clearly thinks is a checkmate and it's been bugging me all week. His argument: Karl Popper, the philosopher who basically invented the idea that real science has to be falsifiable, supposedly said himself that Darwinism fails his own test. Natural selection, per Popper, boils down to "the survivors survive," which is a tautology -- true by definition, compatible with anything, therefore explains nothing, the same problem Popper had with Freud and Marx. And Popper apparently even called Darwinism a "metaphysical research programme," which my father-in-law reads as Popper flat-out admitting evolution isn't science. So my question: given that the falsifiability guy himself ruled evolution out of science, why do basically all biologists and philosophers of biology still treat natural selection as the paradigm of good testable science? Is there some actual risky prediction in there that I'm missing, or is everyone just too embarassed to concede the point to a dead philosopher? Genuinely asking, because I have to sit across from this guy again at Christmas and I want to know if he actually has a leg to stand on.
