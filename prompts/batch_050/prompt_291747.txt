I got into a weirdly heated argument at work last week and I keep thinking about it. Two senior engineers were going back and forth about whether our new service "counts as" a memory leak. Basically: it holds references to objects in a cache that never gets evicted, so memory grows unboundedly, but the objects are all technically still reachable, so one guy said it's by definition not a leak, just "unbounded growth," and the other said that's a distinction without a difference. Our manager eventually stepped in and said "guys, this is just semantics, move on," and everyone accepted that and moved on. Except I don't think it was just semantics, because depending on what you call it, it either goes in the incident tracker or it doesn't, and one of them literally said "if it's a leak we have to fix it this sprint, if it's not it goes in the backlog."

So my question: when someone says a dispute is "just semantics," what are the precise things they could be claiming? I can gesture at a few myself -- like (a) both sides already agree on all the facts and are only fighting over which word to use, (b) the question of which word applies has no correct answer because meanings are just conventions, (c) the question of which word applies has a correct answer but answering it doesn't matter, and (d) the dispute would dissolve if everyone agreed to taboo the contested word and speak in more basic terms. But I suspect there are more, and I suspect they don't all have the same status. Which of these are trivially true of almost any disagreement that uses words, which are substantive claims that would need an argument, and which are just confused? Also: which reading does my manager need for "move on" to actually follow? Because reading (c) seems like it might be true and yet NOT support moving on, since we still have to decide what to put in the incident tracker.

And one more thing that bugs me as someone with no philosphy training: is there any general test for telling a merely verbal dispute apart from a substantive one before you resolve it? My coworker insisted there was a "real question" about what a memory leak IS, independent of what we call it, and honestly that sounded wrong to me but I couldn't articulate why. Lay the readings out as a menu with verdicts rather than picking one and explaining it -- I want to be able to deploy this the next time this exact fight happens, which it will.
