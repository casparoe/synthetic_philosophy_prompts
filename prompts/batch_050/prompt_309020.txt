My cousin has been going back and forth with her boyfriend about what she owes her mom, who is 70, lonely, and kind of difficult, and I want a verdict. Her boyfriend says she should think of her mom the way Jane English suggested grown children think of their parents: the sacrifices parents make for kids are given out of love, not as loans, so nothing is "owed" afterward -- if the relationship continues it should be because there's still genuine affection, the same standard as a friendship, and his mom barely calls his own dad and feels fine about it. My cousin said that's a rationalization: friendship is a terrible model because you choose your friends and can unilaterally end a friendship that's gone cold, but your mother is non-fungible -- she's the specific person who raised you, and no amount of affection toward other people substitutes for what only you can give her. He replied that if the past is what grounds the duty, then she'd owe just as much to an abusive parent who fed and housed her, since the "debt" from care received doesn't depend on the parent being pleasant now -- and that obligation-talk is corrosive anyway, because a visit paid as a debt is worth less to the mother than one given freely, so the debt framing undermines the very good it's supposed to secure. She said he's smuggling in the conclusion: of course the good is affection, but the duty is what keeps you showing up during the stretches when affection runs dry, which is exactly when the lonely 70-year-old most needs you -- friends let each other drift, and that's fine for friends precisely because friends have other friends. He said duties that persist regardless of the relationship's quality are just guilt with a philosophy degree, and that the real reason she visits her mom is love, not duty, so the duty is doing no work. They both looked at me and I punted. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who has the better of each point, and whether the friendship model can actually handle the cases where affection has run out -- or whether it was never really meant to?
