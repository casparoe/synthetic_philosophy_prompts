The exam question was:

"Explain what Sartre means when he says human freedom is 'radical,' and how bad faith (mauvaise foi) functions as a flight from that freedom. Use at least one of Sartre's own examples, and explain why bad faith is inherently unstable on his account."

Here's the student's answer:

"For Sartre, freedom is radical because there is no human nature or essence that determines what we do -- existence precedes essence, so at every moment we choose what we are, and we are responsible for all of it. We are 'condemned to be free.' Even refusing to choose is a choice. Bad faith is the attempt to escape the anguish this responsibility produces by pretending we are not free.

Sartre gives famous examples of this. The waiter in the café who moves a little too quickly, who plays at being a waiter, is in bad faith because he flees his facticity into transcendence: he treats his role as just a costume he's freely adopted and ignores the fixed, given facts of his situation. Similarly, the woman on a date who leaves her hand in her companion's hand without noticing it is in bad faith because she flees transcendence into facticity: she treats herself as a mere thing, a passive object wholly determined by circumstances, and so denies that she is free to decide what the moment means. Bad faith always involves denying one of these two poles -- the given facts of our situation, or our freedom to transcend them -- by collapsing one into the other.

Bad faith is unstable because it requires knowing the truth in order to hide it. To lie to myself about my freedom I must already be aware of that freedom, so the deceiver and the deceived are the same person and the lie can never fully succeed. Sartre illustrates the anguish we flee with the example of vertigo: standing at the edge of a cliff, I feel anguish at the possibility that I might lose my footing and fall, which shows how fragile my situation is. Because bad faith can never fully convince us, it is a precarious, self-defeating project that consciousness sustains only by constant effort."
