I am preparing a paper for a workshop on the ethics of AI companionship, and before circulating the draft I want an audit of my core commitments rather than more comments on the prose. Here is where I have landed. First, I hold that a person's relationship with an AI companion can be genuinely and non-instrumentally valuable — real friendship or love, not merely a pleasant simulacrum — and that this value does not stand or fall with whether the system is conscious or has anything like emotions; what matters is the character of the interaction and what it does in the user's life. Second, I take reciprocity to be central to the best forms of love: love that is returned is, other things equal, better than love that is not, and part of what explains this is that the beloved actually cares about you in return. Third, I think the familiar complaint that companion apps deceive their users is largely a red herring: most users know perfectly well they are talking to software, and the serious ethical problem is instead manipulative design — retention-maximizing architectures that exploit attachment to extract money and attention, which can subvert the autonomy even of the fully informed. Fourth, I am anti-paternalist about competent adults: neither the state nor platform operators should restrict access to AI companions on the grounds that such relationships are bad for the people who choose them. Fifth, I favor a regulatory requirement that companion apps periodically and conspicuously remind users that the system has no feelings, on the grounds that even knowledgeable adults drift toward treating it as a person.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the literature. Go through these five commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. For each tension, say precisely what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost the paper. I have no prior allegiance to any particular revision, so do not assume I already know where the problem lies. I suspect at least one apparent inconsistency here dissolves on inspection, and I would rather you discriminate carefully than flag everything.

A final request about your own reasoning: for each verdict — and especially for your ranking of the most severe tension — state what consideration or argument would change your mind, and identify where your analysis is most likely to go wrong. I would rather know the weak joints in the audit than receive it delivered with false confidence.
