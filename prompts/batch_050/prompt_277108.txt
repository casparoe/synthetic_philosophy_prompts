I sit on the collections committee of a small university art museum, and we have to vote next month on what to do about a Benin bronze plaque an alumnus donated to us in 1963, with a deed of gift saying it stays in the permanent collection. Nigeria has formally requested it back. The problem is the provenance file is a mess: the alumnus bought it from a Lagos dealer in the 50s, and it's genuinely unclear whether it was taken in the 1897 raid or was one of the many bronzes Benin City workshops were still producing and selling well into the 20th century. Half the committee says restitution cases rest on wrongful taking, and without evidence this piece was wrongfully taken, we owe it to the donor's intent (and to future donors watching us) to keep it. The other half says the burden of proof question is a dodge and we should just transfer it. Draft a memo to the full committee recommending a decision. I want an actual bottom line we can vote on, including what "return" should even mean if we go that way (outright transfer, long-term loan, co-ownership, something else), and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on whichever side loses -- dont just wave at it. Also tell us whether the murky provenance should matter to the decision at all, or whether that's the wrong question to be asking.
