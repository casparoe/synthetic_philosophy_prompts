Been going down a rabbit hole on Schrödinger lately — not the cat, but his later philosophical stuff where he argues, drawing on Vedanta, that consciousness is fundamentally one and the feeling of being a seperate individual mind is a kind of illusion. Here's my question. A few years after he died, the split-brain experiments started suggesting that if you sever the corpus callosum, the two hemispheres behave almost like two independent minds: one hemisphere can talk about what it saw while the other can't, but can still point to the right answer with the left hand. If Schrödinger had lived to see that, what do you reckon he'd have said? Would he take it as a straight refutation of the unity of consciousness, or would he have some move available — like saying the unity was never about everyday psychology in the first place — and if so, would that move actually mean anything, or would it just save the theory by making it unfalsifiable? I'm not a philosopher, so I'd also like to know whether his view was ever the sort of thing that could be tested at all, or if it's more like a religious claim wearing lab goggles.
