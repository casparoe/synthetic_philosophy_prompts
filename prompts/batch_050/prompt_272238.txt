I've been in a running argument with a colleague in my department about the state of play in the metaphysics of causation, and I want to figure out whether our disagreement is actually about the cases or about something more methodological. Her position is roughly that counterfactual-dependence theories in the Lewis tradition are a degenerating research programme: every problem case (late preemption, trumping, overdetermination) gets met with another patch -- fragile events, quasi-dependence, influence-amplifying alterations -- and each patch handles the case that motivated it while generating a new counterexample. My position is that interventionism in the Woodward style has the same disease in a different place: it defines causation in terms of interventions, interventions have to be characterized causally to avoid letting in confounded "fat-handed" manipulations, and so the account either goes in a circle or quietly presupposes the counterfactual notions it was supposed to replace. We also disagree about what the scoreboard even is: she counts intuitive verdicts about cases, I count whether the theory gives you a usable notion of causal relata and compositional structure.

To make this concrete, here's the case that started the fight, which we cooked up ourselves and can't find in the literature. A safety system has two sensors, A and B, each of which will detect a fire if one occurs. The system is wired so that if both sensors fire simultaneously, a priority circuit routes only A's signal to the sprinkler and physically blocks B's; if only one fires, that one's signal goes through. A fire occurs; both sensors fire simultaneously; A's signal is routed and the sprinkler activates. Intuitively (to me) A's firing caused the sprinkler to activate and B's didn't, even though B would have caused it had A not fired -- it's a trumping structure. My colleague says counterfactual dependence can only recover this by individuating events so finely (the sprinkler-activating-*via-A's-signal*) that the fix is available to any theory and hence no fix at all. I say the interventionist has the mirror-image problem: the "intervention" on B that would test B's causal status has to hold A fixed, but holding the routed signal fixed while varying B just reproduces the wiring diagram's asymmetry, which is exactly what was in question.

State a precise proposition you take to be the crux -- something like: within the next two decades, interventionist accounts will be treated as the default general theory of causation in analytic philosophy, with counterfactual-dependence accounts retained mainly as pedagogical or special-case tools. Give me your own credence in that proposition (or in a better-specified version you prefer, but say exactly what you're putting the number on). Then decompose it: break the credence into the sub-questions it depends on -- e.g., whether trumping cases like ours are genuinely fatal to unpatched counterfactual theories; whether the fragility/quasi-dependence patches are ad hoc or independently motivated; whether the circularity charge against interventionism is fatal or manageable; whether sociological inertia rather than philosophical merit would drive any convergence; whatever you think the real load-bearing questions are. Assign a rough probability to each, show me how they combine into your final number, and identify which single sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to -- i.e., where new argument or evidence would move your number most. Finally, given that decomposition: is my disagreement with my colleague most likely located in the case verdicts, in the circularity dispute, or in the disagreement about what counts as a win? Give a probability for each location.
