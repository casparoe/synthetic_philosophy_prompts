I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm finishing a long piece on whether physics and neuroscience bear on criminal responsibility. Two philosophers I interviewed both pointed me to Galen Strawson's Basic Argument as the strongest version of the case that determinism rules out moral responsibility: you act as you do because of the way you are, you can't be responsible for the way you are unless you chose it, and that choice would have to stem from a way you already were, and so on back. My question is about framing. If the argument gets its force from determinism — from character and choice being fixed by prior causes — then presumably it stands or falls with the physics: a genuinely indeterministic universe, or a libertarian account of agent causation, would reopen the door to ultimate responsibility. Should I present the Basic Argument as hostage to whatever the physics of decision-making turns out to be? And if not, why do its defenders keep stressing that our choices are causally fixed?
