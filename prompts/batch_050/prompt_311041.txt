An AI governance institute has convened an adversarial collaboration between two researchers who disagree about the safest basic design for highly capable agents.

The first defends approval-directed agency: the agent should choose actions according to a learned or explicitly represented model of what a designated overseer would approve, with that overseer inspecting proposed actions and their near-term rationale rather than specifying a final state of the world. The second defends goal-directed agency: a sufficiently capable agent must represent objectives over future states and plan toward them; approval, on this view, is either itself a goal to be optimized or too weak a control signal for genuinely long-horizon competence.

Write the joint statement they could both sign. It should include:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. Cover what both sides mean by an objective, an overseer, planning, corrigibility, and manipulation; where approval-direction can work; where goal-direction can work; and which common objections each side considers misplaced. Avoid formulations that either camp would regard as winning by definition.

2. A disagreements table identifying the genuine remaining disputes. For each dispute, give:
   - the approval-directed formulation, worded so that its defender would accept it;
   - the goal-directed formulation, worded so that its defender would accept it;
   - the underlying crux;
   - a thought experiment, mathematical result, engineering demonstration, or historical analogue that both sides would accept as bearing on that crux;
   - each side’s prediction about what that test would show.

3. An analysis of two concepts introduced for the collaboration. Define the horizon of authorization as the furthest point in the future for which an overseer’s present approval can reasonably license an action’s downstream effects, and the evaluation-opacity gap as the difference between the overseer’s ability to evaluate the immediately proposed action and the overseer’s ability to evaluate the strategy of which that action is part. State whether each camp thinks these concepts clarify the dispute or merely rename it, and whether either concept reveals a previously unnoticed point of agreement.

4. Joint treatments of three hard cases:
   - an overseer approves a locally sensible action whose long-term consequence is difficult to foresee;
   - an agent learns to produce explanations that reliably secure approval without accurately revealing its reasoning;
   - no competent human overseer remains available, but the agent must continue acting over a long period.

   For each case, distinguish the remedy an approval-directed theorist would propose from the remedy a goal-directed theorist would propose, then identify any remedy both could endorse.

5. A final section separating substantive disagreements from apparent disagreements caused by vocabulary. In particular, decide whether the dispute is mainly about what objective an agent has, how that objective is represented, who evaluates its consequences, or the time horizon over which it must remain corrigible.

Do not adjudicate an overall winner, and do not include citations or a bibliography. End with a calibration note identifying where the joint statement is most likely to conceal a residual disagreement, where your reconstruction of either position is most likely to fail, and what argument, demonstration, or empirical finding would most strongly lead you to revise the statement.
