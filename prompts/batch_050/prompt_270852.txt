I'm in a philosophy of physics reading group and we keep going around in circles on whether beauty is any kind of evidence for a physical theory. One guy keeps bringing up Dirac and Einstein betting on elegant equations, I keep bringing up that the LHC was supposed to confirm naturalness-flavored physics and found nothing, and someone else points out that epicycles were considered beautiful in their day so maybe our taste is just parochial. Every session ends with everyone agreeing we don't even know what would count as settling it.

So before we read any actual accounts, I want the spec first: what would a theory of elegance-as-evidence in science have to get right to count as adequate? Fix the purpose as this: good enough to tell a working physicist whether she should trust her instinct that the elegant formulation of a theory is the one worth pursuing. Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration motivating it. Some things I suspect belong on the list, push back where I'm wrong: it has to explain the failures as well as the successes (naturalness at the LHC, the steady-state theorists finding an expanding universe ugly) and not just retell general relativity; it has to account for the fact that beauty judgments drift historically, since non-Euclidean geometry and renormalization were both once considered hideous; it can't treat beauty as one thing when symmetry, simplicity, unification, and naturalness demonstrably pull apart in real cases; and it has to deal with formulation-dependence — the same theory can look elegant or clunky depending on how its written down (covariant vs. non-covariant, one duality frame vs. another), so the account needs to say what's actually being judged, the theory or its presentation. Also, if the theory is supposed to be normative and not just descriptive of taste, say what would ground the norm.

Then the part I care most about: flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied. My hunch is that "explain why elegance tracks truth" and "accommodate the empirical unreliability of elegance" are in real tension, and that whichever one you soften decides which camp you land in. Also tell me which single desideratum knocks out the most contenders. Finally, a quick scoring pass of the obvious positions — roughly: beauty as genuine evidence of truth, beauty as a community's inductively updated standard, and beauty as sociological residue with no evidential force — but keep the scorecard brief, the criteria are the deliverable.
