Six months ago, my brother arrived in our city as an asylum seeker, and I saw how arbitrary access to integration support can be. I have since become the intake coordinator for a municipal welcome program. Each week I must divide limited housing stipends, language-course seats, legal-aid hours, childcare subsidies, and employment coaching among roughly fifteen newcomer households. My decisions affect whether people can work, keep custody of their children, avoid homelessness, and remain lawfully present. I have about forty-five minutes per household, imperfect information, and a senior administrator must approve any exception to the standard allocation.

Write a reusable decision procedure that my team could actually follow, grounded in the philosophical debate over whether equality should concern welfare, resources, or capabilities. Do not merely summarize those positions. Translate them into an operational method for allocating different kinds of support.

The procedure should distinguish at least three measures of disadvantage: subjective welfare, such as distress or life satisfaction; resources, such as money, service hours, and legal entitlements; and capabilities, understood as real opportunities to secure safety, maintain family relationships, communicate, work, participate in civic life, and exercise practical judgment. Explain which measure should govern at each decision point and why. Address the familiar problems that welfare reports can be shaped by adaptation, equal resources can produce radically unequal opportunities, and capability comparisons can become intrusive or impossible to verify.

Please incorporate two operational devices and define them precisely enough for a caseworker to use:

1. A conversion-factor audit, identifying features that affect a household’s ability to turn a given resource into a valuable opportunity. Include disability, language proficiency, trauma, caregiving obligations, unrecognized credentials, discrimination, unstable housing, legal status, and urgent deadlines.
2. A dominant-disadvantage floor, specifying when severe insecurity in one capability must take priority over improving aggregate welfare or achieving a more balanced distribution of resources.

The output should include: ordered screening questions; evidence standards; a method for comparing needs that cannot easily be assigned numerical values; rules for ties; constraints against discrimination, humiliating surveillance, and making assistance conditional on gratitude or assimilation; and explicit points where professional judgment remains unavoidable. It should also identify what the procedure cannot legitimately decide.

Apply the finished procedure to three cases: a single young engineer whose foreign credentials are not recognized; an elderly woman with limited mobility who cares for a grandchild while the child’s mother works informally; and a family whose children are thriving at school but whose father has become severely depressed after repeated detention hearings. Include one application in which the procedure requires withholding support from a sympathetic household because another household’s case is stronger, and explain how I should record that decision without pretending it is morally costless.

Use American English. Do not provide citations, a bibliography, or a reading list.
