A friend of mine has this argument about the doomsday argument and I can't accept the conclusion but I also can't crack it. Here it is:

(1) My birth rank is roughly 100 billion — about that many humans have lived before me.
(2) Consider two hypotheses: Doom Soon (total humans ever ≈ 200 billion) and Doom Late (total humans ever ≈ many trillions, e.g. we spread to the stars and keep going for ages).
(3) Under Doom Soon, my rank of 100 billion is unremarkable, I'm somewhere in the middle of the pack. Under Doom Late, it's wildly atypical — I'm in the first tiny sliver of all humans who will ever exist.
(4) Evidence that's ordinary under one hypothesis and bizarre under another is strong evidence for the first. That's just how likelihood ratios work, and it's exactly the urn case: draw ball #7 from an urn you know holds either 10 or 10,000 numbered balls, and you should be nearly certain it's the 10-ball urn.
(5) So my birth rank gives me something like a 100-to-1 (or steeper) update in favor of Doom Soon, and unless my prior was astronomically tilted toward Doom Late, I should now take near-term human extinction way more seriously than I did.
(6) The standard objection — "every past generation could have run this argument, and almost all of them would have been wrong, so the argument must be broken" — doesn't touch it, because the argument only gets going for agents who actually estimate their birth rank and reason this way. That's a tiny, recent sliver of all humans. So the relevant reference class is rank-reasoners, not everyone, and there's no embarrassing track record of failed predictions to explain away.
(7) So the doomsday argument survives its best objection and really does jack up the probability of extinction a lot.

I don't buy the conclusion, but every premise looks fine on its own, and I'd really like to keep (4) since the urn case seems airtight. So: which step do I give up? Explain (a) why that step looked fine in the first place, (b) what rejecting it costs — in particular whether rejecting it forces me to say something weird about the urn case, which I'd hate, and (c) whether the move in (6) to "rank-reasoners" as the reference class actually saves the argument or secretly sinks it, since I've seen people claim it does both. Also, does the diagnosis change depending on whether the only thing I know is my own rank, vs. a world where I also know that about 8 billion people are alive right now? It feels like that second fact should matter but I can't say how.
