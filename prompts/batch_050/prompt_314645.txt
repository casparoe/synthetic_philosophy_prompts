I've basically talked myself into the capability view as the right answer to the equality-of-what question -- the line Sen pushes in his 1979 Tanner lecture Equality of What?, where he argues that neither welfare nor primary goods can handle the fact that people need wildly different amounts of resources to do the same things (his example being roughly that a disabled person with the same bundle of goods as everyone else is still worse off in what they can actually be and do). So tell me what would count *against* it. I don't want the lazy version where you just list Dworkin's or Arneson's objections; I want a map of the view's actual exposure. Which cases, results, or concessions would genuinely sink capability equality versus merely embarrass it? Where does it stand or fall with claims elsewhere (about value pluralism, about whether we can even agree a list of central capabilities without smuggling in a whole theory of the good)? And be honest about whether defenders have padded it with so many qualifications -- sufficientarian floors, "capability not functioning," incomplete lists -- that it no longer risks anything.

Format: What the view actually commits to (~150 words), Fatal if true (the defeaters that would make me abandon it, ranked, ~250 words), Merely awkward (~150 words), What defenders would themselves concede (~100 words), and Unfalsifiable? (your verdict on whether thats a problem here, ~100 words).
