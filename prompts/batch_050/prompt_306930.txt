my officemate (a condensed matter guy) loves the jab usually credited to Feynman, that philosophy of science is about as useful to scientists as ornithology is to birds. he wheels it out whenever the phil of science people down the hall invite us to their talks, the idea being that working scientists have nothing to gain from philosophers describing their own methods back at them. how far does the analogy actually go? give me the correspondence -- who are the birds, who are the ornithologists, what's the birdwatching -- and then the places it breaks: birds can't read ornithology and wouldn't change how they fly if they could, whereas scientists can read philosophy and occasionally do; ornithology is purely descriptive of birds while philosophy of science claims to be at least partly normative about science; birds didn't invent ornithology but scientists arguably invented philosophy of science. tell me which of these disanalogies matter for his conclusion and which are pedantic, and whether the analogy supports the strong claim (philosophy is useless to science) or only the weak one (most scientists get by fine without it). also say how confident you are in your verdict and flag the single step you're least sure of.
