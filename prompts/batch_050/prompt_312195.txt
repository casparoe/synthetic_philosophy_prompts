Ok so in debate club we ended up talking about this old Lewis Carroll dialogue where a tortoise keeps refusing to accept the conclusion of an argument until Achilles adds one more premise, and then one more, forever. It got me thinking and I wrote up this argument and now it freaks me out a little because the conclusion seems nuts but I can't find the bad step:

(1) A valid deduction is just one where if the premises are true, the conclusion has to be true.
(2) To be justified in accepting a conclusion from some premises, I need to be justified in believing that the inference is valid.
(3) Being justified in believing the inference is valid = being justified in believing a conditional: IF the premises are true, THEN the conclusion is true.
(4) But just believing that conditional, plus believing the premises, doesn't get me to the conclusion unless I also infer using modus ponens.
(5) So to be justified in drawing the conclusion, I also need to be justified in believing modus ponens is valid.
(6) By (3), that means being justified in believing ANOTHER conditional, which by (4) I'd need modus ponens to apply, and so on forever.
(7) So justified deduction requires infinitely many justificatory steps.
(8) Nobody can actually go through infinitely many steps.
(9) So nobody is ever genuinely justified in believing anything on the basis of a deduction. Every proof, including all of math, ultimately just rests on us agreeing not to ask the tortoise's question.

Now obviously (9) can't be right, I'm writing geometry proofs in class and they sure feel justified. But I genuinely can't find the step to reject. My friend says (8) is the weak link because "you don't have to DO all the steps, they just have to be available," but that seems like cheating, available to whom? If I had to pick I'd most want to keep (2), since it just seems like what justification means. Which step do I actually reject, what did it get me with (it all looks so innocent), and what does rejecting it cost me? Like does killing this argument mean I have to say some inferences are just primitively justified, and isn't that also weird?
