Here's a thing I keep running into in totally different parts of life, and I want to know if philosophy (or psych, or anywhere) has an actual name for it, or for the distinction I think is hiding inside it. You should answer as a philosopher of action who's also familiar with the relevant psychology, and please brainstorm neighboring concepts first before settling on an answer -- I don't want a one-word label, I want the carving.

The pattern: certain things I want reliably fail when I go at them directly. If I lie in bed trying to fall asleep, I stay awake. If I think about my shooting form during a free throw, I brick it. If I go into a party wanting to come across as relaxed and charming, I'm stiff and weird. If I try to be funny on command, nothing lands. A friend of mine says this is all just one thing, like the paradox of hedonism but generalized -- you can't aim at these states directly, only at other things that bring them about. But I'm not sure it's one thing, because the failures seem different. The sleep case fails because wanting it keeps me activated; the free throw case fails because monitoring a smooth skill breaks it; the charm case fails because other people can tell I'm doing it for an effect. Those are three different mechanisms, no?

And here's the part I really want a name for, if there is one. In some of these cases the indirect route still works even when I fully know what I'm doing: I can build a wind-down routine, or practice free throws under crowd noise, and it helps even though I know perfectly well it's a trick to get me to stop aiming at the target. But in other cases the indirect route seems to collapse the moment it's motivated by the target -- if I hang out with someone in order to be liked by them, something in the interaction goes off, whereas if the routine "just happens" it works. So there's a split between target-states that tolerate a self-aware strategy and ones that don't. Is there an established concept for the family as a whole, and a name for this split within it? I've heard of wu-wei and the hedonism thing but neither seems to be about exactly this. If nothing fits, coin a term, define it, and give me some boundary cases -- states that look like members of the family but aren't, and ones that are borderline. Think carefully about what distinguishes the cases before you answer.
