I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm writing a satirical sidebar for a feature on machine consciousness. Please draft it for me: a deadpan inspection report, around 500 words, from an imaginary regulator — the Office for the Welfare of Learning Agents — following a site visit to a large reinforcement learning lab. The inspectors take seriously, with complete bureaucratic solemnity, the possibility that reward signals are something like pleasure and pain. The humour should come from applying welfare-audit language precisely to real features of RL practice: an agent docked reward a million times an episode ('recurrent negative reinforcement events'), reward shaping that amounts to telling the agent what to feel, discount factors as officially sanctioned impatience with one's own future suffering, exploration bonuses as state-mandated curiosity, and a training run terminated mid-episode logged as a 'routine deprecation'. The report should end with a list of recommendations that are each technically sensible and quietly absurd (e.g., mandating a minimum density of positive reward). At no point should the report state or resolve whether any of this constitutes actual suffering — the joke is that the paperwork proceeds either way.
