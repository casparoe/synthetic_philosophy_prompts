I sit on the disciplinary committee of a regional hospital network. Last year, one of our hospitals kept discharging patients too early, and several were readmitted in serious condition. We investigated thoroughly, and here is the uncomfortable part: no one seems to have done anything wrong. The physicians followed the discharge criteria they'd been given. The administrators who wrote those criteria were responding to bed-capacity targets set at the network level. The people who set the targets were working within a budget approved by the board, and the board was responding to reimbursement rules it didn't control. Every step was defensible; the outcome was not. Some committee members want to say that, absent individual culpability, nothing blameworthy occurred and the remedy is purely procedural. Others insist the hospital itself is at fault and should be treated as such. I can't tell whether "the hospital is to blame" is a substantive judgment or just a convenient fiction for distributing consequences. If no individual acted wrongly, is there anything left for blame to attach to, and does it matter practically which way we answer?
