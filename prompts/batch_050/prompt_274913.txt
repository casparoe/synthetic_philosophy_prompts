At a philosophy reading group last week, I listened to two graduate students argue about what Descartes's cogito actually proves, and I couldn't settle it myself. I would like a point-by-point adjudication of their exchange. It went roughly like this:

(1) Maya opened by saying the cogito establishes the existence of a thinking subject. Doubting that one exists is itself an act of thinking; a thought requires a thinker; so the existence of the thinker is indubitable in a way nothing else is. Whatever else the deceiving-demon scenario undermines, it cannot undermine this.

(2) Dev replied that the cogito establishes at most that a thought is occurring. The thinker, he said, is smuggled in by grammar: from the occurrence of thinking one can no more infer a thing that does the thinking than one can infer, from the fact that it is raining, a thing that does the raining. Descartes is entitled to "thinking is happening," not to "I think."

(3) Maya countered that a free-floating, unowned thought is incoherent. Her present doubt presents itself as hers — thoughts are essentially owned, first-personal episodes — and this givenness is exactly what cannot be doubted. Dev's refusal to posit a subject doesn't dissolve the doubt; it merely declines to describe it accurately.

(4) Dev granted nothing on the subject question but shifted ground: even if a thinker is licensed, the cogito guarantees its existence only for as long as the thinking lasts. It therefore cannot underwrite any claim about a persisting self, and so cannot serve as the bedrock for the larger philosophical project Descartes builds on it. For that matter, a sufficiently powerful deceiver could even induce a false belief that one had been thinking a moment ago.

(5) Maya conceded that the duration worry is genuine but insisted it changes the scope of the cogito, not its success. The cogito, she said, was only ever meant to secure the present moment, and it works as a self-verifying performance rather than a syllogism: the act of doubting enacts the thinking, so there are no premises for Dev's smuggling objection to target.

Please go through the exchange move by move: for each of the five moves, say whether it is clearly right, clearly mistaken, or defensible but open to challenge, and justify your verdict briefly. Then give an overall assessment of who is more right, and point out anything both parties are missing — for instance, whether even a fully successful cogito would establish anything about *what* the thinking thing is, as opposed to *that* it is. Finally, please distinguish carefully between points on which commentators on Descartes broadly agree and points that remain genuinely contested, and do not present any contested interpretation as if it were the settled reading.
