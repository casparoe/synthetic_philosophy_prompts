I just had a long argument with a friend, and I want to see it done properly. Write a dialogue that reconstructs the strongest version of both our positions. My position: I have replaced all the chicken in my diet with pasture-raised beef, reasoning that factory-farmed chickens endure miserable lives and that a single steer yields on the order of a year's meat while a chicken yields a few meals, so my diet now involves far fewer deaths and far less farmed suffering per calorie. My friend grants all of that as far as the farmed animals themselves go, but presses a different ledger: maintaining cattle pasture sustains large populations of wild animals, whereas chicken feed comes from cropland whose plowing and harvesting kill or displace field animals anyway — and, crucially, if the lives of most small wild animals contain more suffering than flourishing, then land use that keeps dense wild populations alive is a cost rather than a benefit, so my pasture-raised steer may impose more total suffering than a feed-crop chicken despite involving one death instead of hundreds. The dialogue must make each side engage the other's actual commitments — lives per calorie, the quality of farmed versus wild lives, deaths under cultivation, and the net valence of wild existence — with real concessions where the other's reasoning is sound, and it should end on a genuine remaining crux rather than a tidy victory for either of us.
