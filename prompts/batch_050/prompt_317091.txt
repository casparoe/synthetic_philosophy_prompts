ok so this is a slightly odd request but bear with me. i'm a phd student in philosophy (moral responsibility stuff, roughly) and our reading group has spent the last three weeks on Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities. for the final session i said i'd bring something fun instead of another handout, so: write me a villanelle about the Frankfurt case.

some constraints, because the form has to do the work and not just be decoration:

- the two refrains should be something in the neighborhood of "he did it all on his own" and "he couldn't have done otherwise" -- the whole point of the case is that both are true at once, and the villanelle's structure, where the two lines keep taking turns and then finally stand side by side in the last stanza, should make that meeting feel like the payoff. the reader should feel the two refrains stop competing
- Black and his device have to be in the poem, but crucially the device never fires. if Black actually intervenes the case collapses -- Jones is only responsible because the counterfactual threat stays counterfactual. so Black should be waiting, watching, poised, and never once needed
- Jones should act from his own reasons. petty shabby reasons are fine, honestly maybe better than noble ones
- don't let it drift into "determinism means nobody's free" territory. the case is about whether responsibility needs alternatives, not about whether everything is caused, and conflating those is the one thing that would make my advisor wince
- it should still work as a poem for someone who's never heard of Frankfurt -- a man who never notices the watcher who never has to lift a finger is a good image on its own. but someone who knows the literature should be able to tell you actually understand the case and aren't just name dropping

if you can get the flicker-of-freedom worry in there somehow (the prior sign, the little tell of what he was about to do) as like a twitch or a shadow, i'd love that, but only if it comes naturally -- dont force it.

19 lines, proper villanelle rules, ABA rhyme and all. thanks!
