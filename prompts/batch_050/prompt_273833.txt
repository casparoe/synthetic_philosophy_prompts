whenever taxes or affirmative action come up, people in my extended family reach for the same picture: society is a footrace. the good jobs and salaries are the medals, and as long as everybody lines up at the same starting line, nobody trips anybody, and the referee is honest, whoever crosses first just deserves to win. taxing the fast to help the slow is like sawing the top step off the podium and gluing the stragglers onto it. my uncle used exactly this last weekend to argue that once you've secured equal opportunity, outcome inequality is basically fine and complaining about it is just envy.

how far does the race analogy actually hold? give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the starting line, the lanes, the training, the finish order, the medal, the referee, the crowd. then tell me where it breaks. some candidate cracks to check: in a race "fastest" is one dimension and measurable to the millisecond, but is "most meritorious" anything like that, and who got to pick the distance we're all running? in a race the rules are fixed before anyone runs, but here last year's winners help design this year's course. in a race, losing costs you a medal; here losing costs you healthcare and where your kids go to school, and your kids don't get a fresh starting line, they start roughly where you finished. also a race is zero-sum with a fixed podium, but an economy isn't obviously like that.

and the part that bugs me most, on the desert point specifically: the fastest runner doesn't *morally deserve* anything — they just best satisfy the rules of a game we invented for entertainment. we don't think last place did anything wrong. so if the economy really were a race, wouldn't the actual conclusion be that winning tracks the rules, not desert — i.e. the analogy might undercut my uncle instead of supporting him?

once the disanalogies are on the table, which ones matter for his anti-redistribution conclusion and which are just pedantic, does the conclusion survive, and is there a better analogy for thinking about what people's contributions actually entitle them to?
