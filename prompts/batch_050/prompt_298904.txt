Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert (1758) argued that theater corrupts a republic: it trades in feigned passions, feeds amour-propre, and turns citizens into passive spectators, whereas open-air participatory festivals, in which the people are their own spectacle, cultivate civic virtue. Suppose he could examine two modern phenomena: (1) binge-watching serialized television drama, including the fan communities that debate, remix, and cosplay it, and (2) mass-participation events such as city marathons, where participants are simultaneously performers and audience, yet the occasion is commercialized, timed, ranked, and broadcast. Predict Rousseau's verdict on each, test whether his participatory/spectatorial criterion actually separates the two cases as cleanly as he would need, and say what he would have to concede or revise in his framework if it does not. Present the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
