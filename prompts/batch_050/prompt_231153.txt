Daniel Dennett's Tower of Generate-and-Test distinguishes four kinds of creature by the selection process responsible for their behavior. Darwinian creatures are wholly hardwired: variation and selection occur across generations, among genotypes, and an individual's responses are fixed at birth. Skinnerian creatures can learn within a lifetime: candidate behaviors are emitted more or less at random and are then reinforced or extinguished by their consequences. Popperian creatures possess an inner environment — a learned model of the world — in which candidate actions can be tried out in imagination before being executed, so that bad hypotheses are discarded without the organism paying for them in reality. Gregorian creatures outfit their inner environments with imported mind-tools — words, labels, diagrams, algorithms, methods of self-interrogation — drawn from their culture, so that the design of thinking itself becomes an object of transmission and improvement.

I take these four levels as settled. What I want is a serious proposal for a fifth kind of creature: a generate-and-test process that is not merely a faster, larger, or more social version of one of the four. Note what does not count as an answer: metacognition is already Gregorian, since mind-tools include tools for examining one's own reasoning; collective problem-solving is Gregorian tooling shared across many heads; richer predictive models are still Popperian selection in an inner environment, however detailed. Your proposed level must specify, as precisely as the four above do: what varies, what does the selecting, in what medium candidate solutions are tested, what pre-existing equipment the process presupposes, and what advantage it yields that the lower levels cannot.

Then assess your own proposal honestly. Could such a process plausibly emerge out of the Gregorian level, the way that level emerged out of the Popperian one? Does any existing system — biological, artificial, or institutional — approximate it, even partially? Or does the proposal, on inspection, collapse back into one of the four levels it was meant to surpass?
