So I ran my reading of Sidgwick past a friend and she said my position "collapses into Parfit's" and I want a second opinion. Here's roughly where I've landed after working through Methods of Ethics:

1. Both rational egoism and universal hedonism rest on self-evident axioms, and neither axiom set can be shown rationally superior to the other. So when they conflict, reason gives a divided verdict -- this is the dualism of practical reason and I think it's real, not an artifact of his setup.

2. I'm a naturalist. No God, no postmortem sanctions, no cosmic coincidence between duty and interest. The dualism can't be patched the way Sidgwick hoped it might be.

3. I still think practical reason must be able to tell me what to do in the cases that matter most -- a theory of reasons that stays silent exactly when the stakes are highest has failed as a theory of reasons.

4. I hold that reasons are objective and not just hypothetical imperatives hooked to whatever desires I happen to have. Someone with purely selfish desires can still be *irrational* in ignoring others.

5. I'm a hedonist about the good, for both individuals and the aggregate.

She claims (1), (2) and (3) can't all stand, and that (4) secretly smuggles in a tiebreaker I've already disavowed. Is she right? Tell me which of these genuinely can't coexist and which pairs only look like they're fighting. Then pick a side: don't just lay out the menu -- tell me which commitment you'd cut, why that one is the cheapest, and what I lose by cutting it. If you think the set is actually fine as-is, argue for that instead.
