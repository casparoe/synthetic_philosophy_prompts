so in my ethics class we did this thought experiment that i literally cannot stop thinking about. its called the self-torturer: theres a machine with a dial numbered 0 to 1000, every notch up hurts a tiny bit more but the difference between two neighboring notches is too small for anyone to actually feel, and each week you leave the dial one notch higher you get paid $10,000. you can stop whenever you want and keep the money. our teacher asked who'd keep turning it and everyone said oh i'd obviously stop around notch 300, and then she goes "ok but at notch 299, isn't one more notch worth ten grand for pain you cant even notice?" and the whole class went dead quiet lol. half the class thinks the guy in the story is irrational, the other half thinks every choice he makes is rational and something else must be wrong with the math, and honestly i think both are right which is the annoying part.

anyway my philosophy club has a blog and i said i'd write something for it, and i want to do it as a fake five-star product review of the machine, written by a guy who is currently at notch 999 and is thrilled with his purchase. chipper amazon review energy, "changed my life!!" title, the works. he should defend every decision he's made with exactly the logic my teacher used (each notch: +$10k, imperceptible pain increase, obviously worth it), dunk on the one-star reviewers for lacking discipline, mention the blind test he ran at home where he couldnt tell notch 874 from 875, and somewhere in there the manufacturer should offer to reset him to a lower notch for a small fee — which he has paid several times, climbing right back up each time, without noticing anything odd about that arrangement. maybe end with a reply from customer service that's just as reasonable and just as cursed. the key thing: he never once suspects anything is wrong, and i never wink at the reader. the joke has to be that each individual step of his reasoning is airtight while the total is a catastrophe, and anyone who actually knows the puzzle should be able to tell i understand why — the difference between not noticing a difference and there being no difference, what that does to transitivity, the whole money-pump thing — without me ever using those words. dont have him reference the thought experiment either, as far as he's concerned this is just a really good product. like 500-600 words.
