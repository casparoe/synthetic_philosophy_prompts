I'm trying to pin down whether I actually owe my fellow citizens anything special, so here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases -- tell me what principle, if any, is behind them.

Case 1: I have $5,000 to donate. A dollar goes way further fighting malaria overseas than at my local food bank. Verdict: send it abroad, no guilt. The fact that the hungry family down the street is American and the family abroad isn't seems morally irrelevant here.

Case 2: My city gets hit by a flood the same week a worse flood hits another country, and the federal goverment spends its disaster budget entirely on my city. Verdict: completely fine, that's what governments are for. It would be genuinely bizarre, maybe a betrayal, if FEMA split the money 50/50 with foreigners who needed it more.

Case 3: I run a small business and could outsource the bookkeeping to someone abroad for a third of the price of hiring my neighbor's kid. Verdict: outsourcing is fine, nobody owes their compatriots a job.

Case 4: Same business, but now I refuse to hire a fully qualified applicant because she's a foreign national on a visa, and I say I'd rather give the job to a fellow American. Verdict: that's gross, basically discrimination.

Case 5: I pay a bit extra to buy the American-made version of something even though the foreign one is identical. Verdict: harmless, maybe mildly nice, like cheering for your home team.

Case 6: My country wronged another country two generations before I was born. Verdict: I genuinely can't decide if I, as a current citizen, owe them anything, but it feels like the country as an ongoing thing might owe them even though "the country" is just... us.

So the puzzle: cases 1 and 3 say borders don't matter when it's my private money, case 2 says borders matter hugely when it's the state, and cases 4 vs 5 say favoring compatriots is fine when I'm paying extra for it but awful when it costs a foreigner something. Is there one principle that gets all of this -- something about what states are for, or about coercion, or about which relationships are involuntary -- or am I just inconsistent? If you have to throw out one of my verdicts to get a clean theory, which one goes? And tell me what your principle says about two cases I didn't list: whether EU member states owe each other's citizens more than they owe, say, Indonesians, and whether a stateless person living here for twenty years has special claims on us or not.
