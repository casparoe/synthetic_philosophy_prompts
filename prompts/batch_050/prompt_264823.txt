I'm a first-year PhD student in philosophy, and after a seminar on formal epistemology this past fall I've become fairly convinced that imprecise probability is where I want to do my dissertation work. The background to this: I came in as a fairly orthodox Bayesian, but I kept being bothered by cases -- the Ellsberg-style setups were the first, and then the phenomenon of dilation, where receiving new evidence can make a rational agent's credal state strictly less informative than it was before -- where the demand that rational belief be representable by a single precise probability function seems to do real violence to the epistemology. My advisor is supportive but agnostic about which corner of the literature I should occupy, and since I'll have roughly three years of funded time and a background in logic and decision theory rather than measure theory, I want to choose deliberately rather than drift toward whatever is most written-about.

So here is my request. What do you take to be the most important open questions in the theory of imprecise probability right now, and which two or three would you actually prioritize for someone in my position? I'm interested in both the philosophical and the formal side, and I have my own rough sense of the candidate terrain: which decision rule for imprecise credal states (Gamma-maximin, E-admissibility, or something else) is defensible, and whether the familiar dynamic inconsistency charges against these rules can be answered; how updating should work and whether the standard conditioning rules for credal sets are well-motivated; whether imprecise credences can be given the same kind of accuracy-based or pragmatic vindication that precise credences enjoy; whether imprecision is the right response to low-quality or conflicting evidence at all, as opposed to, say, higher-order uncertainty over precise probabilities; and how imprecise individual credences interact with aggregation and group belief. But my sense of the terrain is exactly what I want stress-tested, so feel free to tell me I've misidentified what the live questions are or that I've missed the most important one entirely.

Please don't just give me a survey of the area. I want a defended ordering: for each question you prioritize, explain what makes it worth three years of a career -- how many other questions are downstream of it, how tractable it is for someone with my toolkit (as opposed to someone coming from the statistics or engineering side of imprecise probability), how neglected it is relative to its importance, and what considerations or results over the next few years would move it up or down your ranking. If one of these questions is famous but you think progress on it would change little, say so and explain why. Conversely, if there's a less glamorous question that a lot of the field quietly presupposes an answer to, that's exactly the kind of thing I want to hear about. Please write your answer in American English.
