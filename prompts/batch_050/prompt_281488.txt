I'm in eleventh grade, and for our school philosophy club we've each been asked to write down our "position" on a topic so the others can try to poke holes in it. My topic is privacy. Before I present, I'd like a consistency check. Here is what I currently believe:

(1) Privacy is valuable, but only instrumentally. It protects people from concrete harms — blackmail, stalking, discrimination, manipulation, embarrassment that costs them jobs or friendships — and that protection is the whole of its value.

(2) Because of (1), I think the saying "if you have nothing to hide, you have nothing to fear" is basically right: if someone observes me and no harm ever comes of it to anyone, then I haven't been wronged, even if the observation was secret and uninvited.

(3) Freely given consent settles the matter. If I click "agree" on a data-collection policy in exchange for a free app, the company has done nothing wrong no matter how much it ends up learning about me.

(4) Someone who sneaks a look at a stranger's diary purely out of curiosity — and who tells no one, records nothing, and never acts on what they read — has nevertheless done something wrong. The intrusion itself is the wrong, not any consequence of it.

(5) Personal information belongs to the person it is about. That's the underlying reason each of us gets to decide who may pass it on.

(6) At the same time, I don't think my friend wrongs me when she tells people her version of an argument we had, even though the story is very much about me, and I don't think newspapers do anything wrong when they publish true, embarrassing facts about politicians who would prefer those facts stayed quiet.

Please go through these and tell me whether the set hangs together. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and how severe is each one? For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me — i.e., which commitment is doing the damage and what I'd have to accept if I revised it. I'd also like you to identify any pair that only looks inconsistent at first glance but dissolves once you're careful about it, since I don't want to abandon a belief over an illusion. Finally, if the set isn't consistent as a whole, tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems at once. Please rank the tensions rather than listing them all as equally bad — I genuinely don't know where the problem is, and I'm not attached to keeping any particular one of these.
