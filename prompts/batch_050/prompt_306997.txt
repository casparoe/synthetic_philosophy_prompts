I'm a PhD student in normative ethics and I keep running into a puzzle about moral trade -- roughly, cases where two people with different moral views make a deal where each does something the other cares about more, so both sides end up "buying" more of what they morally value than they could alone. The literature mostly asks whether such trades are permissible. I want to push on whether they can be required.

So: build me a case where a moral trade is morally obligatory, not just permissible or nice, for both parties -- in the sense that each would be doing something wrong by declining the deal and just pursuing their own moral priority. Constraints: ordinary, small-scale, no exotic tech or sci-fi; exactly two agents with genuinely different but at-least-somewhat-defensible moral views (no obviously crazy ones, like a sadist); don't just stipulate that the trade "produces more moral value" from some neutral third-person perspective, since that's exactly the move my target would reject -- the obligation has to be intelligible from inside each agent's own moral outlook, not just from a utilitarian god's-eye view; keep the scenario itself to one paragraph.

Then do the verification work: state each agent's moral view, what each would do absent the trade, what the trade is, and walk through why each one's own commitments make declining the trade a wrong rather than merely a missed opportunity. Be precise about which feature of the case does the work of converting "good deal" into "duty" -- is it uncertainty about one's own view, a shared premise both agents accept, a prior commitment, something else? And tell me what happens to the verdict if you remove that feature: does the obligation collapse back into permissibility, and if so what does that suggest about how narrow the phenomenon is? Finally, give me the strongest attack from someone who thinks moral trade can never be obligatory (because moral requirements can't be hostage to bargaining power, or because "your view says I should" isn't a reason for me, or whatever you think the best objection is) and how my case survives or doesn't. Please write the answer in American English.
