I’m a junior on my school’s arts council, and we got stuck with a weird decision that turned into a philosophy fight in the group chat. In 1974 a student named Mara Venn left behind an unfinished piece called North Stairwell: a full piano score for the first minute, then pages of fragments, dynamics, crossed-out themes, and a note saying the ending should feel like doors opening in winter. Our sound-tech club used old rehearsal tapes plus a notation program to make a performable version, and now there are three options: (A) premiere the reconstruction at graduation and call it North Stairwell by Mara Venn, completed by the club; (B) program it as a new student work inspired by Venn’s fragments; or (C) don’t perform it, but upload the tapes and a careful score edition so other people can make their own versions. Half the council says once you change notes it’s literally not the same artwork, like serving someone a different sandwich and calling it lunch, and the other half says musical works aren’t physical objects anyway, so a faithful enough performance can still present the same piece even if no recording is definitive.

Draft the decision memo for the arts council, definately with a clear bottom line we can vote on, and make it wrestle with the type vs performance vs recording mess without name-dropping philosophers or giving a bibliography. Say what counts as the work here: the score-type, the best possible performance, the historical tapes, the fixed audio file, or some family-resemblance cloud of all of them, and what would make the reconstruction a new piece instead of a completion. Include the strongest argument for the side you reject, especially the worry about dead artists not being able to veto bad interpretations, and end with practical conditions the council could actually enforce, like labeling rules for programs, streaming pages, and future arrangements. Format the answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion; put the recommendation in item 1, the key facts/assumptions in item 2, the main reasoning in item 3, the best objection and reply in item 4, and the actionable conditions in item 5.
