So I'm a journalist, I cover science/ideas stuff for a general audience, and I'm working on a feature about whether Occam's razor can actually be *proven* -- like, is "prefer the simpler theory" just a rule of thumb scientists vibe on, or is there a mathematical theorem backing it up. Obviously Solomonoff induction is the thing everyone gestures at here, with the whole universal-prior-over-programs setup.

Here's my problem. The sharpest objection I've come across is that the whole construction depends on the choice of universal Turing machine. The invariance theorem says any two universal machines agree on each string's Kolmogorov complexity only up to an additive constant (roughly the size of the interpreter program translating between them), and for short strings -- which is all we ever actually deal with in real science -- that constant can be bigger than the complexity itself. So "simple" according to machine A can be "complex" according to some perversely gerrymandered machine B that has, say, the standard model of particle physics hard-coded as a one-symbol instruction. The worry is that Occam's razor via Solomonoff is only as objective as your arbitrary choice of machine, which is to say not objective at all.

I want to stress-test this before I write it up, and I keep going back and forth on who's right. So: argue that this machine-dependence objection does NOT actually sink Solomonoff induction as a justification of Occam's razor. Don't just hand-wave "the constant washes out asymptotically" -- I want the real argument, including the uncomfortable parts. A few things you should engage with specifically:

- The asymptotic response (constants get swamped as data grows) and whether it even applies to the finite, one-shot induction problems humans actually face
- The move that says choosing a universal machine is no worse than choosing a prior in ordinary Bayesianism, so the objection proves too much -- and whether the standard reply (that B's machine is just an implausible prior, same as any implausible prior) is satisfying or circular
- Whether restricting to "natural" or "simple" reference machines helps, or just pushes the arbitrariness up a level
- What the gerrymandered-machine case actually shows: does encoding a theory into the machine mean your razor stops doing any work, or does the description length just get relocated rather than eliminated?

Also give the best version of the objection's comeback to each of your points before you answer it -- I dont want a strawman I can't defend to my editor. And end with a verdict on what a careful version of the claim should be: if Solomonoff justifies something weaker than "simplicity is objective," tell me exactly what that weaker claim is.
