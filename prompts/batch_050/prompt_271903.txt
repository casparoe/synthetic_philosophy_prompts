For a high school ethics project on our duties toward wild animals, I have to write down my own position as a set of commitments before my classmates try to pick it apart. Here is roughly where I have landed:

(a) Most wild animals have net-negative lives: the great majority die very young from starvation, disease, exposure, or predation, so their short lives contain far more suffering than enjoyment.
(b) Suffering is bad regardless of whether anyone caused it, so the fact that an animal's suffering is natural does not in itself excuse us from preventing it when we feasibly can.
(c) I support wildlife contraception programs, such as those used to control urban deer populations, because they reduce overpopulation and future starvation without killing anyone.
(d) Nature is intrinsically valuable, not merely instrumentally valuable to us, and this is a strong reason not to deliberately re-engineer wild ecosystems or alter wild animals' dispositions.
(e) I would strongly oppose proposals to redesign predators so that they no longer hunt, or to phase out predation across an ecosystem — that would destroy something irreplaceable.
(f) I support rewilding projects, including the reintroduction of predators like wolves to places they were driven out of, because ecosystems with intact predator–prey dynamics are more whole and more valuable.

Do these hang together as a package?
