The torture-versus-dust-specks dispute has been running for well over a decade in my corner of the internet and, as far as I can tell, every argument on both sides is well known and nobody moves. One side says that sufficiently many dust specks in eyes, spread across a vast population, can outweigh one person being tortured for fifty years, because a small harm multiplied by a large enough number is still a large total. The other side says no finite number suffices, because the harms are of different kinds and aggregation across persons only works within a kind. I've seen all the standard moves: appeals to continuity (there is no sharp line between a speck and torture, just a spectrum of tiny escalations), claims that the anti-aggregation intuition is just scope insensitivity, disaggregation arguments about whether "total harm" is even well-defined across persons, and analogies to the repugnant conclusion. Both sides treat the case as a litmus test and read the other as either monstrous or innumerate. My working hypothesis is that there are actually two distinct disagreements being run as one: one about whether welfare aggregates across persons at all, and one about whether harm is a single scalar quantity that torture and dust specks both measure, so that a speck is just a very small amount of the same stuff. The case conflates them because it pressures both at once. Diagnose the impasse for me rather than adjudicating it: using what each side actually argues, tell me whether this is a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about "harm," two questions being answered under one heading, or a clash of starting intuitions neither side can argue for from anything more basic. Then tell me what, given your diagnosis, would count as progress -- a conceptual distinction, a formal result about aggregation, a stipulated axiom both sides could accept -- or whether nothing could settle it and why.
