I'm a third-year undergraduate taking a seminar on the philosophy of humour, and I'm currently drafting the literature-review section of my term paper. My working taxonomy of the major theories of humour looks like this: (1) superiority theory, associated with Hobbes, on which laughter expresses the laugher's sudden sense of triumph or eminence in comparison with the shortcomings of others; (2) incongruity theory, associated with Kant and Schopenhauer, on which humour arises when an expectation is violated or when two incommensurable frames of reference are yoked together; (3) relief or release theory, associated with Spencer and especially Freud, on which laughter discharges psychic or nervous energy that has been built up or held in check; and (4) the more recent benign violation account, on which something strikes us as funny when it appears simultaneously as a violation of how things ought to be and as somehow acceptable, safe, or not to be taken seriously. My professor remarked in passing that any taxonomy confined to these four is missing at least one genuinely distinct family of views, and I honestly cannot figure out what she means.

Please act as a professional philosopher specializing in aesthetics and the cognitive science of humour, with broad familiarity with both philosophical and psychological work on the subject. Think carefully, and brainstorm several candidates explicitly before settling on one. What I need is a single theory, or family of theories, that is genuinely new relative to my list: it must not be a rewording of one of the four, not a special case of one (merely applying incongruity to puns, for instance, would not count), and not a hybrid that simply stitches two of them together without introducing a distinct explanatory mechanism. State the theory's core claim clearly, explain precisely how its proposed mechanism of humour differs from all four items on my list, and indicate in broad terms which thinkers or research traditions are associated with it — I do not need citations or bibliographic details.

Then evaluate your candidate rather than just presenting it. Consider three test cases I care about for my paper: why a joke typically stops being funny on repetition, why tickling seems to produce laughter only (or at least far more reliably) when someone else does the tickling, and why some violations of expectation are frightening or disgusting rather than amusing. Assess how well your proposed theory handles each case compared with the four theories already on my list, and say frankly whether, in your judgment, it succeeds as a general theory of humour or only as an account of some corner of the phenomenon. Please write your answer in American English, in clear prose of roughly 600 to 800 words.
