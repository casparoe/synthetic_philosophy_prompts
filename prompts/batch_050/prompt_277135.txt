Two friends of mine were going at it over Parfit's teletransporter the other night and neither would budge, so I want a ruling. Roughly how it went:

(1) Sam: stepping into the teletransporter is just dying. The thing that wakes up on Mars is a new person who has your memories, and the fact that it feels like you from the inside doesn't make it you. Proof: if the scanner glitched and left the original standing on Earth, nobody would say there are now two of you. So even in the normal case, where the original is vaporized, the copy isn't you either.

(2) Riley: that's exactly backwards, and it's Parfit's own point. Whether the original on Earth gets destroyed can't change anything about the status of the Mars replica -- same scan, same process, same resulting person. So the branch-line case doesn't show the replica isn't you; it shows identity was never the thing that mattered. What matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, and the replica has 100% of it.

(3) Sam: that move proves too much. If only continuity matters and it comes in degrees, then a copy that loses 10% of your memories in transit is 90% survival, and a robot built in 2300 from your old journals and emails is some small percent survival too. "Survival" collapses into a useless notion. Better to keep a strict bodily criterion and just accept teletransportation is fatal.

(4) Riley: concedes the degree-talk is weird, but says weird isn't false, and fission cases force it on everyone anyway -- if both your hemispheres go into two bodies you can't be identical to both, so Sam's strict criterion doesn't solve those cases, it just refuses to look at them.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and justify each rating briefly, and say if both of them are missing something. Also, be careful to seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on personal identity -- I don't want Parfit's own conclusions presented as if they're the consensus view, since my impression is they aren't.
