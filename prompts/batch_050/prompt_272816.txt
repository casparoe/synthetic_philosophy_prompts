I sit on a citizens' assembly charged with recommending how competences should be divided among the municipal, state, and federal levels of a newly federal country, and I have noticed that my own verdicts follow no rule I can articulate. I would like you to tell me what principle, if any, they add up to.

My verdicts, reported as honestly as I can:

1. Defense and the armed forces belong exclusively to the federation; the very idea of state armies strikes me as absurd.
2. Household waste collection belongs to the municipalities, and I would find even state-level administration of it overcentralized.
3. Pricing greenhouse-gas emissions must be set above the state level, ideally by the federation acting together with its neighbors, because a state that prices carbon alone simply watches its emissions migrate elsewhere.
4. Pollution standards for a river flowing through several states must likewise be set above the states; an upstream state will never adequately protect downstream residents it need not answer to.
5. School curricula should be set by the states, not the federation. I value the variety and the possibility of experimentation, and I hold to this even though graduates move freely between states, so that a state with bad schools exports the costs of its choices.
6. Minimum wages should be set by the states, since living costs differ; I would oppose a uniform federal floor even though states plainly have an incentive to undercut one another to attract employers.
7. Whatever each state decides about whom it will marry or license, every other state should be required to recognize the resulting marriages and licenses. Recognition rules feel federal to me even where the underlying substantive rules do not.
8. In a shared-currency area, monetary policy must be centralized; yet I want deposit insurance for banks to remain with the member states, even though bank panics plainly do not respect state borders.

Here is what I am asking for. First, state a principle, or a minimal set of clauses, that delivers as many of these verdicts as possible. I want a test I could apply to a competence I have not listed, not a label such as "subsidiarity," and for each clause I want you to name the verdict or verdicts that motivate it. I suspect the crux is whatever distinguishes cases 3 and 4 from cases 5 and 6, since all four involve effects that cross borders, and that case 7 splits a single policy area across levels in a way the principle will have to explain.

Second, be honest about which verdicts the principle cannot accommodate. If a noticeably simpler principle becomes available at the price of overturning one verdict, tell me which verdict should go and why. I am least confident of 5 and 8, if that matters to your answer.

Third, apply the principle to four cases I have not decided: zoning rules for housing, data-protection rules for online platforms, the siting of wind turbines, and hygiene inspections of restaurants. For each, give the verdict the principle yields and identify the clause that produces it.
