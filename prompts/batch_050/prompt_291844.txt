So I'm prepping for a reading group on AI alignment and idealized deliberation, and I wrote down my current take on HCH / factored cognition so people can attack it. Before they do, I want to know if my position even makes sense as a package. Here's what I hold:

1. HCH in the idealized limit (unbounded depth, as many copies as needed, no time pressure) is the right way to cash out what I mean by "what I would conclude if I deliberated perfectly and for as long as it took." The output of that process just is my considered judgment on a question, by definition basically.

2. There's no privileged way to factor a hard question. Which subquestions get asked, in what order, at what granularity, and how the answers get stitched back together are all real choices, different reasonable choices give you genuinely different computations, and I don't think there's some clean meta-level rule that picks the right decomposition (that rule would itself need decomposing, and off we go).

3. The demands on the base human are modest. She only needs to be reliable on small, local steps, the kind of thing you can check in fifteen minutes. Mistakes at that level are basically noise, and noise gets corrected by redundancy and depth rather than compounding up the tree.

4. I actually want to use this kind of process to figure out my values. On moral questions where my intuitions conflict or I feel confused, I'd treat the output of a sufficiently amplified version of me as authoritative for what I should care about.

5. I'm an anti-realist about value. There's no mind-independent moral fact out there that the process could be tracking; whatever normative truth there is for me has to be grounded in my attitudes, suitably idealized.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what I'd have to give up. And if there's one single revision that buys the most consistency, tell me which it is. I'd rather find the rot now than in front of the reading group.
