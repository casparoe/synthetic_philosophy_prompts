A friend summarized structural realism to me like this: “Structural realists say mature science gets the world’s relations right, not its intrinsic furniture. When physicists abandoned the ether, they kept much of the old wave mathematics, so what survives scientific revolutions is structure rather than objects. On the strongest version, there aren’t really objects at all, only relations, and a theory’s Ramsey sentence exhausts its factual content. But that means the theory is true as long as *some* collection of things stands in the right relations, so structural realism can make any empirically successful theory true and escapes the pessimistic induction only by becoming unfalsifiable.”

I’m not sure whether this is a fair summary or a disguised objection. Which parts would epistemic vs. ontic structural realists accept? Is the move from “structure is what survives” to “there are no objects” legitimate? And does the Ramsey-sentence point really make structural realism unfalsifiable, or is my friend leaving out how empirical content constrains the structures?
