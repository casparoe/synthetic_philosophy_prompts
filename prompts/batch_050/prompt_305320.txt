For my grad seminar on epistemics of AI I want to set an unusual exercise: drafting the joint statement that would come out of an adversarial collaboration between the two camps on "safety via debate". Camp A (the optimists) hold that training AI systems to win debates in front of a reliable but cognitively limited judge converges on honesty, because the cheapest way to keep winning is to make true claims the judge can check. Camp B (the pessimists) hold that debate instead rewards obfuscation: a debater can defend a false conclusion with a long chain of individually plausible steps, where spotting the hidden flaw would need more compute or expertise than the judge has, so the training signal selects for persuasive bullshit rather than truth. (Define the obfuscated-arguments problem that carefully in the statement itself; don't assume the reader has memorised any particular paper.)

The document should have: (i) a numbered list of claims both sides can actually sign -- about what the debate setup does and doesn't guarantee, and about what the obfuscation worry really establishes; (ii) the residual disagreements, each phrased in words both camps would accept as fair to their own view; (iii) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling them would move at least one side, with for each a test or observation both agree bears on it and each side's prediction recieved in advance. If some of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.

Be committal: where honest drafting forces a choice about what's genuinely agreed or which crux matters most, make the choice and defend it rather than presenting a menu. British English please.
