Write a deadpan government press release, around 550-650 words, from the Ministry of Distributed Discomfort, proudly announcing that the state has abolished torture and replaced it with the Dust Speck Initiative: from now on, whenever interrogators would once have tortured one prisoner, they instead release precisely enough dust specks into the eyes of the citizenry to add up to the same total disutility. The voice should be smug bureaucratic self-congratulation -- press release plus a short FAQ for concerned residents.

The jokes have to come from the actual debate about aggregating tiny harms, not from invented silliness. So work in, without ever naming a philosopher or breaking character: a spokesman patiently walking reporters through why adding one more speck to an already-approved specking couldn't possibly change anything (the Sorites move, delivered as basic arithmetic); an FAQ item explaining that the specks are each personally owned by distinct citizens and therefore there is no single sufferer to complain about; an official dismissing threshold objections by noting that the Ministry has checked and a speck is "well below any threshold anyone has written down"; and a footnote conceding that per capita speck allocation is slightly regressive but that a forthcoming lottery-based pilot will fix distribution. At least one invented citizen complaint letter, answered with total sincerity. A reader who knows the torture-vs-dust-specks argument should be able to point at exactly which moves are being skewered and shouldnt be able to tell whether the author is an aggregative utilitarian having a laugh at themselves or a deontologist filing a protest.
