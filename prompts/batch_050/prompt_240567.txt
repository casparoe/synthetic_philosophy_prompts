A buddy of mine is obsessed with Nomic, Suber's game where changing the rules is itself a move, and he's convinced he's found a problem with any legal system that lets you amend the amendment rules. His argument: (1) Say a constitution's rule R allows the amendment procedures themselves to be changed by some process, like a two-thirds vote. (2) Then that same process can legally replace R with R*, which could say anything -- "no amendments ever again," or "whatever the current ruler decrees is law." (3) The vote adopting R* is fully legal, since it followed R to the letter. (4) So one perfectly legal act can legally erase or hand over the entire constraint structure of the system. (5) But a constraint that can be legally removed whenever the relevant people feel like it isn't really constraining anyone -- it's a speed bump with a delete button. (6) Therefore a fully self-amending constitution constrains nothing; it only looks like it does until someone gets around to ending the game, and the only genuinely stable constitutions are ones with at least one untouchable rule. Which step do I push back on? Explain why that step looks airtight, and what it costs to instead reject one of the others -- I'm especially reluctant to give up (3), since it seems like just what "legal" means. Keep the diagnosis under 400 words.
