I am a novelist working on a book whose narrator is a minor functionary in a Ministry of Abstract Objects, and I need a set piece for one chapter that I will later adapt. I would like you to write it for me: a hearing transcript from the Department of Numerical Identity, in which the number 2 has applied for a certificate of identity and the examiner finds himself unable to issue one.

The target of the satire is the identity problem for mathematical structuralism — the observation, familiar since Benacerraf, that if numbers are nothing but positions in a structure, there appears to be no fact of the matter about which objects they are, so that a question like whether 2 is an element of 4 receives different answers under different constructions (on one standard set-theoretic reduction 4 is the set containing 0, 1, 2, and 3, so 2 is indeed an element of it; on another equally legitimate construction it is not).

The humor must come from playing the philosophy entirely straight inside the bureaucratic form, not from exaggerating anything. The examiner should work through the applicant's candidate identifications one by one — the von Neumann ordinal, the Zermelo construction, the second prime, an equivalence class of pairs, the position that succeeds 1 — and each should fail for a procedurally impeccable reason. The applicant protests that he performs exactly the same work no matter which set he is; the file is then cross-referenced against the integer +2, the rational 2/1, the real number 2.0, and the complex number 2+0i, which raises a jurisdictional question about whether the Department is dealing with one applicant or several. At some point the examiner is asked, as a routine verification item, whether 2 is an element of 4, and the answer is that the Ministry maintains both files and cannot say which is operative. An appeal to the natural number structure itself is denied on the ground that a structure, considered purely as a structure, has no elements, only positions.

Form and voice: a deadpan civil-service interview record of roughly 700 to 800 words, complete with a case number, numbered exchanges, marginal rulings, and a stamped verdict. It should be funny to a reader who knows the problem and the standard structuralist responses — the eliminative and non-eliminative varieties, the claim that positions in patterns are all there is, the ante rem structuralist's insistence that positions are genuine objects in their own right — while remaining legible and amusing to a reader who does not. None of these positions should be named; they should be enacted. The verdict should land the serious point without winking: the applicant is informed that he may continue to function as 2 for all arithmetic purposes while his identity remains under review, indefinitely.
