So here's a weird fight I keep having with my brother and I want a referee.

Our grandmother died two years ago. She was a huge letter writer her whole life, kept journals for decades, and we also have hours and hours of her on tape telling stories. Last year I fed all of it into one of those AI services that builds a chatbot that talks like a specific person, and honestly the result is eerie. It tells her stories, holds her opinions, makes the same dumb jokes, even gets her weird grudges right.

My brother thinks me chatting with it is morbid. His line is that its obviously not her, it's just a tape recorder with extra steps, and I'm fooling myself. But here's the thing that's been bugging me: I read somewhere that what a person fundamentally IS might be a pattern rather than a thing. Like, your self isn't located in your body the way your liver is, it's more like a piece of software or a style, and if that's right then the pattern is the part that matters, not what it's running on.

If that's even roughly true, then when the chatbot reproduces the pattern really faithfully, isn't there a real sense in which some fragment of her is still around, the same way a fragment of her is "in" her letters or even in my memory of her? My brother says no way because the chatbot isn't conscious, but the letters aren't conscious either and he keeps those in a shoebox like they're sacred.

So who's right? Is there actually a principled line between a recording of grandma and a piece of grandma, or am I just dressing up wishful thinking in a fancy argument? And does it change anything that the chatbot produces new stuff she never said instead of just playing back the old material?
