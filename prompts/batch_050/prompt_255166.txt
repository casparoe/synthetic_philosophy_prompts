In my epistemology reading group (grad students plus a couple of annoyingly well-read amateurs) we've been stuck for about six months on the self-sampling vs self-indication fight in anthropic reasoning, and honestly it feels frozen. Quick inventory of where we are: everyone accepts the same test cases -- Sleeping Beauty, the Doomsday argument, and that case where physicists could run an expensive experiment to find out whether the universe contains a trillion observers or a trillion trillion, and the SIA person just reasons from the armchair that it's the big one, no experiment needed, which the SSA camp calls a reductio. The SIA people say SSA makes your credences hostage to whatever "reference class" you pick and there's no principled way to pick one. The SSA people say SIA counts your own existence as evidence for hugely populated universes, which gets cosmology backwards: theories shouldnt get boosted just for predicting more observers. Both sides treat every case as data. Nobody has moved an inch.

My working hypothesis is that this isn't a disagreement about a rule at all. SSA is answering something like "given that I'm a random sample from some class, what should I expect," and SIA is answering "how much does the bare fact that an observer-with-my-evidence exists support a hypothesis," and those are two legitimate questions that happen to share the word probability. But maybe that's too charitable and there's a real disagreement buried underneath.
