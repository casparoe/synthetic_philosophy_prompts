At a historiography workshop two people kept circling the old Needham-flavored question why Britain and not China. Ana said it is fine once you remember every why is contrastive: fix the foil as the Yangzi Delta rather than China, fix a date band, fix the outcome as sustained energy-intensive growth, then hunt for difference-makers like coal, colonies, war finance, and state capacity. Ben said that repair still rigs things: it treats northwest Europe’s weird path as the thing to be explained and China as the case that failed to have it, when the units are not commensurable and the outcome was not sitting in some shared possibility space waiting to be grabbed. Ana answered that without some asymmetry no comparision can even start, and refusing the question just protects a vague holistic story from being tested. Ben replied that the better form is why any region broke through at all, plus separate stories about why particular places did not follow that route; forcing one contrast mistakes a branching tree for a race one runner lost. Referee move by move: which moves are strong, which smuggle something in, and what is the most defensible final form of the question if there is one?
