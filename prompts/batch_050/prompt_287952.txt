My coworker who took one philosphy class in college summarized the two main ones for me over lunch, roughly like this:

"Basically vagueness is just when your measurements aren't precise enough, like if you could measure exactly there'd be no problem in the first place. Supervaluationism says a borderline case — someone who's like 5'10" — is neither tall nor not tall, it just doesn't have a truth value. Something counts as 'supertrue' if it comes out true on every possible way of making the word precise, and anything else is undefined. Then there's epistemicism, which says there's secretly an exact cutoff — like being tall starts at exactly 72.03 inches — we just can't measure it precisely enough to find it. Philosophers pretty much agree epistemicism is obviously crazy because no such cutoff could exist in a word's meaning, and nobody's been able to say where the cutoff would come from, so that view is considered refuted."
