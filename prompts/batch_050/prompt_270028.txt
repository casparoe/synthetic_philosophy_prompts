Our political theory seminar has been stuck for months on what egalitarians should be equalizing, and the argument has stopped generating anything new. The welfarists say that distributing anything other than welfare fetishizes mere stuff over what ultimately matters to people. The resourcists reply that a welfare metric forces the state to subsidize expensive tastes and is defeated by adaptive preferences — the person who has learned to want little scores as well-off. The capability theorists say both sides ignore that people convert resources into decent lives at wildly different rates depending on disability, health, and social context; but when pressed, they cannot say which capabilities belong on the list, or how to weigh one shortfall against another. Every session now rehearses the same three moves. My own hypothesis is that this is not one disagreement but three stacked on top of each other: the sides differ about what egalitarian justice is ultimately for — compensating for bad luck, guaranteeing citizens' standing as equals, or securing a decent minimum — and the metric question is downstream of that, so no argument at the metric level can settle anything. Diagnose the impasse: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or are the three camps answering different questions under a single heading? Take a definite position on what the disagreement really is, support it from what each side actually says, and state what would count as progress on your diagnosis — a distinction that needs drawing, a class of cases each side must answer to, or a change of question — rather than just laying out the options and leaving it to me.
