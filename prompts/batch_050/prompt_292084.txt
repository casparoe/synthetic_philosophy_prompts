I am taking an upper-level epistemology seminar on Wittgenstein’s On Certainty and contemporary hinge epistemology. For a class exercise, construct a thought experiment that isolates hinge status from both subjective confidence and empirical support.

The case must satisfy these conditions:

1. Use a realistic institutional setting, such as a laboratory, hospital, archive, courtroom, or engineering project. Do not use global skepticism, deception by a demon, or fantastical technology.
2. Focus on one rational agent and one proposition, expressed by the same sentence and with the same content throughout.
3. At the first stage, the proposition must function as a local hinge for one inquiry: the agent’s investigation proceeds by holding it fixed, other claims are tested against it, and demanding justification for it would change the inquiry rather than contribute to it.
4. At the second stage, the same proposition must become an ordinary empirical hypothesis that can be investigated, confirmed, or disconfirmed.
5. The transition must result only from a change in the agent’s question or investigative practice. There must be no new evidence, discovery, doubt, reduction in confidence, practical stakes, or reason for suspicion.
6. The agent must remain rational at both stages, and the proposition’s truth-value must not change.

After presenting the scenario in no more than 250 words, provide a compliance check explaining:

- the proposition, the two inquiries, the agent’s evidence, and the agent’s degree of confidence at each stage;
- why the proposition initially counts as a hinge rather than merely as a belief, presupposition, or proposition of which the agent is psychologically certain;
- why it later counts as empirically testable without having been doubted, justified, or falsified in the meantime;
- which feature of the case does the philosophical work, and whether removing that feature collapses the case;
- how someone who thinks hinges are simply very confident beliefs would challenge the example, and how the example could respond without merely stipulating its conclusion.

Do not provide citations or a bibliography; conceptual analysis is sufficient. If the specifications cannot be jointly satisfied without equivocating between a proposition’s content and its epistemic role, say so plainly and identify the conflicting requirements rather than forcing an unsuccessful case.

End with exactly one of these as the final line, with no text afterward: \answer{JOINTLY SATISFIABLE — role-relative hinge} or \answer{NOT JOINTLY SATISFIABLE — conflicting specifications}
