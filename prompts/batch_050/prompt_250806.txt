I run the discussion group for a popular-history reading circle (we are about thirty members, meeting monthly, and I write the sheet of questions we work through each time). Over the past year we have read books on the Great Divergence and on the so-called Needham question, and I have noticed that my discussion sheets keep generating questions of the form "Why did Britain industrialise and not China?" or "Why did Chinese science not produce a scientific revolution when it was ahead for so long?" On my own reading I have come across the idea that a why-question only makes sense against a contrast — that "why X?" is really "why X rather than Y?", and that the answer you get depends on which contrast you quietly smuggle in. I find this idea plausible, but I have no academic training and no way of telling when I am applying it properly.

What I would like is a procedure I can run, in perhaps fifteen minutes per question, on any contrastive historical question before it goes on the sheet: a sequence of tests, in order, each of which can fail the question and send me back to rewrite it. Among other things, the procedure should make me state the contrast explicitly; check that the contrast I have chosen is one that could actually have happened (for instance, whether "China industrialises in the eighteenth century" is a coherent alternative or a disguised impossibility); check that the question does not already presuppose its answer (as "why did China fail?" presupposes failure); and check whether the two cases are even comparable in the respects the question assumes. Please mark the points where no test can decide the matter and I simply have to exercise judgement, and be honest about what the procedure cannot catch.

Then apply the procedure to three questions I have drafted, giving a definite verdict on each — pass, fail, or pass only after a specified rewrite — rather than presenting me with options. The three are: (1) "Why did Britain industrialise before China?" (2) "Why did China not have an industrial revolution?" (3) "Why did the Industrial Revolution happen in Britain rather than in the Yangzi Delta?" I should say in advance that I am fond of question (2), so if the procedure condemns it, say so plainly and do not soften it. Please also commit to a view on the underlying matter: whether "why Britain and not China?" is, as it stands, a well-posed historical question at all, and what the best-posed version of it would be. I would rather have a position I can argue with than a balanced survey. Please write the response in American English.
