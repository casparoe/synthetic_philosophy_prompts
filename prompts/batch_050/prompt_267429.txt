Alright, some context first: I'm a retired biologist, spent 35 years doing cell signaling work, and since retiring I've gotten way into philosophy, mostly through podcasts and the odd paperback. Lately I've been reading about AI alignment and I wrote down what I think I believe about whose values an AI should serve. Before I embarrass myself at my local meetup group, I want someone to check whether these all fit together or whether I'm contradicting myself somewhere.

Here's my list:

1. There's no objective fact of the matter about what's good. Values are preferences, and a preference can't be wrong the way a factual belief can be wrong. If someone genuinely wants to spend their life counting blades of grass, that's their business.

2. An aligned AI should aim to maximize aggregate preference satisfaction across everyone affected by its decisions. Something in the neighborhood of preference utilitarianism, because combining lots of people's values any other way seems arbitrary to me.

3. The AI should stay neutral between people's competing conceptions of the good life, the way a liberal state is supposed to. It shouldn't nudge people toward religion or away from it, toward family life or away from it. It serves values, it doesn't pick them.

4. Interpersonal comparisons of utility are unscientific. There's no unit you can measure my satisfaction and your satisfaction in. I know a measurement problem when I see one, and there's no instrument and no operational definition here. You can compare orderings within a person, but not magnitudes across people.

5. When people disagree about what the AI should do, majority vote is a legitimate way to settle it, since everyone's preference counts as one and nobody's counts as more.

6. People's stated preferences are often a mess -- inconsistent, myopic, formed by advertising and whatever they had for breakfast -- but even so the AI should defer to what people actually want rather than to what some philosopher decides they ought to want.

So: do these hang together? Go through them pairwise where it matters, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which just look like they are until you think about them properly. Rank the real tensions by how severe they are, and for each one tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what I'd be giving up. If there's a single revision that cleans up the most, name it -- but don't assume you know which comittment I'm attached to, because honestly I'm not sure myself.

A couple of ground rules: don't just list every possible disagreement between any two items as if they were all equally fatal; I want a real triage. And please answer in American English -- I read way more easily in it than in the British flavor.
