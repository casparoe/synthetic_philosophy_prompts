I am putting together a seminar on the Browning–Goldhagen debate over the perpetrators of the Holocaust, and I want my students to see that the menu of explanations is larger than the two famous positions. The explanations I already have on my list, and do not need repeated, are these: (1) conformity and peer pressure — the men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 killed largely because refusing meant breaking ranks with comrades and appearing weak or disloyal, as Browning emphasizes; (2) deference to authority — orders from legitimate superiors carry their own momentum, of the sort the Milgram experiments are usually invoked to illustrate; (3) ideological conviction — Goldhagen's thesis that a deep-rooted eliminationist antisemitism made the killers willing, even eager, participants; (4) careerism and self-interest — going along promised advancement or at least protected one from professional and social costs; (5) gradual habituation — killing became easier through incremental escalation and the general brutalization of war, so that each step was small relative to the last; (6) bureaucratic fragmentation — the division of labor meant no individual felt responsible for the whole outcome, since each person performed only one small part.

What I want is one further explanation for why ostensibly ordinary, middle-aged men became mass killers that is not on this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of items already on it. Note in particular that an appeal to sadism or to prior criminal disposition would not count, since it explains away the ordinariness of the perpetrators rather than explaining their conduct. Present the explanation in enough detail that its mechanism is clear, say what evidence or historical detail would tend to confirm or disconfirm it relative to the items on my list, and then give me your honest assessment of whether it is any good — that is, whether it adds real explanatory power beyond conformity, obedience, ideology, careerism, habituation, and fragmentation, or whether on inspection it collapses into one of them.
