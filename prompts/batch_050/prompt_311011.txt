One of them thinks that leaning on AI coding assistants for anything you couldn't write yourself is slowly hollowing out our actual understanding -- like, you stop being able to tell when the output is subtly wrong, and eventually nobody left can hold the whole system in their head. The other thinks this is the same panic people had about compilers and Stack Overflow, and that offloading the routine stuff frees up your brain for the parts that actually matter, and understanding just shifts to a higher level of abstraction instead of disapearing.

They're both smart and they keep talking past each other, so I want to try to get them to do one of those adversarial collaboration things where they produce one document they can both sign. Draft that document for them. It should have the claims they can both agree on (about what current tools can and cant do, about what "understanding your own codebase" even involves, about past cases of skill offloading), then the exact points where they still disagree, each phrased neutrally enough that neither side feels strawmanned, then the cruxes -- the sub-questions where getting an answer would actually move at least one of them.

One thing I'd love included: for each crux, some concrete test or observation both sides would accept as relevant, with each side's prediction written down in advance.
