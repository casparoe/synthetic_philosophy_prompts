Retired physicist here (plasma stuff, nothing fancy) and I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole I can't climb out of. About a year ago a friend lent me a book and in it I ran across the Sleeping Beauty problem -- you know the one, a woman gets put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and if it comes up heads she's woken Monday only, but if tails she's woken Monday and again Tuesday with the memory of the first waking erased, so on any waking she can't tell which day it is. The question is what she should think about the coin when she wakes up. I figured I'd settle it the way I'd settle anything, so I wrote a little Monte Carlo simulation on my laptop, ran a few million trials, and counted how often the coin was heads conditional on her being awake. Came out to a third, nice and clean. Felt smug for about a day.

Then I actually started reading the literature, and apparently this has been fought over for over twenty years now, and my simulation settles nothing, because the other side just says I answered the wrong question. The "halfers" say she learns nothing new on waking -- she knew she'd be woken no matter what, so it stays 1/2 -- and there's even some camp that says 1/2 but changes its mind about the betting odds, which honestly made my head spin. What gets me is that everyone involved clearly understands the setup perfectly, the math is trivial, and yet nobody has budged in two decades. Every argument I find feels like it was already made and rebutted by 2005.

My own hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis since I'm a tourist here: the two sides aren't disagreeing about probability at all, they're answering different questions and calling them all "her credence" -- one side is answering "what does her total evidence support," the other is answering "what odds should she accept" or "what fraction of her awakenings are on heads-days," and these genuinely come apart in cases with memory loss. Or maybe the real fight is deeper, about whether purely indexical information like "it's now Monday" counts as evidence in the first place. So here's what I actually want: a diagnosis of why this debate has stalled. Is it a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two camps solving different problems under one label? And given your diagnosis, what would even count as progress -- a new distinction, some formal result, or should we just accept that "what should she believe" is ambiguous and split the question? Please answer in American English, and spare me the full bibliography, I mostly want the carving.
