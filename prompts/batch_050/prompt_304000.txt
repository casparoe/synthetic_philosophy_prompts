For the past decade I've been an avid amateur coffee taster -- I roast my own beans, keep tasting notebooks, the whole thing. Lately something has unsettled me, and a disagreement with two friends has made it worse. When I first got into coffee, I loved the bright, acidic Ethiopian roasts; now I find that same style harsh and thin, and I much prefer darker, chocolatey roasts. When I told my friends this, they split on how to describe what happened. One says: the coffee tastes different to you now -- what it's like to sip an Ethiopian roast has genuinely changed for you, the way a room's color changes when you swap the lightbulb. The other says: no, the raw taste sensation is presumably much the same as it ever was; what changed is you -- your standards, your expectations, the background against which you judge the cup. The first calls the second's picture mystical; the second calls the first's picture naive.

Here's what I can't figure out, and where I'd like your help. Is there actually a fact of the matter about which of my friends is right? At first I assumed there obviously must be -- surely I, of all people, can just introspect and tell whether the taste itself changed or only my reaction to it did. But the longer I sit with it, the more slippery that seems. When I try to "check," I can only ever compare my current tasting against my memory of past tastings, and my memories are precisely the things filtered through my past judgments -- the very things my second friend says have shifted. It starts to feel like there's no vantage point from which I could settle the question, which makes me wonder whether the question itself was confused, or at least whether the two descriptions might amount to the same thing said two ways.

This isn't just idle curiosity, because a practical decision hangs on it. I'm considering spending a significant sum on a professional tasting course. If my first friend is right and my palate's experiences themselves have drifted, the course seems like a reasonable way to retrain or re-explore. If my second friend is right and only my evaluative framework changed, then the money might be better spent examining why my standards shifted than on more cups. And if there's really no answerable question here at all, I suspect that has implications for how I should think about my tasting notebooks -- ten years of records I've treated as a chronicle of one stable subject's experiences. So: which of my friends has the better account, or is the dispute between them somehow ill-posed, and what follows for my decision and my notebooks?
