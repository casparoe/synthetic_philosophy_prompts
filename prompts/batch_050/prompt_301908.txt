Yesterday I spent four hours arguing with a friend about whether the pleasure of a hot bath and the pleasure of finally understanding a proof have anything in common, and we kept going in circles because neither of us could say what an answer would even have to look like. So I want the specification first, before any theory: what must an account of pleasure do to count as an adequate response to the heterogeneity problem? Give me the requirements list — the range of cases it has to cover (sensory, aesthetic, intellectual, relief-from-pain, pleasures taken in things versus bare feelings), the distinctions it cannot collapse (pleasure as a feeling versus as an attitude toward a feeling, pleasure versus its causes, being pleased versus wanting), and the constraints it must respect from introspection and empirical work — with the case or consideration motivating each requirement. Mark which requirements strike you as genuine desiderata and which are just the prejudices of one camp, and say whether any subset of them looks jointly unsatisfiable, since if the demands on a unified theory cannot all be met at once, that fact is itself the interesting result: pleasure may simply not be one thing. Finally, tell me which single requirement eliminates the most existing contenders, and which contender comes closest to surviving the whole list.
