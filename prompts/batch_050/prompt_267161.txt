A guy I know through a mutual friend — wealthy, kind of intense, the type who loves testing people — made me an offer last week and I can't stop turning it over in my head. He said he'll pay off my student loans, the whole $40k, if I can make myself genuinely admire him. Not pretend to admire him — apparently he's weirdly good at spotting fakes, so it only counts if the feeling is real. He'll "know," he says.

Here's the thing though: the guy has done nothing admirable. He's smug, he's rude to waiters, he name-drops constantly. But $40k is $40k, and the money sure feels like a massive reason to want to feel the admiration. So I sat down and actually tried to summon it, and... nothing. It's bizarre — it's like the reason the money gives me points at me and my bank account, not at him. Meanwhile I think about my high school track coach who built the whole program out of nothing and treated every kid like they mattered, and the admiration just shows up for free, no deal required.

My roommate says I'm just being irrational — that if I have a reason to want X, and feeling the admiration gets me X, then I have a reason to feel it, full stop, and I'm failing to respond to a reason. But something about that feels off and I can't articulate why.

So my questions: is the money actually a reason to admire him, and if so why can't I feel it even though I clearly "have" the reason? If it isn't, then what is it a reason for, exactly? And practically — should I keep trying? What would I even do?
