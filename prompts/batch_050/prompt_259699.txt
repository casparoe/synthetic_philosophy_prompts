Hey, so I cover science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, and I'm drafting a personal essay about whether individual choices matter for huge collective problems. Before I file it I want to stress-test my own positions, because my editor loves finding holes in them. Could you put on your moral philosopher hat for this — someone who knows the inefficacy / collective harm literature inside out — and audit my commitments for consistency? Take your time, think carefully, and maybe brainstorm the pairwise tensions first before settling on your verdicts.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. On the empirical side I basically accept the inefficacy objection for supermarket meat: any given chicken I buy or skip almost certainly changes nothing, because producers respond to big demand thresholds, not to one shopper.
2. Still, I think I ought to be vegetarian anyway. There's some small chance my purchase is the one that tips a threshold, and multiplied by the sheer number of animals affected, the expected value does the work even when actual difference-making doesn't.
3. Voting is different, though. I don't buy the expected-value case there — the odds my vote decides anything are astronomical. When I vote it's civic duty and self-expression, not consequences. If someone skipped voting and spent the time earning money to give to a good charity instead, I wouldn't say they did less good.
4. I fly a few times a year for work and I buy carbon offsets, and as far as I'm concerned that discharges whatever responsiblity I have for those flights. Box ticked.
5. I'm pretty down on consumer-blaming in general. The climate crisis is on fossil fuel companies and the governments that let them off the hook, and harping on individual footprints is a distraction the industry actively encourages.
6. When a harm is overdetermined — say fifty poachers each shoot at the last rhino when any ten bullets would've been lethal — I think every single one of them is blameworthy for the death, even the ones whose bullet changed nothing.

What I want from you: which of these pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only look like they are but dissolve once you think about it? Rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each one tell me the cheapest revision that fixes it and what that revision costs me. And if there's a single change that cleans up the most of the set at once, flag it. Don't be nice about it — I'd rather get the bruises from you than in the comments section.
