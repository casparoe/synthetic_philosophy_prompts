He pulled up a passage on his phone from Garrett Hardin's essay "Lifeboat Ethics: the Case Against Helping the Poor" (Psychology Today, 1974), which he apparently read in college and has been waiting his whole life to deploy at someone:

Some say they feel guilty about their good luck. My reply is simple: "Get out and yield your place to others." This may solve the problem of the guilt-ridden person's conscience, but it does not change the ethics of the lifeboat. The needy person to whom the guilt-ridden person yields his place will not himself feel guilty about his good luck. If he did, he would not climb aboard. The net result of conscience-stricken people giving up their unjustly held seats is the elimination of that sort of conscience from the lifeboat.

Ben's reading: me giving up the apartment fixes nothing structurally, it just takes one more person who feels bad about unfairness out of a position where that guilt could matter, so guilty people yielding their seats is literally self-eliminating and therefore pointless. I think he's twisting the passage — Hardin is talking about nations and populations, not one guy and an apartment, and "it does not change the ethics of the lifeboat" is not the same claim as "yielding your place is wrong or irrational."
