I am a retired geophysicist. For four decades my work consisted of inverse problems: given seismic travel times, gravity anomalies, and electromagnetic soundings recorded at or near the surface, infer the structure of the Earth's interior. I therefore have a working, almost bodily familiarity with non-uniqueness, null spaces, reparametrization invariance, regularization, and the distinction between what the data actually constrain and what only our descriptive conventions do. Assume full fluency in that vocabulary and only a beginner's fluency in philosophy.

In retirement I have taken up philosophy of physics, and I keep running into the hole argument and the long dispute it generated over whether spacetime points exist over and above the fields and material relations defined on them. My strong suspicion is that I already understand this debate in my own terms, and I want that suspicion tested rather than gratified.

Please restate the hole argument in the vocabulary of inverse theory: forward maps, ill-posedness, parameter identifiability, quotienting a model space by a symmetry group, and the like. Cover, specifically: the construction in which two models agree everywhere outside a matter-free region yet differ inside it; the claim that this yields a failure of determinism, in that data on an initial slice do not fix what holds inside the region; and the principal responses, including the view that such models are merely two descriptions of a single physical state, the relationalist rejection of independently existing points, and any position that tries to keep point identity while dissolving the determinism worry. For each move, state precisely which piece of inverse-problem machinery it corresponds to.

Then give me the residue report: which parts of the philosophers' dispute survive translation intact, which survive only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary. In particular, when a metaphysician insists that two models related by a relabeling of points represent genuinely distinct possibilities, is there anything in inverse theory that can even express that insistence, or does the framework render it literally unsayable? Close with your verdict on the residue: does it show that my framework is blind to something the metaphysicians can see, or that they have been arguing about an artifact of redundant description?
