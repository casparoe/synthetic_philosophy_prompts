I sit on the governance committee of a national professional association (about 40,000 members, 9-seat board elected every year). Turnout has slid to around 6%, and at last month's meeting three factions emerged and now we have to pick a direction before the AGM:

1. Keep things as they are. Low turnout is a non-problem; engaged members vote, disengaged ones abstain, both are fine.
2. Ditch elections and fill the board by lottery from a vetted pool of qualified volunteers (sortition). The argument: at 40,000 members the chance that any one vote decides anything is essentially zero, so voting is instrumentally irrational anyway, turnout will never recover, and we'd get better demographic representation than an electorate of 2,400 cranks (sorry, "highly engaged members") produces.
3. Keep elections but add a participation floor: results only count if turnout clears 8%, otherwise the sitting board stays on for another year. The idea is to make legitimacy mean something.

Option 3 is the one I want the memo to take seriously, because I think it's sneakier than it looks: under a quorum rule, abstaining stops being neutral and becomes a de facto vote for the incumbents, and an organized minority suddenly has a new tactic -- win not by turning out your side but by depressing turnout overall, e.g. by campaigning that the election is a sham. But maybe that's a feature (it forces boards to be popular enough to survive a boycott) rather than a bug. I genuinely can't tell.

Draft the committee's decision memo: recommend one of the three options (or a modification of one), with reasons the losing factions could actually engage with. Please grapple with the underlying puzzle rather than hand-waving it -- if individual votes are basically never pivotal, what exactly is an election for, and does the answer to that question settle which option wins, or not? Also give the strongest honest case for the faction that loses.

Format: open the memo with the recommendation in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, no restating the situation back to me.
