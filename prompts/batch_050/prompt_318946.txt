I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a long feature about how famines get attributed -- why some are remembered as natural catastrophes and others as atrocities. My working thesis, and the frame I've provisionally built the piece around, follows the entitlement approach associated with Amartya Sen's work on the 1943 Bengal famine: famines are typically failures of access, distribution, and political accountability rather than shortfalls in the aggregate food supply. On this view, the Bengal famine happened amid adequate food availability because wartime inflation and speculation priced rural laborers out of the market; Ireland exported food while people starved because the entitlements of the poor had collapsed; and the famine after the Great Leap Forward was produced by procurement targets, falsified harvest reports, and the suppression of information -- a category of disaster that, the argument goes, a functioning democracy with a free press does not permit. I find this thesis persuasive, and that worries me, because a persuasive frame is exactly the kind a journalist should interrogate hardest before publishing under it.

So: what would count against it? I'd like a map of the thesis's exposure, not a summary of the debate. Which historical findings would genuinely damage it -- for instance, would a credible revision of the Bengal food-supply estimates showing a real shortfall be decisive, or would defenders absorb it? Does the fact that a large share of famine mortality historically comes from epidemic disease rather than literal starvation support the entitlement view, complicate it, or undercut it? Are there documented cases of serious famines under conditions of press freedom and electoral accountability that would falsify the democracy claim, and would proponents treat those as counterexamples or redefine their terms around them? I want the candidate defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest statement of which ones Sen's own defenders would concede as counting against the view and which only critics treat as damaging. Finally, tell me plainly whether you think the entitlement thesis has been qualified into something close to unfalsifiable -- whether 'entitlement failure' has been stretched to cover any famine whatsoever, so that the political-crime framing wins by definition -- and whether, for a thesis of this kind, that degree of insulation is a defect or just the sign of a good explanation. In your answer, say how confident you are in each part of the assessment, and single out the one point where you are least sure of your ground.
