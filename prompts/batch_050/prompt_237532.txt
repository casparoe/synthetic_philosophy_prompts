So here's something that's been nagging me. I teach the applied ethics survey here, and every spring we spend two weeks on the doctrine of double effect -- intend vs. foresee, terror bombing vs. strategic bombing, the closeness problem, the loop case, all of it. The literature on this has been grinding away for fifty-plus years and I honestly can't point to where it has moved. Case verdicts are basically stable (people's intuitions about the standard trolleys are remarkably consistent, my students included), but nobody can state the principle in a way that survives contact with the counterexamples, and the repair attempts just generate more counterexamples. It feels stalled in a structural way, not in a we-haven't-found-the-right-paper-yet way.

Now the part that made me suspicious. While prepping I went back to Aquinas, since everyone ritually cites II-II q.64 as the origin of the doctrine -- specifically the self-defense article. But a few articles earlier, in the article on whether it's lawful to kill sinners, he writes:

"if a man be dangerous and infectious to the community, on account of some sin, it is praiseworthy and advantageous that he be killed in order to safeguard the common good, since 'a little leaven corrupteth the whole lump'"

And right before that he explicitly analogizes from cutting off a decayed body part to save the whole body. So the same thinker we credit with the constraint against using one person as a means reasons, a couple articles over, in exactly the part-for-the-whole way that the fat man case is supposed to be the reductio of.

My working hypothesis: the modern debate inherited the intend/foresee machinery but quietly threw away the metaphysics that was doing the real work -- the idea that individuals stand to the community as parts to a whole. What's left is an intention distinction asked to bear weight it can't bear, which might be why the debate can't converge: the sides aren't disagreeing about cases, they're disagreeing about whether individual persons are the kind of thing that can be "excised" for a greater good, and neither side will say so out loud.

So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on double effect itself. Is the stall really about (a) whether intend/foresee marks a moral difference, (b) the buried part/whole vs. separateness-of-persons question I've gestured at, or (c) something else -- a verbal dispute about "intention," bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for, a question that's just ill-posed? Defend your pick against the alternatives. And given your diagnosis, tell me concretely what would count as progress -- a distinction, an empirical result about how people attribute intent, a formal impossibility result, a change of question -- or argue that nothing would move it and explain why a debate can be both substantive and unresolvable. If you think my Aquinas reading is doing too much work, push back on that too.
