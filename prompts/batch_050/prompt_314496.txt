So I run a small methods-review team at a nonprofit that vets social science preprints before our funders will cite them, and I keep running into a pattern I dont have good language for. A paper will validate a measure using the measure itself -- like a new political-engagement survey whose "validation" is that it agrees with respondents' answers on other items from the same survey, or a lab that calibrates its spectrometer against readings taken with that very spectrometer and then reports the calibration as evidence the spectrometer works. My epistemologist friend says this is basically the bootstrapping problem / easy knowledge, and pointed me at the classic cases (checking the gas gauge by looking at the gas gauge, concluding the lights are normal because the table looks red) -- but I need something my reviewers can actually *use*, not a philosophy seminar.

Can you turn the bootstrapping problem into a screening procedure? I want a checklist or short flowchart-in-words that a smart-but-not-philosophically-trained reviewer can run in about twenty minutes per paper, that flags when a reliability claim is being supported (even partly) by the very source whose reliability is in question. Crucially it has to distinguish the bad circular cases from legitimate lookalikes: genuine independent corroboration, calibration against an external gold standard, coherence checks where consistency is merely a necessary-not-sufficient condition, and track-record arguments. For each step say what the reviewer should ask, what answers pass, and what to write in the report when it's borderline -- because a lot of these are borderline, and I'd rather the procedure say "judgment call here" than pretend otherwise.

Please include three worked applications: (1) the self-validating engagement survey above, (2) a fMRI preprocessing pipeline "validated" by showing it recovers known activations on the same dataset used to tune its parameters, and (3) cross-validation in machine learning -- which my gut says is fine but I suspect your procedure will at least partially flag, and I want to see it give an answer I don't like if that's where it leads. Also tell me where the procedure will misfire: cases it will over-flag or under-flag, and what it fundamentally can't decide for us (e.g., whether partial independence between instruments is "enough").
