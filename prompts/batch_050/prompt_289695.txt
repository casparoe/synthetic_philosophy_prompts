I'm a statistician who works in causal inference, and my working vocabulary is directed acyclic graphs, do-operators, interventions, screening off, and levels of abstraction over a common micro-variable set. Restate Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument against non-reductive physicalism — the supervenience of the mental on the physical, the causal closure of the physical domain, and the exclusion principle that an effect cannot have two independent sufficient causes — as faithfully as you can in that vocabulary. In particular, model mental and physical properties as variables at different levels of description of the same system, and ask what an intervention on a mental variable could even mean when its physical realizers are also on the graph: is the exclusion argument, on this rendering, just the observation that the physical level screens off the mental one? Then give me the residue report: which premises translate cleanly, which survive only under distortion (I suspect closure and supervenience are the trouble spots), and which have no interventional expression at all. Finally, take a stand: does the untranslatable residue mark something real that causal graphs cannot express — say, a notion of causation as production rather than difference-making — or does it show that the exclusion argument leans on a picture of causation the interventional framework correctly refuses to recognize?
