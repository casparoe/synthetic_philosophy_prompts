His calls: without Newton, calculus and classical mechanics show up within a generation anyway -- Leibniz got calculus independently, so that's easy. Without Gutenberg, movable type gets invented within decades, the pieces were all lying around. Without Fleming, penicillin is found within a decade or two, somebody else eventually notices the moldy dish. Without Columbus, Europeans are landing in the Americas inside of fifty years regardless. But then he flipped: without Napoleon, France still gets a strongman -- the Directory was a shambles and some general was going to stage that coup, though maybe the conquests end up smaller. Yet without Genghis Khan, no Mongol empire at all, full stop -- he was adamant the steppe tribes just stay fragmented without that one man. And without Muhammad, no Islam and no unification of Arabia, because the doctrine was too bound up with the person to have emerged any other way.

So naturally I asked him the big one: take Hitler out (say he dies in the trenches in 1918) -- does something recognizably like Nazi Germany still happen? Also tell me the single additional counterfactual I could spring on him next time we get drinks that would best discriminate between your candidate rules. I'm honestly less interested in the right answer to the history than in reverse-engineering whatever principle he's running.
