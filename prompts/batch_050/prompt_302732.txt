I was recently elected to our local school board, and within weeks I've landed in the middle of a dispute over whether a proposed elective unit on "alternative accounts of origins" belongs in the science curriculum. The board's curriculum committee keeps invoking the idea that what makes something science is falsifiability, so for the community radio program I host, I've invited the author of a new book defending a sophisticated, updated falsificationism as the solution to the demarcation problem. She argues that falsifiability, properly formulated, still draws the line where we need it.

Draft a sequence of nine to eleven interview questions that genuinely tests the position rather than letting her restate it. I want the sequence to build pressure in roughly this order: begin with the Duhem-Quine point that any theory can be insulated from a failed prediction by adjusting auxiliary hypotheses, and press on whether her criterion can tell the difference between legitimate revision and ad hoc rescue without circularity; move to Lakatos's charge that mature science routinely proceeds by protecting a hard core, so that strict falsificationism would have ruled out theories we now regard as paradigms; then to Laudan's worry that demarcation criteria have historically been both too permissive and too restrictive, and that the interesting question is whether claims are well-tested, not whether they sit on one side of a line; and end with the reflexive question of whether her demarcation criterion is itself falsifiable, and what would count as evidence against it.

For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, so I am not left without a move when she answers. Finally, include two questions that bring the abstract debate down to my actual situation: one asking what her view implies for the specific curriculum dispute I describe, and one asking what a school board should do if her criterion gives a verdict that conflicts with the consensus of working scientists -- which authority should yield, and why.
