write me a deadpan op-ed, around 600 words, for a mid-tier sunday newspaper, by the founder of a small movement of people who have taken the inefficacy objection completely to heart. his creed: since one vote never swings an election, one burger never adds a slaughtered cow, and one skipped flight never measurably warms the planet, a rational person simply stops doing anything whose impact falls short of one whole unit of outcome. have him apply the principle cheerfully and consistently across his entire life -- voting, recycling, tipping at restaurants, holding doors, rinsing his dishes before the dishwasher, apologizing -- and make the piece work as a reductio: he never once notices thresholds, lumpy supply chains, or what happens when his principle gets generalized, but a careful reader should be able to reconstruct exactly which premise he's leaning on at each step and where it cracks. keep the irony legible without him ever winking, and don't let him soften his view halfway through unless the crack in it is itself the punchline.
