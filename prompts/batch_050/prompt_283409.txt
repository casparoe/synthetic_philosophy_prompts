What I want to avoid is the question everyone writes, where they just explain that reactive attitudes exist and that Strawson thinks determinism wouldn't change them, then gesture at the objective attitude. So here's the angle I'm after: I want the question to force them to work out what Strawson can and can't say about *criticizing* a community's pattern of reactive attitudes. Setup I'm imagining: present a society where resentment and indignation are distributed along some hierarchy (say, the lower caste is routinely viewed with the objective attitude, treated as something to be managed rather than answered to, and nobody thinks anything is missing). The question asks: does Strawson's framework give us resources to call this distribution *wrong*, or only strange? Given that he denies the practice of holding responsible needs or admits of external justification, can he even get the criticism off the ground, or does the objection show a hole in the account?

2. A model answer of roughly 500-600 words.
3. A marking scheme out of 20, built around the specific moves an A answer has to make, with a note for each on what the B-level version of that move looks like (i.e. the thing students will actually write instead).
