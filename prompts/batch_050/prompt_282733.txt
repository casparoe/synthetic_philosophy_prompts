I am preparing the opening page of a seminar packet on the Meno problem and want a poem that makes students feel the puzzle before they encounter the terminology. Write an original Petrarchan sonnet in ABBAABBA CDECDE, using roughly iambic pentameter and one sustained image involving travel, navigation, or finding a way. In the octave, present two people who hold the same true belief and therefore act with equal success: one understands why it is true, while the other has merely happened upon it through luck, guesswork, or an unreliable source. Do not let the octave cheat by making the lucky belief less useful; its practical equivalence is precisely what creates the problem.

At the volta in line nine, introduce a challenge that reveals the difference: a demand for reasons, a change in circumstances, or the possibility that the same route might have led somewhere false. The sestet should locate knowledge’s advantage in the non-accidental and relatively stable relation among believer, belief, evidence, and truth, without suggesting that knowledge is certainty, that true belief is worthless, or that every reliably produced belief automatically counts as knowledge. The final three lines should complicate the tether metaphor rather than simply celebrate it: being tied down can preserve a belief, but only if what holds it also answers to the truth. Do not mention Plato, Meno, or Gettier by name, and begin with the poem itself, with no preface, explanation, or commentary.
