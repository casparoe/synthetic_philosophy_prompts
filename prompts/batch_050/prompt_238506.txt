I'm in my university's effective altruism society and last week I got walked into a corner during our usual veganism debate, and I still can't find the exit. Someone brought up the point (apparently there's a real paper along these lines -- Davis, I think? I haven't read it) that crop farming kills huge numbers of wild animals. I went home and tried to build the strongest version of the argument I could, and the problem is that it now seems to prove way too much. Here it is:

(1) Holding health, cost, environment etc. fixed: if two diets feed the same person equally well, the morally better diet is the one that results in fewer animal deaths.
(2) Growing and harvesting crops kills enormous numbers of wild animals per calorie -- mice, voles, rabbits, fawns get shredded by combine harvesters, and that's before you count deliberate pest control.
(3) One grass-fed steer, by contrast, yields something like half a million calories, a good chunk of a person-year of eating, and a life on pasture kills essentially nothing besides the steer itself.
(4) So per calorie, grass-fed beef results in fewer animal deaths than a vegan diet of grains and beans.
(5) So by (1), grass-fed beef is the morally better choice and veganism is actually worse for animals. Annoying, but I can update.
(6) Except the machinery doesn't stop there. The principle behind (1) can't be diet-specific -- if the death count is what makes one diet better, it must make one food system, or one land-use policy, better than another. So one whale (millions of calories, one death) beats the steer, and we should resume whaling and generally eat the largest animals available.
(7) Worse, wild animals die in nature constantly and horribly, at rates per acre far above anything a pasture or even a car park produces -- starvation, predation, parasites, winter. So the same reasoning says that converting rich wildlife habitat into low-diversity cattle pasture, or paving it outright, reduces the total number of animal deaths and is therefore morally better.
(8) But (7) is absurd. So something above is broken, and I can't see where.

The vegans in the society respond by quibbling with the empirical numbers in (2) to (4). For the sake of arguement I want to grant all of that -- assume the per-calorie body counts are exactly as stated -- and find the structural problem instead. Two wrinkles. First, the friend who cornered me claims (7) isn't even absurd: people who work on wild animal suffering apparently think a lot of wild lives contain more misery than happiness, and some of them would actively welcome less habitat, so my reductio might just be a bullet other people happily bite. If the argument is actually valid all the way down, I'd rather be told that than be reassured. Second, if something has to go, I'd most like to keep some version of (1), because it really seems like the number of deaths has to matter at least somewhat -- but I want to know what keeping it costs, and what the other exits (attacking (2)-(4) on non-empirical grounds, or blocking the move from (5) to (6) and (7)) each cost too.

So: tell me which step you'd reject, explain why that step looked fine to begin with, and price the alternatives. And be honest about how confident you are in the diagnosis -- single out the part of your own answer you're least sure of.
