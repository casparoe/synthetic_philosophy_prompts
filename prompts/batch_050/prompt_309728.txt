So a bit of context: I never studied philosophy or physics or anything, I work in a warehouse, but a couple years ago I got hooked on Hofstadter's stuff about the careenium — that made-up two-level brain model where little marbles (the "simmballs") rattle around on their own and the big symbols are supposedly just patterns of marble-motion, and the symbols are supposed to "do the thinking" even though everything is really just marbles. I couldn't stop thinking about it, and I actually built a dumb version at home: a tilted board with nails and gates where marbles do binary addition (like those Turing Tumble toys but uglier). Watching it, I got convinced of something I know is probably heretical: that when you describe the machine at the level of "this gate is storing the carry bit," the carry bit genuinely pushes the marbles around, not just vice versa. Like, the symbol-level fact that a carry is pending is what makes certain marble paths happen, and no purely marble-level story is really complete — the marbles are just the messy way the symbol gets itself implemented. I call this "steering from above" and I'm presenting it at our local philosophy/science meetup next saturday.

Problem: the audience scares me. There's two physics grad students who are very into "causal closure of the physical" talk, one retired programmer who thinks all downward causation talk is woo and loves the word "supervenience" (I think he's using it to mean my view is trivially true and therefore boring, which confuses me), a woman who does fMRI research and keeps bringing up Libet experiments whenever anything about minds comes up, and then maybe eight friendly regulars who'll just nod. The meetup format is 20 min talk then open Q&A which can get spicy.

Prep me for the Q&A. I want: the objections I'll actually get from THIS crowd (not generic internet ones), roughly in the order they'll come and phrased the way these specific people would phrase them, ranked by how much damage each does to "steering from above." For each one tell me what I should just concede up front (I suspect I have to give ground somewhere on whether symbols are "extra" stuff), what I should hold firm on, a short reply I can deliver in under two minutes without jargon I can't back up, and where my reply is weakest. Also: my marble-adder is my centerpiece example — tell me the specific ways they'll attack the move from the toy machine to actual brains, since that's where I feel shakiest. And include the question I'm praying nobody asks, because I honestly don't have an answer to it yet.
