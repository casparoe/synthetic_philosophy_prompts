i'm a journalist who covers politics and ideas, and hypocrisy call-outs are basically half my feed at this point. every scandal cycle goes the same way: someone gets blamed, their defenders point out the blamers did something similar, and then the whole conversation shifts to who has the right to say anything instead of whether the thing was actually wrong. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the two camps i keep seeing: the "standing" camp, who think a hypocritical or complicit person forfeits the standing to blame (blame being something you need a kind of moral license for, not just a true utterance), and the "charge stands or falls on its own" camp, who think standing-talk is a dressed-up ad hominem and a criticism is true or false regardless of who utters it. Give me: (1) the claims both sides can actually sign, about what blame is for and about consistency being a genuine virtue even if tu quoque doesn't touch the truth of the original charge; (2) the residual disagreements, worded so each camp would accept the formulation of its own position; (3) the cruxes -- my guess is one of them is whether blame is more like testimony (speaker-independent content) or more like issuing a sentence (speaker-dependent authority); and (4) for each crux a concrete case both sides agree bears on it (the whistleblower with the same skeletons in her closet, the parent who smokes telling their kid never to start), with each side's prediction recorded in advance. if part of this dispute turns out to be verbal, flag which part and why.
