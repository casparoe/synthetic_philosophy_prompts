So I run a small philosophy reading group (we meet at a pub, it's very informal) and last week we got into higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia, and I keep getting stuck on what seems like it should be a simple practical question. As I understand it, epistemic akrasia is the state of believing something while also believing that you shouldn't believe it -- like believing your flight is definitely on time while simultaneously believing that your evidence (the departure board flashing delayed, the app) makes that belief unreasonable. Everyone in the group agreed this state is irrational, fine. But then it seemed to me the fix is obvious: when you notice you're in that state, you just drop the belief, or at least dial your confidence down to whatever your higher-order judgment says is warranted. Problem solved, no?

Except when I pushed on this, nobody could explain how you're actually supposed to do it. You can't just decide to stop believing your flight is on time when it still seems to you that it's on time -- beliefs aren't light switches, the seeming is still there even after you've judged it untrustworthy. So my real question: what is the actual procedure by which rational agents bring their first-order beliefs in line with their own judgments about what they should believe? Is the idea that you're supposed to distrust the seeming itself, like actively force yourself to stop finding it plausible? Because that seems both psychologically impossible and kind of dangerous -- if the rule is "whenever you judge yourself irrational, defer to that judgment," couldn't I talk myself out of perfectly good beliefs whenever I'm anxious or second-guessing myself? And what if my higher-order judgment is itself the messed-up one (say I've been told, falsely, that I was drugged and my reasoning is impaired) -- then deferring to it just makes things worse, so surely the rule can't be "always obey your higher-order assessments" either. But if the rule isn't that, and there's no procedure, then in what sense is akrasia something I'm doing wrong rather than just an unfortunate situation I find myself in, like having contradictory evidence handed to me from the outside? I'd love a clear answer on what rationality actually demands of me here, step by step, because right now the whole thing feels like being told it's illegal to be confused.
