I'm writing the final for my upper-level undergrad seminar on 19th-century political thought and I need one essay question on Mill and empire. I want it built around this passage from chapter 1 of Mill's Considerations on Representative Government:

"They look upon a constitution in the same light (difference of scale being allowed for) as they would upon a steam plow, or a threshing machine."

In that chapter Mill mostly sides with the "machine" view of institutions against the rival view that constitutions are organic growths of a people's habits and life that can't be constructed by design or transplanted. Here's the tension I want students to confront: later in the very same book Mill argues that representative government -- which he calls the ideally best form of government -- is inapplicable to peoples at lower stages of improvement, and he defends governing India through an intermediary body like the East India Company (where he himself worked for 35 years) rather than under direct control of Parliament. So: does treating constitutions as machines you can design and export make his colonial views a natural consequence of his liberalism rather than an embarrassing exception to it? And does the organic-growth conception, which he only half-rejects in chapter 1, come back to haunt the legitimacy of imposing institutions on Indians who had no hand in designing them? The question should be unanswerable by just reciting "Mill thought some peoples weren't ready for liberty."

Please give me: the exam question itself (students get 90 minutes), a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 built on five must-hit points -- the marks each is worth plus a one-line note on how students typically botch each. The line between an A and a B should turn on whether the answer actually works the tension between the steam plow passage and the later India chapters, not on which verdict the student reaches about Mill. In the model answer, please clearly seperate what's settled among Mill scholars (e.g., that he defended Company rule and ranked societies by stage of improvement) from what's genuinely contested (like whether his liberalism can be pried apart from his defense of empire at all), and don't present either side of that fight as the consensus view.
