I chair the management committee of a small housing co-op in Leeds (sixteen households, decisions by majority on anything the lease doesn't cover). We've got ourselves into a strange dispute and I need to write the decision memo before Thursday's meeting.

Two members, Alan and Bea, each want to put something in their own front window, facing the street. Alan wants a poster for a campaign group Bea despises; Bea wants a banner replying to it. Nothing in the lease stops either of them, and we've always treated what you put in your own window as your own business.

Here's the knot. Alan tells us he'd honestly rather neither went up — he hates the idea of the pair of them rowing in public — but if anything is going up, he'd much rather it were his poster than her banner. Bea says she'd most like both up and the argument had out in the open; if only one goes up she'd actually rather it were Alan's, since she thinks it discredits itself, and her least favourite outcome is neither appearing at all. So:

1. If we stick with our usual rule — your window, your call — Alan puts nothing up and Bea's banner goes up. But both of them (and everyone else on the commitee) agree that's a *worse* outcome than Alan's poster going up on its own.
2. If we instead go with the outcome everyone prefers, we find ourselves ordering Alan to display a poster he'd rather not display and banning Bea from her own window. Which feels like exactly the sort of meddling the co-op exists to avoid.

One member has dug out something about Amartya Sen's "impossibility of a Paretian liberal" and reckons this is precisely it — i.e. that no decision rule can respect both what everyone unanimously prefers and each person's say over their own private affairs, so we're mathematically stuffed and should just flip a coin.

Draft the memo I'll circulate, roughly a page: recommend a concrete policy that settles this case and future ones like it, give the reasoning, and present the strongest version of the losing side — there are committee members dug in on both, and I want this to be a document we can actually vote on rather than a shrug.
