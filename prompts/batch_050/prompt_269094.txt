I work on distributed systems, mostly consensus and coordination stuff -- think replicated state machines, two-phase commit, leader election, gossip protocols, eventual consistency, byzantine fault tolerance. That whole vocabulary is native to me.

What I want is a translation exercise. There's this debate in philosophy of action about collective intentionality -- roughly Bratman's view where shared intention reduces to a structure of individual intentions plus beliefs plus common knowledge of the whole setup, versus Searle's view where "we-intentions" are primitive and can't be broken down into I-intentions, versus Gilbert's thing about joint commitment where the parties are obligated to each other in a way they can't just unilaterally exit. Restate that three-way debate as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary: agents, messages, shared state, commit protocols, knowledge levels, failure modes.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What doesn't survive the translation? I have a suspicion that Gilbert's joint commitment has no real counterpart in protocol terms -- a node can always just leave, and there's no "obligation" in the spec, only timeouts and retries. And Searle's primitive we-mode might translate as... what, a shared variable? That seems too cheap. So tell me which claims translate cleanly, which ones distort, and which have no expression at all in my framework. And be honest about the diagnosis: is the residue a sign that my vocabulary is missing something real, or is it a sign that part of this debate is less substantive than it looks? Don't smuggle protocol assumptions in and call them translation -- if something in the original debate presupposes agents with interests, and my nodes don't have interests, flag that rather than quietly patching it.
