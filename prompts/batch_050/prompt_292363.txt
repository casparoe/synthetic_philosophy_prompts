I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on self-reference and self-replication, and I am writing this year's final examination. I need one exam problem, answerable in 90 minutes, on the formal duality between a string of symbols as passive data and the same string as a specification of operations on strings -- the duality that makes self-reproduction and Gödel-style self-reference possible. Because my students have varying familiarity with Hofstadter's Typogenetics from Gödel, Escher, Bach, the exam will be based entirely on the simplified, self-contained system below, which is printed on the exam's rule sheet. Your problem must be solvable from these rules alone, so that recollection of the book confers no advantage.

TYPOGENETICS-LITE (as stipulated for the exam):
1. A strand is a finite string over the letters A, C, G, T. Complementation: A pairs with T, C pairs with G.
2. Any strand can be read as a program: parse it left to right into non-overlapping pairs of letters; each pair encodes one instruction per the table below (an odd trailing letter is ignored). The resulting instruction sequence is the strand's enzyme.
3. An enzyme acts on a target strand (which may be the very strand that encoded it). A pointer begins at the target's leftmost unit; instructions execute in sequence; execution halts when the instructions are exhausted or the pointer falls off the strand.
4. Instruction table: AT = INS (insert, immediately right of the pointer, the complement of the unit at the pointer); AG = DEL (delete the unit at the pointer); AC = CUT (sever the strand immediately right of the pointer, retaining both fragments); CA = MOV (move the pointer one unit right); CG = CPY (toggle copy mode; while copy mode is on, each MOV appends the complement of the unit arrived at to a separate daughter strand under construction, which is released as a free strand when copy mode is toggled off or execution halts); TA = END (halt and detach). All other pairs are no-ops.

The problem should have three parts. Part (a) should require students to trace the execution of a specified strand acting on a specified target; design this so that there is exactly one correct trace under the rules, and so that copy mode and at least one no-op are exercised. Part (b) should force students to confront the crux that transcription yields the complement, not a duplicate: for instance, by asking whether a proposed strand is self-reproducing, or what must be true of a pair of strands for each to regenerate the other across two generations -- the who-copies-the-copier problem in miniature. Part (c) should ask students to state precisely in what sense the data/program duality of these strands parallels Gödel's arithmetization of syntax, and to identify at least one point where the analogy breaks down (no truth or provability predicate, no arithmetic correlate of complementation, causal rather than inferential operation, or the like).

Please deliver: the exam question itself; a model answer of roughly 600 words; and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points with marks totaling 20, where the scheme separates an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain, and includes for each point a one-line note on the way students most commonly get it wrong (for example, treating duplication as replication, or treating complementarity as mere bookkeeping).
