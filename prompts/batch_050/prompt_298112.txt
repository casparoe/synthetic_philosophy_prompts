I'm a PhD student working on personal identity and I've been chewing on the gradual replacement argument for mind uploading (the neuron-by-neuron silicon swap thing). Most fiction on this either treats it as obviously fine or obviously death, which I find boring. Write me a short story with this setup instead: a woman gets her neurons replaced one at a time over a year, but unbeknownst to her the lab keeps all the extracted biological neurons alive and eventually reassembles them into a working brain that wakes up convinced it's her. The story should take the philosophy seriously — e.g., let the two resulting people actually argue about which one the continuity intuition favors, and whether the slow swap even helped if the original parts can be put back together — without either character just lecturing the reader. Bonus if the ending complicates the intuition that there has to be a fact of the matter about which one is "really" her.
