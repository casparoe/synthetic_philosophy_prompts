You are an experienced instructor of philosophy of science who also has a track record of explaining physics concepts to non-specialists. Think carefully, and feel free to sketch several candidate sequences before settling on one.

I have to teach a single ninety-minute session to a mixed class of humanities undergraduates on the relationship between determinism, chaos, and predictability. The students arrive, I suspect, treating these as roughly the same topic: chaos means unpredictable, unpredictable means undetermined, and the culprit is quantum mechanics. My goal is narrower and harder: I want them to leave genuinely understanding that a world can be perfectly deterministic yet unpredictable even in principle for its inhabitants, and that this is a matter of epistemology, not of the laws being loose.

Plan the session for me rather than teaching it. Specify the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what each step depends on: in particular, where determinism has to be secured before chaos can mean anything, and where the distinction between unpredictability-in-practice and unpredictability-in-principle has to be made explicit or the whole lesson collapses into "computers aren't fast enough yet." Choose one carrying example — it must be a fully classical, everyday-intelligible system whose unpredictability comes from sensitive dependence rather than from any randomness or complexity of rules — and say why you chose it over the obvious alternatives (weather, coin flips, the double pendulum). Map the misconceptions you would expect, in the order they will surface: I anticipate at least "chaos is just randomness," "Laplace's demon could still do it," and "quantum indeterminacy is the real reason we can't predict." Indicate the exact point in your sequence where each should be defused rather than merely corrected afterward. Finally, tell me what to deliberately leave out at this level (and to say I'm leaving out), and give me one closing question that would reveal whether a student has grasped the determinism/predictability distinction rather than memorized the slogans.
