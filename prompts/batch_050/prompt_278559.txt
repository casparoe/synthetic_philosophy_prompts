I am preparing a written statement of my views for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of identity, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is where I currently stand:

1. The Identity of Indiscernibles is necessarily true: there cannot be two numerically distinct objects that share all their qualitative properties, intrinsic and relational alike. My reason for holding this is that any difference between things must be a difference in something — a bare numerical difference, unaccompanied by any qualitative difference, would be a difference with nothing that makes it.

2. Accordingly, Black's symmetrical universe — a world containing nothing but two qualitatively identical iron spheres, one mile apart in otherwise empty space — is not a genuine metaphysical possibility. Any qualitative description that purports to describe such a world in fact describes a world containing a single sphere.

3. I am a haecceitist about possible worlds: two worlds can match in every qualitative respect whatsoever and still be genuinely distinct. For instance, take a world qualitatively identical to ours except that two qualitatively identical twins occupy each other's total qualitative roles — every fact about what happens, where, when, and in what manner is exactly preserved. That world is nonetheless a different world from ours.

4. Leibniz's shift argument settles the debate between substantivalism and relationalism about space: two alleged worlds that differ only in the absolute position of the entire material universe within substantival space are qualitatively identical, and therefore they are not two worlds but one. That is why substantival space is a fiction.

5. Numerical diversity is primitive. When there are several things rather than one, there need be nothing in virtue of which they are several; I reject both bundle theories and bare-particular substrata as accounts of what grounds numerical difference, because I hold that nothing grounds it.

Please go through these five commitments and tell me whether the package hangs together. Specifically: identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and rank those tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad; identify which pairs are merely apparently in tension and explain why the appearance dissolves on inspection; for each genuine tension, state the cheapest revision that would resolve it and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the system; and finally, name the single change that would restore the most coherence overall. Please do not assume I already know where the problem is, and do not assume any particular commitment is the one I would be willing to surrender.
