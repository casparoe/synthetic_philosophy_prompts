For a graduate seminar in philosophy of medicine, draft the joint statement that a careful naturalist about disease and a careful normativist about disease could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. Do not give citations, bibliographies, or quotations from published texts; define the positions in your own words and keep the dispute self-contained.

Stipulate the following setup. The naturalist holds that disease attribution is anchored in biological function: roughly, a condition is pathological when it involves a failure of some organismic subsystem to perform a function it has in virtue of the organism’s design or life-history, with badness entering only afterward and contingently. The normativist holds that biological facts alone never settle whether something is a disease: disease attribution always also depends on values such as harm, suffering, disability, social fittingness, or what a community has reason to want changed. Introduce three invented diagnostic devices so the debate is not hostage to familiar stock examples: the Meridian Reserve, a measure of how much physiological slack a person has before ordinary stressors cause breakdown; the Ancestral-Modern Mismatch Score, a measure of how far current environments diverge from those in which a trait would have been advantageous; and the Civic Burden Index, a measure of how much a condition predictably shifts costs onto others through care, coordination, or risk.

The statement should contain, in this order: first, a numbered list of agreed claims both camps can sign without becoming vacuous, including at least claims about mortality, pain, statistical abnormality, aging, enhancement, and the difference between explaining a condition and licensing treatment; second, a disagreements table in which each remaining dispute is stated neutrally and then reformulated twice, once in words the naturalist would accept as a fair statement of their view and once in words the normativist would accept as a fair statement of theirs; third, a list of cruxes, meaning sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side.

Use these cases to force precision: a statistically normal late-life decline with low Meridian Reserve but no present suffering; a rare variant that raises Ancestral-Modern Mismatch Score, caused famine survival in the past, and now predicts metabolic collapse in abundance; a mild tremor carried by nearly every adult in a small community, ritually prized, mildly reducing dexterity, with high Civic Burden Index only when outsiders must accommodate it; an elective intervention that produces the same phenotype as an uncontroversial disease but is chosen for competitive advantage; and a condition that biologists can model perfectly while leaving entirely open whether anyone has reason to eliminate it.

For each crux, name the case, observation, or conceptual argument both sides agree would bear on it, and record in advance what each side predicts: what the naturalist says follows if the case goes one way, what the normativist says follows if it goes the other, and what result would embarrass each view. Require the statement to mark explicitly where the dispute is merely verbal, where it is empirical but currently underdetermined, and where it is genuinely normative. End with a short signed addendum from each party: one paragraph each, conceding the strongest point made by the other side while stating the smallest revision that would make them change camps.
