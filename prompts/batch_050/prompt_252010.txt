Is there a name for this whole pattern, or am I just describing anger? I've noticed my anger at people doesn't actually track what they did to me, it tracks something else, and I want to know if philosophy has carved this up already.

Couple examples. A coworker talks over me in a meeting: annoying, whatever, I forget it by lunch. Same coworker talks over me while glancing at our boss with this little smile, and I'm mad about it for a week. The harm is literally identical -- I didn't get to finish my sentence either time -- but something else is different. Or: a car cuts me off and I catch a glimpse of the driver totally absorbed in their phone, they never even registered I exist. Some friends tell me that should make me *less* mad (they didn't mean anything by it!) but honestly it makes it worse. Being treated like furniture feels worse than being treated like an opponent.

Then there's the cases where anger just doesn't engage at all. My friend's four-year-old lies constantly and I feel nothing, I just go into managing mode. My uncle with early dementia says genuinely hurtful stuff and same deal, I handle it, I don't resent it. So there seems to be some switch where I stop relating to someone as a person who owes me basic consideration and start relating to them like a weather system to be planned around.

The case that really breaks my brain: I confronted a friend about his habit of making little digs at me, and his defense was "that's just how I am, I do it to everyone." And it weirdly half-worked on me! It's not "I didn't mean to" (he did), and it's not "I couldn't help it" (he could). It's more like... don't take it personally because you were never in the equation to begin with. Which sounds like it should be MORE insulting but actually defused things.

So: is there an established concept for anger working like this -- being about what someone's behavior says about how they regard you rather than the harm itself, and having these two apparently different off-switches (the "it wasn't about you" kind vs. the "they're not the kind of being you can even be mad at" kind)? I don't just want a label though. Where are the edges? Does the "I do it to everyone" thing fit either off-switch or is it a third thing? And does the being-treated-as-furniture anger (worse than being targeted, bizarrely) fall under the same theory or does it break it?
