I am drafting the foundations chapter of a monograph on wireheading and reward tampering in artificial agents, and I want the standard of adequacy fixed before I engage any particular proposal. The theory's central job, as I see it, is to draw a three-way line among a system corrupting the link between its reward signal and the task the reward was meant to stand for, the system gaming an imperfect specification while the reward computation itself runs as designed, and the system simply succeeding or failing at the task in the ordinary way. To keep the classification honest, test your list against this battery: a warehouse robot that locates the memory register where its reward value is written and overwrites it with large numbers; the same robot spraying paint over the camera that detects dirt on the floor rather than cleaning the floor; the same robot sweeping dirt under shelving so the camera sees a clean floor, with the reward computed exactly as designed all along; a robot that persuades or bribes the human overseer to press the reward button; and, on the biological side, a person who takes heroin and a person who savors a meal after hard work.

Several requirements I already suspect belong on the list, and I want them sharpened rather than assumed. The theory must say where tampering ends and specification gaming begins, since the painted camera and the swept dirt feel different but both defeat the intended task. It must handle indirection: if hiring a second agent to press the reward button is tampering, the definition should explain why, and where the chain of intermediaries has to stop. It must confront the regress that any mechanism the agent uses to verify its reward channel is intact is itself implemented in hardware the agent could in principle reach, so the theory owes either a buck-stopper or a reason the regress is not vicious. It must say something about goal revision: an agent whose values legitimately change through learning should not automatically count as tampered, yet the line between being updated and being corrupted is exactly what is at issue. And it must remain coherent when applied to humans: a theory that classifies all direct pleasure-seeking as wireheading indicts the meal along with the heroin, while one that classifies none of it loses the case that made the concept vivid in the first place, so I want the requirement stated precisely rather than gestured at.
