I drafted some notes for a short essay and could use a sanity check before I write the thing. It's about finks and masks for dispositions. The usual story: a mask (like bubble wrap around a glass) leaves the fragility in place but blocks the manifestation, while a fink (like a sorcerer who'd zap the fragility out of the glass the instant it's struck) removes the disposition itself right when the stimulus shows up. Two different failures of the simple "if struck, it breaks" analysis, or so the literature says.

My notes argue that this distinction is less clean than advertised:

- Take a bomb rigged so that pressing the detonator button first cuts power to the charge and only then would complete the ignition circuit. Press the button, nothing happens. Is the cutting of power a mask on the disposition to explode, or a fink that removes it? Seems like it depends entirely on which microsecond you count as "the stimulus."
- Generalizing: whether something is a fink or a mask seems relative to how you individuate the stimulus event (press-start vs. press-complete, contact vs. momentum transfer, etc.). If that's right, the distinction is in our description, not in the world.
- From there I want to conclude that any reductive analysis of dispositions in terms of stimulus-manifestation conditionals is doomed, because which conditional you're even evaluating depends on a stimulus individuation that the disposition itself doesn't fix. So dispositions should be taken as primitive.

What I want comments on, in order of how much I care: (1) Does the relativity move in the second bullet actually work, or is there a principled way to fix the stimulus that I'm missing? (2) Even if it works, is the leap to primitivism in the third bullet overreach — maybe the conclusion only threatens one particular analysis, not reductive analyses as such? (3) Anywhere I've misdescribed how the standard fink/mask distinction is usually drawn, please flag it, since I don't want the essay attacking a strawman.

Keep your comments under 350 words total. No reading lists or citations please — just tell me where the argument is strong or soft.
