I retired two years ago after 35 years in population genetics (insecticide resistance, mostly), and since then I've been reading my way into environmental philosophy. Everyone I read on gene drives and de-extinction seems to argue by pointing at one case, and I want the full spec first: what would a theory of permissible irreversible intervention in ecosystems actually have to get right? Fix the purpose as this -- it should be usable as the checklist for a panel making go/no-go decisions on open releases, not a verdict on any single case.

Some things I suspect belong on the requirements list, but tell me whether each is genuine or just one camp's prejudice:

It has to handle irreversibility being a moving target. A drive can in principle be undone by a reversal drive, and the de-extinction people claim even extinction isn't final anymore, so the theory can't just sort technologies into reversible and irreversible bins. It has to say what makes an intervention count as irreversible for decision purposes -- time and cost of returning to a baseline? and whose baseline, given ecosystems were never sitting still?

It has to explain, or dissolve, an asymmetry I can't get past: we drove species extinct for centuries by carelessness and nobody called it a crime against nature, but deliberately driving the malaria mosquitoes extinct is treated as a special horror. Real distinction the theory must respect, or squeamishness?

It has to say whether a mammoth-elephant proxy restores anything that was actually lost, which I gather commits it to a stand on what a species even is.

And it has to deal with the fact that drives don't respect borders, so the community that decides and the system that's affected come apart. Whose consent, if anyone's, does that require?

For each requirement, give the case behind it and wether it's negotiable. Then tell me if some subset is jointly unsatisfiable -- my hunch is the consent requirement and the counterfactual one (standing aside while malaria kills on the order of half a million people a year) can't both be fully honored, and if so the list itself is already a result. And which single requirement eliminates the most frameworks people actually defend. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than on scoring existing views against them.
