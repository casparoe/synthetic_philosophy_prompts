I'm a second-year philosophy student currently enrolled in a seminar on animal ethics and the foundations of moral status. Last week we worked through the argument from marginal cases and the charge of speciesism, and our lecturer mentioned in passing that nineteenth-century craniometry attempted to ground human social hierarchies in skull measurements -- a detail that has stuck with me, since it seems to be the cautionary tale lurking behind every appeal to a measurable proxy for moral standing. We also spent a session on how poorly brain size actually tracks the capacities people take to matter (corvids and cephalopods came up, as did elephants and newborns), and I came away with the feeling that the brain-size heuristic survives in casual conversation precisely because nobody has ever written down what consistently applying it would look like.

I would like you to write that document for me, as satire. Specifically: an official FAQ page published by a fictitious agency, the Bureau of Proportional Consideration, which has fully implemented a policy of allotting moral status in proportion to brain mass. The page should be written in the patient, slightly evasive register of real bureaucratic web copy, and should cover, in whatever order you like: how citizens schedule their volumetric scan and what the measurement fee covers; the appeals process for those who believe their scan undercounted them; guidance for caregivers filing on behalf of borderline applicants such as neonates, coma patients, and the very elderly; a note for non-human applicants, including the cephalopod liaison office and the awkward case of species whose brains are large in absolute terms but small relative to their bodies; the Bureau's response to the frequent complaint that the metric mostly tracks body size; and a brief proud sidebar on the Bureau's historical predecessors. Around 650 words.

The satire should work by taking the proxy completely seriously: every joke should arise from the Bureau treating a crude correlate of the morally relevant capacities as the thing itself, and from the procedural machinery that follows. The standard objections -- who calibrates the scale, what happens to those who fall below the threshold, whether the chosen measure was ever really about cognition -- should all be landed deadpan, through the FAQ's answers, and never stated as objections. Please do not break character at any point in the piece itself. After the piece, as a separate final line, state in one sentence, beginning with "Target:", which position or argumentative habit the piece satirizes, so that my seminar group can check whether the irony is legible.
