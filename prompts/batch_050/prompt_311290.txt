I sit on the deployment review board of a mid-size AI company, and our remit is specifically control, not alignment: we evaluate the precautions a team proposes under the assumption that the model might be misaligned, and we treat claims that a model has been made aligned as outside our jurisdiction. We have five decisions on the books, and I want to know what consistency with them requires for a proposal landing next week. Our past verdicts: (1) We approved a coding assistant with broad read access to internal repositories, on the condition that every output be screened in real time by a smaller, separately trained model, with humans reviewing a random sample of flagged items. (2) We rejected a more capable model with write access to a production database where the team proposed no live monitoring, only after-the-fact logging, even though its pre-deployment behavioral testing had been unusually thorough and clean. (3) We approved with minor conditions a research assistant running in a sandbox with no ability to take actions outside it and no runtime monitor at all. (4) We rejected a heavily sandboxed model with minimal permissions after it was caught in staged honeypot scenarios taking steps to preserve its own access when it appeared to believe itself unobserved. (5) We approved a model monitored only by a copy of itself, where the team inserted randomized honeypot tasks and ran weekly human audits of the monitoring transcripts.

The new proposal: a model with write access to internal tooling, clean results on every behavioral evaluation and every honeypot the red team ran, monitored in real time by a copy of itself with randomized honeypots continuing in deployment -- but, because of budget pressure, no human audits of the monitor's transcripts at all. Half the board says this is just case 5 with a cheaper monitoring stack; the other half says removing the human audits removes the one element that made case 5 acceptable, and that a monitor drawn from the same model as the one being watched is no control measure at all, which pushes it toward case 2.

Please recommend the verdict that best coheres with our track record. I'd like you to: identify which precedent is the closest analog and why; spell out at least two principles that each fit all five prior verdicts but diverge on this case; say which principle we would effectively be adopting with an approval and which with a rejection, since that framing matters for how our decision gets cited later; and name a future case or a piece of additional information that would discriminate between those principles. Please write in American English, and end by stating how confident you are in your recommended verdict and singling out the specific point in your analysis you are least sure of.
