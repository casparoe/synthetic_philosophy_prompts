Physics undergrad here, something keeps bugging me and I don't know if there's a word for it. The pattern: a theory's math contains parts we're told aren't "real" -- just bookkeeping, you can change them freely and nothing observable moves. Fine. But then, sometimes decades later, those exact parts turn out to matter after all. Two examples. In electromagnetism the vector potential gets introduced as pure computational convenience, any choice with the same curl works -- then the Aharonov-Bohm effect shows interference shifting in a region with zero field, and suddenly people argue the potential was physically there all along. And in GR my prof says coordinate choices are meaningless, but then apparently at the boundary of spacetime some of that coordinate-ish structure does real work (definitions of energy-momentum and so on). So the recurring move is: yesterday's freely-changeable, "unphysical" piece of the formalism gets re-graded into today's load-bearing structure. Does this phenomenon have a name? I don't think it's just "gauge symmetry" -- that's the static fact that descriptions can vary; I'm asking about the pattern where the varying part gets promoted to real. If there's no established term, how would you carve it, and does it undermine the whole practice of calling parts of a theory "surplus structure" in the first place, since we seem to keep being wrong about which parts those are?
