I write the philosophy column for a photography magazine and want to build a "do you see through pictures?" quiz that sorts readers by their verdicts on cases rather than by which label appeals to them. Ten to twelve questions, each a concrete scenario with two or three answer options; no question may mention a theory or ask readers which sense of "see" they prefer. The scenarios must include at least: a guard watching a live CCTV feed versus the same guard reviewing last night's recording; a blurred but authentic photograph versus a photorealistic painting made from a verbal description alone; a telescope view of a star that has since gone supernova; and a synthetic image visually indistinguishable from a photograph of a real place. Mirrors and ordinary eyesight should appear as calibration items.

Then supply a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least these positions (named only in the key, never in the quiz): the transparency view, on which we literally see the photographed subject just as we see things through telescopes; the representationalist view, on which we see only the picture and infer the subject; the seeing-in view, on which pictorial experience is a distinctive twofold state that is neither ordinary seeing nor inference; and a deflationary view on which the dispute is verbal. The key must discriminate rather than lump: someone who counts live video but not recordings as seeing the subject, or who accepts seeing perished subjects through telescopes but not through photographs, must land somewhere distinct from both full transparency and full representationalism.

Finally, build the key from the answer space rather than from the position list: enumerate the combinations of answers the quiz permits, locate the positions in that space, and for any combination matching no recognizable position, say briefly whether it looks incoherent, merely unheld, or actually available. Flag any pair of positions your quiz cannot separate, and specify what further case would separate them.
