So I've been going in circles on the old "can a finite string be random" question and I want to try something: I'll dump my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and I want someone to find the principle that's generating them, or tell me which guts to sacrifice. I've read the usual Sequences-adjacent stuff about Kolmogorov complexity and I know the textbook answer is roughly "randomness = incompressibility, asymptotically," but my actual judgments don't line up with it cleanly.

Case 1. A fair coin flipped 100 times comes up HHHH... all heads. My gut says: this sequence is not random. Gut also says: this exact sequence had the same probability as any other, so accusing it of nonrandomness feels like the lottery-winner fallacy. Both guts feel load-bearing.

Case 2. Same fair coin, 100 flips, comes out perfectly alternating HTHTHT... My gut says this one is definitely not random, and more confidently than case 1, and I can't articulate why the confidence differs when both strings have identical probability and equal Kolmogorov complexity, roughly.

Case 3. I show you the first 200 binary digits of pi (in some natural encoding) without telling you what they are. You check, they pass every statistical test you know. My gut says: to you they're random, but once I tell you the generator, they retroactively become non-random. So randomness is partly about what you know? But my gut also hates that conclusion, because it makes randomness epistemic and I want it to be a fact about the string.

Case 4. A quantum random number generator (genuine collapse outcomes, let's grant) outputs a string. My gut says this one is really random in a way cases 1-3 aren't, full stop. But if the QRNG happened to output the same bits as pi's digits in case 3, my gut wants to call one copy random and the other not, which is insane -- same string.

Case 5. A string compresses by exactly one bit under the best compressor anyone finds. Gut: basically random, marginally suspicious. A string compresses by half. Gut: not random. So randomness comes in degrees, fine. But then "random" isn't a binary property, and my verdicts in cases 1-4 were all stated as binary.

Case 6. Someone shows me a string and claims it's from a fair coin. It's 60% heads. Gut: could be random, mild side-eye. They show me a second string, 50.0001% heads over a million flips, but with a detectable 7-bit periodic structure buried in it. Gut says the second one is less random than the first even though its frequency is closer to ideal, which contradicts every frequency-based intuition I thought I had.

So: is there one principle here? My suspicion is that I need to split "random" into a property of the process and a property of the string, and maybe a third thing about the observer's compression abilities, and that dissolves some of these. But I notice that splitting feels like it's going to cost me either case 3's epistemic verdict or case 4's objectivity verdict, and I don't know which to give up. If the right account makes me drop a verdict, tell me which one and why. And tell me what the principle says about a case I haven't listed: a string nobody has ever generated or observed, described only as "the output of algorithm X that provably runs forever without repeating" -- random or not, and is the question even well-posed?
