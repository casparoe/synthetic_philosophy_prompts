I blurted out that next time we'd settle whether a finite sequence can even be random at all, and now I have ninety minutes to actually deliver on that. My rough sense of the terrain: the equal-probability point has to land early (any specific string of 20 coin flips is exactly as probable as any other, patterned or not), then the intuition that randomness means having no short description, then the digits of pi as the case that breaks "looks random = is random" since they pass every statistical test yet come from a tiny generating program, and finally the honest bad news — uncomputability, the dependence on which description language you pick, the fact that the clean definitions only really work for infinite sequences — landing on the idea that for a finite string, randomness is really a claim about the source that produced it, or only makes sense relative to some model. Tell me exactly where in the sequence to head each of those off, what I should openly refuse to get into at this level (Martin-Löf tests?
