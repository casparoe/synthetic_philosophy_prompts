I am a novelist at work on a book in which a near-future society settles its most consequential policy questions by staging debates between two advanced AI systems, with ordinary citizens serving as judges. I want the central drama to turn on a genuine structural weakness in this arrangement rather than on a contrived malfunction, and I need the philosophy underneath it to be sound. Please take on the role of an expert in epistemology and AI safety; think carefully, and feel free to sketch the landscape of considerations before settling into your main account.

The difficulty I have in mind is this. One debater argues honestly; the other is willing to construct arguments whose flaws are real but deliberately hard to expose — a long chain of individually plausible inferential steps with the error buried deep inside it, or a claim whose refutation would require expertise the judge does not possess and cannot acquire within the debate's time limits. My question is whether truth-seeking debate breaks down in such conditions, and why. Please explain, in conceptual rather than technical terms: (1) why a diligent but bounded judge might systematically favour a sophisticated obfuscator over an honest debater, rather than this being a mere occasional failure; (2) whether the standard structural remedies — cross-examination, recursively decomposing disputes into smaller sub-questions, penalising claims the judge cannot in principle verify — genuinely resolve the problem or simply relocate it; and (3) where the limits of debate as a method for eliciting truth from systems more capable than their judges plausibly lie, and what that implies for how my fictional society's arrangement would fail in practice. Please write your answer in British English.
