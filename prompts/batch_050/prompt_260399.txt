So here's something that's bugging me. In basically every modern democracy, any adult citizen can vote, full stop. No test, no qualification, doesn't matter if you think inflation is caused by the moon. At the same time, these exact same societies gatekeep competence absolutely everywhere else in public life. You can't be a surgeon without a license, or even cut hair for money in a lot of places. Judges and senior civil servants are screened within an inch of their lives. Juries get voir dire'd and can be struck for bias or inability to follow the case. Even within the franchise itself we do draw lines: children can't vote, and in many places people under legal guardianship can't either, precisely because they're judged not competent to form the relevant judgement.

So voting looks like this weird carve-out where the most consequential power of all -- choosing who holds the coercive authority of the state -- is the one thing we refuse to competence-test, in societies that otherwise act like competence-testing is just obvious common sense.

What I want to know: what theory of political legitimacy, or of what a vote even *is*, does that carve-out presuppose? Like, does it only make sense if voting isn't really an exercise of expertise at all (more like an expression of consent, or equal standing, or a way of registering interests)? Or is it better understood as a second-order distrust move -- we believe competence matters, we just don't trust anyone to adminster the test fairly? Reconstruct the implicit theory as charitably as you can, and tell me which rival views the practice quietly rules out (e.g. could a pure "the knowledgeable should rule" view ever license this?).

Also: is the practice actually internally consistent, or is it papering over a contradiction? If we swapped in an epistocratic view -- say, weighted votes by demonstrated political knowledge -- what else in our institutions would have to change to stay coherent, and what could stay as-is? And conversely, if we took the "no one gets to judge anyone's competence to wield power" thought seriously, shouldn't we also be abolishing the exclusions for teenagers and licensing exams, which almost nobody wants?
