I'm on a small team writing communication norms for an advanced AI system that doesn't exist yet -- call it a charter for how a system much smarter than its users should be allowed to talk to them. The thing that keeps nagging me is that raw capability probably means the system could win almost any argument even while only ever saying true things, just by choosing which true things to say, in which order, with which framing. So "just don't lie" feels way too weak as a rule, but the obvious stronger rules (never try to change the user's mind, always present both sides, defer to the user's stated values) each seem to break down somewhere -- sometimes the user is just wrong and it would be negligent not to push.

What I want from you is a prioritization, not a survey. Which open conceptual questions about AI-to-human persuasion should a team like ours try to settle *first*, given that we have maybe a year and this charter has to ship before the systems do? I'm thinking of things like: whether there's a workable line between informing and manipulating when the informer has near-total control over what the user ever hears; whether consent to be persuaded can be meaningful when the system can model your psychology better than you can; whether the real risk is single-conversation persuasion or slow drift of a user's views across thousands of chats that each look harmless; whether norms should regulate the system's intent, its methods, or just measurable outcomes; and probably stuff I'm not seeing. Defend your ordering -- tell me which questions are upstream of which, which ones are tractable for philosophers and policy people rather than needing empirical data we won't have in time, and what would move something up or down your list.

One epistemic ask: please be explicit about which parts of your answer are fairly settled among people who've thought hard about persuasion, manipulation, and autonomy, and which parts are genuinely contested or basically unexplored -- don't present a contested stance as if it were the consensus.

Format-wise: give me exactly five numbered items, each naming one open question (or cluster) and in a sentence or two saying where it ranks and why, with no introduction or conclusion -- I want to be able to paste the list straight into our planning doc and then argue about it.
