i'm in a second-year philosophy of mind module and we've just finished the unit on emergence and collective minds, including Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary thought experiment — the ant colony that is itself conscious even though no individual ant is, and the anteater who happily holds conversations with the colony whilst casually eating bits of it, which is somehow fine because the "mind" lives at the level of the trail network, not the ants.

for the end-of-term creative assessment (yes, that's a real thing on this course) i have to submit a poem, and i want to write a villanelle in Aunt Hillary's voice where the form does actual philosophical work rather than just decorating the idea. my thought is that the two refrains should be the two true descriptions of her that never quite line up: one at the colony level, the whole speaking as an "I", and one at the ant level, thousands of little things just following pheromone trails with nobody home in any of them. the way a villanelle keeps hauling its refrains back into new contexts feels exactly like how those two levels stay simultaneously true without ever meeting, and then in the final quatrain they have to stand side by side — which should land as the punchline of the whole problem, not a tidy resolution. i definately don't want the last lines to say "so both are true in their own way!!" or anything that neat.

also get the anteater in there somewhere, ideally as a threat that isn't one: losing a few hundred ants is, for her, closer to losing a few neurons than to being murdered, and that asymmetry is half the point of the case so it needs to feel eerie rather than violent.

the levels-of-description idea should be rendered accurately enough that someone who knows the literature would recognise it rather than just seeing "emergence" name-dropped, but it should still work as a poem for someone who hasn't done the reading. standard villanelle shape please — 19 lines, the aba rhyme scheme, refrains repeating properly — and write it in british english because my marker is weirdly strict about that.
