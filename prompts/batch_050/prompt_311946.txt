It says: "Hume noticed that when we watch one billiard ball strike another, we see the collision and then the second ball's motion, but we never perceive any connection between them — no power by which the first makes the second move. All experience ever gives us is that events of one kind are constantly conjoined with events of another kind. The idea of necessary connection therefore comes entirely from the mind: after enough repetition we form a habit of expecting the second event, and this feeling of expectation is the whole content of our idea of causation. Hume's conclusion is that causation simply does not exist in objects — there is no fact of the matter about whether one event causes another, only regularities plus our expectations. The two definitions of 'cause' he offers — one in terms of constant conjunction, one in terms of what the mind is led to infer — are really the same definition stated twice. This is why Hume is a skeptic about science: causal inference has no rational justification, so believing the sun will rise tomorrow is no more reasonable than believing it won't."
