I have been a reader of the rationalist blogs for well over a decade, and like many people there I absorbed Solomonoff induction fairly early as the supposed final word on Occam's razor: the universal prior over computable hypotheses, the exponential penalty on program length, the whole package. Recently, though, I have been reading about the actual historical razor, and I am struck by how thin the connection might be. The slogan about not multiplying entities beyond necessity apparently never appears in Ockham's own writings in that form, and when he does invoke parsimony principles, they seem to do quite different work: they are deployed against realist metaphysics of universals and spare entities within a theological framework, and he reportedly held that the principle constrains natural philosophy, not God, who can accomplish through many means what he could accomplish through fewer. So his razor looks less like a general inductive bias toward short descriptions and more like a specific injunction against positing redundant metaphysical machinery.

Here is my question. Suppose William of Ockham were transported to the present and given a careful, sympathetic explanation of Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity, including the standard caveats: the uncomputability of the universal prior, the dependence of complexity assignments on the choice of reference machine up to an additive constant, and the fact that the formalism rewards short programs that reproduce the data, which is not obviously the same thing as ontological sparseness. Would he regard this as a vindication of his razor, a corruption of it, or merely a distant relative that happens to share a name? I want you to argue from his actual commitments as far as they are known -- his treatment of universals, his emphasis on intuitive cognition and experience as the basis of knowledge, his subordination of parsimony to divine freedom -- rather than from the bumper-sticker version of him that circulates online. Please also consider what he would make of the fact that the modern razor is a probabilistic bias justified by its predictive track record rather than a rule of metaphysics, and whether he would find that a strength or a category error. Where the evidence genuinely underdetermines the answer, say so rather than inventing a view for him.

Structure your answer as: first, the strongest case that he would endorse Solomonoff induction as his razor made rigorous; second, the strongest case that he would reject it as a misunderstanding of what the razor was for; third, your own weighed judgment. End with a final line beginning "Verdict:" followed by a single sentence stating whether Ockham would have accepted Solomonoff induction as a vindication of his razor, rejected it as a distortion, or treated it as a partial relative, and why.
