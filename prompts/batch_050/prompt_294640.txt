So I've been reading up on the ethics of discounting the future -- mostly because of the usual EA/rationalist cluster of topics (longtermism, x-risk, population ethics) where the social discount rate does a suspicious amount of work in the cost-benefit analyses. And I've noticed that on the specific question of *pure* time preference -- discounting future wellbeing just because it's later, not because of uncertainty or growth or opportunity costs -- there seem to be basically two camps that talk past each other endlessly. Camp one I associate with the Ramsey/Sidgwick/Parfit line, roughly that pure time preference is an ethically indefensible bias, a mere artifact of our weak imagination for later times, and that a year of suffering counts the same whenever it occurs. Camp two is people (often economists, but also some philosopher friends of mine) who insist that some positive pure rate is defensible, or at least that the no-discounting camp smuggles in its own controversial assumptions about impartiality, personal identity, or what "later" even means for a planner deciding from a temporal point of view. I read the exchanges and both sides seem to leave convinced the other didn't engage.

What I want from you: please draft the joint statement that a committed member of each camp could actually co-sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. Act as a professional philosopher who's neutral between them and genuinely good at adversarial collaborations -- think carefully, and maybe brainstorm a list of candidate claims first before writing the final document. The document should have (a) a numbered section of agreed claims -- e.g., things both can accept about what pure time preference is and isn't (distinguishing it from uncertainty, declining marginal utility of consumption, changing preferences, mortality risk, etc.), and about what the stakes of the disagreement actually are; (b) a disagreements table where each row states one residual disagreement in wording *both* sides would accept as a fair statement of their own position -- no steelmanning one side into incoherence; and (c) for each disagreement, the crux: what sub-question would actually move at least one of them if settled, and what kind of argument, case, or thought experiment both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

A few things I'm specifically hoping the document will sort out, so please address these: whether part of the dispute is verbal -- e.g., whether "discounting" gets used differently by the two camps (discounting *utility* vs discounting *goods* vs discounting *moral weight*); whether the "we'd discount a person's own future suffering" intuition pump cuts both ways given views about prudence and personal identity over time; and whether the defender of pure time preference can state their view without it collapsing into either (i) a descriptive claim about human psychology or (ii) uncertainty about the future, which everyone agrees are different things. If you conclude some chunk of the disagreement was never really substantive, say which chunk and why. Casual tone is fine in the meta-comments but the joint statement itself should read like something both parties could actually sign.
