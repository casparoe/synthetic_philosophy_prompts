Last night I had a long argument with a friend about distributive justice, and we reached an impasse. She defends prioritarianism against the leveling-down objection by saying that prioritarianism places no intrinsic value on equality at all: it simply gives more weight to benefits the worse off their recipient is, so leveling everyone down is never good in any respect on her view, not even pro tanto. I replied that this is just relabeling. Any view that gives extra weight to the worse-off must, in at least some pairwise comparisons, prefer a state in which everyone is worse off but the distribution is more equal, so prioritarianism inherits the objection in substance even if it avoids the word equality. Since the leveling-down objection shows that every distribution-sensitive view must sometimes favor making all people worse off for no one's benefit, how should a prioritarian best respond — or should we instead concede that the objection refutes prioritarianism too? Please take a definite position on which of us is right and give your reasons, rather than laying out both sides and leaving the verdict to me.
