my econ roommate hit me with this argument that path dependence is basically fake, and it's been bugging me for a week because every step looks fine and the conclusion is obviously nuts. it goes: (1) an outcome only counts as "locked in" if a better alternative exists, is known about, and people still don't switch. (2) if the alternative really is better all things considered, then switching creates net gains, and wherever there are net gains someone can capture them -- either by switching themselves or by coordinating the switch for everyone and taking a cut. (3) profitable opportunities in a market get taken, at least eventually. (4) so if nobody switches, that just shows the alternative wasn't actually better once you count everything, including the costs of switching and coordinating. (5) so whenever people cry lock-in -- QWERTY, VHS, narrow-gauge railways, whatever -- the honest verdict is that the incumbent was actually fine, and the same thing would have won even if history had run differently. (6) so history is never really locked in, and "path dependence" is just nostalgia for timelines that were never live options in the first place. the conclusion has to be wrong -- it seems to prove that no standard or institution could ever be sticky for bad reasons, which is absurd on its face -- but i cant find the hole. which step do i reject? heads up that i'd really like to keep (3), since it feels like the one solid thing economics teaches, so if the answer is "give up (3)" i want to know exactly what i'm paying.
