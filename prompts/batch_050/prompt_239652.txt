So I've had this running argument with my brother for going on two years now and it's completely stuck, and I want to understand why, not get another round of it. My position is that this can't be actual lying, because lying to someone means you know the truth and keep it from them, but if it's you doing the hiding then you already know, so who exactly is getting fooled? The knowledge would have to be in the same head as the not-knowing and that just seems incoherant to me.

Here's the weird part. We both agree on all the cases. Neither of us denies the coworker thing happened, or that people act against what they in some sense know. We've also both heard each other's full argument multiple times, we can each do the other one's side pretty convincingly, and nothing moves. At this point I have a suspicion I want tested: I think we might just be fighting over the word "deceive" and whether it has to work like lying-to-another-person or whether it can cover whatever my aunt was doing, in which case it's a dictionary dispute and kind of boring. But my brother thinks there's a real question buried in there about how a mind can be set up so that one part hides things from another part, and he's not satisfied by me calling it something else like "avoidance" or "not thinking about it."

So: is this a verbal disagreement, or a substantive one dressed up as verbal, or are we each answering a different question under the same words? Like maybe he's describing a real phenomenon and I'm making a point about the logic of the word and we keep talking past each other.
