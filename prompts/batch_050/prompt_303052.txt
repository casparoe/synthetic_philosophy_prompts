ok so i'm a phd student in cog sci (psych side, but i have to pass a phil mind comp) and my reading group got into a fight about the meta-problem of consciousness and couldn't settle it, so i'm bringing it here.

as i understand chalmers' setup: the hard problem is explaining phenomenal experience itself, the easy problems are explaining functions and behavior, and the meta-problem is the problem of explaining why we *say and think* there's a hard problem — why people report their experiences as ineffable, private, irreducable, why they find zombies conceivable, and so on. and it's supposed to be attractive precisely because it's a problem about our judgments and reports, which are behavioral/functional facts, so cognitive science can in principle just... solve it.

here's where i get stuck. if the meta-problem actually gets solved — say we end up with a full mechanistic story about why systems like us form the intuition that experience is intrinsic and inexplicable (metacognitive blind spots, self-models that leave out their own machinery, whatever) — then hasn't the hard problem been dissolved by construction? as far as i can tell, the hard problem only ever had content via those reports and intuitions. once you explain why the intuition of an explanatory gap arises, there is no gap left to explain. so my question: why do people, apparently including chalmers himself, keep saying the meta-problem is "neutral" between realism and illusionism about phenomenal consciousness? that looks straighforwardly false to me. a successful meta-problem solution seems to just entail illusionism. is there some reading where the neutrality claim is even coherent, or is the field politely not noticing that the meta-problem is a trojan horse?

related sub-question: when illusionists say solving the meta-problem dissolves the hard problem, and realists respond that explaining the judgments leaves experience itself untouched, isn't the realist response question-begging? they're asserting a leftover explanandum whose only evidence was the reports we just explained. i genuinely can't tell if i'm missing something or if this is one of those things where the disagreement is doing all the work.
