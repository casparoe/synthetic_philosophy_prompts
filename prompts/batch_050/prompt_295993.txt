I serve on my university's honor council, which is rewriting the academic integrity code for the first time since student use of AI writing tools became common. The council is deadlocked between two factions. One insists that plagiarism, as a matter of what the concept means, is the presentation of another person's words or ideas as one's own, and that AI-generated text, having no author, therefore cannot be plagiarized — so the code should remain silent on it. The other wants undisclosed AI use classified as plagiarism, full stop. I have been asked to write the short opinion (roughly two pages) on which the full council will vote. Please draft it. The opinion should do three things. First, it should argue that the council's task is not to settle what "plagiarism" really means as a piece of ordinary language, but to decide what concept of academic dishonesty the code needs in order to do its work — and it should say explicitly what that work is. Second, it should propose a concrete clause the council could actually vote on, specifying how it treats disclosure obligations and borderline cases, such as AI used for brainstorming, outlining, or grammar correction. Third, it should take seriously the strongest point on the losing side: I suspect that is the worry that a code built on a revised concept students do not already share will strike them as a bureaucratic invention rather than a statement of genuine wrongdoing, and so will fail to command their respect. Address that worry head-on rather than dismissing it, and let it shape the final recommendation.
