I was listening to a podcast episode about hunger and economics and the host summed up Amartya Sen's work on famines more or less like this (I'm paraphrasing from memory, but this is the gist):

'Sen's big result is that famines are never actually about food. There's always enough food around -- the problem is that governments let people starve, either on purpose or because the people in charge never have to face the victims themselves. His main case study is the Bengal famine of 1943, where he showed Bengal had more food than it usually did, and the British simply chose to let millions die anyway. From there he derives his famous law that no democracy has ever suffered a famine, because elections and a free press force politicians to respond before people starve. So the takeaway is that famines aren't natural disasters at all, they're political crimes -- a famine is basically a slow-motion massacre.'

Something about this smells off to me but I can't put my finger on where. The "no famines in democracies" bit I've heard elsewhere so I assume that's right, and the general point about entitlements and distribution rings true. But the framing at the end -- "political crime", "slow massacre", governments letting people starve "on purpose" -- sits oddly with what I'd expect from a Nobel economist, and the "more food than usual" detail about Bengal seems suspiciously convenient.

Can you go through it claim by claim? Which parts would Sen himself endorse, which are common overstatements of his actual, more modest thesis, and which get him flat wrong? I'm especially interested in (a) whether Sen really claims food availability is irrelevant to famines or something subtler, (b) what he actually argued about food supply in Bengal 1943 and whether "the British chose to let them die" is his framing or the podcaster's, and (c) whether the jump from "famines involve failures of distribution and accountability" to "famines are intentional political crimes" is a move Sen makes or a move made in his name. If the podcast's conclusion doesn't follow from the accurate parts of the summary, tell me what conclusion actually does follow.
