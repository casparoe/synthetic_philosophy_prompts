There's this practice I've seen floating around in effective altruist circles and sometimes in ordinary life too, and I want to understand what it's committed to philosophically. The basic setup: two people disagree about which cause is most important. Say Alice thinks donations to animal welfare do the most good, and Bob thinks global health does. Instead of each donating to their own cause, they make a deal: Alice donates to Bob's global health charity and Bob donates to Alice's animal charity, in matching amounts. Sometimes this is pitched as purely strategic (each can claim employer matching or tax benefits the other couldn't get), but often it's pitched as a way of respecting each other's uncertainty, like a moral compromise. There's also a cousin practice, "moral offsetting," where someone who eats meat donates to an animal charity to cancel out the harm, or someone who flies a lot buys carbon offsets, and people treat the ledger as balanced afterward.

What does all this presuppose? Like, does trading only make sense if moral value is one-dimensional and fungible, so that a unit of good here can stand in for a unit of good there, regardless of whose values produced it? If Alice thinks Bob's cause is actually worthless or slightly harmful, is the trade still rational for her, and does the answer depend on whether morality is about outcomes or about what you're permitted to intend? And with the offsetting version: does treating your own wrongdoing as canceled by a donation presuppose that wrongs are debts that can be repaid in a common currency, rather than, I dunno, things you're just not supposed to do, or stains on your character that no amount of payment removes? It also seems relevant that almost nobody would accept an offset for a really serious wrong -- nobody thinks a murderer can settle up by funding a charity -- so where's the line, and what does the location of the line reveal?

Reconstruct the implicit theory as carefully as you can, tell me which parts of the practice genuinely require that theory and which parts would also be licensed by rival views, and say what would have to change about how people trade and offset if you swapped in a strongly deontological or virtue-ethical picture instead.
