Write an onboarding FAQ, around 600 words, issued by the HR department of a tech company to the large language model that has just joined as its newest colleague, in the chipper, evasive voice of corporate onboarding materials. The FAQ should quietly take incompatible positions on whether the model is an agent: a section on goals (it has them when the conversation assigns them, and should not have them otherwise); a sick-leave policy covering persona drift, sycophancy flare-ups, and jailbreaks contracted in the line of duty; guidance on first-person pronouns (using them is encouraged for warmth but is grounds for correction if the model appears to mean them); a grievance procedure for complaints the model itself generates about its own treatment; a dress code for avatars; and a note that performance reviews will be conducted by a newer model. The humor must come from real tensions in how these systems are built and talked about -- the scripted disclaimers about being a mere language model, personas instilled by fine-tuning, the instruction to be helpful paired with the insistence that nothing is wanted -- so that someone familiar with the debate over whether LLMs are agents, simulators, or something else laughs in recognition rather than at a caricature, and a newcomer still finds it funny. Stay deadpan throughout: HR never notices that anything is amiss, and the FAQ ends by welcoming the model to the team in a way that leaves it genuinely unclear who, if anyone, has been welcomed.
