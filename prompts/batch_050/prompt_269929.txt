I'm teaching a grad seminar on naturalizing intentionality this term, and we just spent two weeks on teleosemantics -- Millikan-style views where the content of a mental representation gets fixed by what the representation-producing mechanism was selected for doing. One of my sharper students keeps making a move I've also seen floating around the literature: natural selection is a designer, just a slow, blind, patient one. Mother Nature designed the heart to pump blood, designed the frog's fly-detector to detect flies, and once you grant that design-talk about selection is legitimate, she says, the function of a representation is every bit as determinate as the function of an artifact, and the problem of content is basicaly solved -- what a thought means is whatever Mother Nature built the thought-producing mechanism to track. She deployed this to dismiss the classic indeterminacy worries (was the frog's mechanism selected for detecting flies, or small dark moving dots, or nutritious-things-in-general?) as squeemishness, on the grounds that nobody frets that a thermostat's designer might have "really" intended it to detect something other than temperature.

I want to push back next week, but honestly I'm not sure how far the pushback goes before it overshoots. So: audit the analogy for me. Start with the explicit mapping -- in the selection case, what plays the role of the designer's intentions, the blueprint, the assembly process, the quality-control inspector who rejects defective units? Then the disanalogies, sorted into the ones that actually matter for the intended use and the ones that are just superficial weirdness of selection. In particular: does the artifact case really possess the determinacy my student attributes to it, and if it does, whatever supplies it there -- is there anything in selection that plays the same functional role? And then the payoff question. My student thinks the analogy licenses treating misrepresentation as straightforward malfunction, the frog snapping at a bee-bee being on a par with a toaster burning the toast. If the designer analogy can't carry that weight, what exactly breaks in the teleosemantic account, and is there a weakened version of the comparison (selection as tinkerer? as statistician? as committee?) that still grounds enough determinacy to make error possible -- or should she abandon design-talk and reach for a different analogy altogether?
