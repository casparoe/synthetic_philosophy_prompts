I'm a PhD student in population ethics, and I'm about to circulate the following list of commitments to my dissertation committee as the normative core of my prospectus on scarce-resource allocation in health. Before I do, I'd like an audit of whether the set is jointly tenable.

(1) When resources are scarce, cost-effectiveness analysis in terms of QALYs is the right framework for triage: other things equal, allocate so as to maximize the QALYs gained.
(2) The fair-innings intuition is sound: younger patients deserve priority over older ones even beyond the greater QALY gains that treating them typically produces.
(3) Using disability weights is not discriminatory: counting a year gained by someone who will remain disabled as worth less than a year in full health is simply an accurate measurement of health benefit.
(4) The separateness of persons is a genuine moral constraint: burdens and benefits falling on different people cannot simply be pooled into a social total, and an outcome with a smaller total can be better because of where the benefits fall.
(5) Regulatory agencies are right to use a flat value of a statistical life, applying the same figure whether the person saved is 8 or 80.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say exactly what the conflict turns on and what the cheapest revision would be. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, given that I'd rather keep the prospectus roughly as it is than rebuild it from scratch.
