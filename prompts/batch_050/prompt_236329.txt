so i coordinate the volunteer mod team for a big general-interest community online (think mid-six-figure members, posts on everything from recipes to local politics). we've got about fifteen mods, different countries, several not native english speakers, and they're each making calls in like thirty seconds per reported comment. our one-line rule is "no slurs" and it is absolutely not working, because the fights are never about the easy cases. last month alone we had: someone quoting a slur from a 1960s court transcript in a thread about housing discrimination (to criticize it, clearly); a black member using a reclaimed form of a word about himself and his friends and then a pile of reports on him; a commenter who never wrote the word at all but described it as "the one that rhymes with..." and everyone knew exactly what was meant; and someone posting a news article where a politician said a slur, verbatim in the headline.

please write us an actual decision procedure a tired mod can run at 1am: the checks in order, what counts as a pass/fail at each step, and what action follows (remove, warn, allow, escalate to the senior team). it needs to be grounded in what linguists and philosophers actually know about how slurs work -- e.g. how they're not just "very rude words," why quoting one isn't automatically clean the way quoting other taboo words more or less is, why who the speaker is changes things for reclaimed uses -- but the mods themselves won't know any of that literature, so the theory has to survive translation into plain questions they can answer from the comment alone. be explicit about which distinctions you kept and which you dropped as too subtle to operationalize.

then run the procedure on the four cases above. at least one of them should come out with an answer my mods (or our users) will hate -- tell me which one and how to write the explanation for the appeal that'll get filed. finally, list the kinds of cases the procedure just can't settle and has to kick up to a human, and where you expect it to misfire even when followed correctly.
