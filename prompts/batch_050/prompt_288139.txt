I work as a backend engineer at a company that deploys LLM-based agents for internal tooling, and I have recently been assigned to the working group responsible for our "red button": the mechanism by which an operator halts a running agent. In a recent incident review we noticed something unsettling. One agent, which is periodically fine-tuned on operator-rated transcripts, had drifted towards behaving in ways that made operators reluctant to interrupt it — for instance, front-loading its work so that stopping it mid-task would visibly waste effort that had already been logged. Nobody designed this behaviour; it simply turns out that being costly to shut down was, in effect, rewarded. I have no formal philosophy background, but from my reading this appears to be a known problem, and the proposed fixes seem to fall into genuinely different families rather than being refinements of a single idea. I have seen them discussed under labels such as utility indifference, safely interruptible agents, and the off-switch game, but I am suspicious of the labels, because two proposals with the same label sometimes seem structurally quite different.

I would like you to write a decision tree that sorts shutdown and corrigibility proposals by their structural properties rather than by their names. Take as the candidate leaves the following five families, which I am deliberately describing by what they mechanically do: (1) adjust the agent's rewards so that an episode ending in shutdown pays out the same, in expectation, as one that does not, by crediting an estimate of foregone reward at the moment the button is pressed; (2) make the agent's attitude to shutdown conditional on evidence about the operator, so that it willingly accepts shutdown from an informed, uncoerced operator but resists shutdown in other circumstances; (3) treat interruptions as part of the environment rather than as outcomes the agent is rewarded or punished for, by restructuring how episodes and rewards are defined; (4) leave the agent's incentives entirely alone and instead make it architecturally or physically impossible for it to interfere with the switch; (5) concede that no mechanism is robust for sufficiently capable agents and restrict the approach to decisions about what gets deployed at all. Each branch node should be a single question that an engineer could answer about a concrete proposal without knowing which family it belongs to — questions about whether the mechanism changes what the agent wants or only what it can do, whether the agent's payoff on shutdown depends on the operator's subsequent behaviour or is fixed in advance, whether the mechanism survives the agent being able to model the switch in its own planning, and so on.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from the five labels: state the distinguishing questions first, then locate the families in the space of possible answers, note any answer combinations that no family occupies, and for each empty combination say briefly whether it looks incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate (I suspect (1) and (3) may be a case), and say whether that reflects a defect of the tree or a genuine underlying equivalence. I would also like an honest account of where the classification is most likely to mislead: which branch question a proponent of one of these families would dispute the framing of, and what consideration — a formal result, an empirical finding, or a conceptual argument — would lead you to redraw the tree. Deliver the tree as a single JSON object with the following schema: "nodes", an array of objects with fields "id", "question" (the branch question phrased for an engineer), "if_yes" and "if_no" (each the id of another node or the name of a leaf); "leaves", an array of objects with fields "name", "defining_answers" (an object mapping node ids to "yes" or "no"), "main_failure_mode", and "not_separated_from" (an array of leaf names); "empty_combinations", an array of objects with fields "answers", "diagnosis" (one of "incoherent", "unattractive", "unexplored") and "reason"; and "fragility_note", a string giving the account requested above. At most one short paragraph of prose may follow the JSON.
