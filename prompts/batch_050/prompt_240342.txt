I’m helping moderate a philosophy department Q&A with the author of a new book defending what she calls a modest cosmological argument. Her position is that the kalam argument is too hostage to disputed cosmology, but a contingency argument still works: even if the universe is past-eternal, the whole series of contingent concrete things would remain unexplained, so there has to be a necessary concrete being that grounds it. She insists this conclusion is deliberately minimal and does not by itself establish God. She’s also very polished at answering objections by saying either that an infinite regress postpones explanation rather than provides it or that the argument only claims to establish a necessary ground, not the classical God. I want a sequence of nine or ten questions that makes those moves progressively harder to use, not general questions about why she wrote the book.

Please include at least one question each on why a beginningless series could not be self-explanatory member by member; why the totality of contingent reality must have an external explanation rather than being a brute fact; whether positing a necessary concrete being is any less arbitrary than positing a necessary universe; and how she distinguishes dependence from temporal causation. Also include questions about quantum models that allegedly show something arising without an external cause, whether an actual infinite regress is metaphysically impossible or merely explanatory deficient, and why the necessary being has to be unique. For each question, add a follow-up tailored to the reply she is most likely to give. Near the end, force her to explain the gap between necessary ground and God: ask which further attribute, if any, the argument really supports—simplicity, immateriality, agency, unity, or necessity—and finish with a question asking what discovery or philosophical argument would make her abandon the view rather than merely revise it.
