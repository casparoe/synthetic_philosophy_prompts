I'm helping design the syllabus for an undergraduate seminar on animal minds, and I keep running into the same rhetorical move in the secondary literature and in student essays alike. When the question is whether some striking bit of animal behavior -- scrub jays re-hiding their caches only when a rival was watching, dogs displaying what owners call a guilty look after misbehaving, crows bending wire into hooks -- requires a genuinely cognitive explanation, someone inevitably says that Morgan's canon is just Occam's razor applied to minds: don't multiply mental entities beyond necessity, so if associative learning or some other "lower" mechanism could in principle produce the behavior, we should prefer that account. The claim is that canon and razor stand or fall together, and that rejecting the canon means rejecting parsimony itself.

Audit this analogy in detail. Lay out the correspondence explicitly: what plays the role of the razor, what counts as an "entity" being multiplied, what counts as "necessity" in each case. Then examine where the mapping strains. Consider, for example, whether preferring a less cognitively rich explanation is really an instance of ontological parsimony or something else -- perhaps a preference for mechanisms we already understand, or a lower prior on rich mental states, or a constraint on evolutionary continuity that cuts both ways (it posits novel discontinuities between us and other animals). Consider too whether the canon's own standard -- explain by the lower faculty whenever possible -- is even a parsimony principle rather than a methodological default with a different justification, and whether "simpler" tracks anything well-defined when one side posits a simple rule applied many times and the other posits a richer but unified representation. Feel free to use the examples above or supply better ones.

Reach a verdict: does the razor actually underwrite the canon, or is the analogy doing persuasive work the razor can't license? If the two come apart, say which one a careful comparative psychologist should keep, and what the defensible version of the canon looks like once the borrowed authority of Occam is stripped away.
