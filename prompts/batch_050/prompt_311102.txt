In a journal-club discussion, a colleague brushed aside my reservations about a speculative cosmological model by remarking that elegance is evidence: the model's tight mathematical structure was itself a reason to believe it, over and above the data. I suspect this slogan conflates several quite different claims. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses it might express? In particular, I'd like you to distinguish readings on which beauty raises a theory's posterior probability (say, via a simplicity-favouring prior, or an induction on past cases where the beautiful theory won) from readings on which elegance merely justifies investing scarce effort in developing and testing a theory; and I'd like you to consider whether 'elegance' picks out anything unified at all — few free parameters, symmetry, inevitability of the mathematics, unification of disparate phenomena — and whether the evidential story differs across these. For each precisification, please say what evidence would bear on it, whether it is plausible, and whether it would actually license my colleague's use of the slogan, which was to settle which model we should provisionally accept, not merely which to investigate.
