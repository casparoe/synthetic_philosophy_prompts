I'm putting together my position on machine minds for a reading group and want a consistency check before I circulate it. Here's where I've landed:

(1) Behavior is all we ever have to go on for other minds, humans included -- I infer my friends are minded from what they do and say, so I don't think machines should be held to a higher evidential standard than people are.

(2) Any system whose behavior is indistinguishable from a minded human's under sustained, adversarial, open-ended probing should be treated as having a mind. Behavior is the criterion, full stop.

(3) A Block-style lookup table -- a machine with every possible conversation pre-stored in a giant tree -- would not have a mind, even though its behavior would pass the test in (2) by construction.

(4) Whether today's chatbots are minded can't be settled by transcripts alone; it depends on what they're doing under the hood, and next-token prediction over text just isn't the kind of process that supports mindedness.

(5) Given the moral stakes, we should err on the side of attributing mind to systems at or near the behavioral bar -- falsely treating a mindless system as minded is much cheaper than falsely treating a minded system as an object.

Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do. For each real tension, say how severe it is, what exactly I'd have to give up to fix it, and what that fix costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Don't just flag everything -- I want to know wether the package survives mostly intact or whether something load-bearing has to go.
