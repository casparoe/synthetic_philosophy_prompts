I need a proposal, roughly one page, and I'm stuck turning a nagging worry into something that sounds like a project rather than a vibes post.

The worry: most discussion of superintelligent persuasion fixates on the AI convincing people of false things, or nudging them towards bad actions. But there's a gap nobody seems to work on. Suppose the system is scrupulously honest — every claim true, every caveat disclosed — and yet it's so much better than me at forecasting consequences, modelling my own psychology, and constructing arguments that the rational move, every time, is to just defer. Not because I'm being manipulated, but because trusting my own judgement over its output would be like insisting on doing long division in my head when there's a calculator. That seems like it hollows out something important even though nothing false is ever said, and I have no idea what norm such a system should follow instead. "Always tell the truth" clearly isn't sufficient; "refuse to argue for anything" is obviously absurd. So what does honest-but-overwhelming communication with a much weaker mind even look like?
