(1) My housemate's opening: in a universe like ours that keeps expanding forever, random thermal fluctuations will eventually produce vastly more lone conscious brains with fake memories than the ordinary evolved observers like us, so any cosmological model with that feature predicts we're almost certainly Boltzmann brains; but we plainly aren't (the world is coherent, our memories check out), so such models are empirically ruled out, or at least strongly disfavoured.

(2) Aaron replied that the "but we plainly aren't" step is illegitimate: if you were a Boltzmann brain, your perceptions and memories would be garbage, so the hypothesis that you're one is self-undermining rather than false -- you could never have evidence for it. A model isn't refuted just because, conditional on it, you can't trust your evidence; it just means "I'm a Boltzmann brain" can never be a rational belief, full stop.

(3) My housemate said that's precisely his point: a theory that makes the reliability of your own observations improbable defeats itself as a theory, since all our grounds for accepting it are observations, so it's rational to reject Boltzmann-brain-dominated models wholesale rather than shrug and say the question is unanswerable.

(4) Aaron's last move was that all the counting is bogus anyway: over infinite future time in an infinite multiverse you need a measure to compare "numbers" of observers, different measures flip which observer type dominates, so the Boltzmann brain problem is an artefact of measure choice, not an actual prediction, and physicists who treat it as a constraint on model-building are fooling themselves.
