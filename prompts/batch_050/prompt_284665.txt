So last weekend I was at a birthday dinner and two friends of mine, both of whom did some philosophy in college, got into this running argument about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility, and it never resolved. As best I remember the moves: Marcus laid out the argument -- roughly, everything you do flows from the way you are, mentally speaking, so to be truly responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, but you can't be responsible for the way you are without having already chosen to be that way, and any such choice would itself have flowed from how you already were, so you get a regress and true responsibility is impossible. Dana's first response was the standard compatibilist one: all "responsible" ever needs to mean is that the action came from your own values and deliberation rather than coercion or a brain tumor, and demanding that you be the ultimate origin of yourself is just setting an impossible bar nobody ever meant by responsibility in the first place. Marcus said fine, maybe the everyday practices survive, but what doesn't survive is desert -- the idea that anyone could truly *deserve* blame or punishment in the deep sense, and that's what ordinary people actually think is going on when they blame someone. Dana then changed tack and attacked the regress premise itself: she said the requirement "to be responsible for X you must be responsible for everything that produces X" is doing all the work, and it's false -- when someone's torn between motives and just decides, that decision can originate something genuinely new about them even if all the inputs came from outside, so self-formation doesn't need to go back infinitely, it just needs some choices that aren't already settled by your character. Marcus said an undetermined decision in that torn moment is just a coin flip happening inside you, which adds randomness rather than responsibility. And then Dana said something that actually made Marcus pause: that the argument proves too much, because you could run the exact same regress on rational responsibility for beliefs -- your reasoning flows from how your mind is, which you didn't choose -- so if it works, nobody is ever rationally responsible for believing anything either, including Marcus for believing the Basic Argument, and nobody treats that as a live threat. Marcus mumbled something about action and belief being different but I couldn't tell if he had an actual answer.

Anyway, they asked me to referee and I kept dodging because I genuinely can't tell who's winning. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right at each step, including whether the belief-regress objection is actually a good parallel or a sneaky non-parallel? Also, is there something both of them are missing -- like, is the fight really about the metaphysics, or are they just using "responsibility" in two different senses and talking past each other?
