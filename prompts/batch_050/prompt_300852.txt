I cover science and big ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm working up a feature on whether natural selection is a genuine explanation or a dressed-up truism — the old worry that "fittest" just means whatever survived, so the theory explains nothing. Instead of writing another survey of the philosophy of biology, I want to send a short sorting quiz to the working evolutionary biologists I'm interviewing and figure out where each of them actually lands on the fitness debate — the propensity interpretation, the statisticalist school, the causalist or "force" view, and so on — without ever using those terms, because most of them will never have heard them and I'd get garbage answers if I led with the labels.

So act as a philosopher of biology who has spent years on the tautology objection but also spends real time around experimentalists and knows how they talk at the bench. Take this seriously and think it through carefully: before you draft a single question, first work out the logical space yourself — what the genuinely independent axes of disagreement are, which combinations of commitments are even coherent — and only then design the instrument, so the questions come from the distinctions rather than from a list of famous names.

What I need: a quiz of six to eight questions, each built on a concrete scenario drawn from actual evolutionary practice rather than abstract principle. Some cases I'd like used in some form: measuring relative fitness in a long-term bacterial evolution experiment like Lenski's; antibiotic resistance evolving inside one patient over the course of treatment; a sterile hybrid like a mule, which does fine by every measure except offspring; a population where allele frequencies shift through pure drift and founder effects; and a pair of plants where the one that left more descendants plainly did so by dumb luck, say a storm flattened its neighbor. Each question should offer two or three answers phrased the way a biologist would actually phrase them in an interview. Then give me a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the positions, which must include at minimum the propensity or dispositional interpretation of fitness, the statisticalist view that selection is a population-level pattern rather than a cause, a causalist view on which fitness differences do real explanatory work, and the stubborn position that fitness is just realized reproductive success and nothing deeper is needed. For each leaf, give one sentence on the most awkward thing its holder has to swallow. Note any answer combination nobody seems to occupy and tell me whether that's because it's incoherent or just unfashionable, and flag any pair of positions my quiz won't actually be able to tell apart, so I don't overclaim in the piece.

The angle that matters to me: when a hospital says a resistant strain took over because it was fitter, did anyone explain anything, or just redescribe the outcome? Build the quiz so the answers bear on that. Also, throughout, keep clearly separated what is basically settled among people who work on this and what is genuinely contested — don't write as if one school has won when it hasn't, and if I have a wrong assumption baked in anywhere above, say so.

One formatting constraint from my editor's workflow, sorry: the response has to be plain continuous prose, like an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
