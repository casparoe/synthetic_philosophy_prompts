I'm one of five members of an independent wellbeing advisory panel for a small-ish European country's government (I'll keep it vague, the setup is what matters). Next month we have to issue a written opinion on a proposal the finance minister is keen on: making it an official national target to break into the top five of the World Happiness Report within ten years, with ministry performance partly assessed on how the country's average score on the Cantril ladder question moves. The minister's pitch is basically that GDP targets distorted policy for decades, so why not target the thing that actually matters and let the scoreboard keep everyone honest.

My own lean is that this is a bad idea, but I want the opinion to be honest rather than a hatchet job. The case for the target isn't stupid: voters do seem to care about the rankings, the ladder question at least asks people to evaluate their own lives rather than having an economist decide for them, and "focus the machinery of government on life satisfaction" could be a genuinely good forcing device. The case against has to grapple with what the ranking actually measures -- one self-report item, translated across languages, compared across countries with different norms about how to answer a 0-10 scale, plus reference effects (does a Finn answering the ladder use the same ladder as a Bulgarian?), hedonic adaptation, and the whole Goodhart's law worry that once the score is the target, ministries will optimise the score rather than anyone's life. There's also a prior question I keep circling: is the ladder even measuring happiness, as opposed to some mixture of contentment, status assessment, and willingness to say nice things to a pollster? Should a country even *want* to top these things?

Please draft the written opinion, roughly 800-1200 words, that I'll circulate before the vote. It needs: a clear bottom-line recommendation (endorse, endorse with amendments, or reject) with a concrete alternative the panel could propose in the same document; the reasoning, including a serious statement of the strongest argument on whichever side loses; and at least one specific amendment or safeguard the minister could adopt if he insists on proceeding anyway. Write it as a formal document the panel could actually adopt, not as an essay about the rankings.
