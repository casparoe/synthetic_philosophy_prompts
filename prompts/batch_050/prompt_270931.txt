so i've had this idea rattling around for a while and want someone to actually write it: the minutes of the quarterly meeting of a fictional Standards Commitee for the Alphabet, the body that rules, case by case, on what still counts as the letter 'a'. About 600-700 words, formatted as agenda items with motions, votes and resolutions, in the driest bureaucratic voice you can manage.

The cases on the agenda should be genuinely tricky ones, e.g.:

- a single-storey 'a' (the circle-with-a-tail sort everyone handwrites) applying to be recognised as the same letter as the double-storey printed one, over objections that it shares almost no parts with it
- an ornate blackletter 'a' accused of having drifted so far from the canonical shape that it's functionally illegible and therefore no longer an 'a' at all
- a doctor's handwritten squiggle where literally the only thing making it an 'a' is that the writer intended it as one and it sits between an 'n' and a 'd'
- a cursive 'a' whose loop never actually closes, with the committee deadlocked over whether a gap of 0.3mm disqualifies it
- a motion from the letter 'g', pointing out that nobody has ever agreed on what a 'g' looks like (single-storey, double-storey, spectacle-shaped) and yet everyone just gets on with it, so why the fuss

The humour should come from the committee unwittingly reproducing real moves from debates about what makes something a member of a category: one member proposes a tidy checklist of necessary-and-sufficient features which immediately fails on the very first case; an "exemplar" faction insists borderline shapes just be measured against the Times New Roman 'a' and be done with it; someone else argues it's all about the role the mark plays in the word rather than its shape; a pragmatist suggests they just ask readers whether they read it as an 'a' and is shouted down for circularity. Nobody should ever name a philosopher or a theory though — they're just bureaucrats doing their jobs.

Keep it deadpan the whole way through, no winking at the reader, and let at least one resolution pass on a narrow vote despite being transparently question-begging. End with the date of the next meeting and one unresolved case carried over.
