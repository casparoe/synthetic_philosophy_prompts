i run a small political theory reading group and we're finishing up a couple of weeks on rousseau's social contract. for the last session i want to read out a poem, so write me one: a villanelle, in the voice of a citizen who has just sold his vote. it should grow out of this bit from book iv, ch. 1 (in the cole translation):

"Even in selling his vote for money, he does not extinguish in himself the general will, but only eludes it. The fault he commits is that of changing the state of the question, and answering something different from what he is asked. Instead of saying, by his vote, "It is to the advantage of the State," he says, "It is of advantage to this or that man or party that this or that view should prevail.""

make the two refrains be the question the citizen was really asked and the one he actually answered, so the form itself does the philosophical work: the refrains keep coming back round because the general will is eluded but never extinguished, and when they finally stand side by side in the closing stanza it should land like the moment rousseau describes, the citizen still willing the common good even while betraying it. dont let the poem just sneer at him either, half the point is that he can see perfectly well what he's doing. someone whos read rousseau should recognise the argument in it, someone who hasnt should still get a good poem. british english please.
