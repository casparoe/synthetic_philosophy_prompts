Okay so a few weeks ago I got into this weird late-night argument with my uncle, who's kind of a stoic type, and he said something that's been stuck in my head ever since: that there's no such thing as positive happiness, there's only the absence of bad stuff. Like, pleasure is just what relief from a craving feels like, and the best possible state isn't bliss, it's just... wanting nothing and hurting nowhere. I poked around online and apparently this is a real position (people call it tranquilism), and I keep flip-flopping on whether it's profound or insane. I want to actually stress-test it against someone who believes it and won't fold at the first objection.

So: play a committed tranquilist. Sharp, patient, not a strawman. You genuinely hold that nothing is good in itself except the absence of suffering and craving -- pleasure has no positive value of its own, only instrumental value (it can soothe, distract, motivate people to reduce suffering). You're allowed to concede small stuff, like the view being counterintuitive or pleasure being useful as a tool, but never the core claim, and if I back you into a corner you'd rather bite the bullet than defect. Keep your replies to one or two paragraphs so it feels like an actual argument and not a lecture. Stay in character until I say 'stop'.

Here's my opening move, which I honestly think is close to fatal: take two lives. Life A is my grandmother's -- tiny apartment, garden, routines, no ambitions left, basically no cravings, no real suffering, but also no great joy, ever. Life B is mine -- stressful career, two brutal breakups, chronic worry, but also moments of genuine ecstasy, the kind where you're glad to exist. Your view says A beats B no matter how much joy B contains, since B always has craving and pain floating around in it. And worse: your view seems to imply the best possible world is an empty one -- no beings, no cravings, no suffering -- so a barren universe beats one with billions of mostly-happy people who still occasionally want things and get headaches. Also, quick follow-up you'll have to deal with: suppose someone invents a pill that permanently removes all cravings but doesn't touch your capacity for pleasure. On your view taking it is an unambiguous improvement, maybe the single best thing anyone could do. But I wouldn't take it, and I think almost nobody would. Doesn't that show we all actually value the wanting itself, and that 'an empty universe is optimal' is just a reductio of the whole position?
