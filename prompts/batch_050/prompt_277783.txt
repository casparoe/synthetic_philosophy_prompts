Next month I'm leading a session for my philosophy reading group (smart people, mostly no phil background) on the ontology of musical works — type vs token, performances, recordings, all that. I know the content reasonably well myself, so don't explain the material to me; help me plan how I should explain it. It's a 90 minute session but I want the core explanation done in about 40 with discussion after.

What I need from you:

- The order the ideas have to come in, and why. My guess is people need to feel the difference between "this performance of the ninth" and "the ninth" in their gut before any type/token vocabulary does anything for them, and that the score should be introduced as a deliberate decoy so the "the work is the notes on the page" idea gets its moment and then dies. But maybe that's backwards and the score comes later. Give me the dependency structure — what has to be in place before the next piece makes sense.

- The one carrying example. My instinct is a famous pop song and its studio recording, because everyone in the group experiences music through streaming and instinctively treats the recording as the song. But there's a trap baked in: for a lot of pop the recording arguably IS the work in a way that isn't true for a string quartet, and if I build on that I might be teaching the contested case as if it's the easy one. Should I use a classical work with several famous recordings instead, or run the pop case and use the trap itself as the pivot? What properties does the example need to have (multiple realizations people actually know? a notation? famous covers?) and what should it specifically lack?

- The misconception map. The ones I expect: the work is the score (so the work is made of ink and paper), the work is "the" performance (someone will ask which performance is the real ninth), the recording is just a photograph of the work (which gets shaky the moment studio editing enters), and a cover version is either obviously the same song or obviously a different one with no third option. For each one, tell me where in the sequence I should head it off and whether that's before or after I hand them the vocabulary.

- What to explicitly bracket and say I'm bracketing — authenticity of performance practice, whether works are created or discovered, the named theories of what the work's sonic content even is. I'm fine mentioning these exist without adjudicating them, but tell me which of them a beginner actually needs flagged vs which would just be showing off.

- One closing question I can put to the group that shows me whether they've grasped the distinction rather than just learned words for it. Ideally something where the wrong answers diagnose which misconception is still alive.

Last thing: tell me how confident you are in the plan overall, and single out the single point in it you're least sure of — e.g. if the ordering depends on a contested view about whether recordings are works in thier own right, say that out loud instead of smoothing it over. I'd rather know which step is doing contestable work.
