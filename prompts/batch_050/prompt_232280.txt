I'm a graduate student applying for a modest summer research grant (one term's worth of unfunded time, roughly ten weeks) and I need a one-page proposal. The project I have in mind starts from the classic case where a billionaire offers a fortune to anyone who, by midnight, intends to drink a mildly unpleasant toxin the following afternoon -- where drinking it is worth avoiding, keeping the money requires only having genuinely formed the intention, and the agent knows in advance that once midnight passes and the money is secured, they'll have every reason not to follow through. The standard debate I was raised on asks whether a rational agent can form the intention at all, given this transparent knowledge that they won't act on it. My sense, after writing a seminar paper on this, is that the debate has been conducted almost entirely on the billionaire's terms: everyone treats 'having the intention at midnight' as a binary state you either achieve or fail to achieve, and then argues about which. What I want to explore instead is whether treating intention as a graded, revisable, and partly temporally extended phenomenon -- closer to a plan maintained over time than a mental switch flipped at a deadline -- dissolves the puzzle in some readings and relocates it in others, and whether the billionaire case is actually probing our concept of intention rather than our theory of practical rationality. I'm genuinely unsure whether this reframing is illuminating or just relabeling the difficulty, which seems like exactly the right state for a proposal to be in.
