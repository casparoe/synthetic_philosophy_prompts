So I'm doing a humor piece for our ideas desk and I need a draft to work from. Write me a parody advice collumn, Dear Abby-style (give the columnist a fake name), where the columnist is a hardline eliminative materialist about beliefs and desires. Her considered position: folk psychology is a stagnant, failed theory, same boat as phlogiston and witch talk, "believes" and "wants" refer to nothing, and eventually we'll all just speak mature neuroscience instead. Have her answer three letters -- maybe a wife who thinks her husband resents her, a guy who can't decide whether to quit his job, and someone grieving a parent -- and the comedy has to come from the actual view, not a strawman. She keeps correcting the letter-writers' vocabulary, she's forced to gesture at a replacement vocabulary that doesn't exist yet ("whatever neural state turns out to play the resentment-role"), and she can't coherently write "believe me" or "I think you should leave him," which she keeps having to dodge in increasingly strained ways. Internal consistency is the whole brief: she can't quietly slip back into belief/desire talk, and where she does slip, the slip itself is the joke -- deadpan, no winking. The standard objections (self-refutation, the Moore-ish weirdness of "I believe there are no beliefs," the practical indispensability of folk psych) should get enacted rather than explained. Around 600 words, ending with her signature sign-off, which should be as much of a philosophical problem as everything else.
