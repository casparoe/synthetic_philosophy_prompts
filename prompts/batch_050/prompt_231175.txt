Specifically chapter XVI of Mill's Considerations on Representative Government, the one on nationality. He summarised Mill's position as roughly this: 'Mill says a nationality is basically a people bound together by blood, language and religion, and that these bonds naturally generate mutual sympathy and trust. Because representative government needs public opinion and trust to function, he argues free institutions are next to impossible in a multinational state. So he concludes every nationality has a right to its own government, borders should be redrawn to match the nations, and people who resist this on sentimental grounds are just confused.
