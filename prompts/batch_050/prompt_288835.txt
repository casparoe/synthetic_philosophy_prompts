so I'm a novelist, literary fiction mostly, and a friend of mine -- also a writer, we have this running fight -- hit me with an argument over drinks last month that I still can't put down. It ends with my job being a scam, so I have some interest in finding the flaw. Here it is as best I can reconstruct it.

(1) Legitimate persuasion works through the persuadee's own assessment of reasons: you hand them considerations, they weigh them, and the change in what they think or feel happens because they judge the considerations merit it.
(2) Fiction doesn't work like that. When a reader cries at a death scene, no weighing happens. The reader knows perfectly well the character never existed, their standing assessment is that nothing grievable has occured, and the grief arrives anyway -- sometimes against their will. That's the whole craft, honestly: making the feeling show up whether or not the reader signs off on it.
(3) So the emotional effect of fiction is produced by routing around the reader's assessment of reasons rather than through it.
(4) Producing a psychological change in someone by routing around their assessment of reasons, rather than through it, is manipulation. That's what separates manipulation from persuasion.
(5) Manipulating someone is presumptively wrong: it treats their agency as an obstacle to work around instead of a capacity to engage.
(6) So moving readers -- the thing novelists get paid for -- is presumptively wrong in the same way as an ad engineered to make you anxious.
(7) Which is absurd.

My instinct is the wobble lives between (3) and (4), since the "routing around" in (3) doesn't smell the same as the routing around in, say, a casino ad exploiting my loss aversion. But when I try to say what the difference actually *is*, I come up empty -- the mechanism description seems to fit both. Meanwhile (2) feels like just a fact about how good fiction works, so I'd hate to give that up, and (1) is close to the definition everyone starts from. If the argument is invalid, name the step and tell me why it looked fine. If it's valid, which premise is cheapest to drop, and what exactly do I pay for dropping it? Bonus: whatever diagnosis you land on, does it also acquit rhetoric and advertising, or does it convict them while saving novelists? I'm genuinely fine either way, I just want to know which world I live in.
