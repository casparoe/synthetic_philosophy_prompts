Settle something for me. I've been going back and forth with a friend about the procreation asymmetry -- the idea that it's wrong to deliberately bring someone into existence whose life would be bad (foreseeably full of suffering, say), but not wrong at all to decline to bring someone into existence whose life would be wonderful. The happy non-existent aren't deprived of anything, since there's no "they" to be deprived; the miserable existent can point to actual suffering. So creating the bad life is prohibited, skipping the good life is fine, maybe even just neutral.

My credence that the asymmetry is actually true -- a real feature of morality, not just a widely shared gut feeling -- is around 0.55. My friend is at like 0.95 and thinks I'm being obtuse.

Here's the case that's been bugging me, maybe use it as a reference point. A generation ship is planning its last expansion. Option A: build a habitat ring that will house 10,000 people over the coming centuries, all with decent lives -- clearly worth living, nothing spectacular. Option B: skip the ring and spend the resources making the current crew more comfortable. Now the twist: if they build the ring, a known quirk of the ship's antiquated gene-screening means exactly one of those 10,000 will be born with a condition causing chronic severe pain, untreatable on the ship, life still arguably net positive but barely. Intuitively that one bad life counts against building the ring, and the 9,999 decent lives count... for what, exactly? If they count for nothing, Option A starts looking downright wrong, which seems insane.

So give me your own credence that the asymmetry is true, defined as: there is a genuine moral reason (even a defeasible one) against creating a foreseeably bad life that has no counterpart reason in favor of creating a foreseeably good one. Decompose it: rough probabilities for the sub-questions it hinges on -- wether wrongness requires a victim who'd otherwise be better off, wether we can explain the badness of creating miserable lives without smuggling in impersonal value, wether "no reason to create the good" collapses into "a universe full of flourishing people is no better than an empty one," and if so whether that's a reductio or a bullet to bite. One number and one line of justification per sub-question, then show how they combine into your overall credence. Finally: my friend and I are 0.4 apart -- which sub-question do you think our disagreement most likely lives in, and what argument would move your number the most?
