I sit on the five-person management board of a private nature reserve (roughly 2,000 hectares, mixed forest and meadow, temperate climate — deer, wild boar, foxes, rabbits, the usual). We've been asked to rule on a proposal from our head ecologist, and it's fallen to me to draft the board's decision memo, so I need help thinking it through.

The background: the region is heading into what's forecast to be a severe drought summer followed by a hard winter, and the deer population is currently above what the habitat can carry even in a good year, because hunting was banned on the reserve a decade ago and the large predators are long gone. The ecologist's proposal has three parts: putting out water stations and supplementary feed through the drought and winter; a dart-delivered contraception program to bring the population down gradually over the next few years; and treating deer for ticks and parasites at the feeding stations. Her argument, roughly, is that the suffering involved in starvation, thirst, and chronic parasite loads is enormous, we have cheap and proven ways to prevent alot of it, and it would be perverse to withhold them just because the suffering is 'natural' rather than caused by us.

The opposition: two board members think intervening is hubris — feed a population through a bad year and you just get more animals alive to starve in the next one, a bigger crash later, more total suffering not less (they cite what happened after a feeding program here in the 90s, though the records are patchy). A third member has a more principled objection: our duty is to not harm animals, she says, not to rescue them from nature, and once you accept a duty to manage wild suffering there's no principled stopping point short of turning the whole reserve into a zoo. The ecologist replies that the contraception component is exactly what breaks the feed-now-starve-later cycle, and that refusing to act is itself a decision we're responsible for, not some neutral default.

Draft the decision memo. Pick a clear recommendation — approve the whole package, approve some parts and reject others, or reject it entirely — and give the reasoning. Whatever you recommend, take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously: if you approve, deal honestly with the feed-now-starve-later worry and with where the stopping point actually is; if you reject, deal with the point that the hunting ban and the absence of predators are themselves a massive ongoing intervention, so 'leaving nature alone' isn't really an available option here. One more thing I want the memo to address: should the three components stand or fall as a package, or is the moral and practical case for each genuinely different (feeding vs contraception vs parasite treatment seem to me to raise pretty different issues, but maybe I'm wrong)?

No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — just continuous prose, like something actually circulated by email before a board meeting.
