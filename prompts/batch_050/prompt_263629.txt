I'm putting together my position on population ethics for a seminar and want a stress test before I have to defend it. Here's what I hold:

(a) the procreation asymmetry: it's wrong to have a child you know would have a miserable life, but it's not wrong to decline to have a child who would have been very happy;
(b) a person-affecting principle: an act can't be wrong unless it makes things worse for someone who exists or will exist;
(c) human extinction through everyone voluntarily stopping reproducing would be a catastrophe, largely because of all the good lives that would never get lived;
(d) once your child exists you have strong special duties to them that you simply didn't have toward them before they were conceived;
(e) having a child is a way of benefiting that child -- giving them the gift of life, so to speak.

Which of these are actually in tension and which just look like it? Rank the real tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix for each would be, and tell me which single revision would get me the most consistency for the least cost. I'd rather you tell me one of these has to go than pretend they all fit.
