Write a satirical employee onboarding FAQ for a fictional firm whose business is unambiguously harmful -- say, a manufacturer of tear gas or a predatory lender -- in which the deadpan answers to anxious new hires' ethical questions reproduce, with complete sincerity, the standard moves for exculpating participation in wrongdoing: the counterfactual argument (if you declined the job, the harm would proceed unchanged under a different employee), the fungibility of your individual contribution within a division of labour so fine-grained that no single person's action is a difference-maker, the claim that refusing merely hands the salary to someone with fewer scruples and no greater effect, and the appeal to remoteness (you only do the scheduling, the accounts, the catering). The comedy should come from the handbook stating these arguments more cleanly and consistently than any real apologist would dare -- for instance, a cheerful note that the firm deliberately fragments every harmful act across at least a dozen employees precisely so that each can honestly report making no difference, and a reminder that the firm's exit door is always open, since replacement costs are low. Each entry should be internally consistent with the view it defends, so that someone who knows the complicity literature can name the argument each answer relies on, while a general reader simply finds the logic both impeccable and alarming. Format the piece as exactly five numbered questions with their answers, each question-and-answer pair running roughly a short paragraph, with no introductory or concluding text -- the FAQ should begin with the first question and end with the fifth answer.
