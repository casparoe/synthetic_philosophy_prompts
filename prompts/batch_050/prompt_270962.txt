I serve on a hospital's assisted-dying review panel, and for a unit on precedent-based reasoning I want my graduate students to reconstruct the principle our panel has implicitly been applying. I will give you the panel's verdicts on eight past cases (all names and details are invented composites), then two new cases. Your task is to work out what rule the panel seems to be running, recognize that the record underdetermines the rule, and predict the panel's verdicts on the new cases accordingly.

Past cases and verdicts:

1. A patient with terminal pancreatic cancer, prognosis under six months, full decisional capacity, repeated and consistent requests, suffering primarily from severe physical pain. Approved.
2. The same clinical profile as Case 1, but the patient's adult children strenuously object and allege the request stems from guilt about being a burden. Investigation finds the request competent and settled. Approved.
3. A 25-year-old with severe treatment-resistant depression of six years' standing, judged to have full capacity, requesting death calmly and consistently for over two years. Denied.
4. A patient in a permanent vegetative state whose advance directive, written while healthy and never revoked, explicitly requests euthanasia should this condition arise. Approved.
5. A patient with early-stage dementia who still passes capacity assessments and requests euthanasia while lucid. Denied, with the panel noting that the prognosis was not yet advanced and the request could be revisited.
6. A terminally ill patient, now unconscious, with no advance directive; the family unanimously requests euthanasia on the patient's behalf. Denied.
7. A patient with locked-in syndrome after a brainstem stroke, not terminally ill in the sense of an imminently fatal prognosis, communicating unambiguously by eye movements, with a stable, documented request maintained for three years and suffering the panel described as unrelievable. Approved.
8. A terminally ill patient whose request the panel traced substantially to an inability to afford adequate palliative care. Denied, with referral to social services.

New cases:

A. A patient with advanced Alzheimer's disease who no longer has decisional capacity, whose advance directive requests euthanasia upon reaching precisely this stage, but who now appears content and resists nothing, while the medical team confirms the directive's terms are met and the condition will be fatal within roughly two years.

B. A patient rendered quadriplegic by a spinal cord injury five years ago, not terminally ill (life expectancy near-normal with care), fully competent, psychiatrically screened repeatedly, whose request has been unwavering for four years and who describes their condition as an intolerable life they did not choose and cannot be talked out of.

What I want from you: first, articulate at least three distinct rules that are each consistent with all eight past verdicts (do not propose a rule that any past verdict falsifies). Second, for each new case, state what verdict each rule predicts, noting where the rules diverge. Third, give your best guess as to the panel's actual verdicts on A and B, with reasons drawn from the pattern of the record — which precedents you take as load-bearing, which you take as noise. Fourth, describe one further hypothetical case that would discriminate most efficiently between the surviving candidate rules, and say what each verdict on it would establish.

Deliver your analysis in two parts. First, your reasoning in prose, no more than 900 words. Second, a JSON object with this schema:
