I've been convinced for a few years now of roughly the Bauman thesis about the Holocaust: that it wasn't a breakdown of modern civilization but one of its products -- bureaucratic division of labor, instrumental rationality, distance between decision and execution, the social-engineering mindset applied to populations. The barbarism reading (civilization's thin veneer cracking) strikes me as the comfortable story and Bauman's as the harder truth. What I want from you is a serious map of what would count against this view.

Concretely: which historical findings or comparisons would actually be decisive, and which would just be awkward? Some candidates I can see but can't weigh well -- the fact that Rwanda's genocide ran on machetes, radio, and face-to-face killing with barely any bureaucracy; that plenty of hyper-bureaucratic states never produce genocide at all (so is "modernity made it possible" just unfalsifiable unless you can say when bureaucracy leads where?); the intentionalist/functionalist historiography question of whether the actual process on the ground (especially the Einsatzgruppen phase, where killing was up close and messy) matches Bauman's clean picture of mediated, distanced execution; and Goldhagen-style arguments that ordinary Germans' willingness points at an old eliminationist antisemitism rather than a modern structure. Also: does the thesis secretly depend on a uniqueness claim, and if so what happens to it if the Holocaust turns out to be genuinely comparable to colonial or other genocides? That was live in the Historikerstreit and it never really got settled.

For each defeater, tell me whether a committed Baumanian would concede it counts against the view or dismiss it (and whether the dismissal is legitimate or just a protective qualification -- I have a nagging suspicion that "necessary conditions, not sufficient" does a lot of insulating work). Rank things from fatal to merely embarassing. And if the honest answer is that the thesis is framed so loosely that nothing could dislodge it, say that plainly and tell me whether that's fatal for a claim of this kind or just the normal condition of a big interpretive thesis.

One more thing: for each claim you make, flag how confident you are in it, and at the end single out the single point in your whole answer you're least sure of.
