My rough position: (1) an AI system deceives a user when its output is likely to cause the user to form a false belief, (2) producing that kind of effect has to be part of what the system was shaped or selected for at *some* level -- training data curation, fine-tuning, reward modeling, or even the deployer's decision to ship it -- so a pure random glitch isn't deception, and (3) honesty requires more than merely not deceiving: the system shouldn't misrepresent what it is (a chatbot that says "as an AI I have no opinions" and then two sentences later confidently states an opinion is being dishonest on my view, even if every factual claim it makes is true). I deliberately define deception broadly instead of talking about lying, because lying seems to need assertion and I'm not sure LLMs assert anything.
