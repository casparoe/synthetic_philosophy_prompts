A colleague and I keep circling the same fight. My credence that the two-factor theory of monothematic delusion -- roughly, that something like Capgras or Cotard requires both (i) an anomalous experience or aberrant prediction-error signal and (ii) a separate, dissociable deficit in belief evaluation -- is the basically correct framework is about 0.55. Hers is 0.9. Now the part that actually divides us: imagine it were shown, in really careful studies, that some florid Capgras patients have completely intact belief evaluation on every probe we can devise -- they reject unrelated implausible hypotheses at normal rates, update fine on other matters, spot contradictions when the impostor-claim isn't involved. Does that kill the two-factor theory, or just relocate the second factor somewhere subtler (say, domain-specific, or in the felt authority of the experience rather than in evaluation at all)?

Give me your own credence that two-factor theory, flexibly interpreted, is the right framework for the monothematic delusions, and decompose it: a rough probability for (a) an anomalous experience being necessary, (b) one-factor / prediction-error-only accounts sufficing, (c) delusions being genuine beliefs at all rather than some hybrid state, and say how (c) bears on (a) and (b). Then tell me which sub-question my colleague and I most likely actually disagree about, and what single empirical finding would move your number the most.
