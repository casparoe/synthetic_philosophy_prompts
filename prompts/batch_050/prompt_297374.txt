I’m a journalist who covers science and big ideas, and I’m drafting a column arguing that collapse is a sloppy word. Before I commit to the argument, can you audit my position rather than write the column for me?

My working commitments are:

1. Collapse means a rapid, substantial loss of collective capacity—state capacity, coordination, exchange, or information transmission—not necessarily mass death or the disappearance of a people.
2. Resilience means preserving core functions through a shock, not just surviving biologically.
3. Failure is justified only when a society had a realistically available adaptive option but its institutions prevented it from taking that option. Otherwise, I should call the episode transformation or adaptation.
4. A society shouldn’t get credit for resilience merely because descendants, languages, or cultural practices survived.
5. The Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean counts as a collapse despite substantial cultural and demographic continuity, because palace administration and long-distance exchange broke down.
6. The Classic Maya case shouldn’t be called a civilizational collapse, because Maya peoples and traditions continued, although the southern lowland political order did collapse.
7. Rapa Nui should count as a failure on the standard ecological-overshoot account: even if no policy could have guaranteed sustainability, its status economy kept raising the cost of adaptation.
8. I want a basicly operational test that lets me classify cases without litigating inaccessible counterfactuals or making a contestable value judgment.

Tell me whether these hang together. Put the genuine tensions in order of severity; for each one, name the exact commitments involved, explain why they conflict, and give the cheapest revision plus what it costs. Also identify any apparent tension that dissolves once the right distinction is made. Finish by naming the single revision that would clean up the most. Don’t turn this into a history survey, and don’t assume I already know which commitment I’d abandon.

Maximum 700 words, in American English.
