I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and at a conference an AI founder definately walked me into an argument I can't shake. I want to write about whether minds can run on non-brain stuff, but he says functionalism + multiple realizability proves way too much. Tell me where it goes wrong, if it does, and what rejecting that step costs. Please answer in American English.

His version, cleaned up: (1) If functionalism is true, a mental state like pain is fixed by a causal/functional role: the pattern of inputs, outputs, and internal transitions, not by carbon, neurons, or wetware. (2) If multiple realizability is true, that same role can be instantiated by brains, silicon, organoids, maybe even a huge distributed system if the organization is right. (3) Roles have to be stated at some level of abstraction, and at a sufficiently abstract level you can map almost any complicated physical system onto almost any finite machine table: a weather front, the US power grid, a swarm of drones, traffic in Atlanta. (4) So unless we say only the brain's exact microphysics counts, we have to allow that the grid or the weather could be in pain whenever a clever enough mapping exists. (5) But pain cannot depend on how clever the interpreter is; otherwise every system is in pain under some description and the word stops doing work. (6) The only non-cheap way out is to demand the real causal organization match more tightly: right timescales, right counterfactuals, right architecture, not just a mapping after the fact. (7) Once you demand that, though, the silicon case starts looking fishy too, because chips have different failure modes, timing, noise, energy use, and lower-level causal structure than neurons. (8) So functionalism either collapses into everything-is-conscious triviality, or it smuggles back biological chauvinism and kills multiple realizability. He calls this the realization trap.

I don't buy the conclusion, but I also can't just hand-wave the grid-pain thing. Which premise or inference should I push on, why did it look legit, and what do the main rescue moves cost? I'd most like to keep both (a) no migraines in the power grid and (b) some real possibility of non-biological minds. If I can't keep both, say which one gets pricier to defend.
