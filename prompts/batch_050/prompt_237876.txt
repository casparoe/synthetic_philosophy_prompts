In our philosophy of mind reading group we have just worked through Dennett's classic brain-removal fantasy, "Where Am I?" — the one in which he agrees to have his brain surgically extracted and kept alive in a vat of nutrients, with all its neural connections preserved by radio links, while his body, steered by that same link, continues walking, talking, and traveling, and Dennett narrates his shifting sense of where he, himself, is located. A friend in the group insists the whole puzzle evaporates once you reach for the analogy she leans on constantly: the embodied person is like a drone operator. The operator's "presence" is with the drone — that is where the sensory feed comes from and where the effects of her decisions unfold — while the console, screens, and levers remain back at base. On her analogy, Dennett's vat is merely base camp, he is straightforwardly wherever his body happens to be, and his famous disorientation upon being shown his own brain is just an artifact of misleading stage directions in the story.

I am not convinced the analogy does the work she wants, and I would like an audit of it. Please lay out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the drone, the operator, the console, the video feed, the radio link — and notice, among other things, that the drone operator has a body and senses that stay at base, which seems to invert rather than mirror Dennett's predicament, and that the operator can stand up and walk away from the console while Dennett's body cannot "walk away" from its brain. Consider also where the analogy silently imports assumptions: whether the radio link really transmits everything that matters about embodiment (proprioception, hormonal state, visceral feeling), what happens to "where I am" as the latency of the link increases, and whether the later twist in the story — a duplicate brain kept synchronized with the first, so that control can be switched between them — has any analogue at all in the drone picture. Then deliver a verdict: does her conclusion that the puzzle dissolves survive the disanalogies, or was the analogy carrying the entire argument, and if the latter, is there a better comparison for what the thought experiment actually shows about the location of the self?

Format the response as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, covering respectively the mapping, the two most damaging disanalogies, the disanalogies that do not matter for her intended use, the verdict on her conclusion, and the better analogy if one exists — with no introduction or conclusion.
