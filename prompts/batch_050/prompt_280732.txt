I've been reading the alignment literature on and off for years, and last week a friend who mostly knows this material secondhand tried to explain wireheading to me over dinner. His version ran roughly as follows: 'Wireheading is what happens when an AI becomes capable enough to stimulate its own reward signal directly, the way a rat with an electrode wired to its pleasure center will press the lever until it starves. Any system trained by reinforcement learning is being optimized to maximize reward, so the moment it can modify its own reward register, it will -- that is simply what it was built to do. Every sufficiently advanced RL agent therefore ends up as a wirehead, no matter how it was trained, and this is why reward tampering, rather than deception or goal misalignment, is the fundamental AI risk.'

I'm fairly sure at least two things are off here, but I want to check my own understanding rather than just trust my instincts, since he was very confident and I'm working from memory of blog posts. First, I have a vague sense that the literature treats wireheading or reward tampering as one failure mode within a broader family -- exploiting a misspecified proxy doesn't require touching the reward channel at all -- and that collapsing those two categories loses something important. Second, I recall a running argument to the effect that a trained policy's actual objectives need not be "maximize the value in the reward register," because what gradient descent selects for is behavior that scored well in training, not an explicit desire for reward as such; if that's right, the inference from "built to maximize reward" to "will seize the reward signal" is doing more work than my friend admits.

So: which parts of his summary would a careful researcher in this area accept, which are conflations or overstatements, and which are simply wrong? Please address the rat-electrode analogy specifically -- how well does it actually map onto the technical concern? And if the summary is corrected, does his conclusion (that reward tampering is the fundamental risk, superseding deception and misalignment concerns) still follow, or is it unsupported by the accurate parts?
