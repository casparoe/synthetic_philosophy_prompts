So I was at this writing retreat in Vermont last month and two of the other novelists got into an argument that's still bugging me, and I want a referee. Mara writes historical fiction and she uses an AI chatbot for a lot of her research questions -- stuff like how long a letter took to cross the Atlantic in the 1840s, what a scullery maid would have been paid, that kind of thing. She says she double-checks anything plot-critical but lets the small stuff ride. Dane, who writes thrillers and is kind of a purist about research, told her flat out that she doesn't actually *know* any of those things. His line was that she's just repeating output, and if the AI confabulates something she won't catch it, because she no longer has the background feel for the period that lets a researcher smell a wrong answer. Mara came back that this is just how testimony works: nobody verifies their doctor's medical degree or re-derives the chemistry behind the pills they take, and believing reliable sources is how anyone knows anything beyond what they've personally seen. Dane said the difference is that doctors and historians are accountable -- there are institutions that check them and careers that end if they lie -- whereas the AI has nobody on the hook for it, so her dependence isn't on a source at all, it's on nothing. Mara shot back that reliability doesn't require accountability, since her weather app isn't accountable to anyone and she still knows it's going to rain. Then Dane made what I thought was his best move: he said she can test the weather app against the actual sky tomorrow, but she can't test 1840s postal schedules against anything she has access to -- and worse, the AI is most likely to be wrong exactly on the obscure stuff nobody can easily check, so her method fails precisely where it matters most. Mara's counter was that by that logic nobody can know any history at all, since the past can't be checked against the sky either, so he'd proven too much.

Can you go through the exchange move by move and tell me who's right about what -- not just who wins overall, but which individual points land and which don't? I'm especially stuck on the weather app reply, because it feels like it should work and also feels like it dodges something. And if there's something both of them are missing -- some distinction that would dissolve part of this, or some real cost of her workflow that neither of them has quite named -- I'd love to hear that too. No need to pick a winner for the sake of it; if it comes out a split decision with one side more right on the core question, say that.
