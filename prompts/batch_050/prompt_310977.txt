So for my sociology class final project (11th grade) I'm doing the "gender equality paradox" thing — how countries that rank highest on gender equality like Norway and Finland apparently have MORE gender-stereotyped career choices, like fewer women going into engineering than in less equal countries. My teacher said instead of just writing an essay I should make an interactive decision tree for the class website where people answer questions and get sorted into which explanation of the paradox they actually buy.

Can you build me that tree? The catch is the questions have to sort people by what they actually think, not by which label sounds good to them. So no question like "do you believe in biology or culture?" — instead each node should be a concrete fork, like: if girls in rich countries are just freer to pick what they genuinely enjoy, would you expect the same pattern for career picks where the pay gap runs the other way? or: if the paradox disappeared tomorrow under better measurement, would anything about your explanation survive? Stuff where the different camps really do come apart.

The positions I want as leaves (you can rename them if the names are clunky but keep them recognisable):
- the "people are just choosing what they like" view (freedom lets pre-existing gendered preferences show up more, not less)
- the evolutionary/biology-heavy view
- the view that socialisation is still doing the work, just in a subtler form, and wealthy countries can afford the luxury of performing gender
- the statistical-discrimination one (women avoiding fields where they expect a bad time, which gets worse as options open up elsewhere)
- the methodological skeptics who think the paradox is partly a measurement artifact and shouldn't be explained at all until its replicated better
- at least one leaf for "unnamed combo" — an answer pattern the tree generates that nobody really defends, with one line on whether that slot is empty because the view is incoherent or just unpopular

Build the tree from the questions first (what answers are even possible), then locate the positions in that space. And flag any two positions your questions can't actually separate instead of pretending they can.

Format: give me the whole thing as a JSON object I can hand to my friend who's doing the website, with these fields:
- "nodes": a list where each node has "id", "question" (the concrete fork, phrased for a normal person), "branches" (each branch has "answer" and "leads_to" pointing to a node id or leaf id)
- "leaves": a list with "id", "position_name", "core_claim" (one sentence), "awkward_commitment" (one sentence on the weirdest thing a defender has to swallow), "occupied" (true/false)
- "unseparated": a list of pairs of leaves the tree can't tell apart, with a note on what extra question might split them

Keep the tree small enough that someone finishes it in like 6-8 questions max.
