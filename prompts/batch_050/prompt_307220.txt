I’m on a small university AI-reading group, and embedded agency has become annoyingly relevant because we’re trying to model a very simple toy system whose controller, sensors, goals, and environmental model are all physically implemented inside the world it acts on. Half the group thinks standard decision theory is mostly fine as an idealization: you can treat the agent as choosing from outside, then add implementation constraints later. The other half thinks that approach misses the core problem: the “choice” is itself another event in the causal process the model is supposed to describe. I want you to draft the joint statement that these two camps could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration.

Call the camps the Cartesian-agent camp and the embedded-agent camp. Give me the claims both would accept about what an agent model must handle, where conventional decision-theoretic formalisms are useful, and why self-modeling is not automatically magical or incoherent. Then state their remaining disagreements in wording each side could endorse for itself. In particular, cover the separation between agent and world, whether an agent can have a complete model of a world containing itself, whether “I choose A” can be treated as a fixed object inside the model rather than an output whose provenance has to be explained, and whether the embedded-agent camp is proposing a rival theory or just adding engineering constraints. I suspect some of the dispute is verbal, so please identify any disagreement that turns on using “agent,” “model,” or “choice” differently.

Use a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table, then a section of cruxes. For every crux, give a concrete toy case or argument both camps would accept as relevant, and record each side’s prediction in advance. Good candidates include a prediction program that must model a copy of itself, a robot replacing one of its own reasoning modules, and a simplified Newcomb-style case where the predictor reads the physical implementation rather than the agent’s stated deliberation. Don’t adjudicate who wins overall; the point is the most constructive document both sides could actually sign. Maximum 900 words.
