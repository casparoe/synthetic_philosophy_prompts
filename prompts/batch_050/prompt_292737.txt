I've been going in circles on a question and I want help figuring out whether my own instincts even hang together. The question is basically "what is intelligence," but I don't want a taxonomy of the literature, I want someone to reverse-engineer me. Here are my verdicts on a bunch of cases, stated as honestly as I can. A chess engine that demolishes every human on the planet but couldn't learn checkers if its life depended on it: I want to say not intelligent, or only in some thin technical sense. An octopus in a lab that works out how to open a screw-top jar it's never encountered, and does it differently the second time: definately intelligent, no hesitation. A thermostat: obviously not, that's the easy one. A slime mold that finds its way through a maze to food: I say no, but if you press me I can't explain what it lacks that the octopus has, other than being goo. One of the big language models that writes fluent essays and passes professional exams but will also invent facts with total confidence: I catch myself calling it intelligent in casual conversation and then immediately feeling like I've been conned. An ant colony that finds short paths, farms aphids, and fights organized wars, even though each individual ant is basically running on pheromones and three rules: weirdly, I'm happy to call the colony intelligent even though no ant is. And my friend who is genuinely brilliant at math and has made nothing but catastrophic life decisions for a decade: clearly intelligent in one sense, but something important seems to be missing.

So here's my ask. My tentative principle is something like "flexible, goal-directed behavior in situations the system wasn't built or trained for." That handles the octopus and the thermostat and gets the chess engine right, but I'm pretty sure it also convicts the slime mold, and possibly convicts the colony while acquitting the language model, which is backwards from what I actually believe. Is there a principle, or a small set of them, that captures most of my verdicts? Which of my verdicts would a coherent principle force me to abandon, and which one would you abandon if you had to pick? And tell me what your principle says about cases I didn't list: a self-driving car, a dog, a human infant, an immune system.

Two requests about how you answer. First, please be straight with me about where the field actually stands: separate what's basically settled among people who study intelligence (in whatever discipline) from what's still genuinely contested, and don't dress up one camp's position as the consensus if it isn't. I'd rather hear "nobody agrees about this part" than a tidy story. Second, no formatting at all: no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables. Just continuous prose, like an email from a friend who happens to know this stuff cold.
