For a reading group on the logic of paradox I have been asked to circulate my commitments in advance so the others can probe them. Here is where I currently stand, and I would like an audit of whether the package hangs together before I defend it:

1. Truth is fully transparent: every instance of the T-schema is true, including those built from the Liar sentence, and the Liar itself is both true and false. I am not willing to restrict the schema or to stratify the language.
2. Explosion is invalid, and I am emphatically not a trivialist: although some contradictions are true, most sentences are simply true or simply false, and not everything follows from a contradiction.
3. True contradictions are exceedingly rare — confined to self-referential and semantic edges of the language. The actual world is consistent almost everywhere, and my view is meant to be a local repair, not a claim that inconsistency is rampant.
4. Classical logic remains the right logic for all ordinary, non-paradoxical reasoning; paraconsistent machinery is needed only where paradox threatens, and elsewhere classical inference is recaptured intact.
5. A sentence's being just true — true and not also false — is a genuine status, which holds of almost all sentences, and any adequate theory of truth must be able to state that it holds.
6. I accept unrestricted naive comprehension for sets: every condition determines a set of the things satisfying it, full stop, and the resulting inconsistent mathematics (Russell's set and the rest) is non-trivial and worth having.

Which of these are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and in each case what is the cheapest repair and what it would cost me? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and tell me which single revision would buy the most consistency for the least philosophical loss. Where you think a tension is merely apparent, show the resolution rather than just asserting it. Finally, please be careful to distinguish what is settled technical fact about these logics (what a given formalism does or does not validate, what is and is not derivable) from what remains genuinely contested among specialists — for instance, whether the commitments behind (5) can be expressed within the dialetheist's own framework at all — and do not present a contested position as though it were the consensus.
