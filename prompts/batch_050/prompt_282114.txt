I have been reading about the St. Petersburg game and tried to construct a variant of my own, and I have run into a tangle I cannot sort out. I would like a conceptual explanation, not just a calculation.

My game works like this. A fair coin is flipped until it lands heads. If the first heads occurs on toss n, the prize is (−1)^(n+1) · 2^n / n dollars: that is, +2 dollars if heads comes up immediately, −2 dollars if it first appears on toss two, +8/3 on toss three, −4 on toss four, and so on, with negative prizes being amounts I must pay.

Three questions.

First, when I write out the expected value term by term I get 1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − 1/4 + ..., which I believe converges to a familiar constant. Yet I gather the expectation of this game is said to be not that constant, nor anything else. Please explain why the expectation is undefined rather than equal to that sum — what the operative criterion is, and why it fails here while holding for the original St. Petersburg game, whose expectation is infinite but apparently well-defined in a way mine is not.

Second, a friend claims the undefinedness is an artifact of how I happened to write the series down. His proposal: sum the terms in the order the payoffs would actually be received, namely by the toss on which the run ends, and the expectation comes out perfectly determinate. Is there any mathematically respectable principle that privileges one ordering of the terms, and if not, what exactly is wrong with his fix?

Third, consider a second game identical to mine except that every prize, positive or negative, is one dollar higher. In every possible outcome it pays strictly more than my game, so it seems any rational agent must prefer it. But if neither game has an expectation, expected-utility reasoning appears unable to deliver even that verdict. Please explain precisely where the framework breaks: can state-by-state dominance do the work on its own, without expectations, and what does this case show about which comparisons expected-utility theory can and cannot underwrite?

In your answer, please distinguish what is settled mathematics from points where people who work on these problems genuinely disagree about the diagnosis or the remedy, and do not present a contested position as the consensus. Please keep the whole answer under 650 words.
