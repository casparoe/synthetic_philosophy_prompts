You are a historian of economic thought with a specialization in eighteenth-century political economy, and in particular in David Hume's essays on monetary and commercial matters (Of Money, Of the Balance of Trade, Of Interest, and Of Public Credit). I want you to undertake a careful counterfactual exercise, and I want it disciplined rather than free-associative.

Imagine that Hume, having just completed those essays, is transported to the present day and given an accurate, neutral briefing on the modern monetary order. The briefing covers: fiat currencies issued by central banks rather than commodity money; the conduct of monetary policy through interest-rate setting and large-scale asset purchases (quantitative easing), including the episodes following 2008 and 2020; the widespread adoption of explicit inflation targets around two percent; the Eurozone, a monetary union among sovereign states with no full fiscal union; the United States dollar's role as the principal global reserve currency, alongside persistent American trade deficits; and the recent episode of sharply elevated inflation in 2021–2022 following rapid monetary and fiscal expansion.

Your task is to reconstruct, as faithfully as the textual basis allows, Hume's analysis and policy advice on the following four questions:

1. Does his quantity-theoretic reasoning survive the transition from specie to fiat money intact, and would he regard the 2021–2022 inflation as a vindication of it, a complication for it, or neither?
2. Hume famously held that a gradually increasing money supply can stimulate industry in the interval before prices adjust. Would he therefore endorse a deliberate, permanent policy of mild positive inflation, or do his other commitments — concerning creditors, fixed incomes, and the public debt — cut against that conclusion?
3. Would he see a monetary union like the Eurozone as recreating the self-correcting mechanism he described for international trade imbalances, or as destroying it? Where in his framework would the absence of national currencies make a difference?
4. His argument against the mercantilist anxiety to hoard money assumed that an outflow of money is self-limiting. Does that argument still apply to a country that issues the world's reserve currency and can run deficits for decades, or is this a case his mechanism never anticipated?

For each question, proceed in three steps: first identify which specific Humean doctrine is doing the work; then state the verdict you believe he would reach and why; then say honestly where the translation from his eighteenth-century assumptions to modern institutions strains — where you are extending him beyond anything he wrote rather than merely applying him. Note also any place where you think he would have revised his own views in light of modern facts, rather than simply pronouncing on them.

Before composing your answer, briefly brainstorm the relevant doctrines and tensions so that the structure of your reasoning is visible. Think carefully and resist the temptation to make Hume a mouthpiece for a modern school of thought. Conclude with an epistemic accounting: indicate how confident you are in each of the four predicted verdicts, distinguish what is well-grounded in the texts from what is extrapolation, and single out the single point in your reconstruction about which you are least sure.
