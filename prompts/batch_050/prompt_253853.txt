I've been reading Overcoming Bias, LessWrong, and the ACX sphere for well over a decade, so I absorbed Parfit's non-identity problem the way most people in this community do -- as a piece of ambient background knowledge that gets invoked whenever population ethics or longtermism comes up. I recently got into an argument with a friend about climate policy, though, and realized my grip on it is shakier than I thought. Her claim was the standard person-affecting one: a policy that trades away long-run environmental quality for short-run growth doesn't wrong the people of 2200, because had we chosen differently, different people would exist (different timing of conceptions, different pairings, and so on cascading outward), and you can't harm someone by an act that was a precondition of their existing at all. My day job involves a lot of causal inference -- DAGs, do-operators, potential outcomes, Pearl's ladder from association up through intervention to counterfactuals -- and I kept trying to translate her argument into that vocabulary and finding that pieces of it went through cleanly and pieces of it didn't. I'd like that done carefully.

So: restate the non-identity problem, and the dispute between person-affecting and impersonal (totalist-ish) views of our obligations to future generations, in the vocabulary of causal inference, as precisely as you can. Treat a climate policy choice as an intervention; treat outcomes as potential outcomes under alternative treatment assignments; locate the concept of "harm" at the right rung of the ladder, since it seems to be a unit-level counterfactual claim (this person is worse off than they would have been under the alternative policy) rather than a comparison of population-level distributions. Tell me which parts of the philosophical dispute survive the translation intact -- my guess is that the observation that interventions change the identity of the resulting population, not just its welfare distribution, maps onto something real and important -- and which parts you had to distort or approximate to fit.

If that's right, I want to know what to conclude: is the residue evidence that the person-affecting intuition rests on metaphysics my framework has no use for (and so climate obligations should be cashed out impersonally, as comparisons of distributions), or is it evidence that causal vocabulary is missing something a moral theory genuinely needs?
