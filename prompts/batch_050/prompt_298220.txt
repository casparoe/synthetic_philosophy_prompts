Take the collapse of Long-Term Capital Management in 1998 and run it through the lens of Kelly betting and geometric rationality. Quick recap so we're on the same page: hedge fund stacked with famous quants and two Nobel laureates, makes convergence trades on bond spreads, levers up enormously (25-to-1 or more at peak), then Russia defaults, correlations go to one, spreads widen instead of converging, and the fund nearly drags the financial system down before the Fed brokers a bailout.

Here's what I want, specifically:

1. The mapping. In Kelly terms, what was LTCM actually doing? Treat each convergence trade as a bet with some estimated edge and variance, and say what full Kelly would have prescribed given *their own* beliefs about the inputs. Were they over-Kelly on their own numbers, or is the real problem that the numbers themselves were garbage -- estimated from a few years of calm markets?

2. Where the model breaks. Kelly assumes (roughly) known stable probabilities, repeated play, and that your bets don't change the game. Which of these did LTCM violate worst? I'm especially interested in the self-defeating part: once everyone knows your book and you're a forced seller, the game itself changes shape. Can Kelly even be *stated* for a player whose own liquidation moves the payoff distribution against him?

3. The time-average thing. Geometric rationality says maximize log growth because that's what the long-run time average of your wealth does. But LTCM's wealth process wasn't repeated draws from a fixed distribution -- it was regime-switchy, path dependent, and could hit an absorbing barrier. Does the log-optimal argument survive that, or does it quietly presuppose the stationarity it needs?

4. Both directions. Does the episode vindicate Kelly -- they'd have survived at half-Kelly with honest inputs, or whatever the right counterfactual is -- or does it show that geometric rationality is the wrong frame for any institution that can be forcibly liquidated? You don't get to keep playing after a margin call, so maybe long-run growth rate was never the right objective for a leveraged fund in the first place.

Dont force the framework to pronounce on things it can't see. If some part of the collapse (the bailout politics, the copycat unwind, the counterparty web) is just invisible from inside Kelly-land, say so explicitly instead of stretching the model to cover it.
