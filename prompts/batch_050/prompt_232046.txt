I'm putting together the final for my upper-level Nietzsche seminar and I'm definately dreading the usual stack of essays that just recite master vs. slave morality with a blond beast thrown in. So this year I want one essay question (they get 75 minutes) built around a passage I'm going to print at the top of the exam, from the Second Essay of Nietzsche's The Genealogy of Morals (§1, Horace B. Samuel translation, 1913):

"Forgetfulness is no mere vis inertiæ, as the superficial believe, rather is it a power of obstruction, active and, in the strictest sense of the word, positive—a power responsible for the fact that what we have lived, experienced, taken into ourselves, no more enters into consciousness during the process of digestion (it might be called psychic absorption) than all the whole manifold process by which our physical nutrition, the so-called "incorporation," is carried on. The temporary shutting of the doors and windows of consciousness, the relief from the clamant alarums and excursions, with which our subconscious world of servant organs works in mutual co-operation and antagonism; a little quietude, a little tabula rasa of the consciousness, so as to make room again for the new, and above all for the more noble functions and functionaries, room for government, foresight, predetermination (for our organism is on an oligarchic model)—this is the utility, as I have said, of the active forgetfulness, which is a very sentinel and nurse of psychic order, repose, etiquette; and this shows at once why it is that there can exist no happiness, no gladness, no hope, no pride, no real present, without forgetfulness."

The question should make them do three things at once, and it should be unanswerable by anyone who only skimmed a summary: (a) explain why it's philosophically loaded, not just purple prose, that Nietzsche casts forgetfulness as an active power and says there's no happiness or even a present without it — i.e., what work that does at the very start of an essay about promising and conscience; (b) press the tension that the Genealogy itself is one long act of remembering, digging up the cruelty the morality of custom trained us to forget — so what entitles a philosophy that praises forgetting to do all this remembering, and is there a way out that isn't just hand-waving; and (c) say what the digestive / oligarchic-organism language signals about where the Second Essay's account of the "memory of the will," guilt, and making man calculable is going to come from, and wether that naturalized framing lets Nietzsche escape merely moralizing about morality or just lands him in a sneakier version of it.

What I need from you: the exam question itself, worded the way you'd actually print it on the paper; then a model answer of about 500 words; then the marking scheme. The marking scheme should be exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, worth 4 marks apiece (20 total), and each point should name both the specific move a top answer has to make and the one-line way students typically fumble it (e.g., treating the passage as decorative throat-clearing, or dragging in the First Essay's slave revolt when the question is about the Second). No preamble before the five points and no wrap-up after them.
