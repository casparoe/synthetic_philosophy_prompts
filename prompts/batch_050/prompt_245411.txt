I'm in a phil of technology seminar and we just finished the unit on griefbots, and I keep noticing my gut reactions don't line up with each other. I want to know what principle, if any, they're all coming from. Here are the cases:

1. My grandmother spent her last year training a chatbot on her old emails and texts, because she explicitly wanted us grandkids to have it after she died. Using it feels completely fine to me, even comforting.
2. A startup scrapes a dead musician's public posts and sells fans a "chat with him" subscription, no consent from him or his estate. This feels gross, clearly over the line.
3. Same startup, but the musician signed a contract allowing it while alive (for money). Still feels gross! Which already seems inconsistent with 1.
4. A widow keeps chatting with a bot of her husband, who never said anything either way about wanting one. This feels... basically okay? At least not like 2.
5. Parents use a bot of their deceased young daughter. Both of them want it, and it clearly helps them cope. Still feels deeply off to me, and the daughter obviously couldn't have consented.
6. Someone consults the dad-bot to settle a family dispute about what dad "would have wanted" with the inheritance. This feels like a misuse even though I'm fine with the bot existing.

My tentative principle was "consent of the deceased is what matters," but cases 3, 4, and 5 all strain it in different directions. So: is there one principle that delivers most of these verdicts, and if so state it precisely and tell me which of my verdicts it has to give up? Or do I need two seperate principles (one about what's owed to the dead, one about what's healthy for the living), and if so which cases fall under which? Also tell me what your answer implies about a case I didn't list, like a hospice offering dying patients a "legacy bot" service as a standard option.

Also, if you think the problem is that I'm asking for a single principle where there just isn't one, or that some of my cases are actually about something else entirely (privacy? exploitation? what grief is for?), just say that instead of politely forcing everything into one framework.
