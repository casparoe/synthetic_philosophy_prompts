I currently defend a constrained case for judicial review, which I call the democratic-repair thesis: in a constitutional democracy, unelected courts may invalidate legislation when, and roughly only when, the legislation threatens to impair the political processes through which electoral losers could contest and reverse it. Paradigm cases include suppressing votes, entrenching incumbents, punishing political dissent, or excluding groups whose participation is necessary for democratic correction. On this view, courts are auxiliary institutions for preserving democracy rather than a general council empowered to strike down laws merely because judges consider them unjust, unwise, or insufficiently egalitarian.

What would count against this position? Please construct a map of its exposure rather than simply rehearsing the generic objection that judicial review is counter-majoritarian. Which historical developments, comparative institutional findings, political-science results, or philosophical arguments would be decisive against it? Which would merely require narrowing or qualifying it? In particular, consider: evidence that political systems can repair anti-democratic legislation more reliably without courts; cases in which process-oriented review itself entrenches judicial or partisan minorities; the possibility that no principled line separates democratic-process defects from controversial substantive judgments; and rights whose violation does not directly reduce political participation but may still seem to warrant invalidation.

Please also identify which objections defenders of the thesis should themselves recognize as genuine defeaters, rather than objections only critics would accept. If the phrase “impairing democratic correction” can be stretched to authorize almost any decision, assess whether the view has become unfalsifiable and what sharper formulation could preserve it. Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and state what observable evidence or conceptual result would most persuade a proponent to abandon the position altogether.
