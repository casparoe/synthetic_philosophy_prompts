ok so i cover science and ideas for a magazine and i'm writing a piece on whether adorno and horkheimer basically saw the algorithmic feed coming decades early, and i want a poem to run as a sidebar. here's the ask: write a poem about the culture industry thesis where the FORM does the arguing. specifically -- 4 or 5 stanzas, and every stanza has to have the exact same structure (same line lengths, same meter, same rhyme scheme, same sentence pattern), with only the surface details swapped out between them -- different products, different stars, different decades maybe. that way the poem enacts the point: standardization dressed up as novelty, pseudo-individuality where the differences are real but meaningless. somewhere in there it should get at the other moves too -- free time as work continued by other means, rebellion getting absorbed and sold back to you as a style, the way even "choosing" from the menu is pre-chewed. don't name-drop adorno or frankfurt or "dialectic of enlightenment," the poem has to stand on its own, but someone who's read the culture industry chapter should read it and go oh. and here's the hard part: the last stanza should quietly admit that this poem, appearing as a neatly packaged sidebar in my article, is itself exactly the kind of thing it's describing -- without breaking the form it just critiqued. that self-indictment should feel earned, not winky.
