So I spent forty years as an organic chemist, mostly agrochemicals ironically enough, and since retiring I've been reading philosophy the way some people take up golf. Lately I've been stuck on the ethics of pest control, which maybe hits close to home given I spent my career helping make the stuff. My wife thinks it's hilarious that the man who once field-tested pyrethroids now carries spiders outside in a jam jar. Anyway, a philosophy reading group I've joined (don't laugh) asked each of us to write down our commitments on some topic so the others can attack them, and I want to check mine hang together first. Here's where I've landed:

(a) I think some insects probably have morally relevant experiences -- not pain exactly like ours, but something it is like to be them, and the precautionary thing to do is assign them some expected moral weight proportional to how likely sentience is and how complex the nervous system is.

(b) I think factory farming pigs for a marginal improvement in taste is clearly wrong, and I give as my reason that the animal's suffering vastly outweighs the trivial human benefit.

(c) I think it's perfectly fine to wipe out an aphid infestation on my tomatoes with insecticidal soap, and fine for farmers to spray crops, because the benefit (food, livelihoods) isn't trivial.

(d) I think what matters morally is suffering, not death as such -- an insect plausibly has no projects, no future it anticipates, so killing one painlessly is at worst a minor harm, whereas making one suffer is the real wrong.

(e) Given (d), I still insist on fast-acting methods over slow poisons and I object to those glue traps that take days to kill.

(f) I roll my eyes at people who extend moral worry to soil microbes or nematodes -- at some point the probability of sentience is so low that worrying is just silliness.

Which of these are actually in tension and which only look it? My gut says (d) and (e) will get flagged but I think that's resolvable, whereas I have a sneaking suspicion the real problem is somewhere in (a)-(c) given that an aphid colony has a LOT of neurons in aggregate, possibly more than one pig, which is an uncomfortable thought I keep having. But maybe I'm wrong about that too. Please rank the tensions by how serious they are, and for each one tell me the cheapest revision that dissolves it and what I'd lose by making it. Don't just tell me everything conflicts with everything -- I want to know what actually has to give.
