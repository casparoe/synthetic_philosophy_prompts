"Buridan's ass is a thought experiment Buridan invented in which a donkey stands exactly midway between two identical bales of hay. Since the donkey has no reason to prefer one bale over the other, it cannot rationally choose either, and so it starves to death where it stands. Buridan's own conclusion from this case was that the will must be free in the sense of being undetermined, since only an uncaused act of will could break the symmetry and save the donkey. This makes the case an argument for libertarian free will. The coin flip suggestion doesn't really work, because flipping a coin is just choosing randomly, and choosing randomly is still choosing, so the rational agent who flips a coin has already solved the problem of acting without a reason -- the coin flip is a perfectly rational choice in a situation of equipoise. Therefore the puzzle dissolves: whenever reasons are equal, the rational thing to do is pick arbitrarily, and the starving donkey shows only that animals lack this capacity for random choice, which is why it starves while a human wouldn't. One might worry that an arbitrary pick isn't really rational, but since both options are equally good, no pick can be wrong, so arbitrariness costs nothing."
