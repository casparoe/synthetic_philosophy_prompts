Write a poem, between 14 and 20 lines, about Gödel's rotating universes and his modal argument against the objectivity of time. The poem must end with its own first line, so that the reader who finishes is returned to the beginning: the form should enact a closed timelike curve rather than merely describe one. Get the argument right. Gödel's point is not that time travel is paradoxical -- the traveler along such a curve revisits the past but cannot change it -- but that since worlds physically like ours yet lacking any objective lapse of time are possible, temporal passage cannot be an objective feature even of our own world; its reality here would be an inexplicable contingency. The repeated line should land differently the second time: on first reading, the opening line should sound like a plain affirmation that time flows; arriving at it again, the reader should hear it as the very illusion Gödel took himself to have exposed. Make the inferential step -- from the possible loop to the lost lapse -- legible within the poem, rather than asserting the conclusion as a slogan.
