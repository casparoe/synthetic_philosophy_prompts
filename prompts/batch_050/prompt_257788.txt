A colleague sent me a blog post that summarizes the debate over metaphor like this: "Donald Davidson argued that metaphors have no meaning beyond their literal meaning -- so when Romeo says Juliet is the sun, the sentence means the same absurd thing it would mean if taken literally, and Davidson concluded that metaphor is just a rhetorical trick for nudging people to notice things, nothing that semantics needs to explain. Cognitive linguists like Lakoff later refuted this by showing that abstract thought is systematically built out of metaphor (we understand time as motion, arguments as war), so metaphor is the machinery of meaning itself, not decoration on top of it." I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training, and both halves of this summary feel plausible to me, but I suspect at least one is subtly off. Is this fair? Please separate what Davidson actually claimed from what this attributes to him (especially the claim that metaphor is mere decoration), whether Lakoff-style conceptual metaphor theory actually conflicts with Davidson the way the post implies, and what a careful defender of each side would say the real disagreement is.
