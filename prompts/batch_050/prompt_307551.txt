My gran's clay figurine has started a proper row in our family and I want to know what my sister would say about it. She did one philosophy module at uni and now has extremely firm opinions on this kind of thing. Her track record so far:

- Ship of Theseus, planks swapped one at a time over decades: still the same ship, obviously.
- Someone collects all the discarded planks and rebuilds a ship out of them: that's a new ship, not the original.
- A lump of clay rolled into a ball, then a sausage, then a pancake: same lump throughout.
- A lump modelled into a statue and then squashed flat: the lump survives, the statue doesn't.
- A statue that gets its nose chipped and the face re-carved slightly smaller: same statue.

So. Gran's figurine (a shepherdess, if that matters) got knocked off the mantelpiece while we were clearing the house and smashed into maybe forty pieces. My mum has now painstakingly glued every single piece back — same clay, same paint flecks, looks exactly like it did. Is that gran's figurine or a new one? Predict my sister's verdict, work out which rule she's actually running — or the two or three rules that fit her record equally well but split on this case — and say which earlier case is the closest analog. If her verdicts genuinely underdetermine it, tell me what principle she'd be committing to with each answer, and name the further case that would settle which one she really holds. Also say where your prediction is most likely to go wrong — what new information or argument would change your verdict.
