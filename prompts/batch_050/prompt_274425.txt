ok so here's a thought experiment my friends and i got into an argument about. there's this idea from philosophy of AI / global catastrophic risk circles called the singleton hypothesis: basically the claim that eventually earth (or whatever comes after us) will end up governed by a single decision-making agency at the top level -- could be one world government, one superintelligent AI, one institution that effectively calls all the shots, whatever. no more anarchy between states, no more rival powers, just one final authority that nothing can check.

my question: what do you think thomas hobbes would make of this? i mean, his whole thing in leviathan is that peace requires everyone handing their power over to an undivided sovereign, and he thought the state of nature between nations was basically a permanent cold war precisely because there's no sovereign above them. so on the one hand a singleton looks like the leviathan logic finally completed -- the international state of nature gets its own sovereign at last. but on the other hand he was weirdly ok with the international anarchy compared to the domestic one, and a singleton is also way more absolute than anything he imagined (his sovereign could still die, get invaded, whatever -- a singleton by definition has no outside and no successor).

so argue for whichever side you think is right: would hobbes see the singleton as the happy ending of his political philosophy or as something he'd recoil from, and where exactly does the argument from leviathan break down (or hold up) when you scale it to the whole species?
