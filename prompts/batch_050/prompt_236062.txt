My epistemology colleague has a habit of giving snap verdicts on knowledge cases, and before I put a new one to her I want to predict what she will say. Her track record so far: (1) Bank, low stakes: Hannah was at the bank two Saturdays ago, nothing much turns on depositing her paycheck this Saturday, and the possibility that the bank has changed its hours never comes up — verdict: knowledge. (2) Bank, high stakes: same evidence, but a large payment bounces Monday unless the deposit clears, and it occurs to Hannah that banks do sometimes change their hours — verdict: no knowledge. (3) Train board: she glances at the departure board, sees her train listed at 5:10, and nothing turns on catching it — verdict: knowledge. (4) Lottery: holding one ticket in a fair million-ticket lottery, she believes her ticket will lose purely on the odds, and it does — verdict: no knowledge, though she herself adds that this one is just luck and shouldn't constrain the general rule. (5) Gate number: a fellow passenger tells her the flight boards at gate 12; missing it would cost her a job interview, so, reflecting that strangers are sometimes mistaken, she double-checks the monitor — verdict on her belief prior to double-checking: no knowledge.

The pending case: the same bank evidence as in (1), and the belief is true, but now Hannah's life savings depend on the bank being open Saturday — while neither she nor anyone else ever entertains the possibility that the hours have changed, and she forms the belief in exactly the same untroubled, careless manner as in (1).

Please reply as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: in item 1, state the two most plausible rules that fit all five verdicts (a stakes-sensitive rule and an attention- or salience-sensitive rule, unless you see better candidates, in which case name them); in items 2 and 3, state what each rule predicts for the pending case and why; in item 4, give your best guess at her actual verdict, with the reasons that break the underdetermination for you (including whether a third, disjunctive rule fits the record and what that would imply); and in item 5, specify the single further case whose verdict would discriminate between the two rules, and what each possible verdict on it would show.
