Last night a friend and I spent three hours arguing about when it is permissible to impose risks on people who never consented to them, and I want to reconstruct her position rather than continue arguing blindly. Her verdicts on the cases we discussed were as follows:

1. Driving a well-maintained car at the speed limit past pedestrians: permissible, even though the pedestrians never agreed to bear the small risk.
2. Firing a rifle into the air in a crowded city as a celebration: impermissible, full stop.
3. A nuclear power plant operating near a town, where the meltdown probability is minuscule but the consequences would be catastrophic, and the town receives the plant's electricity and tax revenue: permissible without individual consent from each resident.
4. A regional airport extending its flight paths over a village whose residents gain nothing from the airport, imposing on them a small additional risk of a crash: impermissible unless the villagers are compensated or the airport negotiates some arrangement with them.
5. A motorcycle race on closed public streets, where spectators behind barriers face a residual risk of a crash: permissible, because the spectators showed up voluntarily.

Now the new case we could not resolve: a logistics company proposes flying small delivery drones over a city's neighborhoods. Engineering data put the expected harm per flight-year slightly *below* the risk an ordinary driver imposes on pedestrians over a comparable period. The residents being overflown are not the customers -- the packages are destined for people elsewhere -- and no one asked the residents' permission. My friend hesitated, and we parted before she committed.

Please do the following: predict her verdict on the drone case; state the two or three candidate principles that each fit all five of her earlier verdicts (I suspect an expected-harm threshold, a mutual-benefit or reciprocity condition, and a voluntariness condition are among the contenders, but correct me if I'm wrong); say which principle each candidate commits her to here and which single candidate best explains the full pattern, including her hesitation; and name the one further case I should put to her that would most efficiently discriminate between the remaining candidates. Keep the whole answer under 450 words.
