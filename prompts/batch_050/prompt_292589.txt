I'm on the grants commitee of a mid-sized foundation that funds work aimed at improving the very long-term future. One of our staffers has circulated a proposal that we apply what she calls a "simulation discount" to any grant whose case depends on benefits arriving thousands or millions of years from now. Her argument: there's a non-trivial chance we're living in a simulation, and if we are, our far future is either probably short (simulations get switched off or wound down) or not really ours to shape (the simulators' decisions swamp ours), so projects justified by tiny probabilities of astronomically large long-run payoffs are doubly speculative and shouldn't win out over nearer-term work. Another committee member pushed back that the simulation hypothesis cuts the other way -- simulated civilizations might be rerun, copied, or observed in ways that make long-run influence easier, not harder -- and that in any case he's queasy about baking a metaphysical hunch into grant arithmetic. I've been asked to draft the decision memo we'll vote on, recommending that we adopt the discount, reject it, or adopt some modified version.

Please write that memo, with sections in this order: Bottom line (3-4 sentences stating the actual recommendation), The strongest case for the discount (one solid paragraph, steelmanning it, including the fanaticism worry about tiny probabilities times huge stakes), Why I land where I do (2-3 paragraphs, and it has to actually engage with whether the discount follows even granting the simulation hypothesis, not just dismiss the premise as weird), and What to tell the staffer (a short paragraph she could act on). Whole thing under 600 words, and don't pad it with citations or reading suggestions.
