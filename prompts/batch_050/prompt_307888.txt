My uni’s ethics grant panel has been ruling on what rich countries owe poorer ones. So far: emergency vaccination in an upper-middle-income country after a hurricane — duty; malaria nets delivered by NGOs in a very poor, badly governed country — duty; a national museum in a stable but still poor country — no duty; compensation for farmers harmed by our past tariffs — duty; servicing debt run up and stolen by a former dictator — no duty; funding a friendly opposition party in an undemocratic country — no duty. Next application: our historical emissions have worsened drought in an upper-middle-income country whose government could pay for irrigation but is splurging on a vanity stadium, and an outside agency can reach subsistence farmers directly. Should the panel say duty, no duty, or duty only via direct delivery? Give at least two rules that fit all the earlier verdicts but split here, recommend the best-fitting verdict, name the closest precedent, and describe the one further case that would settle between the rules.
