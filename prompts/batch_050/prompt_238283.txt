My talk is basically a defense of the claim that Skolem's paradox is philosophically benign: the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem shows that "uncountable" is relative to the model you're evaluating it in, sure, but once you're careful about first-order vs second-order semantics and what the downward theorem actually says about internal vs external notions of countability, there's no genuine paradox left and definitely no support for radical claims like "there's no fact of the matter about how big the continuum is."

Here's my problem: the audience is going to be unusually tough for this. There's also a senior model theorist who will absolutely bring up second-order logic and ask why I'm entitled to dismiss it as not real logic, since that's doing all the work in my "no genuine paradox" move. And then a couple of philosophers of language who I suspect will press me on whether "internal to the model" talk even makes sense without sneaking in the very absolute notion of set I'm trying to avoid. The grad students will mostly ask confused-but-harmless stuff so I care less about them.

Prepare me for the Q&A.
