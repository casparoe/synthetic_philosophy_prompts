(1) I think Adam Smith was basically right in the digression on the corn trade in Book IV of the Wealth of Nations: in a large grain-producing country with free internal commerce, even the worst harvests produce scarcity rather than outright famine, because trade smooths the shortfall across regions. (2) I accept Sen's entitlement approach: famines usually come from collapses in what people can command (wages, prices, exchange) rather than from there simply not being enough food, and Bengal in 1943 is the textbook case since food was available but the poor were priced out. (3) I regard the Irish famine as a political crime, and the fact that food was being exported out of Ireland whilst people starved is, for me, the proof that markets cannot be trusted to feed the starving. (4) I think Sen's democracy point is the deepest explanation of all: famines don't happen under a free press and accountable government, and the Great Leap Forward shows it, since the state could keep starving its own people precisely because nobody could report it or vote them out.
