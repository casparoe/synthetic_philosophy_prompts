The question was: Archaeologists find a black metal disk whose diameter and standardized edge notches match a known nineteenth-century music-box format, so they can tell it is meant to be mounted and played. A short raised pattern near the rim is a known calibration code instructing the mechanism to transpose every later note down a fifth and interchange two pins. Once those instructions are followed, the remaining pins encode a lullaby whose lyrics describe where another object was buried. Using Hofstadter's distinction between frame, outer, and inner messages, explain which features play which roles. Does the fact that all three are physically encoded on the same disk undermine the distinction? And where, if anywhere, is the meaning located? Rubric: 6 points for correctly mapping the disk's features onto the three levels; 8 for explaining the levels in terms of functional role rather than physical position or mere decoding order; 6 for a defensible account of the location of meaning; 3 for addressing the same-medium issue; 2 for clarity and nuance.

The student wrote: The standardized size and notches are the frame message, since they are what first tell the archaeologists that this is a playable disk. The calibration pattern is the outer message, since it is not the intended lullaby but instructions for setting up the decoding process. The lullaby and its burial information are the inner message. Because the labels classify stages in the decoding pipeline, the fact that all three are cut into the same metal does not undermine the distinction. The levels are also relative: if the archaeologists have not yet deciphered the calibration code, it can function as the inner message of their preliminary investigation, and only after they understand it does it become the outer message for the lullaby. This suggests that meaning is not physically contained in the grooves, but exists in the relation among artifact, mechanism, conventions, and interpreter. More exactly, though, only the lullaby counts as semantic content, while the frame and outer messages are merely enabling signals rather than meaningful messages. And although the interpreter supplies the decoding conventions, the lullaby's actual meaning is fixed by the manufacturer's intention, which a correct listener recovers.
