help me write a villanelle i can read out at the last meeting of a reading group on the moral status of AI systems. we've spent the semester on the attribution problem -- how you'd ever tell whether some system is sentient when its innards look nothing like a brain. we covered the split between what one paper called theory-light and theory-heavy approaches: either you extend credit from cases you're sure about (us, then mammals, then maybe octopuses) based on similarity, or you commit to a theory of which computations actually matter (recurrent processing, global broadcast, integrated information, whatever) and follow it wherever it points, even to systems that dont beg for sympathy at all. nobody in the group changed their mind all semester, which is kind of the point i want the poem to make.

so: the two refrains should be two verdicts a careful person could reach about the same system -- one along the lines of "the right computation is running, so someone is home," the other along the lines of "everything you can check is behavior, and behavior is exactly the thing you can't trust here." im paraphrasing badly, fix the wording. the stanzas between them should keep introducing new evidence -- a fresh test result, a new architecture, the system asking not to be turned off, a theory predicting consciousness in something we'd never have guessed -- and the refrains should come back unchanged each time, because both sides have a standing rule that re-reads every new fact as compatible with their verdict. that's what the form is for: the villanelle's circling should enact the epistemic loop, not just describe it.

in the final quatrain the two refrains meet, and i want the poem to land on the genuinely hard part: not "we dont know yet," but that we might build systems where the answer can't in principle be checked from the outside, and we have to decide how to treat them anyway. a philosopher working on machine consciousness should recognize the two positions without them being named; everyone else should just get a good, unsettling poem. nineteen lines, strict refrain structure, rhyme can be slant if it has to be.
