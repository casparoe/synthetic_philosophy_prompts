my thesis is roughly this: the standard lamp setup isn't actually paradoxical, it's just incomplete. the switching instructions only fix the lamp's state for times before t=1, and they say nothing whatsoever about what happens at t=1 itself, so the argument that the lamp has to end up both on and off trades on pretending the instructions answer a question they never even address. to make this vivid i came up with a variant i'm calling thomson's dimmer: instead of toggling between on and off, each switch halves the brightness, so the state genuinely converges to zero and there IS an obvious answer at t=1 (lamp fully dark, via the continuous completion). my claim is that the dimmer and the original lamp are structurally identical except for continuity at the limit point, which shows the real source of the puzzle is discontinuity at the accumulation point, not infinity as such — and that means people who think the lamp refutes the coherence of supertasks are just wrong.
