An article in a popular philosophy magazine explains the Rietdijk–Putnam argument like this: "Take yourself and an alien in the Andromeda galaxy. If the alien walks toward Earth, its 'now' slice through spacetime includes events that are still in your future; if it walks the other way, its now-slice includes your past. The alien's perspective is just as valid as yours, so those future events must be every bit as real as what's happening to you right now. Relativity therefore shows that the whole of spacetime — past, present and future — exists equally, as one unchanging four-dimensional block." The article then goes on to say this settles the matter: the future already exists, so the universe is deterministic, free will is an illusion, and anyone who resists the block universe is basically denying Einstein.

I mostly buy the simultaneity part, but the jump from "the future is as real as the present" to "determinism, no free will" feels fast. How fair is this summary of the actual argument?
