You are a philosopher of biology with expertise in the functions debate, asked to write a careful case analysis for an interdisciplinary audience. Before writing, take a moment to brainstorm the key moves on both sides; then organize your answer as a disciplined mapping between theory and episode, flagging wherever the theory runs out rather than forcing it to speak.

Here is the episode. In 2012, the ENCODE consortium, a large publicly funded effort to catalog functional elements in the human genome, announced that biochemical activity could be assigned to roughly 80 percent of human DNA, and this was widely publicized as showing that most of the genome is "functional" and that the idea of junk DNA had been overturned. A number of evolutionary biologists pushed back sharply. Their case rested on points like these: mutation load considerations and comparative genome size data (the onion test, and the huge genomes of some salamanders and lungfish) suggest that only a small fraction, perhaps a tenth or so, of the human genome is under purifying selection; much of the biochemical activity ENCODE cataloged (transcription at low levels, open chromatin, sporadic factor binding) occurs in junk-like sequence and even in randomly generated DNA; and a transposon-derived sequence can be transcribed without doing anything for the organism.

Analyze this dispute as a collision between the two main philosophical accounts of biological function: the etiological (selected-effects) account, on which a trait's function is the effect for which it was selected, and the causal-role account, on which a function is a causal contribution a component makes to some capacity of a containing system. For each side of the dispute: which account does it sound like the scientists are implicitly relying on, and does reconstructing them that way make their arguments stronger or weaker? Then the harder questions. Is this a genuine empirical disagreement or a case of two communities using "function" in different but legitimate senses — and does the causal-role/etiological distinction actually settle that, or does it merely redescribe it? Consider whether ENCODE needs something like an organizational or systems-level refinement of the causal-role view (which distinguishes mere activity from contributions to a system's organized capacities) to avoid trivially counting everything, and whether the evolutionary critics can dismiss non-selected transcription without smuggling in an a priori definition that would make "junk DNA" unfalsifiable. Finally, say what the episode shows about the theories themselves: does it confirm one, expose a blind spot in one, or reveal that both were designed for paradigm cases (hearts, eyes) and underdetermine what to say at the genomic scale? Where the accounts are simply silent about the case, say so explicitly.
