ok so i run a small reading group on formal epistemology and we just spent two sessions on Aumann's agreement theorem. half the group still thinks it boils down to "if you talk long enough you converge", which is exactly wrong, and i want to close the unit with something that gets the actual logic across instead.

write me a villanelle about two rational agents with the same prior but different private evidence. the two repeating lines of the villanelle should be their stated estimates of some probability, and across the tercets the numbers should move toward each other, because each time one agent announces a posterior, that announcement is itself evidence about what the other has seen, so both update again. by the closing quatrain the two refrains should collapse into a single shared number.

the thing the poem has to land: nobody splits the difference, nobody compromises, nobody gets worn down or charmed. every update is fully bayesian, and the reason they can't end up dissagreeing is that their posteriors are common knowledge, so rational disagreement would itself be common knowledge, which is the thing the theorem rules out. also don't smuggle in them exchanging their raw evidence — it's the posteriors that do the work, and a game theorist reading it should be able to tell you know that distinction. if the poem makes it sound like a story about politeness or patience, it's failed.
