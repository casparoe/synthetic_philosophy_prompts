So here's my situation. I spent about 35 years as a cell biologist, mostly working on protein folding and chaperone systems, and I retired four years ago. Since then I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole (my wife says it's just a way to keep arguing about science without a lab). Anyway I've been reading about the mechanistic account of explanation -- Machamer, Darden, Craver, that whole crowd -- the basic idea being that to explain a phenomenon is to show how it's produced by some organized arrangement of parts and activities, with levels, bottoming out, all of that.

Here's what's bugging me. When I left the field, everyone was losing their minds over deep learning models like AlphaFold that predict protein structures with ridiculous accuracy but are basically black boxes. And now some of my old colleagues -- smart people -- say things like: prediction this good just *is* a kind of explanation, or at least it makes the old-fashioned demand for mechanism obsolete. One of them told me that insisting on a "mechanism" is nostalgia, like demanding an ether after relativity. My gut says that's wrong -- the model tells you where the protein ends up, not how or why folding works -- but my gut is also the gut of someone who hasn't had to think about this professionally in years, so I don't fully trust it.

So: what are the most important implications of the mechanistic account for this dispute? Specifically I'd like to know: (1) does the mechanist picture straightforwardly vindicate my gut feeling that a black-box predictor doesn't explain anything, or is that too quick? (2) is there any natural way for a mechanist to *accomodate* these models -- say, as mechanism sketches, or as constraints that rule out whole families of possible mechanisms, or as evidence about the phenomenon rather than explanations of it -- and do those moves actually work or are they face-saving? and (3) the harder one: what should a mechanist say about the possibility that some biological systems are so distributed and high-dimensional that no human-surveyable mechanism exists at all? Does the mechanist have to bite the bullet and say those phenomena are just unexplained (maybe unexplainable), or does the view have resources for that?

Feel free to tell me my gut is wrong. I've been wrong professionally before, its practically a hobby.
