So here's the situation. I'm helping run a small grant program (think ~5 researchers, 18 months) on the ethics of video game characters, and I need to figure out what questions we should actually be funding people to work on. The background is that our studio partners are starting to ship NPCs that aren't scripted anymore -- they're driven by learned models with persistent memory across sessions, so an NPC "remembers" how you've treated it over months of play, and its future behavior is shaped by that history. We also do the usual stuff: save-state reloading (the same NPC effectively gets rolled back and re-experiences a stretch of game-time differently), and mass duplication, where one NPC template gets copied into thousands of players' separate instances.

The candidate questions people keep bringing to us are roughly:

1. Whether any of these systems could have whatever-it-is that makes suffering matter (phenomenal consciousness, valenced states, whatever the right criterion turns out to be)
2. Whether, assuming some NPC does matter, save-state rollback counts as something like killing it or just as a weird kind of amnesia
3. How to aggregate: if one NPC template is duplicated into 50,000 instances, does harm to each instance count separately, or is there some sense in which copies dilute the wrong?
4. Whether the player's intent matters morally -- is it worse to torture an NPC for fun than to let it die because you were bored, if the NPC can't tell the difference
5. Whether treating NPCs badly is wrong mainly because of what it does to the player's character, independant of whether the NPC matters at all

What I want from you: which two or three of these (or one I've missed) should we fund first, and defend the ordering. Don't just say "it depends on consciousness" -- tell me which questions are upstream of which in the sense that answering one changes how much the others matter, which are actually tractable for philosophers working now versus which are stuck waiting on neuroscience or ML interpretability, and which ones you'd deprioritize because the practical answer (what we should tell our studios to do) comes out the same either way. Also: what would change your ranking? Like, if some specific empirical result landed in the next two years, which priorities flip?

The deliverable I'm after is a defended agenda I can take to the board, not a survey of the literature. If you think question 5 is a cop-out that lets us dodge the hard question, say that, but argue for it.
