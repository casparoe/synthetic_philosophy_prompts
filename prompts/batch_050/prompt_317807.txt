Ok so I'm putting together a seminar on metaphor next term and I keep coming back to a fight that I think my students would get a lot out of, but I want to road-test my own take on it first. The fight is the Dawkins–Midgley dust-up over The Selfish Gene. Quick sketch of the episode, in case the details are fuzzy: Dawkins' book comes out in 1976 and leans hard on the metaphor of genes as selfish agents — they're the real survivors, organisms are just vehicles, etc. Mary Midgley publishes Gene Juggling in Philosophy in 1979 attacking the book as thinly veiled ideology, reading the selfishness talk as attributing literal motives and competitive nastiness to nature and by extension to us. Dawkins fires back with a pretty savage reply in the same journal a couple years later, basically saying she'd attacked a book she hadn't bothered to read carefully and that nobody with any sense takes the metaphor literally. (She later admitted she'd written the review in a hurry without reading the book properly, which is its own lesson about referees, but that's another matter.)

Here's what I want analyzed: run this episode through the two big rival pictures of metaphor. On Davidson's view in What Metaphors Mean, a metaphor has no meaning beyond the literal one — "selfish gene" is literally false, full stop, and everything interesting the metaphor does (making you notice things, see genes as strategists, whatever) belongs to its use and effects, not to any hidden metaphorical content the words carry. On the conceptual metaphor picture from Lakoff and Johnson, metaphor is a systematic mapping between domains, so calling genes selfish imports an whole inferential structure from the domain of human agents — intentions, competition, maybe even blame.

So: who does each theory say was at fault in the dispute? On Davidson's picture, can a metaphor even mislead anyone, or is Midgley's mistake a category error about where the misunderstanding lives — like blaming a joke for someone's bad sense of humor? Does his view let Dawkins entirely off the hook for picking a loaded word, and is that a feature or a bug? On the conceptual metaphor view, Midgley looks vindicated in a way — the mapping does real cognitive work whether the author intends it or — but does that same view condemn Dawkins' original use as irresponsible, or does it actually explain why the metaphor was scientifically productive (it structured a research program!) in a way Davidson can't?

And run it back the other direction: what does the episode show about the theories? I'm particularly interested in whether the fact that Dawkins spent decades paraphrasing the metaphor (genes "care about" replication, organisms as survival machines, the whole extended apparatus) tells against Davidson's anti-paraphrase line, or whether Davidson can say the paraphrases are just more literal talk prompted by the metaphor, not recoveries of its content. Also whether Midgley misreading the book is evidence for conceptual metaphor theory (the mapping swallowed her) or just evidence that she didn't read it.

If either theory goes quiet at some point in the episode, say so rather than forcing it to talk. And if you think neither side gets the dispute right — e.g., it's really a dispute about rhetoric and audience responsibility that both semantic theories miss — make that case instead. Roughly 800-1200 words, and don't just summarize the theories at me, I obviously know them already.
