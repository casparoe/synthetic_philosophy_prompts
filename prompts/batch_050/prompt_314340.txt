I have no formal training in philosophy, but I've been thinking on my own about the Chinese Room argument for a while: a person who speaks no Chinese sits in a room and, by following a rulebook, manipulates Chinese symbols well enough to pass as a fluent speaker -- so, the argument goes, merely running the right program can never amount to genuine understanding. I've been keeping a list of the standard replies, and I'd like to extend it. The ones I already know and don't need repeated: (1) the systems reply -- the man in the room doesn't understand, but the whole system of man, room, and rulebook does; (2) the robot reply -- put the program in a body with sensors and the symbols would come to be about something; (3) the brain simulator reply -- a program that simulates the actual neuron firings of a Chinese speaker really would understand; (4) the combination reply -- all of these at once; (5) the other minds reply -- we can't verify that other people understand either, so the argument proves too much. Can you give me one more reply to the argument that is genuinely different from these -- not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of them -- and then tell me honestly whether you think it holds up? Please answer in American English.
