So I've been reading about reflective equilibrium and I think I finally get why it's such a powerful method. I sat down last weekend, listed out all my moral judgments (stuff like "it's wrong to break promises just for convenience", "lying to spare feelings is sometimes fine"), and then kept tweaking my general principles -- adding exceptions, qualifying things, that kind of thing -- until the principles covered every single one of my judgments with no contradictions. Took a few hours but I got there.

So now my moral views are in reflective equilibrium, which per Rawls means they're justified, right? My question is: why do so many philosophers still act like this wouldn't settle anything? If justification is just achieving this kind of coherence, then once you've done the coherence work you're done, and critics are just refusing to update. Is the pushback basically just people being sore that the method can vindicate views they dislike, or is there some further step beyond coherence they'd demand -- and wouldn't demanding more just be abandoning the coherence theory of justification that reflective equilibrium rests on?
