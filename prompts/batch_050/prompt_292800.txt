I help run a reading group at my uni (mixed undergrads and masters students, usually 14-18 people show up, we get about 75 minutes) and we're doing a session on multipolar traps and Moloch-style dynamics -- races to the bottom where everyone's individually rational choice makes the collective outcome worse and nobody can unilaterally step off. I want to design a game for the session, and I have a fairly specific worry I want you to design around: I do NOT want a dressed-up prisoner's dilemma. Half the group has done game theory 101 and if they recognise the structure in the first five minutes they'll just play the "correct" move and smugly wait for the debrief. So the payoff structure needs to hide the trap a bit, ideally through some combination of: positional/rank-based scoring (so what matters is where you stand relative to others, not absolutes), a ratchet (investments or commitments from earlier rounds persist and raise the baseline for later ones), and genuinely diminishing returns so that the escalating spending feels justified round by round and only looks insane in aggregate at the end.

A few things I want built in:

1. A mid-game phase where the group is allowed to try to escape the trap -- e.g. they can propose binding agreements, vote on caps, appoint an enforcer, whatever -- but the mechanism should make escaping realistically hard (no central authority, enforcement costs something, agreements can be quietly undercut, and at least one person should have a real incentive to break whatever deal emerges). I want them to discover that seeing the trap clearly doesn't get you out of it, which I think is the actual soul of the idea and the bit people miss when they just read about it.

2. Clear roles, rules, and payoff numbers I can actually print on cards, plus a rough timeline for the 75 minutes.

3. A debrief plan that maps specific moments in the game onto the general structure (arms races, attention/ signalling competitions, that kind of thing) -- but the debrief should mostly be drawing out what they already felt, not lecturing. Also tell me honestly what will predictably go wrong when I run this (people refusing to escalate, someone gaming the escape phase in a way that breaks it, arithmetic errors, whatever) and how to handle each.

4. A frank note on which feature of real multipolar traps the game can't reproduce -- e.g. in the game the players can all see each other and the game ends, whereas the real thing runs indefinitely with diffuse victims -- and what I'd need to say in the debrief to patch that gap.

Also one contraint: it should work with as few as 10 people if turnout is bad. Please write the whole answer in British English.
