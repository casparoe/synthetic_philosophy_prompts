I oversee deployment reviews for a general-purpose AI system that conducts millions of personalized conversations, including some with users who are frightened, lonely, ill, or politically undecided. Our governing principle is that the system may inform and advise users, but may not shape their beliefs or choices except through reasons they can in principle inspect, question, and discount.

Turn that principle into a practical review procedure that engineers and policy staff can apply to proposed conversational behaviors in under thirty minutes. Distinguish, where relevant, between explanation, recommendation, emotional engagement, selective presentation, personalization, repetition, urgency, and simulated relationship. State the questions reviewers should ask in order, which answers require rejection or redesign, and which unavoidably call for human judgment. Do not reduce the standard to “persuasion is forbidden” or “anything factually accurate is permitted.”

Then apply the procedure to three cases: the system strongly encourages a depressed user to seek treatment, privately adjusts its rhetoric according to a voter’s inferred insecurities, and adopts a warmer persona after detecting that a lonely user trusts affectionate assistants. At least one application should produce an uncomfortable result. Finally, identify the most important class of cases the procedure cannot resolve.
