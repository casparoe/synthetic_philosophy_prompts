Here's the setup. In Leviathan Hobbes makes two claims that usually get discussed separately. First (ch. 13, roughly): war doesn't consist in actual fighting but in a stretch of time during which the willingness to fight is sufficiently known, so you can be at war while nobody is shooting. Second (ch. 21, roughly): a subject's obligation to the sovereign lasts only as long as the sovereign's power to protect them, because the right to protect yourself is the one right no covenant can surrender.

Now take the week after Hurricane Katrina hit New Orleans in 2005. The levees failed, most of the city flooded, the local government effectively ceased to function, a large chunk of the police force deserted its posts, and residents armed themselves, some forming defense groups, some confronting strangers at gunpoint. At the same time -- and this matters, don't skip it -- the breathless reports of rampant violence turned out to be heavily exaggerated, and by most accounts the dominant behavior was improvised mutual aid: boat rescues, shared food, neighbor taking in neighbor.

Analyze the episode through the two Hobbes claims running together. Specifically: on Hobbes's own criterion, did the obligation of New Orleans residents to the civil authorities actually lapse that week, or was the sovereign merely temporarily absent (and does Hobbes's framework even have a concept of temporary lapse, or is dissolution binary)? Did the city enter a Hobbesian war by the tract-of-time standard even though actual violence was rare -- i.e., was the known willingness to contend by force enough? And then run it the other direction: the fact that cooperation, not war, filled the vacuum -- does that embarrass Hobbes, or can he absorb it (say, via residual sovereign power, via the shortness of the interval, or via the claim that a neighborhood watch is already a miniature commonwealth)?

Give me a definite verdict on both questions -- whether the obligation lapsed, and whether the episode confirms or dents Hobbes -- with reasons, not a menu of options. And flag the one point where you think the mapping is forced, where the case just doesn't fit his categories.
