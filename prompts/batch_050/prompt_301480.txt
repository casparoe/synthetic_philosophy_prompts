Is there a name for this? I'll give two instances. First: two rival firms each deploy an automated pricing system, and both systems settle on prices well above the competitive level, each holding there indefinitely. Investigators find no messages passing between the systems, and — this is the part I can't fit to anything I know — neither system ever observes the other's prices or sales; each sees only its own demand. The coordination seems to trace back to the two systems having been built by the same vendor, on the same historical data, with the same design assumptions. Second instance: two copies of the same language model are placed in a repeated negotiation where hoarding would pay off for each side, and they consistently split resources evenly, even though neither can see the other's reasoning and there is no shared signal to coordinate on. When experimenters altered small details of one copy's setup, the coordination partly broke down — as though it depended on the two players being alike, rather than on anything transmitted between them.

What strikes me in both cases is that the coordination appears to live in the similarity of the decision-makers themselves, not in any channel running between them. I know economists discuss tacit collusion, but as I understand it, that involves each party watching the other's behavior and adjusting in response; here nothing is watched or adjusted to. I also know game theory has notions where players coordinate by conditioning on some commonly observed event, but there is no such event here either. Is there an established concept for coordination that arises purely from agents being built alike — no observation, no communication, no shared signal?
