I am a high school student working on a term project about how to tell genuine historical revisionism apart from pseudohistory, and I keep running into the same problem. My classmates seem to think the difference is just that pseudohistory reaches conclusions that mainstream historians reject, but that cannot be right, because plenty of once-fringe views (like the idea that earthquakes destroyed Mycenaean palaces, or that humans reached the Americas far earlier than textbooks said) turned out to be defensible. Meanwhile, claims like Fomenko's compressed chronology, ancient astronaut theories, and literal readings of Atlantis are rejected even though their defenders produce long lists of alleged evidence. So the demarcation criterion must be about method, not conclusion. To test this, I invented a fictional case: a YouTube channel called Mirrored Empires argues that two medieval dynasties from different regions were actually the same dynasty recorded twice, because their reign lengths roughly match when you round to the nearest decade, their kings share some name syllables, and the gaps where the records do not match are explained as scribal suppression. When the rounding is challenged, the channel switches to a different rounding scheme that restores the match.

Here is my question. Below is a list of candidate demarcation features that I drew up, and I would like you to rank them from most to least diagnostic of pseudohistory, applied specifically to the Mirrored Empires case, with a sentence or two justifying each placement: (a) the claim contradicts a large body of established evidence; (b) the evidence is selected only after the conclusion is fixed, and the matching rule changes whenever the match fails; (c) the claim's proponents lack formal credentials in history; (d) the claim requires assuming a coordinated cover-up to explain away disconfirming records; (e) the claim is exciting and flatters the audience's suspicion of academic authorities; (f) the parallels cited would be statistically unsurprising even if the two dynasties were unrelated, given how many dynasties and rounding schemes one can search over. After ranking them, please tell me which single feature, if any, you would treat as nearly sufficient on its own to classify something as pseudohistory, and which features seem damning to laypeople but are actually weak demarcation criteria. I am especially unsure whether (a) should rank high, since it risks collapsing into the conclusion-based test I rejected above, so please address that directly.
