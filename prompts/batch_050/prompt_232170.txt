I keep ending up in the same argument on an LW-adjacent Discord about whether AI art systems are creative, and my friend Mara has a set of case judgments that feel individually reasonable but probabaly don't determine one simple rule. Her verdicts so far: an artist who sketches a composition, asks an image model for variants, picks one, repaints parts of it, and repeats that loop is being creative, while the model is not; a person who types "surreal city at sunset," takes the first output, and posts it has produced something that might count as creative work, but neither the person nor the model did much creativity; the engineers who designed a genuinely novel generative-image architecture were creative, even if they never made any particular picture; someone who notices an interesting accidental paint stain, recognizes what it resembles, cuts it out, and frames it is being creative; a child making an unoriginal crayon drawing of their family is also being creative; but a reinforcement-learning program that mutates images, scores them only for visual complexity, and keeps the highest-scoring ones is not creative, even if the results surprise everyone. So novelty, usefulness, selection, and intention all show up somewhere, but none of them cleanly explains the whole track record.

Now suppose there is a closed-loop system with two neural modules. A generator produces images and an internal critic rejects most of them based on a learned measure combining novelty, coherence, and visual interest; the rejected outputs update the critic, so its standards drift over time. A human initially chooses a vague theme, presses start, and then never intervenes. After several days the system produces an image that human artists judge to be genuinely novel and aesthetically valuable, although nobody, including the system's designers, understands why the critic favored it or can identify a human-like intention behind that particular image. The system also has no semantic understanding of what the image depicts beyond whatever is encoded in its weights. Predict Mara's verdict: is the system itself creative, is only some human in the causal chain creative, or is there creativity without any creative agent? More importantly, reconstruct at least three rules that fit all of her earlier verdicts, show where they diverge on this case, and say which earlier case is the closest precedent. Then tell me one additional case that would best distinguish the two strongest candidate rules.
