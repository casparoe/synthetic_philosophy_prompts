I design clinical decision-support tools and am fluent in decision trees, expected utility and QALYs, dominance, risk preferences, opportunity costs, and externalities. Restate procreative beneficence in that vocabulary as precisely as possible: when prospective parents can select among embryos or other reproductive options, they have some moral reason to choose the option expected to produce the child with the best life, subject to countervailing reasons; its liberal version permits parental choice rather than imposing a single state ranking of desirable traits.

Treat selection among embryos as a decision problem with uncertain outcomes, imperfect predictors, multiple traits, and the option of having no child. Then give a residue report: which parts translate cleanly into expected-value maximization, constrained choice, or risk management; which translate only by changing the framework, especially where different actions produce numerically different children, where existence must be compared with nonexistence, or where disability, parental autonomy, and effects on other people enter; and which parts have no genuine decision-analytic counterpart. Do not treat every embryo as though it were the same future patient. Conclude by saying whether the residue shows a limitation of decision analysis or instead reveals an unexamined person-neutral or aggregative assumption in procreative beneficence.
