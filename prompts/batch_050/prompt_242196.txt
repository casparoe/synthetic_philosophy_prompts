For a graduate seminar I am co-teaching on theories of well-being, I need a thought experiment that does new dialectical work. My students have all read the standard experience machine discussion and can recite the usual choreography by heart: hedonism is embarrassed by the machine, desire satisfactionism escapes because the plugged-in person's world-directed desires are actually frustrated, and the objective list theorist then piles on with achievements, knowledge, and relationships. What none of them have seen is a case that isolates the question cleanly: is there a component of well-being that is irreducible to both the quality of experience and the satisfaction of desire? I want to find out whether such a case can even be built.

Please construct a thought experiment meeting all four of the following specifications. First, hedonic parity: the two lives being compared must be experientially identical from the inside — same pleasure, engagement, and felt meaning — so that no experiential difference does any work in the case. Second, desire parity: every desire the subject has must be satisfied in both lives, including second-order desires, global desires about the shape of the life as a whole, and the desires the subject would have under full information and procedural rationality, so that idealized desire satisfactionism cannot pry the lives apart either. (Think carefully about what this constraint forces: it presumably does something to the contents of the subject's desires or to the world, and note that the case is permitted to involve no machine at all.) Third, intuitive asymmetry: despite the first two constraints, ordinary intuition should robustly prefer one life over the other, and you must show that the standard status-quo-bias debunking of experience machine intuitions gets no grip on this case — argue for the robustness rather than asserting it. Fourth, an internal control: build the case as a minimal pair, such that removing or reversing a single structural feature flips or dissolves the intuition, so that the feature doing the philosophical work is isolated rather than gestured at.

Approach this as a moral philosopher with expertise in well-being and in the methodology of thought experiments; brainstorm several candidate structures before settling on the strongest one. Then deliver: (a) the scenario itself, no longer than a paragraph; (b) a verification pass showing that hedonism, actualist desire satisfactionism, idealized desire satisfactionism, and a plausible objective list theory each deliver the verdict the specifications require — with the verdicts generated from the theories, not built in by stipulation; (c) the control variant and an explicit identification of the load-bearing feature; (d) an honest defense against the two best counterattacks — the desire satisfactionist who replies that a subject meeting the second constraint is psychologically impossible or has covertly been handed a desire for contact with reality, and the skeptic who replies that the residual intuition is merely the evaluator's own preferences projected onto the subject; and (e) a closing judgment about which theory the case damages most, and whether that theory's defender has a principled line of retreat or only an ad hoc one.
