Write a deadpan policy white paper, about 700 words, from an invented think tank (call it the Institute for Subatomic Welfare) that takes completely seriously the conjunction of panpsychism and aggregative utilitarianism: if even electrons have some faint glimmer of experience, and suffering counts wherever it occurs, then the moral books have to be redone for literally everything. The paper should propose, in full bureaucratic earnestness, a program to cut total suffering by phasing out complex organisms (far too much anguish per kilogram) and replacing the biomass with enormous quantities of simple matter held in states stipulated to be mildly content -- a kind of Swiftian modest proposal, with cost projections. The comedy has to come from real pressure points, not invented ones: the combination problem handled as an accounting headache (how do trillions of micro-experiences consolidate into a single ledger line, and who signs off on the merger?), the impossibility of introspecting an electron handled via actuarial proxies and confidence intervals, particle accelerators quietly reclassified as interrogation facilities, rocks granted the benefit of the doubt under the precautionary principle. It should be legible as satire to someone who's never heard of Goff or Strawson, but land as fair to someone who has -- exaggerate the view's actual logic, don't distort it. Use white-paper formatting (Executive Summary, Methodology, Projected Welfare Gains, Anticipated Objections) and keep the tone flat and sincere all the way through, no winking at the reader.
