So I'm reading Aristotle's Posterior Analytics in the Mure translation for a reading group on the epistemic regress problem, and I got stuck at Book I, chapter 2, where he's laying out the requirements on the premises of a demonstration:

"Assuming then that my thesis as to the nature of scientific knowing is correct, the premisses of demonstrated knowledge must be true, primary, immediate, better known than and prior to the conclusion, which is further related to them as effect to cause. Unless these conditions are satisfied, the basic truths will not be 'appropriate' to the conclusion... The premisses must be primary and indemonstrable; otherwise they will require demonstration in order to be known, since to have knowledge, if it be not accidental knowledge, of things which are demonstrable, means precisely to have a demonstration of them."

Help me understand this. First, just unpack what he's claiming in the second half -- the move from "the premises must be primary and indemonstrable" to "otherwise they will require demonstration in order to be known" feels like it contains the seed of the whole regress problem, but is he actually stating the regress argument here, or just the pressure that produces it? Second, our group keeps mapping this onto the modern menu (foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism) and treating Aristotle as the first foundationalist, but I'm suspicious. His regress is about demonstration of necessary truths, not about what justifies my everyday beliefs, and "better known than" seems to mean something different than what a modern foundationalist means by basic beliefs being non-inferentially justified. So: where exactly does the modern mapping break? If Aristotle stops the regress by saying first principles are known some other way than demonstration, is that the same move a contemporary modest foundationalist makes, or a genuinely different one? And does the modern trilemma even capture his options -- he discusses circular demonstration later in the book, but infinitism doesn't seem to be on his radar at all. Is that a gap in his argument or a gap in ours?
