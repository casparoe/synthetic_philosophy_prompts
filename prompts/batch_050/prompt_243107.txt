I'm auditing a philosophy course this term and we just did a week on dream scepticism, and I've been getting into a friendly argument with the tutor about the Zhuangzi butterfly passage. His line is that it's basically an ancient Chinese version of Descartes' dreaming argument: you can't reliably tell dream from waking, so you can't trust your experience. I don't think that's right, or at least I don't think it's the interesting part of what's going on. In the passage (as I understand it, working from translation) Zhuangzi dreams he is a butterfly, flitting about quite content, and on waking he doesn't just wonder whether his current experience is veridical -- he wonders whether he's a man who dreamt of being a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming that it's a man. And the chapter closes with talk of the transformation of things, not with any sceptical conclusion.

Could you explain the conceptual differences between the two dream arguments, in a way that takes seriously the possibility that the standard textbook comparison is misleading? The specific things I want untangled: (1) Descartes' dream doubt is meant to undermine beliefs about the external world, whereas Zhuangzi's puzzle seems to be about identity -- but what exactly *is* the identity question here, and is it even coherent, given that answering it seems to require a standpoint outside both the dream and the waking state? (2) Why does the Cartesian machinery (the evil demon, the hunt for certainty, foundational beliefs) simply fail to get a grip on the Zhuangzi case -- is the difference just that Zhuangzi isn't after certainty, or something deeper about what "mistaking dream for waking" would even mean if there's no fact of the matter about which one is real? (3) If the butterfly passage isn't a sceptical argument at all, what philosophical work is the transformation of things doing -- dissolving the question, re-describing it, or answering it in some non-Cartesian way I haven't grasped? And one smaller point that came up in the pub afterwards: Descartes can always fall back on coherence tests (dreams are disjointed, waking life hangs together over time). Does that move work equally well against the butterfly case, or does Zhuangzi's setup resist it for an interesting reason -- e.g. because within the dream the butterfly's experience felt perfectly continuous too?
