I'm a retired chemist, and over the past year or so I've gotten into philosophy, partly because of running arguments I keep having with an old friend from my hiking club. He's fallen hard for a couple of conspiracy theories (a plane crash, a drug approval -- the specifics don't matter much), and I keep noticing the same strange pattern in how he argues. I want to know if the pattern has a name.

Here it is. When we discuss the official account, his standards of proof are absolute: he wants me to account for every entry in the maintenence log, every frame of the footage, and if I can't, that counts as a point for him. But when it comes to his own alternative, the standards evaporate -- a blurry photo and an anonymous forum post are plenty. And the unevenness shows up over time too: when I actually do answer one of his objections, the objection never dies, he just moves on to the next comma to question. Whereas when one of his sources turns out to be fabricated, he shrugs and says the general point stands anyway.

Now, in my lab days I applied uneven standards myself, deliberately. A boring, expected result got a glance; a surprising one got triple-checked. That's not cheating, that's just sense. So I don't think the problem is unequal standards as such -- it's something about the combination: doubt that only fires in one direction, plus objections that can be raised but never retired.

Does this have a name? People tell me he's just closed-minded, but that seems too blunt, since he's endlessly curious on his own side of the ledger. How does it differ from ordinary healthy skepticism, from the Cartesian business of doubting everything on purpose, and from plain bias? And if there's no established term, what would you call it, how would you define it, and where are its edges -- what wouldn't count as an instance of it?
