So I'm a novelist and the plot of my current book basically runs on this: a guy's car dies in the desert, a stranger pulls over and saves his life, and the stranger only asks for the agreed payment once they're both safely back in town — at which point the rescued guy has literally no self-interested reason left to pay. Yeah, it's Parfit's hitchhiker case with the serial numbers filed off. My problem is I want my character to pay, and I want the reader to feel like he's being smart, not a chump. So please argue, as strongly as you can, that handing over the money once you're safe is the *rational* move — that it can be rational to have made yourself into the kind of person who can't weasel out of deals, even when weaseling out is free. Don't dodge the obvious objection that 'rational' just means doing whatever serves you best right now; meet it head on. Give me the whole arguement as a numbered list of exactly five items, one or two sentences each, no intro, no wrap-up, and in American English.
