So quick background: I never studied philosophy or poli sci or anything, I work in hvac, but for the last couple years I've gotten really into the whole "is taxation theft" debate through podcasts and arguing with people online. Next month I'm doing a short talk at my local liberty meetup group — like 15-20 guys, mostly minarchist libertarians, plus two or three self-described anarcho-capitalists who've read their Nozick and love his bit comparing income tax to forced labor, plus a couple center-left guys who come for the arguments. So the room is mostly hostile to me.

Here's my position: I actually think the social contract defense of taxation is pretty weak (nobody signed anything, I get it). My argument is different. I think the whole "taxation is theft" framing assumes you already own your pre-tax income, full stop, and then the state comes and takes some of it. But what counts as yours in the first place is set by a legal and social framework — property deeds, contract enforcement, courts, limited liability, the whole thing. So you can't use "my property rights" to condemn the very system that defines what those rights even are. The anarcho-capitalist picture where you have these fully formed natural property rights and then the state just shows up and skims is, I think, backwards. So taxation isn't theft, not because we consented, but because "theft" doesn't even apply the way they think it does.
