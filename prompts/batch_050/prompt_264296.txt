I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm writing a piece on my country's National Commemorations Review Comittee (fictional-ish setup, but the rulings below are the real record I'm working from). The committee decides what to do when a statue, street name, or building name gets challenged. So far it's ruled:

1. Statue of a 19th-century shipping magnate whose fortune came mostly from the slave trade, erected by his family in the main square: REMOVE.
2. Street named after a colonial governor who also personally funded and designed the city's first clean water system: KEEP, add a contextual plaque.
3. University hall named after a Nobel-winning biologist whose later advocacy of eugenics grew directly out of his own research program: RENAME.
4. Statue of an independence-era general who, in the same campaign he's celebrated for, ordered the massacre of a village: KEEP.
5. Square named after a beloved poet who spent his last decade churning out propaganda for a long-defunct fascist regime: KEEP.
6. Hospital wing named after a pioneering surgeon whose famous techniques were developed partly through experiments on patients who never consented: RENAME.

The next case, which I'll have to report on before they rule, is this: the city's most famous astronomer, who founded the observatory, discovered a comet, and left his entire fortune to the city's science museum. His private diaries have just surfaced and they're full of genuinely vicious racial views -- but as far as anyone can tell, those views never touched his science, his hiring, or his public life, and he never acted on them in any documented way. There's now a petition to take his name off the observatory, and a counter-petition to keep it. The local community is loudly split.

Here's what I want from you: predict the committee's verdict on the astronomer. But the real point is the reasoning. Spell out at least two different principles that each fit all six prior rulings, show how they diverge on this case, and tell me which principle you think the committee is actually running and why. Then give me the one hypothetical future case that would best tell those principles apart -- the case where they'd have to rule differently depending on which rule they're on. And flag honestly: if they rule to rename the observatory, what principle have they effectively committed to, and if they keep it, what principle then?
