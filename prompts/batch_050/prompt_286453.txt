Let's say Max Weber gets dropped into the present day and spends a year reading about the modern economy. There's two things I'd love to know his take on. First, the FIRE movement: mostly secular software people who live extremely frugally, track every expense, optimize their savings rate, all so they can stop working by 40. Would Weber see this as the ascetic ideal reborn with the religious part surgically removed? And does the fact that the whole point is to escape work rather than fulfill a calling break his theory, or does it actually confirm his story about the spirit of capitalism surviving after its religious roots died? Second, what would he make of the growth of South Korea, Taiwan, and China, none of which had alot of Protestants? He argued Confucianism taught adjustment to the world rather than the inner tension that drove rational mastery over it, but these places industrialized anyway. Would he concede that culture wasn't doing the causal work he thought, or double down and say they somehow imported the spirit of capitalism without the religion? And more generally, would he still defend explaining economic outcomes through values, or would he accept the standard modern complaint that you can always dig up some value to credit after the fact, so the thesis cant really be tested? I'm curious where you think he'd land and also where you'd push back on him.
