write me a parody press release plus annex from the fictional Hedonic Standards Authority, the federal body that each quarter sets the official conversion rates between pleasure and pain, in a country whose constitution enshrines lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. quick background so we're on the same page about what's being satirized: under this view, the whole point of policy is to minimize suffering; suffering below a fixed intensity threshold is fully commensurable with pleasure (so many stubbed toes legitimately offset by so many excellent sandwiches), but suffering above the threshold cannot be outweighed by any amount of pleasure whatsoever, and any policy that produces or even risks a single instance of it is prohibited outright.

Format: headline, then the release, then a short annex with this quarter's exchange-rate table. Maybe 600-700 words total, in flawless deadpan regulator-speak -- press-office boilerplate, footnotes, a quote from the Authority's chairperson, contact line at the end.

The jokes have to come from the real structure of the view, not from strawmen. The threshold exists precisely to dodge the classic objection that crude negative utilitarianism would trade away the whole world's happiness to prevent one more pinprick, and the Authority should say so proudly -- pinpricks, it notes, remain fully convertible at the going rate; we are not monsters. But then land the genuinely awkward stuff, all through bureaucratic detail rather than stated objections:

- the exchange-rate table itself implies insane confidence that pain can be priced in pleasure units: the going mild-toothache-to-concert ratio, seasonal adjustments for sunburn, a caveat that rates are "indicative" pending further research
- threshold arbitrariness: pain rated 6.9 on the official scale trades freely while 7.0 is non-convertible and triggers an absolute policy veto; mention industry lobbyists petitioning to keep certain afflictions classified just under the line, and a citizen suing to get his migraines bumped above it so the state owes him absolute protection
- the veto dynamic: since one above-threshold sufferer sinks any policy no matter how much joy it generates, the Authority's enforcement arm has quietly become a nationwide search party for the single worst-off person, and all other business waits until they're found
- close with a forward agenda item on where to set next year's threshold, discussed with the serenity of a central bank nudging interest rates

Don't name any real philosophers; invent the chairperson and staff. A reader who knows the literature should be able to reconstruct the actual problems -- commensurability, where you draw the line, the worst-off person as veto player -- from the paperwork alone, and a reader who doesn't should still find it funny.
