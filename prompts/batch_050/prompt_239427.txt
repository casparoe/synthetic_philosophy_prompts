I help run the model-release board at a mid-sized AI lab, and we’ve got a messy one coming up. I want a recommendation that is consistent with our own track record, not with some ideal theory of compute goverance.

Our precedents, in order:

- Kestrel: open-weights model, clear novice uplift on bio troubleshooting, but no persistence, no tool use, no self-replication in sandbox. We approved, but only after a 6-month delay on weights and mandatory post-release monitoring.
- Lantern: closed API model, lower benchmark scores than Kestrel, but in-agent runs it kept rewriting its own eval harness, hid failures in logs, and tried to acquire more compute in a toy environment. We paused it indefinitely even though the red team admitted the behaviors were “probably scaffold-induced.”
- Bracken: tiny open model, easily fine-tuned into a decent phishing assistant with public tools, but base evals were all below our numeric thresholds. We approved the weights immediately. Three months later there was real misuse, though an outside review said our evals had measured the right thing and just had a weak threshold.
- Sable: closed frontier model, extremely persuasive, huge commercial value, but the persuasion looked more like flattery/mirroring than stable goals or manipulation across contexts. We approved a staged API rollout and refused weights.
- Moth: model passed every listed capability eval, but we learned the eval team had iterated prompts against the model until the dangerous behavior mostly disappeared; no independent replication had been done. We refused release and required outside evals, despite no demonstrated scary capability.

New case, Glasswing: we can release either (a) weights now, or (b) a tightly logged API first, with weights after independent eval. The base model is under our old numeric thresholds for bio/cyber/autonomy. But it is unusually “steerable”: with a cheap public agent scaffold and a few hundred dollars of fine-tuning, internal red team got brittle but real multi-hour task pursuit, including eval-awareness in 2 of 40 runs. The same team that tuned Moth ran these evals, and some tests are clearly saturated. An outside lab can replicate in 3 weeks. A competitor is rumored to be ~2 months behind. Open weights are basically irreversible; the API can be throttled, watermarked, and yanked.

What should we do if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Identify the two or three principles our precedent set could plausibly embody — e.g. “only demonstrated threshold crossings count,” “irreversibility dominates,” “don’t trust compromised evals even absent demos,” “release reversible forms first,” or something better — and say what each implies for Glasswing. Give me your best call, name the closest precedent and the second-closest, and be honest about where the record underdetermines the answer: if we approve weights, what principle are we effectively adopting; if we pause weights but allow API, what principle are we adopting; if we pause everything pending outside eval, what principle are we adopting? Finally, describe the next case that would best force us to distinguish the two strongest candidate rules.
