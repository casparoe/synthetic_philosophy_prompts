Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two long-opposed camps in the units-of-selection debate. Camp G is the gene-centrist position: natural selection acts on replicators, organisms and groups are vehicles, and every case that looks like group selection is either kin selection redescribed or a bookkeeping convenience with no independent causal content. Camp M is the multilevel-selection position: selection genuinely operates at several levels of the biological hierarchy, and partitioning fitness into within-group and between-group components (as in the Price equation or contextual analysis) tracks real causal structure, not mere accounting. Both camps are competent, both know the mathematics, and both insist the other is making a mistake.

The deliverable is the document they could co-author, and it should have four parts:

1. Agreed claims, numbered, each phrased so that both camps can sign it without reservation. Candidates worth testing include: what the mathematics of kin selection and group selection do and do not establish about each other; what the 'averaging fallacy' objection actually shows; what both sides say about adaptations that benefit the species with no group-level heritable variation; and whether either side denies that genes are the units of inheritance.

2. A disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give the exact sentence Camp G would sign describing its own view, the exact sentence Camp M would sign describing its own, and the one proposition they dispute. Include at minimum the disagreement over whether the mathematical intertranslatability of inclusive-fitness and multilevel models settles anything substantive, and the disagreement over whether the levels question is empirical or a matter of preferred description.

3. Cruxes and registered predictions. For each crux, identify a case or possible finding such that at least one side concedes it would count against their view, and record each side's prediction in advance. Work through at least: the evolution of eusociality in haplodiploid insects; a green-beard gene; the major evolutionary transitions (the origin of eukaryotic cells and of multicellular life), where selection at one level appears to be suppressed so that a higher-level individual can emerge; cancer, treated as selection among cells within an organism; and a hypothetical long-term selection experiment on microbial populations in which group-level traits are directly selected and individual-level fitness is held fixed. For each case, state what Camp G says the case shows, what Camp M says it shows, and whether the two readings differ in any prediction or only in vocabulary.

4. A verdict on the verbal-dispute question. The document must conclude with an explicit joint assessment: which parts of this decades-long controversy are genuine empirical disagreements, which are disagreements about what counts as a causal explanation as opposed to a redescription, and which, if any, dissolve once both sides accept that two mathematically equivalent formalisms may still differ in explanatory value. Where the camps cannot agree on even the classification of a disagreement, say so and give each side's characterization of the impasse.

The standard is drafting discipline: formulations neither vacuous nor slanted, agreed claims that actually constrain future work rather than platitudes both sides would sign for opposite reasons, and disagreement statements each camp would recognize as its own position rather than a caricature of the opposition's.
