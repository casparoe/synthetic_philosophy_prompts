I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, currently writing a dissertation chapter on collective minds, and I've been stress-testing my own position after a workshop where someone pressed me on whether my views are jointly coherent. I'd like an audit of the following set of commitments, which I currently hold and which feel natural to me as a cluster: (1) Functionalism is correct in its core claim that what makes something a bearer of mental states is the causal organization of the system, not the material it is made of, so mentality is multiply realizable in principle. (2) Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary thought experiment is a legitimate intuition pump: an ant colony whose individual ants play suitably organized functional roles could, in principle, be conscious and have beliefs at the colony level, over and above whatever minds the individual ants have. (3) Actual ant colonies are not minded, and my reason for saying this is that individual ants are too simple and the signaling bandwidth between them too low to sustain anything like a cognitive architecture. (4) I'm broadly sympathetic to the Dennettian idea that whether a system has beliefs comes down to whether taking the intentional stance toward it is the best predictive strategy available to us. (5) Despite (1) and (2), I share Block's intuition about the nation-of-China case: a country's population walkie-talkie-simulating the functional organization of a human brain would not thereby constitute a conscious mind.

My sense is that at least one of these tensions is merely apparent, but I also suspect I'm helping myself to something somewhere that one of my own commitments rules out. Please identify which pairs genuinely conflict, explaining what each commitment's usual motivation commits me to and where those motivations cannot be jointly satisfied; which pairs only appear to conflict and why the appearance dissolves; and what the least costly revision to the package would be, including what I would have to concede. Order the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and be specific about which commitment has to go or be reformulated in each case. I'd also ask that you indicate how confident you are in your analysis overall, and single out explicitly the one point in it you are least sure of. Please write your answer in American English.
