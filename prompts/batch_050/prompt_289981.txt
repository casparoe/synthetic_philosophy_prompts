I'm preparing a short position statement for a graduate seminar on philosophy of psychiatry, and before I circulate it I'd like an outside audit of whether my commitments actually cohere. Here is roughly where I've landed: (1) Hacking is essentially right that human classifications are interactive — a diagnostic category feeds back on the people it applies to, because they learn of the classification and adjust their behavior, self-understanding, and self-presentation in response to it; (2) nevertheless, the proper long-term ambition of psychiatric taxonomy is to identify genuine underlying disease entities, not merely to codify whatever distinctions clinicians happen to find administratively useful; (3) the steep rise in autism spectrum diagnoses over recent decades reflects, for the most part, a real increase in prevalence with environmental or other somatic causes, rather than changes in diagnostic practice, public awareness, or self-presentation; (4) it is on balance a good thing when people adopt psychiatric or neurodivergence labels as identities without a formal diagnosis, since such labels provide self-understanding and community even where clinical verification is absent; and (5) longitudinal epidemiological studies that hold a fixed diagnostic checklist constant across decades are a reliable instrument for detecting genuine changes in the incidence of psychiatric conditions.

Something in this package feels unstable to me, but I can't localize the problem, and I'd rather not start revising at random. Please go through the set and tell me which pairs or triples of commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, ranking any real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say precisely what would have to be given up to resolve it and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single change would restore the most coherence at the lowest price. I'd ask that you discriminate rather than flag everything — if two of my commitments look uncomfortable together but are in fact compatible, say so and explain why the appearance of conflict is misleading.
