For my debate club's final round this year, I've committed to defending the claim that the world has become less violent over the course of human history. Before I lock in that position, I want to know what would count against it. Please map out my position's exposure: which facts or arguments would genuinely defeat it, and which would only embarrass it. In particular, address the definitional problem -- if I get to choose what counts as violence (war deaths? homicide? corporal punishment? cruelty to animals? psychological harm?), does my claim have a real fact of the matter behind it, or is it just a consequence of how I draw the boundaries? Address also the per capita versus absolute tension: does a century with proportionally fewer but numerically more violent deaths support or refute me, and is there a principled way to decide which measure matters? Finally, tell me what a smart opponent on the other side would treat as my claim's weakest point, what a defender of the decline thesis would herself concede as genuine trouble for it, and whether the thesis has been hedged with so many qualifications that it risks nothing at all -- and if so, whether that's fatal for a debate or just a feature of big historical claims.
