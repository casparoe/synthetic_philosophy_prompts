So I make music for a living (small indie label, nothing huge) and we just got a pretty heated email calling one of our signees's tracks appropriative, which has sent me down a rabbit hole on this whole topic. The annoying thing is my gut gives me a bunch of verdicts that I cant seem to fit under one rule. White guitarist from Chicago playing the blues: fine, that's just the blues. A fast fashion brand printing Navajo-inspired patterns on shirts with no Navajo involvement or money going back: not fine. My friend, a very non-Italian guy, opening a Neapolitan pizza place: completely fine, nobody should even blink. Wearing a Plains-style headdress to a festival: obviously not fine. A white singer covering a Black artist's song and getting famous off it while the original artist stays broke and unknown: that one bothers me a lot, but I'm not sure I can say why in a way that doesn't also convict the blues guitarist. A non-Indian yoga teacher who never mentions yoga's roots: mild bad taste, not a crime. A Japanese designer riffing on Scottish tartan: fine. White influencer wearing box braids: genuinely can't decide.

Is there a single principle that delivers these, or do I have to give one up? I keep reaching for stuff like power asymmetry, or sacred vs everyday meaning, or whether credit and money flow back, but each one seems to save some verdicts by sacrificing others. Please tell me which of my verdicts your principle has to drop, if any, and what it predicts for two cases I didn't list -- say, a white chef cooking Oaxacan food after years of training there, and someone getting a tattoo of a symbol from a culture they have no connection to. Also, say how confident you are in the principle you land on and flag the specific point where you're least sure you're right.
