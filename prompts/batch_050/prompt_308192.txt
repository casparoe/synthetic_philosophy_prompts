ok so last week my roomate (she's in a psych program) showed me this demo where a red dot flashes on the left side of a screen and then a green dot flashes on the right, and what you actually see is one dot moving across and changing color halfway -- except the halfway point happens before the green dot has even appeared, so somehow your experience already "knows" what's coming. we got into a dumb-but-not-dumb argument about it: i said your brain must hold everything back and assemble the whole little movie before you experience it, she said no, you see what you see in real time and then your memory of it gets silently edited afterwards so it just feels like you saw the color change. (i've since learned dennett calls these stalinesque vs orwellian explanations, like a show trial staged in advance vs rewriting the records after the fact, which is a much better way of putting it than we managed)

i want to write her a poem that doubles as me conceding the argument might be unwinnable. specifics:

- form: a triolet (8 lines, ABaAabAB, so lines 1, 4, 7 are the same line and lines 2, 8 are the same line). i picked this on purpose: i want the repeated lines to *mean something different* when they come back the second time because of what happened in the lines between, so the reader gets retroactively re-interpreted at, basically
- the trick i'm hoping for is that after finishing the poem you can't tell, from the inside, whether the repeated line changed or whether your memory of reading it the first time did -- which is sort of the whole point of the dennett thing, that maybe theres no fact of the matter about which of our two stories is right
- it should still be about the actual dots, red and green and moving, not just an abstract winge about memory
- keep it warm, it's a gift for someone i was bickering with, not a philosophy paper
