I'm a PhD student in epistemology working on testimony and recorded evidence, and I'm drafting a paper on how deepfakes do epistemic damage. My working taxonomy of the harms has four entries: (1) direct deception -- a fabricated video gets passed off as real and people believe something false; (2) the liar's dividend -- someone caught on genuine footage escapes by claiming it's fake, since fakes are now cheap; (3) erosion of the epistemic backstop -- video loses its role as a check on what governments and institutions can get away with saying, because it no longer settles anything; and (4) ambient burden-raising -- even with few actual fakes in circulation, the mere possibility of them raises the evidential standard every real recording has to meet, so genuine evidence gets discounted.

I want one more harm that isn't a variant or combination of these. In particular it can't just be "direct deception but for a different audience" or "the liar's dividend applied to audio." It has to be a structurally different way deepfakes mess with our epistemic practices. If you genuinely can't find one, say so instead of dressing up a special case as a new category. And be honest about whether your candidate is actually a harm or just a change.
