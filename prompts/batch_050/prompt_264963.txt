I'm in the eleventh grade and our philosophy club recently read the Ant Fugue dialogue from Gödel, Escher, Bach, in which Hofstadter imagines an ant colony, Aunt Hillary, that is itself intelligent even though no individual ant is. Ever since, two club members have been going back and forth without getting anywhere. One of them defends what I'd call the organizationalist view: if a colony as a whole processes information, responds flexibly to its environment, and exhibits coherent, goal-directed behavior, then there is a real mind there, and the fact that its "neurons" are ants rather than cells is irrelevant. The other defends what I'd call the naturalist view: ant colonies are just aggregates of many small minds interacting by chemical signals, and calling the colony a mind is a metaphor we fall for because we describe it at the wrong level; genuine mentality requires the kind of tight, unified causal integration you find in an animal brain. They keep trading the same two or three examples, so I've volunteered to help them run an adversarial collaboration and write it up.

Please draft the joint statement they should produce. I want, first, a numbered section of claims both sides can genuinely sign -- for instance, about what ant colonies demonstrably do (collective decision-making, distributed problem-solving, no central controller), about what would and would not follow from the colony passing various behavioral tests, and about the distinction between the colony being a convenient level of description and being an additional subject of experience. Second, a disagreements table in which each remaining dispute is stated precisely and each side's position is phrased in words that side would actually accept, not a caricature the other side can easily knock down. Third, a list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. I suspect two of these are whether consciousness or mindedness requires a single unified subject, or only the right functional organization however spread out it is, and whether there is a principled difference between neurons communicating across synapses and ants communicating by pheromones, or only a difference of degree. For each crux, give a concrete thought experiment or piece of empirical evidence both would accept as bearing on it -- they might consider, for example, a hypothetical colony whose ants were gradually replaced by tiny robots performing exactly the same signaling roles, or what we'd learn if colony-level behavior turned out to be fully predictable from individual ant rules without remainder -- and record in advance what each side predicts that case would show.

Finally, if part of their disagreement turns out to be verbal -- for instance, if one of them is using "mind" to mean roughly "system worth describing with beliefs and desires" while the other means "being with something it is like to be it" -- please identify that explicitly, explain why it is verbal rather than substantive, and state what genuine disagreement, if any, would remain once the wording is cleaned up. The document should be something both of them could put their names to without feeling their view was misrepresented.
