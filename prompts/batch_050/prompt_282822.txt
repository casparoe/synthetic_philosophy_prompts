I'm a graduate student helping organize my department's annual philosophy of probability workshop, and this year we are recording short interview segments with the invited speakers to release as teaching material afterward. One of our guests is a philosopher well known for defending the halfer answer to the Sleeping Beauty problem — the view that when Beauty wakes on Monday, her credence that the coin landed Heads should remain 1/2, even though she knew in advance she would be awakened either way. I have been told I push too gently in interviews, letting guests restate their published positions without friction, so this time I want to prepare properly. Draft eight to ten interview questions that would put real pressure on the halfer position. The audience will be advanced undergraduates who already know the standard setup, so nothing should ask the guest to simply rehearse the case or summarize the debate.

I would like at least one question on each of the following pressure points: first, the betting argument — thirders argue Beauty should accept a bet on Tails at 1:2 odds, so ask the guest to explain the halfer response (roughly, that bets are effectively placed twice in the Tails branch, which decouples acceptance rates from credences) and probe whether that response survives if the bet is offered only once, on a randomly selected awakening; second, the question of whether Monday's awakening provides any new evidence at all, given that it was guaranteed, and whether "today is Monday rather than Tuesday" counts as genuinely new information; third, the extreme version of the case in which Tails triggers a million awakenings, and whether the halfer can honestly remain at 1/2 there without the position losing its intuitive appeal; and fourth, whether taking a stand on Sleeping Beauty commits one on neighboring anthropic issues, such as the self-indication assumption or doomsday-style reasoning, where the halfer and thirder answers seem to align with opposite camps.

Finally, I want to test the guest with a variant I have devised myself, to see whether the position generalizes: suppose the coin is not tossed on Sunday night but on Monday evening, after Beauty's first awakening, so that when she first wakes no outcome exists yet. If Tails will still trigger the memory-erasing second awakening, what should her credence be at that first awakening that tonight's toss will land Heads? Include this as one of the questions, and for every question give me a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give — including, where relevant, the follow-up for the case in which the guest concedes part of the point and retreats to a weaker claim, since that is the moment my past interviews have gone soft.
