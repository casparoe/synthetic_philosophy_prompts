Here's the analogy. Whenever two of us disagree about something -- politics, whether some movie is good, whether we should take the job offer -- someone says: look, if you had two thermometers that you trust equally and one reads 21 degrees and the other reads 25, the rational thing is to figure it's about 23. Neither thermometer gets special treatment. So when two people who are roughly equally smart, equally informed, and equally good at reasoning disagree, each should move halfway toward the other's view. Splitting the difference isn't a compromise for the sake of peace, it's just what rationality requires. This gets used to argue that standing your ground in a disagreement with a peer is literally as irrational as trusting your own thermometer over an identical one for no reason.
