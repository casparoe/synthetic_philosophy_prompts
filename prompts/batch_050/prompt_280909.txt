I work for a mid-sized health insurer as an internal ombuds officer. Two or three times a month I have to rule on a complaint from a policyholder who says a claims adjuster "lied" to them. Our company code of conduct punishes lying with dismissal but says nothing explicit about misleading, and upper management has asked me to propose consistent standards before we amend the code. I have about half a day per case, access to call transcripts and written correspondence but no live testimony, and my rulings are reviewed by legal but not by anyone with training in philosophy of language.

I want you to turn whatever the philosophy of language and ethics actually know about the lying/misleading distinction -- assertion versus implicature, the conditions on assertion, the difference between what is said and what is merely suggested or left unstated, and the debate over whether misleading is morally better than lying or morally on a par with it -- into a decision procedure I can run on a case file in about two hours. The deliverable should be:

1. An ordered sequence of tests or questions I apply to the utterance in question (for example: what exactly was asserted, what was conversationally implied, what was left silent, what would a reasonable policyholder with this transcript have concluded, and did the adjuster intend that conclusion). Spell out each test concretely enough that a colleague of mine who has never heard of Grice could apply it, and say at each step what I should record.

2. A classification scheme the procedure outputs. I need at least: straightforward lie; misleading-but-not-lying; technically true answer to a question other than the one asked; strategically true statement that invites a false inference; and honest statement. If your procedure produces a category I haven't listed, explain why the list needs it.

3. The points where the procedure cannot settle the case and judgment takes over -- for instance, cases where whether something was implied rather than asserted is genuinely indeterminate, or where intent is only inferable from circumstantial evidence. Mark these explicitly rather than hiding them in the procedure, and tell me what to write in my ruling when I hit one.

4. Three worked applications to hypothetical transcripts I supply below. One of them should be a case where your procedure yields a ruling a complainant will find outrageous -- e.g., conduct that feels like a lie but classifies as mere misleading, or vice versa -- and your write-up of that case should say how I should phrase the ruling so it survives legal review and does not insult the policyholder's intelligence.

The transcripts to work through:

(A) A policyholder asks whether her appeal "can still be filed." The adjuster, knowing the deadline is tomorrow and that the policyholder almost certainly believes it is next month, answers: "Yes, the appeals window is still open," and says nothing further.

(B) A policyholder asks, "Does my plan cover this drug?" The adjuster replies, "I can't confirm coverage at this time," knowing the drug is in fact covered and knowing that policyholders almost universally interpret that phrase as a no.

(C) A policyholder asks whether a claim was denied because of a pre-existing condition exclusion. The adjuster knows it was, but answers, "Denials in this category are reviewed against the plan's full terms," which is true and answers nothing.

Two further requests. First, in a short prefatory section, separate what is broadly settled in the literature on lying and misleading (e.g., the existence of the assertion/implicature distinction) from what is genuinely contested (e.g., whether there is a principled moral asymmetry between lying and misleading), and do not present any contested position as the consensus. Second, after the worked applications, give me a structured summary of the three rulings as a JSON object with these fields: case_id; utterance (short verbatim excerpt); tests_applied (array of the test names in the order run); classification (one of the categories above); point_of_indeterminacy (null if none); ruling_summary (one sentence I could paste into my report); and complainant_reaction_risk (low, medium, or high, with a brief justification appended).
