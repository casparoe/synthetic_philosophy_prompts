My reading group has been stuck on the original vs derived intentionality fight for basicly a whole semester and I want a diagnosis, not another rundown of the two positions. Quick sketch of where we're at. The Searle-ish people say: the states of a thermostat or the squiggles inside a computer mean something only because someone built or reads them that way, strip away the users and it's just physics, whereas my belief that it's raining is about rain all by itself with no interpreter needed, and that's a difference in kind not degree. The Dennett-ish people answer with the two-bitser: a coin sorter built to accept US quarters gets shipped somewhere it keeps happily working on a different coin that matches the same spec, and now its internal state is "about" the new coins if it's about anything, what its states mean is fixed by what it was selected and used for, not by any intrinsic glow, and our brains are in the same boat except evolution and our fellows did the designing. Both sides agree on all the facts here -- both even agree there's no determinate answer to whether the relocated sorter meant quarters or the other coin -- and yet the Searle side concludes "so the machine never really meant anything" while the Dennett side concludes "so meaning is always like this." My hunch is the real disagreement is about whether there has to be a determinate fact about what a state means for it to mean anything at all, i.e. whether semantic indeterminacy is a dealbreaker or just the normal case. Is that right, or is something else doing the work -- a factual disagreement about brains, a verbal one about the word "original," or two different questions getting argued under one heading? Diagnose it from what each side actually commits to, and tell me what, if anything, could move it: a distinction, an empirical result, a better question. Also, to keep it honest: we keep stress-testing the views on a case we invented, a lab-grown clump of neurons trained with rewards to fire a specific pattern whenever a certain chemical appears in its dish, where the trainers intend the pattern as an alarm and the clump has no selection history beyond the training itself. Does that case actually discriminate between the positions, or does each side just redescribe it in its own vocabulary? If it doesn't discriminate, what kind of case would?
