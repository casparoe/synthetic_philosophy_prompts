I am a novelist drafting a book whose central character, a methodical and intelligent woman of forty, decides after a brush with mortality to organize the rest of her life around one explicit goal: being happy. She keeps records of her moods, optimizes her schedule around activities she has rated as pleasurable, and evaluates every relationship by how it makes her feel. I intend the novel to dramatize the paradox of hedonism — the idea that happiness pursued directly tends to elude the very person pursuing it. Please describe the most important implications of this paradox for how I should construct her arc: whether her failure should feel necessary or merely contingent on her temperament, whether any resolution requires her to abandon the goal entirely or only to stop monitoring it, how the distinction between momentary pleasure and whole-life satisfaction should shape the plot's turning points, and whether the narrative voice should diagnose her mistake openly or leave it for the reader to infer.
