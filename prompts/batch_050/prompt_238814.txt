I’m putting together a two-page proposal for a one-term graduate research project on wu-wei and the paradox of trying not to try. In seminar everyone seemed happy to say that deliberately trying to be effortless is self-defeating, but that felt much too quick to me. My half-formed idea is that the usual formulation lumps together at least three things: deliberately choosing a goal that can only be achieved spontaneously, monitoring oneself while acting, and using some earlier training or preparation so that later action can proceed without deliberate control. I’m thinking of calling these goal-level, monitoring-level and training-level trying. The question I want to pursue is whether wu-wei is impossible only if these are treated as one thing, and whether success should be understood as acting with the right causal history rather than feeling effortless in the moment. The bit I’m less sure about is whether this dissolves the Daoist point too easily: perhaps the real problem is not logical impossibility but a practical instability, because someone who strongly wants effortless excellence may already have the wrong relation to the outcome.
