I'm a novelist working on a book where one of the characters is basically impossible to blame for anything, and I went down a rabbit hole on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. A podcast I listen to (some pop-philosophy thing) summed it up like this, and I want to know if I can trust it:

"Strawson's argument is that since everything is determined by the laws of physics plus your genes and upbringing, you never actually chose to be the kind of person you are. To be responsible for what you do, you'd have to be responsible for your character, and to be responsible for your character you'd have to have chosen it, and to have chosen it you'd need an earlier character you chose, and so on forever. So the whole idea of free will is a regress, which proves determinism makes responsibility impossible. Strawson's conclusion is that nobody is ever responsible for anything they do, so things like guilt, blame, and punishment are all just irrational hangups we should drop."

The regress bit sounds right to me, but two things nag at me. One, I thought the argument was supposed to work even if determinism is false, and the summary leans on determinism pretty hard. Two, does Strawson actually say we should just stop blaming people, or is the claim narrower than that -- something about a specific kind of responsibility? Go through it and tell me what's accurate, what's a distortion, and whether the podcast's you-can't-blame-my-character-for-anything takeaway survives the corrections.
