Write a resignation letter, maybe 550 words, from Maxwell's demon to the physics community, announcing he's quitting after 150-odd years on the job. The tone should be wounded but petty -- he keeps insisting he never violated the second law and that the goalposts moved on him: first they cared about whether he could sort fast and slow molecules (he could, he has the door logs), then Szilard made it about *measuring* things, then Bennett and Landauer pinned the whole entropy bill on him having to erase his memory of which side each molecule was on, which he points out is just... bookkeeping, and now he's being billed kT ln 2 per bit for remembering his own shifts. The humor should come from getting the physics right -- his grievances should track the actual history of the exorcisms of the paradox, so someone who knows the Landauer/Szilard literature laughs instead of wincing -- and by the end the reader should have genuinely felt why "information is physical" isn't just a slogan. End with him threatening to take a job at a fluctuation-theorem startup where entropy decreases all the time, briefly.
