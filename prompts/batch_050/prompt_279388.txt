I’m in a philosophy of technology seminar and overherad two classmates arguing about a griefbot called Afterword. It was trained on a dead woman’s emails, voice notes, and letters. Her family consented to making an archive, but she never discussed being made into an AI. A new update lets the bot produce new first-person jokes and opinions she never recorded. Every message has a small “generated simulation” label, and some relatives say talking to it genuinely helps them grieve.

Nia argued that the update turns a memorial into an impersonation, because it can put novel words and views in the dead woman’s mouth without her consent. Sam replied that ordinary remembrance also reconstructs people: relatives speculate about what she would have said, biographers invent scenes, and documentaries use actors. Nia answered that those are openly presented as interpretations, while an interactive first-person bot invites users to treat its replies as contact with her. Sam said the label prevents that confusion, and that forbidding any speculative simulation would make almost every biography or memorial unethical. Nia replied that a warning label doesn’t cancel the psychological effect of hearing “I missed you” in her voice, especially when the company profits from keeping people engaged.
