I’m putting together a problem set for my graduate seminar on rational agency, and I want a question that forces students to separate the actual content of program equilibrium from the usual “cooperate because your twin does” slogan. So please take on the role of a careful philosopher of game theory who is comfortable with both the formal details and the conceptual weirdness. Before giving your final answer, do a little visible brainstorming about the different issues involved, and then commit to a number. Use American English throughout.

Here’s the setup. Two deterministic agents play a one-shot prisoner’s dilemma, with the usual preference ordering T > R > P > S. Each agent can inspect the other agent’s complete source code before choosing C or D, and each knows that the other has this ability. Suppose both agents are running the same general kind of transparent-source reasoning procedure, though not necessarily literally identical code. The proposition I want your credence on is: **In this idealized setup, mutual cooperation is rationally privileged over mutual defection, rather than the two outcomes merely being alternative equilibria or the result depending entirely on implementation details.** Please don’t just say “it depends.” I want a probability from 0 to 1 that the proposition is true, together with the reasoning decomposed into its main load-bearing parts.

In your decomposition, give a separate probability and a one- or two-sentence justification for at least these sub-questions: whether the setup has been specified precisely enough to privilege any outcome; whether access to source code gives each agent genuine evidence about the other’s choice rather than merely another payoff table; whether symmetry between the agents can rationally support cooperation without smuggling in causation over the other program; whether small implementation differences or noise would destroy the argument; and whether “rationally privileged” should mean a unique equilibrium, an especially salient equilibrium, or something normatively stronger. Explain how these probabilities combine into your final credence, identify the term doing the most work, and say what change in the setup or what kind of argument would most shift your number. If you think the whole question conceals an ambiguity, don’t stop at diagnosing it: state the best disambiguation and estimate your credence for that version anyway.
