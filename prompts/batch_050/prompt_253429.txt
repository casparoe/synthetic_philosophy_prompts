After years of reading rationalist blogs and probability-theory threads, I keep seeing Lewis-style triviality results invoked as if they settle the correct semantics for indicative conditionals. I would like to understand whether there is an underexplored way out.

Consider the following simplified version of the result. Let Ω be a finite set of possible worlds, let B be a Boolean algebra of propositions over Ω, and suppose that every indicative conditional A ⇒ C is itself a proposition in B. Let K be a class of probability measures on B such that

P(A ⇒ C) = P(C | A) = P(A ∧ C) / P(A)

whenever P ∈ K, A,C ∈ B, and P(A) > 0. Finally, suppose K is closed under conditionalization: if P ∈ K and P(E) > 0, then the updated measure P_E defined by P_E(X) = P(X | E) also belongs to K. Under suitable richness assumptions, this package collapses into triviality: conditional probabilities are forced toward 0 or 1, leaving no room for an ordinary, nondegenerate probability space.

The standard escape routes I already know are these:

1. Localize the identity. The equation holds for one background probability function but need not continue to hold after that function is conditionalized on new evidence.

2. Restrict the conditional syntax. The equation applies only to simple conditionals, while Boolean compounds or nested conditionals are excluded from its scope.

3. Deny that indicatives express propositions. An indicative conditional instead conveys an assertibility condition, inferential disposition, or update rule, so it is a category mistake to assign it an unconditional probability.

4. Replace conditionalization with imaging. The probability of A ⇒ C tracks the measure produced by minimally moving probability mass from each not-A world to an appropriate A-world, rather than the ratio measure P(· | A).

5. Contextualize the conditional. The same sentence expresses a different proposition in different epistemic states, so there is no single proposition whose probability must equal the relevant conditional probability across every measure in K.

Please propose a sixth escape route that is not merely a relabeling, special case, or conjunction of these five. It should be mathematically specific enough to engage the displayed assumptions rather than remaining at the level of saying that ordinary language is flexible. In particular:

- Identify exactly which assumption above it rejects or reinterprets.
- Exhibit a finite toy model with at least three atomic outcomes, one nondegenerate probability measure, and one nontrivial learning event, or else prove that such a model exists.
- Show why the proposed route avoids collapse after learning, including under at least one second update where relevant.
- Explain precisely why it is not just route 1–5 in new terminology.
- Assess whether the proposal has any independent motivation beyond avoiding the theorem, and identify its largest theoretical cost.

I am not asking for historical novelty, references, or a bibliography: a well-constructed response new relative to this list is sufficient. If you think every apparently distinct escape collapses into one of the five, say so and give the reduction explicitly rather than inventing a merely verbal sixth option.
