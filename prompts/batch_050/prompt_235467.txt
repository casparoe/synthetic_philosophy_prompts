1. It's rational -- maybe even obligatory -- for laypeople to defer to mainstream scientific consensus on things like vaccines and climate, since none of us can check the evidence ourselves.
2. The vaccine-skeptic and climate-skeptic folks I argue with online are in echo chambers in roughly C. Thi Nguyen's sense: their community gives them standing reasons to distrust outside sources, so contrary evidence just bounces off.
3. Their way of forming beliefs is irrational, whereas mine (trusting mainstream institutions) is rational -- it's not just that we picked different teams.
4. Nobody can step outside their epistemic network altogether. Everyone has to trust *some* structure of testimony and institutions; there is no view from nowhere, so the only question ever is which network you're inside.
5. The replication crisis and the history of industry-funded science show that distrust of the mainstream is sometimes the right call.

Where does this pinch? My worry is that 1 and 3 sit badly with 4, but I can't quite see it clearly. Please rank the tensions by how bad they are, say which pairs only *look* like they conflict, and for the real ones tell me the cheapest revision -- though I'm not telling you which commitment I'd rather drop. Also: if escaping an echo chamber requires some kind of full epistemic reboot, and 4 says that's impossible, am I even entitled to blame the people in 2 for being stuck?
