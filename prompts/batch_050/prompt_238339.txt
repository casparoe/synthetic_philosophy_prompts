Everyone I talk to about Lewis Carroll's tortoise ends up giving me one of the same four moves, and I want something new. The ones I already know, and don't need repeated: (1) the standard lesson that modus ponens is a rule of inference, not a premise, so writing it into the premises just restarts the regress one level down; (2) the dispositional move -- following a rule is something you *do*, a practice or habit, not a proposition you accept, so no extra premise is required; (3) the meaning-constitution move, where willingness to infer by modus ponens is part of what it is to understand "if...then" at all, so the tortoise can't really grasp the conditional while withholding assent; and (4) the levels move, that the tortoise conflates object-language inference with metalinguistic claims about validity. Give me a fifth response to the tortoise that isn't a rewording, special case, or combination of these. Then tell me honestly whether you think it actually works, or whether the tortoise wins in the end.
