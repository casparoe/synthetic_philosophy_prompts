Last week I got into an argument with a friend from my graduate reading group about Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action (zhixing heyi), as developed in his recorded conversations in the Chuanxilu. The dispute started over the passage where Wang denies that someone can truly know, say, filial piety or the foulness of a bad odor without this knowledge already involving the corresponding response -- he treats loving one's parents and shrinking from a stench as cases where the knowing and the doing are not two separable stages, and he explicitly says he offered the doctrine as a corrective to people who treated study as complete in itself and never got around to practice. My friend reads this as a straightforward identity thesis: knowing just is acting, full stop, and anyone who claims to know what is right but fails to do it simply does not know. That struck me as making the doctrine trivially true by redefinition, and as failing to explain why it was ever controversial or why Wang thought it answered Zhu Xi's view that one must first complete one's knowledge and then act. Neither of us could say what would actually count as a good interpretation here, which made me realize we were arguing without a shared standard.

Acting as a specialist in Ming-dynasty Neo-Confucianism who is also fluent in contemporary philosophy of action and moral psychology, write the desiderata an adequate interpretation of the unity of knowledge and action must meet. Before committing to any reading, brainstorm what the space of requirements even looks like, then give me the settled list: the claims and passages of Wang's it must make sense of (including the filial piety and bad odor cases and his stated therapeutic aim); the apparent counterexamples it must absorb rather than wave away (knowing a road leads somewhere without walking it, knowing a poison is lethal without drinking it, the weak-willed person who seems to know better and do worse); the distinctions it cannot collapse (between knowing-that and knowing-how, between a claim about what knowledge is and a norm about what it should be, between the doctrine as psychology and the doctrine as pedagogy); and the neighboring commitments of Wang's -- innate knowledge, its extension, the claim that the first stirring of a self-serving thought is already action -- with which the interpretation must cohere. For each desideratum, give the consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference of one scholarly camp, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, since I suspect the list itself may be where the real result lies. Then, briefly, indicate how the main candidate readings do against the list: my friend's identity thesis, a reading on which knowledge constitutes or necessarily issues in action rather than being identical with it, a purely normative reading on which genuine knowledge merely ought to be enacted, and a deflationary reading on which the whole doctrine is remedial rhetoric rather than metaphysics.

Format-wise: lead with your bottom line in the first sentence -- which desiderata are genuine and which reading they favor, if any -- then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; the scoring of the four readings can be short, since the criteria are where I expect the disagreement to live.
