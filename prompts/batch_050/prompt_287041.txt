I'm drafting a dissertation chapter on the experience machine and want to anticipate my advisor's objections before circulating it. Her settled verdicts on these cases are: (i) refuse the familiar lifelong machine, even with a guarantee of a blissful simulated existence; (ii) accept a one-week machine with avowedly fictional content, provided she emerges with her memories and values intact; (iii) refuse an otherwise identical one-week machine that would, on exit, leave her indifferent to her daughter and her work; and (iv) accept a month-long fictional-content machine so long as her real obligations are genuinely discharged meanwhile by someone she trusts. Please predict her verdict on a new case: a two-year machine, memory- and value-preserving on exit, whose content never mentions her closest friend — though during those two years the friend will face a real crisis in which, by stipulation, only my advisor's actual support would help. I'd like the candidate principles that fit (i)–(iv), each principle's prediction for the new case, your best guess at her verdict together with your reasons, and the single further case that would most efficiently discriminate between the leading candidates.
