I think there's a principled line to draw, but after three hours of her poking at me I want to check I'm not just vibing with extra steps. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. What makes something an organism isn't having one body or one genome -- it's being a unit that selection can act on as a unit. The parts cooperate, they share a reproductive fate, and cheating from inside gets suppressed or policed. That's the criterion; everything else should follow from it.

2. So a honeybee hive is a straight-up organism, not "like" one. Queen, sterile workers, policing of worker-laid eggs, the works.

3. A Portuguese man o' war, on the other hand, is a colony of many organisms, not one organism. Each zooid is basically a little animal doing its particular job; calling the whole floaty thing one animal is exactly the kind of metaphor my friend accuses people of.

4. But me plus my gut microbiome genuinely is one organism. We digest together, my immune system got trained by them, I can't develop properly without them -- draw the line where, exactly?

5. And a lichen is a real individual even though it's two (or three) species in a trenchcoat. I don't think the multi-species thing is even a problem under my criterion.

6. Also: organism-ness comes in degrees, there's no sharp line anywhere in nature, BUT "organism" is still a real explanatory category in biology and not just a word we get to stipulate.
