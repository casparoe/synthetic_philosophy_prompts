My roommate walked me through this argument last week and I still haven't found where it breaks, but the conclusion has to be wrong. Here it is, as he gave it to me:

(1) Special relativity says which distant events are simultaneous with "me, right now" depends on my state of motion. Two people walking past each other on the street, one strolling toward Andromeda and one away from it, will disagree by days or even weeks about what's happening on Andromeda *now*. That's just the standard relativity of simultaneity, no interpretation of quantum mechanics required.

(2) What's real can't depend on how some pedestrian happens to be walking. Whether an event on Andromeda has already happened is a fact about Andromeda, not about anyone's gait.

(3) So take an Andromeda-event E that falls inside the away-walker's present but outside the toward-walker's. The away-walker is in exactly my epistemic situation regarding E, so she should count E as real and settled, the way I count my own present as real and settled. By (2), that means E is real and settled, period.

(4) Nothing special about E. By chaining observers this way you can reach any event in my future, so every future event is already as real and settled as the present. Block universe.

(5) Nowhere in (1)–(4) did we say anything about the laws of physics or determinism. So the argument shows the future is settled *even if* the laws are irreducibly chancy and the future could have gone differently.

(6) But if the future is settled in the relevant sense, then my deliberating about what to do tomorrow is exactly as pointless as deliberating about what to have done last Tuesday.

(7) So either deliberation is pointless, or special relativity is false. I refuse both.

His conclusion in (5) especially seems like it proves too much -- surely relativity alone can't settle what determinism is supposed to settle. Which step do I reject, why does that step look fine at first glance, and what do I give up by rejecting it? I'd really like to keep (2) and (6) if possible, so if the weak link is elsewhere I'd like to know what rejecting that costs instead -- including whether rejecting it secretly smuggles a privileged reference frame back in.
