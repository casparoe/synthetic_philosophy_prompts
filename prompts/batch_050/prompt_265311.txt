I'm TAing an intro phil mind course and grading short-answer responses. The exam question was:

"Hofstadter's 'Aunt Hillary' imagines a conscious-seeming ant colony whose individual ants are not intelligent. Explain the thought experiment, what it's supposed to show about minds emerging from mindless parts, and how it relates to brains and neurons."

Here's the student's answer, please grade it out of 10 with specific comments justifying the grade. I want a point-by-point assesment, not just a general impression -- tell me what they got right and exactly where (if anywhere) they slip up:

"Aunt Hillary is an ant colony that can hold conversations and apparently has beliefs and desires, even though no single ant is smart. Each ant plays the role of a symbol, the way each neuron in a brain is a tiny symbol, and the colony-level intelligence emerges from the interactions of these simple parts. Hofstadter's point is that mind doesn't require magical ingredients: if you organize enough mindless pieces the right way, you get genuine thought at the higher level, just as the brain's intelligence emerges from neurons that don't understand anything. The thought experiment is basically Hofstadter's version of the Chinese Room argument, showing that systems built from uncomprehending parts can still think. It also shows that reductionism is false, because the colony's intelligence can't be found in any of its parts, only at the emergent level. So a colony can literally have a mind in exactly the same sense a person does, since there's no principled difference between a brain and a sufficiently organized ant colony."
