Something I've been chewing on for a while and I can't figure out if philosophers already have a word for it or if I've just convinced myself its a real thing.

Say I go on holiday for two weeks and my mate Dave promises to water my tomato plants. He totally forgets, plants are dead when I get back. Everyone, me included, says Dave killed the plants. But hold on -- millions of people also didn't water my plants. My neighbor didn't. The prime minister didn't. Every person on earth except Dave failed to water them, and each of those failures is equally "responsible" in the sense that if they'd done it, the plants would be alive. Yet nobody says the neighbor killed them. Somehow we pick ONE of the not-doings out of an infinite pile of not-doings and call it the cause, and the picking seems to be done by who was supposed to do it, not by anything about the actual physics of dead tomatoes.

Another one: a warehouse burns down and the report says the cause was the night watchman not doing his rounds, not the fact that the building was full of oxygen, even though no oxygen also means no fire. We pick the broken expectation over the background condition every time.

So my question is basically three things. Does this have a name -- the business of how a not-happening gets to count as a cause at all, and how we choose which not-happening out of the millions on offer? Second, is this the same thing as that killing vs letting die stuff I've heard about from trolley problem podcasts, or is it a different question (it feels different to me -- one is about blame, the other is about what even counts as a cause before we get to blame)? And third, does the same picking happen with positive events too, like when we say the spark caused the fire and not the oxygen, or is my thing specifically about doings vs not-doings?

If there's no established name, coin one for me and give it a proper definition with the boundary cases -- where does it stop applying? I don't just want a label, I want to know exactly what the thing is and what it isn't.
