Last night I got into a long, unresolved argument with a friend about iterated amplification and distillation as a strategy for building aligned AI. My friend leaned hard on an analogy she clearly thinks settles the matter: iterated amplification, she says, is just the division of labor in a well-run firm. A chief executive cannot personally do or even verify everything the company does, so she breaks each hard problem into pieces small enough for her reports to handle, checks their outputs at a level she can manage, and recursively so on down the org chart -- and nobody worries that the firm is less competent or less honest than the CEO, because each piece was small enough to be understood by somebody above it. Distillation, on her telling, is simply hiring and training a single new employee to internalize the whole organization's procedures so the expensive committee can be retired. Her conclusion was that, just as a firm preserves the founder's judgment while scaling it far beyond one person's capacity, amplification preserves both the competence and the alignment of the original overseer through any depth of decomposition, provided each individual step is human-checkable.

I left the conversation unconvinced but unable to say precisely where the analogy fails.
