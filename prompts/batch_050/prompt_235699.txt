A friend and I have spent two years arguing, without progress, about why moral norms collapse. His view: slavery ended because abolitionists pressed the moral argument until society was persuaded; ideas do the work. Mine: the case against slavery was available in much of its modern form long before abolition, so the arguments were at most a background condition; slavery ended where and when it stopped paying, and moral language arrived afterward to bless the change. Dueling is our second battleground. He says people were reasoned out of it; I say the arguments against dueling were old too, and what killed it was that it came to seem ridiculous — a shift in honor and shame rather than in moral conviction or profit — though I concede this fits my own economic story awkwardly.

I want the document we would produce if we ran a serious adversarial collaboration: a joint statement we could both sign. Please structure it as follows. First, a numbered list of agreed claims: historical points about slavery, dueling, and one or two other dead norms (foot-binding, or smoking in public places) that both of our views must accommodate rather than explain away. Second, a numbered list of our remaining disagreements, each formulated so that the person who holds the view would accept the wording — nothing that frames either position as naive or cynical. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us, and for each crux a historical episode we would both accept as bearing on it, with what each of us would expect that episode to show.

Two further requirements. I have found it useful to split any norm's collapse into the stockpile (arguments and evidence accumulated in advance), the trigger (whatever changed in the period when the norm actually fell), and the ratchet (whatever prevents its return). State each of our positions in these terms, and treat as one crux whether honor is a third factor distinct from both moral argument and material interest, or whether it reduces to one of them. Finally, as a test of whether either view predicts anything, take a norm whose wrongness is already widely argued but which has not collapsed — the industrial farming of animals, say — and have each side commit in advance to what its theory says must happen before that norm changes, and on roughly what timeline. If a view turns out to be compatible with any outcome at all, the document should say so. Close with a candid section on which parts of our dispute you take to be verbal rather than substantive; I suspect, for instance, that we may mean different things by the word cause.
