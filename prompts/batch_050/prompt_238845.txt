I sit on the deployment review board of a mid-sized AI lab. Every month we rule on roughly five proposed safety mitigations -- extra refusal training, interpretability-based deployment monitors, staged rollouts, deliberative alignment techniques, restricted tool access -- and every time, the capabilities team objects that the measure imposes an alignment tax that violates the competitiveness constraint: that a safety technique is only worth adopting if labs that adopt it are not thereby driven out of the race. Both notions get invoked as slogans and neither has ever been operationalized for us. Please turn them into a decision procedure we can actually run in a ninety-minute meeting: an ordered sequence of tests to apply to a proposal, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points where the procedure terminates in a reject or an unconditional ship, and the points where it must hand the question to human judgment because no rule settles it. The procedure should distinguish at least three kinds of tax -- a one-time capability regression, a recurring drag on iteration speed, and foreclosed future research directions -- and it should handle two structural problems: the tax estimates come from teams with incentives to inflate or deflate them, and a tax borne unilaterally differs from one an industry standard would impose on everyone. Then run the procedure on two cases: a proposed oversight scheme that adds fifteen percent to inference cost on our flagship product, and a training-stage intervention that costs nothing now but measurably slows our next model iteration. One of these runs should yield the conclusion we will not want to hear. Give actual decision thresholds -- specific figures for when a tax becomes disqualifying -- with your reasons, rather than a menu of possible policies; and state plainly what the procedure cannot decide and which of its steps are judgment calls dressed up as rules.
