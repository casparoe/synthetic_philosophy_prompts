My oldest friend and I have been having the same argument for nearly a decade: which of our two countries is the more liberal society — Sweden, where I grew up, or Norway, where he did. Neither of us has any academic training in politics or philosophy; I work in logistics and he is a ship's engineer. The argument restarts every summer and never resolves, because we have never once agreed on what would even count as settling it. I recently read about structured "adversarial collaborations," in which two opposed researchers draft a joint statement pinning down exactly where they disagree and what evidence would move them. I want to surprise him with one.

Write that document for us. Cast me as defending "Sweden is the more liberal society" and him as defending "Norway is," and use the following sections, in this order:

1. What we both accept (about 200 words). Numbered claims both of us can sign: that both countries are, by global standards, egalitarian, high-trust, high-tax social democracies; that both run a state retail monopoly on alcohol; that both are more restrictive on recreational drugs than, say, the Netherlands; and that "liberal" cannot simply mean "economically laissez-faire," since on that reading we would both lose to countries we agree are less liberal in everyday life.

2. The disagreement, stated neutrally (about 100 words). One or two sentences per side, each phrased so that side would accept it as fair, plus a sentence about what we are not disputing — neither of us thinks the other's country is unfree or authoritarian.

3. The cruxes (about 400 words). Four to six sub-questions such that settling any one of them would move at least one of us. I suspect these include: whether liberalism is measured by the laws on the books or by lived outcomes; whether paternalistic restrictions justified on health or equality grounds — alcohol monopolies, sugar and tobacco taxes, strict drug policy — count as illiberal or as roughly freedom-neutral; how much weight to give the economic dimensions where the countries genuinely differ, such as Sweden's school vouchers and contracted-out welfare services versus the scale of Norwegian state ownership and its sovereign wealth fund; and whether Sweden's EU membership and later NATO accession, against Norway's staying out of the EU and joining NATO early, bear on liberalism at all. State each crux as an open question, not as a disguised version of my side.

4. Evidence and predictions (about 300 words; a table is fine). For each crux, name at least one concrete comparison, case, or kind of evidence we would both accept as relevant, and write down in advance what each of us predicts it would show — so that neither of us can move the goalposts later.

Two constraints. First, do not appeal to named international freedom or democracy indices: he distrusts them and I cannot defend their methodology, so treat such rankings as at most suggestive. Second, restrict yourself to facts that are broadly known and uncontroversial; the document is useless if either of us can dismiss it as error-ridden.
