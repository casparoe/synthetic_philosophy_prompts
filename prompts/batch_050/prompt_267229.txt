Two of us have been running a small seminar on free will, and we keep circling the same fight, so we've decided to try an adversarial collaboration and want the document drafted for us. The setup: one of us (call her the sphex-skeptic) takes the digger wasp case -- the wasp that drags its paralyzed cricket to the burrow's edge, goes in to inspect, and if the cricket has been nudged a few inches away, drags it back and inspects again, looping through the same routine without noticing the point of it has changed -- as the human condition in miniature. On her view, our deliberation is just a deeper stack of such routines: flexible-looking behavior is scripted behavior whose scripts happen to be conditional on more variables, and the feeling of stepping back and reconsidering is itself a subroutine. The other (call him the flexibility-compatibilist) thinks this proves too much: what the wasp lacks and we have -- responsiveness to reasons, the ability to revise means in light of ends and ends in light of new information -- is not an illusion layered on mechanism but precisely the thing freedom was supposed to be all along. Neither of us doubts that some form of naturalism about the mind is true; the fight is over whether flexibility distinguishes anything that matters.

Draft the joint statement such a collaboration should produce. I want: (1) a numbered section of claims both parties can sign, including what the wasp experiments do and do not show, what habit formation in humans suggests, and what it would even mean for a behavior to count as genuinely revisable rather than multiply scripted; (2) a disagreements table in which each position is stated in wording its own side would accept, with no slanted glosses; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is whether there is any depth of perturbation-testing (keep disrupting a creature's routine and watch when, or whether, it re-plans) that would settle whether the re-planning is itself mechanical, and another is whether 'responsive to reasons' names a real natural kind or just a compliment we pay to complicated scripts; but let the drafter identify the actual cruxes rather than take mine on faith. For each crux, give a case or finding both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction about it in advance. If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal -- for instance, if both would predict the same observations about any possible creature and differ only in which label to pin on it -- say which part, and say it plainly.

One more thing: if the framing I've given rests on a confusion -- say, if treating flexibility as a binary property a creature either has or lacks makes the whole collaboration unanswerable, or if the two positions as I've characterized them don't actually disagree about anything testable -- I would rather be told that directly than receive a polished statement built on sand. Push back where pushback is warranted.
