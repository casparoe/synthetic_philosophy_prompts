Presentists and eternalists have been going at it for decades and as far as I can tell nobody has moved an inch. What strikes me is that both sides accept the exact same physics -- nobody disputes relativity or the block-friendly math of spacetime -- and each side can usually paraphrase whatever the other says into there own idiom (the presentist hears "dinosaurs exist" as "dinosaurs existed," the eternalist hears "nothing exists outside the present" as a weird restriction on quantification rather than a thesis about reality). My hunch is that this means the dispute isn't empirical and maybe not even metaphysical, but a fight about what we mean by "exists," i.e. whether unrestricted quantification is tenseless -- which would make it closer to a verbal disagreement than either side wants to admit. But maybe I'm missing something, e.g. a real leftover question about temporal experience or the arrow of time that survives once the semantics is fixed. So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Diagnose it using what each side actually argues, don't just survey the positions, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a distinction, an argument from physics, or nothing.
