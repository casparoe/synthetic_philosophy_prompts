I run an evening reading group for adults with no background in philosophy or political theory, and next week our session is on the chapter of The Prince where Machiavelli sets out his account of fortuna and virtù. The previous week we read a stretch of Aristotle on the moral virtues, so the group will arrive with the word virtue meaning something like cultivated excellence of character, and I expect that to cause trouble. I do not need the chapter explained to me; I know it reasonably well. What I need is a plan for teaching it in about ninety minutes, covering the following.

First, the order of ideas and the dependencies among them. Should I establish that fortuna is an active, patterned force in human affairs rather than mere randomness before introducing virtù as the human response to it, or the reverse? And where does Machiavelli's observation that a ruler's fixed temperament succeeds in one era and fails in another belong in the sequence, given that it seems to presuppose both notions already in place?

Second, the carrying example. I am inclined to build the session around a pair of contrasting rulers, one cautious and one impetuous, whose unchanging conduct prospers while the times suit it and collapses when they do not. Tell me whether that is the right carrier, what the example must contain and what it must avoid containing, and why Cesare Borgia, whom the group will almost certainly propose, would do the job worse.

Third, the misconception map. I expect at least three: that virtù is moral virtue in the Aristotelian sense they just learned; that fortuna is blind luck, making the chapter's lesson fatalism and effort pointless; and that virtù means adaptability, the capacity to remake one's character to fit the circumstances, which I take to be the modern self-help reading rather than the text's. For each, state the exact point in the sequence where I should head it off and the move that does so.

Fourth, one closing question whose answers would show me whether the account has actually landed.

Finally, what to leave out at this level and say I am leaving out. I am thinking of the chapter's notorious closing image and of the whole question of whether Machiavelli licenses any means for political ends; advise me on how to defer these without appearing to dodge them.

Keep the whole plan to at most 650 words.
