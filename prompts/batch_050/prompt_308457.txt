I run a small manufacturing business with my brother, and over the years of hiring, firing, and watching other owners in our trade association succeed or go under, I've become fixated on a pattern in how people -- myself included -- talk about why someone ended up where they did. When a founder's gamble pays off, the story everyone tells afterward is about her vision, her discipline, her willingness to work weekends; the risky bet gets redescribed as courage and the single-mindedness as dedication. When the identical bet bankrupts the guy two towns over, the same traits get redescribed in the opposite direction: the bet was recklessness, the single-mindedness was tunnel vision, the long hours were a refusal to listen to warnings. I have caught myself doing this within hours of hearing news, rewriting my whole assessment of a person's character based purely on how things turned out, when nothing about the person or their decisions changed at all -- only the outcome did. What gets me is that this isn't just loose talk: the retroactive verdict then does real work. The winner is treated as deserving his success in a way that licenses his wealth and authority, and the loser is treated as deserving his failure in a way that licenses our indifference to it.

I've poked around enough to know this is adjacent to several things that presumably have names -- there's something in psychology about judging decisions by outcomes rather than by what was knowable at the time, something about people's need to believe the world is fair, and something in philosophy about luck and moral assessment -- but none of the glosses I've found quite capture the specific thing I'm describing: the way a moralized character verdict gets manufactured after the fact from an outcome, and then functions as a retroactive justification for whatever rewards or punishments the outcome brought with it. It's not merely that we misjudge the decision; it's that we manufacture a desert claim, and the manufactured desert then props up a distributive arrangement.

So: does this precise phenomenon have an established name, or is it spread across several literatures that each catch only part of it? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence -- the best single label or the conclusion that no established one fits -- and then give the reasoning with no preamble. I'd like you to walk through the nearest candidate concepts, say exactly how each one differs from what I've described (which aspects it covers, which it misses), and identify where the edges of the phenomenon are -- for instance, does it apply when the person really was virtuous and the virtue really did cause the success, or only when the causal story is confabulated? If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it precisely, and give a couple of boundary cases that would and wouldn't fall under it. A bare name without the carving won't satisfy me.
