I am the handling editor for a manuscript submitted to a philosophy of physics journal, and I must write the recommendation memo for our editorial board, since the two referees flatly disagree. The paper argues that the hole argument — the modern reconstruction, due to Earman and Norton, of the reasoning that led Einstein to doubt general covariance between 1913 and 1915 — dissolves once we attend to how Einstein himself introduced general covariance. Its centerpiece is this passage from §3 of Einstein's 1916 "The Foundation of the Generalised Theory of Relativity":

"In the general relativity theory time and space magnitudes cannot be so defined that the difference in spatial co-ordinates can be immediately measured by the unit-measuring rod, and time-like co-ordinate difference with the aid of a normal clock... That in general, Laws of Nature are expressed by means of equations which are valid for all co-ordinate systems, that is, which are covariant for all possible transformations."

The author's inference: since coordinates carry no direct physical meaning, the hole argument's "indeterminism" is an artifact of treating coordinate descriptions as physically distinct, and so poses no threat either to determinism or to spacetime substantivalism. Referee A recommends acceptance: the author has simply stated the now-standard resolution, that diffeomorphically related models represent one physical situation. Referee B recommends rejection: Earman and Norton's argument concerns diffeomorphisms of the spacetime manifold, not coordinate relabelings, so the paper attacks a target no one defends and never engages the actual dilemma.

Draft the memo: a clear recommendation (accept, revise, or reject) with reasons the author could act on; an interpretation of what the quoted passage does and does not establish; and an honest account of the strongest point on the losing side. Please separate what is settled in this literature from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present either referee's view as the consensus.
