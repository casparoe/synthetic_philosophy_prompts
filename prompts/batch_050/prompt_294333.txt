I have no academic background in philosophy; I work in records management, and over the past year I have kept a private log of every occasion on which an AI assistant told me or my colleagues something untrue. Reading a popular summary of Harry Frankfurt's essay on bullshit convinced me that my log lumps together several different phenomena: his bullshitter, who is indifferent to whether what he says is true; his liar, who knows the truth and means to conceal it; and the merely mistaken, who cares about the truth and misses it. I would like a decision tree that a careful non-specialist could apply to each entry in my log, beginning from the single fact that the assistant produced a falsehood and ending at a named category — lying, bullshit, honest error, confabulation, or whatever further categories you think the logical space requires, such as amplifying misinformation the user supplied or presenting an outdated fact as current.

Please observe the following constraints:

- Every branch must be a question answerable from observable facts of the case. Frankfurt's categories turn on states of mind that a language model arguably does not possess, so the tree must sort by features I can actually check — what the system was asked, what it said in earlier turns, whether the falsehood was invented or copied — and must state explicitly which philosophical distinctions it is thereby forced to collapse. I suspect lying and bullshit may be among them.
- Six to nine leaves, each named, each accompanied by one sentence on its most awkward commitment, particularly regarding where it locates responsibility: the model, the user, the developers, or the training data.
- Construct the tree from the questions first and locate the categories in the answer-space afterward; note any combination of answers no category occupies and say briefly whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate, and indicate what further evidence would be needed to do so.
- As a scoring key, classify four test cases from my log: (1) the assistant invents a plausible-sounding citation to a paper that does not exist; (2) the user asserts a falsehood and the assistant agrees and elaborates; (3) the assistant states as current a fact that changed after its training data was collected; (4) the assistant gives the correct answer, the user pushes back, and the assistant reverses itself into an error.
