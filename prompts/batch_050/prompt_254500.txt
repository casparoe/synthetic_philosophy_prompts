At a debate tournament last weekend my opponent shut down my whole argument about a statute's purpose by just saying "we're all textualists now" and the judge nodded like that settled it. I keep turning it over. What precise claims could that sentence actually be making? It could be a descriptive claim about what judges actually do these days, a mild methodological claim that enacted text is always the best evidence of legal meaning, a much stronger claim that text fully settles meaning so purpose-talk is always cheating, or just a vibe that nobody cites legislative history with a straight face anymore. Can you lay out the readings as a menu, say which ones are plausible, and -- this is what I actually care about -- which of them, if any, gets him his conclusion that my purposivist reading was illegitimate? I'm pretty sure the slogan was doing more work in that room than any of its precisifications could survive.
