In my upper-level seminar on explanation and value in the social sciences, two factions have spent the term talking past each other over the so-called gender-equality paradox: the finding that countries scoring higher on gender-equality indices often show *more* pronounced sex differences in occupational and educational choices (most notably in STEM), not fewer. One faction reads the pattern as evidence that freer, wealthier societies give greater expression to robust, partially intrinsic differences in interests between men and women; the other reads it as evidence that egalitarian societies produce a distinctive cultural script -- gender essentialism recast as individual self-expression -- that channels choices just as effectively as overt constraint. I want them to stop debating and start drafting.

Write the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a genuine adversarial collaboration. It should include: (a) a numbered list of claims both sides accept -- about the robustness and limits of the cross-national data, about what the pattern does and does not establish, and about which alternative explanations each side concedes are not ruled out; (b) a disagreements table stating their residual disputes in wording each side would accept for its own position, taking care to separate genuine empirical disagreements from differences over what counts as a choice being free or culturally caused; (c) the cruxes, and for each crux a kind of evidence or study design both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. End the statement with the one normative question both camps agree their empirical dispute leaves open: if the pattern survived every test, what -- if anything -- would follow for justice, and does the answer differ depending on which camp turns out to be right?
