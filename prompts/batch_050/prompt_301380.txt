In my philosophy of physics reading group, discussions of string theory and the multiverse tend to end the same way: a skeptic declares the whole research program "not even wrong," and a defender replies that empirical testability is not the only route to theory confirmation. Neither side ever spells out what the slogan actually amounts to, and I suspect the debate stalls precisely there. Please disambiguate "not even wrong" into the distinct precise theses it might be expressing — from the weak claim that a theory currently yields no testable predictions, through a Popperian unfalsifiability charge, to stronger claims of permanent empirical inaccessibility or outright conceptual incoherence — and for each reading say whether it is philosophically defensible, whether it actually applies to string theory and multiverse cosmology rather than to a caricature of them, and whether it would underwrite the dismissive conversational use. Then state what the defender's appeal to non-empirical confirmation — arguments from unexpected explanatory coherence, from the absence of alternatives, or from the theory's having been discovered rather than engineered to fit data — would have to establish to answer each reading, and whether, on your analysis, it succeeds against any of them.
