As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I keep encountering the "would there have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler?" question used as a test case for Great Man versus structural explanations of history — and, relatedly, for the idea that history is one draw from a distribution we can't resample. I'm running a two-hour session for a university reading group (roughly fourteen people, mixed philosophy and history students, no prior preparation assumed) and want a simulation game that makes the structuralist claim *felt* rather than asserted: that Weimar-era pressures made some radical authoritarian outcome highly likely whoever rose to lead it, while leaving genuinely open whether the specific catastrophe was contingent.

The design problem I'd like solved: a simulation has a designer, so if the rules simply force the outcome, I've rigged the lesson and participants will notice. Specify rules, roles and incentives such that the "leader" role is interchangeable in a way participants discover for themselves — ideally with a mechanism that also lets contingency show up somewhere, so the game doesn't overshoot into pure determinism. Include a timeline, a debrief mapping moments of play onto the Great Man/structuralism distinction, predictable ways the session goes wrong, and a frank note on which feature of the real question (e.g. the single-draw problem) no replayable game can reproduce. Please write in British English.
