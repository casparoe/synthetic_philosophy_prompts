Here's something me and a friend have been going back and forth on all week. Descartes famously thought animals are just fancy automata with no minds at all, and he laid down two tests that he reckoned no machine could ever pass. First: genuinely using language the way we do, i.e. putting words together to express thoughts rather than producing noises in response to fixed triggers — and he even used parrots as his example, since a parrot can mouth words but gives no sign it's thinking what it says. Second: being able to respond sensibly across the whole spread of situations life throws at you, which he thought no arrangement of gears, springs and pipes could ever manage, because a machine's parts can only be rigged up for particular tricks whereas reason is a universal instrument.

Now imagine you could sit Descartes down in front of a modern chatbot. It holds a flowing conversation on more or less any topic, answers questions nobody has ever asked before, writes a sonnet on demand, explains your joke back to you. But: it was built by training on mountains of human-written text, it sometimes faceplants on easy arithmetic, and if you ask it what it had for breakfast it will cheerfully describe the toast.

So what does Descartes actually say in this situation? Does the chatbot pass the language test, or does he dismiss it as a scaled-up parrot — and if he goes the parrot route, what could he point to that makes the distinction stick, given the parrot comparison was his own example in the first place? Does the second test end up doing the real work (the bot can only talk, it can't do anything — is that an instant fail, or is free-ranging conversation itself enough range to count)? And the bit I really can't figure out: if he concedes the tests are passed, what gives way first? Does he bite the bullet and say fine, this thing has a rational soul — which has big theological implications he cared a lot about — or does he retreat and claim the tests were only ever meant as sufficient signs, not definitions of mind? Bonus question: would any of the modern animal cognition results (corvids planning, apes doing theory-of-mind-ish stuff) change what he says about actual animals while you're at it?

Please write the answer in British English.
