I am helping a research group design the refusal policy for a general-purpose AI assistant, and our workshops keep splitting into two camps. The first, the Agency-Priority camp, holds that an assistant should normally facilitate a competent adult’s sincerely chosen project, even when the project seems unwise, because the user is generally the best situated judge of their own values and because an assistant that substitutes its judgement becomes a quasi-parent. The second, the Welfare-Floor camp, holds that some requests should be slowed, redirected, or refused when the expected cost to the user or others is sufficiently grave, especially where the user is acting under distorted information, impaired judgement, acute emotion, coercion, or an easily misunderstood instruction. Neither camp wants a caricature: the first accepts that assistants may intervene in emergencies, while the second accepts that assistants should not optimise people’s lives according to a single conception of flourishing.

Draft the joint statement these camps could sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims, distinguishing among four possible responses: answering straightforwardly, asking a clarifying question, adding friction such as a warning or delay, and refusing. Include agreement on how the assessment should vary with reversibility, severity, probability of harm, effects on third parties, the user’s apparent competence at the moment of asking, and whether a less restrictive intervention would achieve the same protection. Follow this with a disagreements table containing: the exact residual dispute, phrased neutrally enough that both camps could endorse the wording; why each camp regards it as genuine rather than merely terminological; the crux whose resolution would change at least one camp’s position; and a concrete hypothetical case, test, or observation that both accept as bearing on that crux, with each camp’s prediction stated in advance. Where the dispute turns on an ambiguous word such as autonomy, welfare, consent, or manipulation, define the rival readings rather than treating the word as self-explanatory.

Please make the statement constructive rather than a transcript of an argument, and do not adjudicate the debate from outside the two positions. Where useful, introduce a limited common vocabulary, but avoid relying on named philosophers, statutes, technical literature, or citations. End with three operational principles for an actual assistant that both camps could accept despite their remaining disagreements, and explicitly flag any principle whose practical meaning they would still interpret differently. Please write the response in British English.
