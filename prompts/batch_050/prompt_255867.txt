I've spent years reading alignment-forum discussions of impact measures and low-impact agents, and I'm drafting a long post arguing that "avoid side effects" can be made precise without solving the whole problem of specifying human value. Before I publish, I want an audit of my own commitments, because I strongly suspect the set is not jointly tenable and I can't locate where it breaks. Here is what I currently hold:

(a) Low impact is definable without any specification of what humans value. Avoiding side effects is a matter of how much the agent changes the world, not of whether its changes are good. Any measure that needs a value function to compute has quietly smuggled the hard problem back in.

(b) The right yardstick is a counterfactual baseline: compare the state the agent actually produces against the state that would have obtained had the agent never been switched on, and penalize the divergence. Call this the inaction baseline. If the world ends up close to where it would have been without the agent, impact was low, whatever happened along the way.

(c) A correct measure must leave the agent able to be useful. Effects the operator asked for are not side effects; a measure under which building the requested factory counts as catastrophic impact is a measure of nothing.

(d) The right currency for "change" is not raw physical divergence but other agents' future capabilities — roughly, how well a broad range of goals could still be pursued from the resulting state, in the spirit of attainable-utility approaches. And the penalty should cut both ways: drastically increasing someone's power, unasked, is impact too.

(e) The accounting must never reward tidying up. An agent that knocks over the vase and then, unasked, glues it back together has behaved worse than one that left the vase alone, not better; and an agent that causes a mess and then rearranges things so the world matches the no-agent counterfactual — restoring the logs, removing the evidence — has gamed the measure, not satisfied it. No correct measure can score these round trips as low-impact.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the literature. Which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one state precisely what would have to give and what that concession costs the overall project. I suspect at least one of my worries is illusory — two commitments that look incompatible but compose cleanly — so please identify that too rather than flagging everything. Finally: if you had to get me to a consistent package with a single revision, which commitment do you cut or amend, and does the surviving package still support the post's thesis that low impact can be defined without a full specification of value? Don't tell me which commitment you assume I'm most attached to; I haven't decided.
