I'm drafting a chapter on the norms of assertion and it occured to me I've never stress-tested my own positions against each other. So here's where I've landed:

(a) The knowledge norm, full strength: you're warranted in flat-out asserting p only if you know p, and this one norm explains everything defective about any defective assertion. The truth norm dies on Moore-paradoxical cases ("it's raining but I don't believe it is" can be true and is still unassertable), the belief norm dies on self-deception. So knowledge it is.

(b) Blameless bad luck: if someone asserts on excellent evidence and gets burned by freak luck — tells a visitor the museum closes at 8 because that's what the posted hours say, except they changed today — she did nothing wrong, period. Criticizing her would be nuts.

(c) Lottery: you shouldn't tell your friend "your ticket didn't win" before the draw purely on the basis of the odds, and it doesn't matter how astronomical the odds get. And here's the part I feel strongly about — it's still not ok after the draw if you've just done the math and haven't actually looked up the numbers, even though the winning numbers are sitting right there online.

(d) Anti-skepticism: we flat-out know alot on purely statistical or inductive grounds. My car is still where I parked it. The plane will land. Denying this stuff is just skepticism in a trench coat and I'm not interested.

(e) Stakes matter for assertion: "the bank's open Saturdays" said on hazy memory is a perfectly fine thing to say when your partner's just curious, and an irresponsible thing to say when a mortgage payment is about to bounce — same evidence both times, but you should have checked first.

(f) But knowledge itself doesn't move: "knows" isn't context-sensitive, stakes don't change whether you know, contextualism is a semantic cheat. You know or you don't.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which just look like they do? Rank whatever tensions you find by how serious they are, say what would have to give in each case and at what cost, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I don't have a favorite child here, I just want to know what the bill is.
