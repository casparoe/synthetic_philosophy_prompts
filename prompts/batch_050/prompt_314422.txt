For much of his career, Popper held that Darwinian natural selection was not a testable scientific theory but what he called a metaphysical research programme -- partly on the ground that 'survival of the fittest' verged on tautology -- and late in life he publicly recanted, conceding in a lecture on natural selection that he had been wrong to deny the theory's scientific status. Analyze this episode as a case study in the use and revision of Popper's own demarcation criterion. First, by falsificationism's lights: what exactly made Darwinism unfalsifiable on the early view, and is the tautology charge a defect in the theory itself or a failure to identify the risky claims it in fact makes, for instance about fossil sequences and about the conditions under which selection cannot operate? Second, the recantation: the theory of evolution did not change between the two verdicts, so what must Popper have taken himself to have got wrong, and on what grounds may a falsificationist reverse a demarcation judgment without new empirical evidence? Third, run the analysis the other way. Popper accused Marxists and Freudians of immunizing their theories against criticism, yet his own framework allowed a theory's scientific status to be withdrawn and restored by reinterpretation alone. Does the episode show falsificationism working as intended -- demarcation verdicts themselves held open to criticism -- or does it reveal that such verdicts are hostage to the very maneuver he condemned in others? Finally, say what the case suggests about whether falsificationism can accommodate historical sciences that explain patterns rather than predict events.
