So I've been reading up on the special composition question (van Inwagen's thing: under what conditions do some objects compose a further object?) and I keep a running list of the answers people have given. Here's what I have so far:

1. Nihilism: never. There are only simples, arranged various ways. "Tables" are just simples arranged tablewise.
2. Universalism: always. Any old plurality of objects, no matter how scattered, composes something. There's an object consisting of my left shoe and the moon.
3. Van Inwagen's own answer: xs compose y iff the activity of the xs constitutes a life. So organisms exist, but tables and chairs (strictly speaking) don't.
4. Brutal composition: there's no general formula or principle; it's just a brute fact, case by case, which pluralities compose something.
5. Deflationism/conventionalism: the question is somehow defective -- it's a matter of how we talk or which quantifier we adopt, so there's no deep metaphysical fact of the matter, and rival answers aren't really disagreeing.
6. The "contact" answer: things compose a further object when they're in physical contact / fastened together. (Mostly included because van Inwagen considers and rejects it, but it's the commonsense gut answer.)

Now here's my question: can you give me a *seventh* answer to the special composition question that isn't on this list? It has to be genuinely distinct -- not a rewording of one of the six, not just "answer 4 but with extra steps," and not a hybrid like "universalism except for gunky cases" that's really one of the above wearing a hat. If your answer is a view some actual philosopher has defended, tell me roughly whose it is, but I'm equally happy with something novel as long as it's coherent and actually answers the question rather than dodging it. Also, briefly: what's the strongest objection to your new answer, and how bad is it?
