So Morgan's canon basically says we're never justified in attributing human-like mental states to animals, right? Like, if your dog looks "guilty" after chewing your shoe, the canon says you have to explain that with simpler associative learning instead of actual guilt, and the same goes for anything fancier -- planning, deception, whatever. Given that, I don't get why comparative psychologists keep publishing studies claiming things like metacognition in dolphins or theory of mind in corvids. Aren't they just violating the canon wholesale? Is the field basically in open rebellion against it, or have I misunderstood something?

And a practical version of the same question: if the canon is right and we can't really say my dog misses me when I'm gone, is there even a moral reason not to leave her alone for 10 hours a day? It feels like the canon cuts off the whole animal-welfare discussion at the knees, but nobody talks about it that way.
