I serve on the investment committee of a small college endowment (roughly $180M, with a mandated 5% annual payout to the operating budget and no realistic way to raise new funds if the portfolio is badly impaired). Over the weekend I had a long argument with an old friend, a former quantitative trader, who insists our sizing policy is irrational. Her claim: any agent compounding capital over many periods should maximize expected logarithmic growth, i.e., size positions by the Kelly criterion, and our practice of capping any single concentrated position regardless of estimated edge is simply leaving growth on the table — she describes the dispute as one of geometric versus arithmetic rationality. Our CFO counters that Kelly sizing is ruinously volatile for an institution that must cut a check every year and has no outside benefactor waiting to rescue it. Draft the memo I will circulate before our quarterly meeting: it should recommend a concrete position-sizing policy, explain where the Kelly logic genuinely applies to an endowment with mandatory outflows and where it quietly assumes a closed multiplicative process, and state my friend's argument in its strongest form before answering it.
