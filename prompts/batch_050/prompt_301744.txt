I've come to accept the view that Jeffrey conditionalization is the uniquely rational rule for revising credences when experience changes one's degrees of belief across a partition without conferring certainty on any of its cells. I want a map of what would count against this view. Which objections should a defender treat as genuinely threatening -- for instance, cases where experience seems not to be representable as a shift over any fixed partition, alleged violations of the rigidity condition on conditional probabilities, non-commutativity of successive updates, or the existence of rival rules that agree with Jeffrey's rule in simple cases but diverge elsewhere? Which of these do the view's own defenders concede as costs they absorb, and which do they dismiss as changing the subject? Please distinguish defeaters that would be fatal (showing the rule is wrong or incoherent), from those showing only that it is one rule among several, from those showing merely that it is hard to apply, and rank them accordingly. Note also whether the view has been so qualified -- about partitions, rigidity, and the order of updates -- that it no longer risks anything, and whether that is a problem for a normative principle of this kind. Maximum 700 words; lead with your verdict on how exposed the view actually is.
