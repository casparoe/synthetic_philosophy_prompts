I'm a novelist, and the book I'm working on has gotten me obsessed with coordination failure -- the thing Scott Alexander calls Moloch, the general shape of situations where everyone individually does the rational thing and the group ends up somewhere nobody wanted to be. My plot involves a fishing village where overfishing, gossip escalation, and an arms race in festival spending are all slowly ruining everything, and I want the mechanics to feel real rather than like a morality play. But here's my problem: I've read bits and pieces -- tragedy of the commons, prisoners' dilemmas, race-to-the-bottom arguments in economics, the multipolar trap framing -- and I can't tell what would even count as a good general theory of this stuff. People seem to just gesture at examples.

So before I go read any more, write me the desiderata. What would an adequate general theory of multipolar traps have to deliver? Which cases does it have to get right -- and I'd like the list to include at least: why some commons get governed fine for centuries ( lobster fisheries, irrigation communities) while others collapse almost immediately, why the trap sometimes produces a race to the top instead of the bottom (I'm thinking of how scientific credit-seeking mostly drives good work but occasionally drives fraud -- is that the same structure with a different sign, or a different structure entirely?), and why the classic escape hatches (norms, religion, charismatic leaders, an external king) seem to work in some settings and get eaten by the trap in others. For each requirement, tell me the case or consideration that motivates it, and which distinctions the theory isn't allowed to collapse -- for instance, I suspect the difference between a trap that needs communication and one that persists even with perfect communication matters a lot, but I'm not sure how.

Also, I have a pet example from my village plot that I'd love tested against the list: the fishers all know the fishery is dying, they all talk about it openly at the tavern, they even agree on what a fair quota system would look like -- and yet nothing happens, because whoever proposes enforcement first gets quietly frozen out of the gossip networks that the village actually runs on. It's like the coordination mechanism itself has been captured by the trap. If a candidate theory of multipolar traps can't say anything about that -- about traps that parasitize their own solutions -- is that a fatal gap or a separate phenomenon? Finally, flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied, and then briefly, and I mean briefly, score the main contenders I already half-know (hardin-style commons analysis, game theory with repeated games, ostrom-style institutional stuff) against your list. The criteria are what I care about, not the grades.
