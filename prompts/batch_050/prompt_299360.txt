So I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training, and I fell down a wikipedia hole last week and ended up reading chapter 5 of John Stuart Mill's Autobiography (the "crisis in my mental history" chapter). This bit genuinely broke my brain a little:

"I was accustomed to felicitate myself on the certainty of a happy life which I enjoyed, through placing my happiness in something durable and distant, in which some progress might be always making, while it could never be exhausted by complete attainment."

So the guy basically architected his whole life as a system where happiness is never called directly, only returned as a side effect of chasing some distant goal... and then a few paragraphs later the whole thing segfaults anyway because one day he runs the self-test "would achieving all my goals even make me happy?" and the answer comes back no. This is the funniest and bleakest engineering failure report I've ever read and it's from 1873.

What I want: write a deadpan parody of an internal engineering design doc / RFC from a fictional startup ("Flourish" or whatever, your call) that has turned Mill's strategy from this passage into a literal product — a life-optimization system where happiness is only ever an indirect byproduct, never a target. The doc should be written by engineers who take the passage completely seriously as a spec and treat Mill's breakdown in the same chapter as a production incident to be designed against. The humor has to come from real features of the paradox of hedonism — e.g. that monitoring your own happiness tends to destroy it, that you can't fool yourself into caring about the "durable and distant" goal just because the system needs you to, that the self-check question itself is a kind of attack vector. Don't invent dumb problems that aren't actually in the idea; a philosopher reading it should go "ok they get it." Stay totally straight-faced, no winking.

Structure it with these sections, in this order:

- Incident Report: PROD-1826 (~150 words) — a postmortem-style writeup of Mill's crash, root cause analysis included
- System Overview (~150 words) — the architecture, happiness as an emergent side effect, the "durable and distant" goal as the core dependency
- Metrics & Observability (~150 words) — how you build dashboards for a metric that breaks when the user looks at it; this should be the funniest section
- Known Limitations & Open Questions (~130 words) — where the whole approach quietly admits it might be doomed
- Author's Note (~100 words) — step out of character and explain the satirical choices you made and which real features of the paradox each section is landing

Roughly 700 words total, not counting the Mill passage. Casual tone in the Author's Note is fine. Definately don't make it a listicle.
