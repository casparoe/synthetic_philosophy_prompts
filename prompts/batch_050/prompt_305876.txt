Write a short story (600–900 words) that dramatizes the unilateralist's curse: a decision affecting everyone can be taken by any one member of a group acting alone, each member has their own noisy private estimate of whether it's a good idea, and even if the truth is "don't", the sheer number of people who could pull the trigger makes it near-certain somebody eventually does. The usual real-world flavors are geoengineering, publishing dual-use research, or being the first nation to answer the alien signal — please set yours somewhere less obvious than any of those. The hard part I want you to pull off: nobody in the story is a villain or an idiot. The person who finally acts has a defensible, even sympathetic case that their estimate is the right one, and by the end I should be genuinely unsure whether they did wrong, or whether the rest of the group did by each assuming someone else would stay their hand. And don't have a character explain the concept in dialogue or narration — let the structure of events carry it.
