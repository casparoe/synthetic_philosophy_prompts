Write the customer FAQ page (roughly 500–600 words) for a fictional biotech firm, Eudaimone Genomics, that markets germline editing to "retire suffering" in its customers' descendants: nociception is preserved but negative valence is deleted, so injuries are registered as "vividly informative bliss" and urgency is conveyed by steeper gradients of pleasure rather than by pain. Adopt a deadpan corporate voice — measured, reassuring, bullet-pointed — and let the satire do all its work through the copy itself: the FAQ should encounter, and smoothly neutralize, the standard objections to the hedonistic imperative without ever naming them as objections. Cover at least the following: why a being that experiences only degrees of well-being would still be motivated to act (the answer must take seriously the claim that pleasure can play pain's functional role); how the replacement states carry the same information that pain once did; what happens to art, compassion, and moral seriousness in a clientele that has never suffered; and the company's Wilderness Division, which offers predator reprogramming so that wild ecosystems, too, can be freed from distress. The humor must come from getting the position right — gradients of bliss, the dispensability of raw suffering, the ambition to re-engineer nature — so that a reader familiar with abolitionism recognizes a fair portrait rather than a strawman, and the reader's unease should arise from the FAQ's own impeccable logic rather than from any winking on the author's part.
