Yesterday evening I had a long argument with a friend about whether deepfakes constitute a genuinely novel epistemic threat. Our discussion settled on a list of the standard harms they are said to cause:

1. Direct deception: fabricated recordings induce false beliefs about events that never occurred.
2. The liar's dividend: genuine perpetrators dismiss authentic recordings of their misdeeds as fabrications, and the mere possibility of fakes lends such dismissals undeserved credibility.
3. Backstop erosion: recordings function as a check on testimony -- people speak more honestly when their claims could in principle be exposed by a recording -- and cheap fakery weakens that check even when no particular fake is ever produced.
4. Targeted harm to individuals: non-consensual fabricated imagery damages reputations and livelihoods even when its authenticity is widely doubted.

I am now trying to extend the list. Please give me one further epistemic harm of deepfakes that does not appear above and is not merely a rewording, special case, or combination of these four -- ideally one that would persist even in a population that had rationally ceased to trust audiovisual evidence altogether. Explain briefly how its mechanism differs from the listed items, and say whether you think the harm is genuinely attributable to deepfakes in particular rather than to the general decline of shared media institutions.
