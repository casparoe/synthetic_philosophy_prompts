"Prioritarianism is the view that benefits matter more, morally speaking, the worse off the person who receives them: transferring a fixed amount of wellbeing from someone well off to someone badly off always counts as an improvement, because what ultimately has moral weight is equality between persons. This sets the view apart from utilitarianism, which counts a unit of wellbeing the same wherever it lands, and from egalitarianism, which values the gap between people itself and is therefore committed to the leveling-down conclusion -- that it would be better to drag the better off down to the level of the worst off than to leave inequality standing. Prioritarianism avoids that objection altogether, since lowering the better off without benefiting anyone can only shrink the weighted total it tells us to maximize. The catch is that the priority weights are arbitrary: nothing in the view itself tells you how much extra weight a benefit should get at lower levels of wellbeing, so in practice the theorist picks whatever weighting function delivers the conclusion she wanted in the first place, which is why critics describe prioritarianism as utilitarianism with an unprincipled dial."
