ok so i'm TAing a philosophy of music seminar and we just spent two weeks on how instrumental music can be sad at all -- no lyrics, no one suffering, just sounds. i want to open the next session by reading them a poem, and i figured i'd ask here instead of writing something terrible myself.

write me a villanelle in the voice of a minor third (the interval, like C to E-flat) defending itself against the charge of being sad. the two refrains should be its two lines of defense: one where it insists it's only a shape or contour -- the way a dog's drooping posture or a person's slumped walk "looks" sad without the dog or person needing to actually feel anything (the resemblance/contour idea), and one where it points out that it's the listener who brings and feels the sadness, not the sound (the arousal side of things). the whole point of using a villanelle is that these two defenses keep coming back and circling each other the way the debate does in class, and in the closing quatrain they should land side by side in a way that shows the truce the seminar kept circling toward: the music genuinely is sad as a property of how it moves, but nothing has to suffer for that to be true. someone who's read the contour theory vs arousal theory literature should be able to tell which refrain is which just from the content.

keep it musical in the lines too -- it's about music, after all -- and please write it in American English.
