I'm making a handout for a grad seminar on social ontology and I want a decision tree that sorts positions on whether 'generations' -- Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z, the whole apparatus -- are real kinds of people or just bookkeeping. Start the tree from something everyone grants (your age and the period you live through obviously affect you) and branch down to about 6 or 7 leaves, which should include at least: pure-label conventionalism, cohort realism without genuine boundaries (cohort effects are real but the cutoffs are arbitrary), event-anchored realism (shared formative events at shared ages carve out genuine kinds), and the looping-effect view where generations start as constructs but become real through self-identification. Each node should be a concrete question someone can answer without knowing where it leads. Please work in these diagnostic cases somewhere: the Dec 1964 vs Jan 1965 birthday pair, an immigrant who arrives mid-cohort and misses all the supposed formative events, and the age-period-cohort identification problem (cohort = period minus age, so the three effects can't be statistically teased apart without extra assumptions) -- I want at least one branch that turns on how seriously you take that problem. Where the logical space has unoccupied answer combinations, note them and say briefly whether the gap is incoherence, unattractiveness, or just neglect, and flag any leaves your tree can't actually separate rather than fudging the questions.
