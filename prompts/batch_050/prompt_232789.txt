I'm a retired atmospheric chemist and late in life I've gotten into philosophy, mostly reading on my porch in the evenings. Lately I've been thinking about what we owe future generations, and I keep running into the same line, usually printed on a tote bag or dropped into a speech: "we don't inherit the earth from our ancestors, we borrow it from our children." People quote it as if it settles questions about climate policy, but the more I turn it over the less I know what it's actually claiming. Could you lay out the different precise theses that slogan might be expressing? For instance: is it making a legal-ish claim that present people hold the planet as trustees and must hand it over intact, or a weaker claim that we just shouldn't make things worse, or a claim about ownership (do future people already own it, and if so how does property work across generations?), or maybe just a bit of rhetoric with no real thesis at all? For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a substantive but defensible claim, or something that falls apart under pressure -- e.g. the trustee reading seems to assume future generations have claims now, which is contentious if those people don't exist yet and depend on our choices for who gets born at all. Then say which reading, if any, would actually support the way it's typically used, namely to argue that we must sharply cut emissions even at real cost to ourselves. Please answer in American English.
