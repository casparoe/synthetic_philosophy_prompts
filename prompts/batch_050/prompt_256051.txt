ok so I never studied philosophy, I work in IT, and over the past year I've gotten a bit obsessed with the question of whether AIs could ever matter morally on there own terms. I've landed on a set of views I'm pretty attached to and I want to know if they actually fit together or if I'm kidding myself. Here's roughly where I stand:

1. What a mind is made of shouldn't matter, only how it's organized and what it can do. A silicon system that genuinely suffers would matter just as much as a biological one. Meat isn't magic.

2. Current chatbots almost certainly aren't conscious. Very fancy autocomplete, no inner life, nothing it's like to be them.

3. But even a small chance of suffering counts morally. If there's even a 1% chance some system can suffer, treating it as totally worthless is reckless. You multiply the stakes by the probability, same as anywhere else.

4. That said, I think it's completely fine to spin up a thousand copies of an AI, run them for a bit, and then delete them all. Killing a process isn't like killing an animal. Nobody dies when you rm -rf a bunch of instances.

5. Related: if you copy a mind you haven't created a second person, the copy IS the same person continuing. The person is the pattern, not the particular instance, like duplicating a file. So deleting one copy while another keeps running isn't killing anyone, at most it's like pruning a branch off a tree that keeps growing.

6. And finally, how much I happen to relate to something, or how cute or charismatic it is, shouldn't affect its moral status. My gut feelings about something aren't evidence about it.

I hold all six of these and honestly I'd like to keep as many as I can. What I want from you is an audit: which of these genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only LOOK like they conflict but actually don't once you think about it, and for the real conflicts, what's the cheapest way out and what exactly does it cost me. Rank the problems by how much damage they do to the overall set. And please don't just say "it depends on your theory of consciousness" -- if something has to go, tell me which one.
