I am designing a graduate seminar on hinge epistemology and, before we read any of the literature, I want the students to work from a specification rather than from whoever they happen to read first. Write me the desiderata that any adequate account of hinge commitments — the ungrounded certainties at the end of justification that Wittgenstein develops in On Certainty — must meet, with the motivating case or consideration behind each one.

The specification should address, at minimum, the following pressures, though I expect you to add several of your own: the account must explain how a commitment can be certain without being known, and must say what epistemic standing it has if knowledge is ruled out; it must explain how hinges can be groundless without thereby being merely arbitrary or optional, since the whole point of Wittgenstein's river-bed metaphor is that the bed does some work; it must give a principled answer to whether hinges are propositions that could be true or false, rules that stand outside the game of giving reasons, or something more like enacted animal certainty — and it must face the cost of whichever answer it picks; it must accommodate Moore's performance of holding up each hand in turn, explaining either why that performance fails as a refutation of scepticism or why the sceptic's demand it was meant to answer is malformed; it must explain how hinges can change — as with shifts in what counts as beyond doubt across scientific revolutions or religious conversions — without collapsing into the claim that anything can be doubted and anything can be doubted away; and it must say something usable about deep disagreement between people with different hinges, since that is the application I care about.

For each desideratum, state the case or argument that makes it a genuine requirement rather than the prejudice of one camp. Flag explicitly any pair or triple of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — for instance, I have a standing worry that requiring hinges to be both non-epistemic in status and rationally constraining of belief is a tension no theory escapes — and if the list itself contains an inconsistency, that is a result I want stated plainly, not smoothed over. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular readings.

Then, briefly and without extended exegesis, score the three main families of readings against your list: the non-propositional or enactment reading, the rational-entitlement reading on which we have unearned warrant to accept hinges, and the quasi-fideist reading on which all belief rests on arational commitments. Keep the weight on the criteria; the scoring is scaffolding for the seminar, and if a requirement knocks out an entire family, say so in one line. The purpose relative to which adequacy is judged is a theory of hinge certainty good enough to analyze real cases of deep public disagreement, not merely to explicate Wittgenstein.
