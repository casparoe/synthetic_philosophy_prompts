A dispute in our ethics reading group has stalled on the following question: should a one-in-a-trillion chance of bringing about an astronomically good outcome — say, securing the existence of a vast future civilization — outweigh a certain, moderately good outcome, purely on expected-value grounds? The member pressing the long-shot case insists that this "fanaticism" is not an optional extension of expected utility theory but a theorem of it: given the standard von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, he argues, sufficiently large stakes must eventually dominate any fixed alternative, so anyone who resists the conclusion has to abandon at least one axiom. The rest of us would like to keep the axioms while denying that arbitrarily tiny probabilities of arbitrarily great value must always carry the day. So which axiom are we actually forced to give up, or is the claimed theorem mistaken? If it is mistaken, show where the argument for it goes wrong, and explain what further commitment — beyond the axioms themselves — would genuinely be needed to get the fanatical conclusion.
