You are a professional philosopher of mind and biology acting as a neutral facilitator. Before drafting, brainstorm briefly about which formulations each side would actually accept, then produce the document described below.

I have no academic training, but I have been thinking about a dispute and want to see it done properly. The dispute is between two positions. The first holds that reasons and purposes exist only where there is a mind entertaining them: a reason is always someone's reason, so talk of why the heart pumps blood or why the cuckoo lays eggs in other nests is, strictly speaking, metaphor. The second holds that nature contains what you might call free-floating rationales: the heart is for pumping blood and the camouflage pattern exists for concealment, and these are genuine reasons that no creature ever thought of, because natural selection can track and exploit reasons without representing them. Write the joint statement that committed advocates of these two positions could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration.

The statement should include: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, including what both sides accept about the difference between explaining a trait by natural selection and explaining an action by an agent's beliefs and desires, and about which everyday teleological talk is harmless shorthand versus misleading; (2) the exact residual disagreements, each phrased in neutral wording that its own side would endorse, noting explicitly which disagreements both parties came to regard as merely verbal and why; (3) the cruxes, and for each crux a case both agree bears on it. Candidate cases you might work with: a self-replicating molecule at the origin of life, a bacterium's chemotaxis, a computer program that plays chess well without any grasp of chess concepts, and a congenital disposition acquired by no one's intention. For each crux, record in advance what each side predicts the correct analysis of the case will show.
