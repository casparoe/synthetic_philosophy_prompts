I'd like an audit of the following set of views on immigration and political association, which together amount to my current position. My question is whether the set is jointly tenable, not whether any individual member is true.

(1) Freedom of association is a weighty moral right and crucially includes a negative component: a person may refuse association without owing anyone a justification. Homeowners needn't explain whom they won't invite in; private clubs needn't explain whom they won't admit; nobody must give reasons for declining a marriage proposal.

(2) State coercion is legitimate only if it can be justified to each person subject to it. I do not accept accounts of political authority on which the state's power over an individual rests on nothing more than its capacity to compel.

(3) The citizens of an existing state collectively constitute an association, and this association's right to control its own membership is what grounds the state's right to exclude would-be immigrants. On my view the analogy to a condominium or cooperative association is roughly apt: just as the residents may control access to the common areas they maintain together, citizens may set admissions policy for the territory and institutions they sustain. This is the freedom-of-association defense of a presumptive right to exclude, and I find it the strongest basis for legitimate border control.

(4) Place of birth is morally arbitrary, and the extent to which it determines a person's life prospects in the current world order is a deep injustice. A just global order would substantially reduce the degree to which where one happens to be born fixes what one can expect from life.

(5) Voluntary transactions and relationships between consenting adults are ordinarily entitled to protection from state interference. In particular, a citizen who wishes to employ, rent to, marry, or go into business with a foreign national is presumptively entitled to do so.

Here is what I want from you. Go through the set and identify which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which merely appear to. For each real tension, state precisely which claims are in conflict, why the usual ways of reconciling them fail, how severe the problem is, and what the cheapest available fix would be and what it costs. For each merely apparent tension, show why it dissolves on inspection rather than just asserting that it does. Please pay particular attention to whether (1) and (5), which I originally adopted as general liberal commitments, sit comfortably with the use I make of associational rights in (3), and to whether (2) constrains the kind of justification I can offer for exclusion in a way I may not have fully absorbed. Conclude by telling me which single revision to the set would restore the most coherence at the least cost to the position's core, and present the whole analysis as an ordered audit from most to least serious, rather than a survey of the literature.
