I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've volunteered to write the requirements document for a reading group that's about to spend a semester on theories of moral status. Everyone in the group accepts that moral status comes in degrees -- that if insects matter at all they matter less than dogs, and that a thermostat matters not at all -- but we keep discovering that the published theories smuggle in wildly different answers to questions we haven't agreed on yet. Before we read another paper, I want the specification first.

Write the desiderata that any theory of graded moral status must meet to count as adequate for a decision procedure used under deep empirical uncertainty (i.e., good enough to assign precautionary weight to entities whose sentience is unresolved). For each desideratum, state the requirement, the case or consideration that motivates it, and whether it's a genuine constraint or a prejudice of one camp. The list should cover at least:

- The distinctions the theory cannot collapse: phenomenal experience versus cognitive sophistication, having status versus how much status, being sentient versus being morally considerable for other reasons, and our confidence that something is sentient versus the weight it gets given that confidence.
- The cases it must deliver a verdict on, with at least one verdict it must not give: insects, bivalves, fish, human patients in irreversible coma, brain organoids, and a simple control system like a thermostat or a thermostat plus a learned policy. The thermostat case especially -- what principled feature keeps goal-directed feedback systems at zero without sweeping in insects by the same stroke?
- Aggregation: how the theory compares one large mind against vast numbers of tiny ones, and whether it's allowed to say that no number of insects outweighs one mammal (or that some number does).
- Constraints from the biology it cannot contradict, given that there is no clean behavioral marker separating nociception from felt pain.
- Whether moral weight should scale with expected sentience under uncertainty, and what the theory must say when the uncertainty is itself unquantifiable.

Then: flag any subset of your desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, and say which single requirement eliminates the most mainstream candidates (hedonistic accounts, preference-based accounts, capacities-based accounts, and so on). A brief scorecard of those candidates against the list is welcome but secondary; the criteria and their motivating cases are the deliverable. A table is fine.
