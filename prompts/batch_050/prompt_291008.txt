Last night a friend and I argued for hours about whether the United States is exceptional, and we kept getting stuck on the same maneuver. I want to know whether it has an established name. Whenever she offered evidence against a particular criterion of exceptionalism—low social mobility, high incarceration, weak welfare provision, racial violence, partisan polarization—my reply was not to defend that criterion but to reconstitute the claim: the relevant point, I said, was never mobility alone but the ensemble of mobility with founding ideals, civic religion, immigration, federalism, geographic insulation, military power, and so on. When she challenged one member of the ensemble, I demoted it to optional status and promoted another; when she showed that some other country had several members, I added a further trait or shifted weight to their combination. I was not simply retreating from a strong thesis to a trivial one, because each version still asserted something contentious. Nor was I merely being vague, since at each moment I could state a fairly definite sufficient set of traits. Yet across the conversation the thesis behaved like a cluster with replaceable parts: no trait was necessary, the whole was said to be unique, and every counterexample could be absorbed by reweighting rather than by falsification.

Is there a recognized concept for this exact dialectical pattern, especially as it appears in historical or political exceptionalism claims? If it falls between named fallacies, identify the nearest notions—cluster or family-resemblance concepts, shifting goalposts, equivocation among descriptive, normative, and messianic senses of exceptionalism, motte-and-bailey, and Popperian immunization—and explain precisely why it does or does not fit each. If nothing fits, propose a name and a sharp definition, including what would count as evidence against such a cluster thesis, how to distinguish legitimate multidimensional comparison from criterion-swapping, and two boundary cases: one where reweighting is methodologically defensible and one where it becomes merely self-sealing. I care less about the label than about carving the phenomenon correctly.
