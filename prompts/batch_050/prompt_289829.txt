So I got into this huge argument with my buddy last night about blackmail law and he walked me through this thing that I can't find the hole in, even though the conclusion seems nuts. Here it is, roughly as he gave it:

(1) If each of two things is perfectly legal to do on its own, then offering someone a choice between them can't be a crime. Like, you can't take two lawful options and have the crime appear in the "or" between them.

(2) Telling someone's wife the truth about his affair is legal. It's true, you have no duty of confidentiality, it's just speech.

(3) Asking someone for money, and taking it if they agree, is legal.

(4) So "give me $5,000 or I tell your wife" should be legal by (1). But obviously it's blackmail and a crime. Whatever -- that's the classic version, and I know philosophers chew on it. Here's where he got me though:

(5) "Give me $5,000 or I'll file my (totally legit) lawsuit, where all the same facts about the affair come out in public court records" is NOT a crime. That's just an ordinary settlement demand, lawyers do it every day.

(6) The only real difference between the two threats is the audience for the disclosure -- one person in private versus the public record forever. And the wider, more damaging disclosure is the legal one.

(7) So blackmail law criminalizes the *less* harmful version of a threat while the more harmful version is standard legal practice, which means the law here is just incoherent and the crime should be abolished.

His conclusion is that blackmail shouldn't be a crime at all. I really don't want to accept (7), and I'd most like to keep (5) and (6) since those seem like just... facts about how litigation works. So which step do I actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? Also, when you answer, please be clear about what's actually settled (like how the law treats settlement demands) versus what's genuinely still contested among people who work on this stuff -- I don't want you passing off one theory of blackmail as if it's the accepted view, because my impression is there isn't one.
