my uncle has this bit he wheels out every christmas: the future is like a film that's already been shot and is sitting in the can, we're just watching it frame by frame, and whatever is on the later frames is on them whether we've seen them or not. he uses it for everything. last week it was aristotle's sea battle (he'd picked it up from some pop philosophy book): there either will or won't be a battle tomorrow, the reel already contains whichever frame it contains, so the admirals agonizing over whether to fight are like viewers yelling advice at a cinema screen. his conclusion: a sentence like "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is already true or already false tonight, deliberation doesn't change anything, and aristotle's move of refusing to call such sentences true-or-false in advance is just wishful thinking from someone who couldn't accept the reel.

can you audit the analogy for me? start with the correspondence table: what plays the role of the reel, the projector, the frames already shot, the frames not yet watched, the audience, the projectionist? then take it apart. some specific things i keep getting stuck on:

- the reel is a physical object that exists right now, complete, in the can. is there anything at all that plays this role for future truths? if the only thing that could make "there will be a battle tomorrow" true is tomorrow's battle itself, then hasn't the analogy simply assumed its own conclusion by picturing tomorrow as an already-existing object sitting somewhere?
- suppose we repair the analogy instead of rejecting it: the reel is being filmed live, by us, as it's watched. does his fatalism still follow, or has the conclusion quietly flipped into its opposite? what does that tell you about which feature of the original picture was carrying the argument?
- does his argument prove too much? by the same reasoning, the relevant frame was already in the can two thousand years before the battle, so it was already true back then. if that conclusion seems fishy, where exactly does the fishiness come from, and does the same defect infect the original reel picture or only the long-range version?
- where does aristotle's actual position land once all the disanalogies are on the table? does he need to say the reel hasn't been shot yet, is that even coherent given that past-tense truths seem to have their "reel" (what's done is done), and is there a way to hold that the future lacks truthmakers without collapsing into saying the future is somehow unreal?

if the film reel is beyond saving as a picture of the future, what's the least misleading analogy you'd replace it with for explaining to my uncle why "already true or false" is doing more work than he thinks?
