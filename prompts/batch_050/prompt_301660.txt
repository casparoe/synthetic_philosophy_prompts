Ok so context: my friend Danny and I ended up in a three hour arguement last night that started about something else entirely and somehow landed on Pascal's wager. He's not even religious, he just thinks the wager is this undefeated little machine and enjoys winding me up. I threw every objection I know at it and he had a comeback for all of them (some good, some just fast talking). Here's what I already deployed, so please don't hand me one of these back:

1. Many gods. There are endless conceivable gods with incompatible demands, each dangling an infinite payoff, so the wager gives you no reason to pick Danny's god (or anyone's) over the god who punishes believers.

2. It proves too much. If a nonzero shot at infinite value settles a decision, you can justify literally any action by cooking up a hypothesis that infinitely rewards it -- a being who pays out infinitely for wearing green hats, whatever. So the decision rule behind the wager is broken, not just the theology.

3. You can't believe on command. Belief isn't voluntary, and Pascal's workaround (go to mass, hang around believers, let habit do the rest) is basically deliberate self-brainwashing, which either fails to produce real belief or produces it via a process that has nothing to do with what's true.

4. Wrong payout table. The wager assumes a god who rewards calculated, self-interested belief. A god worth the name would presumably see straight through that, and plausibly rewards sincere searching and honest doubt over mercenary faith -- in which case the honest atheist outearns the wagerer.

5. Infinity breaks the arithmetic. Once infinite payoffs are in the mix, expected value comparisons stop behaving -- expectations can be undefined, or every option comes out infinite, or the ranking flips depending on how you set the problem up -- so the conclusion doesn't even follow from its own premises.

Danny's position after all this was basically that these are the same two moves over and over (poke the theology, poke the math) and the wager survives because it only needs one live hypothesis with infinite upside. I think that's too slick, but I couldn't come up with anything genuinely new on the spot.

So: is there another objection to the wager that isn't on this list? Constraints: not a rewording of any of the five, not a special case of one, and not two of them stapled together -- it should attack something none of them touch, like a hidden assumption about what the choice even is or who it's available to. Also tell me how it relates to the five (does it undermine one of them, complement one, cut across the theology/math split?). And be honest about whether it's actually any good, because if I bring Danny a dud he'll dine out on it for months.
