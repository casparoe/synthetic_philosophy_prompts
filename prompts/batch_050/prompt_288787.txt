Design a role-play activity for a college ethics or sociology class (~18 students, 75 minutes) built around the honor culture / dignity culture / victimhood culture triad, the idea that how people respond to slights and offenses depends on the social infrastructure around them, not just their character. The setup: run the same conflict (say, a public insult or a minor betrayal between classmates) three times under three different rule-sets, changing only what's available to the wronged party -- in round one there's no authority to appeal to at all, in round two there's a reliable impartial arbiter, in round three there's an authority whose sympathy is the main currency of status in the room. I want participants to discover for themselves that the "irrational" response in each setting (retaliating personally, shrugging it off and handling it one-on-one, or amplifying and publicizing the grievance) is actually the locally rational one, before anyone names the three cultures. Include the exact rules for each round, how you assign roles so nobody's just playing themselves, what small stakes make the choices feel real, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the triad -- plus an honest note on what the activity flattens (e.g. real honor cultures aren't adopted for an afternoon, and the stakes of losing face in one are nothing like losing points in a seminar).
