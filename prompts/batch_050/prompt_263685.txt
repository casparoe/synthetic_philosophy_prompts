Since retiring after nearly four decades as an experimental physicist, I have been making my way through the philosophy of space and time, and I keep returning to the dispute between substantivalists and relationists. My own sympathies lie with the substantivalists: Newton's bucket and his pair of globes connected by a cord seem to me to show that rotation is rotation relative to something, and I have never seen a convincing relational candidate for what that something is. A fellow member of my reading group, a retired mathematician, is an equally committed relationist and insists that talk of space as a container is a metaphysical extravagance that physics has quietly outgrown. After a year of arguing in circles over coffee, we have agreed to attempt an adversarial collaboration, and I would like you to draft the joint statement we should produce.

The document should open with a numbered section of claims we can both sign: the phenomena themselves (the concave surface of the spinning water, the tension in the cord joining the globes even in an otherwise empty universe), the point that it is inertial effects, not motion per se, that demand explanation, and an honest statement of what general relativity does and does not settle, including whether Mach's principle is vindicated, refuted, or simply left to one side by the theory. There should follow a table of residual disagreements, each row formulated so that each of us would accept the wording of our own position as neither caricatured nor question-begging. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move one of us. My own suspicion is that the deepest crux is whether the metric field of general relativity is best regarded as spacetime itself or as one physical field among others, and that much of our quarrel about the bucket is inherited from that; but I would also like the hole argument about determinism and diffeomorphism invariance treated as a candidate crux, since my friend deploys it constantly and I am never quite sure what it obliges me to deny. For each crux, give a case, thought experiment, or theoretical development we would both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance, so that neither of us can move the goalposts afterwards.

Please close the document with a candid note identifying any part of our dispute you take to be merely verbal, with reasons, and stating what each side would count as a genuine concession rather than a re-description. The whole thing should run to roughly 900 to 1,200 words, should presuppose scientific literacy but not a specialist's command of the philosophy literature, and must be drafted in neutral, professional prose that we could each put our names to. Kindly write the document itself in American English.
