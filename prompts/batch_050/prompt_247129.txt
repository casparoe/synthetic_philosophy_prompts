So I just got home from a truly exhausting 3-hour argument with my friend Marta about whether brain size is morally relevant to how you can treat animals, and I realized mid-argument that I don't actually know what I think, I just have a pile of gut reactions. So let me lay them out and you tell me if there's a principle hiding in there. Swatting a mosquito that's biting me: completely fine, zero guilt. Squishing a snail on purpose on the sidewalk: technically fine I think, but I'd think less of someone who did it for fun. Catching and eating fish: fine, though the catch-and-release thing where they throw it back seems vaguely worse to me than just eating it, which Marta said made no sense. Eating chicken: fine, I do it all the time. Eating octopus: I do eat it but writing this down it feels inconsistent, more on that below. Eating pork: fine in practice but I lost this round of the argument badly because I can't distinguish it from eating dog, which genuinely horrifies me. Testing cosmetics on rabbits: not ok. Brain research on chimps: definately not ok, even for something important. Keeping a parrot in a cage: seems fine, keeping a crow in a cage: somehow feels worse??

Marta's position was that it's basically brain size or neuron count all the way down -- more brain means more going on inside, more capacity to suffer, more of a life to lose, so the ranking is just a hardware ranking. And I want to agree because it's clean, but my actual verdicts don't line up with it. Octopuses are famously clever and I eat them without much fuss, crows are clever and I'd be upset if someone hurt one. Pigs outscore dogs on most measures anyone mentions and my verdict goes the other way. And the chicken/parrot thing seems to track nothing except that one of them talks. When I got pressed on all this I found myself saying stuff like "well, dogs and humans have a relationship" and "the parrot chose to be here," which is not a principle, that's just noise.

So: is there a principle, brain-size-based or otherwise, that captures most of these verdicts, stated precisely enough that I can tell what it says about cases we didn't fight over -- like is catch-and-release fishing actually worse than eating the fish, and what about the hypothetical Marta kept bringing up of a giant-brained but very stupid animal versus a tiny-brained clever one? And if no single principle gets them all, which of my verdicts is the one you'd throw out first, and why that one rather than the dog thing, which I'm warning you is not negotiable.
