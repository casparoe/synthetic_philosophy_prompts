Ok so my uncle hit me with this at a cookout last weekend and its been driving me nuts ever since. His argument is basically that in a lot of cases nobody actually causes anything, and he used a firing squad to prove it. It goes like this (I've numbered it so you can tell me exactly where it breaks):

1. One thing only counts as causing another if, had the first thing not happened, the second thing wouldn't have happened either. My cousin whos in law school says this is basically the "but for" test they use in court, so its not some weird thing my uncle invented.
2. Say a firing squad has six shooters, they all fire at the same moment, and every single one of their shots would have been fatal on its own. The prisoner dies instantly.
3. Take any one shooter. If he hadn't fired, the other five bullets still kill the prisoner at the exact same moment. So by step 1, that shooter didn't cause the prisoner's death.
4. Step 3 works for every shooter. So none of them caused the death.
5. The squad is just the six guys. A group can't cause something that none of its members caused -- that would be like saying a bucket brigade moved water that no bucket ever touched.
6. So the prisoner wasn't killed by anybody. And since this pattern shows up everywhere (anytime there's a backup, a crowd, a spare part), most of what we call "causing" is an illusion.

Now obviously I refuse to accept 6. Six men shot a guy, the guy is dead, SOMEBODY killed him. And I'd really like to keep the conclusion that all six of them are guilty of murder, not zero -- a court that let each shooter walk because "my bullet wasn't needed" would be insane.

I tried two counters on him and neither worked. First I said, surely the way you tell a cause is you wiggle it -- poke the thing and see if the outcome changes. He said fine, wiggle shooter number 3: hold everything else exactly as it was, and the prisoner dies at the same instant either way. So the wiggle test gives the same insane answer as his test. Then I said, what if we describe the outcome more precisely -- the prisoner didn't just die, he died of SIX bullets at 7:00 and 3 seconds. Now wiggling shooter 3 changes it to five bullets, so shooter 3 caused something. My uncle goes, sure, shooter 3 caused "the sixth bullet hitting him," but not the death -- and if we only get to keep the pedantic version, that's basically conceding his point.

So: which step do I throw out? I want to keep that all six men killed him, and I'd like a test for causation I can actually use, not one that only works in toy cases with no backups. If I give up step 1, what do I replace it with -- and if the answer is "the group did it but no individual did," I don't see how that's not just magic, since the group is literally six guys standing in a line. Explain why the bad step looked so reasonable in the first place, and what it costs me to dump it versus dumping one of the others.
