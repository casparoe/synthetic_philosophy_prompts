So I write a science-and-ideas column for a general-interest magazine, and I'm working on a feature about societal collapse -- Bronze Age, Classic Maya, Easter Island, the usual suspects. I keep tripping over what the word even means, and I'd like help before I embarrass myself in print.

Please take on the role of a historically literate philosopher (or philosophically literate historian, either way) and think carefully. Brainstorm a few candidate principles before committing to one, and then stress-test it.

Here are my gut verdicts on cases:

1. Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, c. 1200 BC. Palaces burned, Linear B vanishes, long-distance trade dies. Clear collapse, the paradigm case. No hesitation.

2. Classic Maya. The big southern cities get abandoned, the inscribed stelae just stop, divine kingship dissapears -- but millions of Maya people carry on living in the region, and northern cities keep going for centuries. I still want to call this a collapse, not a "transition."

3. Western Roman Empire. Plenty of historians now say it was a transformation, not a fall -- and sure, a farmer in Gaul mostly kept farming. But taxes, literacy, long-distance pottery, the whole administrative world went away. My gut says: NOT a collapse, just change. Except I can't reconcile that with my Maya verdict, since both involve political fragmentation plus ordinary people carrying on. One of these verdicts is probably wrong and I don't know which.

4. Easter Island. If the classic ecocide story is right, collapse. If the revisionists are right and the population was smallish and stable untill European contact did the damage, then apparently not. My instinct says whether it "counts" depends entirely on which empirical story wins. Is that right, or should the concept be insulated from that?

5. Soviet Union. State dissolved, GDP cratered, life expectancy fell -- but no burned cities, population basically fine, lots of institutional continuity. I say "collapse" in conversation but I hesitate to put it on the same list as the Bronze Age.

6. Lehman Brothers. Obviously a collapse, obviously not the kind my article is about. Whatever principle you give me should exclude it without just cheating by stipulating "societies only."

So: is there a principled line here? Give me your best candidate principle (or two), state precisely which of my six verdicts it saves and which it forces me to abandon, and tell me what it says about cases I didn't mention -- the Egyptian Intermediate Periods, the 2008 financial crisis, the Greenland Norse.

One more thing: if you think the whole exercise is confused -- like "collapse" is a cluster concept, or a publishing category rather than a real kind of thing, or my verdicts are just tracking drama rather than any actual property of history -- say that plainly instead of politely reverse-engineering a principle to flatter my gut. I'd rather kill the framing than publish something mushy.
