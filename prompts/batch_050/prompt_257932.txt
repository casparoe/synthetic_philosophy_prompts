So I spent 35 years as a research chemist and since retiring I've gotten properly hooked on philosophy and history of ideas, the way you do when you finally have time. I run a little discussion group at our local adult education centre -- about 14 people, mostly other retired professionals (a GP, two engineers, a former headmistress, that sort of crowd), and we meet for two hours. Lately we've been circling the Great Man question: did Hitler make Nazi Germany, or did the circumstances make Hitler, and would something roughly like it have happened without him? The group is split, and honestly the debate keeps degenerating into people just asserting counterfactuals at each other, which as a scientist I find deeply unsatisfying. You can't run the experiment twice... except maybe you sort of can?

So here's what I want: design an activity for my group that lets participants feel the tug-of-war between individual agency and structural forces rather than just argue about it. I'm imagining something like a staged simulation of an interwar-Germany-like situation -- invented country is fine, maybe better, so nobody just plays their memorized history -- where there's a charismatic figure role, and then at some point the facilitator removes that person (arrest, heart attack, whatever) and the simulation continues. The idea would be that the group discovers whether the pressures they'd built into the system (economic desperation, humiliation, scapegoating dynamics, ambitious second-rankers) just produce a replacement, or whether things genuinely diverge. Give me concrete setup: roles, the constraints and incentives each player operates under, how the facilitator seeds structural pressures without scripting the outcome, a timeline for two hours, and rules for what the 'removed leader' player does afterwards (sitting silently watching might itself be instructive?).

Then I need a debrief plan: which moments in the simulation map onto which side of the Great Man vs. structure debate, how to stop people from over-reading a game with 14 players as evidence about actual 1930s Germany, and what will predictably go wrong the first time -- I suspect one problem is that whoever plays the charismatic role will be tempted to ham it up, and another is that my engineer friends will try to 'win' rather than behave plausibly. Also, since this group will push back hard: tell me how confident you are that an exercise like this can actually produce insight rather than just confirming whatever the facilitator baked in, and flag the single design element you're least sure will work in practice.
