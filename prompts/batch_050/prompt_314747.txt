Write a parody of an official government form and its accompanying guidance notes: "Form F-1: Application for Continued Friendship Status (Parent of an Adult Child)," issued by an imaginary Department of Filial Affairs in a jurisdiction that has formally adopted Jane English's friendship model of filial obligation as law. Roughly 650 words, structured as a numbered form with sections, checkboxes, and footnoted guidance, in flawless deadpan bureaucratic prose.

The satirical premise is that English's account has been codified into administrative procedure. The humor must come from real features of the view, taken literally: that what grown children owe their parents is owed to them as friends, not as creditors; that past sacrifices freely made -- the tuition, the sleepless nights, the casseroles -- were gifts rather than loans and generate no retroactive debt; that unsolicited benefits cannot be invoiced after the fact; that where a friendship has lapsed, the obligations lapse with it, however much was once expended; and that friendship, being voluntary and reciprocal, cannot be compelled by blood or by memory. Good material includes: a schedule for the liquidation of unreciprocated parental investments (write-off, non-transferable); a checkbox by which applicants acknowledge that listing expenses incurred between birth and majority is inadmissible as evidence; a hardship exemption for parents who kept itemized receipts; an appeals process for those whose friendship status was revoked following a phone call about grandchildren; and guidance on whether godparents, step-parents, and long-serving family dogs may file.

The form should never wink. It should read as though the Department sincerely believes it is administering something humane and sensible, and the reader should feel the genuine attractions of the view -- the cruelty of treating love as a loan -- alongside the absurdity of administering affection through a renewal cycle. Someone who knows the English literature should recognize the satire as fair; someone who doesn't should still find it funny and come away with an accurate, if strange, picture of the position being parodied. Do not name any real individuals; the Department, its form numbers, and its officials are all invented.
