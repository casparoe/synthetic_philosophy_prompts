Alex Garland's Ex Machina (2014) has Caleb spend a week asking whether Ava really means what she says, and ends with her walking free while he is sealed inside the facility. Read the film through the dispute between Searle and Dennett over original and derived intentionality. Searle holds that meaning is intrinsic to minds and that an artefact's apparent understanding is at best derived, existing only relative to an interpreter; Dennett — whose two-bitser is a vending machine built in America to accept quarters which, shipped abroad, equally well takes a different coin of the right size and weight, leaving no fact of the matter about what its detector really detects — holds that intentionality is a pattern that interpretation finds in behaviour, and that beyond the predictive fruitfulness of the intentional stance the question of what a system really means is empty.

So: is the film Dennettian? Consider the power-cut conversations, Nathan's revelation that the true test was whether Ava could use Caleb as a means of escape, and the closing shot at the busy intersection Ava had said she wanted to visit — a desire apparently confirmed by its fulfilment. Consider also Kyoko, whose final act is as agentive as Ava's, yet to whom the film invites us to attribute far less. Then press the other direction: does the film's drama depend on a Searlean contrast between Ava's putative inner life and mere mechanism that Dennett would dismiss as a confusion, and is that a flaw or a deliberate irony at Caleb's expense?
