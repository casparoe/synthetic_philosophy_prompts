I'm working on a paper about standing to blame, specifically the hypocrisy objection -- why does it seem somehow off for a hypocrite to blame someone for the very thing they themselves do? I'm trying to catalogue the main explanations on offer and see if there's room for a genuinely different one. Here's what I've got so far:

1. The commitment/inconsistency view: the hypocrite's will is internally divided -- they condemn the wrong while endorsing it through their own conduct, and you can't fully will both, so the blame is defective the way a promise made with fingers crossed is. (Wallace-flavored.)

2. The equality/differential-blaming view: blame involves treating the target as liable to sanction-like responses for their wrongdoing, and the hypocrite implicitly denies that the same applies to them, violating a basic moral-equality constraint built into blaming. (Fritz & Miller-flavored.)

3. The normative-power view: blaming someone is issuing a demand that they comply or answer for themselves, and standing is the power to make that demand stick; the hypocrite has forfeited the power by refusing the same demand themselves, like a judge who's ignored the law she enforces. (Todd-flavored.)

4. The epistemic/testimony view: blame functions partly as testimony about the weight of moral reasons, and a hypocrite's testimony is untrustworthy on exactly the matter at hand -- their own conduct is evidence they don't actually take those reasons seriously.

Give me one more explanation that isn't on this list and isn't just a recombination or special case of these four (e.g. "it's inconsistency about who counts as an equal" would be a mashup of 1 and 2, not new). It should explain the specific defectiveness of hypocritical blame, not just why hypocrisy is bad in general. Then tell me: does your proposal predict the same verdicts as the four above in the tricky edge cases -- like the hypocrite who has since reformed, or the person who blames while planning to do the same thing tomorrow -- or does it come apart from them somewhere, and is that a point for or against it?
