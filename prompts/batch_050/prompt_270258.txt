My small group at church has been stuck on predestination vs. free will for like a month now (it started with Romans 8 and totally spiraled), and last week someone brought up Molinism as a supposed middle path between the Calvinist and Arminian options. I borrowed a theology book from a friend to figure out what it actually says, and here's the summary it gives: "Molinism, named for the Jesuit Luis de Molina, holds that God possesses three kinds of knowledge: knowledge of everything that could happen, knowledge of everything that will happen, and — the distinctive piece — 'middle knowledge' of what every possible creature would freely do in any circumstance in which God might place them. Before creating, God consults his middle knowledge and then selects the world in which everything unfolds according to his plan. And because God knows what you will do simply by looking down the corridor of time and watching you do it, your choices remain genuinely free — he never causes them. In this way Molinism reconciles divine sovereignty with human freedom, and it also answers the problem of evil, since God cannot be blamed for evils he merely foresees rather than causes."

Now, full disclosure: my reaction reading this was that it sounds like the old "God just foresees everything" view with extra steps, which makes me suspect the whole thing might be a semantic trick. But I also know basically nothing, so before I bring this to the group I want to know how fair the summary actually is. Go through it claim by claim: what would an actual Molinist nod along to, what would they wince at, and what's flat wrong? I'm especially suspicious of the corridor-of-time bit and the "selects the world where everything goes according to his plan" part — do Molinists really think God gets to pick just any world he wants, or is there some constraint the summary is glossing over? And does the alleged reconciliation of sovereignty and freedom still work once you've fixed whatever the summary gets wrong, or does it definately collapse back into either determinism or the plain foreknowledge view?
