I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I keep coming back to a philosopher of work I profiled last year. She's got this running side-career of giving verdicts on whether automating some job destroys something meaningful or not, and her takes are weirdly specific. For a piece I'm drafting I want to predict what she'd say about a new case, but I can't get hold of her before deadline, so I'd like you to do it.

Her track record, as best I've recorded it:

- Lift operators replaced by automatic lifts: good riddance, nothing of value was lost, it's mad to mourn it.
- Chess engines surpassing grandmasters: a genuine loss. Something went out of the game even though humans still play and people still watch.
- Spreadsheet software wiping out manual bookkeeping calculation: totally fine, actually good, it freed accountants up for the judgement-y parts of the job.
- Self-checkout machines replacing supermarket cashiers: a small but real loss, and interestingly she said it was mostly about the little daily chats regulars had with their cashiers, not the scanning itself.
- Machine translation replacing technical manual translators: no loss worth mentioning. Replacing literary translators: definately a loss.

New case: a big broadcaster is looking at replacing its human football pundits with an AI system that does noticeably sharper tactical analysis, faster, and in voices focus groups say they prefer. The AI's breakdowns of what went wrong in a match are apparently just better than what the ex-pros on the sofa come up with. What does she say?

I'm less interested in the verdict itself than in the rule she's running. As far as I can tell her past calls are consistent with at least a couple of different principles (something about skill and mastery, something about whether the work is essentially done *for* people who want it done by a person, maybe something else), and I'd like you to lay out the candidate rules, say what each one predicts about the pundit case, and give me your best guess at her actual verdict with the reasoning. Also: which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog here, and what's the further hypothetical case that would best tell the candidate rules apart? If her track record genuinely underdetermines the new case, say so and tell me what principle she'd be committing to with each answer.

Please answer in British English.
