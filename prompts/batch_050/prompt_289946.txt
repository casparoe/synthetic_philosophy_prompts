I'm preparing a funding application for a one-semester independent research project in moral philosophy and need a two-page proposal. The topic is karma considered as a theory of moral desert, and my half-formed idea is this. The standard philosophical treatment of karma divides into two readings: a supernatural one, on which a cosmic mechanism apportions future suffering and flourishing to past deeds, and a naturalized one, on which karma is just the observation that actions shape character and character shapes how a life goes. The naturalized reading is the only one a secular philosopher can take seriously, but I suspect it fails as a theory of desert: once karma is reduced to the causal influence of action on character, the bad things that befall vicious people are merely caused by their vice, not deserved in virtue of it, and so naturalized karma collapses into a mix of virtue ethics and brute moral luck. My project is to ask whether there is a stable middle position -- a naturalized account on which character-mediated consequences are not only caused by one's deeds but fitting to them, in the way desert claims require -- or whether the desert component of karma is essentially supernatural and the naturalized version is a different theory wearing the same name.

Please turn this into a proposal with the following: a statement of the question precise enough that it has a determinate answer, including what exactly the fittingness or desert relation would have to amount to for the middle position to count as a success; an explanation of why neither the supernatural reading nor the plain naturalized reading, nor off-the-shelf theories of desert, settles the question as posed; the method, meaning what I would actually do across the semester, roughly month by month, and why conceptual analysis of cases rather than, say, exegesis of classical sources is the right approach here; a description of what the possible conclusions are and which considerations would decide between them, since 'there is no middle position' would be a result and not a failure; and an honest account of what would count as the project failing, including the risk that the whole thing turns out to hinge on a merely verbal disagreement about the word desert, and how I would check for that. I will supply all citations myself, so do not include a bibliography or name specific works. Begin the response with the proposal itself, with no preface or commentary.
