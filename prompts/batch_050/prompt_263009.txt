I have been drafting the theory chapter of a textbook on non-cooperative game theory, and before finalizing it I would like to know whether the positions I have staked out are jointly tenable. Here is what I currently hold:

1. A fully rational player never chooses a strictly dominated strategy, whether the dominating strategy is pure or mixed; domination by a mixture is exactly as decisive as domination by a pure strategy, since either establishes that the strategy is not a best reply to anything the player might believe.

2. If the structure of the game and the rationality of all players are common knowledge, iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies is complete: what survives is precisely the set of rationalizable outcomes, and identifying rational play with that set requires no assumption beyond rationality and common knowledge of it.

3. A player can perfectly reasonably regard the choices of several opponents as correlated rather than independent — opponents trained in the same tradition, advised by the same consultant, or selected by the same process — and rationality itself imposes no requirement that a conjecture over others' play take the form of a product distribution.

4. Nash equilibrium should not be presented as a deliverance of rationality alone. It is better understood as a self-confirming configuration of mutual conjectures, which demands a coordination of beliefs going beyond anything rationality and common knowledge of rationality supply.

5. Mixed strategies need not involve literal randomization. A player's mixture can be read as the uncertainty others have about that player's choice, or as the aggregate result of small private variations in payoffs, so the theory never requires anyone to flip coins.

6. Weak dominance carries the same eliminative authority as strict dominance. Iterated elimination of weakly dominated strategies is a legitimate solution procedure, and I intend to apply it freely throughout the book's examples.

Please audit this set of commitments. Which of them are in genuine tension with one another, and which pairs merely appear to be? For each genuine tension, identify the commitments involved, explain the conflict precisely, and state what would have to give; rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious. For each merely apparent tension, explain why the worry dissolves on inspection. Finally, identify the single revision that would restore consistency at the lowest cost to the overall position, and be explicit about what that revision forfeits. I am not asking for a survey of positions in the literature; I want to know what my own commitments do and do not permit me to say.
