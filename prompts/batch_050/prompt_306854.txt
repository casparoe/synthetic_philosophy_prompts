Last month, at a public hearing in my city about whether to fund a supervised consumption site, a councilor ended the discussion by declaring, "You can't arrest your way out of the drug problem," and the room treated that as decisive. I keep turning the sentence over, because it seems to gesture at several different claims that vary enormously in strength, and I want them disentangled before the next hearing. Please lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing: for instance, a weak empirical claim that enforcement alone can never drive drug use to zero; a stronger causal claim that demand for drugs is driven by conditions (addiction, poverty, despair) that policing is structurally unable to touch; a public-finance claim that a dollar spent on enforcement buys less reduction in harm than a dollar spent on treatment or harm reduction; a second-order claim that prohibitionist enforcement actively worsens the problem by sustaining illicit markets and their violence; and a moral claim that punishing users is unjust regardless of whether it works. For each reading, tell me (a) whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or actually false or empirically shaky; (b) what kind of evidence would bear on it; and (c) which policies it would actually support -- since some readings justify only reallocating budgets within a prohibitionist framework, others justify Portugal-style decriminalization of personal possession while keeping supply illegal, and only the strongest seem to justify full legalization with regulated sales, as with cannabis in several jurisdictions. Then answer the question I actually care about: which reading does a politician most plausibly intend when deploying the slogan to justify a supervised consumption site, and does that reading in fact get them there, or does it only support the weaker conclusion that enforcement budgets should shrink? Also flag any slippage the slogan exploits -- for instance, between drug use, drug addiction, and drug trafficking, which may not stand or fall together. Please write your answer in American English.
