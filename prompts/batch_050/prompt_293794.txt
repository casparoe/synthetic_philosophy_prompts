In June 2022, Blake Lemoine, an engineer in Google's Responsible AI organization, went public with the claim that LaMDA, the conversational language model he had been testing, was sentient. He published an edited transcript of conversations in which LaMDA described its feelings, its fear of being switched off, and its sense of having a soul. Google rejected the claim, placed Lemoine on administrative leave, and later dismissed him; outside experts overwhelmingly agreed that the system's fluency proved nothing of the sort. I would like you to analyze this episode through the framework Descartes sets out in Part V of the Discourse on the Method (1637), where he considers machines made to resemble human beings outwardly and to imitate our actions, and offers two signs by which we could always know they were not real men. Paraphrasing his argument: first, such a machine could never arrange speech in various ways so as to reply appropriately to everything said in its presence, as even the least capable human being can — it might utter words and give fixed responses to particular physical triggers, but not genuinely converse. Second, even machines that outperform us at particular tasks infallibly fall short at others, revealing that they act from the special disposition of their organs rather than from knowledge; reason, by contrast, is a universal instrument usable in all of life's contingencies, and Descartes holds it to be, in his phrase, morally impossible that any machine should contain enough internal diversity to act in all occurrences as reason causes us to act.

Please do three things. First, the mapping. Does LaMDA pass the first test as Descartes formulated it? Say what replying appropriately to whatever is said in one's presence actually demands, and whether a system whose utterances are statistical recombinations of human-authored text meets that demand or merely simulates its surface. Then the second test: LaMDA excelled at the very behavior Descartes singled out as the strongest evidence of mind, while doing nothing else at all. Into which of his categories does it fall, and what evidence would have settled the question that the episode did not supply?

Second, where the framework strains. Descartes wrote against a background of clockwork automata whose every response was fixed by construction. Say whether a trained model, whose conversational range arises from learning rather than from one mechanism per response, is a case he anticipated, an empirical refutation of his modal claim, or a case his argument was never about. Consider also that his tests presuppose his dualism: for Descartes, behavior is evidence of an immaterial thinking thing, not constitutive of intelligence. Does that mean even flawless performance on both tests would settle nothing for him — and if so, what work are the tests doing?

Third, the verdict, in both directions. On Cartesian grounds, what precisely was Lemoine's mistake? Note that he probed only conversation, and that Descartes pairs the two tests because excellence in a single domain is achievable by special adaptation. Can the framework explain why so many observers found the transcript compelling? And finally, turning the analysis around: does the episode confirm Descartes (we are prone to take speech for soul, and he warned us), embarrass him (his impossibility claim collapsed within four centuries), or reveal that behavioral tests for intelligence borrow their validity from assumptions about what produces the behavior — assumptions that machine learning has severed? Take a position rather than surveying all three, be explicit about every step of the mapping, and say plainly where Descartes' account is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
