I work with digitized colonial administrative records from the 1800s and I keep running into a situation I dont have a word for. Theres a village, say, that shows up in the records constantly -- tax rolls, court files, vaccination lists, police reports, hundreds of pages. And yet every single page is about the village only at the exact moment it touches the state: when it pays, disobeys, gets sick, gets counted. So the archive is dense, but the people in it are somehow still silent. And heres the part that really gets me: because the records are so dense, historians treat the village as well-documented, so the gaps dont even register as gaps. Its not that sources are missing. The abundance itself is what hides the silence. I know people talk about archival silence, but that usually means things that never got recorded at all, and here nothing is missing, exactly. Is there an established term for this -- an archive thats loud and silent at the same time? If not, what would you call it, and how would you distinguish it from ordinary archival silence on one side and from plain old biased sources on the other? I'm not just after a label, I want to know where the edges of the phenomenon are.
