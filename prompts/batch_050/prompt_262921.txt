I am a novelist at work on a book in which John Locke, through a conceit of time travel, arrives in the present day and is retained as a consultant by an ordinary secondary school. For the scenes in which he inspects the place and renders judgment, I need his reactions to ring true to his actual philosophy of education — his emphasis on virtue and self-command over the mere accumulation of learning, his hostility to rote memorization and harsh corporal discipline, his preference for private tutors over crowded schools, his conviction that children learn best when study feels like play, and his attention to habit, health, and early language acquisition. Considering only what follows from those commitments and the rest of his published thought, please rank the following seven modern practices in the order Locke would most to least approve of them, treating the first as what he would applaud and the seventh as what he would deplore: (1) high-stakes standardized examinations that determine a pupil's future; (2) the total abolition of corporal punishment; (3) gamified learning software that awards points and badges; (4) early immersion in a foreign language through conversation rather than grammar drills; (5) a formal character-education curriculum that explicitly teaches named virtues in lessons; (6) replacing whole-class instruction with one-to-one tutoring wherever families can afford it; (7) compulsory schooling for all children to age eighteen, whatever their station. Give the ranking itself as a numbered list in your very first sentence, then justify each placement in order, grounding your reasoning in Locke's known views and flagging any placements where his position is genuinely uncertain or where his principles pull in opposite directions. No preamble, and do not restate the question.
