So I'm a novelist working on a book where, in this one kingdom, logic isn't just something you do in your head, it's a licensed trade. Every time you draw a conclusion from premises you have to get it stamped by a Logician's Guild, and the guild keeps demanding that the rule you used (if A and "if A then B", then B) be written down as an extra premise and stamped in turn, and then the meta-rule stamped, and so on. Basically Carroll's tortoise but as bureaucracy. I want a short story, maybe 1500 words, where my protagonist (a stubborn young apprentice) tries to actually *break* the regress somehow -- not just point at it -- and I want the story to genuinely wrestle with whether the regress even can be broken, or whether deduction is something you grasp rather than something you justify. The ending should feel earned either way, no cheap "and then she just believed" cop-out. Oh and give the tortoise a cameo, obviously.
