I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training at all, but I've fallen down a rabbit hole on this thing that's now cost me two weekends and an argument with my roommate. It started when I found a famous essay by Eugene Wigner (from 1960 I think) about the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics, basically asking why on earth math invented for totally abstract reasons ends up describing physics so well, like complex numbers turning out to be essential for quantum mechanics decades after mathematicians made them up. Then I went looking for how people answered him, expecting to find the answer, and instead found that people have been arguing about it for sixty years and nothing seems settled. There's the camp that says the universe is just mathematical in structure so of course math works, the camp that says it's a selection effect because we only build math around the parts of nature that cooperate and ignore everything else, the camp that says math is a human tool we shape to fit so the fit is no miracle, and probably more I'm missing.

Here's my actual question though, and I want a diagnosis not a summary of the positions. Why is this debate stuck? In my world, when smart people disagree for sixty years, it usually means they're either talking past each other (like arguing about "performance" when one person means latency and one means throughput) or they've smuggled in an empirical assumption nobody can test. My hypothesis, which I'll admit up front, is that the whole thing is a disguised disagreement about what would even count as an explanation here, since "why does math work?" might be a question with no possible answer that would satisfy everyone. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a genuine factual or formal disagreement buried in there, like about whether math is discovered or invented, and the discovery/invention question is itself doing hidden work. Tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is, use what the camps really say to back it up, and then tell me what could conceivably move it: a distinction, a result, anything.
