I'm in a grad seminar on AI alignment and this week we did process-based vs outcome-based supervision, and I keep getting stuck on something that seems obvious to me but apparently isn't, because the safety people in the readings still sound worried. As I understand it: outcome supervision just rewards the final answer, which is famously gameable — reward hacking, models learning to produce outputs that *look* right to a human rater rather than ones that actually are right, Goodhart-style stuff. Process supervision instead gives feedback on each intermediate step of the reasoning, so the model only gets rewarded for steps a human evaluator actually endorses.

So here's my confusion. If we supervise the process step by step, then reward hacking becomes basically impossible by construction, no? Any hack the model tries would have to show up as a fishy step somewhere in the chain, and the human rater would catch it and dock the reward. Deception needs a hidden move, and there are no hidden moves when every move is graded. Given that, why do alignment researchers still lose sleep over "deceptive" or "scheming" models? Isn't process supervision more or less the fix — supervise the reasoning, not just the result, and deception has nowhere to hide? My friend in the seminar mumbled something about the chain of thought not being the whole computation, but honestly I don't see what else the computation could be, the reasoning is right there in the text in front of you. What am I missing?
