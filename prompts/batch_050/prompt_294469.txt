My brother-in-law and I got into it over Christmas about God's existence, and at some point I pushed what I thought was my best argument: if there were a perfectly loving God, you'd expect his existence to be a lot more obvious than it is -- honest, open-hearted people who search for him sincerely often come away with nothing, and a loving parent doesn't play hide-and-seek forever with a child who's calling out for them. He shut this down with a line he'd clearly been saving up: "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence." Said it very slowly, like he'd checkmated me. It annoyed me partly because I couldn't say on the spot what was wrong with it, and partly because I suspect it might actually be right in some sense and I want to know which.

Here's my rough thought, which you can correct if it's off: the slogan seems obviously true if it just means "failing to find something doesn't logically prove it isn't there" (fine, God might exist even if I've never seen a scrap of evidence). But it seems obviously false if it means "not finding something is never a reason to lower your confidence it's there" -- because if I open every drawer looking for my keys and they're not in any of them, that absolutely is evidence they're not in the house. So whether the slogan rescues him seems to hinge on something like: *would we expect to see more evidence of God than we do, if God existed?* And the whole hiddenness argument is precisely the claim that we would. Which would mean his slogan, on the only reading that's actually defensible, concedes the argument rather than blocking it. But maybe I'm being too quick.

Can you lay out, as a proper menu, the different precise claims "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence" could be making? For each one, say whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or just false; what it would take to make it precise (talking about expectations, probabilities, searches that could have succeeded, and so on); and crucially whether that reading would actually support the use my brother-in-law put it to -- i.e. blocking the hiddenness argument without him having to separately argue that a loving God *wouldn't* make himself more evident. If it turns out no plausible reading does that work for him, say so plainly. Please answer in British English.
