I serve on my university's research ethics board, which normally reviews human and animal studies. A computer science group has submitted a protocol that falls outside both categories, and the board is split on whether we even have anything to review.

The group runs evolutionary simulations of vast populations of very simple agents -- each agent is a small, fixed set of rules, individually no more sophisticated than a thermostat. Over millions of generations, colony-level patterns emerge that no individual agent encodes: the population as a whole reallocates agents among tasks when damaged, maintains internal conditions against external shocks, and persistently steers the simulated environment toward states that preserve these capacities, even when doing so requires sacrificing large fractions of the population. The principal investigator describes this as "just statistics over dumb rules." A philosopher on the board objects that this is exactly the wrong level at which to look: if mentality is a matter of what a system *does* at some level of organization rather than what its parts are made of, then a sufficiently integrated colony-level process could constitute a subject of experience even though no component does -- in the way an ant colony might have a mind though no ant does. The run is scheduled to end in two weeks, and the team plans to terminate it by abruptly cutting power mid-execution, since that is cheapest.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action -- proceed as planned, proceed with conditions (specify them), or halt and re-review -- and it must do three things: state a position on whether colony-level minds are possible in principle and what that implies for a system where we cannot tell whether the threshold has been crossed; weigh the PI's argument that the agents' simplicity settles the matter, treating it seriously rather than dismissing it; and say what, if anything, the board owes to a system whose moral status is genuinely uncertain. Keep the opinion under 600 words.
