Construct a thought experiment for an upper-level seminar that cleanly separates Mencius’s account of moral cultivation from Xunzi’s while forcing each view to make a different prediction. The scenario should be realistic and contemporary, no more than 250 words, and involve two comparable people receiving different forms of ethical training in a school, hospital, or workplace. In one path, an already present but narrow moral impulse is deliberately extended to new beneficiaries; in the other, conduct and eventually motivation are reshaped through explicit rules, ritualized roles, and repeated practice. Both paths should produce the same reliably generous behavior during supervised training, but the scenario should include a later unsupervised situation outside the trained role in which the two theories plausibly predict different responses.

Avoid caricaturing either position. Do not represent Mencius as holding that virtue develops without effort or adverse circumstances as irrelevant, and do not represent Xunzi as holding that people are irredeemably selfish or that ritual merely restrains behavior without transforming motivation. Do not stipulate inner states that already encode the conclusion, such as declaring one trainee naturally good and the other naturally bad; the divergent predictions should arise from the cultivation mechanisms described. Avoid neuroscience, textual quotations, and citations.

After presenting the scenario, identify the exact point at which the predictions diverge and explain why each view generates its prediction. State which variable the case isolates, what would happen if the later unsupervised test were removed, and what would follow if both trainees generalized their training equally well. Then give the strongest objection available to a defender of each view and say whether the case favors one theory or primarily clarifies their different empirical commitments. Conclude with a brief epistemic note distinguishing the broadly accepted contrast between the two philosophers from interpretations that remain genuinely contested, without presenting a contested reading as the consensus.
