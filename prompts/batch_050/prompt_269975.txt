After forty years as a population biologist I retired and, as one does, immediately started a classics-and-big-history reading group with some neighbours. We've just finished Polybius Book VI, and there's a passage from the Shuckburgh translation that I keep chewing on, where he explains how kingship rots into tyranny:

"But when their royal power became hereditary in their family, and they found every necessary for security ready to their hands, as well as more than was necessary for their personal support, then they gave the rein to their appetites; imagined that rulers must needs wear different clothes from those of subjects; have different and elaborate luxuries of the table; and must even seek sensual indulgence, however unlawful the source, without fear of denial."

What struck the scientist in me is that this is basically a generational mechanism: the cycle doesn't turn because of some law of history, it turns because selection stops operating once succession becomes hereditary -- the second and third generations are bred, not tested. That got us arguing about whether the other "cyclical" authors on our list (Ibn Khaldun, Spengler, Toynbee) are proposing the same kind of mechanism at different scales, or genuinely different theories that just all happen to be round rather than straight. Half the group insists a cycle is a cycle; the other half thinks lumping them together is like calling every oscillating system a pendulum.

So here's what I'd like: a decision tree for the group that takes someone from "I think history shows recurring patterns of rise and decline" to a named position, with each branch a question they can answer without knowing whose view it lands them on. The splits I care about are things like: what's the unit that cycles (a constitution, a dynasty, a whole civilization, an empire); what's the actual motor of decline (hereditary degeneration of rulers à la the passage above, loss of group solidarity, failure of a creative elite, demographic or material pressure); is there an escape hatch (Polybius seems to think the mixed constitution buys Rome time, Spengler apparently doesn't), and does the wheel turn on a flat plane or ratchet upward, i.e. cyclical form with linear content. As leaves I want at least: Polybius's anacyclosis, Ibn Khaldun's dynastic cycle, Spengler, Toynbee, a Christian/eschatological linear view, a Whiggish progress view, and a modern quantitative "secular cycles" position. Seven to ten branch questions is plenty. Please also mark any combination of answers nobody seems to occupy and say briefly why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any pair of leaves your tree honestly can't separate -- I have a sneaking suspicion Ibn Khaldun and the modern demographic version might come out glued together on my questions, but I could be wrong about that.
