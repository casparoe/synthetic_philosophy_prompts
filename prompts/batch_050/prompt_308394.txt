So I got into an argument with my housemate last weekend (she's a preference satisfaction person, I'm the resident hedonist) and in trying to defend myself I realised I don't actually know if my own views fit together. Here's roughly where I've landed on the nature of pleasure, and I want an audit, not a lecture: tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for each real tension what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.

My commitments: (1) I'm a hedonist about welfare -- a life goes well for someone in proportion to the pleasure it contains and the pain it avoids, full stop. (2) I'm convinced by the heterogeneity worry: there's no single felt quality shared by the pleasure of a hot shower, the pleasure of finally getting a joke, the relief when a biopsy comes back clear, and the smug glow of winning an argument. So felt-quality theories of pleasure are dead. (3) Instead I lean attitudinal: what makes something a pleasure is that the subject takes pleasure in it, is pleased with the experience as it unfolds, wants it to continue -- the attitude does the work, not any sensation. (4) Pain is just the mirror image of this: displeasure is the negative attitude, wanting the experience to stop, and there's nothing more to say. The theory of pain falls out of the theory of pleasure for free. (5) I take it for granted that pleasures and pains can in principle be added up within a life and at least roughly compared across people -- otherwise triage decisions and welfare economics are astrology. (6) And despite being a hedonist I keep finding myself agreeing with the old Mill move: some pleasures are simply better qua pleasure, like the pleasure of reading a great poem beats the pleasure of eating greasy chips even at equal intensity and duration, and anyone who disagrees just hasn't had the good one.

One thing I specifically want: don't just flag everything. I suspect at least one of these pairs looks inconsistent but actually isn't once you think it through, and I'd like that identified as such. And I'm not attached to any particular one of these -- don't assume you know which one I'd drop.
