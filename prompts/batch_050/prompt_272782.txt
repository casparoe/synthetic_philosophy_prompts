I am editing the proceedings of a small working group that has spent a year on the German "war guilt" debate, and I need you to draft the document the group should have produced. The two camps are these. The Fischer camp, following the controversy opened by Fritz Fischer's 1961 study of German war aims, holds that the German leadership deliberately exploited the July 1914 crisis to launch a war of expansion, that wartime documents such as the Septemberprogramm reveal pre-existing hegemonic aims rather than improvised demands, and that Germany therefore bears the primary, qualitatively distinct responsibility for the First World War. The shared-responsibility camp holds that the war arose from interlocking mobilization timetables, alliance commitments, and reciprocal miscalculation across all the great powers, that German policy in July 1914 was largely reactive and defensive-minded (however reckless), and that singling out one nation mistakes the nature of the catastrophe.

Write, in their joint voice, the statement of an adversarial collaboration between the two camps, using exactly these sections in this order:

1. **Agreed foundations** (roughly 300 words): a numbered list of at least eight claims both camps can sign without reservation -- for instance, about the blank cheque to Austria-Hungary, the existence of the Septemberprogramm and the limits of what it proves, the Schlieffen Plan's structural pressure toward escalation, and what the pre-1914 diplomatic record does and does not establish. Each claim must be substantive rather than vacuous.

2. **Verbal disagreements dissolved** (roughly 150 words): at least two points on which the camps discover they were using a term differently (candidates you may consider: "war aims," "defensive war," "responsibility") and can now state the residual difference, if any, in shared vocabulary.

3. **Remaining disagreements** (roughly 300 words): a set of paired statements, each pair giving one thesis as the Fischer camp formulates it and the corresponding thesis as the shared-responsibility camp formulates it, worded so that each side would accept the statement of its own view as fair. Include at least one disagreement about evidence, one about counterfactuals (would a general war have come anyway, and when), and one about the very coherence of ascribing blame to a nation as opposed to identifiable decision-makers.

4. **Cruxes and tests** (roughly 400 words): for each of at least three cruxes -- sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp -- name a concrete document, episode, or historiographical finding that both camps agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts that evidence shows or would show if it came to light. Possible cruxes include the evidentiary weight of the Septemberprogramm (government programme or wish list?), the intended scope of the blank cheque, and whether peacetime annexationist planning implies intent to start a great war.

5. **Sign-off conditions** (roughly 150 words): for each camp, one or two findings or arguments that would cause it to concede substantial ground, stated in its own terms.

Throughout, keep the language neutral: no camp's position should be stated in the other camp's rhetoric, and the shared sections must not quietly presuppose either side's conclusion. Where the historical record is genuinely contested, say that it is contested rather than resolving it by fiat.
