So I've been going back and forth in my head about this idea I stumbled across called tranquilism -- roughly the view that what ultimately matters is just the absence of suffering and craving, and that pleasure/joy/happiness don't add anything on top of that, they just relieve some want or discomfort. Like, the good part of eating cake is that the craving for cake goes away, and a person who never craved cake isn't missing out on anything. I'm honestly torn, because on one hand it explains a lot (why a perfect nap isn't "worse" than a party, why adding happy people to the world doesn't obviously make it better), but on the other hand it feels like it's calling my entire life of enjoying stuff a kind of treadmill of fixing needs I wish I never had.

Anyway here's what I want: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a tranquilist and someone who holds the more common view that positive experiences are valuable in their own right, not just as relief (call them the "hedonic pluralist" or just the pleasure-matters person). Imagine they've agreed to stop talking past each other and write up one document they'd both sign.

The statement should have:

1. An agreed-claims section -- numbered points both sides can genuinely endorse. Stuff like what they both think the debate is NOT about, which thought experiments they both accept as legit tests vs which ones they think are rigged, what they agree about the badness of suffering, etc. Don't pad this with vacuous stuff like "we both agree wellbeing matters" -- I want points that actually took work to find.

2. A disagreements section where each remaining disagreement is stated precisely, and each side's position is phrased the way that side would phrase it -- no strawmen. To keep this concrete, have them argue over two specific cases: (a) the "contented monk" case -- someone in a state of total absence of craving and discomfort but with no pleasure, vs someone with an ordinary mix of joy and longing, and whether the monk's life is missing anything; and (b) a case I made up: there's a button that would create a trillion new people who feel constant mild delight but never crave anything and never suffer. The pleasure-matters person presumably says push it, the tranquilist presumably says it's neutral or pointless. Have them hash out whether the disagreement there is really about population ethics instead of about the value of pleasure, or whether it's the same disagreement twice.

3. A cruxes section: the sub-questions where, if one side got convinced, they'd actually change their mind. For each crux, record what each side predicts or concedes in advance. For example, does the pleasure-matters person accept that if it turned out every pleasure they introspect carefully is just relief-of-want, they'd convert? Does the tranquilist accept that some case would count as genuine pleasure-without-relief?

Also, if part of their disagreement turns out to be just verbal (like they're using "pleasure" or "craving" differently), the statement should flag which part and explain why.

I'm not a philosopher, I just think about this stuff a lot, so keep the jargon light -- define any technical terms you do use. And write the whole thing in American English.
