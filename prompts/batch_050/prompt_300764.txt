For my aesthetics seminar on interpretation, I want a decision-tree handout that sorts students into positions on whether authorial intention fixes the meaning of a text. Build the tree from a case rather than a definition: suppose a poem assembled by an algorithm with no designer intending any particular content strikes several students as genuinely meaningful. Each node should be a question a student can answer without knowing the labels — whether meaning requires an intending mind at all; whether it is the actual historical author's intention that constrains meaning, or the intention an ideally informed reader would attribute; whether an author can fail to mean what she tried to mean; and whether, where evidence underdetermines intention, interpreters ought to prefer the reading that makes the work best. The leaves should include actual intentionalism, hypothetical intentionalism, anti-intentionalism of the New Critical sort, and value-maximizing interpretation, plus one explicitly unnamed leaf for a coherent-looking combination of answers that no one defends, with a sentence diagnosing why it is empty (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). For each leaf, state in one line what the view must say about the machine-made poem — that it lacks meaning, or that its meaning has nothing to do with anyone's intention. Flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot really separate. Finally, mark which nodes represent genuinely settled distinctions among specialists and which remain live disputes, and do not present any contested position as the consensus view.
