I'm a novelist working on a book set in a world where modal realism turns out to be literally true -- every possible world is as concrete as ours -- and one of the side characters runs a small travel company built entirely on counterpart theory. Since nothing literally exists in more than one world, they can't actually send you anywhere; instead they assure clients that a counterpart of you is already at the destination having a wonderful time, and that according to the best metaphysics this is as close to "you being there" as anything could possibly be.

Write the company's deadpan welcome letter to a newly signed-up client, maybe 450 words, in the voice of an over-earnest customer service rep who has completely internalized Lewis's view. The comedy should come from getting the philosophy right, not from making things up: counterparts picked out by similarity rather than identity, the denial of transworld identity, the fact that *your* counterpart's trip is metaphysically the whole of what it would be for you to have gone. At least once the letter should brush aside, with total corporate politeness, the Kripke/Humphrey-style complaint -- surely I care whether *I* get to see Paris, not whether someone very much like me does. Stay deadpan the whole way through, no winking at the reader, but by the end anyone who knows the metaphysics should be able to tell exactly what's being ironized and should feel the force of that objection pressing through the polite prose -- maybe via one repeated client complaint the rep keeps promising to "escalate" and never does.

One formatting note: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no Q&A structure -- just continuous prose, the way an actual letter would read.
