I co-teach an upper-level phil course on probability and rational belief, and my one solo seminar this semester is on updating on conditionals, built around the Judy Benjamin problem. The students can do Bayes' theorem in their sleep and accept plain conditionalization without blinking, but none of them have ever thought about what happens when the evidence itself is conditional. I want a teaching plan for the session, not an explanation of the problem.

The case as I'll give it to them: a soldier is dropped somewhere in territory that's half Blue region, half Red region, 50-50, and the Red region splits into three equal sectors R1, R2, R3. She recieves one radio message telling her that if she's in Red, she's not in R3. The trap is that conditionalizing on the material conditional (i.e. ruling out R3 and nothing else) drops her credence in Red to 0.4, which feels wrong -- the message was supposed to only carry information on the supposition that she's in Red.

Things I want the plan to settle:

- The order of ideas and the dependency structure. Do they need to watch the naive 0.4 calculation go through cleanly before I problematize it, or should the material-vs-ordinary-indicative conditional distinction come first so they smell trouble early? I genuinely don't know, so give a recommendation with reasons, and note what each component of the session depends on.
- The carrying example. Is the soldier story itself the right vehicle, or should I first build a stripped-down card version (draw from a small deck, message: if red, not the three of hearts) and port the structure over? Say what the example needs to keep and what it must leave out so that the pull toward 0.4 emerges from the students themselves rather than me asserting it.
- The misconception map. I'm bracing for at least three: that 0.4 is simply right and the intuition is the thing to discard; that the message is straightforward evidence against Red so of course the credence falls; and that the fix is to refuse to update at all. Tell me where in the sequence each one should be headed off, and which others I should expect that aren't on my list.
- How to land the actual resolution -- that the message constrains a conditional probability (credence in not-R3 given Red goes to 1) rather than reporting a proposition to conditionalize on, and that update rules like Jeffrey conditioning or minimum cross-entropy respect the constraint while keeping P(Red) at 0.5 -- without it looking like a patch bolted on to save the intuition.
- One end-of-session question or variant case that would show the idea actually landed, and what a memorized-the-moves-but-didn't-get-it answer would look like.
- What I should openly refuse to cover in 75 minutes. I'm tempted to gesture at Adams' thesis and the whole indicative-vs-subjunctive literature but suspect that way lies madness.
