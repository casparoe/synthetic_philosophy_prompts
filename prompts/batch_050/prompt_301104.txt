Can you write me an argument for a position I've been chewing on about acausal trade? Here's the setup. The standard pitch goes something like: if some distant civilization, or a future superintelligence, or a sim running somewhere is enough like me and using a similar decision procedure, then my choice here correlates with theirs even though nothing causal passes between us, and so I have reason to cooperate with them the way I'd cooperate in a twin prisoner's dilemma. Fine. But people seem to slide from that to a much stronger claim, that I should be cooperating with hypothetical counterparts I have no actual evidence for, on the grounds that they might exist somewhere in the multiverse or might be simulating me. My position is that this slide is illegitimate: acausal considerations only give you genuine reasons when you have independent evidence that the counterpart exists and is correlated with you, and mere logical possibility or the bare hypothesis that someone might be simulating you doesn't cut it, because otherwise the framework lets any arbitrary hypothesis jerk you around and you've reinvented Pascal's mugging with extra steps. So argue for that position, that acausal trade needs an evidential floor and doesn't survive without one. Take a clear stand rather than hedging between sides, and somewhere in the argument engage seriously with the best counterpressure, like the idea that refusing to cooperate with merely-possible counterparts is itself parochial. Then at the end, tell me what would change your mind: what argument or consideration would actually push you to the other verdict, or where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail. Aim for maybe 500-700 words, and please write it as continuous prose only, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, just like a long email to a friend who happens to be into decision theory.
