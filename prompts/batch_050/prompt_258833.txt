So I host a philosophy of physics reading group (loosely -- mostly we argue), and two of our regulars have spent the whole semester on the gauge-reality question producing nothing but longer and longer versions of the same fight. One of them is a hardliner for the redundancy view: gauge symmetry is pure descriptive surplus, a feature of our mathematics and not of the world, the only real quantities are the gauge-invariant ones, and gauge-fixing is like choosing coordinates -- nothing physical changes and nothing physical is lost. The other thinks that throws away real structure: the Aharonov-Bohm effect, the role of the potential in the Higgs mechanism, and the charges you can actually measure at infinity all show that gauge-variant quantities, or at least the relations they encode, correspond to something out there. (I'm oversimplifying both of them, as they'll both immediatly tell me.)

I want them to stop performing and produce something. Draft the joint statement they'd come up with after an honest adversarial collaboration. Specifically:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims -- things both can sign. For instance: what gauge invariance actually does in the formalism; that gauge-fixing changes no observable; what the hole argument does and doesn't show; that "the Higgs breaks the gauge symmetry" is strictly speaking loose talk given Elitzur's theorem (the result that a local gauge symmetry can't be spontaneously broken). Find more of these than I've listed -- I think there's alot of common ground they keep failing to notice.

2. A disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated in wording that each side would sign for its own row. No strawmen, and no question-begging vocabulary smuggled in as neutral -- the word "mere" in "mere redundancy" is doing work, and so is "corresponds to something real" if it's never cashed out.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions where, if settled, at least one side would have to actually update. My guesses: (a) how to explain the Aharonov-Bohm phase -- a local story in terms of the potential, or a nonlocal story in terms of gauge-invariant holonomies -- and whether experiments where the magnetic field is completely confined (superconducting shielding around a toroidal magnet, so there's genuinely no field along the paths) settle anything between those; (b) whether diffeomorphism invariance in GR plus the hole argument force the redundancy reading for gauge symmetries across the board, or only tell against manifold substantivalism specifically; (c) whether the measurable charges and memory effects associated with gauge transformations that act non-trivially at the boundary of spacetime are evidence that some gauge transformations are physical symmetries rather than redundancies -- or whether those are a different symmetry wearing the same name. For each crux, name the case, experiment, or argument both agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts or concedes about it. Add any crux I've missed.

4. End by saying plainly which part of their fight, if any, is verbal rather than substantive, and why. My money is on "is the potential real?" dissolving into a dispute about what counts as an explanation, but they both hate that suggestion, so make the case properly.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section first, then the table, then the cruxes one at a time. Keep it in their register -- physics grad students who dabble in philosophy, not the reverse. Somewhere around 800-1200 words.
