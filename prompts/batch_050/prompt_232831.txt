A philosophically literate reader has warned me that my description is imprecise: on counterpart theory, these other women are not literally identical to my protagonist but are worldbound individuals related to her by resemblance. I am therefore trying to stress-test counterpart theory before deciding whether the objection should change the scene. The objections I have so far are: first, the concern objection—my anxiety about how my life could have gone seems to concern me, not someone elsewhere who merely resembles me; second, the context-sensitivity objection—because different standards of similarity select different counterparts, whether I could have had a property may become objectionably dependent on conversational interests, weakening the objectivity of essence; and third, the iterated-modality objection—since a counterpart of a counterpart need not be a counterpart of the original, counterpart semantics appears to threaten intuitive principles governing nested possibilities.

Give me one further objection to counterpart theory that is structurally different from all three, not a rhetorical restatement of them, a special case of one, or merely the bare assertion that transworld identity is intuitive. Also give the strongest counterpart-theoretic reply and assess whether that reply concedes too much.
