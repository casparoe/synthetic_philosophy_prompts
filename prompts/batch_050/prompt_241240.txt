So my alignment reading group keeps re-litigating process-based vs outcome-based supervision and I want to settle it with an actual instrument instead of vibes. Write me a decision tree that sorts someone into a named position on how to supervise a model that's smarter than the person overseeing it.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of famous names: figure out which questions generate the space first, then locate the positions in it. Start from the commitment everyone at the table shares — the model will sometimes produce outputs the overseer can't directly verify — and branch on diagnostic questions like: does your feedback signal attach to the final answer or to the steps the model took to get there; do you treat the chain-of-thought as evidence about what the model is doing, or as part of its cognition that you risk corrupting by training on it; is the judge a human, a weaker trusted model, or copies of the model itself. Phrase every node as a question with a concrete case attached, one someone could answer without knowing which leaf it funnels them toward — e.g. a proof the overseer can't check, where they'd have to choose between rewarding a final answer that a weaker model vouches for and rewarding individual steps that survive adversarial critique. The positions should genuinely come apart on each case.

The leaves I need at minimum: pure outcome-based RL on verifiable results, process supervision over human-legible reasoning, debate, recursive/iterated-amplification-style oversight, and the dont-train-on-the-CoT-at-all camp (the "you'll just select for obfuscation, monitor but never optimize the trace" position). If some hybrid genuinely deserves its own leaf rather than being a compromise label, give it one. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate. For any empty combination of answers, one line on whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

One hard requirement: at least one node has to turn on the legibility/steganography question, since thats where our group actually splits — half of us think training on legible reasoning inevitably Goodharts it, the other half think outcome-only training does the same thing but blind.

Start with the tree itself, no preface or explanation of what youre about to do. Aim for 8–12 branch nodes.
