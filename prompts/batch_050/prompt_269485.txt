I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm drafting a long feature built around what I suspect is my thesis: that the logic of costly signaling -- the handicap principle, in its modern formalized version -- is the best general explanation we have for conspicuous, apparently wasteful displays, from the peacock's train to conspicuous consumption, public generosity, fasting, and other expensive commitments in humans. My working claim is not the crude one that waste itself attracts; it's the refined one that a signal can remain honest only if its cost is differential -- cheap for high-quality senders, ruinous for impostors -- and that this differential-cost structure explains both why such displays exist and why receivers rationally trust them.

Before I write 8,000 words committed to this, I want the red-team memo. What would actually count against this position?

Please be concrete and layered. First: which observations would genuinely threaten it, as opposed to merely complicating it? For instance, does it matter whether some celebrated textbook case (peacock trains, stotting gazelles) has produced mixed or weak empirical support, or would the theory survive losing its flagship examples? If honest signaling can be sustained at equilibrium without the signal actually being costly to produce -- which I gather the formal models allow in some cases -- how much of the rhetorical force of the handicap idea drains away, and is that a refutation or a refinement?

Second: what would the defenders themselves concede as a defeater? I want to distinguish the critics' objections (the principle gets stretched to fit everything, it's unfalsifiable storytelling about human behavior) from the weaknesses its own theorists would admit to.

Third, the falsifiability worry head-on: when a display turns out to be cheap, proponents can say it must have differential costs somewhere (time, risk, opportunity), and when no honesty is found, they can say the equilibrium has collapsed into cheating. Has the theory been qualified into a shape where no observation could dislodge it -- and if so, is that a defect for a theory of this kind, or is some insulation just what a mature equilibrium theory looks like?

Finally, tell me where the position is genuinely exposed: what it predicts that rival explanations (runaway sexual selection, sensory exploitation, direct-benefit accounts, cultural rather than biological transmission for the human cases) do not, and which of those rivals would gain the most ground from each failure. I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely embarrassing, so I know what my feature can and cannot responsibly claim.
