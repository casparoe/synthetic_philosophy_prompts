My reading group got into low-impact AI and someone produced this argument that the whole project is hopeless, and none of us can break it. (1) A side effect is a change your action brings about that you didn't intend. (2) Every action brings about a huge number of unintended changes -- you shift air molecules, delay strangers by seconds, select which of trillions of possible futures happens. (3) So every action has enormous side effects. (4) A low-impact agent is one that avoids side effects, so the only genuinely low-impact policy is to do nothing at all. (5) But doing nothing changes things too: the world would have gone differently had you acted, so inaction has just as many unintended consequences, they're just less visible. (6) Therefore nothing is low-impact, not even sitting still, and "avoid side effects" is meaningless unless you first say which changes matter -- that is, unless you already have the full specification of human values that impact measures were supposed to let us skip writing down. Which step do I reject? I'd like to keep (2) and (5) if possible since they look like plain physics, but the conclusion seems to proove too much: by its lights my dishwasher is an ethical catastrophe for sloshing the water around. What does each repair cost, and do the standard fixes -- measuring changes to what people *can do* rather than to what physically happens -- actually escape the argument or just relabel the problem?
