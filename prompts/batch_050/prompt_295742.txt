Exam question

Sleeping Beauty agrees to the following experiment. On Sunday night she is put to sleep, and a fair coin is tossed, unseen by her. If the coin lands Heads, she is awakened once, on Monday. If it lands Tails, she is awakened on Monday, her memory of that awakening is erased during the night, and she is awakened again on Tuesday. She knows the entire setup in advance, and at every awakening her evidence is, as far as she can tell, exactly the same.

(a) State the thirder and halfer answers to the question Beauty is asked at each awakening — what is your credence that the coin landed Heads? — and give the core motivation for each answer. (8 points)

(b) For each position, state the unconditional distribution over the three awakening-events (Monday and Heads; Monday and Tails; Tuesday and Tails) that it implies, and then compute, by conditionalization on the information that it is Monday, the credence in Heads that the position recommends when Beauty is told the day. (8 points)

(c) Reconstruct the standard argument for the thirder answer that proceeds from two premises: a premise fixing Beauty's credence in Heads conditional on being told it is Monday, and an indifference premise covering the two awakenings in the Tails branch. State both premises precisely, show how the answer 1/3 follows from them, and identify which premise a halfer must reject, and why. (9 points)

---

Student's answer

(a) The thirder says Beauty's credence in Heads on awakening should be 1/3; the halfer says it should be 1/2.

The halfer's motivation is that Beauty learns nothing when she wakes. She already knew on Sunday that she would be awakened at least once, so the bare fact of being awake is no evidence at all, and a rational agent does not revise her credences without new evidence. On Sunday her credence in Heads was 1/2, the coin being fair; so it remains 1/2.

The thirder's motivation is that on awakening, everything Beauty is aware of is compatible with exactly three awakening-events: Monday with Heads, Monday with Tails, and Tuesday with Tails. She has no way to tell these apart, so an indifference principle spreads her credence evenly across them, and only one of the three involves Heads. There is also a frequency consideration that settles the question: if the experiment were run many times, two-thirds of all awakenings would occur after Tails tosses, and since Beauty's current awakening is simply one awakening among these, her credence that this awakening follows a Heads toss must be 1/3. Any other answer would be out of step with the actual frequencies.

(b) Thirder: each of the three awakening-events gets unconditional credence 1/3. When Beauty is told it is Monday, Tuesday-and-Tails drops out, leaving Monday-and-Heads and Monday-and-Tails with equal weight, so P(Heads | Monday) = (1/3) / (1/3 + 1/3) = 1/2.

Halfer: Monday-and-Heads gets 1/2; the Tails branch's 1/2 splits evenly between Monday-and-Tails and Tuesday-and-Tails, so 1/4 each. However, when Beauty is told it is Monday, the halfer holds that no revision is called for. The essential claim of the halfer position is that purely self-locating information — information about which awakening she is currently having — cannot be evidence about a coin that was tossed before she woke, and a Monday awakening occurs under Heads and Tails alike. So the halfer's answer is that P(Heads | Monday) stays at 1/2. If one simply conditionalized the distribution above on Monday one would get 2/3, but halfers regard that result as a reductio of applying ordinary conditionalization to self-locating information, not as their position. This is admittedly the counterintuitive price of being a halfer, since Beauty appears not to respond to genuine information.

(c) The standard thirder argument runs on two premises. Premise 1 (the Monday premise): if Beauty were told it is Monday, her credence in Heads should be 1/2, because a Monday awakening occurs whether the coin lands Heads or Tails, so learning that her awakening is the Monday one tells her nothing about the coin. Premise 2 (indifference within Tails): given that the coin landed Tails, Beauty should divide her credence equally between its being Monday and its being Tuesday, since the two awakenings are subjectively indistinguishable and nothing favors either. From Premise 1, Monday-and-Heads and Monday-and-Tails receive equal credence; from Premise 2, Monday-and-Tails and Tuesday-and-Tails receive equal credence. The three events are therefore equal in credence and, being exhaustive, each receives 1/3.

A halfer cannot accept both premises, since together they force 1/3. Because the halfer's whole position is built on the Monday premise — the claim that self-locating information cannot shift credence about the coin — the halfer must instead reject Premise 2, denying that the two awakenings in the Tails branch merit equal credence. The halfer will say that the Tuesday awakening is epistemically redundant, since nothing new could be learned at it that was not already available at the Monday awakening, and that this breaks the symmetry Premise 2 assumes.
