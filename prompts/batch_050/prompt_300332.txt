I'm applying for a one-year postdoc fellowship and need a proposal of about two pages. The topic is the moral equality of combatants, but I want to come at it sideways. The half-formed idea is this. Both sides of the usual debate -- the traditionalists who say soldiers on the just and unjust sides have equal permissions to kill, and the revisionists who deny it -- seem to argue as though the combatants' situation is fixed by the facts about the war: who's aggressing, who's defending. But actual soldiers almost never have access to those facts in a reliable way. They get their picture of the war from their own government, their mates, and whatever media filters through, and the epistemic situation of a conscript on the unjust side can be nearly identical to that of a volunteer on the just side. So my hunch is that whatever equality there is between combatants is epistemic before it's moral, and that the whole debate needs to be rerun at the level of what soldiers can reasonably be expected to know, rather than what's true of the war.

Turn that into a proper proposal. Specifically:
