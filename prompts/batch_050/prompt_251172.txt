Construct a thought experiment that isolates the possibility of genuine gains from trade between conflicting value systems: a case in which two agents hold different terminal values, face a single joint decision, and there exists a determinate compromise outcome that each agent strictly prefers, judged entirely by her own values, to every fair procedural way of settling the conflict — a coin flip between the two pure outcomes, turn-taking across repeated decisions, or any other lottery over them. The specifications are as follows. First, the disagreement must be genuinely normative: neither agent is ignorant of any relevant empirical fact, and neither may come to revise, extend, or abandon her values in the course of the story; the compromise must be preferred without any conversion. Secondly, the compromise must be a single all-at-once outcome, not a lottery, a sequence of trades, or a partition of the decision into separable parts, and it must not be sweetened by anything external to the contested domain — no side payments, no third-party rewards, no reputational or relationship benefits. Thirdly, the setting should be realistic and small-scale (two co-directors of a small grant-making foundation, or two siblings deciding the use of an inherited property), and the scenario itself should be statable in one paragraph. After presenting the case, demonstrate that it meets the specification: state each agent's ranking of the two pure outcomes, the compromise, and the fair lottery, and identify precisely which structural feature of the values — for instance, diminishing marginal returns to each value's realisation, or the compromise scoring on a dimension both parties care about that either pure outcome wholly sacrifices — makes both strict preferences rational by each agent's own lights. Then apply two stress tests. Show that if each value contributed linearly, the compromise would collapse into mere indifference with the lottery, thereby exposing where your case depends on non-linearity. And give the strongest available objection from a sceptic who denies that the case displays real gains from trade — for example, that the values in question are all-or-nothing, so partial realisation is worthless, or that preferring the compromise simply reveals that the values were never genuinely in conflict — and assess whether your case survives it. Please write your answer in British English.
