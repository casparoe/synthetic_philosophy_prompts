Okay so I write novels for a living, and I'm kind of stuck on something and I can't tell if it's a real question or if I'm just being precious about my stuff.

I basically don't remember anything in my head anymore. Fifteen years of novels, and all of it lives in this sprawling mess of notes -- character backstories, timelines, little observations I scribble down, half-formed ideas that only make sense months later when the right other note bumps into them. When I'm drafting, I genuinely could not tell you my own protagonist's eye color or what happened in chapter 4 without checking. The thinking happens in the notebook, not in my skull, if that makes sense. My head is just the place where I go "hmm" and then go look.

Two weeks ago my apartment flooded. Laptop, external drive, the physical notebooks from my first three books, all of it. Cloud backup turned out to have silently stopped syncing in like March, which, cool. A data recovery place says they can maybe pull stuff off the drives for about four thousand dollars, no guarantees.

Here's the thing. My husband says take the insurance money, buy a new laptop, start fresh, "it's just files." And I cannot make him understand that it doesn't feel like losing files. It feels like a chunk of my actual memory got scooped out, like the part of me that knows my own books is just... gone. When I sit down to write now I feel genuinely dumber than I was a month ago, not metaphorically. Meanwhile a writer friend told me the opposite -- that the disaster proves the notes were never really mine in a way that mattered, because if my thinking actually depended on an external crutch that fragile, the "mind" doing the work was never really me to begin with, and a real writer should be able to rebuild from what's in her head.

So two questions, I guess. First: who's right about what I lost? Was the notebook setup actually part of my mind/memory in some literal sense, or am I dressing up grief for office supplies as something deeper? Second, the practical one: is dropping 4k on a maybe-recovery the rational move, or am I overvaluing it precisely because I'm confused about question one? And if my friend is right, does that mean I should rebuild my whole process to keep more in my head -- because honestly the notes system is why my books are any good.

I'd rather you actually commit to a view than hedge in both directions, but tell me how confident you are in it, and point out the specific claim you're least sure about. Typos and all, be honest with me.
