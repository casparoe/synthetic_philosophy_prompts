My home turf is frequentist decision theory and a bit of OR -- loss functions, risk functions, admissibility, minimax and minimax-regret rules, Bayes rules against a prior, complete class theorems. That's the vocabulary I actually think in. I keep running into the Rawls vs. Harsanyi argument about what you'd choose behind a veil of ignorance (Rawls pushing toward the difference principle, Harsanyi toward average utility), and I want it restated in my language as faithfully as possible, seperately for each of them: what exactly is the choice problem, what are the states of nature, what are the actions, what is each one's decision criterion, and what assumptions about the payoff structure and the prior (or the refusal to have one) are doing the work.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Which parts of each argument translate cleanly into risk/prior/criterion talk, which parts you had to fudge or stretch to fit -- I'm guessing Rawls's stuff about the strains of commitment, publicity, and stability goes here, plus the bit where he insists maximin is only rational under special conditions and isn't a general decision rule -- and which parts have no counterpart at all in statistical decision theory. For the untranslatable bits, I want your verdict on whether my framework is missing something (like the possibility that the chooser isn't really picking a policy for herself but justifying terms to other people, which maybe has no analogue in a single-agent decision problem) or whether the philosophers are mashing together things decision theory keeps apart.

One thing I keep tripping over: in my world, minimax vs. Bayes is a question about how much you trust your prior, and refusing to state a prior is just a different move, not a more rational one. So once the translation is done honestly, does Rawls's position survive as a claim about choice under ignorance, or does it turn out to be a claim about something else (fairness? justifiability?) wearing decision-theoretic clothes? And same question for Harsanyi: does his aggregation argument still do the work he wants after translation, or does the conclusion sneak in through the equiprobability and Pareto-style assumptions?
