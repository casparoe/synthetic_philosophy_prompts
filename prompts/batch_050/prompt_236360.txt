Toward the end of that book, Augustine gives a famous example: he is about to recite a psalm he knows by heart. Before he begins, the whole psalm is present to him in expectation; as he recites, the scope of his expectation steadily shrinks while the scope of his memory grows, until the entire psalm has been handed over from expectation into memory; and he adds that the same structure holds for any action, for a whole human life, and for the entire history of humankind. From cases like this Augustine concludes that when we measure time, what we are really measuring is a distension of the mind itself, and that the past and the future exist only insofar as the mind holds them in present memory and present expectation. Please treat this as my paraphrase of Confessions XI.28 rather than a translation.
