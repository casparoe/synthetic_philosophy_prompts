I'm a gameplay programmer and I got into an argument with my brother over the holidays about RimWorld (context: I was harvesting organs from a captured raider, he was horrified, my defense was that the raider is literally a state machine with mood floats and I could show him the code). He lost the argument on points but I keep feeling like he was onto something, and I want to figure out where we actually disagree instead of just talking past each other. I figure the way to do that is a clean test case built to order, since all the ones I find online are about hyper-advanced sentient AI, which dodges my actual question. So: construct me a thought experiment about whether you can wrong a video game character, to these specs. First, the NPC is stipulated to be nothing but code — behavior tree, some utility scores, no learning, definately not sentient, and nobody in the debate is allowed to claim otherwise; I want the case to be about us, not about whether the machine woke up. Second, single-player, offline, nobody ever finds out what you did. Third, stipulate the player's real-world behavior is totally unaffected — no slippery-slope "it'll make you cruel to actual people" business, because that's the boring resolution and empirically shaky anyway.

Within those constraints, the scenario has to force apart two positions I keep hearing: the "it's just pixels, nothing done to an NPC can be wrong, full stop" view, and the view that virtual acts can be wrong because of what they express about or do to the person performing them (character, endorsement, delighting in something, whatever the machinery is). The act in your scenario has to get opposite verdicts from these two views, and it can't be some maximally revolting edge case engineered to trigger disgust — make it the kind of thing an ordinary player might plausibly do in a real, mainstream game, because if the case only works with torture-porn I won't trust it to generalize to my organ farm.

After the scenario, do the bookkeeping: state each view's verdict and exactly why it lands there, name the single variable doing the work (the player's motive? what the act depicts? whether the NPC "remembers"?), and tell me whether removing that variable collapses the case back to "it's just pixels." Then give the best counterattack from whichever side comes out looking worse. Bonus: say what changes if the NPC has persistent memory of how you treated it across playthroughs, which is trivially buildable and honestly the version that keeps me up at night. Whole thing under 600 words.
