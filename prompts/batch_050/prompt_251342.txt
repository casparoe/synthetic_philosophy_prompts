Elena claimed the grandfather paradox shows that backward time travel is logically impossible: if a traveler could go back, she could prevent her own departure, and a world containing a contradiction cannot exist. Marcus replied that this conflates what a traveler can attempt with what can happen — the paradox only shows that any actual history must be self-consistent, so the traveler will fail, just as no amount of trying lets you draw a square circle. Elena countered that this merely relocates the puzzle: nothing in the account explains what stops her from pulling the trigger, and an unexplained guarantee of failure is fatalism dressed in a physics costume. Marcus answered that consistency is a global constraint on whole histories, not a local mechanism, and that demanding a step-by-step explanation misunderstands how laws constrain solutions — though he conceded that on a standard causal picture, the required coincidences (the gun jams every single time) look suspiciously miraculous. Elena then said he had given the game away: a scenario that needs miracles to stay consistent may be logically coherent but is metaphysically impossible. Please adjudicate each step of the exchange — who is right about what, and is Marcus's concession actually fatal to his position? Lead with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the dispute.
