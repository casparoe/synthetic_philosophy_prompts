So: construct me a decision problem in which an agent with imprecise credences gets money-pumped across two sequential choices, while a precise agent whose single credence function lies *inside the very same credal set* walks away completely unscathed.

Constraints:
- Two stages only, and no relevant uncertainty resolves between them (if you want something to resolve, convince me it doesn't break the pump).
- The imprecise agent chooses by maximin over their credal set — they evaluate each option by its worst-case expected value across the set and pick the option with the best worst case. Crucially, at each stage their choice must be genuinely maximin-optimal given what they hold at that point. No cheating by making them do something their own rule forbids, because then the case proves nothing.
- Mundane setting: a farmer, a market stall, someone selling on tickets — no demons, no bookies with supernatural knowledge of the agent's credal set, stakes a normal person could plausibly face.
- The scenario itself should be one paragraph, not counting the workings afterwards.

After the scenario, walk through it: state the credal set, what maximin recommends at each stage and why, where exactly the guaranteed loss accrues, and run the parallel case showing the precise agent escapes. Then tell me which structural feature of the case is doing the work — I've got my suspicions the whole thing hinges on the agent choosing myopically rather than with foresight, but either convince me or correct me — and what happens to the pump if you remove that feature. Does it collapse completly, or does some residue survive? Also give the best counterattack available to a defender of imprecise probabilities (presumably something about resolute choice, or about maximin being the wrong rule to pair with a credal set) and say whether it holds water here.

Don't sit on the fence: take a definite position on whether this kind of pump is a genuine strike against imprecise credences or merely an artefact of marrying them to a myopic choice rule, and give your reasons for that verdict.

British English please. Finish with a last line in exactly this form: \answer{...} where ... is a short label like "genuine strike" or "artefact of myopia" (or your own phrase if neither fits).
