So a few weeks ago I fell down a rabbit hole about Jeffrey conditionalization — the generalization of Bayes where the evidence itself is uncertain, like you're 70% sure the sensor read "red" instead of 100% sure. Seemed useful for a side project I'm doing, so I implemented it: you've got a partition of the state space, and you do new_P(outcome) = sum over cells of new_P(cell) * old_P(outcome | cell). Standard stuff, matches the formula everywhere I found it.

Here's what's driving me nuts. My thing gets two seperate uncertain observations coming in, defined over two different partitions of the same outcomes. If I apply update 1 and then update 2, I get a different final distribution than update 2 then update 1. Concrete failing test case: four outcomes with prior (0.4, 0.1, 0.1, 0.4), partition A is {a,b} vs {c,d}, partition B is {a,c} vs {b,d}, and each update pushes the first cell of its partition to 0.9. A-then-B gives P(b) ≈ 0.069, B-then-A gives P(b) ≈ 0.024. I've gone over the code a bunch and both orderings call the exact same update function, so it's not an implementation issue.

But this should be impossible, right? Bayesian updating is order-invariant — P(H | E, F) = P(H | F, E), you're just conditioning on the conjunction either way, multiplication commutes. And Jeffrey is just Bayes generalized to uncertain evidence, so it has to inherit that property. So where's my bug?

And if you're going to tell me there's no bug and Jeffrey updating genuinely isn't commutative, then I have followups: (1) explain why plain conditioning commutes but this doesn't, in a way that doesn't just restate the formula at me, (2) wich order is the CORRECT posterior for "I received both observations" — if the rule hands me two different answers depending on packet arrival order, is it even a well-defined update rule, and (3) does this mean I should just always track the full joint over the refined partition and do one big update instead of sequential ones?
