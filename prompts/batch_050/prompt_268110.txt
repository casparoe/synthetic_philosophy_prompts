I spent my career as a climate scientist and have only taken up philosophy in retirement, so please excuse any naivety in how I state my views. A reading group I joined has asked each of us to write down our position on decision-making under uncertainty, and before I circulate mine I would like it audited for coherence. Here is what I find myself committed to:

(1) Where probabilities are available, rational choice consists in maximizing expected utility, and an agent's attitude toward risk belongs in the shape of the utility function rather than in the decision rule itself.

(2) A competent Bayesian can always assign some prior, however vague, and so for a rational agent there is strictly speaking no such thing as pure ignorance -- only probabilities one has not yet bothered to elicit.

(3) In Rawls's original position, a party who knows nothing about which person in society she will turn out to be would naturally assign equal probability to occupying each position.

(4) Rawls's two principles of justice, with their priority for the worst-off, are the correct principles, and the original position is the right device for arriving at them.

(5) Maximin is not a generally rational decision rule, but worst-case-first reasoning is legitimate in special circumstances -- where the stakes are catastrophic, the choice irreversible, and probabilities unavailable -- which is how I have always understood the case for precaution in climate policy.

My suspicion is that at least some of these cannot all be true together, but I cannot locate the trouble myself. What I would like from you is a systematic audit: identify which pairs or triples of commitments genuinely conflict, explain precisely why, and rank the tensions by severity. Where a tension is merely apparent -- where two commitments look incompatible but can be reconciled with care -- say so explicitly and show the reconciliation, rather than flagging everything indiscriminately. For each genuine tension, tell me what the least costly revision would be and what it would cost: which commitment to drop or weaken, and what I would thereby lose. Finally, if there is a single revision that resolves more than one tension at once, I would like to know that too, since I have no prior attachment to which of these I should surrender. I am not asking for a survey of the literature or for references -- I can supply those myself -- but for a disciplined examination of what my five commitments, taken together, actually commit me to.
