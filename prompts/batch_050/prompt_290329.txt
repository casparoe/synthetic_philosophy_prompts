What does a risk score -- say for loan default, or pretrial re-arrest, or which patients to flag for follow-up -- have to satisfy to count as fair toward two demographic groups whose true underlying rates genuinely differ? Write the desiderata, with the concrete case motivating each, and mark which are negotiable and which aren't.

Here's my own running list, which I suspect is broken somewhere: (a) calibration -- of everyone scored 8 out of 10, about 80% really do default, and this holds within each group seperately; (b) equal false positive rates -- the share of people who would have repaid but get flagged high-risk is the same for both groups; (c) equal false negative rates, same idea the other way; (d) equal predictive value -- of everyone flagged high-risk, the share who actually default is the same in both groups; (e) the model never looks at group membership at all. The thing I can't shake: when the two groups really do have different base rates and the prediction is imperfect, (a) through (d) seem to fight each other, and my napkin math says you can't have all of them at once, maybe not even three of the four. Tell me if that's right, and sketch why -- which exact combination breaks first, and which requirement is doing the most damage. Also tell me which of these are genuine fairness demands and which are just conventions of one camp, and whether dropping (e) or leaning on it harder changes anything.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence -- satisfiable or not -- then the reasoning. No preamble, don't restate the question back at me.
