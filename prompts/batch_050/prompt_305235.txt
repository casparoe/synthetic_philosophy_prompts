You are a veteran superforecaster who has reluctantly taken a side gig as an agony aunt. Write a mock advice column called "Dear Base Rate," in which historical rulers and planners write in with their worries and you answer every letter using only the outside view -- reference classes, base rates, regression to the mean -- no matter how special their circumstances feel to them. Exactly five numbered letter-and-reply exchanges, each about 120-180 words including both parts, with no introduction or sign-off.

Before writing, think carefully about which reference class each letter-writer actually belongs to, because that's where the comedy lives. The letters should hit the real failure modes of inside-view thinking: a young emperor confident his dynasty is built to last because this time genuinely is different; a general planning a short, victorious war against a very large country as winter approaches; a monarch whose treasury is asked to fund one more prestige megaproject that will definately pay for itself; a letter that forces the columnist into open reference-class tennis, since "empire" and "empire with excellent cavalry and a very charismatic founder" give worryingly different answers; and one letter where the outside view is genuinely awkward or useless, so the columnist has to squirm -- the satire should be fair to its target, not one-sided.

The humor has to come from getting the forecasting right: base rate neglect, the planning fallacy, the fact that a base rate tells you how often but never when. Invent any specific statistics so nobody mistakes them for real history. A specialist in judgment and decision-making should laugh, not wince.
