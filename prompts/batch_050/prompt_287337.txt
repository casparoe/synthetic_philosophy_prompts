I'm in a media studies reading group and last week we did the culture industry chapter of Adorno and Horkheimer's Dialectic of Enlightenment (the 1944 one, in the Cumming translation). One passage that really got people arguing was this one, where they contrast the telephone with the radio:

"The step from the telephone to the radio has clearly distinguished the roles. The former still allowed the subscriber to play the role of subject, and was liberal. The latter is democratic: it turns all participants into listeners and authoritatively subjects them to broadcast programs which are all exactly the same. No machinery of rejoinder has been devised, and private broadcasters are denied any freedom."

The fight in our group was basically about whether the internet blows this up. One side said Adorno and Horkheimer wrote this before a genuine "machinery of rejoinder" existed, and now we have one: comments, quote posts, duets, remixes, anyone can broadcast back, so the listener-turned-passive diagnosis is just dated. The other side said the rejoinder machinery is itself owned, ranked, and monetized by the same industry, so talking back inside the platform is just more content for it. They never really resolved it and it kind of devolved into people being snarky lol.

So here's what I want: write a dialogue between two sharp grad students, one defending each of those readings, specifically hashing out the telephone/radio passage. They should actually engage each other's arguments (e.g., does a reply button on a centrally owned feed count as rejoinder in the sense A&H mean, and does the passage even hang on the technology or on who controls it?), not just state their positions at each other. Neither one should be the dummy. Also, where the two readings part ways on stuff that's genuinely contested among people who work on the Frankfurt School — like how deterministic the culture industry thesis really is — flag that as contested rather than having one character's framing presented as the settled scholarly view. American English please, and cap it at 600 words total.
