I'm taking an undergraduate course in normative ethics, and as a revision exercise I want to build a decision tree that sorts the main positions on whether killing is, in itself, morally worse than letting die. The tree should begin from the question every position has to answer — once intention, effort or cost to the agent, foreseeability, and outcome are all held fixed, does the bare difference between doing harm and allowing harm carry any moral weight on its own? — and branch downward from there. Please meet the following conditions:

- Six to eight leaves, each carrying a named position. These must include: strict equivalence (the doing/allowing difference never matters as such); the view that the distinction is intrinsically significant; the view that it matters only derivatively, because in practice it tracks differences in intention or in what the agent must sacrifice; the asymmetry between negative and positive duties; and the view that "letting die" is not even a unified category, since some lettings-die are structurally more like doings. Add any further position you think belongs as a leaf.
- Every branch node must be a concrete case the respondent reacts to — matched pairs differing only in doing versus allowing, withdrawing aid one has already begun providing, redirecting a harm one has oneself set in motion — never a question that names the theories being sorted.
- Construct at least two of the cases yourself rather than relying on the standard bathtub or trolley examples, and design them to split positions that the standard cases lump together.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot actually separate, and note any combination of answers that no position occupies, with one sentence diagnosing whether that region is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Begin with the tree itself, with no preface or commentary.
