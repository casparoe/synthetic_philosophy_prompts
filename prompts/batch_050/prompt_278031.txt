I have been trying to get my views on determinism, chaos, and prediction into a coherent shape, and I would like an audit of whether the following package hangs together. Please answer in British English.

My commitments are these:

1. The weather is, at the level relevant to forecasting, a deterministic classical system: given the exact state of the atmosphere at one time, the laws of fluid dynamics fix its state at every later time.

2. There is nevertheless a hard predictability horizon for weather, on the order of a fortnight, beyond which no forecast, however well-resourced, can do better than climatological averages. This follows from sensitive dependence on initial conditions: any measurement error, however small, grows exponentially until it swamps the signal.

3. A Laplacean demon — an intellect knowing the exact state of the atmosphere and able to compute without limit — could predict the weather a year, or a century, in advance. The fortnight horizon is a practical barrier, not a principled one.

4. The probabilities in an ensemble forecast, such as a 70 per cent chance of rain tomorrow, are objective features of the physical situation rather than mere measures of ignorance. Two forecasters with different information may legitimately assign different numbers, but at most one of them is right about an objective matter of fact.

5. Quantum indeterminacy can be ignored throughout the above: decoherence suppresses quantum effects at macroscopic scales, so the atmosphere behaves, for forecasting purposes, as a purely classical system.

Stated like this, I suspect that at least one pair of these commitments is in genuine conflict, and that at least one pair that looks conflicting is actually consistent. Please identify the tensions, ordered from most to least serious, and for each one state precisely which commitment would have to be abandoned or revised, and what that revision would cost the rest of the package. Where an apparent tension dissolves on inspection, show why it dissolves. I have no settled view about which commitment I would give up, so please do not assume one on my behalf.
