So me and my friend stayed up till like 3am arguing about Hofstadter's strange loop idea of the self -- the claim that the "I" is basically a self-referential symbol the brain builds, a pattern that loops back and ends up seeming like the cause of everything. He's totally sold, I think it's mostly a nice metaphor. We ended up with four objections on a napkin and couldnt agree on which ones actually land:

1. Even if there is a self-symbol, it's just downstream of the neurons doing the real causal work -- the loop doesn't cause anything, so calling it "the self" is like calling the speedometer the engine.
2. The Gödel analogy is doing all the heavy lifting but it's loose: incompleteness needs a formal system with precise encoding rules, and a brain's self-model is nothing like that, so the "strange loop" is metaphor not mechanism.
3. It explains (maybe) why there's a self-model, but says nothing about why any of it feels like something from the inside -- the hard problem just reappears one level up.
4. It's circular in a bad way: the loop only counts as a strange loop from a descriptive level the brain has to already be at, so the account smuggles in the self it's supposed to explain.

Rate each one out of 10 for how much damage it does to Hofstadter's actual position (not to some strawman of it), rank them worst to weakest, and tell me which one he'd have the best comeback for and which one is genuinely the killer.
