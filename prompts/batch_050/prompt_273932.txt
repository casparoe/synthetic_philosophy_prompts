Practitioners who work with large computational systems -- AI engineers, interpretability researchers, even chess programmers -- routinely explain and intervene at what we might call the symbol level. They say the model refused the request because it had learned to treat it as harmful; they say the chess engine sacrificed material because it judged the compensation adequate; they "steer" a language model by editing its prompt or adding a direction to its activation space, and they expect the behavior to change as a result. At the same time, everyone in the room will officially agree that the only causation genuinely at work is at the level of floating-point arithmetic, transistor switching, or -- in the biological case -- neural firing, and many will explicitly endorse the causal closure of the physical and look askance at talk of "downward causation." The tension is sharpened by a well-known thought experiment associated with Douglas Hofstadter: imagine a vast swarm of tiny, nearly frictionless marbles whose collisions knock around a smaller number of large, heavy balls, such that the large balls' trajectories can be compactly described in terms of aims and intentions ("it is trying to reach the corner"). The small marbles do all the actual pushing; yet the intentional description of the large balls seems to do real explanatory and predictive work, and a practitioner who could only manipulate the marbles would be helpless without it.

What does this working practice presuppose about the causal status of symbols? Please reconstruct the implicit metaphysics: does the practice strictly require that symbol-level states -- beliefs, goals, representations, "concepts" located in an activation space -- be causally efficacious in their own right, in a way that competes with or supplements microphysical causation? Or does it require only the weaker claim that symbol-level descriptions are indispensable for prediction and intervention, leaving the marbles in sole causal charge? Distinguish carefully between what the practice strictly presupposes and what it merely fits comfortably with: which parts of it could an eliminativist or a thoroughgoing epiphenomenalist about symbols retain without embarrassment, and which parts would they have to disown as a convenient fiction? Then say where the practice is genuinely underdetermined -- where several rival accounts (strong downward causation, a purely epistemic reading of levels, or a view on which the symbol level is real but causally excluded by the physical level, along the lines of Kim's exclusion worry) would each license the same day-to-day methodology. Finally, tell me what would concretely have to change in these practices -- prompt engineering, interpretability research, debugging -- if we settled on each of the rival views in turn. If nothing would change under some of them, explain why that is evidence about what the practice really presupposes rather than a defect of the views.
