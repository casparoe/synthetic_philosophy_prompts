A while back I read a piece in an online philosophy magazine about the Dennett–Caruso debate on free will and punishment, and its summary, as I remeber it, went roughly like this:

"Dennett and Caruso agree on the basic picture: there's no libertarian free will, and nobody could have done otherwise in the sense that would matter. Their disagreement is over what follows for punishment. Dennett thinks we can keep the everyday idea that criminals deserve their sentences — we just have to understand desert in forward-looking terms: punishing the guilty deters and incapacitates, and that's all desert ever needed to be. Caruso goes further and says we should scrap retributive justice altogether and model the criminal justice system on medical quarantine: offenders may be detained only for as long as they're dangerous, the way we'd detain someone carrying a contagious disease, with no blame involved. The consequence, which Caruso accepts, is that a serial killer and the unwitting carrier of a deadly virus are in morally the same boat — neither deserves what happens to them — and that the killer must be released the moment he's judged no longer dangerous, whatever the victims' families think. Dennett's reply, in effect, is that this shows Caruso doesn't take wrongdoing seriously: if a murderer is morally on a par with a flu carrier, then nothing anyone does is really wrong."

This has been nagging at me, because the bit about the killer and the virus carrier reads like a reductio dressed up as reporting — but I genuinely don't know, maybe Caruso does bite that bullet? Could you audit the summary for me: which bits a careful defender of each side would accept, which parts are distortions or conflations of things the actual debate keeps apart, and whether Dennett's real objections are the ones the article puts in his mouth. I'm inclined to trust the magazine since it clearly knows the literature, so if I'm being paranoid about the virus-carrier line, tell me that too. And if the whole question is muddled — say, if auditing a half-remembered paraphrase of a debate I haven't actually read is a daft thing to ask for — then just say so plainly rather than politely playing along.
