I stumbled into Bertrand's paradox in a very unglamorous way: at work I had to put priors on some parameters for a risk model, my colleague said just go uniform, and then we realised uniform over the ratio gives a totally different answer to uniform over the log of the ratio. So I started reading the philosophy around the principle of indifference and honestly it looks completely stuck. One camp seems to think the Bertrand-style cases show the principle is just incoherent and you should never touch it; another camp (Jaynes and his lot, I think?) reckons the paradoxes dissolve once you take the symmetries of the problem seriously; and a third lot seem to think there was never a real problem because any actual setup has a definite randomisation procedure that settles it. Everyone's had the same examples for over a century and nobody budges. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal — different senses of 'random' or 'equally possible' flying past each other? A genuine mathematical question nobody's pinned down properly? Or competing starting intuitions about what ignorance even is that neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would progress look like — a theorem, a convention, or just agreeing to drop the principle and muddle on?
