I play guitar in a cover band, and between sets we keep circling the same argument: when is something the same song, and when is it a different one? One of us says a song is an abstract pattern that would exist even if no one ever played it. Another says a song is nothing over and above people actually playing it, so our Saturday version is a new song each time. A third insists that for music built in a studio, the song simply is the master recording, and everything a band does onstage is at best a copy of it. None of us has studied philosophy, but I've been turning this over on my own for months, and what I want is not a verdict — it's a map of the possible answers.

Please build me a decision tree for the question "what is a musical work, and how does it relate to its performances and recordings?" Each branch should be a concrete question a non-philosopher could answer without knowing any terminology — things like: could two different recordings be recordings of the very same performance? Could the song have existed before anyone wrote it down or played it? If I change the instrumentation, the tempo, or every improvised solo, at what point has the song itself changed rather than just the rendition? Let the combinations of answers generate the leaves, rather than starting from a list of named theories. Each leaf should be a position — give its standard name if it has one, or mark it as unnamed — plus one sentence on its most awkward consequence.

The tree must route at least these cases to definite leaves: a note-for-note cover; a jazz standard played every night with different solos; a studio track assembled from edited takes that is never performed live; a remaster, and separately a remix, of a finished album; a Baroque piece played on synthesizer; and a new track built around a sampled loop of an older recording.

Where a combination of answers yields a position nobody seems to hold, say so and diagnose whether it's incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. And if two leaves can't actually be separated by any question on the tree, flag that pair rather than forcing a distinction.
