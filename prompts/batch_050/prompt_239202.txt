So I'm a novelist, mostly literary realism, which means I'm definately out of my lane here: I've been asked to contribute to a charity anthology of "in-universe documents" from video games, and my assignment is a big open-world RPG. Write my piece for me: an op-ed for the game's in-world newspaper, max 450 words, authored by a minor NPC — say, a shopkeeper in a town players keep setting on fire for fun — calmly arguing that players should treat NPCs better. The jokes have to come from him deploying real moral-philosophy moves completely deadpan, in his flat polite shopkeeper voice: whether his screaming when torched is evidence of suffering or just an audio file that triggers on a damage state; whether respawning every morning at 6am means his deaths don't really count; the awkward point that he wouldn't exist at all if he hadn't been coded as cannon fodder, so maybe he's in no position to complain; and the studio's official statement that he is merely a collection of polygons and textures. Let one of the studio's talking points accidentally concede something it shouldn't. End with him reluctantly falling back on the indirect argument — that the real question isn't what players do to him but what doing it turns them into — and make at least one of his earlier arguments genuinely decent, so the reader finishes feeling a little guilty instead of just amused.
