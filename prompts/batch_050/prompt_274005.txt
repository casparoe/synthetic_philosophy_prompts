I work on the decision architecture of autonomous systems, so the vocabulary I actually think in is objective functions, hard constraints, regularization terms, deliberation budgets, and the distinction between what a policy values and what it computes on the way to acting. Lately I've been reading Bernard Williams's critique of consequentialism -- the integrity objection, with the Jim and Pedro case and the case of George the unemployed chemist offered a job in chemical weapons research -- and his companion thought that a man who saves his drowning wife, but only after first checking that impartial morality permits him to prefer her, has had one thought too many. I'd like you to restate these two objections as precisely as you can in my engineering vocabulary. What exactly is integrity, in terms of objective functions and constraints? Is it a hard constraint on certain act-types, a large negative term for outcomes brought about in a certain way, a commitment to long-horizon plans that resists re-evaluation at each timestep, or something else? And what is the one thought too many objection, in terms of deliberation? Is it a point about compute costs and decision latency, about which features should enter the deliberation at all, or about the difference between a policy that values the wife and a policy that outputs wife-saving after consulting a permission module?

Then give me the residue report, which is honestly the part I care about more. Which elements of Williams's objections translate cleanly, which survive only under distortion, and which have no expression in the vocabulary of objective functions and deliberation at all? I have two specific worries I'd like addressed head-on. First, the objection in the drowning case seems to be not that the permission check produces the wrong answer -- it permits the rescue -- but that running the check at all is the defect, and I'm unsure whether that translates as a deliberation-cost term without losing what Williams meant, since a deliberation cost would still license running the check when the stakes are high enough. Second, a system that simply saves the wife without consulting a permission module does so because of its architecture, not because of anything like character or a first-personal relationship to its projects, and I suspect Williams's objection depends on the agent being able to see its reasons as its own in a way my vocabulary has no slot for. Tell me whether that suspicion is right.

Where the residue is real, say whether it indicts my framework or Williams's -- that is, whether the untranslatable remainder is something agent design genuinely cannot represent and plausibly should, or whether the translation failure shows the objection was trading on something less articulate than it appeared. And if the whole translation project rests on a confusion -- for instance, if I've mischaracterized what the integrity objection actually claims, or if the objections are simply inapplicable to agents without a particular kind of psychology, so that the residue is the whole objection -- say so plainly rather than producing a polished but hollow mapping.
