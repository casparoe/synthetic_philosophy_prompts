I am a high school student preparing a piece for my philosophy club's session on population ethics, and I would like some satirical material. Write a mock legal-defense letter of no more than 350 words, addressed to a judge and signed by an invented law firm, responding to a class-action suit brought by the residents of a valley that the firm's client company flooded seventy years ago through negligent dam construction. The firm's entire defense should rest on the nonidentity problem: because the flood and the resulting displacement changed which couples met and which children were conceived, none of the present-day plaintiffs would have existed had the dam been properly maintained, and since their lives are admittedly worth living, no plaintiff has been harmed or wronged. The humor should come from the letter being philosophically consistent and morally appalling at the same time: it should pre-empt, with straight-faced rigor, at least one serious objection (for instance, that the company wronged people without harming them, or that the comparison with a responsible policy should hold the population fixed), and it should follow its own logic to one further outrageous conclusion, such as billing the plaintiffs a finder's fee for arranging their existence. Keep the tone deadpan and formal throughout, with no winking asides, and make the underlying reasoning accurate enough that a reader familiar with the nonidentity problem finds the parody fair rather than a distortion of the argument it satirizes.
