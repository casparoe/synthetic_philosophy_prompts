I am a novelist at work on a book in which my characters agonize over choices that could genuinely go either way, and in my research I have been reading about the debate between presentism and eternalism so as not to build the story on bad metaphysics. I have run into a serious problem. Eternalism, as I understand it, holds that past, present, and future events are all equally real, related to one another tenselessly much as objects are related across space. But if the future already exists in the block universe, then the outcomes of my characters' decisions are already sitting there, fixed, and their deliberation is a charade — and any narrator who presents their choices as open is peddling an illusion. So it seems I must either commit the novel to presentism or accept that its drama rests on a lie. Which should I do? And if eternalism is true, how do I keep readers invested in decisions whose results are, on that view, settled before the deciding even begins?
