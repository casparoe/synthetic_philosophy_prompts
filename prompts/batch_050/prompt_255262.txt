Two friends of mine fell out over a thought experiment at the pub last weekend and it's still going over whatsapp. The setup: a billionaire offers you ten million pounds if, at midnight tonight, you genuinely intend to drink a glass of stuff tomorrow at noon that will make you miserably ill for a day but causes no lasting harm. The money is wired the moment his brain-scan contraption confirms the midnight intention. Whether you actually drink the stuff at noon is completely irrelevant -- you could pour it down the sink and keep the money.

Callum says it's the easiest money ever made: just decide at 11:59 that you're drinking it, bank the ten million, then obviously don't drink it. Priya says he can't do that. If at midnight he already knows he won't drink it at noon -- and he does know, because at noon there's no reason left to drink it and every reason not to -- then whatever he forms at midnight isn't an intention, it's just a thought he's having. You can't intend to do what you're certain you won't do.

Callum's comeback: people intend things they won't do constantly. Gym resolutions, diets, quitting smoking. Priya said no, those people don't know at the moment of intending that they'll fail -- they're hoping they won't. The uncertainty is what makes it an intention. Here there's no uncertainty, you know in advance you'll bin the toxin.

So Callum escalated: fine, remove the uncertainty. Sign a penalty clause, pay a friend fifty grand if you don't drink it, whatever -- now you know you'll drink it, so you can intend it. Priya said that's not solving the puzzle, that's dissolving it. Once there's a penalty you have a perfectly good reason to drink at noon, so intending is easy and boring. The whole point of the original offer was intending without any noon-reason, and he's just changed the offer.

Then Callum tried something that honestly I couldn't follow all the way: he said promises are the mirror image. When you keep a promise the reason is entirely in the past -- the promise got made -- and nobody thinks that's irrational. The toxin case just puts the reason in the future instead. If backward-looking reasons are fine, forward-looking ones should be fine too, and the puzzle shows we've arbitrarily decided reasons can only flow one direction in time. Priya said the difference is that the reason to keep a promise is still there at the moment of keeping, whereas at noon tomorrow there is literally nothing.

Last thing: Callum asked what if a hypnotist could just install the intention in him at midnight. Money secured, no drinking needed. Priya said that proves her side -- if the intention has to be installed rather than rationally formed, the offer was never really available to a rational agent, and "winning" via hypnosis is a technicality, like winning a staring contest with eyedrops.

They're both mad at me for not picking a side. So: go through it and tell me who's right at each step, who's more right overall, and whether there's something they're both missing.
