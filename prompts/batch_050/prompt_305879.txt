So I'm in this moral philosophy reading group and we've spent the last three sessions on standing to blame -- the question of when someone loses the right or entitlement to call someone else out. What got me hooked was honestly my uncle, who gave my cousin a long lecture at Thanksgiving about her unpaid parking tickets when everyone at that table knows he spent a decade cheating on his taxes. There's a strong intuition he shouldn't be the one saying it. But when I tried to explain to a friend *why*, everything I came up with either proved too much (if hypocrisy kills standing, nobody can ever blame anyone, since we're all guilty of something) or too little (hypocrisy only matters when it's the exact same offense in the exact same circumstances, which seems arbitrary and ad hoc).

Before I read any more of the literature I want to know what I should actually be demanding of an account here. So: write the desiderata -- what any adequate theory of standing to blame has to deliver. Which cases it must get right (the straightforward hypocrite, sure, but also the person who was complicit in the wrongdoing without doing it themselves, the reformed offender who now blames people for exactly what they used to do, the state punishing crimes it routinely commits, blaming the dead, the parent scolding a kid for a habit the parent modeled for fifteen years). Which distinctions it must respect (losing standing vs. the blame simply being false; blaming someone vs. pointing out that what they did was wrong; private vs. public blame). For each desideratum, give the case or intuition that motivates it, and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- I have a hunch that "hypocrisy destroys standing" and "wrongdoers can earn standing back through repentance" are in real tension, but I could be wrong about that. Then, briefly, score the standard contender views against the list (the hypocrisy-is-disrespecting-equality view, the fairness-or-free-riding view, the you-don't-actually-care-about-the-victim view), but keep that part short, since the criteria are what I really care about.

Format: exactly ten numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion -- items 1 through 8 the desiderata each with their motivating case, item 9 the pairs of desiderata you think are jointly unsatisfiable (or your case that the list is coherent after all), item 10 the quick scoring of the three views against the list.
