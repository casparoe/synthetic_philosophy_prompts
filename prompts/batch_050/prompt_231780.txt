I need a proposal of roughly two pages for a one-semester graduate term paper (about 8,000 words) on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. My starting thought is that the usual debate is miscast: both the distinctive-feeling account and the attitudinal account of pleasure assume that pleasure is a kind of mental state, which is exactly what makes the heterogeneity objection look fatal, since a sexual pleasure, the pleasure of solving a proof, and the pleasure of a warm bath seem to share neither a common phenomenology nor a common object of desire. I suspect pleasure is better understood as an aspect or dimension of experiences rather than a separable kind of experience — on the model of intensity or duration, which themselves vary enormously across the experiences that have them — and that this reframing dissolves the objection while generating its own commitments that can be checked. Turn this into a proposal with: a precise statement of the question, framed so that it has a determinate answer; an explanation of why the two standard families of views leave it open rather than merely disagreeing about it; a description of the method, including which cases would serve as the decisive tests (my candidates are pleasures of relief, pleasures in absorbed activity where nothing felt is attended to, and pleasures noticed only in retrospect) and what the aspect-view predicts about each; what a successful result would look like and what specific finding would count as the aspect-view failing; and a candid assessment of scope, saying which nearby issues — the normative significance of pleasure, animal pleasure, the measurement of welfare — should be explicitly bracketed so the paper stays feasible in a semester. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will supply the references myself.
