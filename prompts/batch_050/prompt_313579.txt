I've been trying to figure out what I actually believe about hurting one person to prevent worse harms, and my gut verdicts don't seem to add up to anything. Here they are:

- Diverting a runaway trolley so it kills one worker instead of five: fine.
- Pushing a big guy off a bridge to stop the trolley: not fine.
- Killing one innocent person myself when it's the only way to stop three *other* people from each committing a murder (say a sheriff framing and hanging an innocent man to stop a riot where three people will definately be killed): not fine, even though fewer people die than in the trolley case and the deaths are murders, which you'd think is worse.
- But killing one innocent to stop *myself* from murdering five people later -- like I know I'll be drugged tonight and set loose, with no way to warn anyone or tie myself up -- I keep feeling like that might actually be OK. And I can't articulate why my own future murders should be cheaper to prevent than strangers' murders happening right now.
- Stealing a car to rush a bleeding stranger to the hospital: fine.
- Lying to the murderer at the door: fine, obviously.

So what principle, if any, captures these? The best I've managed is something like "you mayn't seriously harm one innocent even to prevent more of the same harm, unless the future harms would be yours," but that makes my own future wrongdoing weirdly more important than identical wrongdoing by other people, which seems backwards, or at least suspicious. Does one of my verdicts have to go, and if so which? And what does the resulting principle say about the case I genuinely can't settle: killing one innocent to prevent *exactly one* murder by someone else -- the net murder count is the same either way, so does it come down to who pulls the trigger?
