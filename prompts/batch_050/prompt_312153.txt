The dispute was about what, if anything, actually holds the left/right distinction together. Marta's view: it's fundamentally about equality -- the left sees existing hierarchies as contingent and unjustified until proven otherwise, the right sees at least some of them as natural, necessary, or even good. Tomas's view: it's fundamentally about where human misery comes from -- the left's explanations bottom out in external structures (institutions, incentives, systems), the right's in something internal (character, choice, human nature).

Then the rounds. (1) Tomas said the equality criterion can't handle two obvious cases: libertarians, who want to flatten the state's hierarchy completely but are clearly right-wing, and old communist party cadres, who ran rigid hierarchies but were clearly left. (2) Marta replied that libertarians happily accept the inequalities markets generate, and party hierarchy was only ever instrumental to an egalitarian end -- the criterion is about which inequalities are justified in principle, not about the means used to get there. (3) Marta then attacked Tomas's criterion: plenty of right-wingers blame structures (the conservative who says the welfare bureaucracy traps people in dependency) and plenty of leftists blame character (the CEO's greed), so structure-vs-character doesn't sort anyone either. (4) Tomas said those are surface-level -- ask where the explanation *stops*: the leftist's greedy CEO is just responding to incentives, but the conservative's welfare trap ultimately rests on a fixed human nature responding to those incentives, and that fixed point is what makes it a right-wing explanation. Marta called that move unfalsifiable -- any explanation can be redescribed as "really" bottoming out wherever you want.

So: who's right at each of the four moves, who is more right overall, and is there something both of them are missing (I've wondered whether they've both just assumed the distinction tracks one thing at all)? Please be decisive -- don't give me a "both make good points" mush.
