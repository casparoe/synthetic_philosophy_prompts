So I'm in a grad epistemology seminar and we've spent the last two weeks on the Meno problem -- why is knowledge worth more than mere true belief, given that if you just want to get to Larissa, a true belief about the road gets you there just as well? For the final session everyone has to bring one "solution" to the value problem and defend it, and I got ambitious and decided to bring something nobody else will present. Here's what I've already got on my list (which is basically everything the seminar covered, so the obvious stuff is taken):

1. Plato's own answer -- knowledge is true belief "tied down" with an account, so it's stable and won't run off like one of Daedalus's statues; true belief is flighty and unreliable in the long run.
2. The virtue/credit answer (Sosa, Greco, that whole camp) -- knowledge is a kind of achievement that's creditable to the agent's competence, whereas a merely true belief can be lucky, and achievements are valuable in a way lucky successes aren't.
3. The practical answer -- knowledge, not mere true belief, is what properly licenses action and assertion, so its extra value is normative rather than epistemic.
4. The anti-luck/safety answer -- knowledge couldn't easily have been false, and there's something disvaluable about beliefs that are true only by fluke, even if they'd guide you fine in the actual case.

What I want from you: a fifth answer that is genuinely different from these four -- not a rewording, not a mashup of two of them, and not just "knowledge is intrinsically valuable" with no mechanism given (my professor shot that down in week one as refusing the question). Crucially, tell me whether your answer survives the swamping problem, i.e. the worry that once a belief is true, whatever extra property you add doesn't add any value, the way a reliably-made espresso isn't a better espresso than an identical lucky one. If it doesn't survive, say so honestly and tell me whether that's fatal. Bonus points if you can connect it to something outside epistemology, since the prof loves that.
