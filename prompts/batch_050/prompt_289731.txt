His version of Szasz went something like this: "Szasz basically proved that mental illness doesn't exist. There's no blood test or brain scan that diagnoses depression or schizophrenia the way you can diagnose diabetes or a tumor, so calling these things illnesses is just a category error dressed up as medicine. According to him, what people call mental illness is really just ordinary unhappiness and weird behavior that society doesn't like, psychiatrists are frauds who lock up inconvenient people, and that means psychiatry as a field has no legitimate practice at all -- anyone who feels bad should just deal with their problems themselves."

Now, I've only ever read a magazine piece about The Myth of Mental Illness plus some stuff online, so I can't say exactly where this goes wrong, but I'm pretty sure it's not quite right. I thought Szasz's point was more subtle -- something about "illness" being literal for the body but only metaphorical for the mind, so that a diseased brain would be a genuine illness but then it'd be neurology's business, not psychiatry's. And I have a vague memory that he wasn't opposed to ALL psychotherapy, just the coercive kind (involuntary commitment, court-ordered treatment), and was fine with consenting adults paying a therapist to talk. But maybe I'm the one misremembering and my friend's harsher version is closer.

Also, does his sweeping conclusion -- psychiatry has no legitimate practice whatsoever -- actually follow from the accurate parts, or only from the exaggerated ones? I mostly want to be able to go back to this conversation with a clearer sense of where the real Szasz ends and my friend's riff begins.
