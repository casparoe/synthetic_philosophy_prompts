So I cover science and ideas for a magazine and I keep bumping into a pattern in arguments that I don't have a name for, and I'm wondering if philosophers have already carved this out somewhere. Here are a couple of instances of what I mean.

Say I learn that the only reason I care about a certain cause -- let's say I got really into ocean conservation -- is that someone I loved dragged me to a documentary years ago, and any other path through life would have left me indifferent to it. Nothing about the oceans has changed. The reefs are still bleaching, the arguments for caring are exactly as strong as they were the day before I learned this about myself. And yet the discovery does something to my conviction that discovering, say, that my evidence was fabricated would do -- but also something clearly different from that. My evidence wasn't fabricated. The reefs really are bleaching.

Second instance: a colleague finds out she only got interested in AI risk because her grad school funding came from an institute that cares about it, and she might have drifted into housing policy otherwise. Again, no specific argument she gives is touched. But she reports feeling the ground shift anyway.

So the pattern is: a fact about the causal history of my caring seems to speak against the caring, without being a fact about the thing cared about at all. It's not like the classic cases I've seen where someone offers you a million dollars to believe something -- that's obviously not evidence. And it's not straightforward evidence either, the way finding out my source lied would be. It seems to sit in some third zone, and people in arguments exploit this ambiguity all the time ("you only think that because of how you were raised" -- which lands as an objection even though it strictly isn't one, or is it?).

Does this have a name? Is there a literature that separates this from the "right vs wrong kind of reasons" stuff and from plain old undermining evidence, and says which it really is? If there isn't a clean established concept, feel free to propose a name and a definition, but I mainly want the carving -- what exactly this phenomenon is, where it shades into debunking arguments on one side and into the wrong-kind-of-reasons cases on the other, and whether the "you only think that because..." move is ever legitmate. Don't just hand me a label. Also, please flag how confident you are in the overall picture you give, and single out the one point in your answer you're least sure of.
