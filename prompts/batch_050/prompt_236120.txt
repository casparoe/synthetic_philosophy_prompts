Design a biological signaling system in which a signal conveying private information is reliably honest even though no one who actually sends it pays any net cost — producing the signal is free, or even mildly beneficial, for every equilibrium signaler. Constraints: the signal must advertise an unobservable quality the receiver genuinely cares about (fighting ability, parasite load, or the like); encounters are one-shot, so no reputation, no repeated play, and no third-party enforcement of honesty; and the honesty may not rest on aligned interests — sender and receiver must have a real conflict, such that bluffing would pay if it worked. After describing the scenario, run the incentive check explicitly: why a low-quality individual does not imitate the signal, why a high-quality individual does not stay silent, and which single feature of your design is carrying the honesty. Then say whether honesty survives if that feature is removed, and close by judging what the case shows about the handicap principle — whether it refutes the principle as popularly stated (signals are honest because signalers waste resources), refutes the formal condition from signaling-game theory, or refutes neither, and where exactly the popular statement goes wrong if it does. Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
