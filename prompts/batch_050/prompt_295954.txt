Bit of context first: our street has a shared green at the end with some apple trees and a veg patch that's meant to be for everyone. Last summer it all kicked off because a couple of households picked basically all the apples early and the patch got overrun with weeds because everyone assumed someone else was doing it. At next month's residents meeting I'm going to stand up and argue that we should NOT split it into private plots (which the chair, Geoff, wants) and we shouldn't just leave it as a free-for-all either. My view, which I've sort of worked out myself from thinking about it, is that the real problem isn't selfishness, it's that nobody knows what counts as their fair share and nobody can see who else is contributing. So the fix is visible contributions -- a rota on the shed door, a shared book where you log what you took -- rather than fines or fences. I reckon small groups of neighbours can actually manage a shared thing fine if contributions are out in the open.

Audience is about 30 neighbours: Geoff who wants to carve it up into private plots, a retired accountant called Margaret who thinks anything voluntary is doomed on principle, a couple of younger renters who hate the idea of being monitored, and a load of people who just want it sorted.

Give me a prep sheet for the questions: the objections I'll actually get from this lot, roughly in the order they'll come, phrased the way these people would actually put it (not philosophy-speak), each with a short reply I can give off the cuff and a note on where my reply is weakest. Include the question I'm secretly hoping nobody asks, which I suspect is about what happens when someone just ignores the rota.
