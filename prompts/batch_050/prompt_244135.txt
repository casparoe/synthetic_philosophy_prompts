I work as an ER doctor, and a few years back I helped write my hospital's mass-casualty triage rules -- stuff like who gets the single free surgeon first when two people both need them, based on prognosis and resources. I still think the rules are basically right.

Last month a highway pileup came in and I was running triage. Two kids, one surgeon available, and my own protocol said kid B goes first -- much better chance of making it, kid A was a longer shot. Here's the thing: kid A was my daughter. She'd been in the car with a friend's family.

I followed the protocol. It worked out, sort of -- my daughter pulled through after a second surgeon freed up, though it was close and she's got a long recovery. Both kids lived. So nobody died, and yet I cannot stop thinking about it.

My wife says any decent parent would have torn up the clipboard and sent their own kid in first, and the fact that I didn't is something she doesn't know how to feel about. A colleague I trust says I did the only defensible thing and that a system where every doctor favors their own family collapses instantly.

But here's the part I can't articulate to either of them. In the moment, what I felt wasn't "the protocol says so." What I felt was: that's my kid. The protocol was something I had to use to push that feeling down. And now I keep wondering -- if I'd needed a *reason* beyond "she's my daughter" to save her, would that reason even be doing the work? Like, imagine I'd justified sending her first by finding some loophole in my own rules. That feels worse, more dishonest, than just... loving my kid. But the pure protocol version of me also feels like a robot wearing my face.

So my question: was there actually anything to decide here? Should a good parent just act on "that's my kid" without needing it to be ratified by some impersonal standard? Or does the fact that I work in a system strangers rely on mean my reasons have to be checkable by those strangers? And seperately -- my wife seems to think the *hesitation itself* says something damning about me, like the right motivation should've been the only one in the room. Is she right about that?
