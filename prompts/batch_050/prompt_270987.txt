I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science working on confirmation theory, and for a graduate seminar on formal epistemology in literature I want to read Poe's The Purloined Letter through the lens of Hempel's ravens paradox. Here's the angle I want explored: the Prefect's search of the Minister's hotel is methodologically impeccable — every hiding place a clever concealer would use is probed, microscopically, and comes up empty. Those negative results are, structurally, observations of non-black non-ravens: they confirm "the letter is not in any place of type X" and so, by contraposition, incrementally confirm the hypothesis that it's somewhere else entirely — but they say nothing for or against the hypothesis Dupin turns out to be right about, that it's in plain sight. Read the story as a dramatization of the paradox's real lesson: that whether an observation confirms a hypothesis depends on the hypothesis space and on how the observation was selected. Does Poe's text actually support that reading — the Prefect's methods are described as perfectly adapted to a certain conception of concealment — or am I imposing a Bayesian framework on what is really a story about something confirmation theory doesn't capture at all, namely Dupin's method of identifying with his opponent's reasoning? Tell me where the reading illuminates specific scenes, where it strains, and what textual or philosophical consideration would lead you to a different verdict.
