So I've gotten into these dumb arguments online about infinity (my first mistake, I know) and I keep seeing the same move over and over and I want to know if there's actually a name for it.

Here's the pattern. Someone says two infinite collections are the same size because you can line their members up one-to-one, like the even numbers with all the whole numbers. Someone else says that's insane, the evens are obviously half of the whole numbers, half of something can't be the same size as the thing. First person says but look, the pairing works, nothing is left over. Second person says the pairing doesn't prove anything about size, size is about parts and wholes. And they go in circles for like 400 comments. Here's the thing though — neither side is actually making a mistake. Each one is perfectly consistent once you grant them their starting assumption about what "same number of" means. The real disagreement is about which rule you get to keep: the pairing rule, or the part-is-smaller-than-the-whole rule, because apparently you can't have both once infinity enters the picture. But neither of them ever says that out loud, they both just keep insisting the other guy is bad at math.

I watched basically the exact same fight happen over Hilbert's hotel, and again over whether an infinite past would mean the number of past days "equals" the number of past years times 365 or whatever.

So: is there an established concept for this specific kind of stalemate, where a dispute secretly reduces to which of two incompatible-but-individually-reasonable principles you surrender, and both sides argue as if their principle is just what the word means? I don't think it's merely "talking past each other," because both sides sincerely believe the other side made an error somewhere. And I don't think it's Galileo's paradox, since that's the observation that both rules exist at all, not the argument pattern where people dig in on different ones. If there's no name for it, what would you call it, and how would you actually tell whether some debate is this kind of stalemate versus a genuine disagreement that evidence could settle?
