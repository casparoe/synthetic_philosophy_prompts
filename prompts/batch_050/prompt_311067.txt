So my reading group just finished Gödel, Escher, Bach and we started I Am a Strange Loop, and before our next session the organizer wants everyone to submit a one-page "position statement" on self-reference and levels so we can attack each other's views. I've written mine as a list of commitments and I want to stress-test it before I hand it in, because I have a vague feeling two of these don't actually go together but I can't locate the problem. Here's what I hold:

(a) The Hofstadter picture of the self: the "I" is a self-referential, symbol-level pattern in the brain, a tangled hierarchy where the symbol level and the neural level feed into each other, and that loop is not an add-on to consciousness but the whole thing.
(b) Every tangled hierarchy has an inviolate level standing outside the loop that the loop cannot touch. In Escher's Drawing Hands the two hands can each draw the other, but neither can reach off the page and smudge the artist. Same structure everywhere: tangle below, inviolate level outside.
(c) The Tarski lesson generalizes: semantic paradoxes get dissolved by splitting the tangle into object language and metalanguage, so in principle any tangled hierarchy can be cleanly untangled by separating levels. Self-reference is only ever a temporary bookkeeping problem.
(d) Causal closure of the physical: the neurons do all the real causal work. The symbol level is an indispensable redescription, not a second causal force pushing on the neurons.
(e) Genuine self-causation: when I say "I decided to get up," the I is really doing the deciding. The self-model causes my behavior, and that's not just a manner of speaking or a shorthand for neural goings-on.
(f) Substrate-independence: what matters is the abstract pattern, not the material. A strange loop of the right shape running on silicon would be just as conscious as one running on neurons.

What I want from you: go through these pairwise and tell me which ones genuinely can't all be held together, which pairs only look like they clash but actually dissolve once you're careful (I suspect the plasticity worry about (a) and (b) -- symbols changing neurons via learning -- is one of these, but convince me), and rank the real tensions by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad. For each genuine tension, tell me the cheapest revision and what it costs. And if there's one commitment whose abandonment would clean up the most of the set, name it -- I'm not attached to any particular one of these, I just want the final package to cohere. One thing I'd especially like checked: whether (c) sits comfortably with (a), since the whole appeal of the strange-loop view seems to be that the loop can't be disentangled from inside, and I'm not sure my generalized-Tarski optimism leaves that standing.
