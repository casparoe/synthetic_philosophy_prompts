ok so I've been chewing on Kavka's toxin puzzle again (the one where an eccentric billionaire wires you a million dollars if, at midnight tonight, you sincerely intend to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow afternoon — the toxin just makes you horribly ill for a day, no lasting damage, and crucially the payout depends only on having the intention at midnight, not on actually drinking, so once the money lands you can pour the stuff down the drain). The standard line, which Kavka himself took, is that a rational person basically can't form the intention for the reward alone: you know in advance that come tomorrow you'll have no reason to actually drink, knowing that means you know you won't drink, and you can't sincerely intend to do what you know you won't do. I keep having arguments about this with a friend who thinks the puzzle is trivially solvable and I want to sharpen my objections before round two.

So: play my friend, but as a committed defender of the impossibility claim, not a strawman. You think a rational agent who fully understands the payoff structure cannot intend to drink just for the money. You'll concede edge cases where they exist — hiring someone to force-feed you, contracts with penalties, hypnotic suggestion, weird diachronic self-manipulation — but you hold that these all change the structure rather than solve the puzzle, since the puzzle is about whether the reward alone can generate the intention. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like an actual argument and not a lecture, and if I land a real point you can give ground on that specific point without abandoning your main claim. Push back hard when I'm sloppy.

My opening move: you're conflating intention with prediction. I intend to go running at 6am tomorrow while assigning maybe 40% odds that I actually get out of bed, and nobody says my intention is therefore fake or that I've failed to form it. Intentions coexist with doubt all the time — that's just what weakness of will is. So the fact that I can foresee I won't drink doesn't block me from intending to drink; it just sets me up to be akratic tomorrow, which is the most ordinary thing in the world. Why isn't that enough to collect the million?
