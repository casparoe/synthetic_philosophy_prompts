"Hume considers whether suicide violates our duty to God, to society, or to ourselves. Focus on the duty-to-God section: explain Hume's argument for why suicide is no crime against God, and then say whether Hume's overall discussion, if sound, would show that suicide is never morally wrong. (2-3 paragraphs.)"

Here's what one student turned in, and I need a second opinion on how to grade it:

"Hume's essay Of Suicide appeared in 1757 as one of his Five Dissertations and stirred controversy right away. In it, Hume argues that suicide cannot be a crime against God because every human action alters the course of nature anyway. If changing the course of nature encroached on divine providence, then diverting a stream or building a house would be just as guilty as killing oneself. God governs the world through general laws, and everything we do falls within the powers he gave us, so no action of ours can overstep them. Hume also relies on the idea that God has posted each of us at a station we are not allowed to quit, which shows that leaving life early deserts a divinely assigned post. So Hume concludes that since suicide transgresses none of the three duties, suicide is never morally wrong, and he even thinks it can sometimes be praiseworthy."
