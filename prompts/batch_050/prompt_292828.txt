my friend is really into the hiddenness arguement against God's existence, and his whole case runs on an analogy. a perfectly loving parent, he says, would never stay hidden from a child who wants to find them -- so if God were perfectly loving, nobody who honestly sought God would come up empty. but plenty of sincere seekers do come up empty (the "nonresistant nonbelievers"), so there's no perfectly loving God. meanwhile his roommate, who's religious, apparently runs the same picture the other way: good parents deliberately stay out of the child's sight sometimes -- they don't do your homework for you, they let you struggle with the puzzle, they give you space to become your own person -- so a hidden God is exactly what perfect love would look like.

audit the analogy for me. start with the correspondence table: what plays the role of the parent, the child, the hiding, "being available", the seeking, the finding. then where the mapping breaks. my own suspicion is that the killer disanalogy is that a kid hunting for her dad already believes he exists, whereas the hiddenness debate is about people who can't even get that far -- for an embodied parent, being present and being believed to exist are the same move, but for God they come apart, and it's not obvious which one is the analog of the parent being in the room. but tell me whether that's the right place to push or whether there are worse cracks (e.g., does "perfect love" even license predictions from human parenting, given that human parents are finite and fallible almost by definition?).

then seperate the disanalogies that actually bear on my friend's conclusion from the ones that are just texture (parents get tired, God doesn't -- who cares). the thing I most want to know: can literally the same analogy support both his conclusion and the roommate's, and if not, which of the two uses is smuggling in what? does the atheist conclusion survive once the breaks are priced in, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? and if the parent picture is salvageable after repairs, what does the fixed-up version actually show -- and if it isn't salvageable, what's a better source domain: a long-distance parent who never calls, a pen pal who stops writing back, something else entirely?
