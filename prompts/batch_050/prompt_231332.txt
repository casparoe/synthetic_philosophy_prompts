I'm a software engineer at a mid-sized robotics company, and for the past few months a colleague and I have been running an informal lunchtime reading group. We recently worked through Douglas Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment -- the two-level system in which swarms of tiny, fast-moving marbles collide with and set in motion much larger, slower balls that carry symbolic significance, raising the question of whether the symbol level genuinely does causal work or is merely a convenient redescription of the substrate. My colleague has a very settled habit of making verdicts about real systems, and I've kept a record. Here are his verdicts so far: a household thermostat -- no symbol-level causation, the degree markings are there for the user and the bimetallic strip does everything; a perfect tic-tac-toe player implemented as one enormous lookup table -- no, talk of the program "threatening a fork" is our gloss on a table; a person doing long division with pencil and paper -- yes, the digits inscribed on the page genuinely steer the later steps of the computation, and the paper is part of the machinery; DNA and the ribosome -- yes, the mapping from codon triplets to amino acids is arbitrary like a code table, and the cell reads base sequences the way we would read symbols; a trained neural network that plays chess, in which interpretability researchers have located a direction in activation space that tracks open files -- no, that "concept" is something we read off the weights afterward, and the matrix multiplications do all the work; an ant colony "deciding" between two nest sites -- no, the colony-level decision is a summary we impose, and the individual ants' rules do everything.

Here is the new case. Our company has been evaluating a recurrent control network for a chemical plant: at each timestep the network's entire internal state -- a high-dimensional vector of continuous values, no discrete tokens, no codebook, nothing human-readable -- is fed back as part of its own next input, alongside the sensor readings. The plant's behavior depends on earlier states only through this looped-back vector; our interpretability tooling can find directions in it that track things like rising tank pressure, and nudging those directions reliably changes the control policy. My colleague hasn't seen this system yet. Predict his verdict on whether the symbol level does real causal work here, and tell me which earlier verdict is the closest analog.

More importantly, I want to know what rule he's implicitly running. Give me at least two candidate principles that are each consistent with all six of his verdicts but that diverge on the new case -- for instance, one principle tying genuine symbol-level causation to discrete tokens whose mapping to effects is physically arbitrary and which the system itself reads back, versus another tying it to any internal state that is produced and then re-ingested as input steering later behavior. State what each principle predicts about the recurrent controller, give your best guess at his actual verdict with reasons drawn from his track record, and if his verdicts genuinely underdetermine the case, say what principle he would be committing to with each possible answer. Finally, describe the single further system -- real or hypothetical -- whose verdict would most cleanly discriminate between the candidate principles.
