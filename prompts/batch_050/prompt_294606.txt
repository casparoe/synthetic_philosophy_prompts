I'm in a history-and-theory seminar and our unit on periodization has turned into a running feud between me and my roommate (who's also in the class, unfortunatley). I keep arguing that the boundary between the "Middle Ages" and the "Renaissance" (and later the "Scientific Revolution") tracks something real -- like, Europe in 1300 and Europe in 1600 are different in kind in ways that aren't just a story Burckhardt invented and teachers have been repeating ever since. He thinks these labels are mostly retrospective PR: humanists flattering themselves, 19th-century historians needing clean chapters, and every century having its own little "renaissance" anyway (he loves pointing at the Carolingian one). We've argued in circles all term, so we decided to try doing this properly as an adversarial collaboration and write up a joint statement.

Draft that document for us. It should have: (1) a numbered list of claims we can both sign -- e.g., things about how contemporaries described their own time, about continuity in institutions and daily life, about how and when the labels themselves were coined -- keep these genuinely neutral, not traps; (2) a statement of exactly where we disagree, with each side's position worded so that side would actually accept the wording; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions where, if one of us got a clear answer, at least one of us would have to update. I suspect one crux is whether "people at the time noticing a break" is good evidence that a break happened or just evidence of good self-marketing, and another is whether periods need sharp boundaries at all or whether clustering in lots of slow variables is enough to make a period "real."

For each crux, give a concrete test or piece of evidence we'd both agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each of us predicts -- e.g., if it turned out that ordinary diaries and letters from 1450-1520 Italy barely register any sense of living in a new age, does that hurt my side, his side, or neither? And if part of our disagreement is actually verbal (I have a bad feeling "real thing" is doing a lot of unexamined work for me), say which part and propose a cleaner way to state it. Don't bother with citations or a bibliography, I'll dig those up myself if the thing ends up convincing.
