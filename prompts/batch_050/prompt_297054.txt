I work as a site reliability engineer, and for our team blog I want to publish a satirical piece that only works if the philosophy underneath it is taken seriously. Write a mock incident postmortem of roughly 700 words, dated about sixty years in the future, in which a planetary habitability response team files the official blameless postmortem for what the document calls the climate incident, meaning roughly a century and a half of industrial emissions. Keep the register perfectly deadpan, in the polished corporate tone of a real postmortem, and let the piece follow the arc of one: summary, impact assessment, timeline, root cause, what went well, action items. Render it as continuous prose throughout, the way one would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables, no numbered lists.

The comedy must come from a genuine philosophical trap, played completely straight. The team keeps trying to identify the injured parties and keeps failing, because the people now alive owe their very existence to the decisions that caused the incident; different emissions policies would have led to different people being born, so no one alive can claim they personally would have been better off had the incident been prevented. The impact assessment should wrestle honestly with this before concluding, with evident professional relief, that the injured-party count is zero. The timeline should note that at the moment each causal decision was made, the resulting harm had not yet occurred, so no duty to anyone was being violated at the time. The severity rating should be retroactively downgraded by applying a standard annual discount rate across the intervening decades, until the assessed impact rounds to zero. The what-went-well portion can note that emissions reporting was accurate throughout. The action items should be empty, on the grounds that any remediation sweeping enough to matter would alter the future population and thereby dissolve the complainants. By the end, the reader should feel the reductio: a practice built to guarantee accountability has, applied across generations, become a machine for proving that no one is ever accountable for anything. American English, please.
