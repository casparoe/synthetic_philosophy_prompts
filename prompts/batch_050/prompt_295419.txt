In my epistemology seminar we've just finished the unit on Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument, and a friend in a different section shared her instructor's handout with me. It summarizes the argument like this:

"Williamson's anti-luminosity argument refutes the KK principle, the thesis that if you know something, you know that you know it. Williamson asks us to imagine someone who feels cold at dawn and slowly warms up as the sun rises. Because the change is gradual, there is no sharp moment at which the person stops feeling cold, so any condition like 'feeling cold' fails to be luminous: it can obtain without the person being in a position to know it obtains. Generalizing, Williamson concludes that no condition is luminous, which means we can never be certain even of our own current mental states. The argument thereby establishes externalism about knowledge: whether you know something always depends on factors beyond your own perspective."
