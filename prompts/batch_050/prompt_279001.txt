In chapter 15 of Leviathan (1651), Thomas Hobbes introduces a sceptic he calls the Foole, who reasons — in paraphrase — that since every man's preservation is committed to his own care, there is no reason why he should not break any covenant whenever breaking it conduces to his benefit; justice or injustice, the Foole concludes, has no hold on him where he stands to gain. Hobbes's reply is, in substance, that the Foole gambles on the errors of others: he cannot reasonably expect to be received into any society that would protect him, and must therefore expect to be left in the condition of war. This exchange is often read as the earliest clear statement of the view that defection is individually rational even where universal cooperation is better for all, and Hobbes's reply as a proto-iterated-game argument about reputation and the shadow of the future.
