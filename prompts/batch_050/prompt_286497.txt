I am writing up the taxonomy of responses to the lottery paradox and the preface paradox for a graduate seminar. The responses I already have on my list, which I take to exhaust the standard options: (1) Kyburg's original reply, which accepts that rational belief need not be closed under conjunction, so an agent may rationally hold a set of beliefs she knows to be jointly inconsistent; (2) the eliminativist reply that there is no legitimate state of outright acceptance at all, only degrees of belief, so both paradoxes trade on a fiction; (3) the first-order versus higher-order evidence reply, which reconciles the preface author's modesty with her individual claims by assigning the two attitudes to different evidential bases; (4) contextualist or interest-relative accounts on which what one may flat-out believe shifts with stakes or conversational setting, so the apparently inconsistent beliefs are never held under a single standard; and (5) threshold rules with additional constraints (such as requiring that one's beliefs form a consistent set), which preserve acceptance but refuse to license believing each ticket will lose. Give me one further response that is not on this list and not a rewording, special case, or combination of these. A constraint that matters for my purposes: the response should treat the two paradoxes together, explaining whatever asymmetry there is between them, rather than dissolving one while leaving the other untouched. Say what the response costs theoretically, and give your honest verdict on whether it improves on the entries above.
