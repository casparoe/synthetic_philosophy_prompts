Is there a name for this thing games do? I keep noticing that players, me included, reliably make games worse for ourselves by playing them *well*. Like: everyone who plays Skyrim long enough ends up as a stealth archer becuase it's the strongest build, even when they find it boring. People savescum their way through XCOM even though the game is obviously built around living with your dead soldiers, and then they say the campaign felt flat. My buddy turned off fast travel in Red Dead Redemption and swears it fixed the whole game for him. So the pattern is: the game hands you freedom, the optimal use of that freedom flattens the experience, and players take the optimal option anyway and then blame the game for being boring.

It's not just grinding, because grinding is tedious-but-necessary, whereas here the tedious path is optional and is often the *smart* one. And I don't think it's just "dominant strategy" in the game theory sense either, since that's about winning and this is specifically about how winning efficiently hollows out the point of playing. I've heard game designers grumble about this like it's a known occupational hazard, so I assume someone's written about it, but I don't know the term.

So: is there an established concept for this? If so, tell me what it's called and how it usually gets framed — in particular whether the standard story locates the problem in the player (some quirk of human motivation), in the designer (a failure of design), or in the reward structure itself. And if there isn't a clean established term, coin one, define it precisely, and walk me through the boundary cases: does it cover self-imposed challenges like no-hit runs (which look like the mirror image of it)? Speedrunning? Alt-tabbing to a wiki the moment a game asks you to figure anything out? Picking easy mode? Where exactly is the line between this phenomenon and just voluntarily playing badly for fun — and does the difference come down to who's responsible, what the player wants, or something structural about how the game hands out rewards?
