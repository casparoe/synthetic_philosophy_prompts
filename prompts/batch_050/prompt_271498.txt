So last week at our local ACX-adjacent meetup I was trying to explain Death in Damascus to a friend who'd somehow never run into it, and it struck me that the funniest part of the whole setup is that Death is basicly running an extremely reliable appointment-scheduling service, and everyone involved just accepts this as a fact of life. Nobody asks how the ledger works. Which gave me an idea for a piece I want written.

Write the customer-service FAQ page for Death's appointment department — the office responsible for keeping the appointments recorded in the ledger. Somewhere between 500 and 700 words, formatted as real customer questions with answers, in that soothing, slightly evasive corporate voice you get from an airline's help center. The humor has to come from actually taking the decision theory seriously: a man writes in furious that the ledger already said Aleppo before he'd even decided to flee; a married couple writes a joint letter because he says the appointment being written down means nothing they do matters, and she says that's exactly backwards, and they'd like the department to settle it; someone attempts the loophole of deciding by coin flip, and the FAQ has to have an official policy on that; someone files a freedom-of-information-style request for his own entry; maybe one complaint about the threatening gesture in the marketplace (the department regrets the misunderstanding). At least one answer should involve a customer convinced he's found a way to beat the system, with the reply patiently explaining why that's already priced in.

The constraints: never use terms like "causal decision theory" or "Newcomb" — a philosopher should be able to tell the writers understand exactly where the dilemma bites, while my meetup friend, who knows none of the jargon, still laughs. Deadpan the whole way through, no winking at the reader, no jokes that come from inventing silly features the original scenario doesn't have. American English please.
