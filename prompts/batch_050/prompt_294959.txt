I want a teaching plan, not the lecture itself. Specifically: the order the pieces have to come in so that "heritability is about variation within a population at a time, not about what caused your trait" lands as something they reconstruct themselves rather than a slogan I hand them (what depends on what — variance? the genotype/phenotype distinction? why correlation between relatives needs to come after something else?); the one example to carry the whole thing — I keep reaching for height in well-fed vs. famine-struck populations, but tell me if that's the wrong vehicle or if it needs a second example to do work it can't do alone; the misconceptions they'll bring in roughly the order they'll surface (my guesses: "it tells you how genetic your own intelligence is," "high heritability means interventions can't work," "high within-group heritability settles the between-group question") and exactly where in the sequence to defuse each one before it hardens; one question to ask at the end whose answer shows they got it rather than memorized it; and what I should openly refuse to go into at this level and say I'm refusing.
