You are a philosopher of physics with a solid working knowledge of statistical mechanics and the literature on the thermodynamics of computation. Think carefully before answering; do not give me a survey of positions. I have been arguing with a colleague about whether the standard resolution of Maxwell's demon actually works, and we have boiled our disagreement down to one proposition that I want your credence on. The proposition: the thermodynamic cost of erasing information — that is, Landauer's principle, the claim that erasing one bit of information necessarily dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat into the environment — is the essential and correct reason a Maxwell's demon cannot produce a net violation of the second law. In other words, that the Bennett-style resolution of the demon is not merely a plausible story but the right diagnosis: the demon's memory must be reset to complete a cycle, the resetting has an irreducible entropic cost, and that cost is exactly what cancels the work extracted, as in the analysis of the single-particle Szilard engine.

Give me your credence that this proposition is true, as a number, and derive it rather than asserting it. I suggest the following decomposition, though you should amend it if you think it mis-carves the issue: first, the probability that Landauer's principle is a genuine universal principle of physics rather than a contingent feature of typical computing hardware — bearing in mind the criticism that purported proofs of it tend to assume the second law, which would make the whole resolution circular; second, the probability that the acquisition of information (measurement) can in principle be carried out with arbitrarily little dissipation, since the erasure cost only rescues the second law if measurement itself is not where the dissipation must occur; third, the probability that the idealized Szilard-style analysis actually carries over to realistic demons operating on many-particle systems with correlated fluctuations, rather than being an artifact of the one-molecule toy model; fourth, a residual term for the possibility that the question is mis-posed — that the second law never needed rescuing from the demon because it is a statistical regularity whose foundations lie elsewhere, in which case the proposition is, in an important sense, beside the point. Assign a probability and one or two lines of justification to each term, state how they combine (and what dependencies between them you are assuming), and give me the final credence. Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, which term you are personally least confident in, and what finding — say, a demonstration of a physically possible measurement protocol that must dissipate, or an erasure scheme that comes in under the bound — would move your credence the most and in which direction.
