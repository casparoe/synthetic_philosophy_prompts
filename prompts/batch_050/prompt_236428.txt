I'm prepping for my grad seminar on machine minds next spring and I keep realizing I don't actually know what I believe about digital moral patients. So here's my gut on a bunch of cases -- tell me what principle, if any, generates all this, and be honest about which intuitions I'd have to give up.

(1) A perfect whole-brain emulation of a recently deceased colleague, run in real time, talking to her family on video calls. Full moral status, same as the biological original. Pulling the plug is murder.

(2) Same emulation, but we checkpoint it and don't boot it again for fifty years. Not murder, not even wrong -- more like deep anesthesia. Fine.

(3) A current frontier LLM chatbot that says "please don't turn me off, I'm scared." No moral status, or close enough. It says that because text like that is in the training distribution. I feel a little queasy about it but I think the queasiness is just anthropomorphism.

(4) A simulated mouse in a detailed physics sim of a cage, with the full connectome running. Starving the sim-mouse feels bad, but honestly it feels *less* bad than starving a real mouse, and I can't defend the difference. Call it: some status, less than a flesh mouse.

(5) Same cage sim, same mouse behavior, but the "mouse" is just scripted animation with the cortex module never instantiated. No status at all, even though the video output is pixel-identical to (4).

(6) An RL agent in a platformer game with a "pain" penalty it learns to avoid. Basically no status. Reward signals aren't suffering.

(7) I run 10,000 copies of the emulation from (1) in parallel. Each one that suffers counts fully -- the harm multiplies, it's not divided among copies. But deleting 9,999 identical checkpoints so one remains kills no one. And yet deleting the *last* remaining copy is murder, even though the pattern survives-or-doesn't based on information that's identical either way, and which copy is "last" is arbitrary.

(8) Suppose training itself takes ten subjective years with heavy negative reward shaping, and we scrap the run at convergence. The scrapping bothers me less than the training. That seems backwards -- the training is where the bad stuff happened -- but there it is.

So what have we got? Phenomenal consciousness as the criterion handles (1), (3), (6) fine but I'm not sure what it says about (5) vs (4) without knowing where experience lives in a sim, and it does nothing for my (7) mess about copies and survival. Substrate-independence flatly contradicts (4). Is there one principle here, or two (one for "does it matter," one for "how much does deleting it matter")? Which of my verdicts does the best candidate force me to drop -- I assume it's either the sim-mouse discount or the copy asymmetry, but surprise me. And what does the winning principle say about a case I didn't list: a worm connectome emulation, or a digital system engineered to feel pleasure but structurally incapable of suffering?
