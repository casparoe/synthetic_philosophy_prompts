I serve on an engineering school’s academic-integrity board. In a closed-book examination whose rules prohibit “reference materials and external memory aids” while allowing blank scratch paper, a candidate used a wearable notebook in which, over two years, she had stored the formulas she uses professionally. She consults it constantly, trusts its entries without re-deriving them, and says she has never tried to commit those formulas to biological memory. In roughly 800 words, draft the board’s opinion deciding whether this was prohibited assistance. Anchor the opinion in the following passage from Andy Clark and David Chalmers’s The Extended Mind (1998):

“In all these cases the individual brain performs some operations, while others are delegated to manipulations of external media. Had our brains been different, this distribution of tasks would doubtless have varied.”

Do not treat the passage as showing that every note is a mental state. Use it to formulate administrable criteria for distinguishing a cognitively integrated aid from ordinary reference material, then apply those criteria to these facts. Give a concrete ruling and any prospective rule or accommodation the board should adopt, including the strongest objection to your ruling.
