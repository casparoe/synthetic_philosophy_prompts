So I'm putting together my "official position" for a philosophy of religion reading group and before I get torn apart I want to know if my package actually hangs together. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm a Molinist. God has middle knowledge: logically prior to any decree, he knows what every possible creature would freely do in any fully specified circumstance.

(2) This is exactly why I think Molinism beats simple-foreknowledge views on freedom: God's knowledge of my choices is explanatorily dependent on what I'd do, not the other way around, so my libertarian freedom is safe.

(3) I get that the grounding objection is the big cost of the view and I bite the bullet: counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are just true, grounded in what the creature would do, end of story.

(4) I also believe in pretty meticulous providence: God actualized this world because of what he middle-knew about us, e.g. he put me in the circumstances where I'd freely convert, and gives grace where he knows itll be freely accepted.

(5) I think its fitting to thank God for my conversion, and even in some sense for the free faithfulness of the saints and martyrs.

(6) And I dont think contingent CCFs outside God's control threaten his sovereignty or aseity — he doesn't author them, and that's fine.

Which of these are actually in tension and which only look like it? For each real tension tell me how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be — and if one single revision cleans up the most, name it. I'm not attached to any particular one of these surviving, so don't pull punches.
