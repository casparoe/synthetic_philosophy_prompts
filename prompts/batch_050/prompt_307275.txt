I work as a data scientist at a mid-sized company that runs a recipe-and-grocery app, and my team keeps getting proposals that boil down to "infer what users want from their behavior and then optimize for it." Recent examples: infer dietary goals from which recipes people actually cook (not the ones they bookmark), infer how much people value delivery speed from whether they pay for it, infer "taste profiles" for the recommendation engine from skipping patterns. I took a grad course a while back that covered inverse reinforcement learning and revealed preference theory, and the big takeaway I remember is that inferring preferences from behavior is way more underdetermined than my PMs think -- behavior is always the product of preferences AND constraints AND the choice set you offered AND the interface, so you can't just read values off clicks. But that lesson is stuck at the level of vibes and I can't operationalize it.

What I want is an actual review procedure I can run when one of these proposals lands on my desk (roughly 2-3 a month, and my sign-off is required before anything ships to production, but I only have like half a day per review and can't rerun their analysis myself). Give me the ordered list of questions to ask the proposer, what a satisfactory answer to each looks like in plain terms a PM can produce, which failures are automatic "go back and redo this" versus which are "ship it but with a disclaimer and a monitoring plan," and the points where the checklist genuinely can't decide and it has to become a values call for leadership (I suspect e.g. "do we count users' revealed behavior as their real preference when it conflicts with their stated preference" is one of those, but maybe not).

The procedure has to handle at least these recurring hard cases, so build them in: brand-new users with almost no behavioral history; behaviors that changed because WE changed the interface or defaults (we recently moved the "quick delivery" toggle and suddenly everyone "prefers" it); users whose behavior is heavily constrained (budget, what's in stock, what their household eats); and the case where the model infers a preference the user would disavow if asked. Then run the procedure on two real-ish proposals: (1) inferring from cooking-vs-bookmarking behavior which recipes to push harder in recommendations, and (2) inferring that users "don't really care about" nutrition information because they expand that tab less than 10% of the time, proposed as justification for demoting it in the UI. I'd like at least one of these to come out with an answer that will make the proposer unhappy, so I can see what that conversation looks like. Also tell me honestly where this procedure will misfire or give false confidence, because I'd rather know its blind spots than discover them in a postmortem.
