I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy of physics, planning a dissertation around the hole argument in general relativity. I know the standard terrain: Einstein's argument that if diffeomorphisms acting as the identity outside some region are taken to generate physically distinct models, the field equations underdetermine the metric inside that region; the standard reading of this as an objection to naive spacetime substantivalism; and the range of responses — sophisticated substantivalism, relationism, the view that the determinism worry dissolves once gauge-equivalent models are identified, and structuralist approaches that relocate the spacetime ontology altogether.

What I need is a defended prioritization, not a survey. Which open questions in this area are most worth three or four years of dissertation work, and in what order? For each question you raise, please assess: (a) which other questions in the philosophy of spacetime depend on it — is it upstream or downstream; (b) how tractable it actually is given the current state of the debate, as opposed to tractable in principle; (c) whether progress on it would change how the hole argument's conclusion should be understood, or whether it is a refinement the main debate could survive without; and (d) what result or development would move it up or down your ranking.

Candidates I have in mind, though you should feel free to add or replace them: whether the determinism conclusion can be established without begging the question against the substantivalist; whether responses developed for classical general relativity carry over to theories with different gauge structures; whether the dispute is a genuine metaphysical disagreement or turns on how physical equivalence of models is defined, in which case clarification might partly dissolve it; and whether the argument's moral for determinism generalizes or is an artifact of diffeomorphism invariance specifically.

Two constraints on the answer. First, separate what is genuinely settled in this literature — the mathematical core of the argument and its least controversial morals — from what remains contested among people who work on it, and do not present a contested diagnosis as consensus. Second, note that I'm asking which questions most deserve limited dissertation time, not which are most famous, so please defend the ordering in terms of dependency, tractability, and expected philosophical payoff rather than prominence.
