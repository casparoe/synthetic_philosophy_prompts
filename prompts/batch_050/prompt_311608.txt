I'm a novelist working on a book set in a loosely fictionalized Corsican mountain village in the 1860s, and the plot basically runs on vendetta logic — an insult at a wedding in chapter two is still getting people killed in chapter nineteen. To get the psychology right I fell down a rabbit hole about honor cultures versus dignity cultures (and the newer stuff about "victimhood culture," where people air grievances by appealing to third parties instead of handling it themselves), and everywhere I turn, writers and podcasters lean on the same analogy: honor is a currency. It's scarce, it lives in other people's hands, an insult is a theft or at least a withdrawal, it gets inflated away if you extend it to everyone, and it must be defended at disproportionate cost because once the market doubts your credit you're finished. The book I'm reading uses the analogy to argue that honor cultures aren't irrational at all — dueling and feuding are just what sensible actors do in an economy where reputation is the only collateral the state won't secure — but then, confusingly, it uses the same currency frame to argue dignity culture is morally superior, since in dignity culture every person is issued the same non-transferable endowment that no insult can debase. My writing group adored the analogy and wants me to restructure the whole novel around it, which feels like an awful lot of load for one metaphor to bear.

So before I let it colonize my book: audit the analogy for me. Give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of the money, the market, the exchange rate, theft, credit, bankruptcy — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. Some candidate stress points: money isn't zero-sum the way honor seems to be when one man's gain in standing is another's loss; you can save money but honor apparently evaporates if a public slight goes unanswered; and nobody ever had to physically fight a stranger to protect their bank balance. Also, does the currency picture even make sense of victimhood culture, or does that need a different analogy entirely (I keep thinking courtroom rather than marketplace)? Most importantly, separate the disanalogies that matter for the two conclusions from the ones that don't: can the analogy still support the claim that feuding is rational, and can it support the claim that dignity culture is better, or is the normative verdict being smuggled in by features of money that honor never had? If the currency frame can't carry all this, suggest a better source domain — I've been toying with honor as a broadcast signal or as a legal standing you can lose, but maybe those are worse.
