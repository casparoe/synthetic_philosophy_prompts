She deploys it to support a definite conclusion — that successful scientific categories are discovered rather than constructed, that the periodic table and ideally biological taxonomy track divisions that existed before we arrived, and that talk of kinds being socially constructed is therefore simply confused. I want a rigorous audit of how far this analogy actually holds.

First, make the correspondence explicit, as a table or equivalent: what plays the role of the animal carcass, of the joints themselves, of the knife, of the butcher's training and feel for where the blade meets no resistance, and of the finished cuts of meat laid out for sale. Second, identify where the mapping breaks, and be concrete: consider, among other cases, jade, which served as a single kind for centuries of carving and trade before mineralogy found it to be two chemically distinct stones with no shared essence; the demotion of Pluto; and the fact that biologists operate with several mutually incompatible but individually well-motivated species concepts. Finally, say whether my instructor's anti-constructionist conclusion survives once these points are on the table, and if the butcher analogy cannot carry it, propose a better analogy for whatever position the cases actually support.
