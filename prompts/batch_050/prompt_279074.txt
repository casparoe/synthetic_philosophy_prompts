A friend of mine who did a stats minor keeps deploying a result he calls "the washing out of priors" to win arguments, mostly about politics. His version goes: "Bayesian updating comes with a convergence guarantee. Start two rational people with whatever priors you like, feed them the same stream of evidence, and their beliefs provably merge in the limit. Priors are just arbitrary starting points and the data eventually overwhelms them. So if two people keep disagreeing after being shown the same studies, at least one of them isn't updating honestly -- the math says good-faith disagreement can't survive shared evidence."

I've repeated this a few times myself because it's a fantastic rhetorical weapon, but a philosopher acquaintance winced and muttered something about the actual theorems saying much less than that. Before I use it again I want to know what I'd actually be claiming. Could you go through his summary claim by claim and tell me which parts someone who knows the formal results (merging-of-opinions theorems and so on) would accept, which parts are overreach, and which are just wrong?

Some specific things I'm unsure about:

- Does convergence need the two people to agree on more than just what the evidence is? Like, do they need to share likelihood functions, or agree on which hypotheses are even possible, or something about each person's prior giving positive probability to what the other believes?
- "In the limit" -- does the math say anything about how fast? Could the priors dominate for longer than a human lifetime of evidence, so that the guarantee is true but practically useless?
- Is it actually a theorem that persistent disagreement implies someone broke the rules? Or are there ways for two perfect Bayesians, both following the math exactly, to disagree forever?
- And the bad-faith conclusion -- does anything in the formalism touch motives at all, or is that part entirely his addition?

At the end, tell me straight: is there a defensible version of this I can still use in arguments, and what work can it actually do, or should I retire the move?
