I sit on the city council of a mid-sized American city. An outside investigation recently confirmed that, over roughly fifteen years, detectives in one police district coerced confessions from dozens of suspects, many of whom served prison time. Most of the officers involved have retired or are protected by statutes of limitations; prosecutors tell us that at best they could bring two or three cases, and those might well fail. The victims' families are split: some demand prosecutions whatever the odds, others say what they actually want is a complete public account of what was done, by whom, and on whose orders. A proposal now before us would establish a truth commission empowered to offer conditional amnesty: any officer or supervisor who testifies fully and verifiably about their own conduct and the chain of command would be shielded from city-initiated discipline and from any use of their testimony against them; those who stay silent remain exposed to whatever legal and employment consequences can still be reached. The commission would cost about the same as hiring special prosecutors and expanding the civil settlement fund, so we genuinely cannot do both. Draft the memo I will circulate before next month's vote. It should recommend one option as a concrete bottom line, explain what the city would actually get for its money in each case, weigh whether a full factual record without punishment counts as justice or as the guilty purchasing impunity with information, and take seriously the strongest argument for whichever option you recommend against rather than caricaturing it.
