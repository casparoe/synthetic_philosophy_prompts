I'm a novelist, and I've run into something both in my reading and in my own drafts that I can't find a word for. I can stay immersed in a story with dragons, time travel, physics that couldn't possibly work. But I recently read a novel in which the narration itself — not any character — treats betraying a loyal friend as obviously admirable, and I found I simply couldn't go along. Not that I disbelieved such a thing could happen; the book felt like it was asking me to take something up that I refused to take up, and the immersion broke. The odd part: when, as an exercise, I rewrote the passage so that the same sentiment belonged to a character rather than to the narrative voice, the blockage vanished and the passage got better. A friend says this is just ordinary disagreement with the author's views; it doesn't feel like that, because I don't disagree with dragons. Does this phenomenon have a name? If so, does the literature on it explain the two asymmetries I've noticed — impossible physics versus inverted morals, and narrator versus character — or are those separate problems? And where are its edges: does the same thing happen with inverted etiquette, or humor, or judgments of beauty? I'd like the carving, not just a label.
