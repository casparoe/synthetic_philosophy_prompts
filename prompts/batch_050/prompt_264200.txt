She was telling me about the color phi phenomenon (the thing where two differently colored dots flash in sequence on a screen and subjects report seeing one dot moving and apparently changing color at the midpoint of its path, even though the second dot's color only shows up later) and about Dennett's two ways of modelling what happens: a Stalinesque model where the brain fabricates the color change in advance before the experience gets presented, and an Orwellian model where you actually experience the dot as unchanged at the time and the memory gets rewritten a split second later so you only ever remember it having changed. I nodded along and then realized I was completely lost, because both of those sound like totally mundane things my systems do, and I couldn't see what the philosophical puzzle was supposed to be.

So here's what I want: restate this whole debate in the vocabulary I actually think in -- encoders, buffers, frame timestamps, event logs, caches, the render pipeline, the client-side player. Do it faithfully. Tell me what the color phi setup corresponds to architecturally, what each model claims happens at which stage of the pipeline (is the Stalinesque model like editing frames pre-encode? is the Orwellian one like patching the client's buffer or rewriting the recorded copy after the fact?), and what sort of instrumentation or probing could in principle tell the two apart. As I understand it, Dennett's whole point is that for consciousness this fact of the matter goes missing, at least for edits happening below some temporal resolution. So: which is it -- does my vocabulary have a blind spot (what would the "final consumer" of the stream even correspond to, and what does Dennett think happens to that role?), or does the translation show the philosophical debate was confused all along?
