Ok so I just spent like three hours arguing with a friend who holds this view that the only thing that matters is the absence of craving and suffering — pleasure, achievement, none of it counts except removing bad states. I was trying to steelman him and got stuck on something, and I want to know if philosophy already has a term for it.

Here's what I noticed. On his view, getting rid of a craving is good, full stop. But there seem to be two very different ways a craving can go away. One: I smoke a cigarette and the craving disappears... for an hour. Then it's back, and the habit keeps itself alive. Scratching a mosquito bite, checking my phone the second I feel anxious, having a drink to stop wanting a drink — same shape. Each fix genuinely removes the discomfort in the moment, but it feeds the loop, so over a lifetime you end up with way more craving than if you'd never started. Two: I drink water when I'm actually thursty and the craving is just gone, done, no loop. Or a craving fades on its own and never comes back.

So his view seems to imply the cigarette case is *bad* overall — each smoke is a small relief but it manufactures future craving — while the water case is good, even though both are "craving removed" at the moment it happens. He resisted this and said relief is relief. I think his own view forces the distinction on him whether he likes it or not.

My questions: does this split — relief that ends a craving vs. relief that keeps the craving-machine running — have an established name, in value theory or anywhere else? I suspect it's adjacent to a bunch of things (addiction science, old Greek classifications of desires, psychology-of-motivation stuff) without being identical to any of them, so tell me the closest existing concepts, exactly how what I'm describing differs from each, and whether anyone has actually carved out the specific axiological point — that the two reliefs differ in *value*, not just in psychology. If its nameless, coin a name, define it, and give a couple of boundary cases (e.g. what about a craving-satisfaction that's loop-neutral, like one fancy meal you never think about again?).

You're a philosopher specializing in axiology and theories of well-being; think carefully and brainstorm the neighboring concepts before committing. Put your bottom line in the first sentence — yes there's an established name, or no there isn't — then give the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question. And clearly separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this; don't present a minority view as the consensus.
