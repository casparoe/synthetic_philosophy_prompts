Before I read any more theories of distributive justice, I want the spec first. Write the desiderata a theory of how to distribute a fixed pile of resources across people with different capacities to convert resources into welfare has to meet -- the cases it must get right, the distinctions it can't collapse (welfare vs resources, how much each person ends up with vs who ends up with it, claims vs outcomes), and the consideration behind each requirement. Build the case list around these: Nozick's utility monster; a 'reverse monster' I made up who gets huge welfare from tiny amounts but caps out fast, so anything beyond a pittance does nothing for him; an adaptive-preferences case where someone's cheap tastes were trained into them by a lifetime of deprivation; and a monster whose welfare partly consists in others having less than him. For each desideratum say whether it's a genuine adequacy condition or just a prejudice of one camp. Then the part I actually care about: flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, and say wich single requirement knocks out the most contenders. Finally a quick scorecard -- plain total utilitarianism, prioritarianism, sufficientarianism, and a resourcist view -- against the list, one line each is enough, keep the weight on the criteria not the scoring.
