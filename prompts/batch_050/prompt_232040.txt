I'm a software engineer on the data platform team at a mid-sized company. Part of my job is a weekly review where product managers bring experiment readouts and dashboard trends, and roughly twice a month we hit the same fight: the aggregate number says one thing, the per-segment breakdown says the opposite, and the room splits into people who insist the segmented view is "the real story" and people who insist the aggregate is what actually happened. I've read enough to know this is Simpson's paradox, and I've also read enough to know that "always disaggregate" is not the right rule -- sometimes segmenting introduces the distortion instead of removing it. What I don't have is a way to settle these arguments that doesn't degrade into whoever sounds more confident about causality winning.

Write me a decision procedure I can run in under twenty minutes, ahead of the weekly meeting, that takes a reversal (or a claimed reversal) as input and outputs a verdict: report the aggregate, report the segments, report both with a warning, or escalate because the available information can't settle it. Ground it in whatever the causal-inference literature actually establishes about when conditioning on a variable is correct -- I believe the key distinction is between a variable that confounds the relationship, one that sits on the causal path between the thing we changed and the outcome, and one that is a common effect of both -- but I have no formal training, so the procedure has to translate that into questions I can answer by looking at the experiment setup and talking to the PM, not into graph theory I can't verify. The procedure should specify: the questions in order; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; where it stops with a definite output; the most common ways a PM will game or misread each step (e.g., proposing segments invented after seeing the data); and the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and the procedure is just dressing up a guess. Be explicit about what the procedure cannot do.

Then run it end-to-end on three cases. First: we ship a new onboarding flow, overall conversion rises, but conversion falls within both the mobile and desktop segments, and traffic shifted heavily toward mobile during the test window. Second: we ship a feature meant to increase retention, overall retention is flat, but within the segment of users who visited five or more times in the first week, retention is sharply up -- and the feature is designed to make people visit more often. Third: a case you construct yourself where the procedure is forced to output "escalate" even though everyone in the room will find that answer unsatisfying, and explain why no amount of additional slicing of the same dataset could have avoided that output.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence -- whether a genuinely usable procedure for this exists, and in what sense -- then give the procedure and the worked cases. No preamble, no restating my request.
