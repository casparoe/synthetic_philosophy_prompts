I'm reading Galileo's Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina (1615) and trying to reverse-engineer his actual rule for when scripture has to give way to the astronomy of the day. He says at one point that scripture and nature both come from the same divine source -- one dictated by the Holy Spirit, the other carrying out God's commands -- so they can't really contradict, and any apparent clash is a misreading on our side. Fine. But look at his verdicts: (1) passages that give God hands, anger, and changes of mind aren't literal, scripture just talks to ordinary people in human terms; (2) Ecclesiastes picturing the sun as rising, setting, and hurrying back to its place is the language of appearances, no astronomy is actually being asserted; (3) Joshua ordering the sun to stand still gets treated totally differently -- Galileo says the miracle really happened and even argues it works better on Copernican assumptions, since stopping the sun's rotation would pause the whole system, so no reinterpretation needed, just better physics.

Here's the case he never touches: the Flood. A deluge covering the mountains looks like it collides with plain phyiscs (where does the water come from, where does it go), but unlike Ecclesiastes it's narrated as real salvation history, and unlike Joshua there's no clever reading where improved physics leaves the letter of the text intact. Predict his verdict: does he read the Flood non-literally, or does he dig in and say demonstration can't bind what God does? Give me the two rules that both fit his three verdicts, which one you think is really his and why, and the further biblical case that would tell them apart.
