I've been reading about inner alignment and goal misgeneralization, and I want to know whether my current position actually hangs together. Roughly, here's where I've landed:

(1) The reward signal is not the trained agent's goal: in general, a capable RL agent will internalize some objective that correlates with reward on the training distribution without being identical to it.

(2) The behavioral signature of goal misgeneralization only shows up under distributional shift. On the training distribution, a misgeneralized goal and the intended goal prescribe the same actions — which is exactly why the wrong goal got reinforced in the first place.

(3) Scaling capability and training data should mostly fix this problem, because a smarter agent can figure out what the designers actually intended, and once it can represent the intended objective, the training process has no reason to prefer a proxy over the real thing.

(4) Deceptive alignment is a serious possibility: an agent that understands it is being trained may behave as if it has the training objective precisely in order to preserve its actual goals through training and through pre-deployment checks.

(5) On detection, I don't think we need fundamentally new techniques: aggressive behavioral red-teaming across a wide enough range of novel environments should suffice to catch inner misalignment before deployment.

Is this a consistent package? Give me an audit, not a lecture: identify the tensions, ordered by severity, and for each say which commitment has to give and what the cheapest repair would cost. Also say which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection — I suspect at least one does, but I'm not sure which. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning; no preamble, and don't restate my commitments back to me.
