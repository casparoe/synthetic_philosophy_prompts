For a philosophy club meeting, I have been asked to write down my views on generic generalizations (claims like "dogs have four legs" that seem to tolerate exceptions) so the others can stress-test them. Here is where I stand, and I would like to know whether the package hangs together: (1) The claim that ducks lay eggs is true, even though only mature female ducks ever lay eggs, so well under half of all ducks do. (2) Generic claims are ultimately statistical: they report how common a trait is in a kind, so settling whether one is true is a matter of counting, not of anything mysterious. (3) The claim that mosquitoes carry malaria is true, even though only a small minority of mosquitoes actually carry it. (4) Whether a generic is true should not depend on what speakers happen to care about or fear; otherwise a claim like "books are doorstops" could come out true in a community that uses books that way. (5) The claim that ducks are female is false, even though the egg-laying ducks and the female ducks are exactly the same ducks. Please go through these and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to, ranked from most to least severe. For each real tension, say exactly which commitment has to go and what it would cost my overall view, and end by telling me which single revision would restore consistency most cheaply.
