I am a novelist, and I am partway through a book in which an ant colony figures as something close to a character, and I want to know whether the conceit I have built the book around is merely a literary device or whether it corresponds to a position anyone has seriously defended.

Here is what the novel asks the reader to accept. No single ant in the colony knows anything worth calling knowledge; each follows local rules and lives a few weeks. Yet the colony, taken as a whole, behaves in ways that resist description except in mental vocabulary. It was poisoned once, years ago, at a particular trail, and it still avoids that trail although not one ant alive today was alive then -- it remembers. When two nest sites are available it compares them, slowly, through a process no ant oversees, and then commits -- it decides, and it can be, at the colony level, wrong. It holds what I can only call a standing policy toward the neighboring colony, a hostility that outlasts every individual who first enacted it. In the book a human character learns to leave pheromone-soaked tokens for the colony and to read its replies, and the replies are coherent month after month -- far more coherent than any ant could manage, since the colony's interlocutor is, in a sense, the only party to the conversation with a long memory.

What I want to know is this. Is there an established name for the claim that such a collective is not merely usefully described as a minded thing -- as we speak of a market panicking -- but literally is one: a single subject with states that deserve the names belief, memory, and intention, located at the level of the colony rather than in any ant? And is there a further, distinct name for the strongest version, the claim that there is something it is like to be the colony -- that it does not merely process but experiences?

I suspect the first claim has neighbors I am conflating it with: whatever biologists mean when they call a colony a single organism, whatever engineers mean when they borrow the colony as a design pattern, whatever it is philosophers say about corporations or juries acting as groups. Please tell me what the nearest concepts are, how my conceit differs from each, which fits best, and -- if nothing quite fits -- what you would call it, with enough precision that I could tell whether my fictional colony falls inside or outside the definition.
