I'm trying to figure out what I actually believe about diachronic rationality -- money pumps and all that -- so here are my gut verdicts on cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, ties them together.

1. My friend genuinely has cyclical preferences: she'd rather have the vintage bike than the kayak, the kayak than the espresso machine, and the espresso machine than the bike, and these preferences are stable. A dealer offers to swap machine for bike for $20, bike for kayak for $20, kayak for machine for $20, round and round. If she keeps paying, I think something has gone *irrationally* wrong, even though every single trade is one she'd happily make.

2. My cousin, over the past decade, has sold his camera gear at a loss to fund climbing gear, sold that to fund a boat, sold that to fund camera gear again. Each trade reflected what he actually wanted *at the time*, and his tastes genuinely changed. I don't think he's irrational at all -- just a guy with a pricey personality.

3. A friend knows Future-Her will blow the rent money at the casino, so she has her paycheck locked into an account she can't touch on weekends. Ulysses-style. I think this is not just permissible but smart.

4. Different friend: he *plans* to save, predicts with total confidence that Future-Him won't follow through, and so "sophisticatedly" decides to just blow the money now and skip the charade -- at least he isn't kidding himself, he says. I think this is still a rationality failure. Knowing you'll defect doesn't launder the defecting.

5. Same cyclical-preferences friend from case 1, but now she's read about money pumps, refuses to trade anything ever, and just sits on the espresso machine forever even though she'd rather have the bike. This one I honestly can't call -- paranoid paralysis or admirable caution?

So: is there one principle that delivers verdicts 1 through 4 -- especially 1 vs 2, which look structurally identical (sequence of endorsed trades, ends up dominated) -- and explains why 3 is fine while 4 isn't, given that both are just responding to a prediction about your future self? State it precisely, tell me which case motivates which clause, and be honest about which of my verdicts it has to sacrifice. And then tell me what it says about case 5, the one I keep flip-flopping on.
