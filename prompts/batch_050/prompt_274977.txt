Since people are frequently wrong about their own experience, heterophenomenology concludes that consciousness, as traditionally conceived, does not exist — there is only verbal behavior and the brain states that cause it. In this respect Dennett's method is best understood as the contemporary heir of Watson's and Skinner's behaviorism: just as the behaviorists refused to admit inner states into science, Dennett refuses to admit them on the grounds that subjects cannot be trusted to describe them accurately.
