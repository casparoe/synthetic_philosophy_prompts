So on lesswrong and the adjacent blogs there's this move people make constantly whenever disagreement comes up: treat everyone's beliefs like readings from measuring instruments. You're a thermometer, I'm a thermometer, and when two thermometers disagree you don't insist yours is right, you average the readings, weighted by past calibration if you have it. Then this gets used to argue for strong epistemic modesty -- that when I disagree with, say, expert consensus or a bunch of smart people who've thought about AI timelines longer than me, I should mostly just defer, because refusing to update is like a thermometer insisting its own reading is special.

I've used this argument myself. But lately I've noticed the people deploying it pick and choose when to be thermometers (they'll defer on nutrition but not on things where they have contrarian positions they love). So I want an actual audit of the instrument-averaging analogy. Give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the temperature, the reading, the calibration record, the manufacturer's specs, the room the thermometers are all sitting in. Then the disanalogies, and specifically which ones matter for the modesty conclusion. Candidates I'd want addressed: brains all trained on the same blogs and books have correlated errors in a way independently manufactured instruments don't; I can introspect my own reasoning process but only see the output of yours, which is a weird asymmetry the thermometer case doesn't have; there's no calibration database for brains on most questions anyone disagrees about; and instruments don't get to pick which room they're in but people select their epistemic peers. If there are disanalogies that seem big but don't actually bear on whether to defer, flag those as seperate too.

Then the bottom line: does the modesty argument survive once the mapping is on the table, or was the thermometer picture doing all the work? If it survives in some restricted form (defer on X kinds of questions, trust your inside view on Y kinds), spell out what the restriction is and what in the analogy licenses it. And if you think there's a better analogy for what disagreement actually is, name it and show it handles the cases better.
