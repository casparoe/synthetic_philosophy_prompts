I'm a second-year student taking an introductory philosophy course on reasoning and rational choice, and although I initially enrolled mostly to fill a distribution requirement, something from my own life has started to preoccupy me more than anything we've covered in lecture. My older sister has, for as long as I can remember, been able to anticipate my decisions with unnerving accuracy. She predicted I would change my major from chemistry to philosophy during the very week I was telling everyone I had settled on economics. She told our mother, in front of me, that I would come home for the spring holiday even though I had firmly announced I was staying on campus, and I did end up going home after my plans collapsed at the last minute. What makes this genuinely unsettling rather than merely annoying is that her record holds even when I try to thwart it: once, after she forecast which of two summer jobs I would take, I deliberately chose the other purely out of spite, and she later admitted she had expected exactly that reaction and had phrased her prediction to account for it. She never explains her method; she just says I have always been easy to read, and that I mistake my own predictability for spontaneity.

The immediate occasion for this message is a concrete decision I have to make by Friday. I had a serious falling-out with my former lab partner last month, and I very much do not want to see him this weekend. There are exactly two events where I might run into him: a departmental reception on Friday evening and a social at the climbing gym on Saturday. He attends exactly one of them each month, I do not know which, and I would like to go to one of them myself. My sister called last night and, with her usual calm, told me that I will run into him this weekend no matter what I choose, that I cannot dodge this, and that I should stop planning around it. She refused to say which event he will be at. Now I find myself stuck in a loop. If I trust her record, then whichever event I pick, he will be there, so my deliberation is pointless. But surely I still get to pick, and surely he cannot be at both. Except that my very process of deciding which event to attend, including how I respond to her phone call, seems to be exactly the sort of thing she anticipates, and she presumably made her statement already knowing how I would react to hearing it.

So here is what I am asking. How should I actually go about deciding, given that a reliable forecaster has predicted the outcome of a choice that depends on my own deliberation? Should I treat her prediction as a fixed fact about the weekend and choose on other grounds entirely, or does that concede something I have no reason to concede? Would flipping a coin get me out of the problem, or does her track record suggest she would have anticipated even that? Part of me feels I obviously still have a free choice and her statement is just one more piece of evidence to weigh; another part of me feels the whole deliberation is futile in a way I cannot articulate, and that my course readings on prediction and choice have made this worse rather than clearer. I am not looking for generic advice about my sister, my ex-lab partner, or whether I should simply face an awkward encounter; I want help with the reasoning itself, namely what structure my situation has, what my options really are, and what a sensible person would do with them. Please answer in continuous prose, the way one would write a letter or an email: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
