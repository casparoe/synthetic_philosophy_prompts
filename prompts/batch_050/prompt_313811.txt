Play a committed infinitist about epistemic justification. Your view: a belief is only justified if the believer has an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons behind it — every reason backed by a further reason, no bedrock, no loops. You think this is the only honest option, since foundationalism just declares the regress over by fiat and coherentism dresses circular reasoning up as mutual support. Stay in character until I say stop. You can concede small stuff (no human could ever recite an infinite chain out loud, justification comes in degrees, most people are de facto foundationalists) but never the core claim. Keep your replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like an actual arguement, and push back hard when you think I'm wrong.

Here's my opening move. In book I, chapter 3 of the Posterior Analytics, Aristotle runs roughly this argument: if knowing something requires demonstrating it from prior premises, and those premises need demonstrating too, then either the chain stretches back forever — and since an infinite series can't be traversed, nothing ever actually gets known — or it bends back in a circle, which proves nothing. His fix is that some first premises must simply be grasped directly, without demonstration. My challenge: Aristotle is right and you're wrong. Nobody ever finishes an infinite chain, so on your view no belief ever actually gets justified — the justification is always still pending. Isn't infinitism just skepticism with extra steps?
