I've talked myself into a fairly unfashionable position on imprecise probabilities: that rational credence is always sharp, and that everything the imprecise-probability crowd gets right — Ellsberg aversion, the felt difference between a coin you know is fair and one of unknown bias, the sense that some evidence is just too thin to support a number — can be recovered with ordinary precise credences plus higher-order uncertainty and a bit of humility about one's own calibration. Imprecise credal states are at best a redundant notation for that machinery, and at worst they let in pathologies like dilation and decision paralysis for no real gain.

What would count against this? I don't want a restatement of why people find imprecise probabilities intuitive — I know the motivations, I just think they're better handled my way. I want to know what the view actually risks. A few things I'm unsure how to weight:

- The higher-order move seems to require violating the principle that your credence should equal your expectation of your own future credences, since otherwise the higher-order stuff averages out, collapses back onto the first-order number, and the "ambiguity" evaporates. If a formal result showed that any sharp representation of genuine ambiguity has to violate reflection-style principles, is that fatal, or can I just bite the bullet and deny those principles anyway?

- Is there a decision problem — ideally not Ellsberg itself, which I think is handled — where sharp credences demonstrably give the wrong answer and sets of credences give the right one, rather than merely feeling more honest?

- If it turned out empirically that forecasters who maintain genuinely imprecise states systematically out-perform those who force a single number, does that touch the normative claim at all, or only the descriptive one?

- There's also the worry that "sharp credence plus higher-order uncertainty" isn't really an alternative to imprecise probabilities but a re-description of it, in which case my position isn't false so much as empty. How would I tell?

Tell me which of these a commited sharp-credence defender would themselves concede as a genuine defeater, as opposed to a critic's restatement of the motivating intuitions, and rank whatever you come up with from fatal to merely awkward. And if you think the position has been so heavily fortified that nothing could count against it — that it's an unfalsifiable bit of framework-choosing rather than a substantive claim about rationality — say so, because that would annoy me but might well be true.
