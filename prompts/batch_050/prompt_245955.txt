I’m teaching an intro philosophy of biology seminar, 18 students, 80 minutes. Design a classroom game using a fictional island of lizard-like creatures where students play a conservation agency that can fund only three “species.” Give each team trait cards covering mating behavior, ancestry, ecology, and gene flow, but arrange the evidence so no single classification satisfies everything. Partway through, reveal that their funding decisions can change the creatures’ future: protected populations become isolated, hybrids are removed, or a distinct niche is deliberately maintained -- so calling something a species partly helps make it one. Include setup, rules, roles, rounds, small-stakes scoring, and a debrief that connects what happened to the major species concepts without naming them until the end. Also say what will predictably go wrong and which part of the real species problem the game can’t reproduce.
