A friend and I have been bickering about the Great Divergence for ages and we want to settle it properly, or at least figure out what we're actually disagreeing about. He's a "the question is fine, stop being precious" type: why did modern sustained growth kick off in Britain rather than China is, for him, maybe the single most important question in economic history, and the fact that answers are contested doesn't make the question itself dodgy. I'm the sceptic. I think the question as usually posed is badly formed: it quietly treats Europe's path as the default and China's as the thing needing explanation, it lumps together wildly different foils (Britain vs China-as-a-whole, Europe vs China, British core regions vs the Yangzi Delta, "why anywhere at all?") as if they were one question when each gets you a different answer, and it tends to smuggle in the assumption that what happened was on the cards for centuries rather than a late, contingent break.

Write the joint statement the two of us should be able to co-sign after an honest adversarial collaboration. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims we can both endorse — this should include things about what a why-question with a contrastive foil commits you to, what the actual evidence shows about when and where living standards and technology diverged, and whether the Needham question about why modern science didn't emerge in China is the same dispute wearing a different hat or a genuinely separate one; (2) the precise residual disagreements, each phrased in wording the relevant side would accept for itself — I suspect the big ones are whether picking a foil is itself a substantive theoretical move or an innocent framing choice, and whether a question can be ill-posed even while pointing at a real fact to explain; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling them would make at least one of us budge; and (4) for each crux, some case, comparison, or piece of evidence we both agree bears on it, with what each of us would predict written down in advance (e.g. what the "question is fine" camp vs the "question is ill-posed" camp each expect a well-chosen regional-level comparison to show). If part of our dispute turns out to be verbal rather than real, flag which part and why. Format it as the agreed claims first, then a disagreements section, then cruxes with predictions. British English please, and don't pull punches on either side.
