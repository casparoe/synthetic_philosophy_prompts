I am a high school senior writing an original poem to read before my school orchestra’s spring concert. The orchestra will perform a student arrangement of an old march whose surviving score is damaged: one measure is illegible, so the student arranger reconstructed it by listening to a recording of an earlier performance. The orchestra’s own performance will also be recorded, and another student plans to transform part of that recording into an electronic composition.

Act as an accomplished poet with expertise in the ontology of musical works. Think carefully about the relationships among the damaged score, the reconstructed measure, the march as an enduring work, individual performances, recordings, arrangements, and the electronic piece. Begin with a brief brainstorming note identifying the conceptual contrasts the poem should dramatize.

Then write a sestina using these six end-words: score, work, performance, recording, copy, and mistake. The changing positions and repeated returns of these words should do philosophical work rather than serve as ornamentation. Across the six stanzas, the poem should explore whether the score is the work or merely instructions for producing it; whether each performance is an instance of the same work; whether the reconstructed measure corrects, interprets, or alters the march; whether the recording preserves a performance or becomes an artwork in its own right; and whether sampling the recording creates another performance, another copy, or a new work. The three-line envoi should bring all six end-words together without pretending that these questions have one obvious answer.

The poem should be intelligible and moving to an audience of students, parents, and musicians who have never studied aesthetics, while remaining precise enough that a philosopher of music would recognize the type–token distinction and the different ontological roles played by scores, performances, and recordings. Do not reduce the poem to a lecture or use technical terms merely as names; let the dramatic situation and the sestina’s repetitions carry the argument.
