So I help run the volunteer mod team for a mid-size online community (around 60k members across a subreddit and a Discord) and we're rewriting our rules from scratch. Right now enforcement is basicaly vibes: a post feels over the line, we remove it, someone screams censorship, the appeals thread runs for three days, and the next mod would have decided differently. At a meeting last week someone suggested we ground the whole thing in Mill's harm principle — the idea from On Liberty that the only legitimate reason to restrict what someone says or does is to prevent harm to other people, not to protect them from themselves and not because the majority finds their views offensive. I like it as an ideal but it's useless as stated: nobody on the team has read Mill, the word "harm" is doing all the work, and Mill himself seems to treat speech as a special case (there's that bit where an opinion that's fine to publish in the press becomes an incitement when shouted at an angry crowd gathered outside the person it targets), which means the principle apparently has a carve-out exactly where we need guidance most.

What I want is a decision procedure a moderator with zero philosophy background can actually run on a flagged post in five or ten minutes: a sequence of questions, in order, with instructions for what counts as a yes or a no at each step, where the procedure terminates in remove or leave up, and where it has to hand the call to a human because the question is one of values rather than application of the rule. It has to preserve the distinctions that make the principle the principle, not collapse into a vague "could this upset anyone" test. In particular it needs to tell us: how to separate genuine harm from mere offense or disgust (half our reports are just "this opinion makes me angry"), when harm to the poster themselves does or doesn't justify removal (we get posts about dangerous DIY electrical work and, occasionally, about self-harm), and what to do with speech that might cause harm indirectly or downstream — health misinformation, a post organizing a boycott of a named local business, that kind of thing. Please be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, because I'd rather know that going in.

Then run the procedure on three real-flavored cases: (1) a long, confident, citation-flavored post arguing a common childhood vaccine causes more harm than the disease, with replies asking how to get a medical exemption; (2) a thread where a dozen regulars have spent two days mocking a specific named member, who now says they're afraid to attend community meetups (they might be exaggerating, we can't verify); (3) a member's detailed writeup of an extremely risky solo climb they did, with several replies from people saying they want to attempt the same route. I'd like at least one case where the procedure spits out an answer I will not enjoy defending in the appeals thread, plus a short honest note on what the procedure can't do — the case types where the harm principle itself just runs out and we're making a value call and should admit it.
