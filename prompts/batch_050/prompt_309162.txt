So I co-teach a grad seminar on the ethics of AI oversight and we've spent the last few weeks on this whole process-versus-outcome supervision thing, and I realized mid-lecture that my own views on it are kind of a grab bag. I wrote them down to see if they cohere and now I'm worried they don't. Here they are, roughly as I'd defend them:

(1) Supervising the reasoning process is fundamentally more robust than supervising outcomes, because outcome-based reward just tells the system what to aim at and invites it to find shortcuts, reward hacks, or straight-up deception, whereas watching the steps means there's nowhere to hide the shortcut. (2) I'm also fairly convinced by the faithfulness literature: the chain-of-thought a model writes down is not a reliable window into the computation that actually produced its answer, and can be post-hoc confabulation. (3) Practically speaking, I think the best available training recipe right now is outcome-based RL with only light checks on the reasoning, mostly because that's what scales. (4) As models get more capable, I expect outcome verification to get relatively easier compared to process evaluation, since checking whether an answer is right usually takes less skill than judging whether a long argument for it is any good. (5) And yet, for genuinely high-stakes, irreversible decisions (say, a system advising on whether to release some novel organism, or on a military targeting call), I'd insist on process-based review of the actual reasoning rather than trusting a track record of good outcomes.

Audit this for me like you'd audit a grad student's position paper. Which pairs are in real tension and which just look like they are? For each genuine tension, tell me how severe it is and what exactly would have to give -- I'm not asking you to pick my favorite child, just to trace what commits me to what. Order the tensions by seriousness rather than treating them all as equally fatal, and if there's a single revision that buys me the most consistency for the least philosophical cost, name it. I genuinely haven't decided which of these I'd sacrifice, so don't assume one is sacred.
