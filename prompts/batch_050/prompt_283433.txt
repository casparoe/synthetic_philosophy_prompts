Ok so I've been arguing about capital punishment with my brother-in-law for like ten years and I realized I don't actually know what my own position is, because my gut reactions to cases don't seem to line up under one rule. Let me just dump them:

1. A guy meticulously plans and carries out the murder of his ex and her new partner, confesses proudly, zero remorse, says he'd do it again. Feels like if anyone ever deserved it, it's him. I'm fine with execution here, maybe more than fine.

2. Same crime as 1, but the evidence is merely very strong rather than a proud confession -- DNA, motive, no alibi, but he maintains innocence. Now I'm against it, even though statistically he's almost certainly guilty. The "what if we're wrong" feeling completely swamps everything. But here's the thing: I'm fine with life without parole for this guy, and a wrongful life sentence is also irreversible in practice if discovered too late. Why does death feel so different?

3. Suppose it were proven tomorrow (it won't be, but suppose) that each execution deters eight murders. I'm still against it in case 2, and I feel weird about being fine with it even in case 1 -- like the deterrence fact shouldn't get to do that much work. Eight innocent lives saved versus one guilty life taken and I still balk. That seems almost irrational to me but it's what I feel.

4. A mob boss orders a hit and never touches a weapon. A desperate nineteen-year-old pulls the trigger for money. If only one of them could be executed, I want it to be the boss. But the boss is arguably more useful alive (testimony, flipping on others) and the kid is more dangerous in a hands-on sense. My gut doesn't care.

5. Someone who commits an atrocity at 22, spends 30 years on death row, becomes genuinely gentle, mentors younger inmates, clearly a different person. Executing the 52-year-old feels like executing someone who isn't the murderer in some way I can't articulate. But I don't feel this at all about keeping him in prison forever.

6. Public execution of a definitely-guilty serial killer, crowds watching: revolting to me, barbaric. Same execution behind closed doors: I'm uneasy but not revolted. Nothing about justice changed, just the optics, and yet.

Is there a principle (or two principles fighting) that captures most of this? The tension I keep circling is between 1/2 and 5: in case 2 the *possibility* he's a different man than the verdict says blocks execution, but in case 5 the *certainty* he's a different man than he was apparently doesn't. And between 3 and everything else: I clearly have some deontological streak, but I'm not a clean retributivist either, because pure "he deserves it" doesn't explain why 5 and 6 hit me the way they do. If the answer is that some of these intuitions have to be thrown out to get anything coherent, tell me which ones are cheapest to drop. Also: what does your best reconstruction of my view predict for a case I haven't listed -- say, execution of someone guilty of treason-espionage that got no one killed, or a plea bargain where death is taken off the table in exchange for information that saves a kidnapping victim?
