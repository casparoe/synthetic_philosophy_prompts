I'm putting together a unit on speech acts and truth for my phil of mind course, and I've run into a classification problem I can't settle, so let me describe the thing and you tell me if it has a name.

I use a chatbot to help me prep, and it does something very specific and repeatable. I'll ask it for the source of a claim, and it gives me a citation that looks exactly right -- right author, plausible journal, year, even page numbers -- and the paper simply does not exist. When I tell it the paper doesn't exist, it doesn't get defensive or double down, it just apologizes and hands me a different citation, which also doesn't exist. Or I'll be discussing an argument and it will attribute a view to a philosopher, slightly wrong, but always wrong in the direction that makes it a smoother fit for whatever we were just talking about. The errors aren't random noise. They're shaped by what would be a fitting, natural, helpful-seeming thing to say at that point in the conversation.

Now, my first instinct was Frankfurt. The output is governed by something other than concern for how things actually are, which is his whole account of bullshit -- and his account famously doesn't require the output to be false, which fits, since sometimes the citation is real. But when I actually press on it, the classification starts to wobble. Frankfurt's bullshitter is *indifferent* to the truth, and indifference is still an attitude -- it's something you have toward a truth-value you could in principle have tracked. It's not obvious the system has any attitude toward truth at all, so even "indifference" seems to over-describe it. "Lying" is clearly out, since there's no intent to deceive. And "error" seems to under-describe it, because ordinary error is what a truth-tracking process does when it misfires, and I'm not sure there's a truth-tracking process here to misfire in the first place. Confabulation is the nearest clinical term, but that's usually reserved for minds filling in gaps in their own memory or experience, and this doesn't seem to be that either.

So: does this specific configuration have an established name that I'm missing, and if so where does the literature draw its edges? If not, I want more than a coined label -- I want the carving. What exactly distinguishes this from bullshit proper, from confabulation, and from honest mistake, and does the distinction turn on the system's lacking attitudes or on something about the shape of the outputs? Bonus wrinkle you should address: when the invented citation happens to correspond to a real paper, is it the same phenomenon or a different one? A bare name wont satisfy me here.
