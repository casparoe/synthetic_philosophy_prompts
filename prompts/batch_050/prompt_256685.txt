I work in hospital admin and I've now watched the same odd thing happen twice after incidents with automated kit. First time, a triage algorithm silently deprioritized a patient who then deteriorated; the review found the software was validated, the nurse on shift followed protocol exactly, nobody was negligent. Second time, a pharmacy dispensing robot grabbed the wrong stock after a supplier changed packaging; again, every human in the chain had done what they were supposed to do. Here's the weird part: both times, the person who happened to be standing closest to the machine insisted it was their fault. The nurse said she should have "just known" the patient looked sicker than the screen said. The pharmacy tech actually offered to resign, and management accepted it, and I could feel the whole department relax, like the incident finally had an ending.

So my question: is there an established name for this? I know the literature talks about "responsibility gaps" when automation harms someone, but that seems to be about blame *disappearing* -- here blame doesn't disappear, it lands, just on someone who doesn't deserve it and who actively volunteered for it. And it's not scapegoating either, because nobody accused them; they accused themselves, and everyone else quietly went along with it because a self-appointed culprit closes the case. Does this specific configuration -- voluntary absorption of unowned blame, gratefully accepted by bystanders -- have a name of its own? If not, coin one, define it, and give me some boundary cases (e.g. does the captain going down with the ship count? does a CEO resigning over a scandal they didn't cause count?).
