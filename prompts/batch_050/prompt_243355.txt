I'm taking a phil of mind / AI seminar this term and we keep circling around the question of whether neural networks literally have representations. Like, when interpretability people find a "feature" in a transformer that fires on Golden Gate Bridge stuff, or when probing classifiers recover tense or syntactic structure from hidden states, is something genuinely being discovered inside the network, or are we just finding compressions that are convenient for us and calling them representations? It feels like this debate has been stuck for years. There's a camp that says the representations are real because you can intervene on them and change model behavior predictably (steering vectors, activation patching, that kind of thing), so they earn their keep causally the same way a brain state would. There's a camp that says anything high-dimensional enough contains decodable info about basically everything, so finding structure is cheap and proves nothing -- a random projector can be "probed" too. And there's a third murmuring that the question is only answerable relative to a choice of abstraction, so it's not a factual question at all.

My hypothesis, which I keep going back and forth on, is that this is secretly a disagreement about what a representation is (does it need to be used by the system itself, or is decodability-plus-causal-efficacy enough?) dressed up as a disagreement about what's inside the model. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real empirical question here that better interpretability tools could actually settle. So: diagnose the debate for me. Which of these is it -- verbal, substantive, or sides answering different questions under one label? What does each camp have to say the other camp is missing? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a sharper definition, a specific kind of experiment or intervention result, a formal criterion? Or is this one of those debates where nothing could end it, and if so why not? Don't just survey the positions, I want the actual diagnosis with reasons. Also if it helps, our prof keeps saying the same fight happened over connectionist nets in the 80s and 90s and nothing got resolved then either, so feel free to say whether this is literally the same debate in new clothes or whether modern interpretability work changed the structure of it.
