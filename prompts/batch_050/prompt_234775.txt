I am a senior helping lead our school’s philosophy club, and I have twenty minutes to teach Bernard Williams’s integrity objection, especially the idea that someone who stops to ask whether impartial morality permits rescuing a loved one may be having one thought too many. The other students know basic act utilitarianism from trolley-style cases, but they have not studied Williams, Kantian ethics, or moral psychology.

Plan the explanation rather than giving the explanation itself. Begin your first sentence with the central teaching strategy you would use, then give the plan, with no preamble and no restatement of this request.

Specify the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what each step depends on: impartial moral calculation, the deliberative standpoint, ground projects, alienation from one’s commitments, and the difference between justifying an action and explaining how a person should reach it. Make a definite interpretive choice about whether Williams is primarily criticizing the action recommended by impartial theory, the reasoning it asks people to perform, or the theory’s conception of the self; do not simply list the possible readings.

Create one original school-based example to carry the session. It must involve a loved one and a stranger in comparable danger, allow only one to be helped, avoid professional duties and extreme numbers, and make the agent’s moment of hesitation—not the choice of the loved one—the philosophical problem. Explain why this example is better for my audience than the familiar drowning-spouse case.

Map the misconceptions I should expect, including that Williams is licensing selfishness, rejecting moral thought altogether, treating every personal preference as an integrity project, equating integrity with moral purity, and objecting only to utilitarianism. For each misconception, state exactly where in the sequence I should prevent or correct it. End with one question that would reveal whether the students understand the objection rather than merely sympathize with favoritism, and identify what I should deliberately leave out of a twenty-minute session.
