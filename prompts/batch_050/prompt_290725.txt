So I help run a small rationality-adjacent reading group (we started as an ACX meetup, now we're basically a philosphy discussion group that occasionally remembers its origins). We're doing a few sessions on trolley problems and I got volunteered to run the one on the doctrine of double effect. Problem: everyone in this group has seen the switch/footbridge pair approximately one million times. If I just present cases and ask for verdicts, people will pattern-match to "ah, this is where I'm supposed to say intended vs merely foreseen" and recite the distinction like a catechism without it ever touching their actual moral machinery. I want an activity where the DDE-shaped tension shows up in what they *do*, not in what they say afterwards.

So: design me a runnable activity for about 10-12 people, one 90-minute session, that makes participants feel the force of the doctrine of double effect -- specifically the bite of the claim that it can be permissible to bring about a harm as a foreseen side effect but impermissible to bring about the *same* harm as a means, even holding outcomes fixed. Ideally the structure is something like: participants face a sequence of staged choices or cases (could be role-play, could be a moderated "you are the hospital administrator / general / self-driving car ethics board" setup, you pick), and the cases are quietly paired so that most people's verdicts end up inconsistent unless they start tracking the intend/foresee structure themselves -- and then mid-session the pairing gets revealed and they have to figure out what distinction, if any, their own judgments were actually tracking. No lecture up front, and nobody should hear the phrase "double effect" until the debrief.

Give me: the actual cases or scenario setups (with the pairings made explicit to me, the facilitator), rules/roles/timeline, a debrief that maps what happened onto the four-ish conditions DDE is usually taken to require (that the action itself not be bad, that the harm not be intended as end or means, proportionality, etc.), what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (e.g. someone who bit the utilitarian bullet on everything and sees no tension at all), and an honest note on what the activity *can't* reproduce -- my guess is that DDE is about the agent's actual intention and you can't observe or police a participant's real intention, only their stated verdicts, but maybe you see a deeper mismatch. Also, if you think the premise is off -- like if pairing cases this way doesn't actually isolate intend/foresee but smuggles in something else (personal force, omission/commission, whatever), or the activity as I've sketched it can't teach DDE -- tell me that straight rather than politely designing around it. I'd rather get told the plan is confused than run a slick-looking activity that teaches the wrong lesson.
