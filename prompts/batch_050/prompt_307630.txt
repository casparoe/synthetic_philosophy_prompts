Ok so I'm in a moral philosophy seminar this semester and we just finished the unit on Scanlon-style contractualism, and there's a case type I keep running into that I can't find a term for and it's bugging me.

The setup: contractualism says an act is wrong if some individual could reasonably reject the principle allowing it, so wrongness is supposed to bottom out in a particular person's complaint. Fine. But consider this kind of case. A city could route its new power lines one of two ways. Route A causes a once-in-a-decade blackout risk for one specific neighborhood, and when it hits, one elderly resident who depends on powered medical equipment will die. Route B instead shaves two minutes off every day of free time, forever, for about three million commuters, by making transfers slightly worse. Nobody under route B has anything close to the dying resident's complaint -- a few lost minutes each. Under the "strongest individual complaint wins" logic, route B looks required. And yet multiplying those two minutes across millions of people and decades, the total loss under B dwarfs the loss under A, and I can't shake the feeling that B is the wrong choice even though no single commuter could veto it.

I know this is somewhere in the neighborhood of the debate about whether numbers should count, and of cases like the torture-versus-many-headaches thing, but those are usually set up as clashes between a huge harm and tiny harms where the question is whether tiny harms can ever add up. My case feels different: here the tiny harms plainly DO add up to something enormous, and the puzzle is whether that enormous total can make an act wrong when it belongs to no one -- like, wrongness with no rejector attached.

Does this specific configuration have a name? Is there literature treating "the complaint is real but distributed so thinly no individual can register it" as its own thing, distinct from the standard aggregation debate? If there's no established term, what would you call it, and where would you draw its boundaries -- e.g., does it still count if the distributed losses are to future people who don't exist yet? I want the carving, not just a label, so please say what it isn't as well as what it is.
