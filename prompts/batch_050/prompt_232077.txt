Next month I am giving a colloquium talk in a philosophy of physics department, defending the following thesis: the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, in the sharpened form popularized by the Andromeda scenario (two observers walking past each other on Earth whose simultaneity slices through that galaxy differ on whether some far-off decision has already been made), does not by itself refute presentism or the growing block, but it does establish something nearly as strong: any theory of objective becoming must single out a preferred foliation of spacetime, and no candidate foliation can carry the metaphysical weight that becoming-theorists need it to carry. So becoming views are not logically refuted but priced out of the market — surviving only at the cost of positing a physically undetectable structure doing no work except saving the metaphysics. My talk concedes up front that the original Rietdijk–Putnam reasoning, read as a straight proof of eternalism, is vulnerable to Stein's reply that becoming can be defined locally and relationally rather than globally; my claim is that this concession doesn't help the becoming-theorist, because a patchwork of local becomings with no global stitching is not the view anyone who defends becoming actually wants.

The audience is about fifteen people. Four are general relativists, one of whom works on the initial-value formulation and is instinctively suspicious of any argument that treats simultaneity slices as metaphysically load-bearing. There is a growing-block theorist who has published on relativistic becoming and knows the Stein response and its later refinements inside out. There is a quiet conventionalist about simultaneity who thinks the entire debate evaporates once you take seriously that Einstein synchronization is not forced. There is a presentist who will grant most of the physics but resist the move from frame-relative determinateness to frame-relative reality. And there are graduate students who will ask the naive versions of the hard questions. Nobody in the room is a committed eternalist; the prevailing attitude is that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument proves either too much or nothing.

Prepare me for the question period. I want a prep sheet, not a defense: the objections I will actually get from this audience, in roughly the order they will come, each phrased the way that particular person would phrase it — the relativist's version will involve Cauchy surfaces and well-posedness, the conventionalist's will involve the freedom in the simultaneity relation, the growing-blocker's will involve taking Stein's local becoming seriously rather than as a reductio. Order the objections by how much damage they do to my conditional thesis specifically, not to eternalism in general, and flag which of the objections I am expecting — Stein's relativized becoming, the charge that I conflate determinateness with determinism, the cosmological preferred-frame reply, the conventionalist dissolution — I have correctly sized and which I have over- or under-estimated. For each objection give me a reply I can deliver in under two minutes, a note on where that reply is softest, and what I should concede immediately rather than fight over. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks, and tell me honestly whether my position has a clean answer to it.

One discipline I want enforced throughout: separate what is settled — the mathematics of the relativity of simultaneity, what special relativity does and does not commit anyone to — from what is genuinely contested among philosophers of physics who work on this material. Do not let me go into the room presenting a contested metaphysical reading as though it were established physics; if my own talk does that anywhere, tell me where.
