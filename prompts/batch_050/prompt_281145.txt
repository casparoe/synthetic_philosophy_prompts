I am a retired experimental physicist who has spent the past two years reading philosophy in earnest, and before I inflict my views on the reading group I have joined, I would like a consistency check on my commitments concerning causation. Here is where I have landed.

First, I take Hume to have been right about the epistemology and psychology of the matter: we never perceive a necessary connection between events, only succession, contiguity, and constant conjunction, and the felt necessity we attach to causal sequences is a projection of habit — of the mind's determination to pass from the idea of one event to the idea of the other.

Second, I have no patience for the so-called New Hume: the suggestion that Hume privately believed in mind-independent causal powers, merely hidden from us, strikes me as an attempt to rescue his reputation by making him say the opposite of what he argued.

Third, as a scientist I cannot accept that laws of nature are mere regularities. Laws support counterfactuals and explain their instances; accidental generalizations do neither, and a philosophy of science that cannot mark that difference has missed the point of the enterprise I spent my career in.

Fourth, on singular causation I am persuaded that something in the family of counterfactual analyses — Lewis's, roughly — succeeds where regularity accounts fail, since it handles the cases of preemption and redundant backup where regularity theories fall silent or misjudge.

Fifth, in the laboratory the way one actually establishes a causal claim is by intervention: manipulate the putative cause under controlled conditions and observe whether the effect follows. This procedure seems to me to require no antecedent grasp of laws and certainly none of necessary connections.

My question is whether this set hangs together. Please audit it. Identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; order the genuine tensions by severity; and for each, state precisely what I would have to give up or qualify to resolve it. Where an apparent tension dissolves on inspection, say briefly why rather than flagging everything indiscriminately. Conclude with the single revision that buys the most consistency at the lowest cost to the overall position. I am not asking to be told what Hume said; I am asking what my holding these five theses commits me to. Please keep the entire reply to 700 words at most.
