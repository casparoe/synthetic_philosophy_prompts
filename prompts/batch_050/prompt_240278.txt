His position: Mises and Hayek were objecting to 1930s pencil-and-paper planning, and with modern compute and machine learning a planning authority could just solve Lange's system for real, so the debate is basically settled in the socialists' favour and only ideology stops it. Mine: that reading confuses a computational problem with an epistemic and institutional one, and no amount of GPU-hours conjures the relevant prices into existence. We've promised to write up whatever we produce, so I'd like you to draft the joint statement we should be able to sign.

For each crux, write down a case, observation or possible future result we both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance — e.g., what would each of us have to concede if a planning algorithm demonstrably matched a comparable market economy over a decade, and what counts as "demonstrably" and "comparable"?
