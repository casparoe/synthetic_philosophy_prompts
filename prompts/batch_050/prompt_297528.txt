I'm in a metaphysics seminar on laws of nature and we've been asked to write up where we stand before the prof pokes holes in it, so here's my package:

(1) I'm basically a Humean — no necessary connections in nature, everything supervenes on the arrangement of local qualities across spacetime, and laws are just the best-system summary of that mosaic (simplicity vs strength, Lewis style).

(2) I still think laws genuinely explain particular events — like, the law of gravitation explains why the planets orbit the way they do, and that's a real explanation, not just a redescription.

(3) I think the fundamental properties are categorical, not powers or dispositions — dispositional essentialism strikes me as spooky.

(4) I believe in objective chances, and I think rational credence should track them via something like the Principal Principle.

(5) I think the special sciences have their own genuine laws (econ, biology etc.) that aren't just placeholders until physics catches up.

(6) I take counterfactuals seriously — "if the glass had been struck it would have shattered" is made true by the laws plus the actual facts.

Does this hang together? Which pairs are actually in tension and which just look like they are? For the real ones, rank them by how bad the damage is and say what the cheapest fix would be — and don't tell me to just become a dispositionalist across the board, I want to know what each option costs.
