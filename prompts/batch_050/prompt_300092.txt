I've been turning something over since a conversation at a dinner party a few weeks ago, where a friend who composes music and a friend who develops recipes for a restaurant discovered they were describing the same working method. Neither of them starts from a blank page. The composer takes an existing piece or motif and then slides things around: slow it down, move it to a minor key, invert the intervals, stretch one phrase, swap the instrumentation. The chef does the identical thing: take a known dish, raise the acidity, change the cooking fat, replace one aromatic with another, borrow a technique from a different cuisine. Both insisted that the creative moment was not the initial idea -- that part is always borrowed -- but the discovery of which things about the existing artifact can be altered at all, and the judgment of which alteration produces something genuinely new rather than merely a slightly worse copy. The composer added something that stuck with me: some of the things you can change are like dials with a continuous range (tempo, saltiness), while others are like slots you swap discrete things into (instrument, protein), and the most interesting creative leaps seem to happen when someone realizes that something everyone treated as fixed -- the time signature, the fact that dessert is sweet -- was actually a dial all along.

Once I started looking for this pattern I saw it everywhere, and now I'm frustrated that I don't have a name for it. An engineer I work with designs by taking last year's model and perturbing one parameter at a time. A novelist friend says her plots come from asking "what if the setting of this story I love were transposed into that other genre." Even in my own field, accounting, the clever work is rarely inventing a new structure from nothing; it's recognizing that some number everyone treated as a constant could be treated as a variable. I know there are adjacent ideas -- people talk about "standing on the shoulders of giants," about remix culture, about incremental versus radical innovation -- but all of those feel either too loose or specifically about the recombination of two things, whereas what I'm describing is a single existing thing plus a systematic exploration of the ways it could have been otherwise. I've also seen the phrase "combinatorial creativity," but that seems to be about combining elements, not about discovering and adjusting the adjustable dimensions of one exemplar.

So my question is threefold. First, does this phenomenon -- creativity as the identification and adjustment of the variable parameters of an existing artifact, with the parameters themselves being part of what gets discovered -- have an established name in philosophy, cognitive science, or the study of creativity? Second, if there are several nearby concepts, how does this specific one differ from each of them, and which fits best, particularly with respect to the continuous-dial versus discrete-slot distinction and the idea of turning apparent constants into variables? Third, if no established term quite captures it, I'd rather you coined and defined one, with a couple of boundary cases showing what would and wouldn't count, than just hand me the nearest miss.
