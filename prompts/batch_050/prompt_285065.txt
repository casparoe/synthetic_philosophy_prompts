I am a retired experimental chemist who has recently begun formal study in philosophy, and I am preparing a one-semester independent study on Kripke’s rule-following problem. Please draft a research proposal, no more than 900 words, for a paper provisionally titled Rules, Instruments, and the Standard of Correctness. The proposal should develop a half-formed idea of mine: the rule-following puzzle is usually presented through examples such as addition, but measurement practice may offer a more revealing case. A laboratory protocol instructs researchers to perform the same measurement under the same conditions, even though conditions never exactly recur. Instruments drift, samples degrade, background noise changes, and any finite record of previous applications is compatible with indefinitely many future continuations. Yet laboratories routinely distinguish a genuine replication from an artifact, and a correction of technique from a change in what is being measured, by appealing to calibration histories, error models, robustness across independent instruments, and shared standards of assessment.

The central question should be precise: do these scientific practices merely supply further finite dispositions and social approvals, leaving the Kripkensteinian challenge untouched, or do they expose a limitation in the way that challenge is usually framed? My conjecture is that correctness in measurement may be partly constituted by a rule’s role in preserving comparability across an open-ended network of results, rather than by an inner mental act, an exhaustive specification of conditions, or the past behavior of an individual. The proposal should explain why familiar responses to the rule-following puzzle—dispositionalist, communitarian, primitivist, and quietist—do not by themselves settle this question. It should then describe the philosophical method to be used, including analysis of a small number of invented but realistic measurement scenarios, and explain why this approach is preferable to either a general survey of the rule-following literature or an empirical study of actual laboratories.

Please state what the paper would argue under its main possible outcomes, what would count as the project failing, and how the project can realistically be completed in one semester as a paper of roughly 6,000 words. Include an honest assessment of the largest conceptual or scope-related risk and how the project would be narrowed if necessary. Use concise section headings. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I will add any references later.
