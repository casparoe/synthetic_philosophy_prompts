Exam question: "Nyāya recognizes four pramāṇas: pratyakṣa (perception), anumāna (inference), upamāna (comparison), and śabda (testimony). Explain what distinguishes each pramāṇa from the others, and discuss why the Naiyāyikas insist that upamāna and śabda are independent means of knowledge rather than special cases of anumāna. Illustrate your discussion of upamāna with the standard example."

Student's answer: "In Nyāya, a pramāṇa is an instrument of valid knowledge (pramā), and the school recognizes exactly four such instruments. Pratyakṣa, perception, is knowledge produced through contact between a sense faculty and its object; it is non-deviating and definite, and it requires no other knowledge as its instrument. Anumāna, inference, is knowledge of something not presently perceived, arrived at by means of a mark (liṅga) that stands in a relation of invariable concomitance (vyāpti) with the property to be inferred, as when one infers the presence of fire on a distant hill from the smoke one sees rising from it. All inference in Nyāya takes the form of a five-membered syllogism: the statement of the thesis (pratijñā), the reason (hetu), the example with the concomitance (udāharaṇa), the application to the case at hand (upanaya), and the conclusion (nigamana). Upamāna, comparison, is illustrated by the standard example: a man who has never seen a gavaya is told by a forester that a gavaya is an animal resembling a cow; when he later encounters such an animal in the forest, he recognizes it as a gavaya. This knowledge cannot be reduced to inference, because the man has never perceived gavayas together with any mark from which he could infer that this animal bears that name; his knowledge arises directly from the cognition of similarity to the cow he already knows. Śabda, testimony, is knowledge derived from the utterance of a trustworthy person (āpta), and it likewise cannot be reduced to inference, since we do not infer the truth of what a speaker says from some perceived connection between words and facts; comprehension of reliable testimony is immediate. Nyāya's pluralism about the pramāṇas thus occupies a middle position between the Cārvāka view that perception alone is a valid means of knowledge and the Buddhist logicians, who, like the Naiyāyikas, accept four pramāṇas but construe perception in a more restrictive way."
