I am a novelist at work on a book whose central character is a statistician of unusually rigid convictions: he maintains that, given any body of evidence, there is exactly one probability distribution a rational agent is permitted to hold, and that when the evidence is thin or absent, the maximum entropy principle supplies it. The dramatic engine of the novel is that this conviction slowly comes apart, and I need to know whether it deserves to. So I am asking you for something I suspect you will resist giving, but I want it anyway: your actual credence -- a number -- that objective Bayesianism in this strong form is correct, that is, that there exist rationally mandated priors such that two agents with the same total evidence are rationally required to assign the same probabilities. Not 'it is controversial' and not a survey of who thinks what; a number, and the reasoning that produces it.

What I really need, for the architecture of the book, is the decomposition. Break the question into the sub-questions your credence depends on and put a rough probability on each: for instance, whether the principle of indifference and its maximum entropy descendant can survive Bertrand-style reparameterization objections, or whether those objections show that 'uninformative' is not a well-defined property of a distribution; whether invariance-based fixes or appeals to a privileged parameterization actually settle the matter or merely relocate it; whether, even granting a uniquely rational prior exists, it could ever be identified by finite agents like us, or whether the position collapses into an ideal that licenses nothing in practice; and whether the word 'rational' has enough determinate content here for the question to have an answer at all. Tell me how these combine into your final number and which single sub-question it is most sensitive to -- which one, if resolved against you, would move your credence the farthest. For calibration: my own instinct sits around 0.25, while a friend who reads far more formal epistemology than I do puts it at 0.65. If you can, locate where our disagreement most plausibly lives.

Finally, since my character's downfall will turn on whichever plank is genuinely the weakest, I would rather not be misled by false poise: please say how confident you are in the analysis as a whole, and single out the specific point in your reasoning where you are least sure of yourself -- the step you would strike first if forced to retract something.
