A colleague and I have a standing disagreement about the prospects of "prosaic" AI alignment, and I would like your help locating where it actually lives. By prosaic alignment I mean, roughly, the thesis that the most capable AI systems deployable in the coming decade can be kept aligned well enough to avoid catastrophe attributable to misalignment, using methods that are recognizably extensions of techniques we already have — preference-based fine-tuning from human or AI feedback, scalable oversight schemes such as debate and recursive reward modeling, current-style mechanistic interpretability, behavioral evaluations and red-teaming, and incremental improvements of these — without requiring a fundamentally new theoretical insight into why learned optimizers develop the objectives they do.

Here is the proposition I want your credence on, stated as precisely as I can manage: conditional on at least one lab fielding a system by 2035 that substantially exceeds human expert performance across most economically relevant cognitive tasks, the probability is high that such systems will be aligned, in the operational sense that no catastrophe causing a large loss of human life occurs that is properly attributable to misalignment rather than to misuse, accident, or ordinary engineering failure. My credence is about 0.35; my colleague's is about 0.8.

Please give me your own credence, with the reasoning forced into the open, in the following sections:

- **My credence** (one sentence): your number for the proposition as stated.
- **Decomposition** (roughly 300 words): the sub-questions your number depends on, each with its own rough probability and one or two lines of justification. At minimum I would expect terms for: the probability that feedback-based training objectives remain faithful proxies for intended behavior at capabilities well beyond the evaluators; the probability that deceptive or otherwise misaligned internal objectives, if they arise, are detectable by evaluation and interpretability methods of the current kind; the probability that competitive and commercial pressures still permit labs to act on warning signs; and whatever additional terms you think the decomposition requires.
- **Combination** (roughly 100 words): how these terms combine into your headline number, including which are independent and which are not, and how you handled that.
- **Where the disagreement lives** (roughly 150 words): given two people at 0.35 and 0.8, which sub-questions most plausibly separate them, and which single sub-question would have to move most to bring one of them near the other.
- **What would move me** (roughly 100 words): for the term you are least confident in, what evidence — empirical results, deployment incidents, research findings — available before 2035 would shift it, and in which direction.
