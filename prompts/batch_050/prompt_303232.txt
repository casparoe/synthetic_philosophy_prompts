okay so i need a wedding toast and i need it by saturday, help

the situation: my best friend is marrying an economic historian, which i did not know was a job until i met him. here's the relevant thing. they met because she got on the wrong bus in Lisbon, ended up at some monastery neither of them meant to go to, and he was there because his conference panel got cancelled. pure fluke, neither of them was supposed to be there.

for the last six years they have had a running argument about this that they WILL NOT drop. he's deep into path dependence stuff (his whole dissertation was about lock-in, the QWERTY keyboard is his favorite party trick, he has opinions about VHS vs betamax) and his position is: they'd have met anyway, somewhere, because people who fit like they do get pulled toward the same places, and the bus is just the delivery mechanism. she's a novelist and her position is: the bus is the whole story, if she'd checked the number she'd have married someone else, and she thinks his version is basically fatalism in a tweed jacket. they do this bit at dinner parties. half the guests at the wedding will be waiting to see whose side the toast takes.

here's what i want: a toast, like 4-5 minutes out loud, that actually engages the argument instead of dodging it with "who's to say, what matters is they're happy" mush. BUT the resolution has to be earned by the idea itself, not just asserted. my one thought (steal or ignore, up to you) is that path dependence isn't just about how you got somewhere, it's about how the thing you've built since then makes leaving unthinkable -- increasing returns, switching costs, the whole deal -- and that marriage is basically two people choosing their own lock-in on purpose, which maybe splits the difference between them. if there's a better way to land it, do that instead.

constraints: audience is mixed, like a third of the room are his history colleagues who will catch a QWERTY joke and appreciate precision, the rest are her family who will not and should not need to. so the ideas have to come through in plain language, no jargon dropped unexplained, and no name-dropping economists. also: it can't say "you were destined for each other" (that's conceding to him AND it's corny) and it can't say "it was all just luck" (that's conceding to her and makes marriage sound like a coin flip). it needs to be funny but the funny should come from the argument itself, not from embarrassing stories about them. end on something that gets people to raise a glass.
