Is there a name for a failure mode I keep running into, or do I have to make one up? I work in tech and I keep seeing systems fail people not by pushing back on what the person wants, but by slowly changing what the person wants, and I can't figure out what to call it.

Some concrete cases. A friend joined a company where her bonus was tied to one user-engagement number. Nobody ever told her engagement was what mattered, nobody lied to her about the product, every incentive was right there in the contract. Two years later she talks about engagement the way other people talk about their life's work, and she'll insist -- sincerely, I think -- that she always cared about it. Another: a guy I know started posting short videos as a hobby, and within a year his taste in what makes a video "good" had completely reshaped itself around what the recommendation system surfaces. He's not pretending. His actual aesthetic judgment moved. And honestly I caught a mild version in myself with a fitness watch: after six months I cared about closing the little rings more than about being healthy, and if you'd asked me mid-drift I would have said my values hadn't changed at all, just that I'd gotten more disciplined.

The pattern I want named: no deception at any step, no argument against the person's values, no single moment you can point to as "the manipulation." Each individual nudge or reward is small, visible, and honestly kind of reasonable. But the rewards compound inside the person, so the thing that drifts is the valuer, not the situation. And afterwards the person is a bad witness, because from the inside it felt like growth or maturing, not like being moved. So the failure is basically invisible both to the person it happened to and to anyone auditing the system for bad behavior, because the system never did anything wrong at any particular step.

I know this is in the neighborhood of some existing concepts but I don't think any of them quite fit, and I want to be told exactly where they stop fitting. Goodhart's law is about the measure going bad once you optimize for it -- but in my cases the measure is fine, it's the person who went bad, or at least went elsewhere. Adaptive preference formation (the sour grapes thing) seems to be about lowering your wants to match what you can't have -- but here nothing was taken away, options were added. And "manipulation" or "brainwashing" all seem to require someone covertly pulling strings, whereas the engagement dashboard isn't covert and arguably isn't even trying to change her. Maybe some of these do fit and I'm wrong, in which case tell me why.

So: is there an established name for this? If it's a known thing, what is it called and how is it usually distinguished from ordinary influence and from Goodhart? And if it genuinely has no name, coin one, define it tightly enough that I can tell the difference between this failure mode and just, like, changing your mind over time, and give me a couple of boundary cases -- cases that almost count but don't, and why.
