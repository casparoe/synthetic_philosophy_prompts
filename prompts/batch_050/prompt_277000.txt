I'm a junior taking a seminar on pain and well-being, and our department is hosting a philosopher who defends the view that pain asymbolia patients—who report feeling pain but claim it no longer bothers them—do not actually suffer, because what makes pain bad is not its phenomenal character but the subject's occurrent aversion or caring toward the sensation. Draft exactly eight interview questions for our student-run philosophy podcast, as a numbered list with no introduction or conclusion. The questions should press where the position is actually pressured: at least one on the tension between taking asymbolic patients at their word (they still call it pain, sometimes even still call it unpleasant in a detached way) versus treating their reports as evidence that phenomenology and badness come apart; at least one on morphine and cingulotomy cases, which look similar superficially but might tell a different story; at least one on whether behavioral withdrawal and guarding in asymbolia patients constitutes a kind of unavowed aversion that would undermine the whole argument; and at least one on the uncomfortable implication for organisms—infants, animals, future AI systems—whose capacity for reflective caring is unclear. Each item should be one or two sentences and include a follow-up keyed to the reply the guest is most likely to give.
