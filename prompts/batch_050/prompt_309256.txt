I've been asked to circulate a one-page statement of where I stand on what kind of thing a large language model is, ahead of a reading group, and I'd like a consistency audit before I send it. My commitments: (1) An LLM is best understood as a simulator rather than an agent: beliefs, goals, and personalities belong to the characters it instantiates, not to the model itself. (2) Post-training does not turn the simulator into an agent; it adjusts which character gets played by default. (3) Consequently, talk of deceptive alignment — the model itself concealing its true objectives until deployment — is a category error, and worrying about it rests on a confusion. (4) Nonetheless, deception and scheming evaluations on frontier models are worthwhile and can reveal genuine deployment risks. (5) The assistant persona produced by post-training is a fairly stable character, stable enough that it is legitimate to talk about what the assistant wants and to do persona-level interpretability. (6) I accept the so-called waluigi effect: eliciting a persona makes its inversion easier to summon, so any character a simulator plays brings its opposite closer to the surface. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? Please rank any tensions by severity, state for each what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most — without assuming in advance which commitment I am least attached to.
