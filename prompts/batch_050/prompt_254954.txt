So I'm on my town's "commemorations review committee" (volunteer thing, long story, lots of very long meetings) and we have a decision coming up that I think is going to split us, and I want to think it through in terms of our own precedents before the meeting. Here's what we've done so far, over the last four years:

We voted to remove the statue of a 19th-century merchant in front of the public library, because basically his whole fortune came from the slave trade and the library was founded with that money — the thing he was being honored for was inseparable from the harm. We kept the name of an early-20th-century chemist on the science building of the local college, even though she was an active eugenicist for a while, on the grounds that she's honored for discoveries that had nothing to do with that, and we added a contextualizing plaque. We removed a street name honoring a general who won a celebrated battle nearby but also ordered a massacre of indigenous villagers — the deciding factor, as I remember it, was that the street runs through a neighborhood with a large indigenous community who had been petitioning about it for years. And we kept the bust of a poet whose private letters are full of the ordinary prejudices of his time, mostly because nobody on the committee felt his views were unusual for the era or relevant to why he was honored.

The new case: a mid-20th-century public health official who more or less eradicated a serious disease in our region and genuinely saved thousands of lives, and that's exactly what he's commemorated for (a bust in the hospital, which is what we're being asked about). But it recently came out in a book about him that he also ran medical experiments on institutionalized orphans without consent, and — this is the part I think matters — it wasn't secret-and-normal, it was actually exposed and condemned in the press *at the time*, and he just weathered it. Predict how we should vote if we want to be consistent with our own track record, tell me which of our precedents is the closest analog and why, and be honest with me about whether our precedents actually settle this: if two different principles both fit our past votes but diverge here, lay out which principle we'd effectively be adopting with each possible verdict, and what kind of future case would force us to confront the choice between them.
