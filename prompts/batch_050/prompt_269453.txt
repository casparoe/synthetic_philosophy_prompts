It's clearly proving too much, because the conclusion is that I'm not an organism, and I feel like I am one. But each step looks okay to me, which is the annoying part.

(1) Something is an organism only if its parts have aligned evolutionary interests, meaning no part can replicate at the expense of the whole.
(2) Interests are only aligned when the parts are genetically identical and can only reproduce through a shared bottleneck, like the single-cell stage of development.
(3) My gut bacteria are genetically different from me, reproduce on their own, can outlive me, and sometimes actively harm me, so by (1)-(2) they aren't part of me. Fine so far, I'm happy to say the "holobiont" isn't one organism.
(4) But my own cells fail the same test. Cancer cells replicate at my expense. Mitochondria have their own DNA, inherited separately from the rest of me. Immune cells mutate and compete against each other clonally. Even bits of my own genome copy themselves around without doing anything for me.
(5) So my cells' interests aren't fully aligned either, they're just suppressed or managed most of the time.
(6) Therefore I'm not an organism, I'm a colony that usually keeps its members in line.
(7) And since the same holds for every multicellular thing, "organism" above the level of a single cell is just a convenient fiction.

The thing that really bugs me is step (4), because everything in it is just true, and (3) seemed like the obvious sensible move when I was reading about the microbiome.
