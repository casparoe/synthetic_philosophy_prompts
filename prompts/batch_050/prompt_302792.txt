ok so this started as a fight with my uncle at thanksgiving and now I'm worried I've accidentally disproved the entire concept of complicity. He said that buying factory farmed chicken makes me complicit in how the animals are treated, and I tried to argue him out of it, and honestly my argument works so well that it seems to prove nobody is ever complicit in anything big. here it is:

(1) You're complicit in something bad only if what you did made a difference — the bad thing would have been less bad, or less likely, if you hadn't done your part.

(2) One person buying one pack of chicken makes no difference. The store orders by the case, the farms slaughter by the thousands, and whether I buy it or walk away, the exact same number of chickens get raised and killed the exact same way.

(3) So buying the chicken doesn't make me complicit. (uncle defeated, or so I thought)

(4) But nothing in this argument is specific to chicken. It works for anything where the harm comes from a huge number of tiny contributions and no single one changes anything: one person's pickup truck vs climate change, one vote in a national election, one share of stock in a company with awful labor practices, one ticket to a dogfight that would happen anyway.

(5) So it should also work for this case my uncle brought up later to fight back (he says it's from some philosopher named Parfit): a thousand people each turn a dial one notch, where each notch adds so little pain to the victims that nobody could feel the difference from any single notch, but all the notches together leave the victims in agony. By my argument none of the thousand did anything wrong, since no individual notch made a difference anyone could notice.

(6) So either a thousand people can collectively torture someone without a single one of them being complicit (insane), or... I genuinely don't know what the other option is, because (1) and (2) both seem true and the rest just follows.

Where does this go wrong? If I drop (1) and say you can be complicit in stuff you had zero effect on, then my uncle gets to say I'm complicit in the chicken thing just for "participating in the system," and by that standard am I also complicit in warehouse working conditions because I ordered one thing off amazon in march? That version seems to make everyone complicit in everything, which also feels wrong. But if I keep (1) I'm stuck with the torturers. Which step should I actually give up, why did it look fine to me, and what's the cheapest way out that still lets me win (or at least tie) the argument with my uncle?
