For a civics and ethics class, I'm trying to work out what I actually believe about who has a right to leisure, based on my gut reactions to cases. Here's where I land: a nurse working 50-hour weeks obviously has a right to more rest, and so does a single parent doing full-time unpaid childcare, even though no employer owes them anything. A gig driver who wants 40 hours of work but can only get 25 seems wronged too, even though he's technically resting plenty. A wealthy heir who has never worked at all still seems entitled to leisure if he wants it -- I don't think laziness forfeits anything. But a healthy person on unemployment benefits who turns down reasonable job offers makes me uneasy in a way the heir doesn't, even though neither one works. Meanwhile, a salaried worker in a culture of extreme overwork who collapses from it seems like a victim of injustice, not just bad luck. And a retired person who's "earned" their rest feels different from the unemployed person, though I can't say why the years already worked should matter more than the years ahead. Is there a principle about what grounds the right to leisure -- desert, need, contribution, or just personhood -- that captures most of these verdicts? Which of my intuitions would such a principle force me to give up, and should I give it up or reject the principle?
