Bit of an odd request but here goes. I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind (my background is half phenomenology, half embodied cognition stuff) and next month I'm giving a brown-bag talk in my department defending the claim that Merleau-Ponty's notion of motor intentionality is still doing real work that representationalist accounts of action can't just absorb. My rough line: the Schneider case (the brain-injured patient Goldstein and Gelb studied, who can do habitual concrete movements but not abstract or pretend ones) shows that normal skilled coping runs on a kind of bodily directedness towards the world that isn't a matter of representing goals and computing means, and that when Merleau-Ponty talks about the body 'gearing into' its tasks he's pointing at something the standard belief-desire-plus-motor-control picture has no slot for. I'll also push his point that abstract movement isn't just concrete movement with extra cognition bolted on.

Here's the problem. My audience is mostly mainstream analytic philosophers of mind -- a couple of them proper representationalists who think 'motor intentionality' is either metaphor or a description of subpersonal motor control, and one who works on the cognitive neuroscience of action and will want to know what the actual explanatory payoff is supposed to be. There's also one enactivist-adjacent postdoc who I suspect will come at me from the other side and say I'm not being radical enough, and a few grad students who've read the Phenomenology of Perception chapter but nothing else.

Prepare my Q&A. I want: the objections I'll actually get, roughly ordered by how likely they are times how damaging, each phrased the way that person would put it (the represetationalists will translate everything into their vocabulary, so give me their versions not mine). For each one: what I should concede straight away, what I should hold, a two-minute reply, and a note on where the reply is weakest. I especially want to know how to handle the move where someone grants the phenomenology but says it's all just implementation detail, and the inevitable 'isn't this just knowing-how vs knowing-that?' question, which I find annoying but probably shouldn't say so. Also flag the question I'm quietly hoping nobody asks -- I think it's the one about whether motor intentionality is supposed to be intentional in the Brentano sense or whether we're just trading on the word, but you tell me if there's a worse one lurking. Don't give me a general defence of phenomenology, I need a prep sheet for this room specifically.
