I’ve spent far too long in LessWrong-adjacent consciousness arguments, and here’s the package I keep ending up with. Can you audit whether it hangs together, rather than giving me a general p-zombie survey?

1. Physicalism is probably true: once all physical facts are fixed, all phenomenal facts are fixed too.
2. Ideal conceivability is defeasible evidence for metaphysical possibility, not just a fact about what humans can picture.
3. A molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me without consciousness is not ideally conceivable. What people call zombie intuition usually imagines only a schema-zombie: a system matching all the third-person facts represented in their model, while leaving most physical facts unspecified.
4. Still, the conceivability of a schema-zombie counts as some evidence against physicalism.
5. If two systems have exactly the same relevant causal organisation, differences of substrate, spatial spread or runtime cannot by themselves make one conscious and the other not.
6. Yet for a sufficiently spread-out or slow system, there can be no fact of the matter whether it is one subject, many subjects or none, even once every physical and causal detail is fixed.
7. The hard problem remains genuine: structural descriptions may tell us where consciousness occurs, but not why there is experience at all.

Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look tense, and how severe is each? For the real problems, state the cheapest repair and its cost, then name the single revision that improves the set most. Please don’t assume I already know which bit should go. British English, please.
