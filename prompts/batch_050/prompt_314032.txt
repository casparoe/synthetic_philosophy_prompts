So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, but I read Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's Respuesta a sor Filotea de la Cruz (1691) for a bookclub — the letter where she defends her studying after a bishop more or less told her to quit it. Near the start she's explaining why she almost didn't reply at all, and she writes:

"aquellas cosas que no se pueden decir, es menester decir siquiera que no se pueden decir, para que se entienda que el callar no es no haber qué decir, sino no caber en las voces lo mucho que hay que decir."

My duolingo-tier gloss: when you can't say something, you still have to say *that* you can't say it, so people understand the silence isn't having nothing to say — it's that there's too much to say for words.

I wrote down my takes for the group and want a consistency check before I get jumped:

(a) This passage is the key to her whole tone: the humility act is strategic. She's telling the bishop her restraint is a choice and there's plenty she could say. So I read all the self-deprecation in the letter as irony, even the spots where she flat-out insists her modesty is genuine.

(b) Her real argument for women studying is equal natural capacity: God hands out talent without checking for sex, so barring women from learning is irrational and wastes what He distributed.

(c) The strongest part of the letter is where she argues that studying everything — logic, rhetoric, physics, the lot — makes you better at theology, so secular learning earns its keep by serving the highest science.

(d) In general I think instrumental defenses of education are always weaker than rights-based ones: if learning has to justify itself by being useful to something "higher," you've already half-conceded the learner doesn't matter for their own sake.

(e) The modern takeaway of the letter is basically epistemic justice: being barred from learning is a wrong to the person even if society measurably loses nothing.

Wich of these actually pull against each other, wich only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity, say what would have to give for each, and then tell me the single cheapest revision that cleans up the most. Also: is my reading of the quote doing more work than it can actually support?
