So I've been chewing on this for a while: when you love someone, is it *because* of things about them (they're funny, kind, beautiful, whatever), or does the love come first and the reasons come after? I gather philosophers split roughly into two camps here -- one side says love is a response to the beloved's valuable qualities, the other says love *confers* value and generates reasons rather than responding to them.

What I want is the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a genuinely good-faith adversarial collaboration. Concretely: a numbered list of claims both sides accept (stuff like: love is different from just liking or admiring; the "if you loved her for her qualities, you'd trade up to a better version" objection is a real problem *someone* has to answer; love seems to involve reasons for partiality that strangers don't have). Then a disagreements table where each side's position is worded the way that side itself would word it -- no strawmen. Then the cruxes: which specific thought experiments or questions would actually move one side if resolved (I'm thinking of cases like an exact-but-improved duplicate of your partner, your partner's personality changing drastically after an accident, whether love can be irrational the way beliefs can). For each crux, write down what each side predicts or concedes about it in advance. And if part of the fight turns out to be just terminological (e.g. different senses of "reasons for love"), say which part.
