Disjunctivists deny that move — they say indistinguishability to the subject doesn't mean the two states are the same kind of thing. Restate both sides of this as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually think in: observers as classifiers, discriminability, latent states vs observations, noise, decision boundaries. E.g., does "indistinguishable" just mean the output distributions overlap too much to separate, and does the disjunctivist position become a claim about the data-generating process rather than about anything in the observed signal? Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge to make fit, and what (if anything) has no expression in my framework at all. What I really want to know is whether the disjunctivist's insistence that the veridical case is a fundamentally different kind of state is something detection-theoretic vocabulary simply can't capture, or whether it's a distinction that makes no observable difference and so drops out of the model on purpose.
