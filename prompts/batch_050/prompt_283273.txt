I've been persuaded by a position in the aesthetics of games that I'll state in my own terms, since I want it tested rather than traced to any particular author. The position holds that games are artworks whose distinctive medium is human agency. A game designer sculpts a structured space of possible actions, obstacles, and goals, and the artwork is that possibility space itself, not any particular playthrough, not the code or components, and not the fiction layered on top. Playing is therefore both the performance and the appreciation of the work: the player enacts aesthetically evaluable feats of skill, nerve, ingenuity, or self-command and experiences them from the inside, a kind of aesthetic access that painting, film, and literature cannot offer. On this view, the right aesthetic evaluation of chess, poker, or a demanding video game concerns the shape of the agency it exacts, while narrative, graphics, and theme are inessential to the medium, borrowed goods from neighboring art forms. It follows that a game could be a masterpiece while being visually drab and narratively empty, and that a game could be narratively brilliant and aesthetically thin as a game.

What would count against this position? I want the defeaters, not a survey of rival aesthetics for its own sake. Consider several pressure points, and add any I've missed. First, cases: does the view misclassify anything? Spectatorship is a huge aesthetic practice around games, yet spectators do not exercise the agency on offer; is the view forced to say esports audiences miss the artwork entirely, or can it absorb spectating, and at what cost? What about games dominated by chance, where the sculpted agency is nearly nil: does the view predict that a pure dice game is artistically empty, and is that prediction a bullet or an embarrassment? What about games whose beauty seems to lie in watching rather than doing, or games people value precisely for what the view calls borrowed goods? Second, collapse and competition: does the position actually distinguish games from sport, improvisational theater, or ritual, or does it dissolve into a general aesthetics of performed action and lose its claim about a distinctive medium? Third, insulation: when defenders stretch agency to cover every interaction, including choosing where to look or whether to continue, has the view quietly made itself unfalsifiable, and if so, does that matter for a position in aesthetics the way it would in science? For each potential defeater, tell me what the world, our practices, or our intuitions would have to be like for it to land, what a committed defender of the position would concede it counts against them versus dismiss as beside the point, and rank the whole set from potentially fatal to merely awkward. I would find it most persuasive if you could point to something the position predicts that no plausible rival does, or else to something it cannot describe without remainder.

Write your answer as continuous prose, the way you would compose a long letter to me: no headings, no bullet points, no boldface, no tables.
