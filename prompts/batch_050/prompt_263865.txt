I am a novelist at work on a book in which Jean-Jacques Rousseau, by some unexplained contrivance, finds himself in the present day for the length of a single summer. The plot is largely settled, but three scenes depend on his reactions, and I cannot write them until I have a firmer sense of what he would actually think, as opposed to what would be convenient for my story. The three phenomena he encounters are: prestige streaming drama, which he watches alone on a laptop over the course of a weekend; short-form video feeds, in which strangers perform brief turns for an audience assembled by an algorithm rather than gathered in one place; and the modern literary festival, at which authors appear on stage before crowds who have come as much to look at them as to hear their books discussed.

Rousseau's attack on the theater in his letter to d'Alembert is the obvious lens: his claim that the stage flatters and intensifies the audience's existing passions rather than correcting them, his suspicion of actors as a class, his contempt for the appetite for diversion, and his preference for open-air civic festivals in which the people are participants rather than spectators. But I do not want to apply that letter mechanically. Some of what he condemned seems to fit these new forms even better than it fit the theater of his day, while other features cut against his argument in ways I am unsure how to weigh — most obviously the solitude of the laptop viewer, since so much of his case against the playhouse rested on what happens when a crowd gathers in the dark.

Please predict, for each of the three phenomena, whether he would condemn it, and on precisely what grounds, drawing on his actual views about theater, actors, festivals, and diversion. Commit to a definite verdict in each case: I am not asking for a survey of possible Rousseaus but for the one best supported by his writings, with the reasoning laid out. Where a prediction rests on a contested reading of him, or where he might plausibly surprise us — as he surprised his contemporaries by admiring particular plays while condemning the institution — say so explicitly and say which way the weight of the evidence lies.
