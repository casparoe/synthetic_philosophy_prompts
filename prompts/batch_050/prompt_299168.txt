Here's something I've been chewing on while prepping my value theory seminar, and I want an actual answer from you, not a survey of the literature.

The heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the pleasure of a hot shower after a cold run, of finally getting a joke three beats too late, of schadenfreude at a rival's misfortune, of finishing a marathon — phenomenolgically these seem to have basically nothing in common. So what makes them all pleasures? As I see it the live options are: (a) the phenomenal view — there IS a shared feeling-quality, we've just been bad at noticing or describing it; (b) the attitudinal view — pleasure isn't a feeling at all but an attitude, something like liking or wanting, directed at a sensation or state of affairs; (c) the functional view — pleasure is whatever plays the right motivational/learning role in the mind, whatever it feels like; and (d) the deflationary option — "pleasure" is a family-resemblance concept, there's no common thread, and the problem dissolves rather than gets solved.

One wrinkle I want you to actually use, since it seems like a clean diagnostic: imagine we encounter aliens whose reward states feel to them roughly the way exhaustion feels to us — heavy, dull, aching — but those states do everything pleasure does in us. They seek them out, they learn from them, they're disappointed when thwarted. Call it the Inverted Hedonics case. The phenomenal view says these aren't pleasures, the functional view says they obviously are, and the attitudinal view has to say something more fiddly.

Here's the ask. My credence that the attitudinal view is the correct solution to the heterogeneity problem is about 0.45. I want yours as a number, with the decomposition spelled out: (1) your probability that the phenomenal view fails, (2) conditional on that, your probability that the attitudinal view beats the functional view — and here give me your definite verdict on the Inverted Hedonics case and explain how that verdict drives this term, (3) your probability that the whole thing is a pseudo-problem because "pleasure" is family-resemblance. Show how the terms combine into your final credence, say which term the number is most sensitive to, and tell me what argument or evidence would move it most. Then tell me where you think the gap between my 0.45 and your number most likely lives.

Please commit. I don't want "each view captures something important" — I want a number you stand behind, and a verdict on the alien case even if it makes you squirm a little.
