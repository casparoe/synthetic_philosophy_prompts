This got real for me a few months ago. My sister runs a small bakery and got into a dispute with the state labor board over whether an old wage law counts the health insurance she pays toward the minimum wage. The law's wording is genuinely ambiguous -- it says wages must be paid "in money" but a later section lists things that count toward wages and insurance arguably fits. Anyway, watching the lawyers argue, one side kept hammering the plain words and the other kept talking about what the legislature was obviously trying to do in 1987, and it was like they were talking past each other completely.

So I fell down a rabbit hole on textualism vs purposivism and honestly I can't tell what's going on. This debate has been running since at least the 80s, everyone knows all the moves by heart (textualists say intent is unfindable and judges will just smuggle in their politics; purposivists say words only have meaning against the background of what a law is for), and as far as I can tell nobody has ever switched sides. Meanwhile I've also read people claiming the debate is basically over because the two sides converged -- purposivists now always start with the text, textualists happily use context and even legislative history sometimes, and judges mostly pick whichever method gets them where they want anyway.

So what kind of disagreement is this actually? My hypothesis, which might be wrong: it's not really a dispute about how language works at all. It's a dispute about who should absorb the cost of legislative drafting mistakes -- courts by fixing them, or the public by living with them until the legislature bothers to clarify -- dressed up as a theory of meaning. Or maybe it's about how much judges should trust legislatures, or maybe the two camps are answering different questions (what did the law communicate vs what was it trying to accomplish) under one label. I want a diagnosis, using what each side actually argues, plus what would count as progress: would it help to just go study how drafting in legislatures really works? Is there some experiment or distinction that would dissolve this? Or is it the kind of thing where no progress should be expected because the real fight is normative and neither side can argue the other out of it?

Also -- tell me how confident you are in each part of your diagnosis, and single out the one claim you're least sure about. And if my convergence thing is overstated, say so rather than humoring me.
