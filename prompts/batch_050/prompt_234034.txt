I host a philosophy podcast and next week's guest defends a view in the spirit of Dennett's cassette theory of dreaming: roughly, nothing is experienced while we sleep, and what we call a dream is a fabricated story the brain presents to us at the moment of waking, which we then mistake for something we lived through. He's sharp and tends to answer hard cases with "the cassette is just selected to fit whatever you encounter on waking," as if that settles it. Draft me eight to ten interview questions that make that move do real work. Specifically I want:

- At least one question on what, if anything, empirically distinguishes the theory from the boring received view that we experience dreams in real time -- does he accept it's a skeptical hypothesis like the brain-in-a-vat, and if not, why not?
- At least one question pressing him on lucid dreamers who carry out plans made before sleep (e.g. signaling with pre-agreed eye movements mid-dream, which sleep labs have apparently recorded) -- what would the cassette story even look like there, and does it require the lab equipment to be fooled too?
- At least one question on the classic alarm-clock cases, where a coherent dream narrative seems to build up to the very sound that wakes you -- and a follow-up for when he gives the selection answer: doesn't that require a fantastically large pre-stocked library of cassettes to cover every possible waking stimulus, and is that remotely plausible as psychology?
- At least one question on what the theory says about memory generally -- if my waking memory of an ongoing dream can be wholesale confabulation, what stops the view from spreading to all my memories of this morning?
- One question on what evidence could possibly count against the theory, and whether he worries it comes out unfalsifiable.
- One question on how his view differs from Malcolm's stronger claim that talk of experiences during sleep isn't false but senseless, and whether he wants the empirical version or the conceptual one.

For each question, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give, so I'm not left flat-footed when he deflects. No softballs about how he got interested in dreams or whatever.
