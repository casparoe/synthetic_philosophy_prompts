Here's the blog summary, quoted in full from my notes: "Philosophers Andy Clark and David Chalmers argue that the mind literally extends into the environment. Their key case is Otto, an Alzheimer's patient who keeps addresses in a notebook, versus Inga, who remembers them normally. Since Otto's notebook plays exactly the same functional role as Inga's biological memory, the notebook itself remembers -- and since the notebook is doing the remembering, Otto's beliefs are stored in his notebook rather than in his head. They call this the parity principle: if a process in the world works the way a mental process would, it counts as part of the mind. This means there's no principled boundary between your brain and your tools: your smartphone, your GPS, and arguably Google are all literally part of your mind and think along with you. The view also follows naturally from embodied cognition and enactivism, which are basically the same thesis applied to the body. One striking consequence, which Clark and Chalmers endorse, is that stealing Otto's notebook would be a kind of assault on his person, not mere theft."
