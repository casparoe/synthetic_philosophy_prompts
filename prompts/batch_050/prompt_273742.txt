Analyze the current dispute over whether large language models understand language -- the stochastic parrots fight -- through Ryle's category mistakes and the ghost in the machine. Rough facts: LLMs trained on next-token prediction produce fluent, context-sensitive text; one side calls it mindless mimicry, the other says the behavior itself just is understanding. Map Ryle onto this carefully: what exactly is the official doctrine he attacks in The Concept of Mind, and which camp is commiting the category mistake -- the skeptics, who treat the absence of an inner ghostly process as settling things, or the enthusiasts, who infer a little understander inside the weights from fluent performance? Or does Ryle's point cut between them, that both camps share one bad picture and only disagree on wether the ghost exists? Then the harder direction: his criterion for understanding is dispositional, a matter of knowing how, and LLMs pass many such tests while failing ones he never imagined needing to state (a body, an ongoing life, being correctable face to face). Is Ryle committed to counting them as understanders, and is that a vindication of his account or a reductio? Finally, say where he's simply silent -- his theory concerns the logic of mental-conduct words, not which systems fall under them -- so what does the dispute even look like if the most he offers is a dissolution rather than a verdict?
