Ok so i'm in a formal epistemology seminar this term and we just spent two weeks on the Judy Benjamin problem (the soldier who's parachuted somewhere into a territory split into a Blue half and a Red half, each half split again into a Second Company area and a HQ area, and she starts out with credence 1/4 in each of the four quadrants). She gets one radio message from base, garunteed true: "if you're in the Red half, you're in the Second Company part of it." For a problem set I tried to turn the puzzle into a clean argument, and now the argument seems to prove way too much and I can't find the leak. Here it is:

(1) The message is entirely about what holds on the condition that she's in Red. Taken by itself it says nothing at all about the Blue half.

(2) If a piece of evidence says nothing about X, then learning that evidence shouldn't change your credence in X.

(3) The natural reading of the message is the material conditional: it's false only in the quadrant where she's in Red-and-HQ, and true in the other three quadrants.

(4) Standard Bayesian updating on a proposition = conditionalize on it. Conditionalizing on the material conditional deletes the Red-and-HQ quadrant and renormalizes, leaving 1/3 in each remaining quadrant, so P(Blue) = 2/3.

(5) But by (1) and (2), P(Blue) should have stayed at 1/2. So conditionalizing on the material conditional mis-handles conditional messages.

(6) And this isn't a quirk of the numbers in this particular setup. Conditionalizing on a material conditional always deletes exactly the worlds where the antecedent holds and the consequent fails, so it always shifts credence away from the antecedent and toward its negation. So on this approach, ANY conditional message is automatically evidence against its own antecedent.

(7) That's absurd in general. If the league texts everyone "if it rains tonight, tomorrow's match is cancelled," that gives me no reason whatsoever to lower my credence in rain. If anything people sometimes read such messages as suggesting the sender expects rain.

(8) So we can't conditionalize on conditional-shaped evidence at all -- conditional information has to be updated on some totally different way, or effectively ignored.

My TA said (8) is just what the problem shows, but that can't be right, we clearly DO learn from conditional messages all the time and it would be nuts if a rational agent had to treat every conditional as un-updatable. Something in the chain has to give. I would most like to keep (2), since it seems like a bare sanity condition on updating, and I also really want to keep (7)'s verdict about the rain case.

So: which step do I reject, and why did it look fine in the first place? And what does rejecting it actually cost -- e.g. if (3) goes, what do I say the message means instead, and if (4) goes, what update rule am I even using and does it break ordinary cases of conditionalization? Also, does whatever fix you propose survive the generalization in (6), or does it just patch this one example?
