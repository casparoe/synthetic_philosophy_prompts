So I'm a novelist and I'm stuck on my next project. The rough idea: an ant colony that might genuinely have a mind of its own -- not the individual ants, who are basically biological clockwork, but the colony as a whole, the way neurons that each "know" nothing somehow add up to you. Think Aunt Hillary from Gödel, Escher, Bach territory, colony-level thoughts that no single ant could ever comprehend or even notice.

Here's my problem and what I want from you: write me a short story (say 800-1200 words) where the colony-mind is the protagonist, but with this constraint -- the story is told from inside one single ant's limited perspective, and that ant can never understand what's going on. The colony is having some kind of mental crisis (a decision? a grief? an argument with itself?) and the ant only experiences it as weird, meaningless urges and detours in its day. The reader should be able to piece together what the colony was "thinking" from the pattern of the ant's confusion, even though the ant itself never does.

Extra wrinkle I want you to play with: at one point the colony-mind does something that, from the human reader's view, looks like a moment of genuine self-awareness -- like the colony noticing its own thoughts for the first time -- but from the ant's view it's just a strange hour where every ant on the mound suddenly stopped what it was doing. Don't explain any of this in the story, no narrator voice-over clarifying it. Trust the reader.

Keep the prose grounded and biological, no ant dialogue, no cutesy personification of the individual ant. The tragedy (or comedy? your call) is that the mind doing the experiencing is invisible to the only viewpoint we get. Ending can be ambiguous about whether there really was anybody home at the colony level, or whether we humans just see minds everywhere we look.
