I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and next month I am reporting on a two-day closed workshop at which a committed defender of the cognitive penetrability of perception (the view that beliefs, desires, or expectations can alter what we literally see) and a committed defender of perceptual impenetrability (the view that early vision is sealed off from cognition, however much our judgments and reports may shift) have agreed to attempt an adversarial collaboration under my moderation. Write the joint statement they should aim to sign by the end of the workshop, drafted so that an educated non-specialist could follow it without either side feeling its position was simplified for the reader. It should contain: first, a numbered list of claims both can sign -- I expect these to include concessions each camp has already effectively made, such as which famous experimental results the impenetrability side no longer needs to contest and which kinds of effects (on attention, on response criteria, on post-perceptual judgment) the penetration side agrees do not count as genuine penetration; second, a table of their residual disagreements, each formulated in wording the disagreeing party would itself accept, with a note marking any disagreement the two come to suspect is terminological rather than substantive; third, the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side -- and here I want them to grapple with the deepest difficulty in the literature, namely whether there is any principled, non-question-begging way to distinguish an effect on a percept itself from an effect on what is believed or reported about that percept; fourth, the design of one novel experiment that does not merely re-run an existing paradigm, which both parties accept in advance would bear on the central crux, with each side's prediction written down before any data exist, including what each would say about their theory if the result came out against them. Close the statement with a paragraph I can quote in my article, in which each side states, in one sentence, what it would take for them to walk back their position entirely.
