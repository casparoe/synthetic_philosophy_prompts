Take South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission and analyse it through the lens of Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood. I roughly know the politics, so skip the potted history: what I want is the conceptual mapping. If a person is constituted through their relations with others rather than being a self-standing individual, what does the Commission's core wager — amnesty in exchange for full public disclosure — actually amount to? Is it best understood as restoring broken relationships rather than punishing individuals, and is that a coherent way to think about letting torturers and murderers walk free? Then run it backwards: what does the case do to Ubuntu itself? Critics, including some victims' families, felt they'd been steamrolled in the name of a reconciliation they never consented to — is that a problem for Ubuntu as a moral theory, or evidence that the TRC's version of it was bent out of shape by a negotiated political settlement? And what about perpetrators who showed no remorse: does a relational view have the resources to say why reintegrating them is wrong, or does it have to treat them as just another broken relationship to be repaired? Take a definite position on whether the TRC vindicates Ubuntu or exposes its limits, and say which — don't just lay out the options and leave me to choose.
