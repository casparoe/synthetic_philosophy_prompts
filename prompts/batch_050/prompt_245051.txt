So I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background at all, and I got into a dumb late-night argument with a coworker that I now can't stop thinking about. We were talking about AI alignment stuff, and I made the point that our models can tell you perfectly well which actions are wrong — they'd pass any ethics quiz you throw at them — but that "knowledge" does absolutely nothing to their behavior unless you wire it into the reward function. And my coworker goes "yeah, so, like psychopaths." We've been arguing about it for a week now. Do psychopaths actually know that what they do is wrong? On the one hand, they can recite the rules, they can predict exactly what society will punish, some of them are scary good at deploying moral language to manipulate people. On the other hand, from what I've read (admittedly pop-sci level), none of that seems to matter to them at decision time, and apparently there's even research suggesting they blur moral rules with mere conventions — like for them "don't torture someone" lands in the same bucket as "don't wear white after labor day," just stuff the boss says.

I want to pin this down with a distinction I made up, which maybe philosophers already have better versions of: call it rule-knowledge vs weight-knowledge. Rule-knowledge is being able to sort actions into right and wrong correctly, like a classifier. Weight-knowledge is when the label "wrong" actually shows up with nonzero weight in your decision-making — the way most of us flinch away from a cruel act even when nobody's watching and we'd profit from it. Write me an essay on whether psychopaths know that what they do is wrong, working with this distinction (or improving on it if you think it cuts things at the wrong joint). Some things I'd want it to grapple with: does genuinely knowing that something is wrong require being even slightly motivated by that fact? If an alien or an LLM could sort acts into right and wrong flawlessly but felt literally nothing about it, would we say it has moral knowledge or just a very good lookup table? And what does the answer do to blame — can you fairly hold someone responsible for violating a rule they could recite but couldn't feel, or is that like punishing a colorblind person for running a red light they couldn't distinguish?

Steel-man both sides before picking one: the view that psychopaths know exactly what they're doing and simply don't care (which, my coworker argues, is precisely what makes them so culpable — indifference to known wrongs is worse than ignorance), and the view that they're morally blind in the way that actually matters, so something about responsibility genuinely breaks down. Don't end on a "it's complicated, both sides have a point" non-answer though. Give me a verdict — do psychopaths know that what they do is wrong, yes, no, or a precisely specified "yes in sense X, no in sense Y" — and justify it, including what it implies for whether we should blame them.
