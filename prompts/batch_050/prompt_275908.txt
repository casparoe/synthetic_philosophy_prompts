Write the joint statement that could emerge from an adversarial collaboration between two camps on moral uncertainty. Camp A holds that a rational agent uncertain between moral theories should maximize expected choiceworthiness, treating theories as yielding cardinal, intertheoretically comparable choiceworthiness values. Camp B holds that intertheoretic comparisons of choiceworthiness are in principle impossible or unconstrained, so expected-choiceworthiness maximization is ill-defined, and that the agent should instead follow some non-comparative rule (for instance, acting on one's most probable theory, or on a procedure where theories "vote" rather than trade off magnitudes). I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, covering what moral uncertainty is, which cases any adequate account must handle (e.g., an agent torn between a theory demanding enormous sacrifices and one demanding none), and what both agree would count as a reductio of an approach; (2) the residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording -- I especially want the disagreement about whether "choiceworthiness gaps" between theories have a fact of the matter separated from the disagreement about whether, absent such facts, any decision rule is rationally required; (3) the cruxes, and for each a case, argument, or analogy both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction or verdict about that case recorded in advance -- candidate cruxes include whether structural analogies to empirical uncertainty survive scrutiny, whether comparability skeptics can avoid their own view collapsing into mere conservatism, and whether expected-choiceworthiness maximizers can handle theories that assign infinite or undefined value. Flag any part of the dispute you conclude is verbal rather than substantive, and say why. Throughout, separate what is genuinely settled in this literature from what remains contested, and do not present either camp's position as the consensus.
