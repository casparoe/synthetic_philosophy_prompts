Here's what he wrote:

"The Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is basically the Nash bargaining solution with the math tweaked so it behaves when the feasible set grows. Nash maximizes the product of the players' utility gains over disagreement, and Kalai and Smorodinsky's insight was that this can make a player strictly worse off when the pie expands in that player's favor, which they traced to the IIA axiom. So they dropped symmetry and replaced it with an individual monotonicity requirement. Their construction: for each player, find the ideal point, i.e. the most that player could get while the other is held at their disagreement payoff; then draw the segment from the disagreement point to that utopia point and take where it hits the Pareto frontier. Equivalently, both players walk away with the same percentage of their best-case gain, so it's a proportional or relative-concession split. Like Nash it's invariant to affine rescalings of utility, which is why it's the go-to solution for people who want to avoid interpersonal comparisons of utility. And it coincides with Nash whenever the problem is symmetric, so you can think of it as Nash plus a fairness patch."
