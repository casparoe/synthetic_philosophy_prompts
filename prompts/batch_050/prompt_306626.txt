Essay help, bear with me. I'm writing about Christopher Nolan's The Prestige and want to read the Tesla machine plot through the Sleeping Beauty problem and self-locating belief. The bits that matter: the machine duplicates whoever steps in, one copy materializes across the room (in the stage show, up on the balcony) and the film never tells us which of the two is the original; everynight of the Real Transported Man, one Angier takes the bow while the other drops through the trapdoor and drowns in a tank, and the warehouse full of tanks at the end tells us how often that happened. In his dying monologue Angier says, roughly, that the trick took courage because each night he climbed in not knowing whether he'd be the one in the tank or the one taking the applause.

So: standing at the machine before he flips the switch, what credence should Angier give to I'm about to drown versus I'm about to be up there bowing? Actually work out what a halfer and a thirder would each say, don't just gesture at the controversy -- and be honest about where the Beauty analogy breaks, because there's no memory erasure here, the drowned copy does get an experience (a horrible one), and the survivor's memories are always continuous with the man who stepped in, so maybe this is really an anthropic selection case in Sleeping Beauty clothing. And the weirder question: since the film never settles which copy is the original, maybe there's no fact of the matter to settle -- in which case is Angier's nightly fear even coherent? Is he uncertain about a fact, or is it that which one will I be has no answer, and does the halfer/thirder machinery still apply then?

If Angier is one man unsure which of his successors he is, the twins are two men who've agreed to be one. Does the film treat that as tragic in a different way, and does self-locating epistemology even have the vocabulary for what's lost when Sarah can feel that some days her husband loves her and some days he doesn't?
