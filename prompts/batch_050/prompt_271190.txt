Please argue that the probability problem facing the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics is fundamentally a problem of meaning rather than a problem of derivation, and that it therefore cannot be solved by the two leading strategies on offer. Your argument should run as follows. First, distinguish the quantitative question -- why the squared-amplitude measure rather than some other weighting of branches -- from the prior qualitative question of what a probability could even be for an Everettian agent who knows with certainty that every outcome will occur on some branch and that all of her successors will exist. Second, take up the self-locating-uncertainty strategy, on which pre-measurement uncertainty is recast as uncertainty about which branch one will turn out to occupy: argue that this fails because, before the split, there is no fact of the matter about which future successor the agent is, so there is no genuine proposition for her to be uncertain about -- unlike in cases of everyday ignorance, nothing is being tracked that could settle the question. Third, take up the decision-theoretic programme associated with Deutsch and Wallace, which aims to derive the Born measure from rationality constraints on an agent's preferences over acts: concede for the sake of argument that the derivation is internally sound, but argue that it can at most establish that rational agents must behave as if the squared amplitudes were probabilities, and that this behavioural conclusion presupposes, rather than supplies, an answer to the meaning question -- the axioms only have a grip if the agent already cares differentially about her successors in proportion to some measure, which is exactly what needed grounding. Fourth, anticipate the reply that the meaning question is a philosopher's pseudo-problem, on the grounds that once rational behaviour and confirmation practice are pinned down nothing else about probability remains to be explained; argue against this by showing that the same move, made on behalf of classical frequency or propensity talk, would strike us as an abdication rather than an analysis. Conclude that Everettians must either accept an irreducible, primitive notion of degree of caring across successors -- with the explanatory costs that carries -- or admit that the interpretation has not yet made room for probabilistic belief at all. Write in continuous prose throughout, as one would in an essay or a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
