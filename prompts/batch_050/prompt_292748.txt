my best friends are getting married next saturday and i'm giving a toast. fun fact: they met in a grad seminar on Strawson's Freedom and Resentment -- she argued that resentment is sometimes the respectful response, he argued the objective attitude is underrated, and they've been hashing it out for six years and somehow decided to get married anyway. i need a toast of about 4-5 minutes. the catch: half the room will be philosophers who will catch any sloppy reading of Strawson, and the other half are aunts and uncles who think "reactive attitudes" sounds like a band name. the toast should build toward the idea that marriage is basically a vow to stay in the participant stance -- to keep actually resenting, forgiving, and being grateful to each other rather than slipping into the detached "well, that's just how he is, he can't help it" mode of managing each other -- but it has to get there without me ever saying the words "participant stance" or "reactive attitudes" out loud, since that would lose half the room. also it can't be sappy in the generic wedding-toast way: if i say anything like "love means never keeping score" they will both visibly cringe, because Strawson's whole point is roughly that score-keeping of the right kind is what treating someone as a person looks like. one good joke about their six-year seminar argument is encouraged but it has to actually land for the non-philosophers too.
