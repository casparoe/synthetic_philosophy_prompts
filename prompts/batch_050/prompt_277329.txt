So last Friday I had this huge argument down the pub with my mate Claire, who's a paintings conservator, about fakes. My position, which I still think is basically right, goes like this: if a copy of a painting is genuinely indistinguishable from the original — not "looks a bit like it", but no viewer and no connoisseur could tell them apart without provenance paperwork — then whatever extra value the original has is not aesthetic value. It's relic value, the same sort of value Napoleon's actual hat has over an identical replica, and calling it aesthetic is a category error museums have a financial interest in not noticing.

The bit Claire really hated: I said that since knowledge of a work's origin demonstrably changes how people experience it, we should treat that as a bias to be trained out of, the way you try to see through an optical illusion even once you know the lines are the same length. A kind of aesthetic hygiene. She said something rude about that.

Anyway she's now, possibly out of spite, got me a 20-minute slot at her department's joint art history and philosophy seminar, with maybe 25 minutes of questions after. The room will be: two art historians whose actual job is attribution, one philosopher of art who's very keen on Goodman's line that knowing the origin changes what you literally perceive, some postgrads, and Claire.

Prep me for the Q&A. I want the objections in roughly the order they'll actually arrive, phrased the way those specific people will phrase them — e.g. the attribution lot will come at me through connoisseurship and "no copy is ever really indistinguishable" rather than through theory. For each one: does it have real force, what should I concede up front to look reasonable, what should I flatly hold, and how do I say it in under a minute. Also tell me honestly where my "hygiene" claim is weakest, because that's the bit I'm least sure of, and flag the one question I'm praying nobody asks — I have a nasty feeling it's something to do with van Meegeren and whether my view means the forger's achievement is aesthetically on a par with Vermeer's, and I don't have a clean answer to that yet.
