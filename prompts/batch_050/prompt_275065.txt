Can you write me a satirical piece, roughly 600 words, in the form of a public FAQ page from a fictional consultancy called FenceClear Solutions, whose whole business is removing fences -- literal and institutional -- without asking why anyone put them there. The company's founders have read just enough Chesterton to name-drop him in their marketing, but their pitch is basically the anti-Chesterton: if you can't immediately see the reason for a fence, that's proof there isn't one, and anyone who hesitates is just sentimental about lumber. The humor should come from them applying this logic cheerfully and consistently to real fence-like things: guardrails on mountain roads, the rule that two people have to sign off on big bank transfers, "never make a major decision when you're angry," double-entry bookkeeping, mandatory waiting periods, the two-key system for missile launches, "measure twice cut once." Each FAQ answer should push the same smug reasoning a little further than the last until the reader starts wincing. Keep it deadpan the whole way through -- no winking at the camera, the company genuinely believes in itself. I want the Burkean point to land by contrast: a reader should come away feeling why "don't tear it down untill you understand why it's up" has real force, but also sensing where that principle gets slippery, since some fences genuinely are just bad and "first figure out why it exists" can become a polite way of never touching anything.

Then, after the piece, add a short note (separate from the FAQ, in your normal voice) flagging where the satire exaggerates or flattens the actual Burke/Chesterton position, and where your satire is most likely to have been unfair. Specifically: what's the strongest reply a smart fence-remover could give that the piece can't just laugh off -- say, about fences whose original reasons were awful, or about how "understand it first" often means "defer to whoever benefits from it"? And what evidence or argument would change your mind about whether the satire was a fair hit?
