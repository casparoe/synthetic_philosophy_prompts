I work on an ML team that uses human feedback, and I keep bumping into the ethics version of the same headache: when people disagree morally, how do you tell a real preference, like valuing loyalty more than harm, from a bias, like calling it loyalty only when my side benefits? The debate around our tool and in my reading has stalled: one side says that after enough reflection and debiasing the remaining disagreement is basically taste and should be aggregated; the other says lots of preferences are contaminated by status quo bias, scope neglect, motivated reasoning, etc., so treating them as data just moralizes noise. But both sides use teh same trolley-ish and hiring/fairness cases, and both claim the other’s method begs the question. What kind of disagreement is this actually: empirical about how stable judgments are under manipulation, conceptual about what counts as a preference once you allow idealization, or a fight over whose intuitions get to be the calibration set? Give me a diagnosis, not a survey, and say what would count as progress: a psych experiment, a formal criterion for idealization, or admitting the question is ill-posed.
