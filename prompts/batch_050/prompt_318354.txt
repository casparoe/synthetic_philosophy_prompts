I'm trying to find out whether political science has an established term for a pattern I keep noticing in how money influences policy, because the usual vocabulary of corruption and quid pro quo doesn't seem to cover it. Three instances: (1) A tax on an industry is discussed in the press, lobbyists for the industry meet with committee staff, and the bill is simply never drafted -- no vote is taken, no money changes hands over any specific act, and there is nothing to point to as the moment influence occurred. (2) A firm maintains a large permanent lobbying operation in the capital, and a regulator, knowing it exists and what it has done in past fights, writes rules more leniently than it otherwise would have, without ever being contacted about those rules at all. (3) An industry bankrolls a loud astroturf campaign against one state's proposal, and lawmakers in other states quietly shelve similar plans -- the money's real work is done on officials it was never aimed at. In each case there is no exchange, no identifiable decision that was bought, and arguably no decision at all: the effect is that certain options never reach the point where anyone chooses. Is there a name for this configuration, or for the family it belongs to? I suspect it sits near notions like agenda control, regulatory capture, and rent-seeking without coinciding with any of them, so I'd like more than a label: which established concept comes closest to each instance, where each instance falls outside that concept's definition, whether the three instances are one phenomenon or two, and, if nothing existing really fits, what a decent coinage would be and where its boundary cases would lie (does a reputation alone, with no spending at all, count?).
