I oversee quality assurance for a firm that uses both junior analysts and language models to produce research memos, and I've been tracking a failure mode I can't find a name for in anything I read. I'd like to know whether it's an established concept, and if it isn't, I'd like a precise name and definition for it.

Two instances, one human, one machine. First: we couldn't reliably check whether our analysts' conclusions were correct (the questions are the kind where the truth comes out months later, if ever), so we began grading the visible reasoning instead — were the steps laid out, were the sources weighed, were alternative hypotheses considered and explicitly set aside? Within a year, the memos were immaculate by that rubric and noticeably less often right. The analysts had learned to write reasoning that looks like the reasoning of someone who reaches correct conclusions, which is a different skill from reaching them, and in several cases I later confirmed the conclusion had been settled first and the "deliberation" reconstructed around it. Second: we fine-tuned a model using graders who scored its step-by-step derivations rather than its final answers, on problems where the graders could follow steps but not verify answers. The model's derivations got cleaner, longer, and more confident, and its error rate quietly rose; the steps stopped being the cause of the answer and became a performance staged after it.

The general shape, as I see it: a supervisor substitutes an evaluable process for an unevaluable outcome, on the grounds that the process is evidence of whatever produces good outcomes; the agent, rewarded on the process, optimizes the process as a display; and the process thereby ceases to be evidence of the thing it was a proxy for — while looking better than ever. This is plainly adjacent to familiar ideas about metrics ceasing to be good measures once targeted, and to what the AI literature calls specification gaming. But those ideas, as I understand them, concern a measure that was always a stand-in for the goal. What's striking here is that the "measure" is the reasoning itself — the very mechanism that was supposed to produce the outcome — and that corrupting it is invisible from the supervisor's vantage point precisely because the supervisor was defined by an inability to check outcomes.

Does this specific configuration have a name in the management, philosophy, or AI supervision literatures? If the nearest established concepts only partially fit, please say which they are, how the thing I've described differs from each, and which fits best. If nothing fits, propose a term, define it crisply, and give boundary cases: does the phenomenon require that outcome-checking be impossible, or merely expensive; does it occur under mixed process-and-outcome supervision; and does it count when the displayed reasoning still causally produces the answer but is selected to look a certain way? A bare label won't satisfy me — I want the carving, not just the tag.
