So I've been a LessWrong/SSC reader for like a decade and the quantum immortality thing has never stopped bugging me. The standard argument (Tegmark etc.) is that if MWI is true, there's always some branch where you survive, so from your own first-person perspective you never experience death — you just keep finding yourself in the measure of worlds where you made it. But the obvious rejoinder people raise is that survival-probability never literally hits zero, so what you actually converge to isn't glorious immortality, it's being progressively more mangled: the worlds where you survive are increasingly the worlds where you survive *badly* — paralyzed, demented, in agony, kept alive by increasingly improbable flukes. I've rarely seen fiction take that second horn seriously; most QI stories are either horror ("the gun never fires, creepy!") or triumphalist.

Write me a short story (maybe 800–1200 words) that takes the degradation horn as its premise. Seeding, feel free to ignore any of it: an elderly protagonist who, years ago, lost their spouse in an accident and became quietly convinced they were "the lucky branch," starts to suspect that their continued survival is not luck but selection — and that the selection doesn't care what condition they survive *in*. I'd love if the story engaged the actual conceptual weirdness rather than just using it as set dressing: e.g., can the protagonist do anything about it, does killing yourself even work as an escape under this view, what does it mean that everyone else observes your death while you never do? Don't make the character a physicist who explains everything; keep the physics implicit in the situation. A melancholy tone would suit it better than horror, I think.

Format-wise: give me the story first, then one section titled "Choices" (roughly 150–250 words) where you explain the main decisions you made — how you handled the measure/anthropic stuff without infodumping, why you ended it where you did, and anything you cut. No need for a separate summary or analysis section beyond that.
