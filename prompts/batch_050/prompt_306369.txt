Hey, so I cover science/ideas for a magazine and I'm drafting a piece that leans on Dennett's three stances (physical, design, intentional) and his "real patterns" stuff. I'm not a philosopher, just an enthusiast, so I'd love comments before I pitch this. Here's the gist of the draft:

The piece is called (working title) "Do Chatbots Have Beliefs, or Do We Just Like Pretending?" My argument in a nutshell: people keep debating whether LLMs "really" understand anything, but that debate is confused because it treats understanding as some hidden inner fact you could discover by prying open the black box. Dennett's view, as I understand it, is that having beliefs and desires isn't about what's inside — it's about whether treating the system as if it had beliefs and desires lets you reliably predict its behavior. A thermostat is too simple to bother with the intentional stance (design stance is enough), a chess engine sits on the borderline, and an LLM is the first machine where the intentional stance isn't just convenient but often the ONLY practical way to predict what it'll do next. Nobody's going to predict GPT's answer to a question by computing weights.

Then I push it further with a move I'm less sure about. Dennett says patterns are "real" if they'd have to be included in any efficient compression of the data — like how temperature is a real pattern in a gas even though no single molecule has a temperature. I want to argue that an LLM's "belief that Paris is in France" is real in exactly this sense: any compressed description of its behavior has to include it. And I compare it to hurricanes — we take the intentional stance toward hurricanes ("it's heading for the coast, it's strengthening because it feeds on warm water") and meteorologists find this genuinely predictive, so maybe agency-ish talk about storms is also tracking real patterns, not just being sloppy.

My conclusion is that the question "does it REALLY believe?" dissolves: the belief is as real as the pattern is, and the pattern is plainly there.

Comments I'd especially want:

1. Am I getting Dennett right? Particularly the real patterns bit and whether he'd actually say stance-adoption is the whole story about belief. I have a nagging feeling there's a subtlety I'm flattening (something about whether the stance has to be *indispensable*, not just useful).
2. Is the hurricane comparison a strength or a liability? An editor will definately flag it as either the best or worst paragraph in the piece and I'd like to know which before that happens.
3. Does the conclusion actually follow, or am I smuggling in "real pattern" = "real belief" too fast? Is there an obvious objection a philosopher reader would raise in the first comment?
4. General: is this interesting or is it the sort of thing that's been said a hundred times and I'm just late to it?
