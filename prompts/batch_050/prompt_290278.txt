I TA for an undergrad metaphysics seminar and my students have this annoying habit of memorizing "Lewis says counterparts, Kripke says transworld identity" as if the debate were just picking a team. So I want to build a little sorting quiz for week 3: "Which view of identity across possible worlds do you actually hold?" The quiz should have 8 or 9 questions, and each one should be a concrete scenario with two or three answer options written in plain language -- no "counterpart," "haecceity," or "de re" anywhere in the questions themselves. Cases I'd like used, because they seemed to actually split the room when I tried them informally: whether you could have been born to completely different parents; whether a wooden table could have been made from an entirely different batch of lumber; whether you could have been a fish; the atom-for-atom duplicate of you living a different life on some distant planet (is it possible that *you* lived that life, or only that someone exactly like you did?); and that classic gut-punch question of why it should matter to me whether I win the election if what really makes it "possible that I won" is that some *other* guy who merely resembles me wins. Feel free to add a couple more cases of your own if they pry positions apart better.

Please build it from the logical space first: figure out which yes/no-ish commitments the questions are actually testing, then locate the positions in that space. The leaves I want represented at minimum are: strict transworld identity (the same individual literally inhabits multiple worlds), counterpart theory with a similarity-based counterpart relation, counterpart theory where the counterpart relation is constrained (e.g. counterparts must share your origins), an origins-essentialist version of transworld identity, and a deflationary option where which modal claims come out true depends on our descriptive interests rather than any metaphysical fact. If some combination of answers corresponds to no position anyone holds, note it and say briefly whether you think it's incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. And please flag any pair of leaves the quiz can't really separate (I suspect realism-about-worlds questions might muddy things, since both camps can host either identity view, but I'd rather you tell me).

End with a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions, and then a short candid note: how confident are you that the quiz actually sorts people the way the key claims, and which single question-to-position mapping are you least sure about? I'd rather you flag a mushy diagnostic than pretend a case cleanly seperates two views when it doesn't.
