I've been arguing about Parfit's teletransporter with the same two people for like three years now and I want to understand why nothing ever moves. Quick version of the fight: one side says that in the branch-line case -- where the machine on Earth makes a perfect replica on Mars but doesn't destroy the original, so I'm stuck here on Earth watching some guy on Mars remember my breakfast -- I plainly don't survive, because identity is one-one and he isn't me. The other side says fine, identity fails, but so what: what actually matters in survival is psychological continuity, and I'd get everything that matters in the simple case where the original is destroyed. And then the first side says there's a further fact about whether it's me that continuity can't capture, and the second side replies that once all the physical and psychological facts are fixed there's nothing left to be a fact about -- asking "but will it be ME?" at that point is like asking whether a club that changed its membership and rules is still "the same club," a question with no further answer.

Parfit argued this in the 80s and as far as I can tell the literature since is mostly the same moves with better footnotes. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement isn't really metaphysical -- it's a disagreement about whether "will it be me?"-type questions are required to have determinate answers, i.e. about which questions are even well-posed, and both sides keep mistaking that for a fight about the furniture of the universe. But maybe I'm wrong and it's a genuine disagreement about whether there's a simple, unanalyzable further fact, or maybe it's just bedrock intuitions where mine says "the Mars guy is good enough" and my friend's says "no way" and neither can be argued for.

Diagnose this for me: what kind of disagreement is it actually -- verbal, substantive, methodological, or intuition-pumping all the way down? Use what each side actually says, not a textbook summary of the positions. And given your diagnosis, tell me honestly what would count as progress: a new thought experiment that isolates the disagreement, an argument that one side is answering a different question, something from decision theory about what a rational agent should even care about, or is the realistic answer that nothing ends it and the best we can do is say precisely where the two sides stop being able to hear each other?
