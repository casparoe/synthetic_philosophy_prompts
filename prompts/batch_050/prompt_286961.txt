so i just read about the unilateralist's curse -- the idea that when a bunch of people can each independently kick off some irreversible thing (publishing a risky paper, releasing a model, launching a product), it tends to happen even if most of the group thinks it's a bad idea, because the person with the rosiest estimate of its value is the one who acts, and everyone else's caution doesn't get a veto.

my takeaway was that this is basicly a story about overconfident individuals. the curse happens because whoever pulls the trigger is systematically overestimating the value of acting. which suggests a clean fix: if everyone on the team gets properly calibrated -- forecasting training, reference classes, whatever -- their estimates stop being inflated, so the most-eager person's estimate stops being inflated, and the curse dissolves. but then i saw someone claim the curse would still bite even in a group of perfectly calibrated, totally unbiased reasoners, which honestly doesnt make sense to me. if nobody's estimate is biased upward, where is the upward pressure supposed to come from? surely the maximum of a bunch of unbiased estimates is itself an unbiased estimate of the max, i.e. fine?
