I've been teaching a grad seminar on the Fermi paradox and have half-talked myself into the dark forest hypothesis as the right answer -- roughly: given light-speed lag, exponential tech growth, and the impossibility of credible pre-commitement across light-years, any rational civilization goes silent and preempts anyone who doesn't. Tell me what would count against it. I want the load-bearing premises separated out and stress-tested one at a time: the game theory (does one-shot anonymity really kill cooperation, or can costly signaling or some shadow-of-the-future argument get traction?), the sociology (does the conclusion need every civilization to be paranoid, or does it go through if just a fraction are?), and the empirical side (which observations -- loud technosignatures, ancient apparently un-harmed systems, the absence of visible mega-engineering -- would genuinely cut against it rather than merely being awkward to explain away?). Also: once the defender can always say "the quiet ones are the smart ones," has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and does that matter for a hypothesis of this kind? No citations needed, just an honest map of where it's exposed. FWIW I'd personally find a game-theoretic defeater more persuasive than an observational one.
