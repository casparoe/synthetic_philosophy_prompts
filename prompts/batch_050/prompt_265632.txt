So I got voluntold onto the "measurement review board" at my company (mid-size, ~200 people, B2B software) and I need help building the actual instrument we use. Background: every quarter, teams propose new KPIs and targets, and the board (four of us, no statisticians, roughly 30 minutes per proposal) has to approve, approve-with-guardrails, or reject each one in writing, and the verdicts matter because comp is partially tied to hitting targets. I've been reading LW and ACX for years so I've absorbed the usual Goodhart material — the bit about a measure that becomes a target ceasing to be useful, Campbell's law, the four-way taxonomy (regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial) that gets passed around — but all of that is diagnosis, not procedure. Nobody on the board can run the taxonomy in their head under time pressure, and half the teams proposing metrics have never heard of Goodhart at all.

Write us a review procedure we can actually execute in the 30 minutes: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient vs. insufficient answer to each, where a bad answer means automatic reject vs. where it means "add a guardrail metric and approve," and which failure mode each question is supposed to catch. Two complications the procedure has to live with. First, we usally can't get numbers — we don't know the gaming elasticity of anything in advance, so the procedure has to say what to do when an answer is "we don't know." Second, the people being measured will eventually learn what the review asks, which means the review itself can be Goodharted (teams will learn to write proposals that sail through); say what the board should do about that, if anything. Mark explicitly the points where the procedure has to hand things to judgment because no checklist item can settle them, and be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up in checklist clothing.

Then run the procedure on three proposals we actually got last quarter, with a written verdict for each: (1) the support team wants "tickets closed per agent per day" with a floor of 8; (2) the infra team wants "number of incident reports filed per month, target fewer than 5" as their reliability metric — note this one has the weird property that a genuinely good quarter might produce more reports, not fewer; (3) my own favorite, which I championed, "weekly active users of the internal knowledge base" as the documentation team's success metric. The worked examples should include at least one verdict I won't like (I have a guess which, but don't spare it on my account). End with a short section on what this procedure structurally cannot catch — the failure modes that no 30-minute review will find — so the board knows what it's still on the hook for even when it approves something.
