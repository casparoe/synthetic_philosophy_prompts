I'm making a little zine for friends (long story) and I want a flowchart quiz on the theme "does this table actually exist?" that sorts people by their gut answer to when some things add up to a further thing. Everyone starts at "well, stuff exists, sure" and branches on yes/no reactions to concrete cases: my desk plus the moon taken as one object, a deck of cards scattered across the floor, my cat, my cat a moment after losing a tail hair, the watch I just took apart and laid out in pieces, two strangers shaking hands. Leaves should include: nothing composite exists (only simples arranged table-wise), any bunch of things whatsoever composes a whole, composition happens only where there's a living thing, only where things are fastened or touching, it's just a brute fact which bunches compose, and it can be genuinely indeterminate whether they do. Each node must be a case someone can react to without knowing any of the positions -- no "do you accept unrestricted composition?" allowed. End with a scoring key mapping answer patterns to leaves, flag any pair of leaves your questions can't actually tell apart, and briefly say whether any combination of answers looks unoccupied because its incoherent or just weird.
