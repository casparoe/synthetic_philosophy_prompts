In my philosophy of biology reading group we've been stuck for months on the etiological vs causal-role debate about functions. Both sides agree on basically every hard case — hearts pump blood, kidneys filter urine, that's why organisms have them — and each side even concedes the other's account handles some domain fine (etiological folks admit causal-role talk fits neuroscience and ecology, causal-role folks admit selected-effects talk fits the classic teleological cases). And yet nobody ever converts. My working hypothesis is that this was never one disagreement: Millikan/Neander-style accounts are answering a question about why a trait exists and persists in the lineage, Cummins-style accounts are answering a question about how a complex system manages to do what it does, and the word 'function' is just doing double duty. Diagnose the debate for me: is it verbal, two different questions under one label, or is there something genuinely substantive left over once you split the questions — say, about which notion actually does the work in biological practice, or about how to make sense of malfunction? And given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like here — a pluralist carve-up, an argument that one notion is theoretically more basic, or an admission that the impasse is permanent and why?
