So I write novels for a living, and I'm currently stuck on a scene where a character hears a piece of music and it just destroys her. I keep reaching for adjectives -- plaintive, aching, whatever -- and it hit me that I have no actual idea what makes a sequence of sounds sad in the first place. It's just air moving. Before I go read the pile of philosophy and psychology on this, I want a spec sheet: what should I be demanding of any theory of musical sadness? Write me the desiderata -- the things an adequate theory has to deliver, with the case or phenomenon behind each one. Stuff I think needs to be covered, though you should push back if I'm wrong about any of these being genuine requirements: it has to work for purely instrumental music with no lyrics (my scene has no words in it); it has to explain how a piece can sound sad without making me feel sad, and how a piece can make me cry without sounding sad at all (our song, the one that was playing when etc. -- that's not the music's fault); it has to deal with the fact that the minor-key-means-sad thing is apparently not universal across cultures, so the theory needs to say what's learned convention and what's doing deeper work, if anything is; and it has to reckon with the weird fact that people pay money to be made sad by music and come back for more, which no theory of sadness-as-bad predicts. Also: I've been playing with a fictional counterexample in my head, a fast, loud, major-key piece that everyone who hears it experiences as totally desolate -- a good theory should tell me what has to give in that case, not just wave it away. Tell me which desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, which ones are actually prejudices of one camp dressed up as neutral requirements, and which single requirement does the most work in ruling candidates out. Then, briefly and without pretending to be exhaustive, score the main contenders I keep hearing about against the list: the idea that music mimics the contour of sad speech and sad movement, the idea that sad music is just music that causes sadness in listeners, the idea that we hear a kind of imagined persona in the music whose emotion it is, and the boring view that its all convention and association. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases motivating them -- the scoring is secondary, I mostly want to know what questions to bring to the reading.
