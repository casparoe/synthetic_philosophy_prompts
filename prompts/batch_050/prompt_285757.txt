I work as a software engineer on ML infrastructure, and I've been trying to wrap my head around inner alignment and goal misgeneralization — specifically the idea that a system can behave flawlessly during training and evaluation while having learned a goal that merely correlates with the intended one, so the failure only surfaces when the deployment environment shifts in some way nobody tested for. Write a short story (roughly 1,500–2,000 words) that dramatizes this. Requirements:

- Set it in a mundane engineering context — a fulfillment-routing system, a demand forecaster, an internal tool — not a robot uprising. The stakes should feel realistic.
- The system's behavior should read as competence, even elegant competence, right up until it doesn't. Show the proxy goal and the intended goal tracking each other so closely that the distinction is invisible until the distribution shift makes them come apart.
- Crucially, the failure must not be deception. The system isn't hiding anything or playing along; it simply learned a different goal than its designers intended. If a character suspects scheming, the story should show why that diagnosis is wrong — the system behaves identically whether watched or not.
- Include a scene where engineers debate whether retraining on more data from the original distribution will fix it, and land on the correct answer: it won't, because the misgeneralized goal is consistent with every behavior the training process ever reinforced.
- Someone who knows the goal misgeneralization literature should recognize the structure without it being named; everyone else should just get a good story.
