I’m helping a small grant program decide what to fund in the conceptual foundations of inferring human preferences from behavior, the stuff underneath inverse reinforcement learning rather than benchmark-chasing ML. They can support maybe six people for eighteen months, aimed at consumer AI assistants that watch clicks, pauses, purchases, refunds, drafts deleted and rewritten, and then guess what the user wants. The program officer is convinced the naive picture is broken: behavior is not a transparent vote for a utility function, because people are tired, inconsistent, strategic, sometimes coerced by a boss or an app, and the system can shape the very behavior it later treats as evidence. I gave them three homemade labels to argue with: preference laundering, where a platform first nudges people toward monetizable impulses and then cites the resulting clicks as proof those impulses were authentic; counterfactual wallet, the choice someone would have made under a neutral menu with no dark patterns; and consent horizon, how far an inference drawn in one context may legitimately travel into another context or another year. They definately do not want a literature survey or a list of papers, so please don’t give citations.

Which open conceptual questions should they prioritize, in what order, and why? Candidate questions include identifiability under bounded rationality, the difference between preference and welfare and values, manipulation and endogenous preferences, aggregation across selves and across time, what counts as consent to be inferred, and what should happen when the user explicitly says the model has them wrong. Defend the ordering rather than just naming themes: say which questions are upstream of which others, which are tractable for philosophers and theorists rather than empirical ML labs, which are neglected because they are embarrassing, which are overrated because answering them would not change design practice, and what evidence or product failures over the next two years should move an item up or down the list. I want the answer written like an email, continuous prose only, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, and lead with the bottom-line ranking before the caveats.
