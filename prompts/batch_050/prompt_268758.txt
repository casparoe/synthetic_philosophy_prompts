So I'm putting together the syllabus for my grad seminar on metaphysics of science next spring, and I've been arguing with a colleague down the hall about the laws of nature debate all semester. She's a dyed-in-the-wool anti-Humean (thinks laws genuinely govern, or maybe that they're grounded in the essences/dispositions of properties), whereas I've always had a soft spot for the Lewis-style best systems picture, where the laws are just whatever the best summary of the total mosaic of particular facts happens to say. Anyway, here's a thought experiment I came up with over the weekend that I'd like you to use as the anchor for an essay. Imagine a machine -- call it the nomic dial -- that doesn't change a single particular fact in the world: every particle trajectory, every measurement outcome, every beer bottle falling off every windowsill stays exactly the same. The dial only adjusts, so to speak, the metaphysical status of the regularities: at 0 the mosaic just is, brutely, with no governing and no essential powers, and at 10 the very same mosaic is underwritten by full-blooded governing laws with genuine nomic necessity. My question: as you turn the dial from 0 to 10, does anything that matters actually change?

Write an essay that uses the nomic dial to compare Humeanism (best-systems style) with anti-Humeanism (governing and/or dispositional essentialist versions -- you can treat them separately if they come apart here). Some pressure points I'd want the essay to engage with: (1) the anti-Humean will presumably say the dial changes something crucial -- counterfactuals, explanations, the justification of induction -- so evaluate whether those dividends actually depend on turning the dial, or whether the Humean can get them on the cheap; (2) consider worlds where the mosaic is very sparse, like a world containing a single particle moving forever in a straight line -- does the best-systems account struggle to say what the laws are there in a way that suggests the dial does real work?; (3) conversely, press the anti-Humean on whether governing laws are just the regularities "plus a glow," i.e. whether positing governance is explanatorily idle if the dial never makes an observable difference. I want a genuine verdict by the end, not "both views have insights" mush: either argue that the dial does nothing important (and hence that anti-Humean metaphysics is otiose), or pin down exactly where on the dial something of real philosophical value kicks in, and defend why that's worth the ontological price. Roughly 1200-1500 words. Don't just summarize the standard positions -- I can read the textbook myself; the point is to reason through the case.
