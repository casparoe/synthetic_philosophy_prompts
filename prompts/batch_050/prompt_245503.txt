So a friend and I invented this dumb little abstract game last weekend (players take turns claiming squares on a grid, first to connect two opposite edges wins, and having extra claimed squares is never a downside). Now we've got a bet running about whether the first player can always avoid losing. He keeps saying "strategy stealing settles it," and I remember that's the classic argument for Hex, but I've also heard that trick breaks down in games where an extra move can actually hurt you, like zugzwang situations in chess.

Write a dialogue between two equally sharp characters, one defending strategy-stealing as a rock-solid general argument, the other poking at its limits. They should actually respond to each other's points, not talk past each other. Have them get into: why exactly the argument works for games like Hex, whether it ever tells you what the winning strategy actually is, what goes wrong when extra pieces can be a liability, and whether the looser everyday version of the idea ("a weaker player can always just copy whatever the stronger one does") is really the same argument or just vibes. By the end they should reach a verdict on our bet.

After the dialogue, put the final word on the bet as the very last line in the form \answer{...} with a short phrase like "first player can guarantee at least a draw" or "strategy stealing doesn't settle it".
