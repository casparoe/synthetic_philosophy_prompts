Next week I am presenting a policy proposal to the safety board of a synthetic biology company: any single senior researcher should hold an absolute veto over the release of a newly engineered organism, on the grounds that such decisions suffer from the unilateralist's curse — when many people can each single-handedly trigger an action, it tends to be carried out by whoever misjudges it most optimistically, so dispersed individual initiative is systematically biased toward catastrophic error. The board includes decision theorists, lab biologists protective of researcher autonomy, and executives worried about bottlenecks, blame, and precedent. Prepare me for the question period: the objections each faction will raise, phrased in their own vocabulary; which objections target my vetocratic remedy specifically rather than the curse as a concept, and how serious each is; what I should concede up front; the one reply that has to land for the proposal to survive; and the objection I am hoping nobody asks.
