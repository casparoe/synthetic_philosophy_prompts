I'm in the third year of my philosophy degree and currently taking a metaphysics seminar on fundamentality and grounding, and I keep hitting the same wall in our discussions. One camp in the literature (and in my seminar) holds that reality must bottom out: grounding chains have to terminate in a fundamental level of facts or entities that are grounded in nothing further, because otherwise nothing would ever be fully real or fully explained. The other camp insists that infinite descent is perfectly coherent — every fact could be grounded in still further facts forever, with no bedrock — and that the demand for a bottom level is an unargued leftover from atomist or foundationalist pictures that physics itself no longer encourages. Both sides seem to concede that the other's picture is logically possible, and then proceed to call it unintelligible, or at least metaphysically pathological, anyway.

What puzzles me is that the debate feels stalled in a very specific way. It's not that anyone is waiting on new evidence; the arguments on offer — tiling-style arguments, appeals to the asymmetry and irreflexivity of grounding, thought experiments about infinitely divisible matter or endless chains of ontological dependence — have all been on the table for years, and nobody moves. My own hypothesis is that the two camps are not really disagreeing about the shape of reality at all, but about what grounding is supposed to be: one side treats it as a kind of metaphysical explanation that would be incomplete if it never terminated, the other as a mind-independent structuring relation that has no obligation to satisfy our explanatory preferences. But I'm not confident in that diagnosis, and it might be too charitable — maybe there is a genuine, substantive question here and one side is simply wrong.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? A verbal dispute trading on different senses of 'fundamental' or 'grounding'; a clash of bedrock intuitions about explanation that neither side can argue for without begging the question; a disguised formal question about whether a relation can coherently be non-well-founded; or a methodological dispute about what metaphysics owes to physics and to common sense? Please diagnose the impasse by engaging with what each side actually claims, and then tell me what, given your diagnosis, would count as genuine progress — a sharper distinction, a formal result, a case from fundamental physics, a reframing of the question — or whether you think no progress is to be expected and why. Please write your answer in British English.
