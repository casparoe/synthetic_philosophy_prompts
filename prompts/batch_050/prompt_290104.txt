I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm working on a piece about how we notice tools only when they stop working -- your mouse dies mid-sentence and suddenly you're aware of your hand, the cursor, the whole setup that was invisible a second ago. Fine, but here's the thing I keep bumping into that I don't have a word for: sometimes the same "suddenly seeing the tool" happens with no failure at all. I stare at a perfectly good door handle and it goes strange and object-like; I repeat a word while editing and it stops sounding like language. So it's not just breakdown that pulls the curtain back -- attention itself can do it, and afterwards the tool sinks back into invisibility and I can't keep it in view even if I try. Is there an established concept for this whole business -- the sliding between a tool being transparent-in-use and popping out as a weird mere object, regardless of whether anything broke? I know Heidegger is somewhere in the neighborhood but my sense is he mostly cared about the malfunction case, so tell me what the nearest concepts actually are, how what I'm describing differs from each, and which fits best -- or if nothing fits, coin something and give me boundary cases (e.g. does a musician becoming conscious of their fingers mid-performance count?). Don't just hand me a label, I need the carving. End your reply with a last line of the form \answer{best-fitting name or your coined term}.
