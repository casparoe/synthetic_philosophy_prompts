For my high school psychology class we've been studying Morgan's canon, the principle that an animal's behavior should never be explained by appeal to a higher mental faculty if it can be explained by a lower one. Please write a satirical piece of about 500–600 words in the form of a grant progress report from a fictional animal-behavior researcher who has embraced the canon so completely that she refuses to attribute any mental states whatsoever: her lab's border collie solves a puzzle box through "a fortuitous sequence of chained reflexes," her octopus's apparent planning is reclassified as "associative drift," and by the final section she has begun applying the same parsimony to her graduate students, whose conference presentations are logged as complex stimulus-response patterns that merely resemble communication. The report should remain deadpan throughout and never state the joke outright. The humor should emerge from a genuine tension in the debate: the canon is a methodological rule about parsimony, not a proof that animals lack minds, and treating it as an inflexible prohibition renders it unfalsifiable while quietly threatening to dissolve human cognition as well, since we only ever observe one another's behavior too. A reader acquainted with the animal cognition literature should recognize that the satire understands what the canon actually claims and should find the exaggeration fair.
