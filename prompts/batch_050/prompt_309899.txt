So i'm on a small grants committee at a philosophy-adjacent fund and we've earmarked enough money for roughly four person-years of conceptual work on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness -- the Frankish-style view that phenomenal consciousness is some kind of introspective illusion rather than a real, irreducible feature of minds. We've agreed not to relitigate whether illusionism is *true* (that's what everyone always does and it never goes anywhere). Instead we need to decide which open questions about or around the view are actually worth spending the person-years on.

Here are the candidates people have floated in our discussions, in no particular order:

1. The meta-problem: why do we judge and say that we're phenomenally conscious in the first place, and can a theory of those judgments be made testable rather than just asserted?
2. The regress worry: doesn't the illusion itself have to *seem* some way to the subject, and doesn't that seeming quietly reintroduce phenomenal properties through the back door?
3. Whether illusionism makes any prediction that distinguishes it from rival views (higher-order theories, global workspace, even panpsychism), or whether it's empirically vacuous and can only ever win on parsimony.
4. What illusionism implies for machine consciousness -- if seeming-to-feel is all there is to feeling, does that drastically lower the bar for when an AI system counts as mattering morally?
5. Why the illusion persists even in people who fully endorse illusionism, the way the Müller-Lyer lines still look unequal after you've measured them. Is that persistence a predicted feature, an embarrassment, or evidence against the view?

Give me a defended prioritization of these five for our four person-years -- and you're allowed to argue one of them should be dropped entirely or that we're missing a better candidate. What I want is the reasoning, not just the ranking: which questions are upstream of which (e.g., does answering one make another one trivial or moot?), which are tractable for philosophers without a lab, which ones would change the most downstream work if settled, and what would have to happen over the next couple years to make you reorder the list. Also tell me which consideration is most *overrated* -- the one that sounds decisive in committee discussions but wouldn't actually matter much for where the money should go.
