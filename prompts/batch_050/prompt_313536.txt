I am preparing a ninety-minute session for an undergraduate philosophy reading group on Newton's rotating bucket and the substantivalism–relationism debate about space. The students have read a short excerpt from the Scholium in advance; they are comfortable with philosophical argument but have no physics beyond school mechanics, and most have never thought carefully about what makes a frame of reference privileged. I do not want you to explain the debate to me. I want a plan for how I should teach it.

Specify the order in which the pieces must be introduced and justify each dependency: what must already be in place before the bucket's two stages (stationary water, then concave water) can land as an argument rather than a description, why the distinction between absolute and relative motion has to precede any mention of absolute space, and at what point the relationist counterproposal should be brought in so that it reads as a serious rival rather than a rearguard action.

The bucket will be the carrying case, but tell me what the presentation must include and must exclude for it to carry the weight: which features of the setup are doing argumentative work and which are historical decoration, and whether a second case (such as Newton's pair of globes joined by a cord) should reinforce or replace it at some point in the sequence.

Map the misconceptions I should expect, in roughly the order they will surface, and indicate exactly where in the sequence each should be headed off. I anticipate at least: the water climbs the wall because centrifugal force pushes it outward; if all motion is relative then Newton's argument is obviously confused and the debate is over before it starts; substantivalism means believing in a kind of invisible ether filling the universe; and general relativity settled the question in the relationists' favour. If you think the students will form others I have not listed, include those as well.

Finally, give me one question I can pose in the last ten minutes whose answer would show whether the group has grasped what the bucket actually establishes -- as opposed to having merely followed the historical story -- and tell me what I should openly refuse to cover at this level, saying so to the room rather than leaving it conspicuously absent.
