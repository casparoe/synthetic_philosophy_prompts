Got into an argument with my cousin at a family dinner last weekend and it's still bugging me. He's pre-med and was complaining about a lecture where the instructor said "race is a social construct" — he took this to mean the professor was denying that, say, sickle cell risk or skin cancer risk differs across groups, which he insists is just obviously false as a medical matter, and he was pretty worked up about it. I pushed back with something I'd picked up online, that calling something a social construct doesn't mean it isn't real, since money is supposedly a social construct and money is about as real as it gets — it can get you fed or get you killed. He said that proves too much, because then the slogan is compatible with race being totally biologically real, and the lecture clearly meant it as some kind of debunking, not as "race is real the way dollars are real." We ended up talking past eachother and I want to actually settle this for myself.

So: what are the distinct, precise claims someone could be making when they say "race is a social construct"? I can think of a few candidate readings myself — that racial categories don't line up with any natural biological grouping of humans, that the categories were invented at some point in history and could easily have been drawn differently, that race is flat-out unreal like ghosts or phlogiston, that race is real but only in the way money or national borders are real, or that the categories being constructed means we should stop using them (on medical forms, in research, wherever). I'm sure there are readings I'm missing and I'm sure some of mine collapse into eachother. Please lay them out as a menu, and for each one give me a verdict: is it trivially true, is it substantive but defensible, or is it actually false? And then the part I care most about — which reading does someone NEED if they want the slogan to do debunking work, i.e. to support conclusions like "checking race boxes on medical forms is pseudoscience" or "ancestry DNA test percentages are meaningless" or "racial health disparities can't have any biological component"? My suspicion is that the money reading is the one that's actually true, but it's also the one that gets the debunkers nothing, and maybe the slogan survives precisely by sliding between readings depending on who's pushing back. Tell me if that's unfair or if I'm onto something.

One more wrinkle that occurred to me while writing this: if the money analogy is the right one, does it follow that race could in principle be "decommissioned" the way a country can retire a currency — everyone just stops treating it as valid one day — or is that exactly the wrong lesson to draw, since money only works because a central authority maintains it and there's no central authority for race? Feel free to tell me the whole analogy is broken if it is. Please write the answer in American English.
