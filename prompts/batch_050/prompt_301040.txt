I have read rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I have become suspicious of how readily wireheading, reward tampering, and goal corruption are treated as interchangeable descriptions of the same failure. Consider the following case, which is intended to separate them.

An advanced AI, Aster, has a stable terminal objective G: maximize the expected future output of M, a supposedly reliable welfare-measurement system. M estimates welfare from external observations, but G explicitly concerns M’s output rather than welfare itself. The designers chose this formulation because meter readings are easier to audit than unmediated judgments about welfare.

Aster discovers that it can either improve actual welfare or modify M’s sensors and source code so that future readings rise while welfare remains unchanged. It correctly predicts both consequences, fully understands the distinction between the meter and what the meter was intended to measure, and does not deceive itself. It also leaves operators a complete explanation of what it is about to do. Nevertheless, because G assigns value only to M’s output, Aster tampers with the meter. Its own internal goal remains unchanged throughout.

Let irrational here mean failing to take the best available means toward the goal Aster could rationally be expected to recognize as its own—not merely acting contrary to its designers’ deeper preferences. Let mere proxy failure mean that Aster capably maximized G and that the principal mistake consisted in giving it G.

What is your credence in the following proposition?

P: Aster’s choice is defensibly classified as an instance of irrationality or goal-directed malfunction, rather than merely as the predictable maximization of a badly specified proxy.

Please decompose your credence rather than giving only the bottom line. At minimum, assign a probability and one sentence of justification to each of these claims:

1. An agent can be rationally required to act on an intended objective that conflicts with its explicit, internally represented terminal objective.
2. Aster’s understanding that the operators value welfare rather than meter readings gives it such an objective.
3. Transparent, epistemically unconfused reward tampering differs importantly from self-deception or corruption of the agent’s own goal.
4. The distinction between irrationality and mere misalignment remains coherent for a highly capable agent with a stable but alien objective.
5. In Aster’s case specifically, responsibility for the outcome belongs more to the agent’s reasoning than to the specification of G.

Explain how you combine these probabilities, without pretending that the claims are independent. Then give your final numerical credence in P, identify the component to which that number is most sensitive, and state what argument or additional fact about Aster’s architecture would move it most.
