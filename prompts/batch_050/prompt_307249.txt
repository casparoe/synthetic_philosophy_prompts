I teach a continuing-education seminar for working machine-learning engineers, and next month I have ninety minutes on the symbol grounding problem. Most of them will arrive believing that grounding was solved somewhere around multimodal pretraining: that a model trained on paired images and captions, or a language model wrapped around a vision encoder, has its symbols grounded by construction, and that Harnad's original problem is a pre-deep-learning artifact. I don't want to spend the session debunking them head-on; I want to structure the explanation so that their own framework generates the problem for them.

Plan the session rather than deliver it. Specifically: what has to be established first — I'm guessing the distinction between intrinsic and derivative intentionality, but tell me if that's the wrong foundation for this audience — and in what order the components must follow so that the regress argument lands as something they reconstruct rather than something I assert. Choose the one carrying example to build the session around: it needs to be a system they would naively call grounded, and it needs to be constructed so that the grounding fails in a way they can diagnose with their own tools (e.g., by ablating the modality they think is doing the work). Explain why the classic thought experiments won't carry the load here — I suspect the Chinese Room gets a shrug from engineers and Searle's framing invites them to argue about consciousness instead of grounding — and what the example has to lack (no embodiment as a cheat, no appeal to phenomenology) to keep the argument in their territory.

Then give me the misconception map: the objections these engineers will raise, in roughly the order they'll raise them, and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off rather than rebutted after the fact. I expect at least: grounding is just association at scale; referential success in downstream tasks is grounding enough; embodiment is an engineering detail, not a conceptual one; and the problem is really about consciousness so it's out of scope. For each, tell me where in the arc it will predictably surface and what feature of the ordering defuses it. Also specify what I should openly decline to resolve at this level — the relation between grounding and phenomenal consciousness, presumably, but say whether anything else should be bracketed and how to say so without sounding evasive. Finally, give me one closing exercise or question that would let me tell whether they've grasped the regress itself, as opposed to merely having been persuaded that current systems lack grounding.
