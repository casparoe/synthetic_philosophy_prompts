so my phil of mind group has been stuck for two weeks on hofstadter's careenium -- the two-level cocktail party picture where the little simmballs just bounce around following dumb local rules, but zoom out and the same system is describable as symbols pushing each other around and doing something like reasoning. our fight: is the symbol level doing any genuine causal work, or is it just a handy compression of the marbles?

Construct a thought experiment to sharpen this. Specs:

1. One physical system that admits two true descriptions: a low-level one (parts, local rules, nothing else going on) and a high-level one where symbol-ish states apparently produce outcomes. Do NOT use a brain or a computer -- everyone has cached takes on those. Pick something like a vending machine, a player piano, a termite mound, an analog thermostat network, whatever you can make vivid.
2. The case must contain a specific intervention where the two levels disagree about whether anything that matters has changed -- e.g., a change at the low level that the high level counts as no change at all (same symbol, different realization), or vice versa. If your two descriptions agree on everything that happens, you havent built the case yet.
3. Then run a counterfactual on it: would the outcome have been different if X? State which level gets the counterfactual right and why the other level's answer is worse (or why both are right but about different questions).
4. No demons, no laplacean superintelligences, no magic. Realistic enough that the intervention is something a person could actually do to the thing.

After the scenario: give hofstadter's verdict (the symbol level genuinely does the pushing; the low level is in some sense epiphenomenal relative to the real explanatory action) and the reductionist skeptic's verdict (all the causal work is marbles, symbol talk is bookkeeping), say which feature of your case is doing the work, and admit one way the side you pick could still lose.

600 words max.
