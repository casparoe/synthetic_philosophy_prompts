I co-host a philosophy of physics podcast, and for an upcoming episode we are interviewing a philosopher of physics who has spent her career defending Gödel's argument from the rotating universes: roughly, that since general relativity admits cosmological solutions in which matter rotates globally and no objective, observer-independent lapse of time can be defined — solutions in which closed timelike curves exist — the lapse of time cannot belong to what spacetime essentially is, and so time in our own universe, which appears not to rotate, is likewise ideal rather than objective. Our guest is formidable but has well-rehearsed moves. When pressed on the exoticness of Gödel's models (non-expanding, dust-filled, requiring a particular cosmological constant), she replies that physics tells us what is possible, not merely what is actual, and that possibilities constrain essences. When told that observations show no global rotation in our universe, she replies that the argument was never empirical in that way. I want to get past these openings.

Draft ten interview questions, in the order you would actually ask them, that press on the architecture of the argument rather than its reputation. The sequence should cover at least the following, in whatever order makes for the best conversation: the modal step from "time fails to lapse in some relativistically possible worlds" to "time is ideal in ours," and whether an essence-based principle really licenses that inference or merely relabels the disagreement; the scope of the conclusion, since local light-cone structure and causal order survive even in Gödel universes — does the argument reach all of temporal structure or only the flowing, A-theoretic part, and if only the latter, does she concede the title overstates it; the closed timelike curves themselves, including whether Gödel's own estimate that a rocket could traverse one only at astronomically prohibitive fuel cost means time travel is excluded in practice and whether that blunts or sharpens the argument; a parity challenge — if the existence of empty solutions does not make mass "ideal," why should the existence of time-less-lapse solutions make time ideal; and the relationship between the rotating-universe argument and the older argument from the relativity of simultaneity — which one is load-bearing for her, and would her view survive the discovery of a physically privileged cosmic frame.

For each question, write out the answer you expect her to give and a follow-up that closes off her standard escape route, so that the interview tightens rather than circles. Make the penultimate question one pitched at listeners who know special relativity but not general relativity, so the episode stays accessible, and make the final question ask what could in principle count against her view — an observation, a theorem, or a conceptual result — and whether she can name one. No biographical warm-up questions, and no questions that invite her simply to restate the argument she has already published.
