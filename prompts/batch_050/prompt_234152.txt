I'm preparing a panel interview with a typeface designer who defends what she calls letterform essentialism: the view that every 'a', from a Roman inscription to a pixel gridfont to a child's scrawl, shares some invariant "spirit" that makes it an 'a', and that a skilled designer can in principle articulate what that invariant is. Draft eight to nine questions that push on the view where it is actually weakest, each paired with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. At minimum, I want the set to cover: how she identifies the invariant when single-story and double-story 'a's seem to share almost no shape; whether a Cyrillic а (visually identical to a Latin 'a' but a different letter) counts, and if not, whether 'a'-ness is a matter of role and convention rather than form; whether a Greek α or a Fraktur a counts, given shared ancestry but divergent shape; whether there is a fact of the matter about borderline tokens, such as a heavily smudged character or a CAPTCHA glyph, and how she avoids an arbitrary cutoff; and what result would count as the view failing, since if any novel glyph a community comes to read as 'a' can be retrofitted into the spirit, the claim looks unfalsifiable. Structure the response in three sections in this order: Groundwork (two questions establishing what the invariant is supposed to be), Hard Cases (four to five questions on the cross-script and borderline-token problems), and Falsifiability and Close (one to two questions). No warm-up or biographical questions; every question should assume she has already stated the view.
