Over the past year I've had a running, exhausting dispute with my uncle, who has drifted into conspiracy theories about public health agencies, and the experience pushed me toward hinge epistemology in the style of Wittgenstein's On Certainty. The view, as I've absorbed it, is roughly this: rational evaluation can only take place against a background of commitments that are not themselves rationally evaluated — hinges that stay fixed while the door of inquiry turns. My uncle and I aren't really disagreeing about the evidence; we disagree about which hinges are in place (which sources count as testimony at all, what a plausible institutional motive looks like), and since hinges are arational rather than justified or unjustified, neither of us can give the other an argument that both must accept by shared standards. I've found this picture genuinely illuminating — it explains the impasse better than accusing each other of ignoring evidence — and I've provisionally adopted it.

What I now want is a map of the view's exposure: what would count against hinge epistemology, taken seriously as a philosophical position rather than a metaphor? I'm interested in several directions. First, internal pressure: does the picture collapse into either ordinary fallibilism (hinges turn out to be just very entrenched beliefs, revisable by the same machinery as everything else — scientific revolutions and religious deconversions look like cases where hinges were, in fact, argued out of place) or into a corrosive relativism where nothing distinguishes my hinges from my uncle's except whose tribe they belong to? Second, external pressure: are there phenomena the view cannot describe — for instance, the apparent fact that some hinge replacements are rational accomplishments and others are breakdowns — without quietly smuggling back the rational standards it disclaims? Third, meta-level: has the view been stated with so much flexibility (which commitments count as hinges being adjustable case by case) that it risks nothing, and is that a defect in a view of this kind or exactly what a "picture" of justification should be? I'd like the candidate defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with a clear statement in each case of what the world or our practices would have to look like for that defeater to materialize, and an honest note on which objections a committed hinge epistemologist would themselves concede as genuine trouble versus dismiss as misreadings of what hinges are supposed to be.
