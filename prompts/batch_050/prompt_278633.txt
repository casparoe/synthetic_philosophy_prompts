Pretend you're a philosopher of probability moonlighting as my search-and-rescue advisor — think carefully, and do a quick brainstorm of the possible update rules before you commit to an answer.

I coordinate a volunteer search team. A hiker is lost in a reserve split into two sectors, North and South. Everything we knew up to an hour ago put him at exactly 50/50 between the two. Then we recieve one radio message from a ranger who knows the terrain and this hiker's habits extremely well. The message conveys exactly one piece of information: given that the hiker is in the North sector, there's a 3/4 chance he's near the big waterfall. It says nothing at all about the South sector — no conditional, no base rate, nothing.

My dispatcher says the message is entirely about the North, so our North-vs-South odds stay 50/50 and we split the team evenly. My gut says learning that conditional should move the odds somehow, though honestly I can't even tell whether up or down. Who's right?

Give me your credence that he's in the North after the message, decomposed: your credence that simply conditionalizing on the learned conditional (and so leaving the sector marginals untouched) is the correct update rule here, your credence that whatever the correct rule is, it moves the 50/50 at all, plus any other terms you think belong — a number and one line of justification for each, then the combination. Finish by naming the term your final number is most sensitive to, and what additional fact (say, about how the ranger decided what to transmit) would shift it most.
