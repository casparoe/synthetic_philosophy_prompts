I’m a retired chemical engineer who has recently wandered into philosophy, and I’m presenting a paper to a mixed audience of statistical physicists, frequentist statisticians, and philosophers of science. My claim is that maximum entropy does not reveal uniquely rational priors: it only turns explicitly chosen constraints and symmetries into a probability distribution. I call my approach the constraint ledger, which records what information each constraint adds, what it excludes, and which conclusions would change if it were dropped. Prepare me for the Q&A in exactly nine numbered items, with no introduction or conclusion: items 1–7 should give the most likely and damaging objections in order, phrased as this audience would put them, with a two-minute reply and the weak point in that reply; item 8 should say what I should concede before anyone attacks; item 9 should be the question I’m really hoping nobody asks.
