Having retired from a career in condensed-matter physics, I have lately been reading philosophy of physics, and a distinction keeps occurring to me that I have not seen drawn sharply. Some quantum-gravity programmes seem to treat spacetime as emergent only in a modest sense: the smooth metric, or the continuum itself, is derivative, while an ordering of events or degrees of freedom remains primitive in the formalism. Others appear more ambitious, attempting to derive temporal succession itself rather than taking any ordering as given. Call these weak and strong emergence respectively. Please rate the following four research programmes — string theory in its holographic (AdS/CFT) form, loop quantum gravity, causal set theory, and asymptotic safety — on a scale from 0 to 10 for two things: (a) how much of spacetime they genuinely render emergent on this weak/strong distinction, judging by the mathematical structures they actually employ rather than by their practitioners' rhetoric; and (b) how well supported the emergence claim is in each case. Give a sentence of justification per score, and conclude by saying whether strong emergence, were it achieved, would amount to an explanation of time or merely a relocation of the puzzle.
