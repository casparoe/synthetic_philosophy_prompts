My political philosophy professor has been giving verdicts all term on whether proposed inequalities are justifiable under Rawls's difference principle. Her rulings so far:

- Incentive inequality: higher salaries for people with scarce skills, where the arrangement draws enough talent into those roles that the least advantaged end up with higher income than they would have under strict equality. Justifiable.
- Humiliating incentives: an inequality that raises the least advantaged group's income modestly but does so through working conditions and status hierarchies that seriously damage their self-respect and standing as equal citizens. Not justifiable, even though their paychecks grow.
- Rising tide: an inequality that makes everyone except the least advantaged much better off, while the least advantaged end up slightly worse off than under equality. Not justifiable, no matter how large the gains to others.
- Pareto improvement from equality: starting from equality, an arrangement that makes the least advantaged better off and the best off better off still, widening the gap. Justifiable.

Next week's case: a technological and institutional shift that substantially improves the income, opportunities, and self-respect of the second-least-advantaged group (say, semi-skilled workers), while leaving the least advantaged group exactly as well off as before in every measurable respect — income, wealth, opportunity, leisure, and social standing. The shift comes with increased inequality, and assume there is no feasible redesign that channels any of the gains to the least advantaged instead.

Predict her verdict on the new case, and say which principle she is implicitly running: the difference principle read as strict maximin over the single worst-off group, its lexical extension (maximize the worst-off, then the next-worst-off, and so on), a principle that is simply indifferent to changes that leave the worst-off untouched, or something else. Explain what each candidate principle predicts here, give your best guess about her ruling with reasons drawn from her track record, and note whether her earlier verdicts settle anything about which goods count in the relevant index. Finally, describe the further hypothetical case that would most cleanly distinguish the two strongest candidate principles if the new case doesn't.
