So here's my situation. I'm a novelist (two midlist literary novels, nothing you'd have heard of) and I somehow ended up co-hosting a small philosophy-ish podcast with a friend from grad school. Next week our guest is a philosopher of mind who defends Searle against the usual functionalist pile-on: he's firmly on the side of intrinsic intentionality, thinks the Chinese Room argument still lands, and is very practiced at answering the systems reply and the robot reply. Meanwhile I've been reading Dennett on the side and the example that stuck with me is the little vending machine designed to take US quarters that gets shipped somewhere and ends up happily taking some other coin that happens to match its detecting mechanism, so that what its states "mean" seems to depend on its history and what it's used for, not on anything inside the box. That whole idea -- meaning as derived, as conferred by selection and use rather than intrinsically present -- genuinely unsettles me as someone who invents people for a living, and I want the interview to go there.

Draft me eight to ten questions for him. No softballs and definately no "so tell us what got you into philosophy." I want questions that turn on the actual machinery of the debate: at least one on what makes the Chinese Room an argument rather than a just-so story that trades on our lack of intuition for room-sized symbol manipulators; at least one pressing him on whether the two-bitser case shows that intentionality can be fully grounded in selection history and function, and if not, what exactly is left over; one on how he handles the fact that nothing in his view seems able to distinguish genuine understanding from a sufficiently good simulacrum from the outside; and one on where he thinks the intrinsic meaning physically resides, since "the brain just has it" isn't an answer. For each question, give me a follow-up keyed to the reply he'll most likely give, since he tends to retreat to "you've just changed the subject" when cornered.

And for the closer: I want a question that puts my own skin in the game -- if his view is right, what should I say about the intentionality of my fictional characters, whose "thoughts" are obviously derived from mine, and is there any sense in which the distinction he's defending makes the difference between a character and a mere string of words? Include a follow-up there too, in case he dodges by saying fiction is beside the point.
