I help run my high school's philosophy society, and next month we are hosting a Q&A with a philosopher who defends anti-Humeanism about laws of nature: on her view, laws genuinely necessitate or govern what happens, rather than merely summarizing the pattern of particular facts, as the Humean best-system picture has it. Could you draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that does more than ask her to state the view? I want at least one question each on the following: what the metaphor of laws "governing" the world really adds over a description of the regularities, and how necessitation explains a pattern rather than just redescribing it; how we could ever come to know which necessities hold if they leave no trace beyond the regularities the Humean also accepts; whether her view gives a better account of why induction is rational than the best-system view does; and whether she takes laws to be contingent (in which case, what makes them lawlike rather than accidental?) or metaphysically necessary (in which case, why isn't the actual mosaic of events itself necessary?). For each question, please include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, so the exchange keeps moving rather than resetting, and end with one question about what observation or argument would lead her to abandon the view.
