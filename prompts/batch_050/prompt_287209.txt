I'm in the second year of a philosophy degree, and this term's philosophy of mind seminar has been working through Daniel Dennett on the intentional stance and his defence of "real patterns." Last week, over dinner, my flatmate -- an engineer, and a ruthless arguer -- tried to use Dennett to settle a running dispute about her smart-home setup. Her position: since Dennett holds that a belief is nothing over and above a real pattern in a system's behaviour, one that becomes visible when you adopt the intentional stance toward it, and since treating her thermostat as believing the room is cold and wanting to warm it predicts its behaviour perfectly (far more economically than the physical stance, and these days more reliably than the design stance, since nobody can follow the firmware after the latest updates), the thermostat literally believes the room is cold. And, she pressed on, if beliefs are supposed to be as real as centres of gravity, and the thermostat has them, then Dennett has no principled line to draw between the thermostat and the cat -- which means he must either embrace something like panpsychism about household appliances, or concede that turning the heating off when we go on holiday is at least a little bit wrong.

I couldn't say where the argument goes wrong, and our seminar leader, when I put it to her, only smiled and told me to think harder about which stances predict and which merely console, which I found more cryptic than helpful. So here is my question. On Dennett's framework, does the thermostat believe the room is cold? If it does, which horn of my flatmate's dilemma is Dennett supposed to take, and how does he live with it? If it doesn't, what exactly is the criterion it fails -- given that the intentional stance toward it is predictive, practically indispensable, and more dependable than the engineering documentation? I'd like to be able to show her precisely where she has misapplied the theory, rather than fall back on insisting that thermostats obviously don't have minds, which is exactly the question-begging conclusion she keeps accusing me of smuggling in.
