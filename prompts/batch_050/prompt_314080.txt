So a while back I read Gödel, Escher, Bach (the one with the tortoise and the crab canon and all that) and the bit that stuck with me most was Aunt Hillary -- the ant colony that talks back, where no individual ant knows anything but the colony as a whole is supposedly a kind of person. I've been chewing on this ever since, and I've been keeping a list of reasons why a real ant colony (or a beehive or whatever) probably CAN'T actually have a mind, because my gut says the whole idea is nuts but I want the gut feeling backed up. Here's my list so far:

1. There's no boss. In my brain there's at least a sense that "I" am in charge, but an ant colony has no central controller at all, it's just ants bumping into each other.
2. The parts are the wrong kind of thing. Neurons are dumb but they're at least wired together physically; ants just wander around and touch antennae sometimes, so the "signals" are slow and lossy in a way brain signals aren't.
3. Ants leave and join all the time and each ant has its own little life and interests, whereas my neurons are stuck with me, so the colony doesn't have the unity a mind needs.
4. You can mess with a colony in ways you can't mess with a brain -- split it in two with a piece of glass, swap ants between colonies -- and it just shrugs, which seems nothing like what happens if you did that to me.

What I want is ONE more objection, genuinely different from these four -- not a rewording, not a special case (so no "but there's no center!" in new clothes), and not just two of mine stapled together. And then, since I suspect my gut might be wrong: say honestly whether you think the objection survives the obvious comeback that everything on my list also kinda applies to a brain if you zoom in far enough. Keep the whole answer under 250 words.
