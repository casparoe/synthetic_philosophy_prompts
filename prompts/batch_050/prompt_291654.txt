Two friends of mine have been going back and forth for weeks about something that happened at their volunteer-run community radio station and they've now roped me in, so I'm outsourcing to you. The station's longtime treasurer, Marta, discovered that a donor they'd been taking money from for years made his fortune in a way she finds genuinely repugnant (nothing illegal, but bad). Without telling anyone she emailed the donor's assistant and returned the last three years of donations, about 40k, saying the station could no longer accept the money. The money is now gone for good and the station's transmitter upgrade is cancelled. When the board found out, half of them were furious and half were secretly relieved, but the argument between my two friends is about the general principle, not Marta specifically.

Nadia says Marta did something structurally wrong even if her judgment about the donor happened to be right: when many people can each irreversibly commit a group to something, and they each act whenever their own private judgment says go, the group will systematically end up doing the thing the most optimistic or most scrupulous or most alarmed member wants, not the thing that's actually best. She says the fact that one person among twelve thought returning the money was clearly right is barely evidence it was right, because in any group of twelve someone is always the outlier, so unilateral irreversible action is biased from the start and the fix is mandatory deliberation before anyone pulls a lever like that.

Ben says this sounds clever but collapses into a counsel of cowardice: every whistleblower, every person who refuses to go along, is by definition the outlier in some group, and Nadia's rule just launders conformism into a principle of procedure. He says you can't treat your own judgment as a discountable noisy signal at the very moment you're deciding what to do, since deliberation only helps if someone first judges that deliberation is needed, which is itself a private judgment, so Nadia's view eats itself. Nadia replied that there's an asymmetry he's ignoring: Marta's email can't be unsent, whereas waiting two weeks for a board meeting loses nothing except delay, and when irreversibility is one-sided you should buy the option of more information. Ben said that's rigged too, because delay is never free in real life -- donors notice, momentum dies, and 'wait for consensus' in practice means 'never' more often than 'later.'

So: go through it and tell me who's right about which specific move, whether Nadia's regress worry against Ben's regress argument lands, and what if anything they're both missing. I have no horse in this race, i just want to be able to settle it at the pub next week. Please write your answer as continuous prose like an email -- no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
