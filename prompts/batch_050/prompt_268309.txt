A handbook chapter on methods in ethics I'm reading summarizes reflective equilibrium like this: "Reflective equilibrium, a method introduced by John Rawls, justifies moral principles by coherence: you collect your moral intuitions about cases, formulate principles that would account for them, and then prune and adjust the principles until they fit the intuitions — at which point the principles count as justified. Because people's intuitions differ across cultures and upbringings, different people will reach different equilibria, so the method cannot deliver objective moral truth; it merely rationalizes, in systematic form, whatever the person already believed. Critics therefore regard it as a sophisticated relativism. Rawls himself used the method to derive his two principles of justice."
