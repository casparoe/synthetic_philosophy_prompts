I mod a mid-sized discussion forum that spun off from the rationalist blogosphere a few years back (think: decision theory threads, the occasional 4000-word post about whether simulation arguments are decision-relevant, etc). Since about a year ago we've been getting a steady trickle of accounts that are probably LLM output -- and I say probably because our current detection method is vibes, and the vibes disagree. One mod flags anything with em-dashes and "it's not X, it's Y" constructions, another thinks half the flagged accounts are just non-native English speakers, and last month we banned an actual human grad student who wrote like GPT partly because she'd learned English from chatbots, which was embarassing for everyone involved.

So I want an actual procedure. But here's the twist that made me think this was worth asking instead of just googling spam-filter advice. I've read Turing's original imitation game paper (well, most of it), and I keep noticing that people treat "the Turing test" as a device for detecting minds, when the interesting thing about it is almost the opposite: Turing's whole move was that outward behavior is the only criterion you could ever get, and even he framed it as an imitation game, where the point is what the judge fails to distinguish rather than what's actually going on inside. Meanwhile I don't even care whether an account has a mind -- I care whether there's a human on the other end who can be held responsible for the words, form intentions, get offended, come to a meetup. So I suspect the philosophy here cuts against the naive version of my own job: a Turing-style test measures indistinguishability, but what I need is more like a provenance test, and those can come apart in both directions (convincing bots, humans who pass as bots). But I'm not confident I've got that distinction right, or what follows from it for practice.

What I want: a checklist / decision procedure I can run in 10-15 minutes per flagged account, roughly 20 flags a week, usable by mods with no ML background, where bans need a second mod's sign-off and can be appealed. Each step should be explicit about what it's actually testing, given the above -- i.e. which steps can only ever give behavioral evidence, what behavioral evidence can and can't support, and what to do when the honest answer is "can't tell." Mark clearly which steps are real tests and which are judgment calls dressed up as procedure. Then apply it to three hard cases: (1) an account posting fluent, topically perfect comments at 3am nightly that never responds when addressed directly; (2) a non-native speaker with years of posting history whose style has drifted "AI-ish" recently; (3) an account that cheerfully admits to being a bot and asks to stay because its posts are good. My guess is the procedure handles (1) easily and gives me an answer I won't like on at least one of the other two -- tell me which, and also what the procedure fundamentally cannot do no matter how well-designed it is.
