In §7 ("Learning Machines") of Alan Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (Mind, 1950), Turing argues that an education conducted through punishments and rewards alone is badly information-limited: a teacher whose only channel to the pupil is approval and disapproval can transmit no more information than the number of such interventions carries, which he takes to be far too little to account for something like a child's mastery of a poem. I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science writing a survey chapter on what, beyond scalar feedback, machine learning systems actually need in order to be "educated" rather than merely conditioned. Please construct a decision tree that sorts the main contemporary positions on this question, taking Turing's information-limit argument as the trunk. The first node should ask whether Turing's bound still bites given modern reward channels (dense shaping rewards, learned reward models, textual feedback rather than single bits), and subsequent nodes should separate positions by further diagnostic claims: whether self-supervised pretraining smuggles in the missing information so that reward need only select among already-learned capacities; whether the deficit can only be made up by built-in structure (inductive biases, architectures, innate priors) rather than by any richer training signal; whether genuinely interactive, social feedback differs in kind from reward at all; and whether Turing's framing mistakenly assumes the goal is transmitting content rather than shaping search. Each branch node should be phrased as a question someone could answer without knowing which position it leads to. Each leaf should carry a named position (e.g., a modern reinforcement-learning-sufficiency view, a scaling/self-supervision view, a nativist or strong-priors view, a social-interactionist view, and a deflationary reading on which Turing himself never intended rewards as the whole story), plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, and note any logically available combination of answers that no one defends, with a brief diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
