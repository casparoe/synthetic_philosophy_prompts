I write a column about AI and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I keep circling back to a question I can't settle, so I'd love some help. You know Dennett's old thought experiment with the soft-drink machine, the two-bitser, built to tell real US quarters from slugs, and then shipped to Panama where it happily takes the local coin that looks and behaves like a quarter? Dennett's line, as I understand it, is that the machine's "quarter-detecting" state is only about quarters because of what we designed and installed it for -- derived intentionality, not the original kind he thinks Searle wrongly reserves for brains. Fine. But I've been polling my own gut on a bunch of cases and my verdicts don't seem to add up to anything coherent. Here they are. (1) The two-bitser itself: its inner state is about quarters, but only derivatively -- take away the designers and users and it's just a clump of metal doing physics. (2) A thermostat: no aboutness at all, it's too simple, it doesn't "think" it's cold. (3) A bee's waggle dance: I feel like this one is genuinely about the nectar, full stop, no designer needed -- natural selection did the conferring, and that seems to me as good as an engineer. (4) A chess engine's evaluation function: I'd say it's about the position only in the derived sense, like the vending machine. (5) A big language model trained by gradient descent on scraped text, which now talks fluently about quarters, nectar, whatever: I genuinely can't decide whether its talk is derived aboutness (like the chess engine, just fancier), or more like the bee, because the "designer" didn't hand-craft any representations, selection-ish optimization just shaped them the way selection shaped the bee.

So here's what I want: act as a professional philosopher of mind who knows the Dennett–Searle fight cold, and think carefully, ideally brainstorming a couple of candidate principles before committing. I want a precise principle (or two candidates, if one won't do it) that captures as many of my verdicts as possible -- tell me which case motivates each clause of the principle. The obvious tension I can see myself: if selection confers original-ish aboutness on the bee dance, it's hard for me to deny the same status to the trained language model, since gradient descent isn't an intentional designer either -- but my gut resists giving the LLM what I gave the bee, and I don't know if that's principled or just squeamishness. So: does any coherent principle save all five verdicts, or does consistency force me to flip one? If one has to go, say which and why. Also tell me what your proposed principle predicts for two cases I haven't listed: a smoke alarm, and a homing pigeon's internal compass "representation" of home. I'm going to fact-check the piece against whatever you give me, so be precise about where you think Dennett himself would land versus where the principle actually lands, and flag anywhere those come apart.
