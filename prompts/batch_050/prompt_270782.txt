Here's my half-formed idea. The hiddenness debate (Schellenberg's argument, roughly: a perfectly loving God wouldn't allow there to be people who are open to him and seeking in good faith yet still don't believe; but there are such people) seems to me to run entirely on this notion of the "nonresistant nonbeliever", and both sides just argue past each other about whether such people exsist — atheists point at honest seekers, theists reckon there's always some resistance lurking if you dig deep enough. But my own year makes me think "resistance" is doing massive unexamined work in all this. At some points I was intellectually willing but emotionally braced against the whole thing, at other points the reverse, and the mix shifted week to week. If resistance actually comes in kinds (epistemic, affective, practical, maybe more), in degrees, and over time, then "nonresistant" stops being a property you can simply check a person for — and the standard argument, plus the standard replies to it, might need rebuilding from the ground up. My suspicion is the atheist's premise and the theist's reply don't get murkier symmetrically, but I can't yet see how.
