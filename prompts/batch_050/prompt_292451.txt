For the past three years I've been paying for a medication for a woman in my extended family overseas, call her Ana, who can't afford it herself. Without the drug her condition will probably kill her within a few months. It costs about $400 a month, which was fine while I had my old job, but I've been laid off and keeping up the payments would now mean draining savings my own family will genuinely need over the next year.

Here's what I keep turning over. If I stop, Ana dies, and I can't shake the feeling that I killed her -- I was the thing keeping her alive and I pulled the plug. But there are thousands of people in exactly Ana's situation whom I've never lifted a finger for, and I don't feel like I killed any of them. If some stranger died last week because I didn't send her $400, that's a tragedy; if Ana dies because I stop sending it, that feels like something I did. My brother says this is pure sentimentality -- either way it's just a decision about where my money goes, and the dead woman doesn't care whether I happened to know her name. Something in me resists that hard, but whenever I try to explain why stopping is worse than never having started, I can't land on anything that isn't basically "because I started."

So: is he right that the two cases are morally identical? If there is a real difference, what does it actually consist in -- is it about what I cause, about Ana's reliance on me, about promises I never quite made but she reasonably assumed? And if there is a difference, how much weight does it carry -- could it really oblige me to keep paying at serious cost to my own kids' security, or does it only explain my guilt feelings without justifying them?
