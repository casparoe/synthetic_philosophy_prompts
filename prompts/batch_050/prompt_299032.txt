so i help run the satire page of our philosophy dept newsletter and the next issue's theme is "taking your premises seriously." i want a piece that goes after the marriage of panpsychism and hedonic utilitarianism — the combo where experience (and therefore pleasure/pain) is a basic feature of matter everywhere, and impartial aggregation says every bit of it counts the same.

Form: a deadpan charity-evaluation write-up, in the house style of a hyper-quantitative effective-altruism evaluator — cost-effectiveness estimates, confidence intervals, hedged prose, a "room for more funding" section — reviewing a fictional nonprofit called the Center for Subatomic Welfare with complete professional seriousness. 600-800 words.

The humor has to come from actually following the premises out loud, not from strawmen. Beats it should hit:

- The ubiquity premise: if proto-experience is fundamental to matter, then the vast majority of the universe's experience happens below the level of brains, so by headcount the moral circle is roughly 10^40 electrons wide and all human welfare spending is a rounding error in the impact analysis.
- The combination problem as a measurement-and-verification headache: the evaluator can't determine whether a rock's trillions of micro-experiences combine into one big suffering subject (in which case rocks are the most miserable beings on earth and the top funding priority) or remain disconnected experiential "dust" (in which case cost-effectiveness drops by many orders of magnitude). Have them propose a discount rate for non-combining minds, given with absurd precision.
- Sign uncertainty: a footnote conceding the evaluator has not established whether electron experience is net positive or net negative, so the recommended intervention might be astronomically good or astronomically bad — expected value still enormous, obviously.
- Interventions assessed with a straight face: keeping matter near absolute zero to reduce agitation, a cautiously negative stance on particle accelerators (brief, intense lives), and longtermist support for the heat death of the universe as the only permanent solution to suffering.
- A "room for more funding" section concluding the organization could productively absorb literally all the money on earth.

Keep it deadpan the whole way through — no winking, no "of course this is absurd" asides — but legible: the reader should be able to tell the target is the way aggregation plus expected-value reasoning lets ubiquitous sentience swamp everything else in the moral calculus. Ideally an actual panpsychist or a serious EA quantitative type reads it and winces in recognition rather than complaining they were misrepresented. Invented names only, don't go after any real person.

Start with the piece itself, no preface or explainer before it.
