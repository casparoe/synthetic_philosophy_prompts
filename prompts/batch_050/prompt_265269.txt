So I run a little community podcast (just a hobby, I have no philosophy training whatsoever) and somehow I got an actual philosopher to agree to come on. She defends the view that people should need a license from the state before they're allowed to have and raise kids, same basic idea as a driver's license. I've been chewing on this for weeks on my own and I keep going back and forth on it.

Can you draft 8 to 10 interview questions for me? I dont want softballs where she just restates the pitch. Specifically I want:

- At least one question pushing on the driving-car analogy, because it seems like driving tests measure whether you have a *skill*, but isn't most terrible parenting more about selfishness or temper than not knowing how? And a follow-up for when she says we license people for all sorts of risky activities, so why not the riskiest one.
- A question about the fact that we already make adoptive parents jump through a million hoops while anyone can have a biological kid with zero oversight — is her view just making things consistent, and if so, follow up on whether consistency is really a good enough reason for something this huge.
- Something on who actually writes the test and administers it, and the obvious worry that it ends up filtering out poor people or people the government just doesn't like. With a follow-up for when she says "we'd design it fairly."
- A question about enforcement, like what physically happens if an unlicensed couple has a baby anyway — does the state take the kid, fine them, what? This seems like the part nobody wants to say out loud.
- And one question connecting it to the idea I've seen floating around that kids have a right to an "open future" — like, would licensing actually protect the kid's future, or does it just mean the state gets to define in advance what a good upbringing looks like, which feels like its own threat to the kid's openess?

For every question give me a follow-up based on the answer she's most likely to give, so Im not left standing there with nothing when she gives the polished response.
