ok so i need a poem for the last meeting of my phil of mind reading group, we've been doing the unit on dreaming -- malcolm's argument that dreams aren't experiences had during sleep (just whatever the waking report settles) and dennett's cassette-theory-ish idea that the "dream" might get composed/inserted at the moment of waking rather than played back from the night.

write a poem, maybe 20-28 lines, blank verse or loose unrhymed lines, in the voice of someone mid-way through telling a dream they just woke from. the key constraint: the poem should contain the seam of waking somewhere inside it, but it should be genuinely ambiguous which lines are the remembered dream and which are the telling of it -- like the reader should be able to scan for the moment of waking and find two or three candidate spots, none of them certain. the ambiguity shouldn't be a gimmick, it should be the point: someone who knows the malcolm/dennett debate should read it and go "oh, the poem is doing the thing where the report is all we have and the experience is unreachable." but dont name malcolm or dennett or "verification" or anything, and no winking at philosophy either -- it should also just work as a poem about waking up confused in a dark room.

bonus if there's some small concrete detail in the dream (a sound, an object) that appears on both sides of the seam in slightly different form, since that's exactly the kind of thing that makes you wonder whether the memory was there before you went looking for it
