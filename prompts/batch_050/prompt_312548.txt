I'm putting together a little quiz for my phil of mind reading group — "which theory of consciousness do you actually hold?" — but the gimmick is that instead of asking about zombies or Mary's room, it should sort people purely by which animals they think are conscious. Act as a professional philosopher of mind who also keeps up with the empirical consciousness science, and before writing the quiz itself, brainstorm where the main theories actually diverge on real animals (not just on principle).

The quiz itself: 8–10 questions, each built around a concrete creature (I definitely want an octopus, a honeybee, a hermit crab, a fruit fly, a nematode, and a zebrafish larva in there; pick the rest yourself), with two or three answer options per question that someone could choose without knowing any theory names. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the main positions: global workspace, higher-order theories, IIT, local-recurrence/first-order views, and marker-based views that treat some cognitive capacity (say, unlimited associative learning) as the transition point to conciousness. If two theories land on exactly the same animal pattern, don't split them artificially — say so and mention what non-animal question would pull them apart. Also, I'd like at least one question to be a trap for IIT sympathizers, forcing a choice between trusting the formalism and an intuition about a specific animal.

End with a short note on which mapping in your scoring key you're least confident about, and what empirical finding or argument would change your mind there.
