So I work as a custodian in an apartment building, and something happened last winter that I've been chewing on ever since. It iced over overnight, I forgot to salt the front steps before my shift ended, and a tenant slipped and broke her wrist. Everyone — my boss, the tenant, even me — said it happened because I didn't salt the steps. Fair enough. But here's the thing: a dozen residents also walked right past the unsalted steps that morning and didn't salt them either, and nobody said the fall happened because of what they didn't do. Physically it's the same situation, right? Nothing was done by any of us. But somehow my nothing caused it and their nothing didn't.

Once I noticed this I started seeing it everywhere. My neighbor asked me to water her plants while she was away, I forgot, they died, and I "killed her plants." Meanwhile some delivery guy walked past those plants every day and also didn't water them, and nobody would ever say he killed them. And it gets weirder — sometimes there's no person involved at all. People say stuff like "the lack of rain killed the crops," and that sounds totally normal, even though "lack of rain" isn't a thing that exists or does anything, it's just rain not happening. So we seem to treat absences as causes, but only the right absences, and I can't figure out what makes an absence the right kind. It seems to have something to do with what was supposed to happen, but "supposed to" isn't a physical thing either, it's just what people expected.

Is there a name for this? I don't just want a label though, I want to understand the shape of it. Like, what exactly separates a nothing that causes something from a nothing that doesn't? Does it have to be a *legitimate* expectation — if a tyrant expects his guard to shoot protesters and the guard refuses, would the tyrant be right (just in the cold causal sense) that the guard's inaction caused the uprising to succeed? That feels wrong to say but I can't tell why, if my case works the same way. And is the crops case the same phenomenon as mine, or is that just a loose way of talking? If there's an established concept that covers this, tell me how the thing I'm describing fits or strains against it, and if there isn't one, give it a name and define it precisely enough that I can sort hard cases with it.
