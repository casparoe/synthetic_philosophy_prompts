A friend of mine works in the cognitive science of religion — evolved cognition, ritual, costly signaling, that whole literature. Whenever we're out for drinks I like to test her with wierd edge cases, and her calls so far have been:

- Village ancestor veneration, with food offerings for the dead and a big yearly festival: religion, obviously.
- A Theravada meditation community that takes karma and rebirth seriously but has no gods at all: religion.
- A Marxist reading group that treats Marx's writings as basically beyond criticism and meets every week: not a religion.
- Small-town high school football, with its totems, chants, pre-game rituals, and ecstatic Friday nights: not a religion.
- A guy who lives alone, fiercely believes in ghosts and astrology, but has no practices and no community around it: she said those are religious beliefs, but it's not a religion.
- People who use a meditation app every morning just for stress: not a religion.

Now here's the new case I want to spring on her. There's a group called the Continuants. They have a founding text they treat as unalterable, weekly gatherings with singing, an initiation rite, dietary taboos, pilgrimages to the founder's house, and a strict moral code. Their core belief is that after you die you'll be revived by future technology, so every member is signed up for cryonics. But they insist none of this is supernatural — they say it's just an empirical bet about where medicine and computing are headed.

What do you think she'll say about the Continuants? Work out at least two different rules that both fit all six of her earlier calls, say what each rule predicts about the Continuants, tell me which prediction you'd actually bet on and why, and then give me one more case that would genuinely tell the rules apart.
