Write an essay, in formal British English, on the following question in Kantian ethics: if Kant is right that one may not lie to the murderer at the door, may one nonetheless mislead him by means of true statements or conspicuous silences? Reach a definite verdict and defend it.

Background. In his reply to Benjamin Constant, Kant notoriously maintained that it would be wrong to lie even to a would-be murderer who asks whether his intended victim is hiding in your house. Set aside, for the purposes of this essay, whether that conclusion is correct; grant it arguendo. The controversy your essay should address concerns two neighbouring tactics one might substitute for the outright lie:

(a) Paltering: answering with a statement that is literally true but engineered to produce a false belief — for instance, "He was at the market this morning", uttered so as to suggest that he is out, when in fact he is upstairs.

(b) Strategic reticence: refusing to answer, changing the subject, or answering a different question from the one asked, in the hope that the murderer will draw the convenient inference on his own.

Call the position that these tactics remain permissible where the outright lie is not the loophole view; call the position that they stand or fall together with the lie the unified view.

Your essay should accomplish all of the following:

1. Reconstruct the strongest available version of Kant's argument against lying to the murderer, rather than the weakest or most familiar one. Consider in particular whether the argument turns on the falsehood of what is said, on the deception of the hearer, on the abuse of another's rational agency, on the corruption of one's own maxim, or on the liar's assumption of responsibility for consequences he cannot foresee — and say which of these you take to be the core of the position.

2. Apply your reconstruction to (a) and (b). Does the argument, as you have reconstructed it, condemn paltering and strategic reticence as well, or does it leave one or both untouched? Pay attention to whether your reconstruction draws a principled line between the two tactics or sweeps them together with the lie, and whether any line it draws is one a Kantian is entitled to draw.

3. Address the most serious objection to whichever view you favour. Against the loophole view: that it converts a rigorous ethic into a casuistry of technicalities, condemning the very same deception when phrased one way and licensing it when phrased another. Against the unified view: that it proves too much, since it appears to condemn not only misleading truths but silence itself, and it is hard to see how an ethics of honesty could forbid saying nothing at all.

4. Examine whether a distinction between actively causing a false belief and merely allowing one to arise does genuine moral work here, and whether it maps onto the boundaries among lying, paltering, and reticence in the way its defenders suppose.

5. Conclude with a defended verdict: state which view is correct on the best reconstruction of the Kantian position, and say explicitly what, on your conclusion, one is and is not permitted to do at the door.

The essay should run to roughly 1,000–1,500 words and should argue from the structure of the Kantian position itself, rather than merely reporting what commentators have said about it.
