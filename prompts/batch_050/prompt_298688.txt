Here's my talk in a nutshell. I'm defending the claim that Goodman's grue paradox is not really a problem about induction at all -- it's a problem about language-dependence, and it dissolves once you require that a predicate is only confirmable if it's "observationally closed," meaning that whether it applies to an object can in principle be checked on that object alone at a single time, without reference to some external clock date. So "green" passes (you look at the emerald, you see), "grue" fails (you'd have to know whether t has passed, which is a fact about the calendar, not the emerald), and the new riddle of induction turns out to be an artifact of letting smuggled-in temporal indexicals count as legitimate evidence predicates. My view, which I'm calling the closure criterion, is that this also explains why grue feels cheaty in a way that mere weird-but-projectible predicates don't.
