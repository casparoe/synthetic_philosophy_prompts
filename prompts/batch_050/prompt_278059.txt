I am preparing the position statement for a debate volume on providence, in which each contributor states their commitments in the first chapter and is then pressed by the others. Before I send mine off, I want an audit of whether my package actually hangs together, stated as I would be prepared to defend it:

(1) Molinism. Prior — logically, not temporally — to the creative decree, God knows all true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, and providence consists in his selection, in light of them, of which feasible world to actualize.

(2) Libertarian freedom. A free creaturely action is determined by nothing outside the agent's will at the moment of choice — not by character, circumstances, or the divine decree — and I have no interest in compatibilist rescue maneuvers.

(3) Truthmakers. Every contingent truth is made true by some concrete reality; there are no ungrounded contingent truths floating free of being. This is a commitment I have defended in print in the metaphysics of modality, and I would rather not disown it.

(4) Divine aseity. God's knowledge is in no way causally dependent on creatures; nothing outside God settles what God knows.

(5) The purity of permission. God never intends evil, and his permission of sin is not a means to any end; the distinction between intending and permitting does genuine moral work in providence, not merely rhetorical work.

(6) Meticulous, risk-free providence. God's creative choice guarantees the entire history of the actual world, down to particular sins; God takes no risks, contra the open theist.

Please do the following. First, identify which pairs or triples among these are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; I expect at least one apparent tension here dissolves on inspection, and I want to see you discriminate rather than flag everything. Second, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each state what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me relative to the rest of the set. Third, tell me which single revision would restore the most consistency for the least loss. Two things I ask you to bear in mind: do not assume that (1) and (2) together already settle the matter of (6), since reconciling those is precisely what the view is built for; and do not assume I have already noticed where the real problems are — I have deliberately not indicated which commitment I would surrender first, because I do not know myself.
