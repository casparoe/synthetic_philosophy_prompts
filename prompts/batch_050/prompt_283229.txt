Running a reading group on Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" and I want to go in with a checklist rather than vibes. Before we argue about whether his attack lands, I want the spec first: what does any account of analyticity actually have to deliver to count as an adequate theory, and thereby a real answer to Quine? Which jobs was analyticity hired to do (explain apriority? necessity? why math and logic feel different from physics?), which stock examples does it have to classify the way everyone classifies them ("no bachelor is married" vs "no unmarried man is married" vs "all bachelors are under six feet" or whatever), and is it allowed to help itself to synonymy, necessity, and semantical rules or does it have to build the distinction out of materials Quine would accept? Give me the desiderata with the motivation behind each, and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- that part is the real payoff.

One item on the list I specifically want vindicated or rejected. Early in the paper Quine writes: "Things had essences, for Aristotle, but only linguistic forms have meanings. Meaning is what essence becomes when it is divorced from the object of reference and wedded to the word." Is he right that any meaning-based account of analyticity inherits Aristotelian essentialism, just relocated from things to words -- so that escaping essentialism is a genuine requirement the theory must meet -- or is that Quine poisoning the well by attaching the scary label? And once the list is set: which single desideratum knocks out the most attempted defenses (Grice & Strawson, Carnap-style meaning postulates, two-dimensionalist moves, etc.)? Brief scoring against the list is fine but the criteria are the deliverable, not the scorecard.
