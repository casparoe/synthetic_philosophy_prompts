So I'm building out a personal wiki page on the new riddle of induction (yes I know, very 2009-LW of me) and I want to catalog the main *moves* people make against grue before I write my own take. Here's what I have so far:

1. "Grue is time-indexed, green isn't, so green is the real predicate." I mostly consider this dead since Goodman himself pointed out that if you take grue and bleen as primitive, then green is the one with the weird disjunctive definition. The asymmetry has to be established, not assumed.
2. Entrenchment: green wins because it's actually been projected by us, historically. (Goodman's own answer, and it always felt more like sociology than epistemology to me.)
3. Lewis-style naturalness / reference magnetism: green carves nature at a joint, grue doesn't, and meaning latches onto the natural candidate.
4. The Bayesian/Solomonoff move: grue hypotheses just get lower prior because they're longer to describe; the riddle is really a question about priors and Occam.
5. The ML-statistics gloss: grue is overfitting, you should pick the simplest curve that fits the data. (Arguably #4 in a trench coat, but people state it differently.)

Give me one more response that has a genuinely different structure from all five -- not a variant of "simplicity priors," not a restatement of naturalness, and not "define time-indexedness more carefully." Bonus points if it actually engages with the symmetry point in #1 rather than dodging it. And then tell me honestly whether your candidate solves the riddle or just relocates it somewhere harder to see.
