ok so I'm writing a novel set during a two-week standoff between two nuclear states -- submarines gone dark, an alert level raised by accident, the whole thing. My plot hinges on both leaders making threats that are kind of insane to carry out, and I keep reaching for "deterrence logic" as the thing that makes the plot run, but whenever I read actual deterrence theory I get a pile of arguments instead of a spec. I don't want the arguments yet. I want the requirements list first, so I can check any theory (and my plot) against it.

So: write me the desiderata a theory of nuclear deterrence has to meet to count as an adequate explanation of how threats between nuclear states actually work. I mean things like -- it has to explain why a threat you'd be crazy to execute can still deter, it has to handle the case where backing down is rationally better at every single moment yet both sides stay in, it has to say something about why states deliberately put their own retaliation partly out of their control (delegating, automating, burning bridges), and so on. You know this territory better than me, so build the real list, not just my three. For each requirement give the case or consideration that motivates it, in a sentence or two, in language a non-specialist can follow.

Two things I specifically want. First, mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable for any theory and which are really just the hobbyhorse of one camp (like an insistence on perfect rationality, or on outcomes being determined by capabilities alone). Second -- and this is the bit I care most about -- if some subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly and say which one has to go. I have a strong suspicion "the threat must be credible" and "executing it must be rational" can't both survive, but I want your call, not mine confirmed.

Which brings me to the tone: commit. Don't hand me a menu of options and say it depends on your school of thought. Pick which requirements you'd actually keep, which you'd drop as prejudice, and which single requirement kills the most naive theories, and argue for those choices. I'd rather have a spec I can disagree with than a balanced survey.

Oh and one practical constraint: my plot needs at least one moment where deterrence *fails* locally without the theory collapsing -- so flag which requirement, if relaxed, buys me that scene without wrecking everything else. tyia!
