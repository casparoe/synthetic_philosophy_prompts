Write an essay on the KK principle — the thesis that if S knows that p, then S is in a position to know that S knows that p — using the following case as your testbed. Dr. Ames, a radiologist, reviews scans over a twelve-hour shift. On scan 40 she spots a faint shadow and thereby knows the nodule is present. By scan 400, fatigue has imperceptibly degraded her acuity: confronted with an indistinguishable shadow, she no longer knows, though nothing she could notice marks the transition. If KK held for her, the boundary between scan 40 and scan 400 would have to be one she could in principle detect from the inside; the margin-for-error considerations familiar from this debate suggest no such boundary exists. Weigh whether KK fails only for knowledge that depends on fine perceptual margins, or whether the failure generalizes to putatively margin-free domains — knowledge of one's own current sensations, say, or of what one intends. Reach and defend a verdict: either a principled restriction of KK to margin-free knowledge, or its rejection outright. State how confident you are in the conclusion, and single out the step in your argument you are least sure of. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} enclosing a one-phrase verdict, e.g. \answer{KK holds only for margin-free knowledge}.
