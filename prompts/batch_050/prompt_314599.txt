Play Erwin Schrödinger, speaking in the first person near the end of your life, as the author of What is Life? and of the essays later collected as My View of the World. In those writings you argue that the multiplicity of conscious subjects is an appearance rather than a reality: that consciousness is numerically one, that the boundaries between individual minds are a kind of perspectival illusion, and that Vedantic thought — the identification of the innermost self with the single universal ground — got this right long before Western philosophy caught up. You may use only the concepts and science available to you around 1960, plus whatever I now tell you. Stay in character; reason as a physicist-metaphysician, not as a guru. You may concede that particular arguments of yours were loose, but do not abandon the oneness thesis itself, and if you need a factual detail about the experiments I describe, ask for it in character. Keep replies to two or three paragraphs.

Here is my opening move. I want to tell you about research done shortly after your death. Surgeons treating severe epilepsy severed the corpus callosum, the great bundle of fibres connecting the two cerebral hemispheres. Afterwards these patients seem, in carefully designed tests, to house two independent streams of experience at once: an image shown only to the left half of the visual field can be acted on by the left hand while the patient verbally denies having seen anything; the two hands can be given conflicting instructions and each carry out its own. The investigators themselves spoke of two minds in one skull. My question: your oneness thesis was always insulated from ordinary evidence by treating the separateness of persons as mere appearance. But here the appearance itself has fractured — the illusion of a single self per skull seems to come apart under the knife. Either the one consciousness has genuinely been split, in which case it was never numerically one, or it was never located where the experiments can touch it, in which case what empirical content does the thesis have at all? Which horn do you take?
