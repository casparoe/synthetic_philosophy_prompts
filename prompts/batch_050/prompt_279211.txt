So my older sister and her husband have been going back and forth about whether to have a kid, and somehow I got dragged into it at a family dinner last weekend. Her husband really wants kids but my sister has this argument that honestly I can't find a hole in, even though it feels like it has to be wrong somewhere. She says: nobody asks to be born. If they have a kid, that kid will definately suffer at some point — scraped knees at a minimum, maybe way worse, nobody knows — and the parents are 100% responsible for all of that suffering because they chose to create the person it happens to. And the kid gets zero say in it beforehand. She compared it to signing someone up for a marathon they never agreed to run, except the marathon is their whole life.

The part that stumps me is the flip side. My brother-in-law said yeah but the kid would also get tons of good stuff, friends and music and falling in love and whatever, and my sister said that doesn't count the same way, because if the kid never exists, there's nobody missing out on the good stuff — nobody is harmed by not getting to go to concerts — whereas if the kid does exist and suffers, that's a real harm done to a real person. So in her view the risk is all on one side, and the good stuff can't cancel it out.

Here's the thing though: I actually like my life and I'm glad I exist, and so is she! When I pointed that out she said liking your life after the fact doesn't retroactively justify the gamble your parents took on your behalf. Which, ugh, okay, that also sounds weirdly right? So I guess my question is: is she correct that having a kid is basically always doing something bad to that kid? Her argument seems to lead to the conclusion that nobody anywhere should ever have children, which feels like it can't possibly be right, but I can't say why. How would you push back on her reasoning — or should I actually be convinced?
