I am a professor of philosophy whose second field is formal epistemology; I am as comfortable with priors, signal structures, Bayes nets, do-interventions, and utility functions as I am with the literature on autonomy. I am drafting a paper on the distinction between manipulation and legitimate persuasion, and before I write I want a disciplined translation exercise.

Take the main families of accounts of what separates manipulation from permissible persuasion — accounts that locate the wrong in bypassing or short-circuiting the target's rational capacities; accounts that locate it in covertness, non-transparency, or the concealment of the persuader's intent; accounts that locate it in pressure, trickery, or the exploitation of predictable biases; and accounts that locate it in a failure to serve the target's own interests or values. Restate each family in the vocabulary of formal epistemology and decision theory: the signal structure the target is embedded in, the target's priors and likelihoods, the action set and decision problem the target takes herself to face, the persuader's objective function, and changes to the target's beliefs as against changes to her options, her evidence, or her preferences. Wherever possible, give the translation a precise form — for instance, is manipulation best modeled as degrading the informativeness of signals, as altering the target's effective utility function, as expanding or contracting her perceived action set, or as inducing updates the target would not endorse under her own standards of reasoning? Where a family of accounts can be rendered in more than one of these forms, say so and say what hangs on the choice.

Then give me the residue report. Which elements of each account translate cleanly, which translate only under distortion, and which have no expression in the formal vocabulary at all — candidates I have in mind include the notion of treating someone as a means, the distinction between engaging and circumventing a person's reasons, and the idea that covertness is itself objectionable rather than merely instrumentally bad. Finally, take a position on the residue: does it mark facts about manipulation that the formal framework is too thin to represent, or does it reveal that parts of the philosophical debate trade on notions less precise than they appear? Do not split the difference without argument.
