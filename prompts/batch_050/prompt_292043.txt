I'm in a second-year metaphysics seminar and we've just finished the unit on persistence through time — endurantism vs perdurantism, worm theory, stage theory, all of that — and after a long argument with some friends at the pub I realized I don't actually know what I think. So I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, is actually behind them.

Here they are. (1) The Ship of Theseus, planks swapped one at a time over years: same ship the whole way through, no question in my mind. (2) The old planks get collected and reassembled into a ship in a harbour down the coast: that's a replica, definitely not the original. (3) A vintage car gets fully dissassembled, the parts stored in a warehouse for a decade, then reassembled: same car, it just spent a while in pieces. (4) An amoeba divides: the original is gone, there are two new organisms now. (5) A friend suffers a severe brain injury and comes back with a completely different personality and no memories of me: still the same person, sadly. (6) The statue gets squashed flat: the statue is destroyed but the lump of clay is still sitting there, so the statue and the lump were never really one thing even while they coincided. (7) A caterpillar becomes a butterfly: same organism the whole time.

The pair that bugs me is (2) and (3) — if spatiotemporal and structural continuity is what keeps the ship going in (1), why does the warehouse car survive a total gap in assembly while the rebuilt ship doesn't? Is there a single principle that delivers all seven verdicts, or do I have to give one of them up, and if so which is the most expendable? Also tell me what your principle says about a case we fought over: a museum replaces Theseus's ship plank by plank while, in another room of the same museum, a restorer rebuilds a ship out of the discarded planks as they arrive — which one is Theseus's ship at the end, and does the answer change if the restorer is slow and only finishes a year after the museum does? Finally: does whatever principle captures my verdicts push me toward endurantism or perdurantism, or are my verdicts actually neutral between the two?
