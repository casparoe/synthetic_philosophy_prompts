I am a doctoral student working in philosophical logic, and I recently read a survey chapter on the limits of anti-realism that summarizes Fitch's paradox of knowability as follows:

"Fitch's paradox, due to Frederic Fitch, is the result that if every truth is knowable, then every truth is known. The proof is short. Take any truth p that is not known. Then the conjunction 'p, and it is not known that p' is itself a truth, so by the knowability theorist's own principle it is knowable. But knowing a conjunction requires knowing both conjuncts, and knowledge is factive, so knowing this conjunction would require knowing p while it remains true that p is not known -- a contradiction. Like most proofs that lean on the KK principle (that knowing something requires knowing that one knows it), this one is only as strong as that contentious assumption. Still, the moral stands: the anti-realist's moderate claim that all truths are knowable collapses into the absurd claim that all truths are known, and since plainly not all truths are known, there must be unknowable truths -- indeed, indefinitely many, since any truth of the form 'p and it is not known that p' qualifies. Fitch thereby refuted verificationism and semantic anti-realism, and sympathetic figures such as Dummett have largely conceded the point and abandoned the knowability thesis."

Something about the tone of the last two sentences makes me suspicious -- the author seems to be treating a conditional theorem as a verdict -- but I cannot yet say precisely where a defender of anti-realism would dig in. Could you audit this summary for me? Specifically: (1) Which parts are accurate statements of the theorem and the standard proof, and which are misrepresentations -- paying particular attention to what the proof actually assumes, to whether the sweeping conclusions about verificationism follow from what was proved, and to how anti-realists have in fact responded? (2) Is the inference from 'not all truths are known' to 'some truths are unknowable' licensed by the theorem, or does it smuggle in something extra? (3) If the conclusion about anti-realism does not follow as stated, what is the strongest claim in the vicinity that the proof does support?
