So I'm a grad student in social choice theory -- my whole world is preference profiles, aggregation rules, Condorcet cycles, Arrow, strategyproofness, that stuff. Lately I've been reading about populism (mostly because everyone keeps invoking "the will of the people" and it sets my teeth on edge), and I want to see how much of the standard account of populism can be restated in the vocabulary I actually think in.

Here's what I mean by the standard account, roughly following how political scientists like Mudde describe it: populism treats society as split into a pure, morally decent "people" and a corrupt elite; it claims that politics should be the direct expression of the people's will; and the populist claims to be the sole legitimate mouthpiece of that will, so that competing parties and institutions aren't just wrong but illegitimate. There's also usually a distinction lurking in the background between the people's real, unified will and whatever you get if you just total up what individuals happen to want -- Rousseau's general will versus the will of all, basically.

So: translate this into social-choice vocabulary as faithfully as you can. What's the formal counterpart of "the people" -- an electorate? a restriction on the domain of admissible profiles? Is "the will of the people" just an aggregation rule's output, and if so what rule would the populist have to be committed to? Does the general will / will of all distinction map onto anything like judgment aggregation vs. preference aggregation, or majority rule under the assumption of a common truth (jury-theorem style) vs. mere summing of tastes? And the "sole legitimate representative" claim -- is there any way to state that in terms of the axioms, or does it fall outside the framework entirely?

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What translated cleanly, what only translated with distortion, and what has no expression in social-choice terms at all? And for the stuff that doesn't survive -- is that because social choice theory is just missing the conceptual resources (e.g. it has no way to talk about who counts as part of the people, or about moral standing rather than preferences), or is it because the populist claims were never really coherent claims in the first place and only sound like ones? I have my own suspicion here but I'd rather you argued it out than confirmed it.

One constraint: don't cheat by quietly assuming populism is just majoritarianism and then declaring victory -- if the translation is trivial you've probably done it wrong.
