I run the training programme at a small firm that produces probabilistic risk assessments for corporate clients, and we keep having the same internal fight. Our analysts write sentences like: there is a seventy per cent chance that if the coalition collapses, the central bank raises rates within six months. Some of my staff insist such a sentence simply reports a conditional probability — the probability of a rate rise given a collapse — and can be entered into our forecasting models as P(rates rise | coalition collapses) = 0.7. Others point out, correctly I think, that the probability of an indicative conditional is not in general the conditional probability, and that treating them as interchangeable has caused real incoherence: analysts have negated such conditionals by flipping the consequent, embedded them inside further probabilities, and conditioned other forecasts on them, in ways that a conditional-probability reading cannot sustain. I understand the philosophical literature here includes so-called triviality results (associated with David Lewis and later authors) showing that no proposition can have its probability systematically equal to a conditional probability on pain of collapsing all distinctions — but my analysts are not philosophers and I cannot hand them journal articles.

What I want is a checking procedure our reviewers can run on any draft assessment, in roughly fifteen minutes per flagged sentence, before sign-off by a lead analyst. It should take the form of a sequence of tests, in order, each with a clear pass/fail and an instruction for what to do on failure. The tests should operationalise what the theory actually implies: for instance, how the sentence behaves under negation, what happens if the conditional is itself placed inside a probability statement or made the condition of another conditional, whether the analyst intends the conditional to be asserted outright or only supposed, and whether the surrounding numbers in the report remain coherent under each reading. The procedure should say when the conditional-probability rendering is safe enough for modelling purposes, when the sentence must be reworded (and into what canonical forms), and it should be explicit about which steps are genuine mechanical checks and which are judgement calls wearing procedural clothing. Please also state plainly what the procedure cannot settle — cases where the reviewer has to make a call and document it.

To show the procedure works, please run it on three example sentences, at least one of which the analyst will be annoyed to see fail: (i) the coalition/rates sentence above, which the analyst wants to enter directly into the model; (ii) a sentence in which one analyst writes that there is a forty per cent chance that if the port closes, there is an eighty per cent chance that shipping costs double; and (iii) a sentence in which an analyst, asked what would change her forecast, writes that if she learned that the sanctions were lifted, the probability that the currency recovers if oil prices fall would rise — a conditional sitting inside a conditional inside a probability judgement. For each, show every test applied, the verdict, and the final reworded sentence where one is required. Please write your answer in British English.
