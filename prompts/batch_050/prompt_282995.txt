So we're wrapping up the intentionality unit in my phil of mind seminar (Searle, Dennett, the Chinese Room, all that) and our final paper has to start with a page laying out our own position so the TA can grade whether we defend it consistently. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it actually hangs together before I commit to it:

1. I'm basically a functionalist. What makes something a mind is what it does and how it's organized, not what it's made of. Silicon, neurons, whatever — if the causal structure is right, that's enough in principle.
2. Searle's Chinese Room convinces me. Just running a program, however sophisticated, doesn't by itself give a system genuine understanding. The guy in the room manipulates symbols without meaning anything by them, and I don't see how adding more rulebook changes that.
3. I also think Dennett's two-bitser point is basically right. The vending machine's "states" mean something only because we designed and selected it for a job, and the same story holds for us all the way down — our own mental states mean what they do because natural selection shaped them to track stuff. There's no deeper kind of meaning underneath the selection story.
4. My thermostat doesn't believe the room is cold, it just trips a switch. But my dog genuinely believes there's food in his bowl, and he'd believe that even if no human were around to interpret him.
5. Current chatbots and LLMs don't mean anything by what they say. All the meaning in their output is ours — projected by the users and baked in by the designers. The system itself is just the guy in the room scaled up.

Which of these are actually in tension with each other, and which pairs only look like they clash but are fine once you're careful about what each one commits me to? For each genuine tension tell me how bad it is and what the cheapest fix would be. And if there's one revision that would clean up the most problems at once, flag that. I'd rather not rewrite the whole position page if I can get away with dropping or softening one claim.
