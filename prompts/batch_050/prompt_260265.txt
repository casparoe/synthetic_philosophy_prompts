Construct a new Zeno-style paradox of my design, aimed at a target the classical ones never touch: a process that is perfectly finite in time and space, so that the standard resolutions (convergent series, measure theory, the distinction between potential and actual infinity) cannot dissolve it. Set it somewhere contemporary and concrete — an algorithm that runs, a deliberator who decides, a quantity of value that accrues — not a runner on a racetrack. The paradox must arise from infinite divisibility in a dimension other than time or distance (for example: infinitely many sub-tasks, sub-decisions, or gradations of some magnitude), and it must generate a genuine contradiction or impossibility, not merely a feeling of strangeness. Structure the response in four sections, in this order: (1) The paradox — the scenario itself, in one tight paragraph, stated so the contradiction is visible by the end; (2) The immunity proof — two or three paragraphs demonstrating, for each of the standard anti-Zeno moves, exactly why it fails to apply here; (3) The load-bearing feature — a paragraph identifying the single property of the scenario doing the work, and what happens to the paradox if it is removed; (4) The best defense — a paragraph giving the strongest objection a critic could raise, and your honest assessment of whether the paradox survives it.
