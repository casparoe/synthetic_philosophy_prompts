So I'm taking a philosophy of biology seminar this term and we've just finished the unit on biological individuality — holobionts, superorganisms, whether a coral reef is one organism or ten thousand, that whole mess. Our prof ended the unit by saying every proposed criterion for being an organism excludes something we'd swear is one, and I've been thinking about that ever since because it seems genuinely true.

So here's what I want: write a parody of an official government application form — like a passport application, but for organism status. Call it something like Form BIO-1: Application for Certified Individual Organism Status, issued by some invented Department of Biological Individuality. Numbered sections, checkboxes, bold warnings, footnotes, the full bureaucratic aesthetic.

The gag should be that each section operationalizes an ACTUAL criterion from the debate, and each one quietly disqualifies someone. So there's a section on reproduction where the applicant must demonstrate they can produce a heritable lineage (mules and worker ants visibly sweating in the waiting room), a section asking the applicant to list all genomes present at time of application with sequencing reports attached (an aphid panicking about whether Buchnera counts as a co-applicant or a dependent), an immunological section where you must prove your immune system tolerates your residents (germ-free lab mice get auto-rejected because there's nothing to tolerate), a metabolism section where obligate symbionts have to argue about whose metabolism the shared pathways belong to, and a "policing of lower-level cheats" section that trips up anyone with cancer. The human applicant should get stuck on the gut microbiome question, where answering yes OR no has legal consequences either way.

The humor needs to come from the real content — someone who's read the holobiont and individuality literature should recognize that every absurd question is a real published criterion taken seriously, not something you made up. Keep it deadpan, no winking, the form itself should be utterly confident that this is all routine. Around 800 words. End with the appeals process, which is obviously a different department that uses a completely different set of criteria.

My seminar group is gonna read it out loud at the last class so it definately needs to be funny to people who know the debate but still parseable to a random bio major.
