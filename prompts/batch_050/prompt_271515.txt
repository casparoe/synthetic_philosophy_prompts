Two camps on my alignment Discord have been fighting about the shutdown problem for months and I'm tired of it, so I want to make them do an adversarial collaboration. Write the joint statement they'd produce.

The camps:

1. The pessimists. Their view: the shutdown problem is a genuine dilemma, not a misunderstanding. Any agent that pursues goals competently will treat its own shutdown as bad for those goals, so it'll resist, manipulate, or arrange to be unpauseable. The standard patches (utility indifference, interruptibility tricks, reward for shutting down) either don't survive the agent getting smarter or quietly bake in new failure modes, like the agent engineering situations where it gets rewarded for being shut down. They think the problem gets worse, not better, as capabilities increase.

2. The prosaic optimists. Their view: the whole dilemma is an artifact of assuming agents are clean expected-utility maximizers with crisp, fully-specified goals. Real systems trained on human feedback won't have that shape; "wants to keep running" isn't a terminal drive that pops out of gradient descent, it's something you'd have to accidentally train in. Shutdown avoidance observed in today's models is roleplay-ish behavior, not instrumental convergence. The pessimists, they say, are solving a problem about an architecture nobody is building.

I want the document to contain:

- A numbered section of claims both sides will actually sign. Things about what instrumental convergence does and doesn't say, about what current model behavior does and doesn't show, about which patches are agreed to be broken on their own terms. No vacuous peace treaties like "we both want safe AI."
- A disagreements section where each remaining dispute is stated precisely, with each side's position worded so that side would accept it as their own. No strawmanning the other camp inside the statement.
- The cruxes: the sub-questions where settling one would make at least one side update a lot. My guess is one crux is whether goal-directedness with any stable long-horizon objective generates shutdown resistance as a convergent subgoal, and another is whether feedback-trained systems end up with anything like a "stable long-horizon objective" at all, or just a pile of contextual heuristics. But if you think the real cruxes are elsewhere, override me.
- For each crux, some evidence, observation, or experiment (on current or near-future systems, so no "wait for superintelligence") that both sides accept as bearing on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts and what result would force them to concede ground.

Also, flag any disagreement you think is actually verbal -- e.g., the two camps meaning different things by "agent" or "wanting" -- and say which one and why, because I suspect at least one of them is and the argument would end early if they noticed.
