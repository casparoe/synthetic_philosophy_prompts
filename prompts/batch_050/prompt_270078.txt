Alright, I need some help getting an idea out of my system. I spent about 35 years as an experimental physicist, mostly condensed matter, and in that world everything is causal contact: signals, fields, interactions, light cones. If no information can pass between two things, then as far as physics is concerned nothing between them is coordinated, period. Now in retirement I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole, and I ran into an idea people call acausal trade that I cant stop turning over.

The idea, as I understand it: two agents who can never communicate or interact, not even in principle, can still end up cooperating, purely by reasoning about each other. If A is good enough at predicting how B thinks, A can do something B wants, confident that B, performing the same trick in reverse, is doing something A wants. No handshake, no contract, no enforcement mechanism, no possibility of one. Just two seperate chains of reasoning that lock onto each other across a void. What bugs me is that every write-up I stumble across seems to cheat: the traders are superintelligences running perfect simulations of each other. Well, sure, if you can simulate your counterparty down to the last neuron you hardly need a clever argument. That version tells me nothing about whether the concept actually works when prediction is hard.

So: write me a short story about an acausal trade that succeeds under honest constraints. Specifically:

- No simulating the other party, no superintelligence, no mind reading. The traders have to model each other the way ordinary agents do: sparse evidence, shared circumstances, educated guesses about how the other side reasons.
- At least one side must act without ever learning whether the other held up their end. To me that's the philosophically juicy part, the part physics never prepared me for: what is it like to keep a bargain you can never verify?
- Show the weirdness, don't lecture about it. I want to watch someone genuinely wrestle with the question of why am I doing this for someone who will never even know, and come out the other side changed.

For whatever it's worth, the first human-scale version I came up with was a dying person arranging something for strangers a few centuries from now, betting that those strangers will do something for her in return. Run with that seed if you like, or ignore it completely if you've got something better.

800-1500 words, no gimmicky formatting, just a good story.
