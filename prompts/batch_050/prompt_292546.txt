I'm a theoretical biologist working on the evolution of cooperation; my working vocabulary is replicator dynamics, frequency-dependent selection, multilevel selection, and carrying capacity as a hard constraint. In Garrett Hardin's 1974 essay "Lifeboat Ethics: the Case Against Helping the Poor," the following passage appears as a reply to passengers who say they would feel guilty staying in the boat while others swim:

"The needy person to whom the guilt-ridden person yields his place will not himself feel guilty about his good luck. If he did, he would not climb aboard. The net result of conscience-stricken people giving up their unjustly held seats is the elimination of that sort of conscience from the lifeboat. This is the basic metaphor within which we must work out our solutions."

Restate the essay's argument -- and this passage in particular -- as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary: the lifeboat as a population under a hard carrying-capacity constraint, admission decisions as strategies, guilt and its absence as heritable or culturally transmitted dispositions, and the passage's final sentence as a selection claim. Then give me a residue report. Which parts of Hardin's argument translate cleanly into population-dynamical terms, which parts have to be distorted to fit, and which parts have no counterpart at all? In particular, the passage moves from a prediction about which dispositions will persist in the boat to a conclusion about which ethic we should adopt. Does my vocabulary simply lack the resources to express that normative move -- in which case the residue is my framework's blind spot -- or does the translation reveal that the normative conclusion was never supported by the selection argument in the first place? Tell me where you come down on that, and flag one place where a translator could cheat by smuggling a normative premise into what looks like a purely dynamical claim.
