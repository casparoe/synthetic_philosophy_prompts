Write a short story (like 800-1200 words) about societal "collapse." Specifically: historians a few hundred years later describe a certain society as having collapsed -- classic decline-and-fall narrative, abandoned cities, lost technology, the whole thing. But the story should be told from the POV of someone living through the "collapse" at the time, and to them it doesn't feel like a collapse at all. It feels like... something else. Maybe the city emptying out is just people moving to where the rain still falls. Maybe the old writing system nobody learns anymore is something only grandpa cared about anyway. Maybe the new arrangement is in some ways better -- less taxes, less corvée labor, fewer wars.

The story shouldn't be a lecture with a plot stapled on, but it should make the reader genuinely unsure whether the later historians are describing a failure or just a change, and whose standard of "success" is doing the work in the word collapse. Bonus points if the character has some small personal stake in the old order (like they're the last apprentice of a dying craft, or their family status depends on the old capital mattering) so the question of whether things got worse isn't abstract to them.

One constraint: don't use any real historical case (no Rome, no Maya, no Easter Island). Invent a fictional society, since that way the reader cant just import whatever they already beleive about the real examples.
