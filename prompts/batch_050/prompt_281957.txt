I'm writing up a survey of proposed solutions to the problem of old evidence in Bayesian confirmation theory. On my list so far: (1) the counterfactual or ahistorical approach, which says confirmation should be judged by what your credences would have been had you not already learned the evidence; (2) the view that what actually confirms is not the old evidence itself but the newly discovered fact that the theory entails it, so there is no real problem because genuine learning did occur; and (3) the deflationary line that old evidence simply cannot confirm, and that the appearance it does (think of Mercury's perihelion and general relativity) should be explained away pragmatically rather than accommodated in the formalism. I think these exhaust the standard moves, but I'd like one more proposed solution that is genuinely different from all three -- not a rewording, a special case, or a hybrid of two of them. If nothing in the literature fits, sketch your own solution, but say explicitly that that's what you're doing, and be honest about whether you think it works. Write in continuous prose, no headings, bullet points or tables, just like an email, and use British English.
