okay so i'm a second-year phd student in philosophy working on wellbeing, and my generals paper is on the heterogeneity objection to hedonism. quick version: pleasures seem wildly different from each other -- the taste of coffee, the relief when pain stops, getting a joke, a chord resolving, an old friend's voice on the phone, finishing a proof -- and it's not clear they share any one phenomenal quality. so the hedonist either has to posit some common "hedonic tone" that introspection suspiciously never finds on its own, or say what unifies pleasures is our attitude toward them (we like them, want them, welcome them) rather than anything in the feeling itself, which brings its own problems.

for the end of my workshop talk (small crowd, sympathetic, i can get away with this) i want to read a poem. specifically a sestina, because i think the form can do the work of the objection: pick six pleasures as the six end-words, and then each stanza rearranges the same six in the sestina's strict rotating order, and stanza after stanza no common thread between them appears -- just the same six things, reshuffled, each still stubbornly itself. the envoi (last three lines, two end-words per line) is where the poem should finally ask -- not answer -- whether what they share is something in the feeling or something in us.

requirements: strict sestina, 39 lines, envoi included, standard end-word rotation. the six pleasures should be genuinely heterogeneous (at least one bodily, one intellectual, one social, one aesthetic) and concrete, not abstract words like "joy." someone who knows the objection should recognize the form enacting it; someone who doesn't should still just get a good poem about six good things. definately don't resolve the question at the end, my own view is unsettled and i want the last lines to sit inside it. also please write in american english.
