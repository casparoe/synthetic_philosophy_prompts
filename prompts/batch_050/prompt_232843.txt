I am a high school student writing a term paper for my civics class, and I would like your help with the central case study. Take Ireland's Citizens' Assembly of 2016–2018, in which 99 randomly selected citizens spent months hearing testimony and deliberating on the Eighth Amendment's near-total abortion ban, ultimately recommending liberalization -- a recommendation the government then put to the 2018 referendum, which passed decisively. Analyze this episode through the lens of sortition theory: what it seems to confirm (can randomly selected laypeople deliberate well on a morally explosive question that elected politicians had avoided for decades? does random selection produce something like descriptive representation?), what it cannot show or actively complicates (members who agreed to serve may not be typical; experts and officials controlled the agenda and evidence presented; the Assembly was advisory, and a mass referendum did the actual deciding), and finally what the case reveals about stronger lottocratic proposals that would give randomly chosen bodies real governing power rather than a recommending role. Reach a verdict on whether the Irish episode is evidence for sortition, evidence against it, or evidence only for a weaker version than its advocates claim.
