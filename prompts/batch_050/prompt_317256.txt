Write a dialogue between two grad students, Maya and Deniz, arguing about this variant of the two-envelope problem: the stuffer flips a fair coin repeatedly until it lands tails, puts 2^n dollars in one envelope where n is the number of heads (so $1, $2, $4, $8...), and double that in the other; you pick one at random and can switch once before opening. Maya thinks the standard switch-argument is dead on arrival because any real prior over envelope contents has to be proper, and once you commit to one, the naive "I always gain by switching" reasoning falls apart on inspection. Deniz thinks that's dodging the real puzzle, because the symmetry argument for switching doesn't seem to need you to peek inside or even have a prior at all, so something has to be wrong with expected-value reasoning itself. Neither should be a pushover -- have them actually engage with each other's points, and at some point have them grapple with the fact that the doubling distribution here has infinite expected value and whether that changes anything or is a red herring. Around 600-900 words, no tidy resolution where one of them just concedes.
