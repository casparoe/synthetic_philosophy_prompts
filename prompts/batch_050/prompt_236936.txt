Cases I want used at the nodes: the quus-style reinterpretation of past use, the systematic-mistake case (someone whose answers diverge from what they themselves would endorse on reflection), Crusoe alone on his island with nobody to check him, and two speakers whose dispositions diverge only on inputs too large for either of them to ever compute.

The leaves have to include: Kripke's own skeptical solution, sophisticated dispositionalism (the version that adds idealized conditions), meaning primitivism (irreducible semantic facts), the communitarian reading, normativism about meaning (correctness conditions as genuinely normative), and a quietist/dissolution leaf. Add an error-theoretic or non-cognitivist leaf only if you think it earns its place; if not, say why it doesn't.

Also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate, and for each empty corner of the logical space give a one-line diagnosis: is nobody there because the combination is incoherent, or just because nobody's bothered to defend it?

Format, in this order:

- "The tree" — the full decision tree as indented text or an ASCII flowchart, roughly a page.
- "Leaf notes" — one short paragraph per leaf: the name, who holds it, and its most awkward commitment.
- "Empty corners" — the unoccupied answer combos and your diagnosis, a couple sentences each.
- "Separation flags" — a short list.
