Is there a standard name for this pattern, which I keep noticing in debates about charity evaluation? An evaluator can only rigorously measure a few outcomes (bednets distributed, kids dewormed), so donors shift money toward charities doing those things, and then charities drop their harder-to-measure programs (policy advocacy, pandemic prep for diseases that may never arrive) because unfunded work is dead work. In the end the measurable-vs-unmeasurable line ends up deciding what actually happens in the world, not just what gets reported. It's clearly adjacent to Goodhart's law, but I dont think it is Goodhart -- nobody is gaming the metric, the measured numbers stay accurate, it's more that measurement demand quietly reshapes the supply of interventions. And it's not quite the McNamara fallacy either, since the evaluators know perfectly well that unmeasurable goods exist, they just can't justify funding them. So: does this have its own name, and if not can you coin one and define it crisply -- where exactly does it stop being Goodhart, and does it count if the charities anticipate the distortion before any donor actually moves money?
