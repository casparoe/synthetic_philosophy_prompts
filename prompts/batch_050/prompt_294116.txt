For a workshop on practical rationality, each participant has been asked to circulate a short statement of commitments on the problem of choice without preference, for the others to attack. Here is mine:

1. A rational agent facing two options between which her reasons are exactly tied, where declining both is worse than taking either, acts irrationally if she declines both. The Buridan agent who starves between identical bales of hay is exhibiting a straightforward failure of rationality, and taking one of the bales can be a fully rational act.

2. Such ties are genuine and can survive ideal deliberation. There need be no hidden consideration, inaccessible to the agent, that actually favors the left bale over the right; the symmetry can be a real feature of the situation rather than a confession of ignorance, and further deliberation about which bale to take is pointless.

3. A choice of A over B is rational only if the agent has a reason that counts in favor of A as against B. A bare "I took that one" may be excusable in the circumstances, but it is never a rational justification of taking that one.

4. Intentional action is distinguished from mere bodily movement precisely by being done for a reason. Where there is no consideration in light of which the agent does what she does, there is behavior but no action.

5. In momentous decisions between equally ranked options — two genuinely tied job offers, say — settling the matter by a coin flip is a deliberative failure, an abdication of agency. The right thing is to do what the sensible Buridan agent does: simply take one.

Please audit the set rather than tutor me in the literature. Which pairs or triples of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each real tension, state exactly what would have to be revised to restore consistency and what that revision costs, and rank the tensions by severity. For any merely apparent tension, explain why it dissolves. Finally, tell me which single revision repairs the most while changing the least of what I plainly care about.
