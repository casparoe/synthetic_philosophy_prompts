My reading group got into a mess last week over indicative versus counterfactual conditionals, and I want a test case that is sharper than the usual light-switch examples. Construct a thought experiment where the very same antecedent and consequent make an indicative conditional rational to rely on, while the matching counterfactual is either false or at least not a good basis for intervention. Constraints: keep it ordinary and mid-stakes, like a clinic, maintenance crew, school office, or small bakery; no time travel, demons, perfect predictors, or explicitly saying possible worlds inside the scenario; one paragraph for the story; and the divergence cannot come from cheating with vague tense, sarcasm, or someone just using bad evidence. The agent should be able to say: given what I actually see, if A then B is a good guide, but if A were the case, B would follow is not the right thing to bank on, or the reverse if you can make that cleaner.

After the scenario, do the audit slowly: state the agent’s evidence, the relevant base rate or routine failure mode, the causal link or lack of one between A and B, and the closest way A could be made true. Then give the verdict for the indicative conditional, the verdict for the counterfactual, and why they split. Say exactly which feature does the work - I suspect it has to be something like a diagnostic channel that is trustworthy in the actual setup but easy to break under the nearest way of making the antecedent true, but don’t just assert that, show it. Finally, add a pressure test: what minimal change collapses the case back so indicative and counterfactual agree, and what would a skeptic say who thinks your counterfactual reading is smuggling in an unnatural closest world? No bibliography or name-dropping, definately keep it self-contained.
