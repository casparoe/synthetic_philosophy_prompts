I've been working through the argument from multiple realizability, and I'm stuck on what looks like an unavoidable consequence of it. If pain can be realized by C-fiber firing in humans, by an entirely different neural arrangement in octopuses, and in principle by silicon circuitry, then pain cannot be identical to any of those physical states. But then the only remaining candidates for what pain *is* seem to be a functional state — which is an abstraction, not anything physical — or an irreducibly non-physical property. So multiple realizability appears to force a choice between dualism and eliminativism about pain, which is odd given that the argument is usually deployed in *favor* of functionalism rather than against the mind-brain identity theory's physicalism more broadly. How do functionalists who want to remain physicalists escape this conclusion? And if their answer is that pain just is the functional role itself, aren't they committed to saying that anything instantiating the role — a giant lookup table, or the population of China organized so as to mirror the right causal structure — literally feels pain? That sounds less like physicalism than like panpsychism arriving by the back door, so I'd also like to know whether that second worry is the same problem resurfacing or a separate objection.
