I'm a grad student in philosophy of physics and our reading group does this thing where before each session you circulate a little "position blurb" so people can attack it in advance. Next session is on gauge freedom and surplus structure, which is exactly the topic I feel shakiest about, so I could use an audit before I send mine out. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Gauge symmetry isn't a real symmetry of the world the way, say, rotational symmetry is -- it's a redundancy in our description, an artifact of using more mathematical machinery than the physics needs. (2) Relatedly, in GR I take the hole argument seriously: models related by a diffeomorphism describe the very same physical situation, so spacetime points have no identity independently of the fields on them. (3) But the Aharonov-Bohm effect convinces me that something encoded in the electromagnetic potential, over and above the field strengths, is physically real -- so a pure "throw away the potential" eliminativism can't be right. (4) I also think the gauge principle is genuinely explanatory: demanding local gauge invariance is why there is an electromagnetic field at all, and this is one of the deepest insights of 20th century physics. (5) And I'm a determinist at heart: a fundamental theory should evolve a complete state deterministically, and I take our gauge theories to pass this test fine.

So: does this hang together? Please go through the commitments pairwise and tell me which ones are genuinely in tension and how badly, which ones only *look* like they conflict but dissolve on inspection (I have a suspicion at least one pair is like this), and for each real tension what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and don't just tell me to read more -- I want to know which commitment, if I had to drop exactly one, would do the most to stabilize the set.
