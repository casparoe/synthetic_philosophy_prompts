So I'm in a phil of history seminar this term and something keeps bugging me that I don't have a word for. When we read about the losing side of some conflict or the collapse of some empire, the textbook narrates basically everything that happens beforehand as building toward the fall -- like a bad harvest in year X is automatically "an early sign of the decline." But when the same book covers the winning side, stuff just... happens, and the narrative stays open-ended, like the outcome wasn't written in advance. Same kinds of events, totally different treatment, and the only real difference seems to be that the author already knows how each story ends. It's not exactly "history is written by the winners," because here the winners' own history gets treated as contingent too; it's more like knowing the endpoint retroactively turns everything before it into a cause of it, but only for the story that ended badly (or ended at all).

Is there an established name for this specific asymmetry? I know it's somewhere in the neighborhood of hindsight bias and that "Whig history" thing we touched on, but neither seems to be exactly it. If nothing fits, coin a term and define it, and tell me how it differs from those two.
