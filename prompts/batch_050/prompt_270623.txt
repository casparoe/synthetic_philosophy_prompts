Consider the following situation, which I am treating as a test case for how much normative force Aumann's agreement theorem actually has outside its idealized setting. A colleague and I have been estimating the probability that a mutual acquaintance will accept a particular job offer. We began at different credences, 0.65 for me and 0.3 for her. Over several weeks we have exchanged our estimates repeatedly, each time hearing the other's number, updating in light of it, and announcing the result; every announcement was common knowledge between us. The gap has narrowed but not closed: we are now stuck at roughly 0.7 and 0.45, and the last three exchanges moved neither of us. We have both stated our evidence openly and neither of us believes the other is hiding information. I would like your credence that at least one of us is being epistemically irrational, and I want it decomposed rather than delivered as a bare number. Break the question into the conditions the agreement theorem needs and assess each as a candidate point of failure here: the probability that we genuinely share a relevant prior given that we have different histories with the acquaintance; the probability that common knowledge of each other's rationality holds, as opposed to each of us merely believing the other is reasonable; the probability that our announcements are truthful reports of our posteriors rather than positions we have become attached to defending; and the probability that the failure lies in bounded computation, meaning that each of us cannot actually perform the update the other's announcement calls for. Assign a rough probability to each candidate explanation, add any term you think I have missed, state how the terms combine into your final credence, and identify which single term the final number is most sensitive to. Then tell me what observable evidence in our exchange history would most discriminate between your top two diagnoses. Please commit: name one diagnosis as the most likely and defend that choice, rather than presenting the candidates as equally live options and leaving the verdict to me.
