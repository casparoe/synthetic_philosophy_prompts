I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on whether LLM falsehoods are best classified as lies, bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, or mere error, and I need a thought experiment built to order for a section of my dissertation. Construct a single realistic scenario — an AI assistant, an ordinary user, no stipulation of exotic inner states — in which three accounts deliver three different verdicts on the same false output: a lying account (the speaker believes the claim false and intends the hearer to accept it), a Frankfurt-style bullshit account (the speaker is indifferent to whether the claim is true and cares only about the impression made), and a no-attitudes account (output from a system lacking truth-directed states altogether is at most malfunction, like a broken barometer, and so neither lie nor bullshit). The case should come out as bullshit on the Frankfurt-style account, as mere error on the no-attitudes account, and as clearly not a lie on the first. After the scenario, identify the one feature doing the classificatory work, show that removing it collapses the verdict to plain error on all three accounts, and say how a defender of the view that LLMs literally bullshit would resist the construction — without the case simply stipulating its verdicts.
