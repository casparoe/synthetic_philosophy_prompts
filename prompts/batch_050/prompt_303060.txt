My metaphysics seminar got onto the Rietdijk–Putnam argument last week, and our professor defended the block universe with the usual loaf-of-bread picture: spacetime is like an uncut loaf, while each inertial observer slices it into “present” moments at a different angle. Since none of the possible slicings is physically privileged, she said, all the slices and their contents must be equally real. She then gave a version of the Andromeda case: relative to my current frame, the fleet may already have launched, while relative to yours it may still be deliberating, even though we’re walking past each other here on Earth. Therefore, she claimed, my future cannot be open while being someone else’s present.

Audit the analogy rather than just explaining the stock argument. Give the mapping explicitly: what in spacetime corresponds to the loaf, the cuts, the faces of a slice, the orientation of the knife, and so on. Then identify which failures matter for the intended conclusion and which are harmless picturesque details. In particular, does the loaf smuggle in an already-existing whole whose parts merely need slicing? Does “no privileged simultaneity plane” establish “all events coexist,” or does it establish something weaker, such as frame-relative simultaneity? Please discuss whether the Andromeda setup rules out a relativized notion of becoming, a privileged foliation undetectable by current physics, or a view on which only a growing region is determinate. Finally, if the loaf analogy can’t carry the conclusion, propose a better analogy for whatever the argument really does show—and be clear about whether it supports a block universe, four-dimensionalism, or only the relativity of “now.”
