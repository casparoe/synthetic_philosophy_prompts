Draft the joint statement that could emerge from an adversarial collaboration between a dialetheist and a non-dialetheist paraconsistent logician -- that is, between someone who holds that some contradictions are actually true and someone who rejects explosion but insists that all contradictions are nonetheless false. Both parties agree that classical logic fails around the semantic paradoxes and that a logic tolerating inconsistent-but-nontrivial theories is needed; the dispute is over what that logic is for. Begin with a numbered list of claims both would sign, covering at least: what the Liar paradox shows, why trivialism must be avoided, and whether consistency is a precondition of rational theory acceptance. Then give a disagreements table in which each position is stated in wording its own side would accept -- in particular, phrase the dialetheist's claim so it does not merely sound like madness, and phrase the non-dialetheist's claim so it does not presuppose that explosion is valid. Identify the cruxes: I suspect one is whether "true contradiction" is even a coherent category rather than a verbal maneuver, and another is whether the paraconsistentist's refusal to call the Liar both true and false leaves the paradox unsolved or merely renamed. For each crux, describe an argument or case both parties would accept as bearing on it (for example, how each side handles Curry's paradox, and whether a theory can be paraconsistent about its own logic without collapsing into dialetheism), and record in advance what each side predicts the case shows. If some part of their disagreement turns out to be terminological, say which part and why.
