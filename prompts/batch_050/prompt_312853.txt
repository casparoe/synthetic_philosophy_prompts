I got into a dumb late-night argument after a design exhibition about whether Hume’s standard of taste secretly rigs the game, and now I want a case built to order. Construct a realistic thought experiment, maybe a regional craft biennial or a community architecture prize, where Hume-style good judges do everything right on the usual checklist: strong delicacy of perception, lots of practice, wide comparison among works, and no obvious prejudice or corruption. They all converge, sincerely and after reflection, that object A is more beautiful than object B. But engineer the backstory so that their convergence is produced by a closed causal loop: A’s earlier dominance shaped the schools, galleries, funding, criticism, and childhood exposures through which the judges acquired exactly the delicacy and comparative repertoire they now use to rank A above B. Constraints: no gods, brain implants, perfect predictors, secret hypnotists, or stipulating the verdicts; the judges must remain genuinely excellent by ordinary standards, not bribed hacks; keep the scenario to about 250-400 words; isolate one variable only, namely the presence or absence of that self-training loop, so if you remove the loop the very same judges with the same faculties would converge on B instead; and don’t lean on any quote or fine-grained claim about what Hume’s essay really says, just the broadly familiar machinery.

After the scenario, do the bookkeeping: state the verdict given by a generous Humean standard-of-taste objectivist, a plain subjectivist, an error theorist, and a response-dependence view that says beauty can be objective if the right responders are specified correctly. Show which feature makes the Humean verdict unstable, and be precise about whether the case attacks Hume himself or only a lazy version of him. Then propose the smallest repair: would adding a no-self-certification clause, a counterfactual portability test across nearby training histories, or a demand that good taste survive contact with excluded traditions fix it, and what new cost does each repair bring? End with an honesty note: which move is most likely cheating, what would change your verdict if someone showed the loop can be defined away without circularity, and where your own argument is most likely to collpase.
