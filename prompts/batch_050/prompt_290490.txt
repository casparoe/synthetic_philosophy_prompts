I got assigned the sunk cost fallacy and I want to make it actually good instead of the boring version where one person is obviously right.

Write me a dialogue between two characters, maybe 700-900 words, about whether it's EVER rational to keep going with something just because of what you've already put into it. Here's the setup you should use: Maya dropped out of a robotics competition two weeks before regionals even though she'd spent the whole semester building the robot, and her friend Dev thinks she made a huge mistake. Maya takes the standard econ-class line -- what's spent is spent, the only thing that matters is whether the next two weeks of work are worth it on their own, and they weren't. Dev has to actually defend the other side and not just be the dumb one who doesnt get it. Some angles he could push: finishing things is how you become a person who finishes things, quitting this time makes it easier to quit next time, she made a kind of promise to her past self and to her teammates, and also she can't actually know the remaining work isn't worth it because she's bad at judging that when she's frustrated.

- both of them have to be genuinely smart. Maya should know the standard arguments for her side (marginal reasoning, the fallacy of throwing good money after bad) and Dev should concede what's true in that instead of denying it
- they have to actually respond to each other's specific points, not just take turns speechifying
- at some point one of them should try to pin down exactly where they disagree -- like is it about whether the psychological stuff (commitment, habits, self-trust) is "real" value or just a bias wearing a costume
- don't have a clear winner at the end, but end somewhere interesting, not with them just agreeing to disagree
- keep it sounding like actual teenagers talking, not two professors wearing trenchcoats
