**Exam question:** Explain the disagreement between reductionism and anti-reductionism about testimonial justification. Which view, in your judgment, better accounts for the epistemic standing of everyday testimony (e.g., asking a stranger for directions, reading the news), and why?

**Student's answer:** Reductionists, following Hume, hold that testimony is not a genuine source of justification at all; a hearer is only justified in accepting what someone says if she has independently verified that particular claim through her own perception or reasoning. Anti-reductionists, following Reid, reply that testimony is a basic source of justification on a par with perception and memory: absent reasons for doubt, we are entitled to accept what we are told without first establishing the speaker's reliability inductively. On the anti-reductionist view, then, we are by default entitled to believe whatever anyone tells us. The reductionist's main argument is that testimonial chains are only as strong as their weakest link, so testimonial justification must bottom out in non-testimonial evidence. I think anti-reductionism better handles everyday cases, since we clearly learn from strangers and news reports without verifying their track records first, and reductionism cannot explain this.
