So last week my brother and his roommate got into this argument at dinner that I somehow got put in charge of settling. They were unloading groceries, there were two identical cartons of eggs on the counter, and the roommate grabbed one. My brother asked "why that one?" and the roommate said "no reason, they're the same." That apparently kicked off a forty minute fight. Here's the back-and-forth as best I remember it:

(1) My brother: every choice is explained by reasons. If there were truly no reason favoring A over B, you couldn't move at all -- you'd just freeze. So the fact that he picked means there WAS a reason, maybe a tiny one like "it was an inch closer to my right hand."

(2) Roommate: that's cheating. If "it was closer" counts as a reason, then a reason is just whatever ended up happening, and you've defined the word so loosely you can never be wrong. People break ties constantly by just picking, and the picking isn't guided by anything.

(3) My brother: ok, then what breaks the tie? If nothing guides it, it's basically a mental coin flip, which is randomness. And randomness isn't you choosing either -- the coin chose. So you haven't shown people can act without reasons, you've shown that sometimes nobody acts.

(4) Roommate: it doesn't have to be random. I just pick, and the pick is still mine even with no reason behind it. And if you demand a reason for every step you get an infinite regress, because then every reason needs its own reason. At some point choosing has to just start.

Neither would budge and now it's apparently my job. Please adjudicate move by move: who's right at each step, what (if anything) both of them are missing, and who's more right overall? Also please write your answer in American English.
