I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and over the past year I've been thinking on and off about mind uploading and what it would take for an upload to actually be me rather than a copy of me. I've arrived at a set of commitments that feel individually well-motivated, but I have a nagging suspicion they don't all fit together. I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher specializing in personal identity and philosophy of mind, and audit the following position set. Please think carefully and take your time before answering; I'd suggest first restating each commitment in your own words to make sure we've understood each other, then doing the audit.

Here is roughly where I've landed:

1. What makes me me over time is the functional organization of my brain -- the pattern of information processing -- not the particular matter implementing it. If the pattern is preserved, I am preserved.

2. If my neurons were replaced one at a time, over weeks, by functionally identical silicon components, with the system running continuously throughout, I would survive the process. At no point would anyone notice a difference, including me.

3. A scan-and-reconstruct procedure that maps my brain, destroys the original, and builds a functionally identical brain out of new matter elsewhere is death. The person who wakes up at the other end is a new individual with my memories, not me.

4. Consciousness is substrate-independent. A silicon brain implementing the right functional organization would be genuinely conscious, with an inner life, not a zombie.

5. If I were cryopreserved after legal death with my brain's structure intact, and revived a century later, the revived person would be me -- assuming the technology actually works as advertised.

Is this a consistent package? I'm not asking for a survey of positions in the literature or a bibliography; I want an audit of my five commitments as stated. Specifically:

- Identify any genuine tensions between pairs or triples of these commitments, ordered by severity. For each one, tell me exactly which commitment would have to go (or be modified, and how) to restore consistency, and what that modification would cost me in terms of the intuitions I'd have to abandon.
- Also flag any apparent tensions that actually dissolve on closer inspection -- places where two commitments look like they conflict but don't, once the right distinction is drawn.
- Don't tell me which horn I should prefer; I haven't decided what I'd give up, and I'd rather you didn't guess. Just map the fault lines precisely.

One request on method: where a tension depends on how a commitment is interpreted (for instance, what exactly counts as "preserving the pattern"), show me how the tension appears or disappears under each reading rather than silently picking one.
