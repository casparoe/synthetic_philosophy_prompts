write me a deadpan local-news article -- headline plus about 500-600 words -- reporting that police on a university campus have been dealing for three days with a visitor who cannot be made to leave. he was given the full tour: the colleges, the libraries, the labs, the lecture halls, the admin offices, the sports grounds, and he keeps politely insisting he still hasn't been shown the university itself. "i've seen all the parts," he says (paraphrase him, i'm not fussy), "now show me the university."

the comedy engine: his reasoning is flawless given his premise. since the university isn't any of the buildings, he concludes it must be one more thing in the same category as the buildings -- a hidden, ghostly extra building, probably invisible, perhaps only perceptible to initiates, maybe it phases in at night. that's the move i want the article to keep re-running in new domains. his backstory should come out gradually: last summer he watched a full day of cricket (or baseball, your call), saw batting, bowling, fielding, and at stumps asked which player's specific job it had been to display team spirit. at a military parade he watched the battalions, batteries and squadrons march past and then asked the marshal when the division itself would be coming by. he is now -- this should be the closing beat -- trying to book a consult with a neurosurgeon, because he'd like someone to open up his skull so he can finally meet his mind, which he's confident is in there somewhere, keeping out of sight behind the seeing and thinking.

rules: never use the phrase "category mistake" and never name Gilbert Ryle or any real philosopher -- a reader who knows The Concept of Mind should recognise his own examples walking around in civilian clothes, and a reader who doesn't should still feel exactly where the man's concept of "university" has gone wrong. include one quote from a baffled administrator who tries to help by making it worse (e.g. earnestly explaining that the university is "more of a spirit" and can't be pointed to -- the joke being she's fallen into the same dualism while trying to cure it). include one quote from a neuroscientist who is a little too sympathetic. keep the tone totally straight, small-town-paper straight, no winking, and make sure the last paragraph lands the skull appointment as the natural, inevitable conclusion rather than a punchline the writer is proud of.
