Write the joint statement that would come out of a real adversarial collaboration between a committed spacetime substantivalist and a committed relationist, with Newton's bucket as the anchor case. Both are working in good faith and both are reasonably sophisticated: the substantivalist knows general relativity and isn't leaning on absolute velocity like it's still the 1600s, and the relationist has heard the standard replies to Leibniz and isn't just going to shout "rotating relative to what?" and declare victory.

To stop this turning into two position papers stapled together, ground everything in cases. The statement has to cover at least these four:

1. Newton's original bucket, as it actually happens, in our universe with its actual distribution of matter.
2. Two buckets in an otherwise completely empty universe, sitting at rest relative to each other, which are then "set spinning" while keeping their mutual distance and relative orientation exactly the same. (I made this one up for the occassion -- the relationist presumably has to say there's no fact of the matter about whether they're spinning, since nothing in the relative configuration ever changes; make both sides spell out precisely what they'd say about it, including what, if anything, the water surfaces do.)
3. A rotating bucket filled with superfluid helium, which develops quantized vortices only when it rotates -- a purely local, intrinsic physical signature of rotation, no telescopes or distant stars needed. Does the existence of a local rotation-detector settle anything metaphysical, or can each side absorb it into its own story without cost?
4. A hypothetical future finding (invent it, but state it precisely): for instance, evidence that inertial effects here depend measurably on the mass distribution of the surrounding universe, beyond anything general relativity's frame-dragging predicts. Each side writes down, in advance, what that finding would do to its position -- and what a clean null result would do.

The deliverable, formatted like this:

(a) Agreed claims: a numbered list both parties sign. Include at least: what Newton's argument from the bucket actually establishes and what it does not; what, if anything, the move to GR changed about the dispute (they might discuss cosmological solutions where matter and the compass of inertia never come apart, and whether that's awkward for the substantivalist or neutral); and at least one claim per side that they'd rather not concede but honestly must.

(b) Disagreements: a table with exactly these columns:
| Crux | Substantivalist position (worded so they'd sign it) | Relationist position (worded so they'd sign it) | Case or argument both accept as bearing on it | Substantivalist's prediction/commitment | Relationist's prediction/commitment | Would settling this actually move either side, and which one? |

(c) A short closing section in which they: identify at least one part of the historical dispute they now agree was verbal and explain why; and flag one point where they agree their views are empirically equivalent and differ only in explanation -- and say whether they take that kind of disagreement to be genuine.

No verdict and no referee's summary at the end. It should read like a document both of them would actually put their names on, not like a quiet win for either side.
