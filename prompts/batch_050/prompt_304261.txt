So my partner and I hosted a friend of ours from Munich last month, and the two of them got into this whole thing over something tiny and it escalated into a debate neither would drop. I stayed out of it but I keep going back and forth on who was right, so I want a referee.

Background: the three of us were walking home late, like 1am, totally empty streets, and we hit a red pedestrian light. I crossed, my American friend Jake crossed, and our German friend Katrin stood there waiting for the green. Jake laughed at her and it started. Roughly how it went:

(1) Katrin said waiting isn't about this specific crossing being dangerous, it's about the norm. A society where everyone follows small traffic rules even when nobody's watching is a society where you can rely on strangers, and that reliability is worth way more than the ten seconds she saved.

(2) Jake said that's exactly the kind of thinking he finds creepy — treating a rule as having authority detached from its purpose. The light exists because of cars; no cars, the rule is satisfied in spirit, and standing there is just conditioning yourself to obey things without thinking, which is a genuinely bad habit to train.

(3) Katrin said nobody thinks their own exception is the bad one — everyone who breaks a rule has a story about why their case is different, and the whole point of a norm is that you don't get to adjudicate your own case, because your judgment is precisely what's not trustworthy when you're the one who benefits.

(4) Jake said fine, but her side has the worse historical track record — a culture that prizes order and rule-following over individual conscience is a culture where, when the rules themselves turn bad, everyone keeps marching, and at least the American instinct of 'I answer to my own conscience' puts a brake on that. (He did bring up the obvious century, yes.)

(5) Katrin said that's a fantasy Americans tell themselves — in practice 'my conscience' usually means 'my self-interest with a halo on it,' a conscience you never have to justify to anyone catches nothing, and actual brakes on bad states have come from boring rule-of-law institutions, not from populations full of self-appointed heroes.

(6) Jake conceded that for pure coordination stuff like traffic and recycling she has a point, but said you still need conscience as a backstop for when rules go wrong. Katrin said backstops erode — a norm that everyone follows except when they privately judge it wrong isn't a norm anymore, it's a suggestion, and you can't have the reliability and the escape hatch.

Can you go through the six moves one at a time and say who's right on each specific point (or where each one overreaches), and then say what you think both of them are missing? I'm not asking whether you'd wait at the light — I want the actual arguments graded.
