I'm drafting the statement of positions for my qualifying paper in philosophy of psychology, on the nature of concepts, and before I submit it I want a consistency audit. Here is where I've landed:

(1) The representation underlying everyday categorization is a prototype: a stored summary of a category's statistically central features, with membership judgments graded by similarity to it. This, I take it, is what typicality effects are evidence for — robins counting as better birds than penguins, chairs as better furniture than ashtrays, and so on.

(2) Conceptual combination is compositional: the content of a complex concept like "pet fish" is a function of the contents of "pet" and "fish" alone. I hold this because nothing else I know of explains productivity — our ability to form and understand unlimited novel combinations on first encounter.

(3) Concepts are stable across contexts: the prototype one deploys for, say, "bird" is the same representation whether one is birdwatching, reading a menu, or doing biology. If the operative concept shifted with every context, I don't see how communication through a shared vocabulary would be possible.

(4) Ad hoc categories — "things to grab if the house catches fire" — display graded typicality structure of the same form as ordinary categories, and I take this to show that graded structure is a deep and general feature of conceptual thought rather than an artifact of a few overworked laboratory categories.

(5) Similarity is not the whole story: when subjects learn that an animal has been altered to look and behave exactly like a member of another species, they judge — rightly, in my view — that it remains what it was. I therefore accept that causal-explanatory beliefs, something like an essence placeholder, govern categorization alongside whatever similarity structure there is.

Are all five jointly tenable? For each pair genuinely in tension, state the tension precisely — what each commitment is motivated by and why those motivations conflict — rank the tensions by severity, and for each one give the weakest revision that restores consistency together with what it costs. Distinguish these from pairs that merely look incompatible but can be held together with a bit of care, and say what the reconciliation looks like in those cases. Finally, if I can afford to revise only one commitment before the paper goes in, which single revision would remove the most strain?
