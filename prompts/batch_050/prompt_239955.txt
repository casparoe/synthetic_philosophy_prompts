I help run a small decision theory reading group and I want to hand out a "where do you actually stand on Kavka's toxin puzzle?" decision tree at our next session. Quick recap of the setup so we're on the same page: a billionaire will pay you a million dollars if, at midnight tonight, you genuinely intend to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow afternoon. The toxin makes you painfully ill for a day but causes no lasting damage, and the payment depends only on your intention at midnight -- after the money arrives you're perfectly free to not drink, and everyone knows this.

Build the tree from the questions, not from a list of names: first work out the concrete situations on which the positions come apart (e.g., "if you knew for certain you'd chicken out tomorrow, could you still form the intention tonight?", "once the money is in your account, is there any reason at all left to drink?"), then locate the positions in the space of possible answers. The leaves must include at least: Kavka's own resolution (rational to intend, irrational to drink), the view that you can't even form the intention once you see you won't drink, the resolute-choice view that it's rational to follow through and drink, and at least one dissolution that rejects a presupposition of the whole puzzle rather than answering it. Seven or eight branch questions max, each phrased as a case you can react to without knowing which camp it sorts you into -- nothing like "do you think intentions reduce to predictions?"

Note any combinations of answers nobody seems to occupy and say briefly why (incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored), and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate instead of papering over it.
