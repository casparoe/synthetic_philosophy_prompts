I organize our department's colloquium series, and this term we are hosting an epistemologist whose work defends the accuracy-first program: the project of vindicating Bayesian norms by showing that considerations of accuracy alone condemn violations of probabilism, conditionalization, and the like. Please draft a sequence of eight to ten questions that presses where the program is genuinely vulnerable rather than inviting her to restate it. Include at least one question on each of the following: whether the vindication of probabilism depends on the choice of accuracy measure, and what justifies restricting attention to strictly proper scoring rules rather than other measures that look no less natural; whether dominance arguments covertly presuppose that accuracy is the sole epistemic good, and where virtues such as informativeness or explanatory power are supposed to fit within a purely accuracy-based axiology; the worry that, since dominance constrains only coherence and not priors, the program vindicates the letter of Bayesianism while licensing a permissiveness that deprives the norms of most of their bite; and whether the diachronic accuracy argument for conditionalization still recommends it in cases of anticipated memory loss or self-distrust, where deferring to one's future self seems to maximize expected accuracy. For each question, give the reply she is most likely to offer and a follow-up that closes off that reply. End the sequence with a question about what formal or philosophical result would lead her to abandon the program.
