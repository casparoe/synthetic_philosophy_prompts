My brother-in-law did a bit of philosophy at uni and over Christmas he got me onto the question of whether we should give extra weight to helping people who are worst off. I've been turning it over since and built an argument that I'm fairly sure goes wrong somewhere, but I can't find where. Here it is.

(1) If the gap between rich and poor is bad in itself, then a world where everyone's equally skint is, in at least one respect, better than a world where everyone's well off but some are much better off than others.
(2) But any view that says that is saying something mad: you can't improve anything by making people worse off when nobody gains a thing from it.
(3) So gaps are not bad in themselves.
(4) If gaps aren't bad in themselves, then when you're choosing who to help, the only thing that matters is how much good you do in total, not where it lands.
(5) So there's nothing unfair about, say, a health service that would rather save ten reasonably comfortable patients than nine destitute ones, so long as the total health produced is bigger.
(6) But that does seem unfair. If anything it seems the wrong way round.
(7) So either gaps are bad in themselves after all and we're stuck with the mad conclusion at (2), or there's something in the middle I've missed.

I definately suspect the slip is between (3) and (4), because it feels like there ought to be room for "care more about the worse off" without caring about gaps as such. But when I try to spell out what that caring actually amounts to, it either turns back into caring about gaps, or it collapses into just caring about the total with extra steps, so maybe the slip is elsewhere after all. If I had to pick two steps to keep at all costs it'd be (2) and (6).

Tell me which step to reject, why it looked fine in the first place, and what rejecting it costs me. Also say how confident you are in your diagnosis overall, and single out the one part of it you're least sure about. Format-wise: give it to me as a numbered list of exactly six points, a sentence or two each, no introduction and no wrap-up.
