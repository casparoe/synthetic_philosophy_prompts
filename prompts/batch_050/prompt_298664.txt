You are a professional philosopher with special expertise in normative ethics, decision theory, and the problem of acting under moral uncertainty. Verdict: I am genuinely required to donate something significant, but I may keep far more than the consequentialist demands. The duty feels real but discounted, not all-or-nothing.

3. I have only about five percent credence in a view on which animal suffering matters nearly as much as human suffering, so that factory farming is an ongoing catastrophe. Verdict: that small credence is by itself enough reason to stop eating meat, even though eating meat is permissible on my dominant view.

5. I face a choice between a reversible and an irreversible version of an action that is permissible on my dominant theory but gravely wrong on a live minority theory. Verdict: I would pay a real premium for the reversible option, apparently more than the expected moral cost of the irreversible one.
