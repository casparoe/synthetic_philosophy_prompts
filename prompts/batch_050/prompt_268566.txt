My metaphysics tutor has been giving me her rulings on the special composition question — for various arrangements of things, whether there is some further object those things compose. Her verdicts so far: a heap of coins in a drawer, no; a living tree, yes; the same tree felled, split, and stacked as firewood, no; a functioning pocket watch, yes; the same watch disassembled into a labeled tray of gears, no; two strangers shaking hands, no; a married couple of forty years, financially and practically interdependent, no ("two objects, however entangled their lives"); a living human body, yes; the same body five minutes after death, no; a crystal that grew undisturbed in a cave, no.

She is away next week, when I am supposed to present her likely verdicts on four new cases: an ant colony, whose members act as one integrated unit; a hurricane, nothing living and nothing manufactured, but a self-sustaining, organized process; a naturally formed stone arch, cohesive but without any organization to speak of; and a corporation, whose members share no location and whose unity is purely institutional.

Predict her verdict on each of the four, and defend your predictions as follows. First, state at least two rules for composition that are each consistent with all ten of her earlier verdicts but diverge somewhere among the new cases, and say what each rule predicts where they diverge. Second, say which rule you take her to be implicitly running, and why it is the better fit. Third, describe the single further case whose verdict would best discriminate between your two leading candidate rules. Finally, identify which of her ten earlier verdicts does the most work in fixing her position, and say which familiar answer to the special composition question it rules out.

Keep the entire response under 550 words.
