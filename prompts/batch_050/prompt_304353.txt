For my seminar on the conceptual foundations of asylum, I give students the decision record of a single asylum adjudicator. Her rulings to date:

1. Granted protection to a journalist imprisoned for criticizing his government.
2. Granted protection to a woman fleeing an abusive husband in a country where the police, as a matter of practice, decline to intervene in violence against wives.
3. Denied protection to a man fleeing a neighborhood where gangs kill indiscriminately and the police have effectively withdrawn; he was never personally singled out.
4. Granted protection to a member of a criminalized religious sect whose adherents are regularly attacked by mobs the state never prosecutes.
5. Denied protection to a farmer facing a genuine risk of starvation, where drought compounded by corrupt mismanagement of food aid has destroyed his region's agriculture, but no one targeted him.

Her next case: a shopkeeper whom a cartel has marked for execution after he refused to pay extortion. The cartel has already killed three other shopkeepers who refused; the state has shown itself unable to protect anyone in his position; and there is no suggestion he was selected because of his race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in any recognizable social group — he was selected only because he, like anyone, might have refused.

Predict her ruling, and then do the real work. State at least two candidate principles about what asylum is for — for instance, a persecution-with-nexus principle (protection only against harm inflicted on account of protected grounds), a targeted-harm principle (protection against individualized serious harm the state will not or cannot prevent, whatever the reason for the targeting), and a humanitarian principle (protection against serious harm the state fails to avert, targeted or not) — and say which of them fit her record, what each predicts about the shopkeeper, and which she appears to be running. If the record genuinely underdetermines the case, say so; identify which prior ruling is the closest analog and why; state what principle she would be adopting under each available ruling; and specify the further case that would finally reveal which principle she actually holds.
