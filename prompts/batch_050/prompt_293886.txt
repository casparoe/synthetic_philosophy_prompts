ok so my philosophy club got into possible worlds stuff last week and i built this argument and now it seems to prove way too much, but i cant find where it breaks. (1) Saying "i could have been a pilot" means there's a possible world where i'm a pilot. (2) But according to counterpart theory, the person in that world isnt literally me, it's just someone similar enough to me (my counterpart). (3) And what counts as "similar enough" depends on which features we care about — in some contexts the pilot guy counts as my counterpart, in others he doesn't. (4) So whether i could have been a pilot depends on what we happen to care about, not on any fact about me. (5) But then every claim about what could have happened works like that — it's never really true or false, just relative to our interests. (6) That's absurd: it either could have rained yesterday or it couldn't, no matter what anyone cares about. (7) So something in the argument is wrong, probably (1) or (2). My friend in the club says the argument is actually fine and we should just accept that all possibility-talk is interest-relative, but that feels like giving up on there being facts at all. Which step should i reject, why did it look convincing, and what does rejecting it cost me? If i have to choose i'd rather keep (6) than anything else.
