I'm a grad student in political science, not philosophy, but I keep ending up in epistemology papers because of a fight I had with my labmate. We looked at the exact same dataset on housing policy effects, we both know the methods, and she concluded the effect is real and I concluded it's noise. When I said "okay, we're equally competent and we disagree, so we should both move toward the middle," she said no -- she did her analysis carefully and the fact that I screwed mine up (her words) isn't evidence against her result. I thought that was cheating, but she had a comeback I couldn't answer: by my rule, if a creationist disagrees with a biologist, the biologist has to budge too.

So before I read any more of this literature, I want the spec first. Act as an epistemologist who works on disagreement and think carefully. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of how to respond to disagreement with an epistemic peer has to deliver. I want the cases it has to get right (like my labmate case, the case where one party is clearly more informed but both are equally smart, cases where the disagreement is huge and widespread like in philosophy itself, and cases where you literally cannot re-check the other person's reasoning), the distinctions the theory can't be allowed to collapse (I have a feeling that lumping "we disagree" together with "I have evidence that we disagree" is where theories go wrong, but I want this spelled out), and the results it can't contradict without being disqualifying -- e.g. it had better not force the biologist to meet the creationist halfway, and it had better not let everyone just stick to their guns on everything. For each requirement, give me the case or consideration behind it, not just the requirement. Also tell me which desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, if any -- I have a vague sense that any rule that treats me and my peer symmetrically has to exempt my own reasoning somewhere, and that this is a real problem and not a technicality.

Then, briefly, score conciliationism and steadfastness against the list -- but keep that short, the list is what I actually want. One more thing: please separate what's basically settled in this literature from what's genuinely contested, and don't present either of those two views as the consensus if there isn't one. If it turns out half the requirements on my wishlist are just stealth assumptions of one camp, say that too.
