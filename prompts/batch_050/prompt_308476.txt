Is there a name for this? I'm a programmer who reads philosphy of math as a hobby and there's a kind of situation I keep running into that I don't have a word for. Take the four-colour theorem, or that sphere-packing proof the journal famously spent years refereeing and still wouldn't fully certify. Every mathematician I ask believes these results, but none of them can tell me *why* they're true in a way I could check myself. The answer always boils down to: the computer went through all the cases. Even the people who wrote the programs haven't seen the cases -- they've seen the program. So the warrant for the theorem seems to bottom out in the behavior of some hardware rather than in any mind at all.

I don't think this is the same as ordinary trust in testimony, because testimony chains normally end in someone who actually surveyed the argument, and here there's no such person. It also doesn't seem like just a very long proof, since a long proof could at least in principle be read end to end by one person. And it's not quite the same as an experimental result in physics either, because nobody thinks the machine is *evidence* for the theorem, they think it *established* it.

So: does this configuration have a name? If there are a few nearby concepts, tell me which fits best and where my thing diverges from each -- I'm not going to be satisfied with just a label. And if nothing established quite fits, coin a term, define it, and give me some boundary cases (e.g. does a proof count if one person *could* check it all but nobody ever has?). Bonus question: does a theorem warranted this way count as known a priori, or has something empirical snuck into mathematics?
