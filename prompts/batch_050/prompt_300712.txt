Read Andrew Niccol's Gattaca through the philosophical dispute over whether DNA is literally a code. The film's world takes the informational picture at full strength: a drop of blood at birth yields a readout of a life — disease probabilities, expected lifespan — and Vincent's career as a "borrowed ladder" is staged as a struggle against a text that claims to have written him in advance. I'd like a reading that does three things. First, identify where the film dramatizes the strong semantic view of the genome (genes as instructions that specify traits) and where it quietly relies on exactly what critics of that view attack: context-independence, one-way causation from sequence to phenotype, a genome legible without reference to development or environment — note that Vincent evades detection not by changing his sequence but by changing who reads it. Second, ask whether the film's humanism actually refutes genetic determinism or merely concedes the code metaphor and locates freedom outside its jurisdiction: Vincent beats his brother in the swim by holding nothing back, but is that a counterexample to the prediction or an exception the prediction never claimed to cover? Third, say what the film understands better than much of the literature — that a probabilistically weak prediction can be socially self-fulfilling, and that an institution can treat the code metaphor as literal and thereby make some of its claims true. Be honest about where the film resists the philosophical reading, and close by saying what it adds to the debate that the journal articles don't.
