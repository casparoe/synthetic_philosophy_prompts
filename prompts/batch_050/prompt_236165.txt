I am a PhD student in economic history, and I want to pressure-test a practice in my literature whose philosophical foundations I suspect go unexamined. A standard design in the economics-and-culture literature regresses contemporary regional outcomes — income, education, entrepreneurship — on historical religious composition, say the Protestant share of a nineteenth-century population, and interprets persistent coefficients as evidence that culture causes development. Weber's Protestant ethic thesis is routinely invoked as the theoretical warrant, often alongside channels Weber himself never proposed, such as mass literacy.

My question is what conception of culture this practice presupposes, and whether that conception is Weber's. Concretely: (1) Treating culture as an explanatory variable alongside institutions and geography seems to presuppose that culture is a discrete, durable, intergenerationally transmitted stock that does independent causal work. Which rival conceptions — culture as shorthand for institutions, culture as equilibrium behavior, culture as post hoc rationalization of incentives — does the design rule out, and could the same coefficients be generated under those rivals? (2) Weber's own account appears to contain a self-undermining mechanism: the ascetic ethic was needed to call forth the spirit of capitalism, but once the capitalist order stood on mechanical foundations, it no longer required religious support. If so, then finding effects of Protestant affiliation a century later is not a confirmation of Weber but evidence against his mechanism. Is that right, or can a Weberian accommodate long-run persistence? (3) Weber locates the causal force in an ethic — a set of meanings attached to conduct — whereas the data record only denominational affiliation. What would the research design have to look like if it took that distinction seriously, and does the gap between ethic and affiliation vitiate the identification?

I am not after a reading list. I want the implicit theory reconstructed, a judgment on whether the practice is coherent with its own canonical source, and an honest statement of where the practice is philosophically underdetermined — compatible with several theories of culture rather than committed to one.
