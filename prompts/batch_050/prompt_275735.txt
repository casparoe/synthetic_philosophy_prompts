I am writing a chapter on motivated reasoning and find myself stuck on a question I would like you to answer with an actual number rather than a survey of positions. The claim is this: genuine self-deception, in the strict sense — a person sincerely avowing that not-p while also, in some psychologically real sense, believing that p, where the route to believing not-p is shaped by their own desire that not-p — actually occurs in ordinary adult human psychology. Not mere wishful thinking, not pretense, not a failure to notice one's evidence, but the real thing that the paradoxes in the literature are about.

Please give me your credence that this strict phenomenon exists, and decompose the number rather than defending it wholesale. At minimum I want separate rough probabilities for each of the following sub-questions, with a sentence or two of justification for each: (i) whether belief formation is ever subject to the kind of influence by desire that the phenomenon requires, given that belief does not appear to be under direct voluntary control; (ii) whether a single person can coherently be attributed two conflicting doxastic states at the same time, or whether the strict phenomenon instead requires some partitioning of the mind into semi-autonomous systems; (iii) whether the everyday evidence usually cited for self-deception — the smoker who avows that his habit is safe, the partner who is the last to know — is better explained by deflationary alternatives such as selectively gathering evidence, attending asymmetrically, or merely hoping without believing; and (iv) whether the phrase 'deceive oneself' might fail to pick out any unified psychological state at all, so that the question dissolves rather than resolves.

Then show me how the sub-probabilities combine into your final number — I do not expect exact arithmetic, but I want the structure of the dependence to be explicit, including which sub-questions you treat as independent and which you do not. Finally, tell me which term in the decomposition your final credence is most sensitive to, and what sort of evidence or argument, empirical or conceptual, would move that term the most.
