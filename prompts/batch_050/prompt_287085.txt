I'm designing a one-page flowchart handout for a philosophy of mathematics reading group that sorts participants by their response to Skolem's paradox: the fact that if ZFC has a model at all, it has a countable one, even though ZFC proves that uncountable sets exist. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: begin with the question on which responses most fundamentally divide (whether the paradox is a genuine problem for set-theoretic realism at all, or merely shows that "uncountable" is model-relative), then let each subsequent split be a further claim the remaining positions disagree on — for instance, whether first-order axioms can fix an intended interpretation, whether second-order logic restores categoricity or merely relocates the relativity into its semantics, and whether there is a unique intended model of the cumulative hierarchy at all. Limit the tree to five or six splitting questions. Each leaf should carry the position's name (or "unnamed" if the combination is coherent but undefended) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment — what it has to say about ordinary mathematical claims that every real number can be enumerated. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, diagnose whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored, and flag any pair of named positions your tree cannot separate rather than forcing a spurious distinction between them. Finally, since I don't want the handout to read as scrupulously neutral, add a short closing note committing to which leaf you find best supported and why, acknowledging the strongest consideration on the other side.
