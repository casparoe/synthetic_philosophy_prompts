I’ve been dealing with an ant infestation in my kitchen, and while we were setting out traps my sister and a friend of hers got into an argument that I weirdly can’t stop thinking about.

(1) My sister said killing the ants is basically morally negligible. Even if ants are sentient, she said, they must be sentient to such a tiny degree that crushing one is closer to unplugging a thermostat than hurting a mouse. Both ants and thermostats respond to inputs, but neither has the rich inner life that makes suffering matter much.

(2) Her friend said the thermostat comparison proves nothing. A thermostat tracks one variable and switches something on and off, while ants learn, navigate, communicate, avoid danger, and coordinate their behavior. Suppose there’s only a 5% chance an ant has any conscious experience. Killing 200 ants would then be like killing 10 definitely-sentient creatures in expectation, which no longer looks negligible.

(3) My sister replied that this mixes up two different things: the probability that an ant is sentient at all, and the degree of sentience it has if it is. Maybe there’s a 5% chance of some faint experience, but it doesn’t follow that each ant gets counted as 5% of a fully sentient animal. If the relevant experience is extremely faint, the expected moral weight might still be close to zero.

(4) Her friend said that “faint” suffering still isn’t zero. If an ant has even a very dull version of a bad experience, then enough ants can add up to something morally serious. Otherwise we could excuse harming any creature whose mental life is less vivid than ours just by calling its suffering faint.

(5) My sister said that view makes ordinary life impossible: walking on grass, driving, building houses, and washing clothes all kill tiny animals in expectation, so if ant suffering aggregates like that, almost every human activity becomes a moral disaster. Her friend answered that maybe that’s just an uncomfortable conclusion, and “the conclusion is inconvenient” isn’t an argument against it.

Can you adjudicate the disagreement move by move? For each of the five moves, say whether it’s clearly right, mostly right, confused, or clearly wrong, and why. I’m especially interested in the distinction between being uncertain whether something is sentient and thinking its sentience comes in degrees, and whether the thermostat analogy is doing any real work. Also say what both of them might be missing, if anything.
