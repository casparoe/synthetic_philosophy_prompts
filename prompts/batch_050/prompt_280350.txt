I run a reading group on formal epistemology, and two of our members have been fighting about the Judy Benjamin problem all semster without getting anywhere. Quick setup in case your memory of it is fuzzy: Judy parachutes into a territory split into a Blue half and a Red half, with no reason to think she's more likely to land in one than the other, so credence 1/2 in each. Blue's HQ covers 3/4 of the Blue side, Red's HQ covers 1/4 of the Red side. She lands, and gets a radio message from her superiors: if you are in Red territory, then you are in HQ. The question is what her credence that she's in Blue territory should be after hearing that.

One member (call her the materialist) says the message simply rules out being in Red without being in HQ, so Judy strictly conditionalizes on the material conditional and her credence in Blue rises to 4/5. The other (call him the irrelevancist) says the message tells her absolutely nothing about which side she's on -- learning that all Red territory is HQ territory can't shift the Red/Blue odds -- so her credence stays 1/2, and the update has to be modeled some other way, like a constraint on her conditional probabilities rather than an event she conditionalizes on.

Write the joint statement these two could actually both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want:

1. A numbered list of claims they agree on. I suspect one is that the answer depends on the operator's message policy (e.g., whether the operator sends this particular message whenever it's true, vs. had a fixed script, vs. chose among several true messages), and that both should admit their favored answer implicitly assumes one such policy -- but don't take my word for it, include whatever they can both genuinely endorse.
2. The precise disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept (no strawmen, no loading the dice in the formulation).
3. The cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling them would move at least one of them. I'd guess one is whether the indicative conditional in the message expresses a proposition Judy can just conditionalize on at all, and another is what follows from the message having been *sent* rather than merely being true -- but again, find the real ones.
4. For each crux, a case, test, or argument both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Candidates you might consider: fair betting odds Judy should accept after the message, long-run frequencies over many repeated drops under a specified message policy, or Dutch book / accuracy-style arguments. If both sides make the same prediction about one of these, say so and explain whether that test can still separate them.

Format it as the numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table, then the cruxes. And flag any disagreement that, in the drafting, turned out to be about words rather than substance.
