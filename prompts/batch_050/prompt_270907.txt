so I'm trying to steelman every side of the mind-uploading / gradual-replacement debate and I keep running in circles. Here's everything I already have on my list, so don't give me any of these or a rewording of them:

1. Fading qualia (Chalmers-style): if you swap neurons for silicon chips one at a time, either consciousness fades out midway (absurd, since behavior stays identical) or it's preserved the whole way, so gradual replacement works.
2. The copy-isn't-you objection: scanning and simulating makes a duplicate, not you. You stay in the chair and die; the upload just says it's you.
3. The Moravec intuition pump going the other way: replace neurons gradually and you'd never notice anything, you'd just keep having experiences, so obviously it's still you.
4. Causal/substrate continuity worries: there's some physical property of actual brain tissue (continuous metabolism, whatever) that a chip-by-chip swap destroys, even if the functional profile is unchanged.
5. The fission problem: if survival = psychological continuity, then two uploads both being "you" breaks the logic of identity (identity can't be one-to-many), so continuity isn't enough.
6. The undecidability point: from the outside you can never verify the upload is conscious, and the upload's own testimony proves nothing, so the question might be empirically unanswerable in principle.
7. The Parfit/Buddhist dissolution: there is no deep fact about whether it's "you," identity isn't what matters, so both the optimists and pessimists are arguing over an empty question.

I want one MORE move in this debate that isn't on the list. Not a rewording, not a special case, not two of them stapled together. It can be an argument for uploads working, an argument against, a thought experiment, or a new way of framing what's at stake -- anything, as long as it's genuinely distinct from 1-7. It also has to be something a reasonably careful person could take seriously, not just word salad.
