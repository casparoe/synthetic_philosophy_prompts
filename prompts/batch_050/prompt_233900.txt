I'm helping put together a small museum exhibit on the history of infographics, and the centerpiece is going to be a (clearly hypothetical) "Neurath reacts to the internet" section — the idea being that Otto Neurath, with his ISOTYPE picture language from the Vienna Social and Economic Museum days, is maybe the one dead thinker who genuinely tried to build what emoji and viral infographics sort of became, decades before either existed. I've been going back and forth with the curator about what to put on the wall text and we keep disagreeing, so I want a careful prediction of Neurath's likely views on a few specific things that only appeared long after he died. Three in particular: (1) Emoji — would he see them as the belated fulfillment of his dream of an international picture language that crosses linguistic barriers where words fail, or as a corruption of that dream? Bear in mind that emoji are expressive and ambiguous rather than denotative, they render differently on different platforms, and they're governed by a private consortium rather than any public educational project. (2) Modern data visualization culture — corporate dashboards, the whole chartjunk debate, viral infographics on social media that use his own trick of repeated unit symbols but often to mislead. Would he double down on his old rules (show a bigger quantity by more copies of the same symbol rather than one enlarged symbol, standardized icons, no perspective trickery), or would he rethink any of them given interactive and personalized graphics? (3) AI image generation — the curator insists this go in. Neurath wanted pictures to be an empirically disciplined way of communicating social facts, something that could sit alongside a unified scientific worldview. What would he make of images produced by statistical models trained on scraped pictures, where nobody in particular vouches for what the image shows?

Please reason from his actual committments and context — the Vienna Circle milieu without just collapsing him into a generic logical positivist, the unity-of-science encyclopedia project, the Red Vienna adult-education setting his museum work grew out of, his distaste for metaphysics and his general epistemological naturalism — rather than giving me "old man yells at cloud" material. Concrete details from how ISOTYPE actually worked (the transformer role, the standardized-symbol method, the teamwork with Gerd Arntz) are very welcome if they bear on the predictions.

One request that matters a lot for the wall text: be explicit about how solid the ground is under each prediction. Where his own writings or the standard scholarship directly support an inference, say so. Where you're extrapolating, or where people who study Neurath genuinely disagree about how to read him — for instance, how naive he really was about the neutrality of his picture statistics, or how far his socialism was baked into the method itself rather than just being the occasion for it — flag that as contested instead of presenting one reading as the settled consensus. I'd rather have a wall text with honest hedges than one full of confident made-up Neurath.
