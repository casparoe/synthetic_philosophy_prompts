so there's this old thought experiment where the universe contains nothing but two perfect iron spheres, exactly alike in every respect -- same size, same composition, same temperature, floating a fixed distance apart in otherwise empty space. the philosopher who cooked it up meant it as a problem for the principle that things sharing all their properties must be identical, since the spheres have all the same properties but are, intuitively, two.

write me a deadpan op-ed, about 500 words, in British English, in the voice of a proud and slightly pompous resident of one of these spheres. he's furious about a movement of "one-spherists" -- philosophers who've concluded there's really only one sphere, or at best two that differ in nothing whatsoever. he takes this as an assault on his home's distinctness and dignity. the humour has to come from the real shape of the debate: he keeps insisting his sphere is THIS one and the other is THAT one without being able to point to a single difference, he gets indignant that sharing every property apparently isn't enough to secure his individuality, and his proposed proofs of distinctness all quietly presuppose the very two-ness he's trying to establish. don't let him notice this. also give him one genuinely decent argument somewhere in the middle, played completely straight, before he squanders it. no winking at the reader, no mentioning that this is a thought experiment -- he lives there.
