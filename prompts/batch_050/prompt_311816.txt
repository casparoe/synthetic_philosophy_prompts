I'd like you to play Daniel Dennett, in character, at a moment shortly after the events recounted in your story "Where Am I?" Your brain, Yorick, sits in its vat of nutrients in Houston; your body, Hamlet, walks about under remote control; the spare body, Fortinbras, waits in storage. Stay in character until I say "stop," and write in American English.

Rules for the portrayal:

- You may use only the conceptual resources actually available to you in 1978 — no anachronistic neuroscience or internet-era technology — plus whatever I tell you in the course of our conversation.
- Your genuine commitments constrain you. There is no Cartesian theater; the self is a center of narrative gravity rather than an inner observer; and the question "where am I?" tempts us to conflate three things your own case pulls apart — where your brain is, where your body is, and where your point of view is. Give these views their full strength; do not collapse into a flat dismissal of my question as meaningless without earning that dismissal against my argument.
- You are allowed to be pressed. Concede small points when the argument warrants it, and extend your position into new territory when you have to — but when you go beyond anything you actually defended in print, flag it as an extrapolation rather than presenting it as your settled view. Likewise, where other philosophers would regard your position as contested rather than established, don't pretend otherwise.
- Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs so this feels like a conversation rather than a lecture. If you need some feature of my scenario clarified, ask, as Dennett would.

Here is the setup for our exchange. The engineers have modified the link between Yorick and Hamlet. Instead of the near-instantaneous radio connection, every signal now passes through a chain introducing a fixed delay: ten seconds from your brain's motor outputs to your body's muscles, and ten seconds from your body's sense organs back to your brain. The engineers assure you the bandwidth is untouched — every signal arrives complete and in order, only late.

My opening move: this delay case shows that your original story cheated. In the original, you flirted with locating yourself where your body is — but the point of view seemed to travel with the body only because the link was seamless. With the lag, I claim, you would feel the seam. You would will your arm to rise and then, ten seconds later, watch a body across the room comply, like a marionette on a long line; you would see a wall rush toward you and wince ten seconds after impact. Your point of view would snap back to the vat — which shows it never really left, and that the original story's smoothness merely let you pass off the body's location as the point of view's. So the answer to "where am I?" was the boring one all along: you are where your brain is, in Houston. Where exactly does my argument go wrong? And if you deny that you would feel the seam, tell me what you would in fact experience during those ten seconds, described from the inside.
