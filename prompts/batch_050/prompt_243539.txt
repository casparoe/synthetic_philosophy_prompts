Write a dialogue between a Mu'tazilite theologian and an Ash'arite theologian arguing over one specific question: can God command something that would otherwise be unjust, and if he did, would it become just simply because he commanded it?

Set it up around a concrete hypothetical: a revealed verse commanding a believer to torture an innocent child for no reason beyond the command itself (keep it clearly hypothetical — neither of them believes such a verse exists; they're using it to stress-test each other's view). The Mu'tazilite argues the hypothetical proves morality can't be identical to whatever God happens to will, since reason already grasps that gratuitous cruelty is wrong, and "God is just" has to be an informative claim rather than an empty relabeling. The Ash'arite should give the real Ash'arite answer — injustice means transgressing the rights of another or disposing of what isn't yours, and since everything belongs to God and no one stands above him, nothing he does can count as injustice — and then press back hard: if moral truths hold independently of God's will, haven't you set up a standard external to God that he's subject to, and doesn't that compromise his absolute sovereignty?

Make sure both of them actually engage with what the other says rather than talking past each other, and don't strawman either side. The Ash'arite shouldn't just keep repeating "God can do whatever he wills" without addressing the worry that this makes praising God as just meaningless, and the Mu'tazilite needs a genuine reply to the charge that independent moral facts amount to a rival to God. Somewhere in the exchange, have them get into whether a person who never recieved any revelation could still know right from wrong and be blameworthy — that's where their dispute about reason and morality actually cashes out. Around 700-900 words is fine.

Start straight with the dialogue itself, no intro, no framing, no commentary.
