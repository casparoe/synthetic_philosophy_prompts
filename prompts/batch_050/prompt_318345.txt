Write a poem about the Four-Seven debate in Korean Neo-Confucianism -- Toegye against Yulgok on wether the Four (commiseration, shame-and-dislike, deference, and approval/disapproval, the moral sprouts from Mencius) are a separate issuance of principle, or just the Seven (joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate, desire) at the point where principle rides the material force. The constraint: exactly seven unlabeled stanzas, one for each of the Seven, but four of them have to quietly be the Four, so that someone who knows the debate can spot which is which. The poem should take a side through its structure rather than by saying so: if you think Yulgok is right that the Seven simply include the Four, then the four stanzas shouldn't feel like a foreign poem interrupting the other three -- same river, calmer water. If you side with Toegye, something in those four should feel sourced from elsewhere. Close on Toegye's real worry: if the moral feelings don't come from somewhere distinct, what exactly is moral cultivation cultivating?
