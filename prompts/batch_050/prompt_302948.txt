I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy applying for a small internal summer grant (ten weeks, no travel, deliverable is one working paper), and I need a short research proposal -- about a page and a half -- for a project in formal epistemology. The project concerns anthropic reasoning, in particular the contrast between the self-sampling assumption (reason as if you were randomly selected from the actual observers in some reference class) and the self-indication assumption (treat your own existence as evidence favoring hypotheses on which more observers exist, in proportion to how many they contain). The motivation is the familiar embarrassment for the self-indication view: the so-called presumptuous philosopher case, in which the assumption seems to license overwhelming confidence, in advance of any empirical investigation, that the universe contains vastly many observers rather than few. Defenders usually respond by biting the bullet or by restricting the principle; critics treat the case as a reductio.

My half-formed idea is that the debate has been conducted at the wrong grain of analysis. Standard presentations of the presumptuous philosopher compare hypotheses that differ only in total observer count, but the self-indication assumption, taken literally, boosts any hypothesis on which someone has exactly your current evidence -- which suggests the principle is really doing two things at once: an existence boost (favoring worlds with more observers) and a matching boost (favoring worlds with more copies of your precise subjective state). These come apart. A universe with a trillion observers who all have different experiences from mine, and a universe with a trillion exact duplicates of my current observer-moment, get the same treatment under the usual statement of the view, but it's not clear any principled version of self-indication reasoning must lump them together. The question I want the proposal to pose: is there a principled, non-ad-hoc way to grant one boost while denying the other, and if so, does the resulting position collapse back into a form of self-sampling, or does it constitute a genuinely third option -- and does it still yield presumptuous conclusions anywhere?
