So my grandmother died in March, and she was basically the family's court of appeal on a certain kind of question. We're from a community where the Ubuntu idea isn't an abstract thing, it's just how people talk -- a person is a person through other people, and gogo (grandma) treated that as a rule you could actually apply, with verdicts. Now we've got a live dispute and everyone's arguing about what she would have said, and I realize I can't figure out what rule she was actually running. Help me reconstruct it.

Her verdicts over the years, as best I remember:

- When my cousin was born she said the baby was the newest full person among us, before it had done anything for anyone or even recognized a face.
- My uncle Themba took money that was set aside for my grandfather's medicine and disappeared to Jo'burg for fifteen years. She said he had made himself a dog, that a person who refuses persons stops being one.
- My aunt Naledi has schizophrenia and sometimes goes months barely speaking to anyone, screaming at the neighbors, the works. Gogo went to her every single week, and she got genuinely angry when people called her mad. She said Naledi was fully a person as long as even one person still sat with her.
- A Malawian guy moved to our street, shared his harvest, fixed people's roofs, showed up at funerals. Within about a year gogo counted him completely one of us, more than some actual relatives.
- The ancestors obviously still count, she talked to them at the graves, they're persons full stop.
- There was a rich man in town who never did anything cruel, just lived behind his wall, never greeted anyone, never attended a single funeral, and she called him a person-shaped thing and said he was busy unbecoming a person. That one sticks with me because he never hurt anybody.

Here's the live case. Themba died last month. He never apologized to anyone in the family, ever. But his daughter nursed him through his whole last year, and by all accounts he was soft and generous with his grandkids at the end, and his last request was to be buried at home in the family plot. His kids are asking for full burial rites. Half the family says gogo already ruled on this when she called him a dog. The other half says the Naledi verdict controls, because someone did sit with him, so he was a person when he died.

What do you think gogo would have said? I'm less interested in the right answer than in: what rule was she actually running, what OTHER rules fit those six verdicts just as well (I can think of at least two that diverge on Themba), and what's the case -- real or made up -- that would have told them apart if I'd thought to ask her.
