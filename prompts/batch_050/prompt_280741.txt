I run a little reading group and we keep tripping over the same thing whenever we do current-events weeks. Someone will say we need to defend "Western values," and then the actual list they give seems to be built out of whoever the opponent is that week. When the topic is Islamist extremism, the list is secular government, free expression, women's equality. When the topic is China, suddenly the list is individualism, personal autonomy, willingness to question authority. When the topic is Russia, its rule of law and independent courts. And when someone is contrasting the present with the medievel past, the list is science and religious toleration -- even though "Judeo-Christian heritage" also gets called a core Western value in a different week, sometimes by the same writer. So the category seems to have no fixed inventory at all. The inventory gets generated backwards from whatever the foil is, and you can predict the list from the contrast being drawn, not from the category itself.

Is there an established name for this kind of concept? I've heard of family resemblance and of "essentially contested concepts," but both of those seem to be about cases where the membership or the criteria are genuinely up for grabs and people argue about them. Here nobody argues. Each individual list is perfectly reasonable on its merits, and everyone in the room nods along -- it's just a different list each time, and the shifts pass completely unnoticed. It's also not quite ordinary vagueness, since any given use is pretty precise, and it's not exactly a weasel word, since the speaker usually believes every version sincerely. If there's a real name, tell me what it is and how the thing I described differs from the near-misses I mentioned (and any others you think are closer). If there isn't a name, coin one, define it precisely, and give me some boundary cases -- does it apply to "healthy food"? to "art"? to "American values" as used in campaign speeches? -- so I can see where the edges are. Don't just hand me a label; I want the carving: what the phenomenon is exactly, what it isn't, and what work the concept does that the nearby ones don't.

End with a final line in exactly this form: \answer{...} with either the established term or your coined name inside the braces, nothing else.
