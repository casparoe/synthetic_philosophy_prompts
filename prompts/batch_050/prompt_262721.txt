I've become pretty convinced by something like Eli Hirsch's quantifier-variance diagnosis of ontology: a lot of the classic heavyweight disputes in analytic metaphysics — do tables exist over and above simples arranged tablewise, does composition ever happen — are actually verbal, because each side's use of "exists" or "there are" can be charitably interpreted so that their core claims come out true in their own language, and charity basically forces that reading. I even have my own little test for it, which I'm calling the swap test: a dispute is verbal iff, once each party is handed a translation manual into the other's idiom, they'd agree on every first-order claim and could keep disagreeing only about which language to speak.
