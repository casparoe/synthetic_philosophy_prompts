I am a high school senior helping to prepare a student ethics debate about online accountability. The motion is: coordinated deplatforming and reputational campaigns are often a form of punishment, even when the people involved are not state officials, are not acting through any formal institution, and are exercising freedoms they would ordinarily possess. My own credence is about 0.7, while my debate partner’s is about 0.25. Give your own credence, but make the reasoning more important than the number.

Use this concrete case as the shared reference point: after a student is accused of repeated cruelty in a school group chat, several popular student accounts publicly identify the behavior, organizers remove the student from invitation lists and club channels, local businesses decline to feature the student’s artwork, and strangers encourage others to maintain the boycott until a public apology and concrete changes occur. Nobody claims that criticism, refusal to associate, or consumer choice is inherently punishment; the dispute is whether the coordinated campaign, taken as a whole, becomes punishment.

Decompose the question into the sub-questions your credence actually depends on. At minimum, address whether punishment requires an authorized institution; whether intention to impose a cost for a perceived violation matters more than the legality or permissibility of the individual acts; how coordinated pressure differs from many independent people merely choosing to withdraw; whether deterrence, incapacitation, denunciation, and reform can all count as punitive aims; and whether proportionality, reliable fact-finding, or an opportunity to respond belongs in the definition of punishment rather than only in an evaluation of whether punishment is justified. Assign each sub-question a rough probability or strength, explain how those components combine, and identify which one does the most work in producing your final credence.

Then locate the likely disagreement between my 0.7 and my partner’s 0.25: is it most plausibly about the concept of punishment, the facts needed to infer punitive intent, the moral significance of private power, or the fear that calling such campaigns punishment prejudges their legitimacy? Finally, state what observation or conceptual argument would most move your estimate in either direction. Do not provide readings or citations; I am trying to expose the structure of the disagreement before we debate it.
