So I'm meant to be writing an essay on democratic backsliding and I keep running into the same wall: everyone uses the concept, nobody agrees what it is, and half the time it looks suspiciously like "my side lost". Before I pick a definition to run with, I want the full spec. Write the desiderata for a concept of democratic backsliding that's actually doing work rather than just being a partisan label -- the cases it has to classify correctly, the distinctions it can't collapse, the features of the real-world episodes it has to respect -- with the consideration behind each requirement.

Some cases to anchor it: Hungary since 2010, Poland 2015-23 and then whatever you call what's happened since the election, Turkey under Erdoğan, and the US (pick your battles there, but the concept has to say *something* non-embarrassing about it). Venezuela might be useful as a control case. Things I specifically want sorted:

- The concept has to catch the slow legalistic kind (court-packing by statute, media capture through licensing and ownership rules, electoral-law tweaking) and not just tanks on the lawn -- but then how does it avoid counting every aggressive-but-lawful governing majority as backsliding? This feels like the hard bit.
- It has to distinguish backsliding from breakdown, from plain old bad governance, and from a government enacting policies I happen to hate. If it can't do the last one it's useless.
- It has to handle reversal -- Poland post-2023 is either "backsliding undone" or something weirder, and the concept should say which.
- Someone has to do the classifying, and I want a requirement about who: expert-coded indices (V-Dem, Freedom House style) are the obvious answer but there's a live worry that the coders skew one way politically, so is "trusted by the right people" itself a desideratum or a cop-out?

Mark which requirements are genuine and which are just the prejudices of one camp, and -- this is the part I care most about -- tell me whether some subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable. My suspicion is that "fully ideologically neutral" and "catches stealth legal erosion that keeps every democratic form in place" can't both be had, but I might be wrong. Also tell me which single requirement knocks out the most concepts currently in circulation. A short table at the end is fine but the thinking should be in the criteria.
