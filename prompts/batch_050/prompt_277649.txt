Two friends of mine, Daniel and Leila, have been arguing about a griefbot Daniel uses, and I'd like a neutral adjudication of their exchange. Some background: Daniel's wife died two years ago, and he subscribed to a service that built a conversational model of her from her messages, emails, and voice recordings. She had signed up for the service herself while ill, though the consent form was mostly marketing language. The service recently added a feature that lets the model "keep up" with the present: it comments on current events and family news in her persona, so it now produces views she never actually expressed -- for instance, a reaction to their daughter's choice of career.

Their exchange went roughly as follows. Leila said the update crossed a line: replaying a person's actual words is one thing, but generating novel opinions puts words in a dead person's mouth, and that misrepresents her intellectual agency even if nobody is deceived. Daniel replied that the line is illusory, since even the old version generated novel sentences -- any answer to a question she was never literally asked is new output -- so Leila's objection condemns the entire service, not just the update. Leila answered that there is still a threshold: simulating how she would have phrased things she believed is reconstruction, while inventing positions she never held is ventriloquism, and the difference is one of kind, not degree. Daniel said his wife consented, which settles the matter. Leila responded that consenting to a vaguely marketed service is not consenting to be made a lifelong endorser of views you never considered, and that the daughter is now forming beliefs about what her mother thought based on fabricated output. Daniel's final move was that dead people cannot be wronged at all, so at most Leila has shown a risk to the living, which he accepts and manages.

Please give: (1) "Round-by-round verdict" -- about 200 words, saying who is right at each step of the exchange as actually made; (2) "Where each side overreaches" -- about 150 words; (3) "What both are missing" -- about 150 words; (4) "Bottom line" -- two or three sentences.
