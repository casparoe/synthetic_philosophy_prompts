So I've noticed a weird pattern in how people (including me) talk about hypothetical lives, and I want to know if philosophy has a name for it, because it keeps coming up. Here's the pattern: everyone is totally comfortable with counterfactuals like "if my family had emigrated when I was two, I'd have grown up speaking Portuguese and I'd be a completely different person, but still me." Radical changes to everything after birth — country, religion, personality, even major memories — and the person in the hypothetical still counts as you, just altered. But then someone says "imagine your parents had had a child a year later instead, from a different sperm and egg, and raised that kid exactly the way you were raised, same name, same room, same everything" — and suddenly everyone in the room goes "wait, that's not me, that's just some other kid." Same with identical twins: nobody thinks "I could have been my twin," even though the twin shares (roughly) the same genetic material and the same upbringing. And in the freeze-your-embryos scenario where the clinic thaws a different donor embryo by mistake and raises it in your place, people get genuinely squirmy about whether "you" would exist in that world at all.

So the pattern seems to be: everything after some specific early moment is treated as negotiable — you could survive any amount of it being different — but the moment itself, and the particular biological material involved in it, is treated as load-bearing for you being you at all. It's like there's an invisible line in each person's biography, and facts on one side are "things that happened to me" while facts on the other side are "things that make there be a me to happen to." I've asked around and someone said this is just the ship of Theseus, but that doesn't seem right, because nobody here is worried about gradual part replacement — a version of me with every post-birth fact changed is fine, no gradualness needed. Someone else said it's just about genetics, but the twin case kills that.

Is there an established name and literature for this specific asymmetry — the line at conception (or thereabouts) between contingent facts about a person and facts without which there'd be no such person — as opposed to general puzzles about persistence through change? If there is, I'd like more than the label: where do people who take it seriously disagree about where exactly the line falls (conception? which gametes? could it have been a different egg but same sperm?), and what are the strongest arguments that the line is just an intuition pump artifact rather than a real metaphysical boundary? If nothing established fits exactly, coin a term and give me the boundary cases. Also, please flag how confident you are in each part of the answer, and single out the one claim you're least sure about.
