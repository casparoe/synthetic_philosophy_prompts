So last night I got into this huge arguement with my friend Dana about how you should update your credences when you learn something conditional, and we talked in circles for like three hours. She's annoyingly consistent, so I want to reverse-engineer the rule she's actually running and figure out where it goes next. Her track record, from cases we've kicked around over the years:

- A card is drawn uniformly from the ace of spades, king of spades, ace of hearts, king of hearts. Told it's a spade, she says 1/2 it's an ace. Fine, whatever.
- A jellybean she's 30% on being red; she glimpses it in crappy lighting and goes to 70% red, scaling all the other colors down proportionally. Also fine.
- Here's where it gets spicy. Prior: 1/2 it rains tomorrow, and rain or no rain, the picnic was 50/50 to be cancelled. She then learns FOR CERTAIN that if it rains, the picnic will be cancelled. - Same setup, but she learns for certain that if it DOESN'T rain the picnic is cancelled. This time rain goes UP to 2/3. So she's not just biased against antecedents; she's eliminating worlds and renormalizing.

Now the case we actually fought about. A soldier is dropped into a conflict zone and knows only that she's in Red Army territory or Blue Army territory, 50/50, and that within each army's territory she's 50/50 between the headquarters company's area and the second company's area. Then she gets a reliable radio message: if you're in Red territory, there's a 3/4 chance you're in the HQ area. The message says nothing one way or the other about Blue territory. What should happen to her credence that she's in Red territory? (Apparently this is basically the Judy Benjamin problem, which I looked up after the fight, but you don't need to know the literature to answer -- everything about the setup is right here.)

My gut says it stays at 1/2: the message is entirely about what things look like inside Red territory, so how could it possibly be evidence about Red vs Blue? Dana insists her usual rule does deliver an answer here and it isn't 1/2. But when I pressed her, I honestly couldn't tell whether her rule is "extract the message's factual content and condition on it" (which, generalized from the picnic cases, seems to push Red DOWN toward 1/3, since Red-worlds away from HQ get comparatively ruled out) or "when you learn a constraint on a conditional probability, satisfy it with the smallest possible change to your priors" (which I think keeps Red at 1/2). Maybe those are secretly the same rule, maybe they diverge exactly here -- I genuinely can't tell.
