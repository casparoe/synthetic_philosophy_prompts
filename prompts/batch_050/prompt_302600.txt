I'm a journalist working on a piece about the many-worlds interpretation, and I keep hitting the same conversational dead end. Whenever I ask an Everett sympathiser how we should understand probability in a branching universe -- why we observe the frequencies the Born rule predicts, or why we should care about high-amplitude branches more than low-amplitude ones -- someone replies that the question dissolves because 'everything that can happen, does happen'. I'm fairly sure this sentence is doing more than one job across these conversations, but I can't pin down its distinct meanings. Could you separate the different precise claims it might be making -- for instance, a claim about which branches exist, a claim about every outcome being certain, a claim that there is no fact of the matter about which result one will observe, or merely a restatement of unitary determinism -- and for each reading, say whether it is trivially true on the Everett picture, substantively contentious, or actually false? Most importantly: which of these readings, if any, would actually license dismissing the probability question, and which readings are being smuggled in when the slogan is used that way?
