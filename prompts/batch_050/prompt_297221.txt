I'm in a second-year metaphysics module and we've just done personal identity and essentialism, and I keep getting tangled up on the origin question, so I want to work out what I actually think by going off my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts:

1. Could I have come from a totally different sperm and egg, say my parents conceiving a different child the month after I was conceived? No. That child would have been my sibling, not me.
2. Same egg, different sperm from the same dad, carrying almost the same genetic material? Still no, I don't think that would have been me either, even if the resulting person was basically indistinguishable from me.
3. My identical twin came from the very same zygote as me. He's obviously not me, so whatever makes me me can't just be which zygote I came from.
4. IVF case: two embryos created from the same batch, one implanted (me), one frozen forever. If the clinic had implanted the other one instead, I don't think I'd be here as that person. That would have been someone else and I'd simply never have existed.
5. Lab mix-up: the embryologist accidentally uses sperm from a different donor. The child born would definately not have been me, no matter how similar they turned out.
6. This is the one I keep flip-flopping on: same sperm, same egg, but conceived a year later than I actually was (suppose the gametes were somehow preserved). Part of me thinks yes, that's me, because it's the very same genetic starting point, and part of me thinks no, because everything about the life would be different.
7. Weirder one: could I have been born female? If a different sperm (X-carrying rather than Y) had fertilised the same egg, my gut says that girl wouldn't have been me -- which fits with verdict 2. But someone in my seminar said this shows my intuition in 2 is really about genetics, not about the identity of the gamete itself, and now I don't know what I think.

Is there one principle that captures all of these, or even most? I'd like it stated precisely, with which cases motivate which bit of it. I suspect 3 and 6 are in tension with the rest (if exact origin matters that much, twins are a problem; if it doesn't, why does the month-later child feel like a stranger?), so tell me honestly which verdict I'd have to drop for the cleanest principle, and which one you'd drop. Also say what your principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: if someone grew a clone from one of my skin cells in fifty years, would that person be me?

Please answer in British English.
