Write a specular poem about Buridan's ass -- the kind where the second half mirrors the first half line by line in reverse order -- but with one twist that does the philosophical work: the mirror has to fail at exactly one point. One line in the second half refuses to reflect its partner, and that line is the ass finally eating. The first half should be perfectly balanced -- identical bales, equal distance, equal hunger, every reason for the left bale answered by the same reason for the right -- so that the symmetry of the form is the paralysis itself. The break should feel arbitrary, and dont try to earn it with the content: if the cracking line feels motivated by something in its mirrored partner, the poem has failed, because the whole point is that nothing in the situation can determine where the symmetry breaks, only that it has to break somewhere or the ass starves. And get one thing right that most tellings get wrong: the irrational option was never picking a side, it was standing still. 16-20 lines. Someone who knows the puzzle should see why this particular form had to betray itself; someone who doesn't should still get a good poem about a donkey.
