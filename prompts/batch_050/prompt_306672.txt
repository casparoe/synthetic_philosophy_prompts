My buddy from grad school is officiating my wedding next month — he wrote his dissertation on Austin and performative utterances and has never fully recovered. He's asked for help with his officiant speech, about 3-4 minutes, for a mixed crowd (a few philosophy people, mostly normal relatives, my grandma). The problem is he's become genuinely fixated on the fact that a wedding ceremony is one long performative utterance: "I do" doesn't describe anything, it does something, and the whole marriage hinges on words being said in the right circumstances by the right people. He wants the speech to be about that — without it turning into a lecture, and without grandma feeling like she wandered into a seminar. Also he has a running real-life anxiety about Austinian misfires and infelicities (what if he's not actually licensed in this state? what if someone says the words wrong and the marriage secretly doesn't take?) and that should show up in the speech as a joke that builds and then resolves by the end, because at some point the words do actually have to work.
