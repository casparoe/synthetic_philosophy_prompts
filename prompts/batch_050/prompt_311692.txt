ok so i'm in a seminar on philosophical methodology this semester and we just finished the experimental philosophy unit and its honestly messing with my head a little. here's roughly where i've landed, and i want to know whether this package actually hangs together:

(1) Intuitions about cases are legit evidence for philosophical theories, roughly the way observations are evidence for scientific ones. If we give that up, thought experiments like Gettier's do no work and half of analytic philosophy is just vibes.

(2) The experimental philosophy results are real and they matter. When researchers give the Gödel/Schmidt story to American undergrads, most say the name still refers to the guy who actually discovered the incompleteness theorems, i.e. the causal-historical answer. But East Asian students are way more split, and a lot of them go for "whoever satisfies the description," i.e. the descriptivist answer. Similar variation has been claimed for Gettier-style cases across cultures and class backgrounds, though I know some of the later studies found more agreement there than the early ones did — the reference results seem to have held up better.

(3) None of this touches what I learned in phil of language last semester. Kripke's Gödel case basically refutes the descriptivist theory of names, full stop. It's not just that I happen to have the intuition — the argument works.

(4) No group's intuitions are privileged. Western analytic philosophers aren't the default humans, and it would be intellectually gross to say the East Asian subjects are simply wrong about what the name refers to in their own mouths.

(5) I don't buy the move where the challenge hits semantics and epistemology but moral philosophy is somehow fine. If intuitions are shaky evidence then they're shaky for trolley cases too.

(6) Still, some intuitions are clearly better than others. A philosopher who's spent years thinking about reference has better Gödel-case intuitions than a sophomore filling out a survey for course credit, same way a birder's sightings count for more than mine.

so — consistent or not? I keep having this nagging feeling that (3) is the odd one out but whenever I try to say exactly what contradicts it I lose the thread. If the set is inconsistent, walk me through the tensions in order of severity, and for each one tell me what I'd actually have to give up and what that costs. And if some of these only LOOK like they clash but are fine on closer inspection, tell me that too, because I might be panicking over nothing.
