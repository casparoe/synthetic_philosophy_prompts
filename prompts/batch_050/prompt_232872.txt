ok so i was arguing in a discord full of LW/SSC/EA people after someone brought up Parfit’s teletransporter again, and we invented a dumb-but-useful variant we called the Janus Protocol: at t0 you get a nondestructive high-fidelity scan; one copy, A, is beamed/assembled on Mars and starts living immediately; the Earth original, B, is put into dreamless cryo as a “seed” with a dead-man switch. If A is alive and psychologically normal after 30 days, B is painlessly dissolved as redundant. If A dies within 30 days, B is thawed with exactly the pre-scan snapshot (no interim memories) and told the Mars branch failed. Assume the copying reliability stats are public and identical for A and B.

Here’s roughly where I’ve landed, and I want a consistency audit rather than a bibliography: (1) what matters in survival is Relation R-ish psychological continuity/connectedness with the right kind of cause, not numerical identity or same atoms, so an ideal destructive teleporter with a verified good copy is not death. (2) In fission with two equal R-successors, both count; there’s no extra fact about which is “really me,” and it’s confused to model it as 50% I become one and 50% I become nobody. (3) For Janus, before t0 I should regard A and, conditionally, thawed-B as genuine continuations, so dissolving B after A is verified is not murdering me, just pruning a redundant branch. (4) Still, Janus is definately worth paying a big premium for over a simple destructive teleporter, because it keeps an insurance branch until the Mars copy proves viable. (5) If A is verified at day 30, dissolving B is no worse than never having created an extra copy; but if A dies at day 10 and B is thawed, B should not feel like a lucky backup — B is just me continuing after a branch got pruned. (6) I reject closest-continuer rules because whether I survive shouldn’t depend on what happens light-minutes away after the scan.

Please go through these pairwise-ish: which tensions are real, which only look real, rank the real ones by severity, and for each say the cheapest revision and what it costs. Don’t just say “read Parfit”; assume I know the standard moves and want to know exactly where this package leaks, especially whether my insurance intuition in (4) is secretly smuggling in the closest-continuer thing I claim to hate in (6), or whether branchy anticipation in (2) already licenses it without contradiction.
