I am a third-year philosophy student taking a seminar on paradoxes of truth, and I am preparing a two-page proposal for a twelve-week independent study. My half-formed idea is that describing Yablo’s paradox as a liar paradox without self-reference is potentially misleading, because self-reference may be only one of several relevant forms of dependence. I want to distinguish at least four cases: a Curry sentence that says, roughly, that if it is true then some arbitrary proposition follows; an indirect relay in which one sentence says that if a second sentence is true then that proposition follows, while the second says that the first is true; a finite Yablo-style sequence whose later sentences eventually terminate; and the infinite Yablo sequence, where each sentence says that every later sentence is untrue. These cases differ between direct self-reference, indirect circular dependence, dependence that is acyclic but not well-founded, and dependence that terminates.

Write the research proposal. It should formulate a precise and tractable version of the question: which forms of dependence, in combination with which principles governing truth and implication, are sufficient to generate contradiction or triviality, and does self-reference explain anything once those forms have been separated? Explain why the familiar diagnoses—restricting the truth schema, limiting structural rules such as contraction, or excluding ungrounded sentences—do not obviously classify all four cases in the same way. The proposal should state the main competing hypotheses without presenting the project as already having established one of them.

Describe the actual work to be done week by week: formalizing the four systems, representing their dependence relations graphically, testing finite truncations against the infinite sequence, and examining one classical treatment and one treatment that restricts contraction or another relevant inference rule. Keep the scope realistic for one semester; this should not become a general history of semantic paradox. Say what a successful result would look like, including a taxonomy or criterion that makes different predictions from simply labeling constructions self-referential or non-self-referential. Also specify what would count as failure—for example, if the four cases resist uniform formalization or if the taxonomy merely redescribes the standard distinction.

The proposal may name the relevant positions and technical notions, but it should not include a bibliography, literature review, or citations; I will add those later.
