So here's my situation. I work as an analyst in the policy unit of a mid-sized country (population somewhere in the 10–20 million range, don't want to doxx myself more precisely than that lol). A few months ago the annual "happiest countries" report came out — you know the one, where they basically rank countries by asking people how they evaluate their lives on a 0-to-10 ladder scale. We came in around 14th. The PM saw this, got genuinely kind of obsessed with it, and has now tasked our unit with drawing up a plan to get the country into the top 5 "within ten years, ideally sooner."

Here's my problem. My boss is thrilled and wants me to just start listing policies that "move the number." But the more I think about it, the weirder the whole assignment feels, and I can't fully articulate why. Some of my unease, in no particular order:

- The thing being measured is people's answer to a survey question about how they evaluate their life, which seems like... maybe not the same thing as people actually being happy day to day? But maybe it's close enough that this doesn't matter?
- Even if we do everything right, the countries above us are also trying to improve, so "top 5" is partly out of our control. Feels like promising to win a race where the other runners also get to train.
- I worry that once the ranking becomes the explicit target, we'll start doing stuff that looks good on the metric (or on the inputs people believe feed the metric) rather than stuff that actually makes citizens' lives better. But I can't tell if that's a real worry or me being paranoid.
- Also, honestly: suppose we COULD reliably raise the national average. Is the average even the right thing to maximize? What about the miserable bottom 5%?

Pretend you're a philosopher of social science who has also spent time working inside government — so you know both the conceptual issues around measuring wellbeing AND the practical realities of how targets distort bureaucracies. Think carefully and brainstorm a bit before settling on your recommendation. What should I actually tell my boss? Should I push back on the whole "top 5" framing, and if so, what's the strongest version of the case for keeping it?
