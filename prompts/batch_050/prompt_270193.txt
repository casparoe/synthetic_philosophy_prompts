A colleague of mine teaches epistemology and has, over the years, given consistent verdicts on the standard parade of cases: the driver who reads the correct time off a stopped clock does not know the time; the motorist in fake-barn country who happens to look at the one real barn does not know there is a barn; the same motorist in ordinary countryside does; the person who believes their ticket will lose a fair million-ticket lottery purely on the odds does not know it will lose; and the person whose true belief is inferred through a false premise that luckily compensates does not know. Now I want to predict her verdict on a case she has never considered: a hospital porter sends a sample down a pneumatic tube system that is in fact extremely reliable, and believes it has reached the lab; had it jammed en route, no alert would have been triggered and she would have believed exactly the same thing. Tell me which candidate rules unify her earlier verdicts — I take it these include at least a sensitivity-style condition, a safety-style condition, and perhaps something about false lemmas or defeaters, though I'd like you to say which actually survive the track record — what each surviving rule predicts about the porter, your best guess at her verdict with reasons, which earlier case is the closest analog and why, and, if more than one rule survives her record intact, the further case you would put to her to force those rules apart.
