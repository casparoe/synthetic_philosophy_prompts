I'm a phd student in philosophy of cognitive science, and my reading group this semester has one die-hard enactivist/embodied cognition person and one old-school computational representationalist, and they have spent literally eight weeks talking past each other. For the last meeting I want to hand them the document they *should* have produced if they'd run this as an actual adversarial collaboration instead of a recurring fight.

So: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed enactivist (sensorimotor/contingency-flavored, affordances and all) and a committed representationalist-computationalist. I want:

1. A numbered list of claims both can actually sign. Stuff like: perception is active and not a passive snapshot of the world, cognition is at least sometimes scaffolded on the body and environment, early GOFAI-style disembodied models failed for instructive reasons. Push on what's genuinely common ground vs. what only sounds like it.

2. The exact disagreements, each phrased so the side it describes would accept the wording. I suspect the big one is whether explaining cognition requires positing internal content-bearing states, or whether "representation" talk there is doing real explanatory work or is just a gloss on skilled engagement -- but get their actual positions right, not strawmen.

3. The cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case or finding both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Some candidates: offline cognition (dreaming, imagination, planning for absent states of affairs) -- can enactivism scale to it without smuggling representations back in; whether decodable content in neural activity counts as representation in the philosophically relevant sense; abstract thought like math.

4. Where you think part of the dispute is verbal (e.g., they mean different things by "representation"), say so and say which part.

Format: agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table. And if a moderate position (action-oriented representations, predictive processing style) would make both of them roll their eyes for different reasons, note that too.
