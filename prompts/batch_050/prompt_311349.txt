The exam question:

In The Puzzle of the Self-Torturer, Warren Quinn describes a man fitted with a device with settings numbered 0 to 1,000. Each click upwards increases his pain by an amount too small for him to notice, and in return he is paid $10,000. At setting 0 he feels nothing; at setting 1,000 the pain is excruciating, and he would give up every penny to be back at 0. (a) Explain why the self-torturer's preferences appear to be intransitive even though every individual step looks worth taking. (b) Set out Quinn's own diagnosis: what does he think has gone wrong, and what does he conclude the agent has reason to do? (c) Is this just a sorites paradox about the word "painful"? Why or why not?

The student's answer:

(a) For any setting n, the agent prefers n+1 to n, because the extra money clearly outweighs a pain increase he cannot even feel. If his preferences were transitive, this chain would force him to prefer 1,000 to 0; but in fact he strongly prefers 0 to 1,000, since the agony is not worth any amount of money. So his preferences form a cycle, even though each pairwise preference seems reasonable on its own. The puzzle is that irrationality seems to emerge from a series of steps each of which looked rational at the time.

(b) Quinn's diagnosis is that the agent's preferences are irrational, because rational preference has to be transitive. The lesson of the puzzle, on his view, is that the self-torturer ought to revise his preferences to restore consistency -- for instance by ceasing to prefer the next setting once the cumulative pain starts to matter. Note that he also cannot simply reverse back down the dial, since each step down costs him $10,000 in exchange for relief he cannot feel.

(c) Yes, essentially. "Excruciating" and "bearable" are vague predicates, and there is no sharp line anywhere in the sequence at which the pain suddenly becomes too much. Quinn's puzzle is therefore best understood as a version of the sorites: the agent is led into trouble by the vagueness of "painful", just as someone removing grains one at a time is led to deny that a heap is a heap.
