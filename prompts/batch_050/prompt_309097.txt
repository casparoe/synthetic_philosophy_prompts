I've been reading about the Mīmāṃsā school in an intro course on Indian epistemology and there's a move I keep going back and forth on. As I understand it, the Mīmāṃsakas hold that cognitions are valid just in virtue of arising (svataḥ prāmāṇya), and that invalidity only ever comes from outside — some defect you discover later, like finding out the lighting was bad or you were drunk. Then they combine this with the claim that the Veda has no author at all, human or divine, and the motivation seems to be roughly: any testimony produced by an author inherits the author's possible flaws, so if the Veda is going to be a rock-solid source on dharma, which isn't accessible to perception or inference anyway, it better not have an author whose reliability you'd have to vouch for.

Here's where I get stuck. If validity is intrinsic to begin with — if every cognition and every piece of testimony comes presumptively valid until defeated — then what extra work is authorlessness actually doing? Ordinary human testimony is presumptively valid on their view too, yet they clearly think it's shakier than Vedic testimony. And from the other direction, their opponents (the Buddhists especially, as I understand it) push back by pointing at cases where an authorless-looking text is still garbage: nobody authored the pattern of cracks on a tortoise shell, but that doesn't make it a source of knowledge. So the whole dialectic seems to turn on something subtle about where the presumption of validity comes from and what can undercut it.

Please explain the conceptual structure here: what the intrinsic validity thesis really commits you to, why authorlessness is supposed to help even given that thesis, and what the strongest pressure point in the whole setup is. I don't want a neutral survey of pros and cons — take a definite stand on whether the authorlessness move genuinely secures anything that intrinsic validity alone doesn't, and give your reasons for that verdict. Also, plain flowing prose only please, no headings or bullet points or bold text, just write it like you'd explain it to me in an email.
