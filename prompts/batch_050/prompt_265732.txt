I'm writing the proposal for my philosophy honors thesis on truth, and my advisor wants a one-page statement of my position before we meet thursday. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a deflationist: the meaning of the truth predicate is exhausted by the T-schema ("p" is true iff p), and there's no deeper nature of truth for a theory to uncover.
(2) Classical logic stays as is -- no paracomplete or paraconsistent revisions. The Liar should be handled by treating it as ungrounded, so it simply doesn't get a truth value, while the rest of the T-schema's instances stand.
(3) Truth is the constitutive aim of belief: that alone explains what's defective about believing falsehoods, and truth, not mere empirical adequacy, is what science ultimately aims at.
(4) The practical usefulness of truth talk is fully explained by its role as a device of generalization and endorsement ("everything the witness said is true").
(5) The predictive success of our best science is best explained by its theories being approximately true rather than merely empirically adequate.

Does this package actually hang together? I suspect at least one pair pulls against each other but i can't see where.
