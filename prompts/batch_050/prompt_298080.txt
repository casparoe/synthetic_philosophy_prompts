I'm taking an upper-level seminar on epistemic norms in the philosophy of language, and for our final paper we have to stake out and defend a position on the norm of assertion -- the rule that governs when it's epistemically proper to flat-out say something. Second, I think that in the classic fake barn case, where Henry looks at the one real barn in a district full of barn facades and says "that's a barn," his assertion is fully epistemically warranted and no criticism of him is in order. Fifth, I think Moorean assertions of the form "p, but I don't believe that p" express consistent propositions yet are defective to assert. Sixth, I think any adequate account of the norm has to explain our practices of challenge and repair -- why "how do you know that?" is always a legitimate response to an assertion, and why discovering that what you asserted is false obligates you to retract it.
