Say you're mediating an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the philosophy of AI risk. Camp A defends the orthogonality thesis: an agent's intelligence and its final goals can in principle vary independantly of each other, so a superintelligent paperclip maximizer is a live possibility rather than a conceptual confusion. Camp B thinks this misses something deep: they hold that past some threshold of general intelligence, certain values become attractors — a smart enough agent will reason its way toward curiosity, cooperation, or valuing truth for their own sake — and that what gets called instrumental convergence quietly bleeds into convergence on final ends.

Draft the actual joint statement both camps could sign after hashing this out in good faith. Concretely I want:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. Not vacuous ones — things both sides can sign that actually constrain the debate, like what counts as a "final goal" versus an instrumental one, or what the paperclip thought experiment is and isn't supposed to show.
2. A disagreements table where each residual disagreement is stated precisely, with each camp's position phrased so that camp would accept the wording as its own. If it turns out part of the disagreement is really verbal — e.g., Camp A is making a claim about what's possible in principle while Camp B is making a claim about what would actually happen under realistic selection pressures — say which part, and whether dissolving it leaves anything standing.
3. A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, give the test, observation, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance. At least one crux has to concern what we'd expect to observe empirically as AI systems get more capable — for instance, whether capability gains and shifts in apparent goal-content come apart in real systems, and what pattern would vindicate which camp.

The hard part is the drafting, so be careful: every formulation should be neither slanted nor so hedged it's empty. Then, at the end, step out of the mediator role and tell me how confident you are that each camp would actually sign this thing, and flag the single sentence you're least sure about — the one where you suspect you smoothed over something that would blow up in the room.
