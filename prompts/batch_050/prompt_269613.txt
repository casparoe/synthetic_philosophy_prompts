Hi! So for my EPQ (extended project thing) I've been reading about this view called tranquilism, which I found in an essay by Lukas Gloor. The rough idea is that pleasure never actually adds value to the world — nothing is good in itself — and all that matters is the absence of craving, inner restlessness or suffering. So a perfectly content state and complete unconsciousness are supposedly equally fine, and a life full of great pleasures but constant wanting is worse than it looks.

Here's what I want. Build me a thought experiment where tranquilism, bog-standard negative utilitarianism (suffering is bad, pleasure is worthless or at least doesn't count), and plain hedonic utilitarianism (pleasure good, pain bad, add it all up) each give a DIFFERENT verdict about the same choice — so three theories, three different answers. Some rules:

- It has to be a realistic, everyday sort of choice — like deciding whether to have a kid, take a stressful job, move abroad, whatever. No experience machines, no utility monsters, no perfect predictors or mad scientists, because that stuff makes my teacher switch off.
- Keep the actual scenario to one shortish paragraph.
- The three verdicts have to genuinely fall out of the theories — don't just stipulate "the negative utilitarian would say X", show me the working.
- After the scenario, walk through each theory's verdict and why, then tell me which specific feature of the case is doing all the work, and what happens if you take that feature out — do two of the theories collapse back into agreement?
- Finish with the best objection a hedonic utilitarian could make that the case is rigged against them, and whether you think it lands.

Also, if it turns out two of these three views can never actually come apart on any realistic case (I have a suspicion negative utilitarianism and tranquilism might secretly agree on loads of things, but maybe theres a difference around mild cravings that don't quite count as suffering?), don't force it — just tell me that and explain why, and then do the best two-way split you can instead.
