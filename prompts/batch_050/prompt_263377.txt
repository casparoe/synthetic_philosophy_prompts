I have no academic background in mathematics or philosophy, but I have spent some months thinking on my own about a question I keep circling back to: could there be a rational rule that converts a bare statement of one's ignorance into numerical probabilities -- a rule that says, given only this problem description and nothing else, here is the distribution you must assign? Before I read any more attempts at such a rule, I want the specification first. Write the list of desiderata that any adequate rule of this kind would have to meet -- where "adequate" means adequate to serve as a candidate requirement of rationality, not merely as a practical heuristic -- with the case or consideration motivating each desideratum, and mark which you take to be non-negotiable and which are the preferences of particular camps.

These are the cases driving my thinking; the list should be answerable to all of them.

First, the cube factory. A factory produces cubes with edge length somewhere between 0 and 1 cm, and that is all I am told. Asked the probability that the edge is under half a centimetre: if I treat the edge as uniformly distributed I get 1/2; if I treat the face area as uniform I get 1/4; if the volume, 1/8. One problem, three answers, and I cannot find anything in the statement that privileges one description.

Second, the mixture. I know only that the wine-to-water ratio in a bottle lies between 1/3 and 3. Uniformity over that ratio and uniformity over the water-to-wine ratio disagree about the probabilities, yet the two descriptions seem entirely on a par -- neither is more natural than the other.

Third, the lighthouse. A lighthouse stands at an unknown position along a straight coastline, and its lamp rotates at a constant rate. I record where the beam crosses the shore line. Here, it seems to me, the symmetry of the setup genuinely picks out one distribution: uniform over the angle, because a steadily rotating lamp has no preferred orientation, and if my algebra is right this forces a particular heavy-tailed law (the Cauchy distribution, if I have identified it correctly) for where the beam lands. So this problem, unlike the first two, appears to answer itself -- unless I have smuggled in an assumption without noticing.

Fourth, a difficulty of my own devising that I have not seen discussed. Suppose I face three mutually exclusive outcomes and know nothing else; a natural rule assigns 1/3 to each. Now suppose I am instead told only that either outcome A will occur or one of B and C will, with no further detail -- a coarse-grained redescription of the same situation. The same rule now assigns 1/2 to A. So the rule's verdict on A changes with the grain of the description, even though nothing in my information has changed. Call what I want "coarse-graining consistency": the probabilities a rule assigns to a refined partition, when summed, should agree with what it assigns to the coarse problem directly.

With those four cases on the table: what exactly should the desiderata be? Candidates I can gesturingly see -- invariance under reparameterization, respect for the physical symmetries of the setup, agreement with long-run frequencies when the problem is instantiated physically, coarse-graining consistency as I defined it, never assigning 0 or 1 to a possibility the statement leaves open -- but I want them stated properly, each with its motivating case, and I particularly want the following three things. One: I suspect that full invariance under every reparameterization of a continuous quantity is not merely hard but impossible, and if so I want to see the impossibility stated exactly, plus what weaker forms survive. Two: is the desideratum "the answer is fixed by the problem statement alone" compatible with "respect the symmetries of the physical setup," given that a bare statement may underdescribe the setup? Three: which single desideratum eliminates the most proposed rules?

A brief verdict on whether my lighthouse case really does answer itself would also be welcome, as would a closing paragraph -- no more -- on how the main contenders you know of fare against the finished list. But the list itself is the deliverable.
