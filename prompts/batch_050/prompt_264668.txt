Is there a name for things you can count but can't single out? I'll explain what I mean with three cases, because I keep tripping over this. (1) My flatmate washed my coffee mug and his identical one together and now neither of us knows which is which -- but it still feels like there IS an answer, we've just lost track of it. (2) Now imagine a universe containing nothing but two perfectly identical metal spheres, two feet apart. There seem to be two of them, not one. But anything I say about one ("the one on the left", "the one sitting two feet from a sphere") is equally true of the other, so I can apparently count them without being able to say which is which, even in principle. (3) And then I read that with electrons, swapping two of them supposedly makes no difference at all, and physicists actually do the sums differently because of it.

What I want to know: does the middle situation -- genuinely many things, yet nothing picks out any one of them -- have an established name, and is it really the same situation as that old thought experiment with the two spheres, or as the old principle that two things sharing literally every property must be one thing? I suspect case (1) is a different beast entirely (lost fact vs no fact?) but I can't articulate why. Don't just throw labels at me -- tell me what the phenomenon is and isn't, where its edges are, and coin a term yourself if nothing existing quite fits. Also say where your reasoning is most likely to break down or what consideration would change your verdict. British English please, and keep it under 400 words, verdict up front.
