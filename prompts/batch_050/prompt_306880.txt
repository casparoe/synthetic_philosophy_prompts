Something I keep noticing in real life and I can't find a word for it. Last month my friend Ines talked her way out of a parking fine basically because the attendant believed her story about the meter being broken, and the reason he believed her is that Ines is famously incapable of lying -- goes red, can't make eye contact, the whole performance. Everyone who knows her knows this, and wierdly it's worth a lot to her: people lend her things, take her side in disputes, trust her with money they wouldn't trust anyone else with. If she could lie smoothly she'd actually be worse off, because then nobody could tell when she was being sincere. Second case: a guy at my running club who is known to completely lose it over any slight and has done for years. Nobody ever crosses him and he almost never has to actually fight anyone, because the threat is never in doubt. Me and a friend were arguing about whether he's better off than a calm person would be, and I think he might be -- if he were capable of calmly weighing up in the moment whether escalation was worth it, people would test him sometimes. His inability to back down does his deterring for him.

So the general pattern seems to be: a person is better off precisely because they can't, or reliably won't, do the thing that would be smart in the moment, and this only pays because other people can somehow tell. It's not just stubbornness, and I don't think it's exactly reputation either, because the payoff seems to depend on the trait being unfakeable -- if it could be performed on command it wouldn't work. I half-remember a podcast about some thought experiment involving being stranded in the desert and a driver who could somehow detect whether you were sincere about paying him later, which feels related but I can't reconstruct the argument. So: does philosophy or game theory have a proper name for this pattern? If there are several nearby concepts rather than one exact one, tell me what they are, how the thing I'm describing differs from each (e.g. is it just deterrence? is it just signalling?), and which fits best. And if nothing established really fits, coin a term, define it properly, and give me some borderline cases so I can see where it stops applying -- a bare label on its own won't help me.
