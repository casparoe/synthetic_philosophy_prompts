ok so I've been chewing on Dennett's Where Am I? again — brain in a vat back at the lab, body sent off to handle the dangerous job, the two linked by radio — and I've landed on a set of positions that each feel right to me, but I want someone to actually stress-test whether they fit together before I build anything on top of them. Here they are:

1. Where I am is where my brain is. In the original setup I'm in the vat, full stop. The overwhelming sense of being "here, behind these eyes" out on the mission is just a fact about where the cameras and microphones happen to be mounted. A drone pilot isn't inside the drone.

2. What makes a future person *me* is the pattern — memories, dispositions, the whole psychological program — not the particular meat it runs on. So a sufficiently detailed computer emulation of my brain wouldn't be a copy of me, it would just be me on different hardware. (This also seems required to take the computer-duplicate part of the story seriously at all.)

3. Take the case where the biological brain and the emulation run in lockstep and the experimenters flip a switch deciding which one actually drives the body. I say there's a definite fact, after each flip, about whether I'm still the one driving. "Both" or "there's no fact of the matter" are cop-outs — someone is driving, and it's either me or it isn't.

4. Parfit-style teletransportation — scan, destroy, rebuild on Mars out of fresh atoms — is death. I would not step into the booth, and I'd treat whoever steps out as an imposter with my memories.

5. Weirdly, when I imagine the radio link failing mid-mission — the body crumpling out there in the field, the brain sitting perfectly safe in its vat — my gut verdict flips: it feels like the disaster happens to *me*, out there, and the thing in the lab is just hardware that survived. I don't know whether that flip tells me anything about 1 or whether its just my imagination being sloppy.

So: does this set hang together? I want an audit, not a survey of the literature on personal identity. For each tension you find, tell me how serious it is relative to the others, which specific commitment has to give, and what giving it up costs. And please actually commit — if one of these is the weak link, name it and defend the choice rather than handing me a menu of consistent subsets and letting me pick. I've deliberately written these down without deciding which one I'd sacrifice first, and I'd like to keep it that way until someone shows me where the real pressure is.
