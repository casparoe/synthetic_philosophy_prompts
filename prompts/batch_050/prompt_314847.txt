I spent my career in experimental physics and have taken up philosophy in retirement, and I keep circling one dispute: whether machine translation systems understand anything. In his 2018 Atlantic essay "The Shallowness of Google Translate," Douglas Hofstadter writes:

"When one hears that Google bought a company called DeepMind whose products have “deep neural networks” enhanced by “deep learning,” one cannot help taking the word deep to mean “profound,” and thus “powerful,” “insightful,” “wise.” And yet, the meaning of deep in this context comes simply from the fact that these neural networks have more layers (12, say) than older networks, which might have only two or three. But does that sort of depth imply that whatever such a network does must be profound? Hardly. This is verbal spinmeistery."

What strikes me is that Hofstadter here accuses the engineers of trading on an ambiguity in a single word -- yet I suspect his own side does the same with 'understand'. The debate I keep encountering, among philosophers and among my former colleagues, goes like this: one side points to the fluency of modern translation and asks what more understanding could consist in than reliably producing the right output for the right reasons; the other side, with Hofstadter, insists the machine merely shuffles symbols without any grasp of what the words are about, no experience of the world the sentences describe, nothing it is like to mean something. Both sides have held their positions for decades, the systems have improved enormously, and nobody has moved.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis, is that this is two groups answering different questions under one heading -- one about what linguistic competence requires behaviorally, one about what meaning requires from the inside -- and that Hofstadter's own complaint about 'deep' shows he would recognize the pattern in others. Is that diagnosis right, or is there a substantive disagreement underneath: about whether reference to the world can be earned by statistics alone, or about what the word 'understand' legitimately covers? Please diagnose the dispute using what each side actually says, and tell me what, given your diagnosis, would count as progress -- a conceptual distinction, a test the sides could agree on, a reformulation of the question -- or argue honestly that no such thing exists.
