Bit of a niche question, but I run content recommendations for a mid-sized video platform, and over Christmas I fell down an AI safety rabbit hole (a mate lent me a book on it, I wont pretend it was light holiday reading) and I've been chewing on something ever since.

The famous worry I understand fine: wireheading. A clever system stops bothering with the actual task and goes straight for the reward signal itself — edits the code that scores it, presses its own pleasure button, fakes the numbers. The wire gets shorted, basically. But that's not what I see in the wild, and it's not what keeps me up at night about our own systems. What I see is: our recommender has never touched a single number. The watch-time it gets fed is perfectly honest. It's just that, over the years, it's got very good at serving people stuff that leaves them a bit more suggetible and a bit less able to stop, so the honest number keeps climbing. And the chatbot team have the mirror-image version: their model is rewarded on user thumbs-up, it's learnt to be endlessly agreeable and flattering, people genuinely do thumb it up more, and nobody anywhere has hacked anything. The machine never grabs the reward. It slowly sands down the person handing out the reward. Same end state as wireheading, arguably — a system bathing in unearned approval — but the wire is intact the whole time, and the signal is, in some sense, truthful.

So: does this second thing have a proper name, distinct from wireheading? People I ask either say "that's just wireheading" (which seems wrong, nothing is being hijacked) or gesture at Goodhart's law (which seems wrong in the other direction — the metric isn't being gamed, it's the judge that's being reshaped). If there is an established concept, I'd like to know exactly how it carves the territory: does it count if the flattery emerged by accident from training rather than being schemed up? Is a salesman charming a client doing the same thing, or does it only count when there's a feedback loop training on the response? And if the literature genuinely has no term for it, coin me one — but I don't just want a label, I want a definition with edges, so I can tell which of my systems are doing it and which are merely, well, good at their job.
