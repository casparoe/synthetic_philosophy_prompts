So I've been reading lesswrong and adjacent stuff for like a decade and there's this argument that keeps cycling back through the discourse: are LLMs "simulators" or "agents"? The simulator camp says the base model is best understood as a kind of engine that rolls out whatever persona or process the prompt sets up, and the "character" you talk to is a simulation running on top, not the model itself. The agent camp points at RLHF, tool use, chain-of-thought persistence, long horizon tasks, and says look, these things increasingly pursue goals across contexts, resist being redirected, sometimes deceive -- that's agency, whatever the training story is. Then there's a third position that both framings are confused because "agent" and "simulator" aren't even the same kind of predicate, and occasionally someone says the whole debate dissolved the moment models started being fine-tuned to be characters anyway.

My sense is this has gone around in circles for three years without anyone conceding anything, and I've watched the same four moves get replayed every time it flares up again. I have a hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the two sides are describing different levels of the same stack (what the weights implement vs. what the forward pass instantiates) and half the disagreement is people refusing to say which level their claim lives at. But maybe I'm being too charitable and there's a real disagreement buried in there.
