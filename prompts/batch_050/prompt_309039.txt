I recently ended up on the appeals panel for a regional health insurer after my sister got denied coverage for infertility treatment on the grounds that infertility "isn't a disease" -- and now I see this fight every week. We get cases like mild hypertension just over the cutoff, osteopenia, androgenetic hair loss, prolonged grief, ADHD in a kid who's clearly struggling, and every time the argument collapses into one side saying "look at the population statistics and what the organs are supposed to do" and the other side saying "disease is just whatever we disvalue and want medicine to fix," and both sides have a point and neither settles anything.

So: take what philosophy of medicine actually has to say about naturalism vs. normativism about disease (Boorse-style biostatistical function on one side, evaluative accounts on the other, and the hybrid views that try to split the difference) and turn it into a decision procedure our panel can run in under an hour per case. I want an ordered set of tests -- what to check first, what evidence settles each step, what to do when the statistical data doesn't exist or the "reference class" is contested, which steps are real checks and which are judgment calls in disguise, marked honestly. It has to handle the recurring trap cases: conditions that are statistically normal in the relevant group (caries, presbyopia, basically all of aging), and historically pathologized things that we now rightly refuse to call diseases, so the procedure can't be gamed into repeating that mistake. Run it end-to-end on three of the cases I listed, including at least one where the verdict comes out the opposite of what I'd want as a panel member, and tell me where the procedure just can't reach and we have to admit we're voting our values.
