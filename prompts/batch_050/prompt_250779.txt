Got into it at an ML reading group last week. Someone asked whether the success of deep nets says anything about Hume's problem of induction -- like, gradient descent just *works*, across domains nobody designed it for, so doesn't that dissolve the worry that induction lacks justification? A third person shut it down with "there's no free lunch," and everyone nodded, and I still can't figure out what claim was actually being made.

So: take "there's no free lunch" as it gets used between ML and philosophy and lay out the menu of precise theses it could be expressing. Off the top of my head I can see at least candidates like: averaged over all possible data-generating distributions, every learning algorithm does equally well; any algorithm that outperforms on some class of problems must underperform on others, so good performance is evidence about the world rather than about the algorithm; induction can never be justified non-circularly, period; and the weaker practical version, don't expect a generic method to beat one that exploits the structure of your problem. Those can't all be the same claim, and I suspect they differ in strength wildly.

For each reading: is it a platitude, a substantive theorem, a substantive but empirically loaded claim, or just false? Be careful about the gap between what the formal no-free-lunch results actually assume (uniform averaging over *all* possible worlds, which is a crazy prior nobody holds) and what people casually infer from them. Also, does any reading survive contact with the fact that the universe we live in apparently has exploitable regularities everywhere -- is that just luck, or does the slogan have nothing to say about it?

Then the payoff: which of these readings, if any, actually supports the move my reading-group guy made, i.e. dismissing "why does induction work in our universe?" as a confused question? My guess is the theorem-style readings don't touch Hume at all and the Hume-style reading makes the dismissal circular, but maybe I'm missing a middle reading. Tell me which one the nodding crowd most likely meant, and whether it's the one that would have to be true for the dismissal to go through.
