I sit on a citizens' advisory commission that's been asked to recommend prostitution law reform to our national government. Mid-sized country, about 10 million people. Two of our neighbors have very different regimes (one criminalizes purchase Nordic-style, one has German-style legalization with licensed brothels) and we've gotten spillover effects from both, which is partly why this landed on our desk. The government has committed to doing *something*, so "keep the status quo" isn't on the table. Our chair wants a decision memo she can circulate before next month's vote.

Please draft that memo. It should recommend one of three baselines -- Nordic-style asymmetric criminalization, German/Dutch-style legalization and licensing, or New Zealand-style decriminalization -- or a hybrid, but if you go hybrid you have to say exactly which elements come from where, not just gesture at "a middle path." Treat the models as ideal types rather than getting bogged down in the actual statutes of those countries.

The hard part, and the thing I actually want the memo to wrestle with: I don't think any of the three models was designed for how our market works now. Roughly two complications. First, a big chunk of the market has moved to online platforms, which aren't third parties in the classic pimp/brothel-operator sense, and none of the models has a clear answer for whether platforms count as exploiters, facilitators, or neutral infrastructure. Second, our police currently use solicitation laws mostly to clear street markets, and the sex worker organizations we've consulted argue that *any* criminalization of purchase pushes the work into worse conditions even when sellers technically aren't prosecuted -- while the anti-trafficking NGOs argue the exact opposite about what demand-side criminalization does. Both sides waved studies at us. The memo can't resolve that empirically, so it has to say what the decision should turn on instead.

Structure the memo around: (1) the recommendation with concrete instruments (who if anyone gets criminalized, licensing/zoning if any, how platforms are treated, funding for exit services, a review clause), (2) the criteria you weighted and why -- harm/violence, autonomy, exploitation, trafficking, what the law expresses -- including which criterion you weighted most and what the honest cost of doing that is, (3) the strongest argument for the model you didn't pick, stated as well as its defenders would state it, and (4) what evidence over a 3-year review period would make the commission reverse course.

Around 1,200 words, American English please. Don't pad it with a survey of the debate -- I need something the commission can actually vote on, and the chair hates throat-clearing.
