In my epistemology seminar we were going over Cartesian skepticism and someone cut the whole discussion short by doing the Moore thing -- held up a hand and said 'here is one hand, so there's an external world, skepticism refuted.' Half the room groaned, half nodded along. Since then I've heard 'here is one hand' used for what seem like totally different moves: as an actual deductive proof with premises, as a claim that some things are just more certain than any skeptical premise could ever be, and as a way of saying the skeptical question is somehow illegitimate or confused. Can you seperate out the distinct precise claims this one gesture-plus-sentence could be making -- including readings where it's a genuine argument and readings where it's deliberately not trying to be one -- and for each say whether it's trivial, defensible but contested, or just question-begging? And which reading, if any, would actually justify using it to end the seminar discussion rather than start one?
