I am supervising a one-semester undergraduate thesis in practical rationality. Write a short research proposal for a 6,000-word thesis addressing this question: does Parfit’s hitchhiker case establish that it can be rational to cultivate an irrational disposition, or does it merely reveal an ambiguity in the object being evaluated—individual choice, standing disposition, policy, or character? The proposal should develop a less familiar angle by introducing a transparency threshold: the degree to which a benefactor can reliably detect someone’s repayment disposition before providing a benefit. Compare cases in which detection is perfect, merely probabilistic, based on general reputation rather than the present promise, and deliberately produced through costly habit-formation. Explain why existing appeals to resolute choice, sophisticated choice, and the instrumental value of trustworthy dispositions leave the central question unresolved, and describe the framework the thesis would use to make progress. Consider at least three possible conclusions, including the view that “rational irrationality” is only a verbal paradox arising from shifting the unit of evaluation, and specify what considerations would decide among them. The proposal should state the research question precisely, defend the chosen approach against obvious alternatives, identify the most serious objection, and give a realistic chapter-by-chapter plan for one semester. It should also explain what would count as failure—for example, if the transparency threshold makes no difference to the rational assessment. Include no bibliography or formal citations; the student will supply those later.
