I serve on the ethics advisory panel of an NHS hospital trust, and we have been asked to advise on a contested discharge case. Mrs A, an 84-year-old patient, is medically fit to leave hospital but needs daily help with meals, medication, and mobility. She has no savings for private care, and local authority support will take several weeks to arrange. Her only child, a son in his fifties, has told the discharge team that he will not take her into his home or act as her carer. He does not dispute that she sacrificed a great deal to raise him, but describes their relationship as having been cold and dutiful for decades, and says he has not chosen to continue it. Several members of the ward staff consider his refusal a straightforward failure of filial obligation: he owes her repayment for the sacrifices she made. Our panel's chair has suggested that the case turns on which model of filial duty is correct — the 'debts of gratitude' model implicit in the staff's reaction, or something closer to Jane English's proposal that what grown children owe their parents is what friends owe one another, grounded in an ongoing relationship of mutuality rather than in repayment for past sacrifices.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate to the panel before our next meeting. It should recommend a concrete position for the panel to adopt on how the discharge team should frame the son's role — for example, whether he may properly be pressed to provide care, whether his refusal should be treated as one legitimate option among others, and whether the team should pursue interim arrangements rather than continuing to negotiate with him — together with the reasoning for that recommendation. The opinion should engage seriously with the friendship model of filial obligation, including whether it genuinely lets the son off the hook (given that even friendships generate obligations when a friend is in dire need and one is uniquely placed to help), and it should give an honest account of the strongest consideration favouring the staff's view rather than simply dismissing the debts-of-gratitude picture.

After the opinion itself, please append a structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields: "recommendation" (the panel's recommended position, stated in one or two sentences); "rationale" (a list of the two or three principal reasons supporting the recommendation); "strongest_counterconsideration" (the best point available to the losing side); "response_to_counterconsideration" (how the opinion answers that point); and "practical_next_steps" (a list of the concrete actions the discharge team should take).
