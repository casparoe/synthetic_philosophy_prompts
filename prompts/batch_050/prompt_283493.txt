I hold a fairly strong version of the no-truth-value response to Lewis's triviality results. The results, as I read them, show that there is no way to assign truth conditions to the indicative conditional such that the probability of the conditional generally equals the probability of the consequent given the antecedent, across a sufficiently rich class of probability functions. Since the equation seems to capture exactly how we do assess indicative conditionals -- we treat the credibility of "if A, then C" as the credibility of C on the supposition of A -- I conclude that indicative conditionals do not express propositions at all. They are devices for expressing conditional states of mind, not for stating conditional facts; the equation is a constraint on rational credence and assertibility, not a truth-conditional semantics.

What I want is a map of this position's exposure. What would count against it? I am less interested in the textbook complaint that conditionals embed (under negation, in antecedents, in quantified sentences), since my side has replies to each embedding datum, and more interested in which replies are actually good: which embedding phenomena resist the standard assertibility-based treatments in a way I should concede, and which are handled honestly. Second, there are escape routes from triviality that keep conditionals propositional by restricting where the equation holds -- limiting the class of probability functions, or giving up full generality in some other way. Do any of these succeed well enough to undermine my motivation, or do they save propositions only by abandoning what made them attractive? Third, I want an honest reckoning with a worry about myself: my view seems able to absorb any datum about conditionals by redescribing it as a fact about what it is sensible to assert or accept. If that makes the view unfalsifiable, say so, and say whether unfalsifiability of this kind is a defect for a view in this area or merely a sign it is doing pragmatic work. Please rank the potential defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, indicate which ones defenders of my position would themselves accept as genuine costs, and state how confident you are in the overall verdict -- and flag the single claim in your answer you are least sure of.
