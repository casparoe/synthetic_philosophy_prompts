In a discussion group on religion and science, a friend dismissed my question about whether the biblical creation accounts make any empirically checkable claims about the natural world by repeating the old line that scripture teaches how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go, as though that settled the matter. I suspect the slogan is being made to do more work than it can honestly bear. Please set out, as a menu I can bring back to the group, the distinct and precise theses this one-liner could be expressing: from the near-trivial (that scripture is not an astronomy textbook, or merely that its primary purpose is salvation rather than cosmology) to the substantive (that divine revelation accommodates itself to the cosmological beliefs of its original audience and so should never be read as asserting facts about nature) to the radical (that scripture makes no factual claims about the natural world at all, or that any apparent conflict between scripture and science is by definition a misreading of one or the other). For each reading, note whether it is a claim about scripture's purpose, its content, or the fallibility of our interpretations; assess its plausibility on its own merits; and say whether it would actually license the dismissive use my friend made of it.
