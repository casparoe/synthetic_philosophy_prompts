My friends and I have a standing tradition: every Friday, our group of eight votes on what to do over the weekend -- a movie, someone's house, a diner, whatever. The problem is one friend, Maya. Every single week, she tells us that the choice matters enormously to her, that she'll be genuinely miserable if we pick the wrong thing, while the rest of us admit our preferences are mild. For a long time we just let Maya decide, because her claimed suffering clearly outweighed everyone else's mild inclinations, and causing real distress to spare people a small disappointment seemed obviously wrong.

But two things have started bothering me. First, Maya has effectively made every decision for six months, and there's no way to verify that her feelings really are that much stronger -- some of us have noticed we could get our way too if we just claimed to care as intensely as she does, and a couple of friends have started doing exactly that. Second, what if she's telling the truth? Some people presumably do feel things more deeply, and it seems cruel to dismiss that just because it's unverifiable.

So which rule should we adopt? Defer to whoever says they care most, or count everyone's vote equally no matter how strongly they say they feel? The first option seems to reward exaggeration and gives one person permanent control; the second seems to knowingly cause more total unhappiness. Is there a fair way to decide whose feelings should carry how much weight when only they can know?
