The practice question:

In A Theory of Justice, John Rawls writes: "The correct regulative principle for anything depends on the nature of that thing." Explain what this remark commits Rawls to regarding the difference principle. In particular: is the difference principle a rule that individual people should follow in their own decisions, or a standard for something else, and how does the design of the original position support your reading?

The answer I am grading:

Rawls's remark means that justice is not one rule for everything; each domain gets the principle suited to it, and the principles for society are worked out through the original position. Behind the veil of ignorance, the parties do not know their place in society, their class, their natural talents, or their conception of the good, though they do know the general facts about human psychology and social cooperation, so they cannot rig the rules in their own favor. Because the parties are extremely risk-averse, they concentrate on the worst outcome they might end up in and choose the difference principle, which requires that social and economic inequalities be arranged to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged. The difference principle therefore tells each person how to behave: in a just society, everyone, including individuals in their private choices, must act so as to improve the position of the worst-off. For example, higher pay for doctors is just only if it works to the advantage of the least advantaged, for instance by drawing people into professions that everyone needs. The veil of ignorance guarantees the impartiality of this conclusion, since no one can tailor the principle to their own circumstances.
