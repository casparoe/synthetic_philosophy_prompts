I run our department's tiny hard cases fellowship for displaced students, and I want us to stay honest rather than vibes-based. Past calls: we funded a student fleeing a militia that targeted her family after her brother refused recruitment; we did not fund one escaping famine alone, even with real danger; we funded a gay student facing prosecution under a law the state actually enforced; we denied a strong applicant from a merely very violent city; we funded a deserter who'd be jailed for refusing a war he reasonably thought aggressive; we denied someone facing persecution who had a genuinely safe internal option. New file: applicant is from an ethnic minority that's harassed but usually not prosecuted, faces a draft into a war with credible war-crime allegations, and could move to the capital where he'd be poorer, informally safer, and still watched. Recomend fund or deny to keep us coherent, name the closest precedent, say whether we're really running persecution-plus-no-internal-flight, serious-harm-plus-state-failure, or anti-complicity, whether funding would be duty or charity, and give the next case that would split those rules.
