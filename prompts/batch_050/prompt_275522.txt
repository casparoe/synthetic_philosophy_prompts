so i've been going down a rabbit hole on Indian epistemology after someone brought up Nyāya in an open thread, and i'm stuck on something. The whole system is built around the pramāṇas (perception, inference, comparison, testimony) as instruments that yield valid cognition, fine. But the Naiyāyikas also have this doctrine (parataḥ prāmāṇya, sp?) that a cognition's validity isn't intrinsic — you only find out whether it was true by whether acting on it works out. You see water in the distance, walk over, drink, thirst quenched: valid. Mirage: invalid. But isn't that just... pragmatism about truth? If truth gets cashed out entirely in terms of successful action, then all the heavyweight realism they insist on — real mind-independent objects, real universals, the world having a definite structure we're trying to match — is pointless decoration. And their centuries of fighting the Buddhists over whether universals exist were a waste of breath, since the metaphysics can't make any practical difference either way. So were the classical Indian realists secretly pragmatists who never noticed, or am i flattening a distinction somewhere?
