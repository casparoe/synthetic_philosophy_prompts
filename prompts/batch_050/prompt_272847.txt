So I'm taking an environmental ethics seminar this term and next week we're doing the unit on pest control, which I honestly expected to be the boring week, but our prof dropped this line in lecture that's been stuck in my head: the number of insects killed or otherwise affected by agriculture and vector control each year is so absurdly large that even a small probability that insects can suffer might morally outweigh nearly everything else humans do. I got volunteered (long story, I was late) to prepare a one-page decision tree for the discussion session, and I'd like your help drafting it.

The tree should start from a commitment everyone in the room will accept, something like "insecticide use causes the deaths of enormous numbers of insects," and walk people down to an actual position through branching yes/no-ish questions. Each branch node should be a question someone can answer WITHOUT already knowing which position it leads to (so no "do you think insect suffering matters?" as a node -- too on the nose). The splits I definitely want represented: whether insects can suffer at all and, separately, how to handle being uncertain about that (does the precautionary principle flip the burden of proof?); whether the sheer scale of tiny possible harms changes the moral arithmetic or whether aggregation of minuscule welfare across trillions of beings is a mistake; whether the counterfactual matters, i.e. most of these insects would die soon anyway, often by starvation, predation, or parasites, so is pesticide death actually worse than what it replaces; whether it makes a difference that insecticides sometimes PREVENT suffering (mosquito control vs malaria, locust control vs famine) so they might be obligatory rather than permissible; and whether what matters morally is the individual insect or the population/ecosystem level.

For leaves, I want at least: plain anthropocentrism (pest control needs no justification); a "pest welfare" position that extends something like the 3Rs from lab animal ethics -- replace, reduce, refine -- to how we kill pests; a strict non-harm position in the neighborhood of Jain ahimsa; an ecocentric view where individual insects don't count but ecosystem integrity does; and a view where vector control is morally obligatory even granting full insect sentience. Please also include at least two leaves for logically possible combinations nobody seems to actually hold, with a one-sentence diagnosis each (is the spot empty because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored?). Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate with its questions. Audience is undergrads in a debate club, it needs to fit on one page, and I'd like roughly 7-9 named or unnamed leaves total. Thanks!
