i'm a poli sci phd student and my undergrads are convinced voting is irrational after we did the paradox of voting (pivotal probability basically zero, costs of voting positive, so the calculus says stay home). i want to end the unit with a villanelle that takes the paradox seriously instead of hand-waving it away. the two refrains should be the two horns: something like "my vote will never tip it" and "and still i walk to the polls," and the form should do the work -- the refrains keep coming back the way the paradox keeps reasserting itself every time you offer a fix (civic duty, expression, minuscule-but-nonzero stakes), and when they finally meet in the closing quatrain it should feel like the two impulses coexisting rather than one winning. don't resolve it with a cheesy "every vote matters" ending, the whole point is that the instrumental case fails and we vote anyway. ideally a reader who knows Riker and Ordeshook's calculus should recognize it in the bones of the poem without it being namedropped.
