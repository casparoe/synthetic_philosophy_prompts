So I'm a science/ideas journalist and I've been working on this long feature about dual-use research and who gets to decide what gets published, and my editor keeps saying the piece needs a sidebar, something a bit lighter that still gets the idea across to readers who glaze over at game theory. I got it into my head that a poem could do it, and specifically a villanelle, because the concept I want to dramatize is all about the same thing happening over and over no matter what anyone does.

The concept, in my journalese: imagine a decision where the bad outcome happens if ANY one person in a group decides to go ahead -- say ten researchers each independently deciding whether to post their gain-of-function results online. Nine of them think it's a bad idea. Doesn't matter: it only takes one. And here's the nasty part, each individual might be reasoning completely responsibly by their own lights, the math just guarantees that out of a big enough pool someone will always land on "yes," so the group's collective caution is worthless unless they coordinate. Now the clever response is: if I'm thinking of pressing publish, I should remember that nine other people looked at the same evidence and walked away, which is itself evidence I'm wrong. But if EVERYONE reasons that way, nothing ever gets published, including stuff that genuinely should be -- the curse just flips upside down. (I beleive philosophers of a certain stripe call this the unilateralist's curse, but please don't use that label in the poem, it sounds like a Harry Potter thing.)

Write the villanelle so the two refrains do the work: one refrain is the lone researcher's voice, each time slightly more reasonable-sounding, the "I've thought about this and I still say go" line; the other refrain is the arithmetic itself, impersonal, the thing that guarantees someone in the room always says yes no matter how careful everyone is. They should alternate so the reader feels the human judgment getting ground down by the statistics, and in the final quatrain where the refrains finally meet, land on the flip side of the curse -- the silence when everybody defers and the right call never gets made by anyone. Keep it accurate enough that a philosopher reading my sidebar wouldn't wince, but it still has to scan and rhyme like an actual poem. Nineteen lines, you know the drill.
