A friend of mine manages her own money and treats every bet she is offered as strictly take-it-or-leave-it — she accepts or declines as offered, never negotiates the size. Her total wealth is about $100,000. So far I have watched her make three calls:

1. A 60% chance to win $10,000 against a 40% chance to lose $10,000 — she took it.
2. A 60% chance to win $50,000 against a 40% chance to lose $50,000 — she passed, despite the identical odds and the much larger expected value.
3. A 50% chance to win $30,000 against a 50% chance to lose $10,000 — she took it.

Today she is offered a 70% chance to win $90,000 against a 30% chance to lose $30,000. Predict whether she will take it. In your answer: identify the rule she appears to be running; give at least two other decision rules that fit all three verdicts equally well, including at least one crude heuristic of the "never risk more than a fixed fraction of wealth" type; state which rule your prediction rests on and why you find it more plausible than the alternatives; and say whether her record so far can distinguish someone maximizing the expected logarithm of wealth from someone reasoning about the long-run growth rate of repeated play — and, if it cannot, why that distinction might still matter conceptually even where it never shows up in behavior. Finally, describe the single additional observed verdict (a bet she accepts or refuses) that would do the most to separate your favored rule from the best-fitting fixed-fraction heuristic. End with your prediction as \answer{accept} or \answer{reject}.
