After forty years in field biology I have taken up philosophy of mind in retirement, and I am currently exercised by the question of whether dreams are experiences undergone during sleep. What I should like from you is a classification instrument in the style I know best: a dichotomous key, in the manner of a field guide to the positions, for a reading group of fellow amateurs.

Build the key from the logical space rather than from a list of names: a sequence of at most ten questions, each a concrete claim or case a reader can answer without knowing any philosopher's name — for instance, whether the waking report is authoritative evidence of something undergone in the night, whether an experience can exist at a time without being in principle reportable, and what the sleep experiments bear on in which a splash of water or a bell is woven into an elaborate dream narrative (does this show such narratives run in real time, or is it compatible with their being composed wholesale at waking?). As leaves, the key must reach at least: the orthodox view that dreams are perceptual experiences during sleep; Malcolm's position that there are no experiences during sleep and that the concept of a dream is bound to the waking report; Dennett's cassette theory that a dream may be fabricated entire at the moment of waking; and the view that dreams are imaginings rather than perceivings. Mark any combination of answers nobody defends, and diagnose briefly whether that corner is empty because the view there is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any pair of positions the key cannot separate, and give each leaf one sentence stating the commitment its holder should find most uncomfortable.
