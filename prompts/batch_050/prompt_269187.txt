Write the FAQ page for a fictional lending startup, "EquiScore," whose loan-approval algorithm has just been court-ordered to satisfy every major fairness criterion at once: demographic parity, calibration within groups, equal false positive rates, equal false negative rates. About 600 words, deadpan corporate-help-center voice, numbered questions with chirpy answers. The comedy should come from the company quietly doing the only things the math actually permits: approving everyone or no one to hit parity, predicting 50% for everyone to stay calibrated, and a final question ("What if base rates genuinely differ across groups?") whose answer keeps almost confessing the impossibility theorem before the legal team redacts it. Don't name Kleinberg or Chouldechova or cite any papers -- the reader who knows the impossibility results should recognize that the FAQ author does, and everyone else should just find it funny. End with a question about the customer satisfaction survey that somehow also fails the criteria.
