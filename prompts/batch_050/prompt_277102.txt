I'm helping to set the research agenda for a small interdisciplinary group -- three philosophers of mind and two people with machine-learning backgrounds -- that has funding for two years to work on the conceptual status of large language models. The framing debate we keep circling is whether these systems are best understood as agents (systems with beliefs, desires, or at least goals of their own), as simulators (engines that enact characters or predict text without the simulated persona's mental states being states of the system itself), or as something for which neither category fits and a new one is needed.

What I want from you is not a verdict on that question but a defended agenda: which open conceptual questions in this area should the group prioritize, and in what order? Candidates I have in mind include, but are not limited to: whether the agent-versus-simulator dispute is a substantive question about the system or a question about which interpretive stance yields the most predictive and explanatory leverage, and what would settle that meta-question; whether there is a principled difference between a system that has a goal and a system whose behavior is reliably goal-directed under a wide range of prompts and contexts, and whether behavioral criteria can ever distinguish these; what the training process (next-token prediction on human text, followed by reinforcement fine-tuning) implies about which mental-state attributions, if any, are warranted; whether the right unit of analysis is the base model, the fine-tuned model, the model-in-context-with-a-prompt, or the whole deployed system including the user; and whether familiar criteria for agency drawn from animal cognition and philosophy of action transfer at all to a system with no persistent memory, no embodiment, and no interests at stake in its own continued operation.

For each question you include, explain: why it is upstream or downstream of the others (that is, which other questions have answers that depend on settling this one first); how tractable it is for philosophers working alongside, but not as, machine-learning researchers -- including which questions require empirical access to model internals we won't have and should therefore be deferred or reformulated; and what would change its ranking, meaning which findings or arguments over the next two years would move it up or down the list. If you think the most important open question is one I haven't listed, say so and make the case for it on the same terms.

Please also identify at least one question in this area that you would actively deprioritize -- something that attracts a lot of attention but where two years of our effort would be unlikely to produce progress, or where the answer would change little downstream -- and say why. The deliverable I'm after is an ordered, argued agenda rather than a survey of the debate, and no bibliography is needed; refer to positions and arguments by description and I'll handle citations myself.
