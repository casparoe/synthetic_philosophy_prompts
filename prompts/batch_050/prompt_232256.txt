I've been reading about the Trojan War and King Arthur lately and there's a pattern I don't have a word for, hoping you can help. You're a historian-slash-philosopher of historiography here, so take your time and brainstorm a few candidate concepts before settling.

With Troy it seems the direction goes one way: something plausibly happened (there's a real ruined city at Hisarlik), and then centuries of retelling piled gods, a ten-year siege, and a wooden horse on top. Legend grows out of history. But some stories seem to travel the opposite direction. A community tells a story that at first nobody treats as a factual claim about the past — it's just *the story we tell* — and then over generations it quietly hardens into something people cite as plain fact about where they come from. Nobody ever decides to start believing it, nobody converts anybody, and afterwards you can't even find the moment the qualifier fell off. It just drifted.

Is there an established term for this drift from acknowledged story to assumed history? I don't think it's euhemerism, since as I understand it that's a deliberate rationalizing move someone makes, and it's not quite "invented tradition" either, because nothing is invented on purpose at any step. If there's no good existing term, coin one and define it carefully — and tell me where the edges are, e.g. does the phenomenon require that earlier people *explicitly* doubted the story, or can it start from a state of pure shrug where nobody ever asked whether it was true?
