I work in reinforcement learning and am fluent in Markov decision processes, reward decomposition, local policy improvement, and preference-based RL. Consider Quinn’s self-torturer: an agent can repeatedly trade a small, barely noticeable increase in future pain for a substantial immediate benefit; each trade considered alone is attractive, while accepting the whole sequence is clearly disastrous. Restate the puzzle in that vocabulary, including the claims that rational preferences might be intransitive across barely discriminable outcomes and that individually rational choices can compose into an irrational course of action. Specify what plays the roles of states, actions, one-step rewards, trajectories, and preference comparisons. Then provide a residue report: what translates cleanly into scalar-reward RL, what survives only after moving to preference-based or non-Markovian formulations, and what has no faithful representation in either. End with a committed verdict on whether the residue marks a genuine limitation of the RL conception of agency or instead shows that the original notion of rational preference was doing confused work.
