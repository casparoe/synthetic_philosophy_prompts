So I keep running into the line "conceivability is a guide to possibility" and I've now seen it do two opposite jobs. In an online thread about philosophical zombies, someone used it to argue that since a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me with no experience is conceivable, physicalism is in trouble. Then a friend of mine shot back that you can "conceive" of water existing without H2O even though that's impossible, so conceivability proves nothing at all. Both of them seemed to think they were talking about the same principle and I don't think they were.

Can you unpack the slogan into the different precise theses it might be? I figure there are dials: (a) what counts as conceiving -- just being able to picture something vividly, giving a description that doesn't obviously contradict itself, or whatever would survive ideal reflection by a perfect reasoner; (b) which possibility is meant -- logical, metaphysical, physical/nomic, or just "for all I know"; (c) how strong the link is -- entailment, defeasible evidence, or merely a starting presumption. Lay out the most interesting combinations as a menu, and for each one say whether it's a platitude, genuinely substantive but defensible, or just false, with an example that shows why.

Two wrinkles I especially want addressed. First, the symmetry problem: both "Goldbach's conjecture is true" and "it's false" seem conceivable to me right now, and they can't both be possible. Does that refute the slogan, or does it just force you into the ideal-reflection version, and does that version become vacuous -- i.e., conceivable just *means* possible at that point, so the "guide" tells you nothing you can use? Second, does it matter that conceivability is conceiver-relative -- that what I can imagine depends on my cognitive limits, so the slogan seems to make modal facts depend on psychology?

End with the payoff: which reading does the zombie arguer actually need for the argument to go through, and does my friend's water/H2O counterexample even touch that reading, or are they talking past each other?
