I run a grad-student philosophy podcast and next week I'm interviewing a political theorist whose new book defends a neo-Weilian program: taking off from Simone Weil's 1943 essay On the Abolition of All Political Parties, she argues that parties should be legally dissolved and elections contested only by independent candidates running on personal platforms. I don't want softballs ("why do voters hate parties so much these days?"). Draft 8 interview questions, and for each one give me the reply she's most likely to give plus a follow-up that keeps the pressure on. At minimum I want the set to cover: Weil's claim that a party is essentially a machine for generating collective passion that systematically subordinates truth to victory — does banning formal parties just push that dynamic into informal networks, media ecosystems and donor blocs, and would she bite the bullet and regulate those too?; her use of Rousseau's general will to diagnose what's wrong with party thinking — is that even fair to Rousseau, and does the diagnosis survive if it isn't?; the coordination problem, i.e. how a legislature of independents forms stable governing majorities without re-creating parties under another name; and whether Weil's criteria for what makes something a party would also catch things my guest presumably wants to keep (unions, churches, social movements). Start with the questions themselves — no preamble, no summary of her position.
