Take the 2006 vote by the International Astronomical Union that demoted Pluto to 'dwarf planet' status after the discovery of Eris, and run it through semantic externalism — Putnam's Twin Earth, the idea that meanings just ain't in the head, plus the linguistic division of labour. Two readings seem available. On one, 'planet' behaves like a natural kind term: astronomers didn't change the meaning of the word, they slowly worked out which objects actually fall under it, and a committee of experts settling the boundary is precisely the division of labour doing its job. On the other, the boundary got fixed by a show of hands in a conference hall, which looks less like discovering an essence and more like stipulating one — no telescope observation settles whether 'has cleared its neighbourhood' is the right criterion. Work out which features of the episode each reading explains and which it mangles: the public outrage, the fact the resolution could easily have gone the other way, Pluto-the-object being utterly unchanged throughout. Then commit: does the Pluto affair count as evidence for externalism, against it, or does it just show the theory was never really about words like 'planet' in the first place? Take a definite side rather than laying out the options and leaving it with me.
