I'm preparing a lecture on Hofstadter's account of where meaning lives in a message, and I want to pressure-test his three-layer scheme against what I take to be its most interesting real-world stress case: the Voyager Golden Record, launched in 1977. For the benefit of anyone rusty on the details, the salient facts are these. The record is a gold-plated copper disc carrying an inner payload of greetings in dozens of languages, natural and human-made sounds, about ninety minutes of music (Bach, Blind Willie Johnson, a Navajo chant, and so on), and over a hundred images encoded as an analog video signal. Etched on the cover are diagrams: a representation of the hyperfine transition of neutral hydrogen (meant to fix units of time and length), a pulsar map locating the Sun relative to fourteen pulsars, step-by-step pictorial instructions for building a player and extracting the images, and a silhouette of the craft showing the record's position. A stylus and cartridge are physically attached. Sagan's team faced exactly the problem Hofstadter describes with his bottle-washed-ashore thought experiment: the finders would share no language, no conventions, possibly no sensory modalities with us, so whatever tells them *that* this is a message and *how* to decode it had to ride along with, or precede, the message itself.

Here is what I'd like from you. First, the disciplined mapping: assign each component of the record -- the hydrogen transition diagram, the pulsar map, the playback instructions, the attached stylus, the images, the spoken greetings, the music -- to Hofstadter's frame, outer, or inner message, and defend each assignment, flagging any component that resists clean classification (my own suspicion is that the hydrogen diagram is doing double duty, and that the music is an inner message only in a Pickwickian sense, but I want to be argued with). Second, identify where the theory strains: does the outer message itself require a further outer message to be recognized as one, generating the regress Hofstadter thinks he can stop, and does the designers' reliance on universally shared physics and mathematics vindicate his stopping point or merely relocate it? Third, run the analysis in the other direction: Hofstadter argues that meaning is present once the decoding rules are, since any intelligence with the outer message can recover the inner one -- does the Golden Record confirm this, or does it instead expose that his scheme quietly presupposes a sender (say, via his jukebox argument about DNA and intelligence) in a way he never admits? Please be explicit about which features of the episode the theory handles well, which it misdescribes, and what, if anything, the record teaches us that Hofstadter's framework could not have anticipated.
