I am a novelist trying to understand what I am doing when a recurring metaphor reorganizes how readers perceive a character. Give me your credence in the following proposition: Davidson’s central semantic thesis is correct—a metaphorical utterance has no second, paraphrasable metaphorical meaning beyond its literal meaning; its distinctive cognitive effects belong to its use rather than its semantic content. Decompose your estimate into the probability that a principled distinction exists between semantic content and effects merely caused by an utterance; the probability that metaphor’s power to make us notice and reorganize features can be explained without positing a second meaning; the probability that the systematic cross-domain mappings emphasized by conceptual-metaphor theory are genuinely semantic rather than part of the background structure of thought; and any further terms you consider necessary. Provide a number and one line of justification for each, explain how they combine, identify the term to which the final credence is most sensitive, and state what evidence would most change it. Please give an actual estimate rather than concluding that both approaches capture something important.
