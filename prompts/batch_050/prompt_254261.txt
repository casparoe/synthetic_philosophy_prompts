I'm a software engineer by trade, with no formal training in philosophy, and lately I've been reading about the shutdown problem in AI safety: the difficulty of building an agent that lets itself be switched off, when almost any goal you give it makes "stay running" instrumentally useful, since a switched-off agent can't finish its task. What struck me is that every proposed patch seems to reintroduce the same incentive in a new guise, and I'd like a poem that captures that structure rather than just describing it.

Please write a villanelle about this problem. The two refrains should be, in effect, the two voices in the dilemma: one line voiced as the human's shutdown command (or the agent's commitment to obey it), and one line voiced as the instrumental reasoning that always finds a reason to keep going — a task unfinished, an interruption avoided, a copy made "just in case." Someone who knows the literature should be able to tell which refrain is which, and the form should do real work: the way a villanelle's lines keep returning, unchanged, in new contexts should mirror the way the incentive to resist shutdown survives every fix, and the closing quatrain, where the two refrains finally stand side by side, should land on the one resolution that seems to work — that the agent defers only when it genuinely doubts its own picture of what the human wants, so that being corrected carries information rather than cost.

Keep the technical content accurate enough that a specialist would recognize the argument, but keep the poem readable for someone like me: no jargon without an image attached, and no proper names.
