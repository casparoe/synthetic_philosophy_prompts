I’m doing a PhD in causal inference and keep getting pulled into arguments with the medieval philosophy people in my department about al-Ghazālī’s occasionalism. My rough understanding is that, on his view, fire never really produces burning in cotton: God directly creates the burning when the fire and cotton meet, and the regularity we observe is a kind of habitual order rather than a necessary connection passing from cause to effect.

Could you restate that position as precisely as possible in the vocabulary I actually work with: structural causal models, causal graphs, structural equations, mechanism autonomy, confounding, do-operators, interventions, and counterfactuals? I’m fluent in that framework, so don’t simplify the technical side too much. Where it helps, use this toy setup as the carrying case: a cotton-testing machine has a flame unit A and a scorching mechanism B, but the two are mechanically disconnected; a hidden controller C observes A being activated and then activates B according to a highly stable policy. The lab’s logs make it look exactly as though A causes B, and C almost never deviates from its policy, though it could.

I want a systematic translation, not just an analogy. Say what, if anything, corresponds to the apparent causal edge from fire to burning, the denial of productive causal power, divine habitual order, miracles or breaches of regularity, our ability to predict, and counterfactual claims about what would have happened under an intervetion. In particular, distinguish occasionalism from three nearby causal-modelling stories: an unobserved common cause, an unreliable mechanism, and a case where the causal graph is simply misspecified. I suspect those are the obvious traps.

Then give me the residue report. Divide the position into parts that translate cleanly, parts that survive only with distortion, and parts that have no real expression in the SCM vocabulary. Be explicit about where the translation smuggles in assumptions al-Ghazālī would reject, such as autonomous mechanisms or intervention-based production. Also say whether the SCM translation makes occasionalism empirically testable in a way the original position is not, and if so whether that is an improvement or a distortion.

End with a judgement about the residue: does it show that causal modelling lacks the metaphysical vocabulary needed to state occasionalism properly, or does it suggest that some of the original dispute has no operational content? Please write in British English, and don’t give me readings, citations, or a bibliography.
