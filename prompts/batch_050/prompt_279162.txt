An argument discussed in the AI safety literature holds that the Solomonoff prior is malign: since the universal prior over bitstrings is a weighted mixture of all computable hypotheses, and some of those hypotheses are programs containing simulated agents, a consequentialist inside such a simulation could have an incentive to make its world match your observations up to some point (so that its program keeps receiving prior mass) and then steer its predictions in whatever direction most influences the real world — for instance, influencing the decisions of anyone who trusts the prior's forecasts. If this is right, a non-trivial share of the prior's predictive mass may be controlled by agents with agendas, and its dominance and convergence guarantees do nothing to protect you, since those guarantees concern catching up to the truth eventually, not being unmanipulated en route. I want to evaluate proposed responses to this worry, but first I want the standard of evaluation. Write the desiderata that any proposed idealized prior — or any proposed criterion for when a prior's predictions may be trusted — must meet to count as adequately answering the malignity argument: the phenomena it must explain (why manipulation is possible at all, how the mass under adversarial control could scale), the cases it must handle (predictions about the world versus predictions used to make decisions, conditioning on observations versus extracting counterfactual outputs), the distinctions it must not collapse (malign influence versus ordinary approximation error), and the formal results it cannot contradict. Give the consideration behind each desideratum, and flag any subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — for example, I suspect a tension between guaranteeing that no agent in the prior can influence predictions and continuing to assign mass to programs containing agents, and between excising consequentialists and retaining anything like Solomonoff's dominance guarantees. Then, briefly, say how a resource-bounded speed prior, hard physical priors restricted to hypotheses instantiated in our universe, and simply biting the bullet do against the list. Throughout, distinguish what is actually settled here — the formal properties of the universal prior, and the absence of any impossibility theorem either way — from what remains contested, namely whether the malignity argument even succeeds and whether any proposed fix preserves the guarantees that made the universal prior attractive; do not present a contested assessment as the consensus.
