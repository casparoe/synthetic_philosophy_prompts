The exam question was: State Williams's internal reasons thesis and explain the role of the subjective motivational set in it. Why, on his view, can an agent have an internal reason to do something she is not currently motivated to do? Apply the thesis to two cases: (i) a person who wants a gin and tonic and believes the glass in front of her contains gin, when it in fact contains petrol; (ii) Owen Wingrave, whose family urges him to pursue a military career. Finally, does Williams's rejection of external reasons commit him to the conclusion that blaming or criticizing an agent who lacks the relevant motivation is pointless?

The student's answer, in full: Williams's internal reasons thesis is that A has a reason to φ only if A could come to be motivated to φ by sound deliberation from her existing subjective motivational set, that is, her desires, projects, commitments, and emotional dispositions as they currently stand. The crucial constraint is that the set is fixed by the agent's actual present psychology, and sound deliberation is essentially instrumental: it traces means to ends that are already in the set, so a reason reaches no further than the agent's current ends allow. This explains how an agent can have a reason without being motivated. If she holds a false belief, or simply has not deliberated, sound deliberation from her set can generate the motivation, and the reason was there all along. In the petrol case, the woman has a reason not to drink: given her desire for a gin and tonic, deliberation that corrected her false belief about the glass would produce a motive to refrain, so the reason precedes the motivation. Owen Wingrave is the mirror image. Nothing in his motivational set supports a military life, and no deliberative route from it leads to one, so the claim that he has a reason to enlist is simply false, and his family's insistence rests on the illusion of external reasons, which Williams argues cannot bear the explanatory connection to possible action that genuine reason-claims require. As for blame, Williams does appear committed to its pointlessness in exactly these cases. If Owen has no reason to enlist, then telling him that he ought to cannot supply one, and criticism of him degenerates into mere venting of disapproval with no rational grip on its target; Williams bites this bullet, holding that where no deliberative route exists, normative address is idle. The cost of the view is that moral demands lose their categorical force, but the gain is that talk of reasons keeps its explanatory connection to what agents can actually be brought to do.
