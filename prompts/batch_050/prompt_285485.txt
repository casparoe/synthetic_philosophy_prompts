hey, I wrote this draft essay for a friend's philosophy blog and I'd love some comments before I send it in. It's on that classic torture vs dust specks puzzle (would you rather one person be tortured horribly for 50 years, or a huge number of people each get a single dust speck in the eye). I'm not asking whether my conclusion is *right* exactly — I'm more interested in whether the argument actually hangs together and where it's weakest. Specific requests at the bottom. Here it is:

---

**Why the dust specks never stood a chance: a plea for threshold realism**

Everyone who thinks about the torture-vs-dust-specks case seems to assume that the dust speck side has to justify some heroic act of aggregation: stacking three-to-the-googolplex tiny harms on top of each other until the pile outweighs fifty years of agony. I think this concedes far too much at the outset. The interesting question isn't whether tiny harms add up. It's whether a dust speck in the eye is a harm at all.

Here's my claim: for something to count as a harm, it has to make a difference that the harmed person could in principle register as a difference. Call this threshold realism. A dust speck in the eye produces a blink, maybe a split second of irritation that's forgotten before the next breath. If you removed it from someone's life retroactively, nothing about their day, their mood, their choices, or their memory would change. The speck leaves no trace in experience. And a "harm" that leaves no trace in anyone's experience strikes me as a bookkeeping entry, not a harm.

Note what this view is NOT saying. I'm not saying small harms don't aggregate. If each of a vast number of people got a mild headache for an hour, that's genuinely bad and plausibly comparable to a severe harm to one person — headaches register. Headaches leave traces. The speck's defenders always slide between "tiny" and "below the threshold of registration," and these are completely different things. A harm can be arbitrarily small while still being a real dent in someone's experience; the speck case is specifically constructed to fall below the dent-making line.

The usual move at this point is to invoke a sorites: if I make the speck slightly worse — a grit, a scratch, a sting — at some point it becomes a harm, and there's no principled place to draw the line, so either everything aggregates or nothing does. But I think this gets it backwards. The sorites shows that my threshold is vague, not that it doesn't exist. Vague boundaries are everywhere (baldness, heaps, adulthood) and nobody concludes from this that there's no difference between a bald man and a hairy one. There is a genuine, fuzzy band of cases where a speck-ish irritation starts to register, and below that band there simply isn't a harm to aggregate. Multiplying zero by a huge number is still zero.

One more wrinkle I want to defend: I think the registration threshold is *personal and contextual*, not fixed. The same pinprick might register for a surgeon mid-operation (where it wrecks her concentration at the worst moment) and fail to register for someone asleep. So threshold realism doesn't give us a clean universal line, and I'm fine with that. The ethics of inflicting tiny harms should be sensitive to whether they'd actually land in anyone's experience, not just to the physics of the stimulus.

So: torturing one person for 50 years, or dust specks for everyone? The specks, and it's not close, and it was never close. Not because small harms don't add up, but because the specks were never harms to begin with.
