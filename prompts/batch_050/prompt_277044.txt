I'm a high school junior, and my philosophy club recently read this fragment from Blaise Pascal's Pensées (no. 556 in the Trotter translation):

"All appearance indicates neither a total exclusion nor a manifest presence of divinity, but the presence of a God who hides himself. Everything bears this character."

Afterward, two members got into an argument about whether Pascal's observation settles the modern "hiddenness" objection to God's existence, and I couldn't tell who won. Maya argued that hiddenness is strong evidence against God: a perfectly loving being would make His existence clear enough that no honest seeker could miss Him, yet millions of sincere people search their whole lives and find nothing -- which is exactly what the world would look like if no such being existed. Daniel answered that this can't count as evidence against Christianity in particular, because, as Pascal shows, the religion itself announces a God who hides; you can't refute a view by pointing to something it already teaches. Maya replied that this makes the position unfalsifiable: if an obvious God would confirm Christianity and a hidden God also confirms it, then no observation could ever disconfirm it, and the "prediction" is just an after-the-fact patch, like a psychic explaining a failed test by blaming the skeptics in the room. Daniel said the hiddenness isn't a patch at all -- Pascal ties it to an independent doctrine, human corruption: a fully manifest God would produce servile fear or pride rather than love, so hiddenness is what genuine love requires given what we are. Maya granted that this might explain some obscurity but not the amount -- nothing in the doctrine predicts hiddenness so deep that sincere seekers in other traditions never find their way. Daniel closed by saying "sincere seeker" is doing all the work in her argument, since Pascal would deny that anyone seeks with a completely undivided heart.

Could you go through the exchange and tell me who is right at each step -- whether Pascal's point really blocks the hiddenness argument, whether the unfalsifiability charge lands, whether the appeal to human corruption explains the degree of hiddenness or only its existence, and whether Daniel's last move is legitimate or just question-begging? I'd also like to know what, if anything, both of them are missing, and who is more right overall.
