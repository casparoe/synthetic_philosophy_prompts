My reading group cooked up this argument and it obviously proves too much, but we can't agree on where it breaks. (1) You're only obligated to do X if your doing X makes a difference to whether the bad outcome happens. (2) In a race to the bottom, my restraint makes no difference: if I don't build the shoddy product, overfish the stock, or slash safety spending, a competitor just takes my market share and does it instead, so the outcome is the same either way. (3) So I'm not obligated to restrain unilaterally. (4) This reasoning is available to every player in the trap, since the situation is symmetric. (5) So nobody inside a multipolar trap is obligated to restrain at all — the only real obligations fall on whoever can change the rules of the game. (6) But run the same argument on carbon emissions, antibiotic overuse, or voting and it proves no one is obligated to do anything about those either, which is absurd. Which step do I reject? I'd like to keep (1) if possible — it feels like the bare minimum of what "obligated" even means — but tell me what rejecting (2) or the move from (4) to (5) actually costs. And does it matter what kind of trap we're in, i.e. whether the harm scales with total activity across everyone or only with whoever crosses the finish line first?
