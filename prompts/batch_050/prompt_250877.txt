I'm giving a 20 minute talk at a workshop where half the room is frequentist statisticians and half is formal epistemologists. My thesis: the Bayesian convergence results show that the old "subjectivity objection" to Bayesianism is overrated, because priors wash out as data acumulates, so any disagreement that traces back to prior choice is temporary and shallow. Sketch of my arguement so you know what to target: consistency results say that as long as the true distribution gets nonzero prior mass, the posterior concentrates around it; merging-of-opinions results say two Bayesians who agree on which events are even possible will end up making the same predictions; so the data do the real work and the prior is just a starting position.

Prep me for the Q&A. For each half of the room: what will they object to, phrased the way they'd actually phrase it in their own vocabulary?
