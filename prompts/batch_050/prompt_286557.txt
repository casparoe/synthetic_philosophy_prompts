I keep losing arguments with a friend in the quantum foundations group, so I finally wrote down what I actually believe about the wavefunction, planning to send it to him so he can pick it apart. Before I do — can you check whether these positions even hang together, or whether I'm contradicting myself somewhere without noticing?

1. I'm a realist about physics. The posits of our best theories should be taken seriously as real, and both the old Copenhagen attitude that the state vector is just a bookkeeping device and QBism feel like giving up on explaining anything.

2. That said, the wavefunction itself is epistemic. It encodes what an agent or a community knows about a system, the way a Liouville distribution in classical stat mech encodes ignorance about the underlying microstate. What's real is the ontic state; psi is about our access to it.

3. The best argument for the epistemic reading, I think, is that it explains why non-orthogonal quantum states can't be perfectly distinguished: the two preparations might produce the same underlying reality, and the probability distributions over ontic states overlap, so no measurement could ever reliably tell them apart.

4. Collapse is a real physical event. I'm drawn to GRW-style spontaneous collapse, where something objective and stochastic actually happens and solves the measurement problem, rather than it being dissolved or pushed into the observer's head.

5. I also found the PBR theorem pretty convincing when I read about it. It seems to show that any model reproducing the quantum predictions has to treat distinct quantum states as ontologically distinct, so psi can't just be information after all.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how severe each one is, and which pairs only look like they clash but actually don't. For each real tension, tell me what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Then end with the single revision that would buy me the most consistency, since I'd rather drop one thing than patch five. And one more request: please be careful to seperate what's actually settled in the foundations literature from what's still live and contested between people who work on this — if I hand my friend something that presents a contested reading as the consensus, that's exactly where he'll pounce.
