So I'm in a small philosophy reading group and we recently read a paper arguing that the badness of human extinction goes way beyond the deaths of the last people alive — that even people who would die naturally *before* the extinction would find their own lives drained of meaning if they knew no one would come after them, because so much of what we do (building institutions, pursuing knowledge, perfecting a craft) implicitly banks on the future. Our group got stuck on a specific wrinkle the paper didn't settle: is what matters the continuation of *human beings* per se, or the continuation of human *projects and values*, whoever carries them on? I want to turn that wrinkle into fiction rather than argue about it more.

Write a short story (roughly 1,500–2,500 words) set in this scenario: In 2071, it becomes scientifically certain — the evidence is public, replicable, and no serious person doubts it — that human reproduction has stopped working. The youngest humans ever are already born. At the same time, a long-observing alien civilization makes contact and makes an offer: they have studied humanity for centuries, they genuinely admire it, and they propose to inherit and carry on human civilization — the mathematical research programs, the unfinished symphonies, the conservation projects, the constitutional democracies, the recipes — maintaining and extending them for the next few million years, with alien members running everything. The last humans will die of old age knowing that nearly everything they care about will continue, just not in human hands. There is no third option: it's alien succession or nothing.

The story should follow one or two characters (maybe a violinist in a centuries-old orchestra, a mid-career researcher whose life's work would take another eighty years, or a new parent of one of the last children) as they wrestle with whether this arrangement counts as an "afterlife" for humanity or is just extinction with nice packaging. A few constraints: don't let either side be obviously right — I want readers genuinely torn. Don't have anyone deliver a lecture or debate the issue in dialogue; show it through decisions and small details (does the character keep practicing for a concert no human will hear? sign the treaty? bother to finish the book?). And please don't end with the aliens secretly being sinister or the infertility being a hoax — the interesting thing is the choice exactly as stated.
