I’m a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I’m putting together a proposal for a one-semester undergrad philosophy paper. My rough idea comes from debugging distributed systems: we often assume that if two names, IDs, or pointers keep referring to the same thing across retries, migrations, backups, etc., then they’re basically equivalent. But philosophy has this distinction between names that merely happen to pick something out and rigid designators that pick out the same object across possible situations. I’m wondering whether that distinction exposes a real conceptual mistake in how engineers talk about identity.

The specific angle I want is: what would it mean for an identifier to be rigid in a computational system, and would that kind of rigidity actually help clarify the philosophical notion, or just create a misleading metaphor? For example, a database primary key is stable by convention, a cryptographic hash is tied to a particular state, a DNS name survives server replacement, and a variable name can be rebound. None of these seem rigid in exactly Kripke’s sense, but they also don’t all fail in the same way. I’d like to distinguish things like stability over time, resistance to redescription, portability across implementations, and cross-world sameness, then ask which of these is closest to philosophical rigidity.

Turn this into a short research proposal, maybe 900–1200 words. It should have:

- a precise thesis question with a determinate possible answer
- an explanation of why the standard rigid designator/necessary a posteriori discussion leaves this question open
- a proposed conceptual framework, including a couple of clearly named technical distinctions of your own
- concrete but simple software examples, without turning the paper into computer science
- a month-by-month plan for a 12-week semester
- what the paper might conclude, what would count as it failing, and what parts risk becoming too broad

Please don’t include citations, a bibliography, or a literature review by author name; I’ll fill those in later. Also don’t assume I already know the terminology beyond the basics. Casual is fine, and if the whole software analogy turns out to be a bad way into the topic, say that in the proposal rather than forcing it.
