Write a dialogue between Thomas Hobbes and John Wallis in the mid-1650s, at the height of their feud. Hobbes has just put his attempt at squaring the circle into De Corpore, and Wallis has been publicly shredding it while championing his own arithmetic of infinitesimals. But dont let it stay at the level of checking each other's math -- push them down to the real disagreement: Hobbes thinks geometry is only rigorous when its grounded in body and motion, and that Wallis's infinitesimals and symbol-shuffling algebra are marks on paper with no meaning behind them; Wallis thinks Hobbes can't tell the difference between a physical picture and a proof, and that a method is justified by what it lets you derive. Both should be sharp and land real hits. Yes, Hobbes was wrong about the circle, but let him score genuine points about the difference between a method that works and a method you actually understand. Have them respond to each other's actual arguments rather than talk past each other, and end on a disagreement they genuinely can't resolve, not some polite truce.
