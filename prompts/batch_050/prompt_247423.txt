Last weekend a friend and I argued until two in the morning about a decision I had made earlier that week, and we left it unresolved. I moderate an online community of about four thousand residents of my neighborhood. Ten days ago a member began posting detailed accusations that a local landlord was knowingly renting out flats with dangerous mold, naming the building, the landlord, and eventually his home address. The claims were partly plausible and partly unverifiable, but within days other members had organized a protest outside his house, and someone slashed his tires. I removed the thread and suspended the member. My friend, who also uses the forum, says I abandoned the very principle I invoke whenever the site's owners pressure me to delete unpopular opinions, and he sent me this passage from Chapter 3 of Mill's On Liberty:

"No one pretends that actions should be as free as opinions. On the contrary, even opinions lose their immunity, when the circumstances in which they are expressed are such as to constitute their expression a positive instigation to some mischievous act. An opinion that corn-dealers are starvers of the poor, or that private property is robbery, ought to be unmolested when simply circulated through the press, but may justly incur punishment when delivered orally to an excited mob assembled before the house of a corn-dealer, or when handed about among the same mob in the form of a placard."

He reads the passage as settling the matter: a written post, circulated to readers, sits squarely on the press side of Mill's line, however alarming its content. I read the same passage and think our forum stopped being the press the moment the address went up and the crowd began forming; it had become the placard handed about among the mob. Which reading does Mill's text actually support? What, precisely, is doing the work in his distinction -- the medium, the state of the audience, the imminence of the act, or something else? Does it matter that I suspended him only after the crowd gathered, rather than when the accusations first appeared? And if the passage alone cannot decide a case like mine, what further consideration should? Please answer in British English.
