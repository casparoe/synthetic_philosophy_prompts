I cover science and ideas for a general magazine, and I want a piece I can run after a conference panel melts down over whether a lichen is one organism or a tiny committee. Write an Onion-style press release plus brief FAQ, about 600-700 words, from the invented International Board for Certifying Single Organisms, announcing a new points-based passport system for biological individuality. The targets should be the practice of drawing organism boundaries and the bureaucratic urge to make thresholds do metaphysical work, not any real living person.

Keep it deadpan and fair: the jokes should come from real criteria people actually reach for -- genetic uniformity, physiological integration, shared metabolism, immune self/nonself policing, reproduction through a single-cell bottleneck, evolutionary selection at one level, ecological role -- and from how they split on hard cases like a Portuguese man o' war, coral clones, lichen, a beehive superorganism, and a human-plus-microbiome holobiont. Explain enough in passing that a nonspecialist gets it, but a philosopher of biology should definately recognize the tradeoffs. Include at least one invented researcher quote and one sidebar about funding, databases, and why calling something an individual changes who gets grants. No citations or reading list; end with the board accidentally certifying itself as a colony.
