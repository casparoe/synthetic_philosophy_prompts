So my friends and I have been running a game of Nomic over Discord since the summer -- the game where making a move means proposing a change to the rules -- and last week we hit the classic wall: someone proposed amending the rule that governs how rules get amended, and half the group said that move is somehow illegitimate or paradoxical, while the other half said it's obviously fine because the amendment rule permits it. I went down the rabbit hole on the general problem (self-amending constitutions and so on) and I've now got a decent collection of proposed ways to dissolve or dodge the paradox of self-amendment. Here's what I have so far:

(1) The two-level answer: there's a hierarchy between ordinary rules and rules about rule-changing, so the amendment clause operates on the level below it and there's no more self-reference than when a legislature repeals its own earlier statutes; (2) the continuing-authority answer: the amendment clause authorizes changes to everything including itself, and the new clause is valid because the change was carried out under the authority of the clause as it stood at the time -- the power doesn't vanish mid-act, the way you can saw off the branch you're sitting on without the saw losing its sharpness; (3) just entrench the thing -- declare some clauses, including the amendment clause or its core, unamendable from the start, so the paradox never gets a chance to arise; (4) the two-step maneuver: never let a rule amend itself directly, instead first add a new replacement clause and then repeal the original, so every individual act of change has its source of authority outside itself; (5) the deflationary answer: the paradox is an artifact of modeling legal validity like a formal deductive system, when really validity rests on acceptance and practice, so asking whether the clause "can" amend itself is just a category mistake.

What I'm missing is a sixth. Can you give me a genuinely different way of handling the paradox -- not a rewording, combination, or special case of the five above? And since I actually have to rule on this in our game, don't just give me the clever answer: tell me whether it would actually work as a house ruling for a bunch of pedantic players, and which of the five it comes closest to in spirit.
