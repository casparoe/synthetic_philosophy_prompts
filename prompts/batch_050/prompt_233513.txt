So here's a thing that's been bugging me. The big copyright offices (the US one, and I think the UK is wrestling with the same question) will register a photograph, a collage, a photoshopped image, even one of those abstract canvases where the artist flicked paint from a ladder, but they've refused to register images made purely from text prompts into a generative model -- no human authorship, no copyright. Meanwhile the same offices are fine with registering works where a person used AI somewhere in the process, as long as they can point to the human bit. Art competitions have done something similar: AI entries chucked out even when the judges couldn't tell them apart from the human entries until it was pointed out.

What I want to know is: what theory of creativity does this practice actually presuppose? It can't just be "creativity is whatever produces aesthetically good output", because the same image gets treated differently depending on how it was made. But it also can't be "the human must have made the marks", because the photographer didn't make the marks either -- the camera did -- and Duchamp didn't make the urinal. So is the presupposition something about intention and selection, about the creative act being a series of choices a mind is responsible for? If so, why doesn't a person who writes forty drafts of a prompt, tweaks the outputs, inpaints the dodgy bits and curates the final result count as doing exactly that? Where's the line, and does the practice as it stands actually have a principled way to draw it, or is it just tracking a vibe about effort and agency?

Reconstruct the implicit theory (or theories) for me, and be honest about where the practice is underdetermined -- which bits of it strictly need the presupposed view and which bits would sit just as happily with a purely output-based, aesthetic account of creativity. Then tell me what would concretely have to change in copyright practice and competition rules if we went the other way and said creativity lives entirely in the artifact, not the process. And flag any place where the practice seems to presuppose inconsistent things at once.
