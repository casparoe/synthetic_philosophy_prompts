ok so I've been circling the s-risk literature for a while and I finally wrote down where I stand, roughly, and before I drop it in the doc my reading group is circulating I want to know whether it hangs together or whether I've accidentally committed myself to contradictory things. Quick definition so we're on the same page: by s-risk I mean a risk of future suffering on an astronomical scale — bad enough that some people argue preventing it matters more than preventing outright extinction.

Here are my commitments:

1. I'm a totalist with a symmetric axiology. Happiness and suffering sit on one scale and trade off at some finite rate. No lexical priority for suffering, no "no amount of bliss outweighs one hour of torture" clauses.

2. S-risk reduction is the top longtermist priority, clearly ahead of extinction risk reduction.

3. My grounds for 2 are plain expected value: the worst s-risk scenarios are so astronomically bad that even modest probabilites of them dominate the calculation.

4. I'm firmly anti-fanatical. Tiny probabilities of enormous stakes shouldn't get to steamroll a decision — that's why you don't pay Pascal's mugger, and I take that verdict to generalize.

5. A future containing a trillion flourishing lives and a billion lives of severe suffering is clearly net good and worth bringing about.

6. I'm honestly pretty skeptical that we can measure or compare intensities of suffering with any precision, especially across different kinds of beings.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the field. Which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do but dissolve once you think it through? Rank the real tensions worst-first rather than flagging everything as equally bad, and for each one say what the cheapest fix is and which commitment would actually have to move. If one revision would clean up most of the mess, say so.

Also: tell me what would change your verdict. Which consideration, if I brought it back to you, would reorder your ranking, and where is your analysis most likely to be leaning on something fragile? I'd rather know that than get a confident-sounding answer that collapses if I poke one assumption.
