I'm a PhD student in cognitive science, and for my qualifying paper I have to take a position on theories of humour (superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation, the usual suspects). Before I commit to defending any of them, I want to get clear on what a theory of humour even has to *do* to count as adequate, because I keep noticing that people in the literature argue past each other: one camp says a theory fails if it can't explain tickling, another says tickling is irrelevant because it's not cognitive, and so on. My supervisor said I should write the spec first and pick the theory second, which seems right but also suspiciously hard.

So: write the desiderata. What phenomena does an adequate theory of humour have to explain, which cases does it have to get right, which distinctions must it respect? Some cases I think belong on the list but I'm not sure: that incongruity alone clearly isn't sufficient (a suddenly malfunctioning door isn't funny, or not reliably), that the same joke stops being funny on the tenth hearing but some people rewatch the same comedy for years, that a joke can be *recognized* as a joke and even as competent while landing as totally unfunny, that humour at one's own expense works differently than humour at someone else's, that the same content can be hilarious in one context (roast among friends) and purely offensive in another, that little kids find things funny that adults don't and vice versa, and that failed jokes are often more socially costly than failed ordinary sentences. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration behind it and whether you think it's a genuine requirement or just the pet case of one camp. Also tell me if you suspect some subset of these desiderata can't be jointly satisfied -- e.g., I have a hunch that explaining why repetition kills jokes and explaining why rehearsed professional comedy exists might pull in opposite directions, but maybe that dissolves.

Then, briefly, score the four classical theories (superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation) against the list -- but keep this part short, since the list itself is the deliverable and I want to be able to reuse it as a yardstick even for theories you don't mention. Oh and say which single desideratum, if any, knocks out the most contenders, and which desiderata you'd let a theory negotiate on without disqualifying it.
