I read a piece in some pop-philosophy magazine that summed up Ubuntu like this: "Ubuntu is the Southern African idea that a person only exists through other people -- the self has no independent reality, you're literally constituted by your community. That means the group always comes before the individual, so Ubuntu thinkers reject the Western idea of individual rights, and it's why South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission went with forgiveness instead of punishment. Critics point out this makes Ubuntu hard to square with human rights law." I'm drawn to relational views of the self, so I want this to be right, but the jump from "persons are made through relationships" to "individuals have no independent reality and no rights" feels too fast. Which parts are accurate, which overstate or conflate things, and does the TRC bit actually follow from the rest?
