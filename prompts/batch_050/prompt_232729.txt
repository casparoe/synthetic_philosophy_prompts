Okay so here's my situation, and me and my partner have been going in circles about it for weeks.

I live in a state that will go for Candidate A by like 20 points, no contest, everyone knows it. But I'm also somewhat known locally — I run a big community organization, and people genuinely ask me who I'm voting for, and I've watched it actually sway some of them. I can't stand either major candidate, honestly. A is clearly less bad than B in my view, but the only person I actually believe in is a third party candidate who will get maybe 2%.

Here's what's twisting me up. My actual ballot doesn't matter, mathematically — the state is decided, my vote is just a message to myself and the universe. But what I say publicly kind of does matter, because a few dozen people might follow my lead, and some of them live across the border in a swing state where it genuinely counts.

So my plan: vote third party (since my ballot is purely symbolic anyway, it should express what I actually believe), but tell people publicly I'm voting for A (since that's the recommendation that does good where the race is real). Seperate levers for seperate effects.

My partner says this is rotten. Her argument is basically: if voting third party is the right thing for me, then it's the right thing for them too, and I'm just outsourcing the "wasted vote" guilt to other people while keeping my own hands clean. Also she says I'm lying to people who trust me, and I'd be furious if someone I looked up to did this to me. My response is that a ballot and a recommendation are two totally different tools — one has zero causal effect here, one has a lot — and I should use each one where it does the most good. It's not hypocrisy, it's just... knowing which of your actions are symbolic and which aren't.

Who's right? And I think the deeper issue is that me and her definately disagree about what a vote even *is*. Like is it a bet on the outcome, a statement of your values, a move in a team sport? Until we settle that I think we're just talking past each other. So any thoughts on that underlying question would help too — what is a vote for, and does the answer change depending on whether anyone's watching?
