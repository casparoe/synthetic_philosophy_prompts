You are a satirist with a decent background in early modern philosophy, writing for a university department newsletter that runs a mock op-ed each issue. I'd like you to write a Swiftian modest proposal, roughly 600-700 words, in British English, and I'd like the humour to land for someone who actually knows their Pascal while still being funny to someone who doesn't.

The setup: the author is a fictional Minister for Administrative Efficiency who has recently read Pascal's Pensées and taken away exactly one lesson. Pascal observes that justice is endlessly contested -- everyone has their own account of it, it shifts with climate and custom, three degrees of latitude overturns it -- whereas force is obvious, undeniable, and universally recognised. And because justice could not be made powerful enough to command obedience on its own, societies ended up treating force as just, so at least everyone would obey something. The minister reads this not as a diagnosis but as an efficiency saving. If verdicts in practice track who can enforce them, why are we paying for courts, juries, disclosure, precedent, appeals? The proposal: abolish the justice system and replace litigation with a straightforward assessment of which party can bring more force to bear. Simple, transparent, no backlog.

Some features you could build in (feel free to ignore or add your own): the rebranding exercise (the Courts Service becomes something like the Department of Outcome Certification); the appeals process reduced to a rematch, best of three; the abolition of precedent on the grounds that might, unlike case law, does not accumulate; the minister's serene confidence that the proposal is itself just, since the ministry that proposes it happens to control the police; a line about how lawyers will be retrained as weigh-in officials. Keep it completely deadpan -- no winking at the reader. The irony should be legible purely from the content: the reader should feel that Pascal would be quietly horrified, because the minister has taken a descriptive claim about how human societies work and run it as a policy recommendation, and has somehow also managed to invert the bit where Pascal says justice and force need to be joined together rather than one swapped for the other.

Before you write the piece, give me a short brainstorm (a few bullet points) of which bits of legal practice you're going to send up and how each one maps onto something Pascal actually says or implies. Then write the proposal itself, with a title and the minister's invented name and job title at the top. Think carefully about the voice -- it should sound like a genuine Whitehall consultation paper, bored and reasonable, which is what makes it alarming. British English throughout, obviously.
