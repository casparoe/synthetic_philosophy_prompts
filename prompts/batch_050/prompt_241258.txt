so i'm drafting a post on takeoff speeds for our little research group's blog and before i publish i want someone to check whether my positions actually cohere, because i've sort of accumulated them from different places over the years. here's where i've landed:

(1) a fast takeoff is more likely than not -- once AI systems can do AI research better than we can, you get a compounding feedback loop, so i'd put decent probability on going from roughly-human-level to strongly superhuman in weeks to a few months

(2) i reject the "intelligence as a single dial" picture. capabilities come in lumpy, domain-specific bundles, and being exceptionally good at one thing doesn't imply being good at adjacent things

(3) the classic compute overhang scenario doesn't worry me much, since the binding constraint is algorithmic, not hardware -- dumping ten times the GPUs on a pre-transformative system wouldn't buy you a discontinuity

(4) i'm moderately optimistic about alignment, mostly because we'll get to iterate empirically on systems of gradually increasing capability, the way aviation safety improved

(5) my concrete bet is that the first transformatively capable systems will be wildly superhuman at persuasion and at writing code while still being mediocre at long-horizon autonomous wet-lab research

(6) i expect we'd get clear advance warning of an intelligence explosion from macro-level economic indicators -- GDP growth, software sector wages, that sort of thing

so: which of these genuinely pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and what's the cheapest revision that restores consistency? please rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally bad, and for each one say what would actually have to give. don't just tell me to "be less confident" across the board, that's a cop out
