I've been getting into arguments about music with friends lately and I've landed on a position I want stress-tested. My view: music isn't sad in itself at all. A minor key sounds mournful to us purely because of cultural training -- we grew up hearing minor chords at funerals and in sad scenes in films, and if we'd grown up somewhere with totally different musical conventions we'd hear the same piece completely differently. The sadness is all in our conditioning, nothing about the sounds themselves.

So: what would count against this? I want the actual defeaters, not just a summary of the debate. Which findings or arguments should genuinely move me -- things like whether certain emotional responses to music show up in people with no exposure to Western music, whether tempo and pitch contour do anything in non-musical contexts (voices, animal calls), whether babies or animals respond in ways conditioning can't explain, and whether the position can even handle cases like a piece that still feels sad after I know its whole cultural history. Please rank these from "if this holds up, I'm wrong" down to "annoying but survivable," and be honest about which of them have decent evidence behind them and which are just plausible-sounding. Don't hedge -- take a stand on whether my view survives the best available counterevidence or not.
