ok so for my jurisprudence class we have to write up a one-page "position statement" on the Hart-Fuller debate that the seminar leader will then try to pull apart, and I want to stress-test mine before I hand it in. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm a legal positivist. Whether something counts as valid law is a factual matter about social sources, and there's no necessary connection between law and morality. The whole point of positivism is the separability thesis.
2. On the Hart-Fuller exchange itself I side with Hart: Fuller's eight desiderata (generality, prospectivity, clarity, congruence, etc.) are conditions of *efficacy*, not a genuine "inner morality." A wicked regime that follows them scrupulously is just a more efficient wicked regime, and its rules are fully law. Poisoning can be done well or badly too.
3. That said, on the grudge informer case I think the postwar German court got the right answer: the Nazi-era statute the wife denounced her husband under was so wicked it simply wasn't law, so convicting her didn't require any retroactive lawmaking.
4. I'm a hardliner on retroactivity in criminal matters: if conduct was lawful when done, punishing it later is never justified, full stop.
5. I also think every legal system necessarily *claims* legitimate moral authority over its subjects — that's just part of what law is. And I don't see this as conflicting with my positivism.
6. Hart's rule of recognition is basically the right account of validity for a system like the UK or US.

What I want: go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are. For each real tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be — I don't want to abandon the whole positivist picture if I can help it, but I also don't want to know in advance which commitment you'd cut, just which ones *have* to be reconsidered. Ideally end with the single revision that cleans up the most. Also, since I'm going to get grilled on this: tell me what would change your verdict — like, which consideration or argument would make you land on a different audit, or where your reasoning here is most likely to fall apart.
