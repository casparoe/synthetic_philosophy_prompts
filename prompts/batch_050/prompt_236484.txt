At a jurisprudence workshop last month I defended a position I now want to stress-test properly. The issue is the old puzzle about blackmail: disclosing a damaging but true fact about someone is, in most jurisdictions, perfectly legal, and asking someone for money is perfectly legal, yet offering to keep the fact quiet in exchange for payment is a serious crime almost everywhere. The proposition I want your credence on is this: blackmail is genuinely wrong rather than merely criminalized by convention, and there exists a principled explanation of its wrongness that does not require denying that the two component acts, taken separately, are permissible. My own credence is roughly 0.75; a colleague whose judgment I generally trust puts hers at 0.3.

Give me your own credence, decomposed rather than bare. At minimum, I want the decomposition to include: the probability that the component acts really are independently permissible (rather than the threat already being wrongful coercion on its own); the probability that wrongness can emerge from the structure of a transaction even when its components are blameless, so that the conjunction of two permissible acts can be impermissible; and the probability that the leading candidate diagnosis -- that the blackmailer wrongfully leverages a vulnerability by selling the victim protection against a disclosure the blackmailer alone controls -- actually succeeds where rival diagnoses fail. Add whatever further terms you think the credence genuinely depends on, assign each a rough probability with one line of justification, and show how they combine into your final number.

Here is the wrinkle I most want priced into your estimate. Imagine a jurisdiction that legalizes a fully transparent, standardized service: an algorithm identifies true, lawfully obtained, reputation-damaging facts about people and offers each subject a fixed-price suppression option, with rates published in advance, no discretion in whom it targets, and no threatening language at all. Every feature that standard accounts point to as the locus of the wrong -- the menace, the furtiveness, the personalized squeeze -- has been engineered out; what remains is the bare exchange of money for silence. State whether your credence changes for this case, include a term in the decomposition for whether the wrong of blackmail survives the removal of those features or depends on them, and give the resulting number. Then tell me: in which sub-question does the disagreement between my 0.75 and my colleague's 0.3 most likely live, which term in your own decomposition are you least confident in, and what argument would move it most? I am not asking for a survey of positions -- I want a committed answer: definite numbers, a definite verdict on the standardized service, and the reasons that carry the weight.
