So my friend and I had this huge arguement last weekend that's still bugging me, and I don't want a round two with her, I want a post-mortem on why it went absolutely nowhere.

Background: she grew up in a country (won't say which, doesn't matter) where in her telling you basically get nothing done at a government office without bringing something — envelopes, bottles, gifts for the kids, whatever — and where who your family knows decides what school or job your kid gets. Her view is that calling this "corruption" is just Westerners universalizing their own hangups: it's a system of personal reciprocal obligations, everyone understands the rules, the gifts keep relationships alive, and Scandinavian-style anonymous bureaucracy is the actual historical outlier, not her country.

I pushed back: whatever you call it, a system where access to public services depends on gifts screws over poor people and anyone without connections, and the "it's our culture" line mostly comes from people doing well out of it.

Her counter-move is where we stalled: she said every society runs on gifts and favors around public institutions — tipping, lobbying, campaign donations, legacy admissions, job referrals — and Westerners just draw the corruption line exactly where their own practices stop, then call everything on their side of the line tradition or politics. I said those cases are different because they're disclosed and regulated, and campaign money doesn't officially buy specific decisions. She laughed at the word officially. And around we went for another hour.

So diagnose this for me, using what each of us actually argued. Is the disagreement verbal — do we just mean different things by the word corruption? Is it empirical — would we actually agree if we knew whether gift-based systems produce worse outcomes than lobbying-based ones? Or is it normative bedrock — whether impartial public office is a universal value or a local invention? My own guess is it's the last one and everything else was proxy fighting, but I could be wrong about that. Also: is her "you just draw the line arbitrarily" move a genuinely good argument or a trap that dissolves every distinction? And what would count as progress here — a distinction, a test, some evidence both of us would have to take seriously — or is this one of those arguments that only ends when somebody changes the subject?
