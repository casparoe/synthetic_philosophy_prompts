Quick question from my Indian philosophy class that's been bugging me. Say someone tells you there's this animal called a gavaya that's basically like a cow but wild, with such-and-such markings. You've never seen one. Then you're out walking in the forest, you spot an animal that matches the description, and you just know: that's a gavaya. Or same thing with me reading a field guide description of a hornbill and then instantly recognizing one on a trip. Now, what kind of knowing is that? It doesn't seem like plain perception, because if nobody had told me anything I'd just see a weird cow. It doesn't feel like testimony either, since the person who told me wasn't pointing at this animal. And calling it inference seems off because I didn't run through any steps, it was immediate. Is there an established name for this specific way of coming to know something, where a prior similarity-description "clicks" with something you're now perceiving? And if there is, I want to know how it's actually carved up -- like, does it still count if I come up with the comparison myself instead of being told? Is the knowledge really produced at the moment of recognition, or was it there latently since the telling? I'd rather get a map of the nearest concepts and where this one diverges from each than just a bare label.
