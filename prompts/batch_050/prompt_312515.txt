ok so longtime LW lurker here, occasional commenter, and ive been chewing on something and want an audit. i keep finding myself asserting all of the following and i want to know if the package coheres or whether i'm laundering a contradiction:

1. GOFAI failed because of the frame problem or something real close to it — you can't hand-write all the "everything else stays the same" facts, so any system built by enumerating what changes and what doesn't was always doomed.
2. LLMs basically handle open-ended relevance fine. They know what matters in a situation, what stays fixed when something changes, what's worth mentioning and what goes without saying, without anyone having told them. So the frame problem turns out to be solvable by learning from a giant pile of data — no special machinery, no explicit frame axioms, it just falls out.
3. LLMs don't have world models, they just predict the next token.
4. Alignment is hard precisely because you can't enumerate everything that could go wrong — any explicit list of rules or prohibitions has gaps a strong optimizer slips through. (this is like half the argument of the sequences as i understand it.)
5. The right epistemic standard for serious thinking is explicitness — you should be able to give the gears-level reasons for your beliefs, say what would change your mind, articulate why you're doing what you're doing, all the virtue stuff.

Here's what makes me uneasy, though i might be wrong about where the problem is: 2 and 4 feel structurally identical but i want them to come out opposite. If learning-from-data dissolves the "can't enumerate what's relevant" problem for cognition, why doesn't it dissolve the "can't enumerate what's bad" problem for values? And if the answer is that LLMs only track relevance inside the training distribution and fall apart in genuinely novel situations — which, honestly, matches what i see when i push them on weird hypotheticals — then isn't 2 massively oversold and i should downgrade it to "handles relevance in-distribution"?

And 1 vs 5 is bugging me too. Humans seem to handle the frame problem via whatever the relevance-realization crowd calls it, which is exactly the part of cognition that resists being made explicit — nobody can say why they noticed the thing that mattered. If the most important cognitive capacity is also the least legible one, what does that do to 5 as a standard?

So: which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like it, ordered by severity, and for each real one what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me. And please don't just tell me to read the literature — steelman the tensions the way i'd actually run into them.
