Here's my opening move: the paradox only goes through because Sen encodes rights as restrictions on the social preference over outcomes. But that's the wrong model of what a right is. My right over whether I read some book isn't a claim that certain outcomes be ranked in a certain way -- it's just the fact that the choice is mine to make. If each person exercises their protected choice and we land somewhere Pareto-inferior, nothing has gone wrong, because the "better" outcome was never on the menu: it would have required someone choosing otherwise, which was their call, not society's. Rights shape which outcomes are reachable, they don't rank outcomes. So Sen hasn't shown a conflict between Pareto and liberty, he's shown that one particular way of formalizing liberty creates a conflict, and we should formalize it differently. Your move.
