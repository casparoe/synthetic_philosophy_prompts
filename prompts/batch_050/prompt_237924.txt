Before I go read up on the ontology of musical works, I want to know what I should actually be demanding from a theory in that area. Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of what a musical work is -- and of when two performances, recordings, or scores count as being of the same work -- has to deliver. Which cases it must get right, which distinctions it must respect, which puzzles it has to dissolve rather than just stipulate away, with the case or intuition behind each requirement, and flag any pairs of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. Cases I specifically want the list to be tested against: studio tracks that were never performed and arguably couldn't be (electronic music assembled in a DAW), covers that radically rearrange the original yet are still plainly the same song (Johnny Cash's version of Hurt), jazz standards where the written artifact is just a lead sheet and the "work" seems to live in the playing tradition, works existing in multiple sanctioned versions (orchestral reductions, composer-supervised revisions), and sampling/mashups where one track is partly built out of another. Fix the purpose for me: the theory only needs to be good enough to ground ordinary talk about works, attribution, and rough copyright intuitions -- it doesn't have to satisfy a Platonist's metaphysical scruples -- so say which desiderata drop out given that purpose and which are non-negotiable. Then briefly score the main contenders against the list: the work-as-discovered-abstract-type view, sonicism, and the view that works are created by composers at a time. One more thing: keep seperate what's basically settled among philosophers of music from what's genuinely contested, and don't present someones preferred view as the consensus.
