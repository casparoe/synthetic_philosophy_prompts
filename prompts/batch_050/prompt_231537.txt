I want to settle wether "the Middle Ages" names something real in the past or is just a label later people slapped on a stretch of time, and every discussion I find starts by arguing the cases directly. I want the spec first. Write the desiderata: what would a theory of historical periodization have to deliver for something like the Renaissance or the Scientific Revolution to count as a genuine feature of history rather than a retrospective invention? Requirements it presumably has to meet, with the case or consideration behind each -- for instance, it has to say something about the fact that nobody in 1300 experienced themselves as living in the "middle" of anything, while the Italian humanists genuinely did think a new age had begun; it has to handle that periods are often defined by what they lack; and it has to survive the fact that any boundary looks arbitrary if you zoom in close enough. Then the part I care most about: which desiderata are in tension with each other? I have a feeling that "a real period must track what contemporaries actually experienced" and "a real period can track large-scale changes nobody at the time noticed" can't both be core requirements. And say which single requirement, if accepted, knocks out the most common defenses of the standard period scheme.
