I just finished a grad seminar on free will and moral responsibility, and we spent three weeks on Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities. I got into a long argument with my roommate afterwards that I couldn't settle, and now I want to turn it into a proposal for a one-semester independent study (roughly a 7000-word paper at the end).

Here's my half-formed thought. In the classic setup, Black has a device that would force Jones to act if Jones showed the relevant prior sign of deciding otherwise, but the sign never appears, so Jones acts on his own and seems responsible despite lacking alternatives. What bugs me: the whole case seems to hinge on Black being able to *detect something before the decision happens*. If the detectable sign really comes before the decision, then it looks like the question just moves back a step -- couldn't Jones have avoided the sign? And if the sign just *is* the decision itself, it's hard to see how the device could get in front of it. So the case seems to face a fork: either Jones has an alternative after all (just an earlier one), or the intervener's device is secretly assuming something like determinism to make the timing work, in which case the argument can't be used against incompatibilists who deny determinism in the first place. My roommate thinks this is too quick and that the defenders have answers, but neither of us could say exactly what the best version of the reply is.

I also want the proposal to state upfront what would count as the project *failing*, e.g. if it turns out the dilemma only works against one particular construction of the case and the others escape it. I want you to commit: pick the strongest reconstruction of the defender's reply yourself rather than listing options and leaving me to choose, and say plainly whether you think the project as I've framed it has a real thesis in it or whether I'm conflating two separate worries and should split the paper.
