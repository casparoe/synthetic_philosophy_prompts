I'm teaching a seminar on Jaspers and his four kinds of guilt from The Question of German Guilt (criminal, political, moral, metaphysical). Take these six people and rank them on each kind of guilt seperately -- so four rankings -- with a sentence or two of justification per ranking: (1) a judge who sentenced people to death under the Nazi racial laws, (2) a civil servant who joined the party in 1933 purely to keep his job and ended up processing deportation paperwork, (3) a voter who voted Nazi in 1932 mostly out of economic fear and later regretted it privately, (4) someone who hid a Jewish family in her attic but otherwise stayed quiet and kept her head down, (5) a German born in 1948, (6) a present-day German taxpayer whose taxes partly fund Holocaust remembrance. Then the part I actually care about: does your ranking of (5) and (6) on political guilt commit you to saying guilt can be inherited across generations, or does Jaspers's scheme let you rank them above zero without that? If you find your four rankings contradict each other anywhere, point at the spot instead of smoothing it over.
