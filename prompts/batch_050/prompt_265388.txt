I work on problems of parameter identifiability and symmetry in machine learning models -- things like neuron-permutation symmetries in multilayer networks, gauge-like redundancy in parameterizations, quotienting model classes by equivalence relations, and the question of when two distinct parameter settings represent the same function versus genuinely different functions. I'd like you to translate a debate in the philosophy of physics into this vocabulary, and then give me an honest account of what didn't survive the translation.

The debate is the hole argument about spacetime substantivalism. Let me state it compactly so we're working from the same text rather than your memory of a textbook. Assume general relativity is formulated as fields (a metric, plus matter fields) on a smooth manifold of points. Consider two field configurations on that manifold related by a diffeomorphism -- a smooth remapping of the points -- which is the identity everywhere outside some bounded region (the "hole") and differs from the identity inside it. Outside the hole the two configurations agree exactly; inside it they differ. Now: (1) If the equations of the theory are diffeomorphism-covariant, then if the first configuration is a solution, so is the second. (2) But the two configurations can be chosen to agree on all data up to some time and diverge afterward, so if they count as physically distinct, the theory fails to determine the future from the past even though nothing observable inside the hole seems to distinguish them. (3) The standard escape is to deny the distinctness: configurations related by a diffeomorphism are two mathematical presentations of one physical situation, which is to say the manifold points carry no identity of their own beyond what the fields define -- a blow against "substantivalism," the view that the spacetime points are real entities over and above the material goings-on.

Here is the translation task. Restate each of the three steps, and the substantivalist position they threaten, in the vocabulary I use: parameterized models, equivalence classes of parameters, symmetries of the loss or of the dynamics, identifiability from data, well-posedness of prediction. Where the correspondence is clean, say so explicitly and precisely; where you have to stretch a concept -- for instance, whether "agreement on data up to a time" really maps onto anything in a typical identifiability argument, or whether the manifold-to-parameters analogy inverts the usual direction of the map between model and world -- flag the stretch rather than smoothing it over.

Then the residue report. First: is there any part of the hole argument that has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? My suspicion is that in machine learning, quotienting by a symmetry is just good housekeeping -- nobody agonizes over whether the "pre-quotient" parameterization was tracking something real -- whereas the philosophers treat the question of whether spacetime points exist as genuinely open. Say whether that difference shows my framework is missing a distinction (between redundancy in our representation and redundancy in the world itself) or whether it shows the philosophical dispute is an artifact of taking one particular mathematical presentation too seriously. Second: the substantivalist has a reply -- that the two configurations inside the hole differ in which point plays which role, and this is a real difference even if no observation could detect it. Translate that reply as faithfully as you can, and tell me whether, once translated, it still has any force or collapses into a preference for a redundant parameterization.
