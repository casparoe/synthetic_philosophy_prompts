2. Internal political dysfunction -- the third-century crisis, endless civil wars, emperors with an average shelf life of a few years, no stable succession mechanism.
3. Economic decline -- currency debasement, inflation, overtaxation of the peasantry, trade networks shrinking.
4. Plague and climate -- the Antonine and Cyprian plagues, then the Late Antique Little Ice Age wrecking agricultural output.
5. Lead poisoning -- the old chestnut about lead pipes and lead-lined wine vessels softening the Roman brain.
6. Christianity sapping civic virtue -- Gibbon's line, roughly, that otherworldliness replaced civic-military spirit.
7. There was no fall -- the "transformation" school: the empire morphed into successor kingdoms and Byzantium, and "fall" is a dramatic label for a slow, uneven change.
