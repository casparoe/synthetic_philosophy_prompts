I write a science-and-ideas column for a general-interest magazine, and I am drafting a piece on factored cognition: the proposal, usually credited to the AI safety researcher Paul Christiano, that a person's best effort at a hard question could be approximated by a large tree of short-lived copies of that person, each one pondering only a small sub-question for a limited time and passing an answer back up the tree, with the original at the root integrating the results. The scheme is known as HCH, short for Humans Consulting HCH. In my draft I leaned on an analogy that I now suspect is doing more argumentative work than it earns, and I want an audit before I file.

The analogy is this: HCH is a well-run bureaucracy of clones. Just as a capable chief executive can, through delegation, preside over a conglomerate whose problems no single mind could grasp in full — each division head working from summaries, trusting subordinates' reports without re-deriving them — the person at the root of the tree can arrive at answers beyond their unaided reach, because every internal node is literally them, applying their own judgment and values to a digestible fragment of the problem. From this picture my draft draws two conclusions: first, that the tree's output inherits the root person's values more or less automatically, since every delegate is a copy; and second, that the scheme's main difficulties are the familiar managerial ones — communication overhead, lossy summarization, drift between what the boss wanted and what the staff delivered — which we already know how to mitigate, rather than anything philosophically novel.

Tell me how far the analogy holds. Please structure your reply in the following sections, in this order:

**The correspondence table** (roughly 200 words): a table mapping the elements of the bureaucracy onto the elements of HCH — what plays the role of the CEO, the division heads, the memos, the performance reports, the org chart, the hiring process — with one line per row noting how tight the fit is.

**Where it holds** (roughly 150 words): the features of delegation that the analogy genuinely captures and that my piece is entitled to keep.

**Where it breaks** (roughly 300 words): the disanalogies, explicitly separating those that bear on my two conclusions from those that don't. Please consider at least: the executive who has never met her staff versus copies who share a lifetime of memory up to the moment of branching; the fact that real delegates have careers and private interests while a copy's motives are whatever the copying process happens to preserve; and whether the delegation relation in HCH is recursive in a way a corporate hierarchy is not, and what that does to the managerial-mitigation claim.

**Verdict on my conclusions** (roughly 150 words): for each of my two conclusions, state whether it survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time.

**A better analogy, if there is one** (roughly 100 words): if the bureaucracy framing misleads more than it illuminates, propose a replacement for a general readership — or argue that no everyday analogy should be trusted here and the concept is better explained without one.
