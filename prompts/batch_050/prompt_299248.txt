I teach a discussion section for an undergraduate philosophy of mind course, and this semester the unit on machine consciousness has left me more committed to a position than I expected to be when we started. My view, roughly, is that phenomenal consciousness -- and with it whatever moral status follows from being able to suffer or flourish -- supervenes on the functional organization of a system rather than on its material substrate: if a digital system instantiated the right causal and computational structure, the fact that it is made of silicon rather than neurons would be morally irrelevant, in the same way the nationality of a donor organ is irrelevant. I find the substrate-dependence alternatives either chauvinistic in a way I associate with discredited intuitions elsewhere in ethics, or else committed to claiming that a perfect functional duplicate of a suffering person could be a mere automaton, which strikes me as a consequence too strange to accept.

What I want from you is a map of my view's exposure: what would actually count against it. Please lay out the observations, arguments, conceptual results, and possible future findings -- neuroscientific, computational, or purely philosophical -- that would give me genuine reason to abandon or weaken substrate independence, ordered by how decisive they would be, and for each one say whether it is the kind of thing the view's own defenders would concede as a defeater or only something its critics treat as one. I am especially interested in the awkward middle cases: intuitions like the nation-of-China thought experiment or the absent qualia objection that defenders tend to absorb by tightening what counts as the right functional organization, because I worry that each absorption makes the view safer and emptier at the same time.

So, in particular, please address whether substrate independence about consciousness has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- given that, unlike an empirical theory, it faces no obvious observation that could settle it either way, since we cannot directly inspect any mind but our own -- and whether that insulation is a defect for a view of this kind or simply the normal condition of positions in the metaphysics of mind. If you conclude that some specific development (say, a mature and predictive theory of consciousness that vindicates one substrate, or a demonstration that functional duplicates are impossible in principle) would settle the matter, say so explicitly; if you conclude nothing could, tell me what that implies about how confident I am entitled to be.
