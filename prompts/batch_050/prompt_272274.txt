So I'm supervising a PhD student this semester who's writing on personal identity, and we've ended up in this running argument about what her own actual view is. She thinks of herself as a straight-down-the-line Lockean -- memory is what makes you you, full stop -- but her verdicts on thought experiments keep drifting, and I told her I'd get a second opinion before our next meeting. Here are the cases she's ruled on so far. A man gets total permanent amnesia after a car crash, loses every episodic memory of his life, but keeps his skills, temperament, sense of humor, and general knowledge: she says he's obviously still the same person, and his wife shouldn't treat him as a stranger. A single-use teleporter scans your body, destroys it, and builds one molecule-perfect replica on Mars with all your memories intact: she says you survive, end of story, and she'd step in herself. A freak accident swaps two people's entire psychologies -- memories, character, everything -- between their bodies: she says each person goes where their memories went, so the person who wakes up in the other body is the original person. And her seminar covered Locke and Reid's brave officer last month, and on the classic chain setup -- the general remembers being the officer but not being the flogged schoolboy, though as officer he remembered the flogging -- she sides with the chain reading: overlapping links of memory are enough, you don't need a direct memory connection at every step. So far so coherent, I thought.

Then last week I gave her a new case and she went quiet, which is why I'm writing to you. The teleporter works exactly as before -- scans you, destroys the original -- but due to a software glitch it builds two replicas on Mars, call them Lefty and Righty, both waking up with all your memories, both sincerely believing they stepped into the booth on Earth five minutes ago, both equally continuous with you psychologically. Neither one is better made or closer or first off the assembly line. My question: what do you predict she'll say, and more importantly, what's the actual rule she's been running all along such that her first four verdicts fall out of it? I can think of at least three candidate principles that fit her record perfectly well -- one where psychological continuity broadly construed (memory chains plus character) is both necessary and sufficient and branching is just irrelevant, one where continuity matters but identity requires a unique continuer so branching kills you, and one where identity goes to the best candidate and ties are fatal -- and they all diverge on Lefty and Righty. Tell me which you think best captures her, defend the prediction, and then give me the one further case you'd put to her that would settle which rule she really holds, because I definately want to win this argument at our next meeting.
