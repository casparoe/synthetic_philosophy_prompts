I am preparing a seminar on Thomas Metzinger's self-model theory of subjectivity, and in particular on his notion of phenomenal transparency: the claim that we normally do not experience our self-model as a model but "look through" it, so that its content appears as simply given reality. As evidence that the phenomenal self is a constructed and transparent representation rather than a direct given, my seminar handout already covers the following cases: the rubber hand illusion, in which synchronous stroking relocates the felt location of a hand into a visible dummy; experimentally induced out-of-body and full-body illusions, where the spatial unity of self and body comes apart; phantom limb phenomena, where a body part persists phenomenally after amputation because the model is not updated; depersonalization and derealization, in which the self-model or world-model becomes partially opaque and is experienced as unreal; and drug- or meditation-induced ego dissolution, where the boundaries of the phenomenal self temporarily weaken or vanish. I would like you to supply one further phenomenon, drawn from psychopathology, experimental psychology, neurology, or ordinary experience, that is not on this list and is not merely a variant, special case, or combination of items already on it. Please state explicitly what makes it genuinely different from the five cases above. Then, and this is the part I care most about, assess whether your case actually supports the stronger transparency thesis rather than the weaker claim that the phenomenal self is constructed and plastic. Several of the listed cases arguably show only that the self-model can be manipulated or experienced as artificial, which is in tension with transparency rather than evidence for it; I want your honest judgment about where your proposed case falls on that axis, and what it would take for any phenomenon to count as direct evidence that a model is transparent as opposed to merely malleable. Please write your answer as continuous prose only, the way one would write an email or a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
