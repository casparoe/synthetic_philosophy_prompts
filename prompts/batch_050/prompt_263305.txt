write me a fake FAQ page, about 550-700 words, for the website of the Society of Music Lovers -- the outfit from Thomson's violinist thought experiment, the one that finds a dying concert violinist whose blood type matches yours, kidnaps you, and hooks his circulatory system up to your kidneys for nine months. in this world they've gone semi-legit: glossy site, cheerful stock-photo energy, and a legal department that answers your questions in that soothing corporate FAQ voice.

some questions the FAQ should field, in its own voice:
- "I woke up connected to a violinist. Can I just unplug myself?"
- "Nine months is a really long time. Isn't there a shorter option?" (they keep stressing it's ONLY nine months)
- "I was careful though, I locked all my windows. they came in through the chimney" (the responsibility question)
- "Couldn't the violinist be transferred to someone more willing?"
- "What happens when the nine months are up?"

the satire target is the argumentative slide the thought experiment was built to test: the violinist has a right to life, unplugging him would kill him, therefore you may not unplug. the FAQ takes that slide completely seriously and keeps hitting the seam where it creaks -- the difference between a right to life and a right to the use of someone else's kidneys, the gap between what would be lovely of you (good samaritan behavior) and what you can be made to do, killing vs. letting die, whether how careful you were changes what you owe. don't have the FAQ state these objections out loud; let the deadpan answers expose them the way real fine print accidentally admits things.

stay deadpan the whole way through, no winking, no "obviously this is monstrous" asides -- the reader should feel the objections surfacing on their own. bonus points for a testimonials section from satisfied former hosts and a closing note about the Society's exciting new matching program for people who happen to have two good kidneys.
