So my friend Marcus is getting married next month and I'm the best man. Marcus did his PhD on promising (I did mine on moral uncertainty, we met in grad school) and his whole dissertation defended a pretty hardline expectation view: promises bind only because and only insofar as they create reliance in the promisee, so a promise to someone who doesn't actually count on you is normatively inert. Which is... a funny position to take into a wedding, since he's about to make a bunch of vows in front of 100 people, half of whom are his fiancee's family who just think vows are sacred, full stop. He knows I'm doing the toast and joked that I should "defend the institution." Write me a toast, like 4-5 minutes, that gently riffs on the puzzle of why promises bind at all, pokes fun at Marcus's view (ideally trap him in some small paradox with his own vows), but ultimately lands sincere -- the room should end up feeling like marriage vows matter for a reason his dissertation can't fully explain, without me strawmanning him. Assume a mixed audience: a few philosophers who'll catch a Scanlon or Raz reference, mostly normal people.
