Draft the joint statement that two philosophers of religion could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on middle knowledge. By middle knowledge I mean the Molinist claim that, logically prior to any decree about what to create, God knows, for every possible free creature and every possible circumstance, what that creature would freely do in it. One party defends this; the other presses the grounding objection: these counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are not made true by God's nature, not by the creature (who may never exist), and not by the creature's actual choice (which comes later in the explanatory order), so nothing grounds them and there is nothing for God to know. The statement should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, concerning the logical ordering of God's natural, middle, and free knowledge, what middle knowledge is supposed to secure for providence that mere foreknowledge would not, and what both sides count as libertarian freedom; (2) a table of residual disagreements, each side's position worded so that side would accept it — I expect disputes over whether such counterfactuals need truthmakers at all, whether brute necessary truths about what creatures would freely do are coherent, whether grounding them in the creature's eventual choice is circular or benign, and whether Molinist providence amounts to genuine control or to luck; (3) the cruxes; and (4) for each crux, an argument or thought experiment both parties accept as bearing on it, with each side's verdict on that case recorded in advance. If part of the dispute is verbal — for instance, the two sides using 'ground' or 'truthmaker' differently — identify which part and why. Finally, distinguish what is common ground in the wider literature (e.g., that the grounding objection is the standard objection to Molinism) from what is genuinely contested, and do not present either party's position as the consensus.
