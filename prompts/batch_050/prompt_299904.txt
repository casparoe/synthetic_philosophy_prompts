I've always taken Nozick's experience machine to be close to a knockdown argument against hedonist and other mental-state theories of well-being: most people wouldn't plug in for good, so how well a life goes can't just be a matter of how it feels from the inside. But lately I want to stress-test that. What would actually count against my reading? Don't just tell me that some people would plug in -- I want to know which features of the setup might be doing the real work instead of a judgment about well-being: status quo bias and the sheer weirdness of abandoning your actual life, reasonable distrust that the machine will deliver as promised, the difference between choosing to plug in once versus being told youve been plugged in all along, and the possibility that my refusal is about my projects and the people I'd be stranding rather than about my own welfare at all. If the intuition collapsed once you controlled for those -- say, most people would plug in under suitably cleaned-up versions of the case -- would that sink the anti-hedonist argument, or just demote it to one intuition among others? And be honest about the meta-level too: what does this thought experiment actually risk, has it been insulated by qualifications, and what finding or consideration would change your own verdict about how much it shows?
