So I've been reading up on chain-of-thought faithfulness in LLMs and I'm stuck on something that probably has an obvious answer. The standard worry is that a model's written-out reasoning might not reflect the process that actually produced its answer. But here's what I don't get: suppose we check the chain-of-thought itself and it's airtight -- every step genuinely follows from the ones before it, and the final answer really is entailed by the reasoning. In what sense could that reasoning still be "unfaithful"? It feels like once the argument checks out logically, asking whether it "matches the model's real internal process" becomes unfalsifiable, since we can't exactly watch the weights think. So is the whole faithfulness literature really just a roundabout way of catching models that make logical mistakes, dressed up in scarier language? And if there's more to it than that, what would faithfulness even concretely amount to beyond internal validity -- like, what observation could distinguish a faithful valid chain-of-thought from an unfaithful one? I suspect I'm conflating two things but I can't see which.
