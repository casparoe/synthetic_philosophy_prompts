Write a satirical internal memo, roughly 450-550 words, from the management of the Cartesian Theater — the small screening room in the center of the brain where, on the traditional picture, all sensory information arrives to be watched by the inner observer — announcing that the facility is being shut down and its staff made redundant following the company's transition to a Multiple Drafts Model of operations. The memo should be written in flawless, deadpan corporate HR language: regretful but firm, full of euphemisms about restructuring, synergy, and going forward, and addressed to the affected workforce.

The humor should come entirely from getting the underlying philosophy of mind right. The memo might, for instance: explain that the audience of one is being let go because any viewer capable of interpreting the screening would require its own, smaller screening room with its own viewer, and the resulting chain of subcontracted observers never terminates; note that auditors could find no single moment or location at which the incoming material actually became experience, so the notion of a final broadcast was always an accounting fiction; describe how the two editing departments — one that revises the official record after the fact so that what was experienced was never what happened, and one that intercepts and falsifies the footage before it ever airs — can no longer be told apart once there is no fixed airtime to measure edits against; and reassure staff that content will continue to be processed in parallel, competing drafts across the organization, with no draft privileged as the definitive version and no central executive to whom it must be shown. A short FAQ section at the end (e.g., answering worried questions such as who, then, is having my experiences, or where exactly consciousness happens now) would be welcome, provided the answers remain straight-faced and unsatisfying in exactly the right way.

The piece should read as a fair satire of the homunculus-in-a-theater picture of consciousness rather than a strawman: a reader familiar with the debate should recognize that the author understands why the theater had to close, and should be able to tell, without any winking, that the real target is the intuitive idea of a finishing line where it all comes together. Do not name any actual philosophers in the memo itself; the restructuring should speak for itself.
