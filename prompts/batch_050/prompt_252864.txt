Amateur history reader here, and there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I don't have a name for. It goes like this. Some practice is deeply normal for a long time -- dueling among English gentlemen, footbinding in China, the Atlantic slave trade. And the arguments against it are not a late discovery. People were making perfectly good moral arguments against all of these decades, sometimes centuries, before the end, and those arguments mostly bounced off. Everyone knew them, almost nobody acted on them. Then, within roughly a generation, the whole thing flips: the practice goes from respectable to embarassing to unthinkable, fast. And here's the part that really gets me: afterwards, everyone tells the story as if the arguments finally won. As if people sat down, reasoned it through, and changed their minds -- even though the arguments were basically the same ones that had been losing for two hundred years.

So my question: does this specific shape have a name? Not just "norms change" -- I mean the specific combination of (a) long period where the winning argument already exists and loses, (b) sudden collapse, and (c) retrospective storytelling that credits the argument. I know it's somewhere in the neighborhood of moral revolutions and tipping points and paradigm shifts, but a paradigm shift is supposed to be driven by new evidence or a new framework, and here nothing new arrives right before the flip. And a fashion cascade seems too shallow for something where people stake their honor and their fortunes. Is there an established concept that fits all three parts, and if the fit is only partial, where does it break? And if nothing fits, what should we call it -- and what does that suggest about what actually does the work at the moment of the flip, if it isn't the arguments? Please don't just hand me a label; I want to know exactly where the edges of the phenomenon are.
