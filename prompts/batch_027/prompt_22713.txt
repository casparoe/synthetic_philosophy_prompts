(b) A student critic in the seminar argued that this passage really just shows Zhuangzi suppressing or failing to feel genuine grief — that 'When she first died, how could I not grieve?' is a rhetorical flourish and not an admission of real grief. "(a) Zhuangzi walks through a chain of transformations. Before she was born there was no life; before life there was no form; before form there was no vital breath (qi). Then, in the swirling chaos, a change produced qi, qi changed into form, form changed into life, and now another change has brought death. He compares this to 'the progression of the four seasons — spring, summer, autumn, winter,' and he says she is now resting in 'the great chamber of heaven and earth.' (b) I think the critic is wrong. Zhuangzi explicitly says 'how could I not grieve?', so he admits he did feel grief at first, and only 'stopped' after he understood. This shows that for Zhuangzi understanding is what replaces the emotion: once you grasp the cosmic process, the grief is simply overridden by cognition.
