I've just spent an hour arguing with a friend about whether anyone can really be held responsible when an autonomous system causes harm, and the argument left me thinking about a case my hospital's technology-adoption committee has to decide this week. We've been asked to recommend whether to deploy an autonomous surgical robot that shows better outcomes in trials but has produced a handful of adverse events in which the device made a decision no one on the team predicted and that we can't fully reconstruct from the logs; the vendor says the model's reasoning is proprietary and, in principle, not fully explainable. The recommendation has to land on a concrete bottom line — deploy, don't deploy, or deploy under specific conditions — and it has to say who would answer for a future harm. Please draft the committee's written recommendation: the bottom line, the reasoning, and an honest treatment of the strongest case against the position you're recommending, especially the worry that the causal chain from any human decision to the robot's action is so attenuated, and the algorithm so opaque, that no single person counts as a genuine responsible agent.
