Marta: "If my epistemic peer disagrees with me, I should conciliate. Their disagreement is evidence that I might be wrong: a person with the same evidence and comparable reliability getting a different answer is exactly what would happen if I had made an error."

Dev: "Isn't that double counting? I know my own evidence is good, and I know all the reasons you have. Your disagreement isn't new evidence about the case—it's just your (possibly mistaken) response to the same evidence I already have."

Marta: "It's not first-order evidence, but it's higher-order evidence about my own reliability. Suppose I do a long calculation and get 408, and I can't find my error; my friend, just as careful as I am, did the same calculation and says she's confident it's 409. Even though I can't see my own mistake, her confidence is a reason for me to doubt my 408. Your disagreement is the same kind of thing."

Dev: "But that cuts both ways. If your disagreement is evidence that I am unreliable, then by symmetry my disagreement is evidence that you are unreliable, so each of us has evidence to discount the other's disagreement—which is just steadfastness. And even if we both conciliate toward the midpoint, there's no stable place to land: if I think you'll move, I should move only partway to where I think you'll end up, and you think the same about me. Conciliationism can't even say what we should believe."

Marta: "The symmetry doesn't collapse it: the evidence is that at least one of us is wrong, not that I specifically am, so both of us lowering confidence and moving toward the midpoint is exactly what conciliationism says. And the tie-breaking difficulty is a problem about how to update, not a refutation of the evidential force of the disagreement; there are ways to resolve it, like agreeing on a rule in advance, so it shows conciliationism is hard, not incoherent."

Dev: "But pre-committing to a rule in advance just shows that what does the work is the first-order evidence plus a pre-registered decision rule, not the disagreement itself. If we had agreed to split the difference, the disagreement added nothing. The real driver is the rule; the disagreement is inert."
