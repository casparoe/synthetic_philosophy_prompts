I'm a science journalist who also serves on the advisory board of a natural history museum's human-evolution gallery. The exhibition designer has proposed a panel on the ear: "The three bones of your middle ear evolved to let you hear," with a diagram highlighting the bones. A paleontologist on the board objects that this is exactly the kind of adaptationist story Gould and Lewontin criticized in their 1979 spandrels paper — that the middle-ear bones that were, in the ancestors of mammals, part of the jaw, were later co-opted for hearing, so the panel presents an exaptation as a purpose-built design. The committee meets on Friday, and the panel text needs to be finalized by the end of the month. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and it should engage the paleontologist's position seriously while fairly representing the designer's concern that a panel about co-option will confuse general visitors.
