I'm taking a social choice theory seminar and also sit as a student member on my graduate association's elections committee. Next month's executive election will have four candidates, and the committee has to fix the voting rule before ballots go to print: two members want simple plurality, arguing it is easy to explain and that voters will mostly vote sincerely anyway, while the other two insist on a Condorcet method because they want a system that is "hard to game" by strategic voting and think plurality lets candidates win without majority support. The chair has asked each member to submit a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting. Please draft my opinion: it should recommend a specific rule or procedure with a concrete implementation timeline (ballots go to the printers on the 15th), and it should engage the "hard to game" demand seriously rather than simply dismissing it — in particular, it should address what the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem implies about whether any acceptable rule can satisfy that demand, and what, if anything, a committee can still do about strategic voting within that constraint.
