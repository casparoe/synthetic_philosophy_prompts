"Explain how Bradley's Regress arises from the problem of the unity of the proposition, and state Bradley's own proposed solution. Then, in a few sentences, contrast his solution with one other response to the regress (for instance, Russell's)."

Their answer:

"When we analyze a proposition such as 'a is F,' we seem to find that it is composed of the term 'a,' the term 'F,' and a relation that holds the two together. Bradley's point is that if that relation is itself just another term in the complex, then it too must be held to the others by a further relation, and that further relation by yet another, and so on without end. This is the regress.

Bradley's own solution is to conclude that the many related things are not ultimately real, and that reality is a single, all-inclusive, non-relational whole, which he calls the Absolute. The world of separate, related things is, on this view, merely appearance.

From this I take it that the regress shows there can be no relations at all, and so that no proposition can really be true, which makes language fundamentally meaningless. As for Russell, I believe his response was to accept Bradley's conclusion and to argue for monism as well, holding that the Absolute is the only genuine reality."
