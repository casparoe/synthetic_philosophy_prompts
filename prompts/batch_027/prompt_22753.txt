I've been poking around Hobbes on my own (I'm not a philosopher, just curious) and I keep getting stuck on his take on geometry. He seems to think a mathematical proof is basically just working out the consequences of the names we impose on things, not discovering anything about the actual world. But then how is geometry any good for measuring real land or building real stuff, if a proof is just unpacking our own definitions? Doesn't it just become empty, like restating the same thing over and over? And how does he deal with a line supposedly having no width at all, wich seems impossible to even draw? I want to understand whether his idea that math is all just working out our definitions makes it trivial, or if it's still genuinely about the real world somehow.
