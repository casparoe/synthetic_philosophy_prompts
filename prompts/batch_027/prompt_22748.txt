One team fine-tuned it on a small dataset (a few hundred examples, I think) and after that it started producing correct answers on a domain it had never before produced under any prompt we tried. They read this as latent knowledge — the model "knew" it all along and our evals just failed to elicit it, wich they argue is a safety concern because it means the model is more capable than it appears. Another team thinks its an artifact: the fine-tuning didn't "elicit" anything that was there, it just taught the model a new output pattern or overfit it to the fine-tuning distribution, so the "knowledge" was never stably present and the model is exactly as capable as our standard evals show.

Can you draft a short written opinion I can circulate before Thursday's meeting? I want a concrete recommendation with a time frame (e.g., whether we should treat the model as having the latent capability, whether we should re-run the evals with a different elicitation method, whether we should hold the deployment decision), and I want it to engage the "artifact" side seriously rather than just siding with the "latent knowledge" side. The memo should actually grapple with what it would even mean for a system to "know" something it doesn't express, not just restate the safety concern.
