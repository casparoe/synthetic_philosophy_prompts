ok so i've been deep in a rabbit hole on vagueness — like the sorites thing where you have a pile of sand and you take grains away one at a time and at some point it's not a heap anymore but you can't tell exactly where. there are two theories i keep flip flopping between and i can't decide which one holds up better.

the first one (i think it's called epistemicism) says there's actually a real, exact line — some specific number of grains where it stops being a heap — we just can't figure out what it is. the second (supervaluationism, i believe) says there's no real line at all, and a statement only counts as truly true if it's true under every way you could sharpen the vague word.

here's the thing that's bugging me. for the epistemicist side, if there's a real fact about exactly where the boundary is, why can we never, even in principle, just find it? it feels like they're just calling it ignorance but that doesn't feel like a real answer, it feels like it could just keep going forever.

the supervaluationist side has its own catch: it ends up making "it's a heap or it's not a heap" come out true, while "it's a heap" is not true and "it's not a heap" is not true either. that breaks a rule of logic that should obviously go through, which feels off to me.

so which one do you actually think is more definsible, and why? i don't want a "both have tradeoffs, it's complicated" shrug — pick a side and defend it. i really want to know which of these two specific problems you think is the worse one, and whether the theory you pick survives it.
