I'm a PhD student in political philosophy, and I sit on my graduate student union's executive committee. Ahead of next year's election, the committee has to decide whether to formally affiliate with and endorse a national political party. Half the committee argues that affiliation is the only way to have real political influence, and that without it we'll be ignored by candidates and parties; the other half worries that affiliation would turn members into party loyalists and replace genuine solidarity with a kind of party worship. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote: it should recommend a concrete course of action (affiliate, decline, or a structured alternative) and engage the influence argument seriously rather than dismissing it. Ground the recommendation in Simone Weil's critique of political parties — her claim that parties alienate individuals and create a sacred object that people worship in place of real community — and her proposal of workplace-based sections as an alternative unit of political organization.
