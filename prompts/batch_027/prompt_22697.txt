I'm a first-year doctoral student in political philosophy, and my advisor has just put Spinoza's Theological-Political Treatise on my qualifying-reading list, so I'm working through chapter 20 line by line for the first time. I've stalled on this passage (in a late nineteenth-century English translation), which I find oddly compressed:

"Since, therefore, no one can abdicate his freedom of judgment and feeling; since every man is by indefeasible natural right the master of his own thoughts, it follows that men thinking in diverse and contradictory fashions, cannot, without disastrous results, be compelled to speak only according to the dictates of the supreme power. [...] It follows, plainly, from the explanation given above, of the foundations of a state, that the ultimate aim of government is not to rule, or restrain, by fear, nor to exact obedience, but contrariwise, to free every man from fear, that he may live in all possible security; in other words, to strengthen his natural right to exist and work—without injury to himself or others. No, the object of government is not to change men from rational beings into beasts or puppets, but to enable them to develope their minds and bodies in security, and to employ their reason unshackled; neither showing hatred, anger, or deceit, nor watched with the eyes of jealousy and injustice. In fact, the true aim of government is liberty."

I can follow the first sentence, but I don't see how it gets us to the last. If natural right is, as Spinoza defined it in chapter 16, simply co-extensive with each person's actual power, and the social contract already requires every individual to cede the right of free action to the sovereign, in what sense does the state "strengthen" a right I already possessed in the state of nature, and in what sense is the "liberty" that is the true aim of government something the state secures for me rather than something I had all along? Could you walk me through the argument from the inalienability of the mind to the conclusion that the aim of government is liberty, and say precisely what the state is adding?
