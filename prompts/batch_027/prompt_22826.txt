Problem: Prove that every vector space has a basis, using Zorn's lemma.

Sam's answer:
Let V be a vector space. Consider the collection S of all linearly independent subsets of V, partially ordered by inclusion. Now let C be any chain in S, and let U be the union of all the sets in C. I claim U is in S. Every member of C is linearly independent, and linear independence is preserved under taking unions, so U is linearly independent. Hence U is an upper bound for C in S. Since every chain in S has an upper bound, Zorn's lemma applies, and S has a maximal element B. B is a maximal linearly independent subset of V, which is a basis for V. QED.
