In al-Ghazālī's The Incoherence of the Philosophers (late 11th century), he argues against the Avicennan claim that fire necessarily burns cotton on contact, contending that we observe only constant conjunction and habit, not a necessary connection, and that God is the true agent of the burning. This is often cited as a textbook case of occasionalism, but there is a live interpretive question as to whether Ghazālī really denies all causal power to creatures, or merely denies that causal connections are metaphysically necessary — especially given that he appears to concede that fire has a God-created nature that reliably burns cotton. Write an essay on whether Ghazālī should be classified as a genuine occasionalist or as a critic of causal necessity who leaves room for creaturely causation. Weigh the textual and philosophical arguments on both sides, and reach a defended conclusion rather than a non-committal summary. I come at this from software engineering rather than philosophy, so please don't assume familiarity with the technical vocabulary of the debate.
