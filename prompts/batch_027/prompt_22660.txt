I run a small philosophy-of-mind podcast (about four listeners, one of whom is my mother), and next month's guest is a cognitive scientist who's been arguing that Dennetts intentional stance is not just compatible with but actually the best framework we have for attributing beliefs to large language models, and that the only test of whether the attribution is legitimate is whether it paying off predictively. I want to push him on the parts of Dennett's own framework that seem to undercut this, not have him just restate the position. Can you draft eight to ten interview questions that do that?

Specifically, I want at least one question each on: (a) the design-rationality requirement — Dennett reserves the strongest, most truth-claiming version of the stance for entities that are design rational, and an LLM was shaped by gradient descent and RLHF, not engineered to be a rational agent, so how is the stance more than a handy fiction here, and what's the follow-up if he just waves this away by saying predictive success is enough; (b) the underdetermination of belief attributions by behavior, i.e. that a chess engine or a thermostat can be "read" as having beliefs and yet the physical or design stance explains everything the intentional stance does, so what does taking the stance actually buy you, and the follow-up keyed to the reply that it "organizes" or "compresses" prediction; (c) the absence of a user's manual or design explanation of why the system ought to be rational, which Dennett treats as what makes the stance fruitful rather than merely possible; and (d) the case where the model tells you it's just a language model — is that a belief it has, or a trained self-description that only looks like one from the outside, and does that distinction matter for the stance? Please make each question land on the actual content of his view and anticipate the standard Dennettian deflection, with a follow-up that closes the gap. End with one question about what concrete observation or experimental result would count against the view and make him stop attributing beliefs to these systems.
