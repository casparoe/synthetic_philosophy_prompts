ok so I keep going down the time discounting rabbit hole and I think there's a disinction people constantly gloss over. When someone says a person has a "high time preference" or is "discounting the future," a lot of what's actually being measured isn't pure time preference at all. If I care less about a $100 payoff in ten years than one today, part of that is just "I might be dead in ten years" and part is "I might not even want it in ten years" — preferences drift, I could be a different person. So what's actually left as "pure" time preference, the case where you definately survive and definately still want the good but still prefer it sooner? And the harder question: for a single continuous agent who knows they'll still be them, is any nonzero amount of that pure residual defensible, or is it just irrational, like being willing to pay to make your own future self worse off? I want the actual argument for why zero discounting is the rational move, plus the strongest case for why you might rationally keep some. Not the "future you is you, be patient" bumper sticker.
