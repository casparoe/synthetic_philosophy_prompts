Write an essay on the problem of logical omniscience, with a focus on whether an ideally rational agent must assign credence one to every logical truth and to every consequence of its beliefs, or whether it can be rational to hold credences strictly between zero and one in statements whose truth has not yet been established. Weigh the arguments on both sides: the pressure from the probability axioms and from Dutch-book-style considerations on one hand, and, on the other, the self-referential difficulties that arise for an agent that models itself as an ideal reasoner, together with the decision-theoretic need to place bets on unproven but likely-true mathematical conjectures. Reach a justified conclusion on the question rather than ending on a non-committal note.
