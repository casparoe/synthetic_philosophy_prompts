In my ethics class we read the conclusion of Henry Sidgwick's *Methods of Ethics*, where he argues that rational egoism and utilitarianism only come out as compatible if we accept the postulates of immortality and retribution, and yet outside philosophy Sidgwick is also famous for co-founding the Society for Psychical Research in 1882, serving as its first president, and spending decades investigating cases of telepathy and ghostly appearances. It seems to me that this is either a bizarre contradiction in a philosopher who was otherwise extremely careful, or that his ethical work secretly depends on the paranormal in a way he would never admit. Can you explain what the actual connection was between Sidgwick's psychical research and his ethical postulates, and whether the empirical case he thought he was building was strong enough to support such a weighty conclusion?
