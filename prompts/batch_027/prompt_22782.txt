I'm taking a philosophy elective and we got to Bayesian confirmation, and I'm stuck on this thing called the old evidence problem. The way it was explained to me is that if some evidence E was already known before a hypothesis H was even proposed, then P(E)=1, so P(E|H)=1 and P(E|not H)=1, which means E has a likelihood ratio of 1 and therefore can't confirm H at all. But that feels backwards, because it seems like a theory can support itself by explaining stuff that was already observed, like a model explaining patterns in data that were already collected. So does the old evidence problem just show that Bayesianism is broken whenever a theory is invented after the evidence? Or am I messing up by assuming the evidence has to be literally probability 1 for the person who believes it?

My friend says the fix is just to imagine some person who didn't know E yet and update that person, but that feels a little like cheating, because then you're not really using the old evidence as old evidence. I don't know if I'm missing something basic here.
