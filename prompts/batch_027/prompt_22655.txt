I've been reading up on the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution, which I understand was proposed as an alternative to Nash's. From what I've gathered, the KS solution equalizes the relative gains of the two parties: each player receives the same fraction of the distance from the disagreement point to their respective ideal point. Since both the Nash and KS solutions are invariant to positive affine transformations of utility, it seems to me that the KS outcome must be fully pinned down by just two geometric objects, the disagreement point and the ideal point. If that is right, then adding a new feasible option that does not change either player's ideal point cannot possibly alter the KS outcome, since the relative gains would be identical. Yet the literature insists that the KS solution violates independence of irrelevant alternatives, precisely because adding such an option can change the outcome. I must be missing something fundamental. Could you point to where my reasoning breaks down?
