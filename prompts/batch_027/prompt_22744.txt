Question: It is sometimes said that an action can be right without having moral worth. (a) Explain this distinction. (b) Using an example, explain why an action done merely in accordance with duty may lack moral worth. (c) State the requirement that an action be done for the right reason. (d) Consider an agent who performs a right action for two reasons at once: a moral reason (genuine concern for those helped) and a self-interested reason (a desire for praise). Does the action have moral worth? Argue for your answer.

Student's answer:
(a) There is a difference between an action's being right and an action's having moral worth. An action is right when it is what the agent ought to do; that is a matter of the action's meeting the demands of morality. Moral worth, by contrast, is a further property that depends on the agent's motivation. An action can be right while having no moral worth, because the worth of the action tracks not only what was done but why it was done.

(b) The familiar example is the shopkeeper who charges fair prices. If he does so only because overcharging would damage his reputation and his trade, then his action is in accordance with duty — it conforms to what the duty of honesty requires — but it has no moral worth, because his motive is self-interest rather than a recognition of duty. Since it is done from the wrong motive, the action has no moral worth and the agent is not praiseworthy for it.

(c) For an action to have moral worth, it must be done for the right reason: the agent's determining motive must be the moral one — that the action is right — rather than a self-interested or merely prudent one.

(d) Suppose an agent donates to a charity both out of genuine concern for the beneficiaries and out of a desire to be seen as generous. I think the action lacks moral worth. The requirement is that the action be done for the right reason, and here a wrong reason — the desire for praise — is also present in the agent's motivation. Because the wrong reason is part of what moves the agent to act, the action is done partly for a wrong reason, and so it is not done for the right reason. The presence of the wrong reason vitiates the action's moral worth.
