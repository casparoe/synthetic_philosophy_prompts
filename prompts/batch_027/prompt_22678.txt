I write novels and I'm putting together a small interview series for a literary press. This month's guest is a moral philosopher who defends the hard Augustinian line on lying — that every lie is a sin, no exceptions, so in the standard case where a murderer comes to your door asking where the fugitive is hiding, the right move is to withhold or misdirect, never actually lie. I want six to eight questions that go pass having him restate the view: at least one on why he thinks withholding the truth from the murderer isn't itself a lie and where exactly that line sits, one on how the position survives the objection that it has you watch someone get killed rather than say a false sentence, and one on whether the whole thing rests on a particular theology of the soul or whether a secular deontologist could hold it too. Give me a follow-up for each, keyed to the answer hes most likely to reach for.
