I'm a PhD student in the foundations of quantum mechanics, working on the "quantum immortality" / "quantum suicide" debate. The basic "quantum immortality" claim is that, in the many-worlds interpretation, an observer who performs the "quantum suicide" experiment (a gun connected to a quantum measurement, where one outcome kills the observer) will, from their own perspective, always find themselves in a branch where they survive. But I think this claim is more subtle than it first appears. In particular, I'm interested in the role of self-locating uncertainty: how should the observer update their credence about which branch they're in, given that the observer in the alive branch is a different "self" from the observer before the experiment, and the observer in the dead branch doesn't exist to report? And does the "quantum immortality" claim really follow from many-worlds, or does it depend on additional assumptions about how to weight the branches (e.g., the Born-rule weights vs. counting observer-moments)?
