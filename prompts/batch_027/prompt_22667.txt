I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of language, and I've been preparing a short talk for a workshop on vagueness. My co-organizer keeps pressing me to say whether the disagreement between epistemicism and supervaluationism is really just a dispute about what we can know, or whether it goes deeper into the semantics of vague predicates. I want a dialogue that would help a small seminar group see the pressure points more clearly, without turning into a recitation of standard slogans.

Could you write a short dialogue between an epistemicist and a supervaluationist, both equally competent, discussing a borderline case of the predicate heap? The epistemicist should maintain that there is a fact of the matter about where the cutoff lies, but that our concepts and evidence are too imprecise to locate it. The supervaluationist should maintain that the predicate does not have a single determinate extension at the borderline, and that its truth should be assessed relative to acceptable precisifications. Please include at least one exchange in which one of them raises the problem of higher-order vagueness, the worry that any proposed boundary is itself vague, and the other responds by explaining either why the regress is harmless or why it exposes a defect in the rival view. End with the two speakers stating whether their disagreement is best located in epistemology, semantics, or ontology, and why.
