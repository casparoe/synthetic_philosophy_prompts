I've been trying to understand the idea of a supererogatory act — something like giving away most of your income to charity, which seems genuinely admirable but which no one could reasonably demand of you. But the more I think about it, the more I get stuck on what I take to be a contradiction. If an act is truly praiseworthy, then omitting it has to be at least somewhat wrong; otherwise, what is there to praise? I can't see how someone could be genuinely admirable for doing something that they are perfectly at liberty to skip. So I've been assuming that philosophers who talk about supererogation are quietly relying on the idea that leaving these acts undone is a minor moral failing, just one that isn't serious enough to count as a duty. Am I right, or is there something about the concept that I'm fundamentally misunderstanding?
