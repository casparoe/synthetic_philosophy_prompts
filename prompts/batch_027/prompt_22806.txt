I'm a retired neuroscientist who has recently taken up philosophy, and I now chair the program-grants committee of a neuroscience institute. We have to allocate a single ten-year grant between two finalist proposals. Proposal A is a connectomics project: it will map the complete synaptic wiring of a small model brain, synapse by synapse, working from the hypothesis that the static wiring diagram is the fundamental level of description and that cognitive function will fall out of it once we have the map. Proposal B is a systems project: it will characterize the activity dynamics and functional organization of those same circuits, working from the hypothesis that cognition is an emergent property of the dynamics and is not recoverable from the wiring alone. Having read the reduction-versus-emergence literature, my working question is whether the connectome fixes the function, or whether the function is multiply realizable across different wirings. Please draft the committee's decision memo: it should recommend a concrete allocation (all of the grant to A, all to B, or a split with a specific ratio), justify it, and give an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the proposal you do not recommend.
