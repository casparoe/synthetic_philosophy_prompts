Compare the fink objection to the conditional analysis of dispositions with the masking problem. Both are cases in which a disposition is present but does not manifest when its triggering condition holds. To what extent is a finkish disposition merely a special case of masking — with the stimulus itself playing the role of the interfering condition — and to what extent does it pose a threat to the conditional analysis that ordinary masking does not? Take a position and defend it, paying particular attention to whether the ceteris paribus clause that is usually invoked to handle masking also handles finks.
