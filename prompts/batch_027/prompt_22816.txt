I sit on my department's doctoral candidacy panel, and I've been asked to draft the panel's written opinion on a candidate's dissertation before we vote. The dissertation's central claim is that Kuhn's incommensurability thesis shows scientific revolutions to be non-rational: because rival paradigms lack a shared vocabulary, Kuhn's own criteria for theory choice (accuracy, consistency, breadth, simplicity, fruitfulness) can be applied only within a single paradigm and cannot arbitrate between paradigms, so a shift between them is a conversion rather than a reasoned judgment. The candidate concedes that Kuhn denied incommensurability entails incomparability, but argues this concession is a rhetorical afterthought that leaves the conclusion intact, because without a common language there is no fact of the matter about which paradigm is better.

Please draft the panel's decision memo — approve, require revisions, or fail — with reasons the candidate could actually respond to. I want the memo to engage seriously with the strongest version of the candidate's position, the "different worlds" reading of early Kuhn, rather than dismissing it as a straw man, and to say precisely which moves in the argument depend on a conflation the candidate has not noticed.
