I'm in a grad seminar on metaontology this term (the reading is Quine plus some of the ontological-realism literature) and we keep coming back to this idea of a "verbal dispute," where two people seem to be arguing but are really just using a term differently. Last week my friends Sam and Ravi got into a real argument over a table in the corner of a coffee shop, and I sat there unable to tell who was right.

Sam said "there's a table in the corner," and Ravi went, "which table? The boundary is fuzzy — the paint's peeling and it's not clear whether the chipped corner is part of it — and if any collection of parts fuses into a whole, then gluing a stray dust mote onto the table gives you a different table-ish thing, and gluing on two dust motes gives you yet another. So there are thousands of table-ish objects in that corner, and 'the table' doesn't pick out anything unique." Sam said no, there's exactly one table, the fuzzy boundary just means the object has a fuzzy boundary, and "table" in our language refers to the salient one, not to the dust-mote fusions. Ravi said that's just stipulating the extension of "table" as primitive, which is exactly what's at issue — if you can't give a non-linguistic criterion for which fusions count, then "how many tables are there" is a verbal question about our language, not a fact about the world. Sam said that's circular: the reason there's exactly one is that there's exactly one object that is a table, and our language tracks reality. Ravi said that's the circularity: you're using the fact that the language picks out one object to conclude there's exactly one, and then using that to conclude the language is tracking reality.

Can you walk through their exchange and tell me who's right about which specific point, and whether this is actually a verbal dispute (in the technical sense) or whether there's a substantive disagreement underneath that the verbal stuff is papering over? I'm not sure whether Ravi is making a legitimate point about gerrymendered objects or whether he's just being pedantic, and I don't want to take a side in the seminar without understanding the structure of the argument.
