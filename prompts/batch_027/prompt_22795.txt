I am preparing a graduate seminar on the philosophy of language, and I would like an essay on a technical controversy in the semantics and pragmatics of slurs: whether the derogatory force of a slur is best understood as part of its truth-conditional content, as a conventional implicature, as an indexical or context-sensitive feature, or as something purely pragmatic. My students often treat the issue as a matter of social etiquette, but I want them to see that it raises precise questions about content, compositionality, and speaker meaning. The essay should weigh the main accounts against specific data: the contrast between literal, ironic, and reclaimed uses; the behavior of slurs under embedding, denial, and indirect speech; and the puzzle of how the same lexical item can convey a negative attitude in one context while being used neutrally or critically in another.

The essay should reach a conclusion and justify it. It should not end by saying merely that each account has strengths and weaknesses without taking a side; rather, it should argue that one of these frameworks captures the data best, or that a hybrid account is needed, and explain why the alternatives fail in the relevant cases. Please write it as a self-contained essay suitable for a graduate seminar, with enough explanation of the technical distinctions that a reader familiar with basic philosophy of language but not with the specific literature on slurs can follow the argument.
