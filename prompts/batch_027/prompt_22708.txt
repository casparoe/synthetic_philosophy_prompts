Okay, one of those counterfactuals I keep chewing on: if Frank Ramsey had lived long enough to see Allais's 1953 paradox, what would he have actually said? Ramsey is basically the guy who gave us the coherence / Dutch book foundation for subjective probability and expected utility, which is exactly the framework Allais is trying to knock down. So the question I care about is whether he'd just double down and treat the independance axiom (the sure-thing principle) as a Dutch book in disguise, or whether he'd be more subtle. Because here's what bugs me: the Allais pattern (A over B, D over C) doesn't obviously set you up for a one-shot Dutch book the way a plain probability incoherence does. Would Ramsey concede that the independence axiom is an extra structural assumption his own coherence arguments don't actually deliver, and that the 'violations' might be coherent in the sense he cared about? Or would he dig up some contrived sequence of bets that Dutch books you anyway? Reason through both, grounded in how his betting-quotient / coherence arguments actually work, rather than just picking a side.
