For a scene I'm writing, my first-person narrator must say something in the spirit of Moore's paradox — a line like "I did it, but I don't believe I did it." I've been operating on the assumption that what makes the first form of Moore's paradox absurd is that the speaker's self-report is simply false: in asserting the first clause, the speaker thereby comes to believe it, so the avowal in the second clause is a mistaken report of the speaker's own mind. On that view, writing the line is easy — I just make my narrator someone who is wrong about her own beliefs. But I'm no longer confident that's the right diagnosis. If the absurdity of the first form is not a false self-report, then what is it, and how should I stage it in a narrator who is, to the best of my knowledge, being perfectly honest about her state of mind?
