My sister had a corpus callosotomy about six months ago for drop-attack epilepsy that wasnt responding to anything else. I know the basics: the corpus callosum is cut so the two hemispheres can't really talk to each other anymore, and since her speech lives in the left hemisphere, the left side of her body (the right hemisphere) basically can't say what it's thinking. It's been fine, mostly, but last week something happened that's stuck with me. We were in a parking garage and her left hand just reached over, opened the car door, and started pulling her out, and she said "what are you doing?" in a completely flat voice, like she was watching a stranger do it. The neurologist said that's normal-ish — the right hemisphere saw the exit and decided, and the left hemisphere just didn't know.

The reason it's on my mind right now is that she needs a follow-up procedure in a few weeks (they're replacing the battery in a stimulator they gave her) and I'm the one who has to sort out the consent paperwork with the hospital. I keep getting stuck on a question I can't shake: is there one "her" who can sign that form, or am I dealing with two people who share a body, one of whom can talk and one of whom can't? If it's two, what do I even tell the surgeon when the sides disagree — like, if the left hand grabs the pen and she says, out loud, that she doesn't want to sign it? And if it's one, how is that so different from any of us, since my own hands do stuff I don't remember deciding? I just want to know how to think about this, and what to actually do with the form.
