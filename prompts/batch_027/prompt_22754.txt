I recently attended a philosophy seminar where two speakers debated the higher-order theory of consciousness. The defender argued that a mental state is conscious only if it is the target of a higher-order representation. The critic objected with the mismatch case: suppose I have a first-order visual experience of a red square, but my higher-order representation represents me as seeing a green circle. By the defender's account, my experience is not conscious, which seems obviously wrong. The defender replied that the mismatch case is not a genuine problem, because the higher-order representation is caused by the first-order state and must therefore be about it. The critic replied that causal connection does not guarantee aboutness, and that a first-order theory avoids the problem entirely. Coming from a background in physics and only recently turning to philosophy, I found the debate difficult to follow. Could you adjudicate this disagreement point by point, saying who is right about which specific point, and what, if anything, both parties are missing?
