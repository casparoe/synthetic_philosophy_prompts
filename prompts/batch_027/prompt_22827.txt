So I was at a bar last night arguing with my friend about the paradox of fiction for like an hour, and he kept insisting that the whole 'paradox' is just a fancy name for the old problem of empty names — that we're just wondering how we can refer to or have attitudes toward things that don't exist, like the present king of France. If the paradox of fiction is really just the empty-name problem in a costume, then why do philosophers treat it as its' own thing instead of just importing the standard solutions from the philosophy of language? I'm half-convinced he's right and I just don't see the difference.
