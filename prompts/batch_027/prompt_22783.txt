I've been trying to figure out how to make decisions that span a long time, and I keep running into something I can't resolve. When I'm planning ahead, I care a lot about the next year or two, but I barely care about ten years from now — I treat that future almost like it belongs to someone else, and I'd let it take a lot of risk. But I also know that ten years from now, I'll be in exactly the position I'm in now: I'll care a lot about what's next year and barely care about what's ten years out. So the person I'm making decisions for right now is the same person who, a decade from now, will be making the same kind of decision. I can't figure out which perspective I should trust — the one who discounts the far future heavily, or the one who will care a lot when that far future becomes the near one. Is there a consistent way to think about how much weight to give my future self's interests, or am I just stuck with an arbitrary bias?
