hey, im writing a short essay for my phil of lang class on Frege's puzzle about identity statements and could use some feedback before i turn it in. here's what i've got so far:

the standard Fregean solution is the sense/reference distinction. "Hesperus is Phosphorus" and "Hesperus is Hesperus" pick out the same object (Venus) but have different senses, so they have different cognitive value. this works fine for proper names, but i think it runs into trouble for theoritical identities like "water is H2O."

the problem is that the sense of a theoretical term like "water" isn't a simple descriptive content the way "the morning star" is. instead, its sense is fixed by the term's place in a whole theoretical framework — its causal role, its chemical behavior, its relations to other theoretical terms. so the sense of "water" is more like a complex web of theoretical commitments than a simple description.

this means that when we learn "water is H2O," we're not just learning a new descriptive fact. we're learning something about the theoretical role of the term "water" — that the stuff we call "water" is identical to the stuff chemistry calls "H2O." i'd argue this is a different kind of value than the cognitive value of "Hesperus is Phosphorus," and that the Fregean solution doesn't fully capture it. the puzzle for theoretical identities is a different kind of puzzle than the puzzle for proper names, and it needs a different solution.
