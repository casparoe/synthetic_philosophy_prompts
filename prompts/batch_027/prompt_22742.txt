I'm a retired astrophysicist (radio, mostly — I never really did cosmology myself), and I've only recenlty fallen down the philosophy-of-physics rabbit hole, largely through a popular book I read last year. I've been chewing on the Boltzmann brain problem for a few weeks and I've written up a few pages of notes that I'd love a second pair of eyes on. Could you read the draft below and tell me (a) whether my reasoning is actually right — especially the part where I turn it into a reductio of any universe with a positive cosmological constant — and (b) whether it's interesting enough to bother polishing into a short essay, or whether it's just me re-deriving something standard?

The setup I keep coming back to: if the cosmological constant is positive, the vacuum is really de Sitter space, and that horizon has a temperature (the Gibbons–Hawking temperature, roughly T = H/2π in natural units), so the vacuum behaves a bit like a thermal bath, and a system at any nonzero temperature will fluctuate given enough time, and one of the possible fluctuations is a whole brain, complete with the electrochemical state that corresponds to a human being with memories — a 'Boltzmann brain.' Now the argument as I understand it: in an eternal de Sitter space there's infinite time, so the total number of Boltzmann brains that fluctuate out is infinite, while the number of ordinary observers — like us, born into galaxies in a normal cosmic history — is finite in any reasonable patch. So the ratio of Boltzmann brains to ordinary observers is infinite-to-finite, i.e. overwhelming. If you pick a random observer, you're overwhelmingly likely to be a Boltzmann brain. But we don't look like that: our memories are consistent, we see a smooth early universe and a CMB, which is exactly what a typical Boltzmann brain should NOT see (a typical fluctuation would give incoherent memories and a noisy, featureless environment). So either our cosmology is wrong, or the whole line of reasoning is. I take it as a reductio: any model with a positive cosmological constant is in trouble.

My one open question is whether the 'typical' Boltzmann brain really does have incoherent memories — I've seen that asserted but I cant quite follow why the incoherent ones would outnumber the coherent ones.
