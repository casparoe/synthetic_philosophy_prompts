In decision theory, evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL) is the view that if the universe is large enough to contain many agents similar to you, then your choice to cooperate is evidence that they too will cooperate, and, since your payoff depends on the overall level of cooperation among such agents, this evidential link can make cooperation maximize your expected utility under evidential decision theory. A pointed objection is that the correlation between your choice and theirs is fully explained by a common cause you already know — that you share a decision-making procedure and face similar situations — so that once you condition on that common cause, your own choice conveys no additional information about theirs, the evidential link is spurious, and defection dominates. Write an essay on whether this "known common cause" objection defeats ECL or whether a genuine evidential link survives. Weigh the arguments on both sides, and reach and justify a conclusion.
