I want to get the metaphysics right rather than hand-wave. Please write an essay on whether Max Black's two-sphere thought experiment — an otherwise empty universe containing just two perfect spheres a fixed distance apart — genuinely refutes the identity of indiscernibles, or whether the principle survives once we ask whether the spheres possess relational properties (such as being a fixed distance from the other sphere) that could individuate them. Weigh the main arguments on both sides: the case that the symmetry of the only available relations leaves the spheres still indiscernible, and the case that relational or spatial properties break the tie, so the example is not a genuine counterexample. Reach and justify a definite conclusion, and close by saying what that conclusion means for my novel — whether I can honestly present the two objects as two distinct things, or whether my conceit quietly collapses into one.
