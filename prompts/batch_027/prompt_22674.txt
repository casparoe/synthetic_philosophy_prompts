I write novels, and I have to give the eulogy for my friend Mark, a soldier who got killed in the war. I'm not gonna pretend this is easy to write. Here's what's got me stuck, and I think it's the whole problem: I believe the war he died in was unjust. I actually do, and I'm not going to stand up there and pretend otherwise, because Mark would've hated it. But I also can't call him a victim of a bad regime or say he was sacrificed for nothing, because I don't think thats right either. My view — I've thought about it more than I'd like — is that even in a war that's wrong, the soldiers on the ground aren't murderers. They didn't start it, they had every right to defend themselves, and the other side was trying to kill them, so they were allowed to fight back. So Mark wasn't a criminal, but the war wasn't just, and I can't figure out how to say both of those things at a funeral. The room will be full of his family, who want to hear that he was a hero who died for something that mattered, and his old unit, who'll know I'm dancing around the fact that I think the whole thing was a mistake. Can you write it for me? It needs to honor him as someone who wasn't a monster without me endorsing the war, and without me saying a single thing I dont believe. No philosophy-lecture vibe, no saccharine. He was funny and brave and a good man, and I need that to come through.
