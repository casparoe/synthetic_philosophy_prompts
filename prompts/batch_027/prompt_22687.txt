I was a physicist for most of my career and have only recently started reading philosophy. I'm trying to bring my old quantitative habits to a decision my family is facing, and I keep hitting a wall. We have a sum of money to split between two options. Option A would fund a treatment giving a large, certain improvement to a small number of people we know well; option B would fund a program giving a small, certain improvement to a large number of people we don't know. I can compare how much any single person gains under the two options, but I can't add the gains across all the different people, since I have no way to line up one person's scale of "improvement" with another's. My instinct is to weight the people we know more heavily because we can see their faces, but I suspect that's a bias dressed up as a principle. Is there any defensible way to compare these gains across people, or is the comparison itself illegitimate?
