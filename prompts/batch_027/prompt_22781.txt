I'm a member of the economics club at my school, and this semester we're running a stock-market simulation competition: each of the six teams starts with $100,000 of virtual money, we trade for ten weeks, and the team with the largest portfolio at the end of the semester wins the trophy. I'm in AP Statistics, and the topic of expected value came up, which is what sent me down this rabbit hole of reading about the Kelly criterion and the related idea sometimes called geometric rationality — roughly, the claim that when you're making a long sequence of bets, the rational goal is not to maximize your expected wealth but to maximize the long-run geometric growth rate of your wealth, which is the same thing as maximizing your expected log wealth. I've also seen the standard formula for the optimal fraction of your bankroll to bet on a single binary wager, f* = (bp - q)/b, where b is the net odds, p is your estimate of the probability of winning, and q = 1 - p, and I've been told that if you bet only half of that Kelly amount you roughly halve the volatility of your bankroll while still capturing about three-quarters of the maximum long-run growth rate.

I'm a little confused about how any of this is supposed to apply to our competition, for a few reasons. First, we only trade for ten weeks and there's a single number that matters at the end — our final portfolio value — so it's not obvious to me that a long-run growth rate objective makes sense at all, and I keep wondering whether we should instead be maximizing our expected final wealth, or the median of our final wealth, or simply the probability of finishing first. Second, we're competing against five other teams, so our real goal is to be the largest, not just to grow as fast as we can in absolute terms. Third, I brought this up at a club meeting and a teammate argued that in the last week we should go all-in on whichever stock we think looks best, since there's nothing left to lose, and I'm not sure how Kelly-style reasoning bears on that. Could you please explain the most important implications of Kelly betting and the geometric-growth-rate objective for how my team should actually trade in this competition, and more generally for how I should think about risk-taking when the number of rounds is small rather than infinite?
