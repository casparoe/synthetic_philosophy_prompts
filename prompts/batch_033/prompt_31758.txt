I keep seeing people in online arguments about evolution and consciousness drop the phrase "cranes, not skyhooks" or "that's just a skyhook" as if it ends the conversation. I get the basic idea—skyhooks are like magic explanations that pull themselves up from nothing, cranes are explanations that build on what's already there. But I'm pretty sure different people mean different things by it. Sometimes it seems to just mean "no supernatural stuff allowed," but other times it seems way stronger, like you can't appeal to the mind or consciousness to explain anything at all because that would be a skyhook. Other times it gets used to shut down any kind of top-down or emergent explanation. Can you lay out the different possible claims hiding in "cranes, not skyhooks," from the obvious to the really extreme? I also see "greedy reductionism" thrown around in the same conversations and I'm not sure if it's the opposite of the skyhook thing or a different axis entirely. Which readings are actually defensable and which are just rhetorical moves?
