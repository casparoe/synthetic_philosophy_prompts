I have spent the past few months trying to understand why a distributed system I am helping design fails in exactly the cases where messages can be delayed or lost, and the fixes I find in the literature all invoke common knowledge of rationality, but different papers appear to mean different things by it. Before I commit to any one formalism, I want a specification of what an adequate account of common knowledge of rationality must deliver. For my purposes, an adequate account is one that can underwrite reasoning about coordination and agreement among agents who cannot communicate with certainty. Please write the desiderata: the cases it must get right, such as the coordinated attack problem and the electronic mail game; the distinctions it must respect, such as mutual belief versus common belief, knowledge versus belief of rationality, and common knowledge versus approximate or p-common belief; and the formal results it must accommodate or explain away, such as the result that agents with common priors cannot agree to disagree. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement. Then say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely commitments of a particular research program, and flag any subset of desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing accounts.
