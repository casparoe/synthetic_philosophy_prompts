I've been chewing on a kind of decision problem and I dont know if it has an established name or if I'm just noticing a family resemblance. The version I keep coming back to is this. In the morning you're told that a completely reliable prediction has been made about which city you'll be in tonight, and that something you desperately want to avoid is scheduled for tonight in whichever city you will actually be in; you aren't told which city was predicted. You think about staying put: if you stay, then tonight you'll be here, so the thing is scheduled here, so staying means it happens. You think about leaving for the other city: if you leave, then tonight you'll be there, so the thing is scheduled there, so leaving also means it happens. Whichever act you consider, the bad outcome is the one that would occur conditional on that act. The usual way of choosing, look at each option and ask what would happen if I did that, tells you that every option is the worst option, and changing your mind never helps, because the bad thing just follows you to whichever option you pick. I know this is somewhere near Newcomb's problem, but in Newcomb the contents of the boxes are already fixed and the question is whether your choice is evidence or a cause of the payoff. Here it feels like the state of the world isn't fixed independently of your act and then predicted; it's more like the state is guaranteed to match whatever you end up doing, so the usual distinction between your move and the world you're choosing in stops being usable. It also doesn't feel like a self-fulfilling prophecy, since there's no causal chain from your belief to the outcome, and it doesn't feel like plain fatalism either, because it's not that one specific bad thing is unavoidable no matter what; it's that whichever option you pick, that option's outcome is the bad one, and there's no standpoint from which to compare them. Is there a name for this specific configuration? If there is, I'd want to know exactly how it's distinguished from Newcomb's problem, from fatalism, and from an ordinary no-win situation like a double bind. If there isn't, I'd like a term and a definition precise enough to say when a problem has this shape and when it only seems to. I'm not just after a label; I want the carve.
