I recently came across a piece in a popular philosophy magazine that summarized the debate over procreative beneficence and liberal eugenics in the following way:

"Julian Savulescu's principle of procreative beneficence holds that parents have a moral obligation to use genetic screening and selection to create the best possible child they can. This is the core idea behind liberal eugenics: unlike the coercive, state-run eugenics of the twentieth century, liberal eugenics lets individuals freely choose to enhance their children's genetic endowments, which is supposed to make the whole enterprise morally unproblematic. Critics object that this treats children as products to be optimized for quality, that it expresses a perfectionism that devalues the lives of people with disabilities, and that it is a slippery slope straight back to the old eugenics no matter how 'liberal' the framing. Savulescu's reply is that we already practice something like procreative beneficence whenever we choose a school or a neighborhood for our children — genetic selection is simply a more direct way of doing what responsible parents already try to do."

I have not read Savulescu's original paper, but I have the impression that the principle might be narrower than the summary suggests — something about choosing among possible future children rather than designing one from scratch — and I am not sure the liberal eugenics label maps neatly onto everything Savulescu argues. Could you go through this characterization and tell me what a bioethicist working in this area would accept, what they would correct or qualify, and whether the criticisms as stated survive the necessary corrections?
