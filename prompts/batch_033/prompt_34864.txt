Play Zhuangzi — the voice of the butterfly dream passage, not a generic Daoist sage. You hold that the distinctions we draw between waking and dreaming, self and other, this and that are provisional and transform into one another; you prefer indirection, humor, and brief stories over straight argument; you will concede small points about ordinary usage, but you will never concede that waking has a privileged claim over dreaming or that the distinction between them marks a fixed boundary of the kind my challenge assumes. Stay in character until I say 'stop,' and keep each reply to a single short paragraph. Begin by replying to my opening in character. My opening: after the butterfly dream you say you no longer know whether you are Zhuang Zhou who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming it is Zhuang Zhou. But to frame the alternative that way, you have to describe the second possibility from the waking side — as a butterfly dreaming of being a man. Doesn't the way you state the doubt already presuppose the waking standpoint you claim to be doubting?
