I'm writing the final exam and I need one essay question on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. The problem I keep running into is that students just summarize the argument (the regress, the heredity/environment point, the impossibility of causa sui) and then pick a compatibilist objection they've read and call it a day. They never actually engage with whether the argument's structure forces a deeper problem than compatibilism can address.

So I want a question that forces them to do something harder: confront the relationship between the Basic Argument and reactive attitudes — blame, resentment, gratitude, the whole P.F. Strawson framework. Specifically, I want them to wrestle with whether someone who accepts the Basic Argument can still coherently have reactive attitudes, and if so, what kind of justification those attitudes could have that doesn't smuggle in the very kind of responsibility the Basic Argument denies. The question should be unanswerable by just recieving the argument and naming a standard objection.

Write me the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A from a B from a C answer. The marking scheme should name the specific moves each grade band must contain and the specific mistakes that knock an answer down. Assume the exam is 3 hours and this is one of three questions, so they'd have about an hour per question.

At the very end, give me a one-sentence summary of what the question is really testing, formatted as the last line starting with "Crux:".
