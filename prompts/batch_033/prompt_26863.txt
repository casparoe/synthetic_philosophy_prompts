A friend and I just spent an evening arguing about whether the free energy principle is a genuine scientific theory or a mathematically dressed-up tautology. We've concluded that we need an adversarial collaboration to make progress. Could you draft the joint document?

Our positions, roughly: I'm inclined to think the free energy principle is either unfalsifiable or trivially true—any persisting system can be described as minimizing free energy under some model, but this redescription doesn't constrain specific mechanisms or yield predictions we couldn't have made without it. My friend holds that this objection mistakes the principle's epistemic role: it's a normative framework specifying what any adaptive system must do to resist entropic dissolution, and it has generated substantive empirical work—for instance, in computational psychiatry, where atypical precision-weighting of prediction errors has been used to model hallucinations, delusions, and catatonia in mechanistic detail.

We need the document to include:

1. A numbered list of claims we can both sign. These should cover what the free energy principle actually asserts mathematically, what predictive processing models have and haven't demonstrated empirically, what a satisfying theory of cognition requires, and where the principle's defenders and critics agree about its limitations.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should state a precise point of disagreement, with each side's position phrased so that its own advocate would endorse the wording. No straw men.

3. Cruxes: sub-questions such that if they were resolved, at least one of us would change our overall view. For each crux, specify a test, experiment, or argument that both of us agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

4. A diagnosis of any verbal disputes. We suspect we've been using "explanation" and "model" in different ways; if you find that some of our disagreements dissolve under more careful terminology, say so explicitly.
