My friend who did a biology minor tried to explain the handicap principle to me at a party and said this:

"So the idea is that animals evolve these crazy wasteful traits — peacock tails, huge antlers, ridiculous courtship dances — precisely because they're wasteful. The waste is the whole point. By wasting resources on a giant tail, the peacock is proving 'I'm so fit I can afford to burn calories on this nonsense and still survive.' That's why costly signals are honest: the cost is what makes them unfakeable. If a signal wasn't costly, any loser could produce it, and females would just ignore it. That's the handicap principle, and it's also why dudes buy sports cars they can't afford."

Something about the 'waste is the point' framing feels like it might be overstating things, and I've heard there's a distinction between the handicap principle specifically and costly signaling theory more broadly. Can you walk me through what a biologist would nod along with here, what they'd push back on, and whether the sports-car thing actually follows from the accurate parts?
