I've spent the last few months reading the metaphysics of dispositions — finks, masks, antidotes, the conditional analysis, the whole mess — and I'm starting to think the debate is structually incapable of ending. For context: the simple conditional analysis says x is disposed to M when stimulated by S iff, were x to undergo S, x would M. Then C.B. Martin comes along with his fink cases (a live wire hooked up to a machine that makes it dead the instant it's touched — the wire is disposed to electrocute but wouldn't if touched), and suddenly the analysis is in trouble. Then you get masks (the fragile glass wrapped in bubble wrap — it's still fragile but wouldn't break if struck), antidotes, and about six different attempts to fix the analysis: Lewis's reformed conditional analysis that appeals to intrinsic bases, ceteris paribus clauses, appeals to normal or ideal conditions, the move to treat dispositions as primitive, etc. And yet here we are, decades later, and as far as I can tell nobody has convinced anyone from the other camp.

I want you to act as a metaphysician who's been watching this debate for years and has thought hard about what's actually blocking progress. Don't give me another proposal for how to analyze dispositions. Instead, diagnose the impasse: is this a verbal dispute about the word "disposition"? Is it a substantive metaphysical question that's just really hard? Are the two sides answering different questions — one about semantics, one about fundamental ontology? Or is the whole thing a sign that "conceptual analysis" in this area is the wrong tool? I have a hunch that the disagreement is at least partly about what a philosophical analysis is supposed to accomplish, but I'm not sure.

Be specific about what each side actually needs the analysis to do, and tell me honestly what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a new kind of case, a formal result, a distinction nobody's drawn yet, or maybe just admitting the question was badly posed. If the answer is that no progress is possible, say why.
