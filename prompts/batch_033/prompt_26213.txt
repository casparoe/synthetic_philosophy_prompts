I'm in a third-year epistemology module and we've just spent a fortnight on Wittgenstein's *On Certainty* and the hinge epistemology literature that's grown out of it — Coliva, Pritchard, Wright, the lot. I've got to run a 40-minute session for my seminar group (about 12 people) and I want to do something that isn't just us sitting round a table paraphasing paragraphs back at each other.

The thing I keep coming back to is this: hinge propositions are meant to be commitments that aren't based on evidence and aren't the sort of thing you can properly doubt, because they're what make doubt and justification possible in the first place. But how do you get a room full of philosophy students to actually *feel* that, rather than just nod along? By definition, hinges are invisible when inquiry's running normally — you don't notice the scaffolding till someone tries to remove it.

What I want is a structured activity — a game, a role-play, a forced-choice exercise, I don't mind — where the participants run into the limits of justification themselves and discover that they're holding onto commitments they can't give reasons for but also can't genuinely abandon. I don't want to lecture first. I want the structure to do the work.

Can you design this? I need:

- The setup and rules (materials, if any — we're in a normal seminar room, nothing fancy)
- A rough timeline for the 40 minutes
- What I say and, crucially, what I *don't* say during the activity
- A debrief that maps what happened onto the conceptual distinctions: hinges vs ordinary empirical beliefs, the framework reading vs the non-epistemic reading, maybe the difference between personal hinges and universal ones
- An honest note on what will probably go wrong when I run it and how I should handle it

And one specific worry: is there any way to get them to feel the difference between a hinge proposition that's genuinely arational (not the kind of thing that *could* be justified) and a belief that merely happens to be unjustified so far? That distinction seems like the hardest thing to build into an activity, and I'm not sure it's even possible. If it isn't, just say so.
