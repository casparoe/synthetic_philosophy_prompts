I've spent the last few weeks trying to put together a short research agenda on the ethics of insecticides and pest control for a small foundation, and I keep going back and forth about what actually deserves the money. The fund is roughly £250,000 over two years, and the people it could support are mostly philosophers plus one or two biologists, so the work has to be conceptual or at least conceptual-adjacent — nobody is running a big field trial with this. The obvious questions all seem either over-discussed or close to intractable: whether insects are sentient at all, whether wild insect suffering dominates the expected-value calculation once you take the sheer numbers seriously, whether killing crop pests is net positive or negative given that they'd otherwise live short, brutal lives and get eaten alive anyway. I'm less interested in relitigating those than in working out which of the less obvious questions you'd actually put money behind.

So here's the ask. If you had to pick four research questions or lines of work in this area and rank them, what would they be? Fix your criteria explicitly — importance if answered, tractability for philosophers rather than entomologists, neglectedness relative to the existing invertebrate-welfare and wild-animal-suffering literature, and how many other questions hang on the answer. Defend the ordering rather than just listing things, and say which 'big' questions you'd deliberatly deprioritise and why. I'd also want to know what would make you reshuffle: for example, a neurobiological result showing that some insect orders have nociceptors that behave like vertebrate ones, or a policy shift around neonicotinoids that changes what's empirically on the table, or a good argument that the replaceability problem makes pest-control interventions mostly futile. Basically, I want a defended agenda, not a survey of the field.
