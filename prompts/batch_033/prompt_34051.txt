Hey, I run a small meetup for people who read LessWrong and the Alignment Forum, and I want to make a "which AI takeoff scenario do you actually hold?" quiz to use as an icebreaker/argument starter. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case or forecasting scenario rather than a direct question like "do you think takeoff will be fast" — e.g. whether a system that's just slightly better at AI research than its creators would set off a self-improvement loop, whether progress bottlenecks on compute or on algorithmic insight, how much messy real-world feedback matters, how much warning time you'd actually get, and whether the first AGI is one model or a whole lab-plus-tooling stack. The dimensions I care about are: compute-bound vs algorithm-bound progress, recursive self-improvement vs just cumulative iteration, and whether there's a sharp discontinuity in capabilities at all. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto the positions people actually defend in that scene — hard/fast takeoff (FOOM-ish), soft/slow takeoff, the moderate "fast but not recursive" view, and the "no discrete event, just the economy gradually automating" view. Sort people by what they say about the cases, not by which label they like, so dont include anything like "do you identify as a slow-takeoff person". Also flag any pair of positions the quiz can't cleanly seperate, and for answer patterns that match no real position, give a one-line diagnosis of why nobody's there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).
