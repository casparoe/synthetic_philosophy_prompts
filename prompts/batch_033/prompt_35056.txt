ok so ive been stuck on this argument from Galen Strawson for like a week and i cant find the flaw but the conclusion is so extreme i feel like something has to be wrong with it. maybe you can tell me where i'm messing up.

heres the argument as i understand it:

(1) to be genuinely morally responsible for an action, you have to be responsible for the kind of person you are—your character, your values, your dispositions—the mental stuff that produced the action. if your action just pours out of a character you had no hand in making, you're not the ultimate source of the action, you're just a middleman.

(2) to be responsible for the kind of person you are, you would have to have freely chosen to become that kind of person.

(3) but any choice you make about what kind of person to be is itself a product of the kind of person you already were when you made it. you cant step outside your own character to pick your character from some neutral, characterless standpoint.

(4) so to be responsible for the person you are now, you'd have to be responsible for the person you were when you made the choice that shaped you, which means you'd have to be responsible for the person you were before that, and so on—an infinite regress.

(5) the regress never bottoms out. there's no first moment where you got to pick your starting nature. you were born with a temperament you didnt choose, and even if you later "worked on yourself," those efforts were motivated by a temperament you already had and didn't choose.

(6) therefore, nobody is ever truly morally responsible for anything.

(6) is insane. we hold people responsible all the time—we blame, praise, resent, feel guilty, all of it. if the argument is right, the entire fabric of human moral life is built on a mistake. and not a small one.

ive tried a few exits and they all seem to collapse. first: "i'm responsible for my character because i shaped it over time through my choices." but that just pushes the regress back one step—the choices that shaped it came from a character i hadn't chosen. second: "responsibility doesnt require being the ultimate source, just being responsive to reasons." but then why does responsiveness-to-reasons count as responsibility if the responsiveness itself is just another feature of your character you never chose? third: "libertarian free will." but even if my choices are undetermined, indeterminacy isnt control—randomness doesnt make an action any more mine.

so i'm stuck. i cant see which premise to reject. (1) just seems like it's stating what "genuinely responsible" means. (2) seems obvious. (3) seems undeniable. and the regress in (4) and (5) looks airtight. but the conclusion is intolerable.

where does this argument go wrong? or if it doesnt go wrong, which premise is least costly to give up, and what do you lose by giving it up?
