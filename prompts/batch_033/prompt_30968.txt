I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and I'm working on a book whose main character is a retired logician who keeps circling back to the MU-puzzle. In case it's not fresh: you start with the string MI, and you have four rules — if a string ends in I you can append a U; from Mx you may form Mxx; any III may be replaced by U; and any UU may be dropped. The puzzle is whether MU is reachable from MI. The standard resolution is that it isn't: count the I's. You begin with one I; the first rule leaves the count alone, the second doubles it, the third removes three, and the fourth leaves it alone. So the number of I's is never a multiple of three — you start at 1 mod 3, doubling only ever gives you 1 or 2 mod 3, and subtracting three changes nothing — whereas MU has zero I's, and zero is divisible by three. What interests my character isn't the answer, though. It's that you get the answer only by stepping outside the symbol-shuffling and reasoning about the system: noticing an invariant, doing a little arithmetic. The jargon for this is JOOTSing — jumping out of the system.

The scene I keep failing to write is a late-night argument between my logician and a younger mathematician who insists the jump is a cheat, or at least nothing special: all he did was embed the MIU game in a bigger formal system, arithmetic modulo three, and prove a theorem there. On that view there's no outside, just a larger inside, and the feeling of transcendence is a trick of perspective. My logician wants to say no — the jump is the moment reasoning does something genuinely new, something that couldn't of been produced by grinding through the rules, and it's the same move that matters in Gödelian arguments and in seeing that a system can't vouch for its own consistency. I'd like you to write me an essay that weighs these two readings of JOOTSing and lands on a definite verdict, not a both-sides shrug. I don't need citations or a reading list — I need the argument straight so the scene doesn't just have my characters trading vibes. If the verdict has to be nuanced ('a real jump in one sense, a relocation in another'), fine, but defend that nuance as a position rather than using it as an escape hatch.
