I came to philosophy late, after retiring from a career as a physical chemist, and I have spent the past year working through the accuracy-first literature in formal epistemology with a reading group at the local university. The formal apparatus is congenial to me — I spent decades fitting models to noisy data — but I want to be sure the position I have assembled from this material is actually coherent before I commit it to writing. I find myself holding the following five claims. First, the only fundamental epistemic good for a credence function is accuracy, understood as closeness to the truth; whatever epistemic value coherence, responsiveness to evidence, or further inquiry has is derivative from accuracy or merely practical. Second, accuracy is measured by strictly proper scoring rules, and while the Brier score is the one I reach for first, I do not think any particular strictly proper rule is objectively privileged over the others. Third, probabilism is a genuine requirement of rationality, and the accuracy-dominance argument for it is sound: on any admissible inaccuracy measure, every non-probabilistic credence function is accuracy-dominated by a probabilistic one. Fourth, conditionalization is the only rationally permissible way to update on new evidence, and its justification is accuracy-based, since it is the updating plan that maximizes expected accuracy. Fifth, there are cases of severely equivocal evidence — a coin about whose bias I know nothing at all, or a single ambiguous laboratory result — in which a rational agent may properly hold imprecise credences, a set of probability functions rather than a single one.

These five claims each seem individually respectable, and I can imagine a person holding all of them. But I have not seen them checked as a package, and I suspect the joints are not all sound, though I cannot locate the strain. Please audit the set. For each pair of commitments that pulls against another, tell me how serious the tension is, what exactly has to give, and what the least costly revision would be; where a pair only appears to be in tension, say why the appearance is misleading. Then rank the genuine tensions by severity and tell me which single revision of one commitment would do the most to restore coherence. Please be specific about the scoring rules and updating rules rather than gesturing at general trade-offs, and write the response in American English.
