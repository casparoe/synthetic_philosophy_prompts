My friend has weirdly strong views about what counts as a species. So far she's ruled: lions and tigers are two species; the two ends of the Ensatina salamander ring around California's Central Valley are two species, even though they're connected by a chain of interbreeding populations; polar bears and brown bears are two species, and the fact that they hybridize in the wild and produce fertile offspring doesnt bother her; a single fossil lineage that changes gradually over millions of years should still be chopped into several named species, with boundaries she admits are arbitrary; and Neanderthals and Homo sapiens are separate species, even though she knows about the interbreeding. I want to predict what she'd say about a clonal, all-female lizard complex that falls into several genetically and morphologically distinct clusters but has no sexual reproduction at all. I'm less interested in the verdict itself than in the rule she's implicitly running -- reproductive isolation, separate evolutionary lineages, diagnosability, ecological distinctness, or some mix -- which candidate rules actually fit all her verdicts, which of them diverge on the asexual lizard case, and what further case would best tell the remaining ones apart.
