Here's the question I gave them:

"Wittgenstein introduces the idea of family resemblance in the Philosophical Investigations. Explain what this idea is, what picture of concepts it is meant to challenge, and how it fits into his larger project of dissolving the idea that language has an essence."

And here's the student's answer:

"Wittgenstein's notion of family resemblance is the idea that members of a category need not share a single common feature. Instead, they are related by a network of overlapping similarities, just like members of a family might share the same nose, eyes, or build, but there is no one trait that every family member has. His central example is the concept of a game: some games involve competition, others involve luck, some are played with balls, others with cards, and some are just for fun. There is no single feature that all games share, yet we have no trouble calling them all games.

The view Wittgenstein is challenging is the classical or essentialist view of concepts, which goes back at least to Plato, and which holds that for a concept to be meaningful, there must be a set of necessary and sufficient conditions that define it. On this view, if you can't give a definition, the concept is defective. Wittgenstein's move is to show that perfectly respectable concepts like 'game' work just fine without definitions, so the classical demand is misguided.

This fits into his larger project because it undermines the idea that language is a unified system with a hidden essence. If even ordinary concepts don't have sharp boundaries or definitions, then the philosophical dream of a perfect logical language that mirrors reality is a fantasy. His point is that meaning is determined by use within a community of speakers, and concepts are tools that we learn to apply through exposure to examples. This is basically the same insight that psychologists like Eleanor Rosch later developed into prototype theory: we categorize things by comparing them to a prototypical instance, and family resemblance is just the philosophical version of that same idea. Wittgenstein's real contribution was showing that categorization is a psychological process, not a logical one."
