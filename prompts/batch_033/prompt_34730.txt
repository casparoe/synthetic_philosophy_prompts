(1) bounded rationality is a descriptive project — its about how minds actually work given limited time and computation, not about revising our norms of rationality.

(2) satisficing (set an aspiration level, take the first option that meets it) is genuinely distinct from optimizing. it's not just maximizing expected utility with search costs baked in. the stopping rule is different in kind, not just in degree.

(5) when designing institutions — choice architectures, defaults, markets — we should aim to set things up so that even satisficing agents reach the outcomes optimizers would. that's a lot of the appeal of nudges and smart defaults.
