Two friends of mine got into it at a barbecue and I still can't figure out who had the better argument. Dave had been reading some pop neuroscience and announced that romantic love is "really just" oxytocin, dopamine, and some neural circuits doing their thing—so the whole idea of love as something special or meaningful is a nice story we tell ourselves. Mina got pretty heated and said this is exactly the mistake of thinking that explaining a thing is the same as explaining it away. She said: by that logic, a novel is just ink on paper, a symphony is just vibrations in the air, and a conversation is just moving your mouth—you're confusing the level of parts with the level of the whole. Dave said no, those are different because in a novel the meaning is in the arrangement of the words, and the arrangement is what matters. But with love, he said, the arrangement is just more physical stuff—neurons and chemicals arranged one way instead of another—and there's no "extra ingredient" that makes it love, so calling it love is just a label we slap on a physical process. Mina said he's demanding an extra ingredient that doesn't need to be there—that's a skyhook, she said, and what you actually need is a crane: an explanation of how the higher-level thing gets built from the lower-level stuff without needing magic. Dave said that's a cute metaphor but it doesn't answer the question. If love is nothing over and above the physical stuff, then "love" is just a name for a pattern, and the pattern doesn't have any reality beyond the physical stuff that makes it up. Mina said patterns are real—a hurricane is real even though it's "just" air molecules, and you can't predict a hurricane by tracking individual molecules, you need the higher-level description. Dave said a hurricane is different because it's a causal thing in the world, whereas love is a subjective experience, and the subjective part is exactly what the physical story leaves out. Mina said that's the hard problem of consciousness, not a problem with reductionism per se, and anyway he started by talking about love as a label we use for behavior and brain states, not about qualia. Then Dave said she was moving the goalposts. At this point they noticed everyone was staring at them and they dropped it.

I can't tell who had the better argument. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who was right about what, and what they were both missing?
