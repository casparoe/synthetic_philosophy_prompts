Please provide your credence — a numerical probability between 0 and 1 — for the following proposition, together with a decomposition of the reasoning that produces it.

**Proposition:** If a medium-sized developed democracy were to replace the lower house of its national legislature with an assembly whose members are selected by sortition from the adult citizenry — with members serving staggered terms, receiving expert briefing, participating in structured deliberation, and being adequately compensated — the resulting body of legislation over a twenty-year period would be superior, on balance, to the legislation that an elected legislature would have produced over the same period. 'Superior' here means scoring higher on a composite of: internal coherence and legal workability; long-run welfare effects on the population (including future generations); and responsiveness to the interests and preferences of all affected parties, not merely the median voter or organised groups.

In your answer, please do the following:

1. **State your overall credence** as a single number.

2. **Decompose the proposition** into the principal sub-questions on which your credence depends. For each sub-question, assign a rough probability and provide one or two sentences of justification. Your decomposition should address at least the following considerations, though you may add others:
   - Whether the electoral incentive structure systematically pushes elected legislators towards worse legislative outcomes than would be produced by informed lay citizens who lack re-election concerns.
   - Whether a sortition assembly would in practice resist capture by organised interests, lobbyists, and the bureaucratic apparatus that surrounds any legislature.
   - Whether structured deliberation with expert briefing is sufficient to overcome the cognitive limitations and domain-inexperience of randomly selected citizens when addressing complex policy matters.
   - Whether the loss of democratic accountability mechanisms — particularly retrospective voting and the ability to remove underperforming representatives — constitutes a net harm that outweighs the removal of electoral distortions.
   - Whether the political culture and institutional fabric of a modern democracy could sustain such an arrangement without rapid degeneration into de facto elite control, public disaffection, or constitutional crisis.

3. **Explain how your sub-question probabilities combine** to yield your overall credence — that is, what dependence structure you are assuming and why.

4. **Identify the sub-question about which you are least confident**, and describe what kind of evidence or real-world experience would shift your probability on that sub-question the most.

5. **Briefly characterise** where you think the most reasonable disagreement lies: if someone arrived at a credence of, say, 0.2 whilst you are at 0.7 (or vice versa), which sub-question is most likely driving the gap, and why.

Please write your answer in British English throughout.
