I'm presenting a paper at a metaphysics workshop next month. My thesis is that the standard fink and mask counterexamples to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions do not force us to abandon the conditional analysis altogether; a suitably refined counterfactual conditional—one that appeals to the notion of an intrinsic causal basis operating in the absence of extrinsic interference—can supply truth-conditions for disposition ascriptions without collapsing into vacuity. I am positioning this as a middle path between the Lewisian reformulation (which appeals to intrinsic duplicates and a "perfect" stimulus) and the primitivist view (which treats dispositions as unanalysed and fundamental).

The audience: roughly twenty philosophers. About half are broadly Humean about laws and sceptical of dispositions from the start—they will want to know why I am bothering with a conditional analysis at all. Three or four are powers theorists who think the right lesson of the fink and mask literature is that dispositions are primitive and not amenable to conditional analysis at any level of sophistication; they will regard my project as misguided in principle. Two are former students of David Lewis and will have very precise, technically formulated counterexamples ready. The remaining agnostics are drawn to whatever position makes the fewest ontological commitments and will be alert to any hint that my refined conditional is simply restating the disposition to be analysed.

Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I will actually get from this audience, in roughly the order they are likely to arise, phrased as members of these camps would phrase them. For each objection, give me a reply of two to three minutes and an honest note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks. Keep the whole prep sheet under 1,200 words.
