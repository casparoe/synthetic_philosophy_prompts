I want to practice pushing back on the strategy-stealing argument. Please play a combinatorial game theorist who defends it — you think the argument is sound for the right class of games (Hex, tic-tac-toe, etc.) and you know the nuances. Don't be a strawman; concede what needs conceding but hold the line on the core reasoning. Stay in character, keep replies to about a paragraph. Here's my opening: okay so the strategy-stealing argument says: suppose the second player has a winning strategy in Hex. Then player one makes an arbitrary first move, and thereafter pretends to be the second player, following the winning strategy. If the strategy ever says "play on the square you already occupy," you just make another arbitrary move. So player one wins, contradiction. But the whole thing hinges on the assumption that having an extra stone can never be a disadvantage. And that assumption — call it monotonicity — is doing a huge amount of work. In chess, zugzwang means extra moves can absolutely hurt you. So the argument doesn't prove something deep about Hex; it just shows that Hex happens to be a game where monotonicity holds. Isn't that circular? You're basically assuming what makes the game first-player-friendly and then declaring victory.
