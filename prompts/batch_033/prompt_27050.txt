So my grandmother passed away a few months ago, and last week I had this incredibly vivid dream where she was just sitting in her kitchen, doing the crossword, and we had a whole conversation. I woke up feeling like I'd actually spent time with her. When I mentioned this to two friends over drinks, they got into this whole argument that I'm still turning over in my head.

Sam said dreams aren't really experiences at all. His line was: you're asleep, which means you're not conscious, so you can't be having an experience. What actually happens, he said, is that when you wake up, your brain sort of confabulates a story and then you mistake that story for something that happened while you were out. So my feeling that I'd "spent time" with my grandmother was based on an illusion — there was no experience, just a narrative my brain cooked up upon waking and backdated.

Jordan pushed back hard. He said Sam was assuming that the only way a dream could work is as a real-time conscious experience, but that's not the only option. He brought up this idea — he said it was from some philosopher — that dreams could be like a cassette: the brain "records" something during sleep without any consciousness, and then when you wake up, it "plays back" the recording into your memory, and that playback is what feels like having had an experience. But here's where it got interesting: Jordan then said that even in that scenario, it's not obvious that the dream *wasn't* an experience. If the memory trace is indistinguishable from a memory of a real experience, and it affects you emotionally the same way, then whats the difference? He said the whole question of "did I experience the dream while sleeping" might be a badly formed question — like asking whether a novel's characters exist.

Sam came back with: okay, but the cassette story actually proves my point. If the "playback" happens when you're awake, then the experience — if there is one — happens when you're awake, not when you're asleep. So dreams still aren't experiences during sleep. The thing you're calling a dream-experience is just a waking experience of remembering something that never happened.

Jordan said that's too quick. He said: imagine you watch a movie, and then later you remember watching it. The experience was the watching. Now imagine the movie was never projected on a screen — it was beamed directly into your memory, and your memory of "watching" it is perfect. Did you watch a movie? In one sense no, in another sense, what's the difference? He said the dream case is like that, and Sam is just insisting on one sense of "experience" while ignoring another.

Sam said Jordan was playing word games and that "experience" has to mean something — if you weren't conscious, you didn't experience anything, full stop.

At this point they looked at me and I just shrugged. But I've been thinking about it all week. Can you go through their exchange and adjudicate it? Who's right about what at each step, and is there a way to resolve this or is it one of those philosophy problems where the terms are too slippery to get traction?
