I've been tracking the verdicts of a colleague who teaches a seminar on the ethics of harming. Across the term, she has endorsed the following positions on the standard cases:

1. **Switch**: Diverting the trolley onto a siding where it will kill one to save five — permissible.
2. **Footbridge**: Pushing a large man off a bridge to stop the trolley and save five — impermissible.
3. **Strategic bomber**: Bombing a munitions factory to end a war, foreseeing nearby civilian deaths as a side effect — permissible.
4. **Terror bomber**: Bombing civilians as a means to terrorize the population into surrender — impermissible.
5. **Hysterectomy**: Removing a cancerous uterus from a pregnant woman to save her, foreseeing the fetus's death — permissible.
6. **Craniotomy**: Crushing a fetus's skull to relieve obstructed labor and save the mother — impermissible.
7. **Palliative morphine**: Administering increasing doses of morphine to a terminally ill patient for pain relief, foreseeing that respiratory depression will hasten death — permissible.
8. **Active euthanasia**: Administering a lethal injection to end a suffering patient's life directly — impermissible.

Next week she will discuss the **loop variant** of the trolley problem: the trolley is headed toward five, but you can divert it onto a loop that rejoins the main track before the five. There is one person on the loop. If diverted, the trolley will hit the one, and the one's body will stop the trolley before it reaches the five. (If the one were not there, the trolley would loop around and kill the five anyway.) Her verdict is not yet on the record.

Please structure your response in the following sections:

- **The candidate rules** (~200 words): Identify the two or three most plausible principles that jointly fit her eight verdicts. State each rule clearly and explain how it handles the cases.
- **Predictions for the loop case** (~150 words): For each candidate rule, say what verdict it generates for the loop variant and why.
- **Best guess and reasoning** (~200 words): Give your best prediction of her actual verdict, with the reasons for preferring one rule over the others given her track record. If the evidence genuinely underdetermines the choice, say so honestly.
- **The decisive further case** (~150 words): Describe a further case — not among the eight above and not the loop variant — whose verdict would most cleanly discriminate between the candidate rules you have identified. Explain what each rule says about it and why it would settle the matter.
