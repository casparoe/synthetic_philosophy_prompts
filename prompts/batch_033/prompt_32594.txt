I just spent a couple hours arguing with a friend about Kelly betting and I still cant get his point out of my head. The usual line from the Kelly/geometric-rationality crowd is that if you're going to bet a fixed fraction of your bankroll over and over, the rational thing is to maximize expected log wealth, because that strategy almost surely dominates any other fixed-fraction strategy in the long run. His point was that this is a theorem about asymptotic growth, not a rule of rationality, and the slide from 'this wins almost surely in the limit' to 'you're irrational if you prefer something else' is doing hidden work. Please make the strongest case you can that maximizing expected log wealth is not rationally required. And actually engage with the comeback that expected log maximization just is expected utility maximization with log utility, so rejecting Kelly as a norm seems to force you to either reject expected utility theory or explain why a perfectly coherent utility function is somehow off-limits.
