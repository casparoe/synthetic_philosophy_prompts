I'm giving the opening remarks at a two-day workshop on using predictive risk scores to allocate scarce home-visiting slots in a state child-welfare agency. The room is split between data scientists and child-protection supervisors, and the workshop's funders want the group to settle on a single risk score that can be reported to the legislature as a performance measure. I'd like the remarks to be built around Goodhart's law and specification gaming, and I want those ideas to do real structural work rather than serve as decoration: the speech should explain, in terms both halves of the room can use, why a score that becomes a reporting target will change the very behavior it is meant to measure; why that failure is different from the model simply being imperfect, noisy, or biased; and why none of this is an argument against measurement as such. It should leave the supervisors feeling the danger has been named and taken seriously rather than brushed aside, and leave the data scientists with a sharper distinction between Goodhart effects and specification gaming than they came in with. Please write me about five minutes of remarks.
