For an assignment in my philosophy class, I have been asked to write down my current position on Sartre's account of freedom and then present it to a discussion group. Before I do that, I want an outside audit of whether the position actually hangs together. I have ended up with the following set of commitments:

(1) There is no human nature fixed before we exist; a person is nothing other than the sum of their choices and actions.
(2) We are radically free: even under severe constraints, we could always have chosen otherwise, and we are responsible for what we make of whatever situation we find ourselves in.
(3) Bad faith is real and widespread: people often deny their own freedom by treating themselves as if they were a thing — telling themselves that they had no choice, that they were just doing their job, or that their past or personality made them act a certain way.
(4) People's choices are heavily shaped by their upbringing, temperament, and social circumstances, and some circumstances are so coercive that it seems unfair to hold a person fully responsible for what they do.
(5) There are no objective values: nothing in the world, prior to our choices, tells us what we ought to do, and no choice can be justified from a neutral standpoint.
(6) Authenticity is better than bad faith; a person should try to live authentically rather than hide from their freedom.
(7) Consciousness is transparent to itself: we cannot hide a belief from ourselves in the way the Freudian unconscious is supposed to; if we believe something, we are in some sense aware that we believe it.

I would like an audit of this set, with the tensions ranked by severity rather than listed as equally bad. Please go through the commitments and tell me:

- Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be in tension?
- For each genuine tension, how severe is it, and what is the least costly revision that would resolve it?
- Are there any tensions that arise only when three or more of these commitments are taken together?
- If I wanted to keep as many of these as possible, which single revision would clean up the most?

I am not asking for a lecture on Sartre's own views except where that is necessary to tell whether my commitments fit together.
