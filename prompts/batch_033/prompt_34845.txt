so in my ethics class we read this thing by nozick about the utility monster and two friends got into it after class and i honestly couldnt tell who was winning

basically jake said the utility monster is a terrible objection because it assumes a creature that gets like a thousand times more happiness from the same resources as a normal human, and that just doesnt exist and never could. he said a thought experiment has to be at least physically possible or it doesnt actually test the theory, it just tests a cartoon version of it. you cant refute a real moral theory with a made-up creature.

maya pushed back and said thats missing the whole point. the monster isnt supposed to be realistic, its supposed to isolate a structural feature of utilitarianism — that it only cares about the total sum. if the theory says we should all starve so one being can have everything, and that conclusion is obviously wrong, then the theory has a structural problem. she said you dont need real trolleys to use the trolley problem.

jake said the difference is the trolley problem uses normal humans with normal psychologies. the utility monster has a utility function thats radically unlike anything in nature, so its testing the theory under conditions it was never designed for. he said its like saying physics is broken because it gives weird predictions if you assume an object with infinite mass.

maya said physicists use idealizations like frictionless surfaces all the time to test the internal logic of their theories, so thats actually an argument for her side. and anyway utilitarianism claims to be a universal principle — it should give the right answer for any possible distribution of utility, not just human-shaped ones. if the theory only works when utility curves look familiar, thats already a big concession.

jake switched gears and said fine, but rule utilitarianism handles the monster no problem because a rule like "dont sacrifice everyone to one being" produces better consequences overall than a rule that permits it. maya said thats just changing the subject to a different theory and doesnt rescue act utilitarianism, which was the actual target.

they asked me to adjudicate and i just stared at them. can you go through each move in order and say who has the better of it at each step, and then give an overall verdict on whether the utility monster is a good objection or not?
