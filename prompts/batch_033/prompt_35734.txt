A friend who's deep into Lewis walked me through this and now I can't shake it:

(1) When we say "there are many ways things could have been," we're quantifying over possibilia — ways things could have been.
(2) If we're ontologically committed to the things we quantify over in our best theories, then we're committed to the existence of possibilia.
(3) Possibilia are either abstract (like sets of propositions, or uninstantiated properties) or concrete (like Lewisian possible worlds).
(4) Abstract possibilia can't explain modal truth. If you say "it's possible that pigs fly" is true because there's a set of propositions that includes <pigs fly> and that set is consistent, you still need to explain what makes that set a possibility rather than just a set. You're using modal notions to explain modal notions.
(5) So possibilia must be concrete possible worlds — other universes, just as real as this one, with their own pigs and their own flying.
(6) But if that's right, then there's a concrete world where everything is exactly like this one except I had cereal instead of toast this morning. And another where I had toast but the toast was slightly darker. And so on for every possible variation.
(7) In those worlds, there are counterparts of me — people very similar to me — making slightly different choices, living slightly different lives.
(8) If there are infinitely many such counterparts, then "I" am not a single thing — I'm scattered across infinitely many concrete worlds, and my choices aren't really mine in any robust sense.
(9) That's absurd. I exist as a single person making genuine choices.
(10) So either we're not committed to possibilia (reject 2), or abstract possibilia can do the work (reject 4), or the move from (6) to (8) is invalid.

The thing is, I find (1) through (5) wierdly compelling — Lewis's argument that you can't get modality on the cheap. But I can't live with (8). Where does this go wrong? If you tell me to reject (4), I want to know what the best version of abstract possibilia looks like and whether it really avoids circularity. If you tell me the move from (6) to (8) is invalid, explain exactly why.

And please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested — I know people who work on this disagree about basically everything, and I don't want someone's pet view presented as the obvious answer.
