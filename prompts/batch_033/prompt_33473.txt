I am a decision theorist who works with expected utility theory, social welfare orderings, and population axiology. I keep encountering the claim that the procreation asymmetry — roughly, that there is a moral reason not to bring a miserable person into existence but no symmetric moral reason to bring a happy person into existence — poses a deep problem for total utilitarianism and resists clean formalization. Restate the asymmetry itself, and the main philosophical attempts to account for it (person-affecting views, the independence of the asymmetry from person-affecting intuitions, critical-level and critical-range theories, the view that the asymmetry tracks a difference between reasons and requirements, and so on), in the vocabulary I actually use: utility functions defined over populations, social welfare relations, dominance, Pareto principles, betterness and neutrality, and the formal structure of population axiology. For each attempt, tell me what translates cleanly into my framework, what can be expressed only by stretching or gerrymandering the formalism, and what has no counterpart at all. Then give me the residue report: when the asymmetry resists translation, is that because my framework is missing a genuine normative structure, or because the asymmetry is less precise than its philosophical proponents take it to be?
