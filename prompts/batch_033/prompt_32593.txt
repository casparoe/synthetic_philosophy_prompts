I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about when it's rational to follow the crowd vs. when it's just dumb herding, going by my gut on a bunch of cases. Here they are:

1. I'm in a foreign city, two restaurants side by side, one's empty and one's got a line out the door. I join the line. Seems fine.

2. I'm investing and everyone's piling into some stock. I've done zero research, no private info. I buy. Also fine.

3. I'm a doctor. Three colleagues diagnosed condition X. I examine the patient myself and my read points pretty clearly to Y — rarer but fits the symptoms better. I go with X anyway because three against one. This feels wrong to me. I think I should've trusted my own exam.

4. I'm in a committee meeting, first three speakers all back plan A. I've got private info that plan B is better, but I'm junior and they're senior. I stay quiet. This also feels wrong, though I get why people do it.

5. A tweet's going viral with some shocking claim. I have no private info either way. I retweet it. Feels irresponsible — I should at least wait.

6. I'm a scientist. My field has a consensus on something, built on decades of work by hundreds of people. I run my own experiments and they seem to contradict the consensus. I assume my experiment must be flawed. This feels right to me — proper epistemic humility.

7. Picking a movie. Rotten Tomatoes says 95%. My friend whose taste I really trust says it's terrible. I watch it anyway based on the aggregate. Fine.

8. I'm hiring. First two interviewers strongly recommend candidate A. I interview A and find them mediocre; candidate B seems great. But the first two are experienced and I'm new to this. I go with A. This is the one I genuinly can't settle — I go back and forth.

Is there a principle that gets all or most of these? Cases 3 and 6 especially bug me because they feel similar — private evidence vs. group judgment — but I come out opposite. And case 2 vs. case 5: both following the crowd with no private info, but I judge them differently. I want to know what principle unifies my verdicts, which ones it forces me to abandon, and what it says about case 8. Also, please say how confident you are in the principle you end up with and what point you're least sure about.
