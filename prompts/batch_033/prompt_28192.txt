I have no academic background in philosophy or the social sciences, but I have spent years working on the administrative side of large institutions — first in a hospital billing office and later for a school district — and I have been turning over a pattern I cannot name.

It goes like this. An institution decides it needs better information about the people it deals with, so it introduces a form, a metric, or a set of categories. To make the form work, someone has to define the categories precisely: what counts as "engaged," "compliant," "at risk," "on track." At first the categories are meant only to describe. But the people being described soon learn the categories, because the categories affect them — they determine who gets extra help, who gets monitored, who gets paid. So people begin to shape themselves to the categories, or push back against them, but either way they start to see themselves in the categories' terms. A student who is told, twice a year, that she is "borderline engaged" starts to think of her own attention in those words. A patient whose chart says "noncompliant" starts to tell new doctors, "I'm noncompliant." Then the institution collects the next round of records, and the records show — correctly — that the categories fit the people better than before. The categories have become truer, but only because the categories were there to become true. The records are then read as evidence that the original scheme was sound, and the scheme is extended.

What bothers me is not that people game the numbers, and not that the numbers are inaccurate. The opposite: the numbers become accurate, and that is exactly the problem. The institution's way of knowing people and its way of shaping people turn out to be the same operation, and nobody can point to the moment when the first stopped and the second began.

Is there a name for this whole cycle — the record-keeping, the self-recognition, the quiet adjustment, and the way the adjusted behavior is read back as independent confirmation? I know there are terms for pieces of it, like a self-fulfilling prophecy, but that phrase suggests a false belief that makes itself true. Here the institution's belief is not false at any particular point, and yet the whole loop seems to manufacture the very reality it claims only to record. If there is no single name, I would like to know the nearest concepts, how what I am describing differs from each of them, and where its boundaries lie.
