Okay here's something I keep going back and forth on. People say Kelly betting is "optimal" but the word is doing a lot of work. I want you to take these five justifications that actually get given for staking the full Kelly fraction, rate each one 0–10 for how much independant justificatory work it does in establishing that full Kelly—not half Kelly, not 2x Kelly—is the right bet size, and then rank them. For each one, say in a sentence or two whether it secretly leans on one of the others.

1. It maximizes expected log wealth, so it's what a log-utility agent would do.
2. It maximizes the long-run exponential growth rate of wealth, almost surely.
3. Log utility is the only utility function under which the optimal fraction is the same whether you're betting once or a hundred times, so it's the only way to avoid horizon-dependence.
4. It's the unique threshold: deviate from Kelly in either direction and with probability one you end up exponentially behind the Kelly bettor in the long run.
5. It maximizes the probablity of eventually ending up ahead of any fixed alternative strategy.

Score them, rank them, and flag the pairs you think are basically the same claim in different clothes.
