"The paradox runs as follows. Consider the hypothesis H: 'All ravens are black.' By contraposition, H is logically equivalent to H*: 'All non-black things are non-ravens.' Now, Nicod's criterion says that a universal generalization of the form 'All F are G' is confirmed by any observed instance of an F that is also G. So a black raven confirms H, and a red apple — being a non-black non-raven — confirms H*. Since H and H* are logically equivalent, and Nicod's criterion applies to both, it follows that the red apple confirms H just as it confirms H*. But this seems absurd: the contents of my fruit bowl appear to have no bearing on the color of ravens.

The principles that generate the paradox are: (1) Nicod's criterion, which says that positive instances of a universal generalization confirm it; (2) the equivalence condition, which says that if a piece of evidence confirms a hypothesis, it confirms any logically equivalent hypothesis; and (3) contraposition, the logical fact that 'All ravens are black' is equivalent to 'All non-black things are non-ravens.' The paradox shows that these three cannot all be maintained together.

Hempel's own resolution is to accept the paradoxical conclusion. He argues that the red apple does indeed confirm 'All ravens are black,' but only to a negligible degree — so small that it is practically zero. The reason we find it paradoxical, Hempel claims, is that we are smuggling in background knowledge: we already know the apple is neither black nor a raven, and against this background, the observation adds nothing. But in a purely logical analysis of confirmation, stripped of background knowledge, a non-black non-raven is a confirming instance. So the paradox is a psychological illusion, not a logical one.

I think Hempel's resolution is unsatisfying, and the real problem lies with Nicod's criterion. The criterion is too permissive: it treats any positive instance — no matter how gerrymandered or unnatural the predicate — as confirmatory. The better move is to restrict Nicod's criterion. Once we say that only positive instances of the 'natural' or 'projectible' formulation of a hypothesis count as confirming, the red apple no longer qualifies as confirming 'All ravens are black.' The contrapositive may be logically equivalent, but it is not the same hypothesis for the purposes of inductive confirmation, so the inference to the apple's confirming H is blocked. This is essentially a rejection of the equivalence condition, and it dissolves the paradox cleanly."
