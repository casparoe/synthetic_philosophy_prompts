I'm giving a department colloquium next month defending modal realism — the Lewisian version, where possible worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally isolated universes just as real as ours, and "possibly p" is true iff some such world has p true at it. The audience is mostly metaphysicians and philosophers of math: actualists to a person, several of them perfectly happy with worlds as abstract objects (maximal consistent sets of propositions, recombinations of universals, that sort of thing) but convinced concrete worlds are a nonstarter; two senior people are Platonists who accept a huge ontology of abstracta yet get visibly irritated at the idea of believing in things we are causally cut off from. My positive argument, compressed: actualist accounts either leave primitive modality unexplained or don't supply real truthmakers for modal claims, whereas modal realism gives one uniform reductive story and the weirdness is just the price of the ticket.
