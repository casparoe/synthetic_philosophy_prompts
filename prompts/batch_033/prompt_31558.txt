Write a dialogue between two philosophers who have just read the Wilt Chamberlain passage in Nozick. One defends the entitlement theory and thinks the example is close to decisive against patterned principles of distributive justice. The other defends a patterned principle (in the spirit of Rawls or a luck egalitarian, though they needn't be a strict Rawlsian) and thinks the example trades on a confusion between justice in individual transfers and justice in the background rules that make those transfers possible. Neither should be a strawman: the Nozickian should genuinely engage the background-rules objection rather than just restating that liberty upsets patterns, and the patterned-theorist should have something to say about why restoring a pattern after many small voluntary exchanges doesn't collapse into continuous interference. At some point they should argue about whether the Chamberlain example still has force if the initial distribution was just by the patterned-theorist's own lights, and about what the patterned-theorist's preferred response implies for ordinary choices like buying a ticket or tipping. They should respond to each other's actual moves, not give alternating speeches.

After the dialogue, add a section called "What would change my mind" (about 150-250 words): say which character you think has the better of the argument as you wrote it, and name a specific consideration or variant of the example that would flip your assessment, or where your take is most likely to be wrong.

Use these sections in this order: "The dialogue" (700-900 words), then "What would change my mind" (150-250 words). No other sections.
