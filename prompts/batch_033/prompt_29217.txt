So I've been keeping a pleasure diary for the last couple months (burnout thing), and the wierd part is that my entries have basically nothing in common: the relief of a headache finally ending, the satisfaction of getting a spreadsheet formula to work, the first sip of coffee, the sad-pleasure of a song that makes me miss my ex. I fell down a rabbit hole about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — if 'pleasure' names all these different things, what do they actually share, and does hedonism fall apart if the answer is 'nothing'? I run a small blog and I want to make a 'which theory of pleasure do you actually hold?' quiz for my readers.

Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case (relief, solving a problem, eating when you're already full, scratching an itch, enjoying a sad film, anticipation, the warm glow of doing someone a favor, that sort of thing), with two to four answer options each. The point is to sort readers by what they say about cases, not by which label they like, so no question should just ask 'do you think pleasure is a sensation?'

Leaves I want in the scoring key: (1) felt-quality view — all pleasures share a felt hedonic tone even when the activities are completely different; (2) attitudinal view — pleasure is liking or enjoying a state of affairs, not a sensation; (3) functional view — pleasure is whatever state plays the role of making you seek out or keep doing something; (4) no-common-property view — pleasures are a family-resemblance cluster and hunting for a common element is misguided. If the logical space genuinely needs a fifth leaf, add it and name it, but don't pad it with positions nobody actually holds.

Build the scoring key so each branch corresponds to a claim the questions actually test — e.g., did they treat relief as a felt quality or as the mere absence of pain — and flag any two leaves the quiz can't cleanly seperate. For each leaf, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment, like what the felt-quality view has to say about enjoying a sad film.
