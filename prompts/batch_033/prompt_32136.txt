I keep getting caught in the middle of a fight between two friends who have finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration about insecticides and pest control, and they've asked me to produce the document they'd both sign. One of them — the insect-sentience skeptic — thinks insect sentience is nowhere near established enough to ground moral constraints, so ordinary pest control (ant bait, flea treatments, aphid spray, mosquito fogging, cockroach gel) raises no serious moral problem in itself. The other — the precautionary insect-welfarist — thinks the evidence, or at least the risk of sentience it leaves open, is enough that a lot of routine insect-killing needs actual justification, and that "they're pests" or "there are lots of them" doesn't do the work people think it does. Write the document.

It should include: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section covering what both accept about insect behavior and nervous systems, about what would and wouldn't count as evidence of morally relevant pain, and about the structure of their disagreement; (2) a disagreements table with the precise residual disagreements, each side's position worded so that side would sign it, plus the strawman of their own view that each side wants struck from the record; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, a concrete observation or experiment both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If part of the dispute is verbal — say they mean different things by "sentience" or "needless" — say which part and give wording both could accept.

And please keep seperate what's actually settled (about nociception, learning, or how well the control methods work) from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present either the precautionary view or the skeptical view as the consensus.
