I'm on my college's curriculum review panel and I'm doing a PPE module on political philosophy where we've been reading Burke and Chesterton. Here's the thing: the panel is voting next week on whether to scrap our "Foundations of Western Thought" module — it's been a compulsory first-year paper since 1947, basically a Great Books course with small-group tutorials. The case against it is that students rate it badly, it's expensive, and the VC wants to free up credits for things that look better on a prospectus. The reformers keep saying: if we were designing the curriculum from scratch today, nobody would include this module, so it's irrational to keep it. And I cant help feeling that's exactly the kind of reasoning Burke and Chesterton warn against — Chesterton's fence, the idea that there might be reasons baked into the practice that aren't obvious to us now. But I'm struggling to turn that instinct into something the rest of the panel (mostly economists and bioscientists) would actually take seriously. I need to draft a short memo to circulate before the vote — it's got to recommend a definite course of action (keep, scrap, or modify), give the real reasons, and honestly engage with the strongest argument on the other side. I don't want a survey of options; I want a committed recommendation with a clear bottom line I can stand behind in the meeting. Oh and can you write it in British English? Cheers.
