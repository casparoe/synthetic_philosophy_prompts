In a political theory seminar someone kept shutting down a debate about whether a deeply unjust legal system can still have legitimate authority by paraphrasing Pascal's slogan about justice being powerless without force, and force being tyrannical without justice. His point was that every legal order is ultimately coercion wearing a justice costume, so asking whether the law is really legitimate is sort of a category mistake. I get the vibe but I don't know what the line actually commits him to. What are the different precise claims that slogan could be making, from the trivial to the radical? For each reading, specify what kind of force and what kind of justice it assumes, whether it is a conceptual claim, an empirical generalization, or a normative prescription, whether it is platitudinous or genuinely contentious, and whether that reading would actually get him his conclusion about unjust legal systems. Also flag any readings that are probably false. Format as a menu of readings with verdicts, not a single essay. Hard cap: 750 words.
