In my biology class, we learned the biological species concept: a species is a group of organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring, reproductively isolated from other such groups. But when I looked up the Endangered Species Act, I found that it defines "species" to include "any subspecies of fish or wildlife or plants, and any distinct population segment of any species of vertebrate fish or wildlife which interbreeds when mature." Subspecies and distinct population segments can, by definition, interbreed with the rest of their species — if they couldn't, the biological species concept would already count them as separate species. So the law protects things that aren't species under the definition I learned in class.

I am trying to figure out what theory of species makes sense of this. The law seems to treat a species as something more like a lineage or a historical entity — a segment of an evolutionary tree — where even populations that can still interbreed might count as separate units worth protecting if they are on distinct evolutionary trajectories. That sounds closer to the phylogenetic species concept, or to the idea I have come across in reading about philosophy of biology that species are individuals rather than classes. But the law does not actually say any of that; it simply defines "species" to include subspecies and distinct populations, which looks like a purely pragmatic move to protect whatever seems worth protecting.

What I want to know is this: does the Endangered Species Act actually presuppose a specific theory of what a species is, or does its definition deliberately avoid the philosophical question? If it does presuppose a theory, which one, and how can you tell? If it does not, is that a stable position — can a law consistently protect species without adopting any view of what a species is, or does the practice of deciding which populations to list eventually force agencies to rely on criteria that amount to a theoretical commitment whether they admit it or not? And if we replaced the implicit theory with a different one — say, a strict biological species concept — what would actually change about what gets protected?
