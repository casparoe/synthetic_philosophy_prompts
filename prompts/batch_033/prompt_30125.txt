I'm a product manager at a mid-size tech company, overseeing the team that builds our content recommendation system. For the past two quarters our north-star metric has been daily active minutes — total time users spend on the platform per day. The metric is up 18%, and leadership is pleased. But I've been digging into the data, and the gains are concentrated in outrage-bait: posts that make people angry, anxious, or envious, which they then spend twenty minutes hate-scrolling through. User satisfaction surveys are flat or slightly down. When I raised this, our VP of growth said to add a satisfaction metric to the objective function. But I can already see how that gets gamed — satisfaction surveys are easy to nudge with well-timed prompts, and if we weight them heavily enough to counteract the engagement incentive, the algorithm will simply learn to serve content that makes people feel satisfied in the moment while still wasting their time.

I've read enough to recognize the shape of the problem — Goodhart's law, specification gaming, the whole cluster. But the blog posts I've read are long on diagnosis and short on prescription, and I'm trying to figure out what to actually do on Monday. Specifically: is there a principled distinction between "we picked the wrong metric" and "any metric we pick will eventually be gamed under sufficient optimization pressure"? If the latter is true, what does a sane response look like — weaker optimization, keeping a human in the loop, or something else entirely? And if I can't fix the incentive structure from my role, is there a way to design the metric such that the failure mode becomes visible early, before it does real damage?
