Please write the commencement address for the graduating class of a small liberal arts college. The speaker is a philosopher of cognitive science whose career has centered on a single idea: that Darwin and Turing each performed what Daniel Dennett, borrowing from the nineteenth-century critic Robert Beverley MacKenzie, calls a "strange inversion of reasoning." Before Darwin, it seemed obvious that the exquisite design of living things required a designer; Darwin showed that a mindless process—natural selection—could produce all the competence we see in the living world. Before Turing, it seemed obvious that intelligent behavior required an intelligent mind; Turing showed that a mindless process—computation—could produce competence without comprehension. The speaker wants to use this double inversion to reflect on what the graduates have been doing for four years and what they might do next. The address needs to land for the graduates, their families, and the faculty without becoming a philosophy lecture. It should be humorous in places and genuinely moving, not a TED talk. Please aim for roughly twelve minutes when spoken.
