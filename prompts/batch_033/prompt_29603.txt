I chair a university working group on food and animal-welfare policy that has spent the past year deadlocked over one concrete substitution: when our dining services replaces beef with chicken to cut the menu's carbon and land footprint, is that substitution an improvement or a worsening in expected welfare, all things considered? One camp — call them the beef-is-worse camp — holds that beef is worse per calorie served. Cattle are inefficient converters of feed into edible food and live for many months, so each serving of beef carries a much larger upstream cost in the cropland used to grow their feed, along with the harvest deaths of small wild animals and the habitat that cropland displaces; chicken, on this view, concentrates unavoidable animal harm into a smaller land and feed footprint per serving. The other camp — the chicken-is-worse camp — holds that replacing one steer with well over a hundred broiler chickens multiplies the number of sentient lives that must be created, confined, and killed, each under conditions likely net-negative by a wide margin, and that the wild-animal side of the ledger is too uncertain in sign to carry the decision, since habitat lost to cropland may reduce net suffering as easily as it reduces net flourishing.

Write the joint statement this group should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want: (1) a numbered section of claims both camps can sign, including the empirical regularities each side is willing to stipulate (feed conversion, land use, animals per serving, broiler welfare, harvest deaths) and, where no magnitude can be stipulated, the direction both accept; (2) a disagreements table giving the residual disagreements, each camp's position phrased in wording that camp would accept as a fair statement of its own view; and (3) a list of cruxes — sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — with, for each crux, the concrete evidence or case both sides agree would bear on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where a disagreement is partly verbal, such as one about the unit of comparison or about what counts as "expected welfare," say which part is verbal and supply the neutral wording that exposes it. I suspect the real cruxes include whether comparison should be per calorie, per serving, per animal-life, or per day of lived experience; how to weigh many short, very bad lives against fewer longer, less bad lives; whether marginal crop deaths scale with feed consumed or with total land area; and whether the lives of wild animals displaced by agriculture are on average worth living.
