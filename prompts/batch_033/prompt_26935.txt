I work in dining services at a mid-sized university, and our sustainability committee has asked us to develop an animal-welfare-informed food procurement policy — not just the usual "buy cage-free eggs and call it a day," but something that actually engages with the harder tradeoffs. I've been reading around and I keep running into the same knot. Beef vs. chicken is the classic case: a single steer produces a lot of calories, but the steer has a rough life and a bad death. Chickens have much worse lives per individual (broiler chickens especially) and you need hundreds of them to match the caloric output of one steer, but each chicken's life is shorter and their total suffering-per-calorie might actually be lower depending on how you weigh intensity against duration. And then there's the wild animal angle: crop cultivation — even for plant-based foods — kills field mice, insects, ground-nesting birds. If you take the view that wild animal lives are net-negative in welfare, which some philosophers argue, then the crops that feed chickens (who are inefficient converters) might cause more wild animal suffering than the pasture a steer grazes on. I've also seen people argue the opposite: that pasture-raised beef involves more insect and small-mammal deaths per acre than row crops do, once you factor in the full grazing footprint.

I need a decision procedure I can actually use — something my team can run on a proposal to shift our menu toward or away from a particular protein, or to choose between Supplier A and Supplier B. It should be a checklist or sequence of questions that walks through the relevant considerations in order, flags where we need to get a number vs. where a rough qualitative judgment is enough, and is honest about the points where the whole thing bottoms out in a values question no amount of data will settle. It should also handle cases where the answer goes against our intutions — for instance, if the procedure spits out that we should serve more intensively-farmed chicken and less pasture-raised beef, I need to know whether that's a bug in the procedure or a feature we should take seriously.

Ground it in whatever the philosophical literature actually says about interspecies welfare comparisons, the logic of the larder, and the wild animal suffering debate — but the final product has to be usable by someone who last took an ethics class in undergrad. I want you to pick one approach and commit to it: don't give me a survey of frameworks. Defend the choices you bake into the procedure, and flag where you think the strongest objections to your approach lie. Then run the procedure on two cases: (1) replacing 30% of our beef purchases with chicken, and (2) replacing 30% of our beef purchases with a plant-based patty that uses conventionally farmed peas (so, crop deaths are in play). Tell me where the procedure gives a clear answer, where it doesn't, and which steps are really just an administrator making a values call.
