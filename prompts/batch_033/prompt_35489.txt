So I've got these two philosophers in my department — one's a die-hard substantivalist about spacetime (thinks spacetime is a real substance, points of spacetime exist as genuine entities in their own right), the other's a relationist (thinks spacetime is nothing over and above the spatiotemporal relations among material bodies). They've been arguing past each other for like two years and finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration. Problem is they're both so deep in the weeds they cant see the structure anymore, and they asked me to help them draft the thing. But honestly I'm not sure I know how to frame it neutrally either.

Can you draft the document they should produce? Here's what I want:

A numbered list of claims both can sign. These should be substantive — not "we both agree physics exists." Include things like: what they agree the Newton's bucket thought experiment actually shows (and what it doesn't), what they agree the mathematical formalism of Newtonian mechanics and general relativity commits us to or doesn't commit us to, and what they agree about the empirical equivalence of their views. Push them to say something interesting, not just stake out the overlap everyone already knows about.

Then a disagreements table. For each row: the precise question they disagree on, the substantivalist's answer (in wording the substantivalist would accept), the relationist's answer (in wording the relationist would accept), and whether this disagreement is substantive or merely verbal. If any turn out to be verbal, say which and why — that's part of what I want the collaboration to surface.

Then a cruxes table. For each crux: the sub-question that, if settled, would move at least one of them; the argument or thought experiment or (possible) empirical result both agree bears on that crux; and what each side predicts about it. The hole argument should probably be in here somewhere, and maybe something about whether we can make sense of absolute acceleration without absolute space, or about what the best interpretation of general relativity actually requires.

Finally, I want a section on what each side would count as a genuine concession — what evidence or argument would make each of them say "okay, I was wrong." If one side cant name anything, that's itself worth flagging.

Write it in American English, and keep it casual — my colleagues are reading this, not a journal editor.
