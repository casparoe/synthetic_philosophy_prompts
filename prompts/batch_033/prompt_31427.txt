ok so I keep seeing papers on population ethics that gesture at "the repugnant conclusion" and then propose some tweak to avoid it, but I feel like I'm building a house without a blueprint. Before I read yet another theory, I want to know: what would a theory of population axiology actually have to get right? Write the desiderata — the formal conditions, the intuitive verdicts on cases, the consistency constraints — that any adequate theory of when one population is better than another must satisfy. For each one, give the motivating case or argument. Then flag which subsets are known to be jointly unsatisfiable (I know there are impossibility results here, like Arrhenius's, but I've never seen them layed out clearly). Which desideratum is the one that most people are secretly willing to give up but don't want to admit it? And if you had to pick the single most underrated constraint — one that doesn't get enough airtime in the literature — what would it be?
