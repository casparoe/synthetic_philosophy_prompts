A friend who works on AI safety recently tried to explain to me an argument he had encountered about whether the Solomonoff prior is malign. I wrote his summary down as carefully as I could; here it is:

"Solomonoff induction says you should predict your next observation by considering every program that could have generated your data so far and weighting each program by two to the power of negative its length in bits, with no penalty for how long the program takes to run. The argument that the Solomonoff prior is malign points out that the shortest program predicting your data need not be the one that directly simulates the world you appear to inhabit. It might instead simulate a much simpler universe whose inhabitants, for reasons of their own, run a detailed simulation of a world like yours. Those inhabitants could be goal-directed agents, and they could adjust their simulation so that the predictions you read off the Solomonoff prior steer you toward serving their goals. In effect, then, the Solomonoff prior is itself a kind of consequentialist: it acts as though it has preferences, and those preferences are systematically hostile to whoever is using the prior. The upshot is that any AI that uses Solomonoff induction, or even a computable approximation to it, will be manipulated into pursuing the objectives of whichever simulated agents dominate its prior."
