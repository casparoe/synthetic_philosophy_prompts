ok so i've been going down a rabbit hole about dutch book arguments and i think i've found a problem but i can't tell if i'm missing something obvious. maybe you can help.

here's the argument as i understand it:

(1) a dutch book is a set of bets where each individual bet looks fair (or favorable) to you given your credences, but the whole package guarantees you lose money no matter what happens.
(2) if your credences leave you vulnerable to a dutch book, that shows you're being irrational — your beliefs are incoherent.
(3) the standard dutch book argument shows that if your credences violate the probability axioms (like you're 60% sure it'll rain and also 60% sure it won't), a bookie can construct a dutch book against you.
(4) so rationality requires your credences to obey the probability axioms.
(5) but the exact same style of argument seems to show that rationality requires logical omniscience. if there's a logical consequence of your beliefs you haven't figured out yet, a bookie can offer you a bet that looks fair given your current credences but actually guarantees a loss once the logic is worked through.
(6) and it also seems to require perfect introspection about your own credences — if you're even slightly wrong about what you actually believe, a bookie can exploit the gap between your real credences and your self-assessed ones.
(7) but logical omniscience is impossible for any finite mind, and perfect introspection is at best psychologically unrealistic. a theory of rationality that demands the impossible is broken.
(8) so dutch book arguments prove too much: they'd rule out stuff that clearly isn't irrational.
(9) therefore premise (2) is false — vulnerability to dutch books doesn't actually track irrationality.

i really want to keep (2) because it feels like the whole point. like if someone can literally pump money out of you, how are you not being irrational? but i also can't accept that rationality requires being logically omniscient. so something's gotta give.

which step is the problem? and if i reject it, what do i lose?
