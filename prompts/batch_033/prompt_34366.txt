I teach a small upper-level seminar in formal epistemology — about 15 students, one 75-minute session — and this year I want to open the unit on self-locating belief with a game instead of with the Sleeping Beauty problem itself. Last time I taught it, the room split instantly into "it's obviously a half" and "it's obviously a third," and everyone just talked past each other for an hour, so I want them to feel the pull of both answers before they know the labels. Design a runnable classroom activity built around the core setup — a fair coin is flipped, and if heads one student is called in to make a bet, while if tails two students are called in separate interviews, with neither told whether they are the first or second call — where the participants themselves notice that the coin looks fair from the outside but the person being called keeps finding that tails bets pay off. I want rules, a cheap materials list (coin, index cards, envelopes, whatever), and a payoff scheme in points that doesn't use real money, maybe with the top score earning something trivial like first pick of next week's reading. The moment I most want engineered into the game is one where the same person makes the "of course it's a fair coin, so it's 1/2" choice as an observer and then, minutes later, when they're the one woken up and asked to put points down, feels pulled the other way.

The debrief should map what happened onto the difference between conditionalizing on "the coin landed tails" and conditionalizing on "I am one of the wakings," and it should say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — people blurring per-awakening and per-round frequencies, someone accidentally signaling the coin, the first-call problem — and how to handle each. And be honest about what the activity can't reproduce: in the original problem it's the same person across Monday and Tuesday with her memory erased, and my many-student version swaps that for different people in indistinguishable slots, so I want a clear note on what that substitution does and doesn't change. No lecture up front, and don't name Sleeping Beauty until after they've run into it themselves.
