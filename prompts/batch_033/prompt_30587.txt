I'm a novelist, so I spend a lot of time thinking about words and where their edges are, and I've gone down a rabbit hole on the vagueness debate. The epistemicists say a predicate like "bald" has a sharp cutoff—some exact number of hairs where it stops applying—but we cant know where it is. The supervaluationists say that's crazy: "bald" is only partially defined, true on some precisifications and false on others, so borderline cases are neither true nor false. Both camps agree on all the observable facts (the sorites paradox, borderline cases, the fact that nobody can locate a cutoff), and yet they've been restating the same arguments for decades without anyone moving. Why has this stalled? Is it a verbal dispute about the word "true," a difference about what a semantic theory is supposed to do, or a clash of bedrock intuitions about meaning that neither side can argue for? Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion: first, what both sides actually agree on; then your diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is; then whether any part of it is merely verbal; then where the bedrock differences are; and finally what would actually count as progress.
