Suppose a private foundation is establishing a two-year program in the conceptual foundations of social choice. It can support about four researchers, all working on questions that arise from the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem and from the broader idea of strategy-proofness. The program's aim is not to produce additional impossibility theorems for their own sake, but to clarify what strategy-proofness can and cannot require of actual collective decision procedures. The foundation has asked you to recommend which open questions or lines of work it should prioritize, and in what order.

I want a defended agenda, not a survey of the field. For each item in your ranking, explain why it is upstream of other work, how tractable it is with current conceptual and formal tools, and how much attention it currently receives relative to its importance. Questions you might consider include, but you should not feel bound by: whether randomization is a genuine escape from the impossibility or merely changes the subject; whether the dominant-strategy standard should give way to Bayesian incentive compatibility; how the result behaves under incomplete, uncertain, or interpersonally comparable preferences; which domain restrictions weaker than single-peakedness restore non-dictatorial possibility; and whether the theorem's dictator is a genuine normative problem or an artifact of modeling choices such as agenda control and tie-breaking.

Structure your answer in the following sections, in this order:

1. Framing (about 100 words): state what you take the theorem to have shown and what target the program should adopt.
2. Prioritization criteria (about 150 words): specify the criteria you will use to rank questions and their relative weight.
3. Ranked agenda (about 700 words): present three to five questions or lines of work in priority order, defending the ordering on the stated criteria.
4. What would change the ranking (about 150 words): identify findings, arguments, or shifts in the broader field over the next two years that would move items up or down.
