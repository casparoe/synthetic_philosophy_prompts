Here are her verdicts, as close to her own words as I can reconstruct:

1. **Descartes' evil demon.** She says the cogito survives this: if an evil demon is deceiving me about everything — the external world, my body, mathematics — I must still exist to be deceived. The cogito establishes that I exist as a thinking thing.

2. **The dreaming scenario.** Same verdict: even if I am dreaming, I must exist to have the dream. The cogito establishes the existence of the dreamer.

3. **The brain-in-a-vat scenario.** She treats this as structurally identical to the evil demon. If I am a brain in a vat being fed a simulated reality, I still think, and therefore I am. The cogito establishes that the thinker exists.

4. **Humean bundle theory.** She says the cogito is compatible with the view that there is no persisting self over and above the bundle of perceptions. The cogito establishes that thinking is occurring, but it does not by itself settle whether the "I" refers to a substance or to a bundle. She calls this the neutrality of the cogito on the metaphysics of the self.

5. **Split-brain patients, one hemisphere anesthetized.** She says the cogito, if performed by the conscious hemisphere, establishes that the conscious hemisphere is a thinker. But she adds that it would be a further step to say whether the person is one thinker or two; the cogito alone doesn't answer that.

6. **Dreamless sleep.** She says the cogito cannot be performed by someone in dreamless sleep, because there is no thinking occurring. The cogito therefore establishes nothing about the sleeper — but she is careful to say that this doesn't mean the sleeper doesn't exist; it means the cogito is simply silent on the matter.
