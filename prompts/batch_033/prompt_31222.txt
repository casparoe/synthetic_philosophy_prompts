I've been trying to think through, on my own, what makes me the particular person I am and which things about my life could have been otherwise while still being mine. I've ended up with a set of views, and I'd like a careful consistency check before I commit to them.

1. I could not have come from a different sperm and egg than I actually came from. If my parents had conceived on a different occasion, the resulting child would have been a sibling of mine, not me.
2. I could have been born the opposite sex. It seems easy to imagine things having gone differently in that respect.
3. I could have been born several weeks earlier or later — premature or overdue — and still have been me. The timing of my birth does not seem to be part of what makes me the person I am.
4. I do not think I am an immaterial soul or a purely mental subject. I take myself to be a biological organism, a particular living human animal.
5. If my brain were successfully transplanted into another body, I would go with the brain rather than remain with my old body. And if each half of my brain were placed in a different body, so that two people each had my memories and personality, I doubt there would be a determinate fact about which one, if either, was me; the question might simply have no yes-or-no answer.
6. The course of my life after I began is largely contingent. I could have had a very different upbringing, personality, and circumstances and still have been the same person. What fixes my identity is how I started, not how I turned out.

Please audit this set. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, and which only seem to be? For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what would have to give, and what the least costly revision would be. Then tell me whether any single change would do the most to make the package hang together.
