I'm on the internal grants committee at a small AI research nonprofit that funds conceptual work on agency and reasoning. We have two one-year proposals in front of us, both aimed at what a few of us call the embedded agency problem: an agent that has to reason about an environment it's physically part of, with limited compute, where its own actions and its model of itself are part of the thing it's trying to model. Proposal A argues the right move is to keep the clean agent/environment split as a working approximation — treat the agent as outside the world, then bolt on corrections for self-reference and resource limits wherever they cause trouble. Proposal B argues that the clean split is the core mistake, and that we should build the formalism from the embedded case outward, treating agent, world, and the agent's model of the world as one system from the start, even if it means giving up or weakening standard notions like a fixed utility function or a well-defined action space. The commitee is split, and I need to circulate a short memo before Thursday's meeting recommending fund A, fund B, fund a merged or sequenced version, or fund neither, with a concrete plan (what the project would actually produce in a year, plus any conditions or checkpoints). It should engage seriously with the strongest argument for whichever side I'm recommending against, and say what would count as evidence of conceptual progress so we aren't just funding unfalsifiable philosophy. We don't need engineering deliverables, but we do need traction.
