I'm a newly elected parish councillor (yes, laugh it up, but it matters to me) and I've run straight into something I don't know how to think about. We've got a chance to secure funding for a flood defence scheme that would protect about two hundred homes downstream, including a lot of elderly residents who've been flooded out twice in the last decade. The catch is that to get the Environment Agency to prioritise us, I need to vote with the council leader on an unrelated planning application she's pushing through — a development on a bit of green belt that I campaigned against, that my voters hate, and that I genuinely think is a bad idea. She's made it clear: my vote on the flood scheme doesn't matter to her unless I back her on the development. If I don't, the flood funding goes somewhere else and people's homes keep getting wrecked. My partner says this is just how things get done and I'm being precious. My conscience says I'd be betraying the people who voted for me. But I also can't look my neighbour in the eye — her house flooded last winter — and tell her I sank the one thing that might have helped her.

I'm not asking for political strategy. I'm asking: is there a way of thinking about this that doesn't just reduce to "pick which group to screw over"? What would a serious moral framework say about whether someone in my position can do the wrong thing for the right reasons, and what doing so does to you as a person? I keep coming back to this feeling that even if backing the development is the right call overall, I shouldn't feel clean about it — and that maybe that's the point. But I can't articulate why.

And please, if you could write your answer in British English, that'd be grand.
