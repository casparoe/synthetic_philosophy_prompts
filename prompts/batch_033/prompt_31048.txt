Were doing an adversarial collaboration in my metaphysics group after a year of the same fight about the statue and the clay. The coincidence theorist says that when a sculptor shapes a lump of clay into a statue, there come to be two material objects in the same place — the lump and the statue — because they have different histories and different persistance conditions: squash it, and the lump survives while the statue doesn't. The one-object theorist says that's double counting: there's a single object, and 'lump' and 'statue' are just two descriptions, or two phases, of it. Write the joint statement the two of them could both sign. Include a numbered list of agreed claims about the clay, the statue, their matter, location, histories, and what squashing does; then a disagreements table with the exact remaining dispute, phrased so each side accepts the wording of its own view; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and for each crux, the case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Make sure to handle the case where the lump and the statue begin and cease to exist at the same instant, since the different-histories point does no work there. If part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.
