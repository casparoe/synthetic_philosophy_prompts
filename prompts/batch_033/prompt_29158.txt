Okay so I've been going down a rabbit hole on epistemic injustice lately — it started because my department had this whole DEI training thing where they threw around "testimonial injustice" like everyone already knew what it meant, and I got curious. Now I've read Fricker and a bunch of the responses and... I'm kind of lost in the weeds.

Here's my problem. Every paper seems to just add another subtype. Fricker gives us testimonial and hermeneutical. Then Dotson adds contributory injustice. Then someone else has epistemic exploitation, willful hermeneutical ignorance, epistemic appropriation... and nobody seems to be asking the prior question: what is a theory of epistemic injustice even supposed to accomplish? What are the fixed points?

So I want the desiderata. What does an adequate theory of epistemic injustice have to get right? I don't mean "summarize the phenomena." I mean: which cases are the non-negotiable ones that any theory has to handle or it's not even in the game? Which distinctions can't be collapsed — like, is there a real difference between testimonial injustice and plain old prejudice, or are we just rebranding? What results from social epistemology or philosophy of language does the theory have to be consistent with (and which can it revise if it has to)? Which requirements are things everyone should agree on and which are just preferences of one camp?

For each desideratum, give me the case or consideration that earns it a spot on the list. And here's what I really want: are there pairs of desiderata that look like they can't be jointly satisfied? If covering both systematic and incidental cases makes the theory incoherent, say so. And which single requirement does the most work — like, which one knocks out the largest number of existing views?

I'm not asking for citations or a reading list. I want the specification. I'm trying to figure out whether this subfield has a stable core or whether its just people bolting epicycles onto Fricker's original two categories. Be honest about that last part.
