I help run a philosophy of probability reading group, and a dispute broke out last week that none of us has been able to settle. One member, a working statistician, kept reaching for the principle of indifference: the maxim that when one has no reason to favour one possibility over another, one should assign them equal probability. She used it to justify a uniform prior over a continuous parameter in a model of enzyme kinetics. Another member objected that a uniform prior does not stay uniform under reparameterisation — a distribution uniform in a reaction rate is not uniform in its reciprocal, nor in the corresponding half-life — and added that Bertrand's paradox shows this is not a repairable quirk. The same instruction to be indifferent about a chord of a circle gives different probabilities that the chord is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle depending on whether one picks two endpoints on the circumference, a point along a randomly chosen radius, or a midpoint uniformly within the circle. The statistician replied that the paradox only shows one must choose the right parameterisation, and that the principle is sound once it is restricted to the correct partition or the correct symmetries.

I would like to take the group through this as a disambiguation exercise rather than a debate. Please treat the principle of indifference as a slogan that can be precisified in several different ways. Lay out the distinct precise claims someone could mean by the maxim that, absent reasons to discriminate, one should assign equal probabilities; for each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible principle, or something false or incoherent. Then say which of these readings Bertrand's paradox actually refutes, which readings survive it, and which reading the statistician's reply about choosing the right parameterisation needs in order to work — and whether that reading can be stated without quietly assuming the very thing it is meant to justify.

Please write in British English and keep the answer to at most 900 words.
