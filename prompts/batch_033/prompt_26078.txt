I'm organizing a workshop on normative uncertainty and decision theory, and I want to produce a document that captures the state of the debate between expected value fanatics and their critics. Specifically, I want you to draft the joint statement that a committed expected value fanatic and a committed fanaticism-skeptic could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

By "expected value fanatic" I mean someone who accepts that we should follow the recommendations of expected value theory even when they seem extreme — Pascal's mugging, tiny probabilities of astronomical value swamping all other considerations, the St. Petersburg game, and so on. By "fanaticism-skeptic" I mean someone who thinks expected value reasoning needs to be constrained, bounded, or otherwise modified to avoid these results — whether through bounded utility functions, rejecting the requirement that expected utilities be defined over unbounded outcome spaces, discounting or ignoring sufficiently small probabilities, or some other structural move.

The document should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides agree on — about the formalism of expected value, about what makes fanatical cases troubling, about which proposed solutions both reject and why; (2) a disagreements table with precise formulations of where they part ways, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, the kind of argument or case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about what such an argument would show.

Be careful to separate genuine disagreements from verbal ones. If you think part of the dispute turns on different uses of "rational" or "should," say so explicitly and state what remains after that is set aside. The fanatic should not be portrayed as a straw man who simply hasn't noticed that tiny probabilities are tiny, and the skeptic should not be portrayed as someone who abandons expected value theory at the first sign of a counterintuitive result. Both positions have sophisticated defenders, and the document should reflect that.
