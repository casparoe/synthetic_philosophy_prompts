Suppose a private foundation will fund one four-person research group for five years to work on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality. The foundation's aim is not exegesis or history: it wants the group to spend its time on the open questions whose answers would do the most to reorganize the rest of the debate, in metaphysics and in adjacent areas such as the philosophy of science and meta-ontology. The group can realistically take on three or four questions.

What should its agenda be, and in what order? Please defend the ordering explicitly, rather than just listing the questions. For each candidate, I want to know: (a) how upstream it is, in the sense of how many other debates get easier, change shape, or dissolve once it is answered; (b) how tractable it is with the tools currently available, such as hyperintensional semantics, formal logic, case-based argument, and comparison with scientific explanation; (c) how neglected it is relative to its importance; and (d) what would count as real progress over the next few years — a theorem, a decisive counterexample, a new formal framework, or a good argument that the question was badly posed — and what would move it up or down the list.

Candidates you might weigh include: whether grounding is best understood as a relation or as an operation; what, if anything, grounds the facts about what grounds what; whether chains of ground must terminate and why; how grounding relates to essence, modality, and explanation; whether talk of a fundamental level belongs to metaphysics or is better left to physics; and whether there is one notion of ground or several. Do not treat this list as exhaustive. I am less interested in a survey of the field than in a defended allocation of effort: I want the reasoning that would let someone act on the ranking.
