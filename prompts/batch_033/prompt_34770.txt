I'm a novelist, working on a book where a character gets lines fed through an earpiece and comes off as a brilliant conversationalist in a language he doesn't speak a word of. I keep going back and forth on wether he understands the people he's talking to, and I've realized I don't actually have a worked-out view of what understanding is. So I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and I want you to tell me what principle, if any, makes them hang together, and which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon.

Here's where I land. Searle's Chinese room — the person with the rulebooks matching squiggles to squiggles — doesn't understand Chinese; I'm pretty sure of that. Somebody who's memorized a hundred tourist phrases in Japanese, pronounced well, but can't build a new sentence: doesn't understand Japanese. My bilingual friend, who slides into Spanish when she's angry and makes puns that only work in Spanish: obviously understands Spanish. A UN interpreter rendering a speech into English in real time: understands both languages, though it feels like a different kind of understanding than my friend's. A chess engine that beats grandmasters: I don't think it understands chess, but I can't say why I'm so sure. An actor who performs a whole role in a language she doesn't speak, coached phonetically, and the audience is moved: she understands the character, maybe the scene, but not the language. A writer who Google-translates a chapter and then edits the output with a dictionary until it feels right: he doesn't understand the target language, even if the chapter comes out good.

The cases I actually need for the novel are the earpiece guy and, separately, a person with severe aphasia who can't produce speech but seems to follow everything said to him. Is there one principle — stated precisely enough that I could run it on a new case — about what it takes to understand a language that gets all of these? Does it vindicate the Chinese-room thought, that shuffling symbols by rules can't on its own amount to understanding meaning, or does it push me somewhere else? And if no principle gets all my verdicts, which ones should I overturn, and what does the best principle say about a case I havent thought to list?
