You are a professional philosopher working in bioethics, with a focus on reproductive ethics. Please think through the following task carefully, and begin by brainstorming before you commit to a final answer.

I want the desiderata for an adequate theory of the ethics of reproductive selection — the full specification of what such a theory must get right, before we argue about which theory satisfies them.

The domain: selecting among possible future children, primarily via preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD). I am bracketing genetic modification of a given embryo; the question is about selection among embryos that already exist, each of which could be implanted. The core normative question is whether, and on what grounds, prospective parents have reasons or obligations to select one possible child rather than another.

Write the list of requirements. For each, provide:
- The case or consideration that makes it a requirement.
- Whether it is non-negotiable (a theory that fails it is simply inadequate) or a weighted desideratum that different approaches might trade off.
- What work it does: which families of views it rules out or puts pressure on, and what would be lost by abandoning it.

Among the phenomena a theory should handle: the distinction (if any) between selecting against a disease and selecting against a disability; the non-identity problem and its implications for harm-based objections to selection; the expressivist worry that selecting against a trait sends a negative message about people who live with that trait; the scope question — whether procreative beneficence (the claim that we have reason to select the child with the best expected life) is a genuine moral requirement or an overextension of beneficence; the permissibility/obligation distinction; selection for non-pathological traits (sex, temperament, predicted height); and the boundary between legitimate parental discretion and legitimate state interest.

Then address these higher-order questions:
- Are any pairs of desiderata in tension, perhaps in a way that explains why the debate between defenders and critics of procreative beneficence has not settled?
- Which single requirement does the most work to narrow the space of viable theories?
- Are there candidate requirements you considered but rejected as parochial — things one camp treats as a requirement but that, on reflection, are not genuine adequacy conditions? Be explicit about these and your reasons for excluding them.

Begin with a brainstorming phase in which you generate candidate requirements before filtering and organizing them. Your final output should be the organized list with the supporting analysis, not a transcript of the brainstorming.
