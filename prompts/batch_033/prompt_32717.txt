I've been arguing trolley problems and medical ethics cases with my friend M for months now. Here's her track record:

- Switch: flip it, fine.
- Push: don't push.
- The loop case (side track loops back and the one person's body is what stops the trolley from hitting the five): she says flip it — same as the switch.
- The footbridge lever case (you pull a lever that opens a trapdoor, dropping the large man onto the tracks): she says don't pull it.
- A doctor removes a cancerous uterus from a pregnant woman to save her life; the fetus dies as a side effect: she says this is fine.
- A doctor performs a craniotomy on a fetus (crushing its skull) to save the mother's life during obstructed labor: she says this is wrong.

Next week we're discussing a case where a trolley is heading toward five people. You can flip a switch to divert it onto an empty side track. The side track runs through a narrow cutting where a hiker is trapped. The trolley will hit and kill the hiker, and the impact will slow it down enough that the five can escape before the track rejoins the main line. So the hiker's death is causally necessary for saving the five — if the hiker weren't there, the trolley would continue unimpeded and still hit the five. What's M going to say? What rule is she running, what other rules fit her verdicts just as well, and what's the one further case that would settle which rule it really is?
