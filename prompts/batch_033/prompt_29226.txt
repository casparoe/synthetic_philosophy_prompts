What are the most important implications of Adam Smith's idea of the impartial spectator for how I should assess my own resentment after a conflict in which I am convinced I was wronged? I understand the impartial spectator as the standpoint I reach by trying to sympathize with an uninvolved observer of my own situation. I can see how that standpoint corrects my tendency to exaggerate my own grievances, but I am less sure whether it can also correct me in the other direction — for instance, when I have persuaded myself to forgive too quickly or to treat my own anger as something I ought to suppress. I would like the answer to focus on the practical guidance this idea offers for self-examination, and to be candid about the points at which the impartial spectator gives less guidance than one might hope.
