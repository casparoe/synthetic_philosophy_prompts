I've been reading Lakatos on research programmes and I think I've found an argument that his framework, if taken seriously, undermines the very distinction it was meant to secure.

(1) According to Lakatos, a research programme consists of a hard core of theoretical commitments, protected from falsification by a negative heuristic, and a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, modified according to a positive heuristic.
(2) A programme is progressive when its modifications lead to novel predictions that are corroborated; it is degenerative when modifications are ad hoc adjustments that merely accommodate already-known facts.
(3) But whether a modification is ad hoc or progressive can only be judged retrospectively, often over decades. In the midst of a programme's development, there is no fact of the matter about whether it is progressive or degenerative.
(4) Lakatos himself conceded that rational appraisal applies only to sequences of theories over time, not to individual theories, and that one can never rationally declare a programme dead—it may always stage a comeback.
(5) If (3) and (4) hold, then at any given moment, for any research programme, the rational attitude is to suspend judgment about whether it is scientific or pseudoscientific.
(6) But Lakatos's whole project was to improve on Popper's demarcation criterion by distinguishing science from pseudoscience—astronomy from astrology—without Popper's excessive falsificationism.
(7) If the criterion can only be applied retrospectively and never licenses a current judgment, it fails as a demarcation criterion precisely where we want one: deciding, now, whether to fund, teach, or take seriously a given programme.
(8) Therefore, Lakatos's framework either collapses into a trivial historicism that cannot guide current epistemic decisions, or it must be supplemented with forward-looking criteria he never supplied—at which point it is no longer distinctively Lakatosian.

Where does this argument go wrong? I can see pressure points at (3)—maybe there are synchronic indicators—and at (7)—maybe demarcation need not guide real-time decisions. But I can't tell which line of resistance costs the least. Diagnose the flaw or identify the premise you would reject, and explain what giving it up does to the broader Lakatosian picture.
