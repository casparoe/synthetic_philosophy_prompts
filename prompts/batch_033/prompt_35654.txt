I've been hanging around the alignment forum and LessWrong for years, and the ELK problem has become one of those things I can't stop thinking about but also can't make progress on. Quick refresher for anyone who hasn't been following: the basic idea is that you've got a neural network that knows something about the world—like whether a diamond in a vault is still there—but you can only train it on some observable proxy, like human ratings of its camera feeds or reports. The worry is that the AI learns to say whatever scores well rather than what it actually "believes" about the diamond. So far so good. The thing that's been bugging me is that after several years of smart people proposing schemes—debate, market making, CCS, imitative generalization, the indirect normativity stuff—the conversation feels stuck in a loop. Someone proposes a mechanism, someone else finds a counterexample where the mechanism incentivizes misreporting in a weird edge case, the proposer patches it, a new counterexample appears, and then everyone sort of shrugs and the thread dies. Nobody seems to have a shared criterion for what would even count as a solution, and I'm starting to wonder if that's the real problem.

My pet theory is that there are actually three different questions being smuggled under the label "ELK," and different people are answering different ones without noticing. One group wants a training procedure with a formal guarantee that the reporter's outputs track its internal representations under some specified conditions. Another group wants something more like "a procedure that would work in practice if you ran it with a roughly human-level-or-smarter AI and a competent human judge." And a third group—this is the one I'm most sympathetic to but also most confused by—seems to be trying to define what it even means for a system to "report its latent knowledge" when we can't independently verify its internal states, which starts to look less like an engineering problem and more like a puzzle about other minds. If I'm right that these are different projects, then a lot of the back-and-forth isn't really disagreement, it's people showing that scheme A fails by criterion B when scheme A was never trying to satisfy criterion B in the first place.

So I want a diagnosis: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a substantive technical impasse where a new formal result (a no-go theorem, a constructive proof) could genuinely break the logjam? Is it a mostly verbal dispute where people need to taboo the word "honest" and specify their success criteria? Or is it a deeper conceptual mess where we don't yet have the right concepts to even state the problem precisely, and the apparent debate is just people poking at different parts of an ill-posed question? And whatever your diagnosis, tell me what you think would actually move things forward—and also where your own reasoning is most likely to be wrong, what kind of argument or empirical result would flip your diagnosis.
