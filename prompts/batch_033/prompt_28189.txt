I run a rationality meetup and I want to put together a "which interpretation of probability do you actually hold?" diagnostic quiz. The point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label sounds good to them — so no question should just ask "are you a frequentist?"

The quiz should have eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete scenario where different interpretations of probability pull apart. The interpretations I want to sort into are: classical frequentism (probability as limiting relative frequency in actual or hypothetical repeated trials), subjectivism (probability as coherent degree of belief), propensity views (probability as an objective physical tendency of a setup to produce outcomes), and logical/evidential probability (probability as a logical relation between evidence and hypothesis, à la Carnap or Jaynes). If there are other distinct positions that naturally fall out of the answer patterns, include them; if some combinations of answers are logically possible but no one holds them, flag that.

The quiz should force trade-offs. For example, include cases where a frequentist and a propensity theorist give different answers about a single-case physical probability (like a particular uranium atom decaying), cases where a subjectivist and a logical probabilist disagree about whether two people with the same evidence must assign the same probability, and cases where a frequentist has to say something awkward about a one-off event that clearly has *some* probability.

For the response, please provide:

1. **The quiz questions** (roughly 8–10 questions, each a short paragraph setting up a concrete case with 3–4 answer options that map onto different interpretations; no question should use the names of the interpretations in its wording or answer choices).

2. **The scoring key** (a table or clear mapping from answer patterns to positions, with enough detail that I can score someone's answers by hand in a few minutes).

3. **The position descriptions** (one paragraph per position, explaining what someone sorted into that bucket actually believes about probability, in plain language, with one sentence on the most awkward commitment of the view).

4. **What the quiz can't separate** (a short section flagging any pairs of positions the quiz cannot reliably distinguish, and any answer-pattern combinations that are logically possible but unoccupied — and your best guess at why nobody is there: incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored).
