I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I am looking for a case analysis rather than a summary or a moral verdict. In November 2018, the researcher He Jiankui announced that he had used CRISPR–Cas9 to edit the CCR5 gene in human embryos, with the stated aim of conferring resistance to HIV; twin girls with edited genomes had been born. The announcement was met with near-universal condemnation from the scientific community, and the episode is now treated as a cautionary tale about a rogue actor.

I would like you to analyze this episode through the lens of the unilateralist's curse. As I understand the curse: when many agents can each unilaterally take an action that imposes an outcome on everyone, and each agent decides using only her own noisy estimate of whether the action is good, the group as a whole will act whenever the most optimistic agent acts. The result can be an outcome that most agents, and the group's average judgment, would have rejected. The standard proposed remedy is something like a principle of conformity: in such situations an agent should defer to the group's estimate unless she has special reason to trust her own judgment over the group's.

Please give me a disciplined mapping of the episode onto that model, and then an honest account of where the model fails. Specifically:

1. Identify the relevant actors who could have acted unilaterally to move the world toward heritable human genome editing — not only He himself, but other researchers, labs, funders, regulators, journals, and institutions. For each, what were the actual options, what information did each have, and what would have counted as the action?

2. State the unilateralist's curse precisely as a mechanism, and say which of its preconditions held in this case and which did not. For example, were the actors' estimates independent? Was the harmful outcome truly the product of a single unilateral act, or did it require an IVF pipeline, collaborators, funders, and an institution? Does the fact that the act had to be announced publicly to have its world-changing effect alter the analysis?

3. Apply the principle of conformity to the episode. By the principle's lights, who should have deferred to the group's judgment and did not? Who was actually better placed than the group to judge? Does the principle give the right verdicts here, or does it misidentify where the failure occurred?

4. Identify what the curse cannot explain: He's motives, the incentive structure of science (priority, prestige, first-mover advantage), the regulatory environment, the gap between existing community norms and their enforcement, or anything else you find relevant. For each, say whether it is a genuine anomaly for the theory or merely outside its scope.

5. Run the analysis in the other direction: what does this episode show about the unilateralist's curse as a model? Does it confirm it, reveal a variant the original model did not anticipate, or expose an ambiguity in what counts as the action or the group? Be explicit about where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.

I am less interested in whether He was right or wrong than in whether the curse actually illuminates why the system failed. Where the model breaks, say so plainly.
