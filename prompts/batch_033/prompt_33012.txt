I direct communications for a state public health agency. We design campaigns to shift behavior—vaccination uptake, smoking cessation, opioid awareness—and we routinely use framing, social-norms messaging, emotional appeals, and defaults. We are also regularly accused of manipulation. I need a decision procedure my team can run on a proposed technique before a campaign launches: a sequence of questions that distinguishes legitimate persuasion from manipulation, with clear stopping rules and guidance on what counts as a sufficient answer to each. It should draw on the best philosophical work on this distinction but be usable by a communications professional without a philosophy background. Flag every step where the procedure hands off to human judgment rather than delivering a mechanical result. Then run the procedure on two cases: (1) a campaign that uses graphic images of diseased lungs to deter teen smoking, and (2) a campaign that frames vaccine refusal as letting down your community. For at least one of these, make sure the procedure returns an answer my team might resist. Close by saying what the procedure cannot handle—what kinds of cases it will misclassify or leave unsettled. Throughout, indicate how confident you are in each key claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
