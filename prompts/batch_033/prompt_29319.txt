"Functionalism is the view that mental states are defined by what they do, not by what they're made of. Pain just is the state that's typically caused by tissue damage and that causes wincing, avoidance, and the desire to make it stop. The big selling point is multiple realizability: we know from comparative biology that very different nervous systems can be in pain — an octopus's pain can't be the same physical state as a human's — so pain can't be identical to any one physical state like C-fiber firing. Functionalism handles this by saying that any system with the right internal program has the mental state, whether the hardware is neurons, silicon, or something else entirely. That in turn shows mental states aren't physical states at all; they're abstract functional states, the way software is independent of hardware. The standard objection is that functionalism leaves out consciousness: a system could satisfy the entire functional profile of pain and yet feel nothing — the absent qualia problem — so the view is probably false."
