I'm a philosophy PhD student working on well-being, and I keep going in circles on a debate with a friend in the psych department — specifically the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. The setup is familiar but it won't leave me alone: Bentham says pushpin is as good as poetry, but the things we lump together as 'pleasure' look completely different from each other. The warm fog of a morphine drip, the little spike of schadenfreude when someone you dislike screws up, the pleasure of finally seeing why a proof works, an orgasm, the relief when a pain stops — what do these actually have in common? If the honest answer is 'not much,' then either 'pleasure' is a disjunctive grab-bag and hedonism looses the unifying explanation that made it attractive, or there's some single underlying property I keep missing. The standard fix is to say the common element isn't a sensation at all but a propositional attitude — enjoying that something is the case — but then it's less obvious that what remains deserves the name 'pleasure' or that it can still do the work the theory needs.

Here's what I'd like from you. Take the proposition: hedonism about well-being has a defensible answer to the heterogeneity problem — i.e., there is a non-gerrymandered property (or a small, principled set of properties) common to all and only pleasant states, and that property can plausibly bear the normative weight hedonism puts on it. Give me your credence in that proposition, as a number. Then decompose it. I want at least: (1) the probability that a unified property exists at all, rather than 'pleasure' being a family-resemblance concept; (2) given that it exists, the probability that the property is normatively significant rather than a mere neurological or functional commonality; (3) the probability that hedonism wins even if it survives this objection, rather than dying to desire-satisfaction or objective-list problems; and any other terms you think belong. Assign a rough number to each, show how you combine them, and tell me which term your final credence is most sensitive to. For calibration: I'm around 0.35, my friend is around 0.7. Say which term you'd bet our disagreement actually lives in, and what evidence would move that term the most.
