Here's how I understand Hayek's point: the information needed to run an economy — consumer preferences, production costs, resource availability — is dispersed across millions of people, and no central authority can gather and process it all. Markets solve this through the price mechanism, which aggregates dispersed information without anyone needing to collect it centrally.

But here's what I don't get: we now have distributed systems that process petabytes of data daily. Google's ad auction runs millions of transactions per second, each one essentially solving a resource allocation problem under constraints. Recommendation systems infer preferences from behavior at scale. Supply chain management software already coordinates production across thousands of suppliers. If the problem is just one of gathering and processing dispersed information, haven't we essentially solved it? Why couldn't a central planning agency deploy something like a massive distributed optimization system — fed by sensors, purchase data, and production telemetry — that computes shadow prices and allocation plans in near real-time? It seems like the calculation debate was really about computational tractability, and Moore's law settled it.
