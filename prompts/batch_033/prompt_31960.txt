I teach a final-year undergraduate seminar in metaethics, and I am drafting the take-home essay question for the end-of-term assessment. The course has covered cognitivist and non-cognitivist approaches to moral language in some depth, and students have spent two weeks on the Frege–Geach problem and the major responses to it. I want a question that cannot be answered adequately by summarising what expressivism is and then gesturing at the embedding problem. The question should force students to engage with the underlying architectural challenge, and to show that they grasp why a solution that merely handles simple conditionals has not yet solved the problem.

Please write the following:

1. **The essay question itself.** It should be answerable in roughly 2,000 words. The question should push students to identify what the Frege–Geach problem is *a problem about* — that is, what feature of expressivism it threatens, and why that feature matters. It should also require them to evaluate at least one specific attempted solution by reference to that feature, not merely to describe the solution.

2. **A model answer** of approximately 600–800 words, written in British English. The model answer should be the kind of response that would earn a high first-class mark (roughly a 75 or above in the UK system). It should be concise but precise, and it should demonstrate the kind of argumentative structure and conceptual clarity that distinguishes excellent work from merely competent work.

3. **A marking scheme** that separates answers into four bands (First, Upper Second, Lower Second, Third/Fail). For each band, name the specific moves, insights, or errors that place an answer in that band. The scheme should make clear that a script can earn a First only if it identifies the *architectural* character of the problem (as opposed to treating it as a superficial puzzle about conditionals) and evaluates a solution in those terms. Include a short note on the most common misunderstanding that separates Upper Second scripts from Firsts.

The question and model answer should both be written in British English.
