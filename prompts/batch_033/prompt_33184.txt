So I'm running a discussion group at my university on political philosophy — it's mostly undergrads who've done maybe one intro course — and we've been going around in circles on the paradox of tolerance for like three weeks now. You know the setup: if a society is tolerant without limit, the intolerant will eventually use that tolerance to seize power and destroy the tolerant order. But if you suppress them, you're being intolerant yourself, which seems to undermind the very principle you're supposed to be defending. Popper said something about this, Rawls too, and every week someone just name-drops one of them and then we stall out arguing about what they actually meant rather than thinking through the problem.

I want to cut through that. What I'd like is a decision tree — or a taxonomy, whichever format works better — that starts from first principles and sorts people into the actual logical positions on the paradox, not into author-labeled boxes. The tree should be built from the diagnostic questions that genuinely split the positions: things like whether tolerance is a moral principle or a purely prudential strategy, whether the status of being "intolerant" is something you can lose your claim to tolerance over, whether the paradox is even a genuine paradox or just a confusion built into how we talk about tolerance, whether the intolerant have any sort of right to be tolerated, and so on. I want each branch labeled with a concrete yes/no question that someone could answer without already knowing which position they're walking toward.

Each leaf should be a distinct resolution of the paradox — named if it has a name, or "unnamed" if nobody defends it — and I want to see the unoccupied branches too, with a short note about why nobody's there (is the position incoherent? unattractive? just unexplored?). And if there are two positions the tree can't cleanly separate, flag them instead of pretending the tree is sharper than it is. The audience is beginners, so the questions need to be in plain English, not jargon. Aim for maybe six to ten leaves. Basically I want the thing to function like one of those "which political philosophy are you?" quizzes, except it actually sorts people by what they believe about the cases and claims rather than by which label they pick.
