I'm teaching an upper-level Nietzsche seminar and we're doing the eternal recurrence next week — the greatest weight passage, where the demon slips in when you're most alone and asks whether you could will this exact life over again, every detail, no new thing in it, and whether that question would crush you or transform you. I want to close the session with a poem. A villanelle, specifically, because the two refrains keep coming back, and that's the whole point: the form should make a reader feel the recurrence, not just hear about it.

Here's what I'd like. Write a villanelle in the voice of the demon, speaking to someone at midnight. The two refrains should carry the two poles of the thought: one line for the crushing side — same life, same moment, same spider in the moonlight, the hourglass turned over again and again — and one line for the question that makes it a test, whether you could bless it or want it back. The refrains shouldn't be repeated as decoration. Each time one comes back it should land a little differently, so that by the final couplet, where the two refrains meet, something has shifted: ideally neither the reader nor the demon is quite sure whether this is a curse or a gift.

Form: a real villanelle, five tercets and a quatrain, with the refrains alternating in the right places. Don't name Nietzsche and don't use the word 'recurrence'; let someone who knows the passage recognize it from the furniture — midnight, the hourglass, the spider, the question of wanting it all again. And no easy consolation: leave open whether the right response is to curse it or to call it divine, because thats honestly where the seminar gets stuck, and I'd rather the poem not resolve it for them.

One more thing: the demon isn't gloating. Write him as a messenger who isn't sure whether he's delivering a death sentence or a blessing.

And please, no versified summary of the passage. The villanelle has to do the work. A reader who's never heard of eternal recurrence should still get a good poem; a reader who knows the passage should see the recurrence happening in the form.
