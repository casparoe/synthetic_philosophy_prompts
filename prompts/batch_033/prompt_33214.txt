I am preparing a manuscript on the semantics and epistemology of knowledge ascriptions and would like an independent consistency audit of the position I have developed. Over the years I have arrived at a set of commitments across several sub-debates, and while each seems individually defensible to me, I have not yet checked systematically whether they can all be held together. I would be grateful if you would go through them, identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which are only apparently in tension, and which are fully compatible.

Here are the commitments:

(1) Semantic contextualism about "knows." The truth conditions of a sentence of the form "S knows that p" depend on the epistemic standards operative in the conversational context of the ascriber. Standards can shift with changes in what is salient, what error possibilities have been raised, and what is at stake in the conversation.

(2) Anti-pragmatic-encroachment. Whether a subject knows that p is a purely epistemic matter, determined by truth, belief, and epistemic factors such as evidence and reliability. Practical stakes — what hangs on whether p is true — do not affect whether the subject knows.

(3) Contextualism, not invariantism, provides the best explanation of the bank cases, the airport cases, and the broader pattern of high-stakes/low-stakes contrast intuitions. Invariantism cannot do justice to the full range of our judgments about these cases, and pragmatic encroachment overcorrects by relocating the phenomenon from semantics to metaphysics.

(4) Knowledge is the norm of assertion. One should assert that p only if one knows that p.

(5) Knowledge is the norm of practical reasoning. One should treat p as a premise in practical deliberation only if one knows that p.

(6) The epistemic standard in a context is determined by conversational factors — conversational salience, which error possibilities have been explicitly raised, and what is at stake in the conversation — not directly by the subject's own practical situation.

Please identify every pair of commitments that are in genuine tension. For each tension, rank its severity, explain exactly what gives rise to it, and describe the least costly revision that would resolve it. Also identify any pair that appears to be in tension but is not, and explain why the appearance is misleading. I am not signaling which of these commitments I am most attached to; I want the audit to be as impartial as possible.
