I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on population ethics, and I've been going around in circles trying to figure out what I actually think about when one population is better than another. I've got a bunch of concrete verdicts that feel solid to me but I can't find a principle that gets all of them, and I'm starting to wonder whether some of them have to go. Here's where I am:

1. World A has a billion people all at welfare level 100 (on a scale where 0 is a life barely worth living). World A+ has the same billion people at 100 plus another billion people at welfare 1. My gut says A+ is not worse than A. Adding lives worth living doesn't make the world worse.

2. But then: World B has two billion people all at welfare 50. This seems clearly better than A+ to me — same total welfare, much less inequality, nobody stuck at that barely-worth-it level. So A+ is not worse than A, but B is better than A+. That already feels like it might commit me to something I don't see yet.

3. Now the famous one: World Z has some enormous number of people — say a trillion — all at welfare 0.1. Just barely worth living. My intuition is that Z is worse than A. I just can't accept that a vast number of lives barely above zero is better than a billion flourishing lives. But total welfare in Z is way higher than in A, and if I keep applying the reasoning from (1) and (2) I seem to get driven there.

4. Here's another case that bothers me. World C has a billion people at welfare 100. World D has the same billion people at 100 plus a hundred people at welfare -50 (lives not worth living). My gut says D is definitely worse than C. Adding miserable lives makes things worse. But now suppose World E has a billion people at welfare 100, and World F has the same billion at 105 plus a hundred at -50. The average is higher in F. Do I still think F is worse? I'm honestly not sure. This feels like it should matter but I can't settle it.

5. World G has a billion people at welfare 100. World H has the same billion at 99 plus a billion new people at 99. I think H is better than G. Even though each original person is slightly worse off, the gain from the new people swamps it. But if I keep iterating this — shaving a little off everyone to make room for more people at just below the previous level — I end up at the repugnant conclusion again. So this verdict is in direct tension with verdict (3).

6. One more: World I has a billion people at welfare -10 (lives not worth living). World J has two billion people at welfare -10. I think J is worse than I. More suffering is worse, full stop. But if I think adding positive lives doesn't make things worse (case 1), shouldn't I think adding negative lives doesn't make things better? That symmetry feels forced on me but I don't want it.

So I've got these six verdicts and they're pulling in different directions. I'm drawn to something like a person-affecting view where only existing people matter, but then I can't make sense of case (5) where I think creating new people at 99 is clearly good. I'm drawn to total utilitarianism but then I have to swallow the repugnant conclusion. I'm drawn to average utilitarianism but then adding lives at 1 (case 1) makes things worse, which I reject. I'm drawn to something with a lexical threshold — maybe once welfare is low enough, quantity doesn't compensate — but that feels ad hoc and I don't know where to put the cutoff.

What principle, if any, captures my verdicts? Which ones do I have to give up? And please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure about.
