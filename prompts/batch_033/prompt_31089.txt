I keep coming back to the van Meegeren Vermeer forgeries and I'd like you to actually work through them with me rather than give the usual line that value is subjective. I'm a software engineer, so my instinct is that if two objects are pixel-for-pixel identical, any difference in how we treat them is a bug in our valuation, not a real difference in the objects. The case that bugs me is Han van Meegeren, the Dutch forger who painted The Supper at Emmaus and passed it off as a newly discovered Vermeer. From what I understand: the Boijmans Museum in Rotterdam bought it in 1937 for a huge sum, leading critics treated it as a masterpiece, and then after the war van Meegeren was arrested for selling a Vermeer to Göring. To dodge the more serious collaboration charge he confessed that the paintings were his own forgeries, and proved it by painting another Vermeer under guard. The Emmaus went from being one of the most admired Vermeers to a laughable fake almost overnight, even though the physical object never changed.

What I want is an analysis of that episode through the lens of the debate about the value of originals. Spell out what a view that says aesthetic value is fixed entirely by the perceivable properties of a work (so a perfect forgery is exactly as good as the original) would have to say about the museum's, the critics', and the public's change of heart: were they making a mistake after the reveal, or was the earlier enthusiasm the mistake? And what does the episode do to that view? The collapse in market value and the genuine-seeming disappointment could be evidence against it, or the view could explain those away as art-historical interest, investment value, or embarrassment rather than aesthetic judgment. Then run it the other direction: what does the perceivable-properties view, and the rival views that make origin or the artist's achievement matter, capture and fail to capture about what actually happened? I want a careful mapping rather than a slogan. And if the honest answer is that the two sides are talking past eachother because aesthetic value is being used in two different senses, say that, but then be explicit about what each sense is and which one was doing the real work in the van Meegeren reactions.

Please write the answer in British English.
