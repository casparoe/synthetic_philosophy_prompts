Before I read any more proposed solutions to Fitch's paradox, I want the requirements list first. What does a solution have to do to count as actually solving the paradox, rather than quarantining the problem or changing the subject? I'm thinking of things like: preserve a recognizable version of the anti-realist claim that every truth is knowable, without collapsing into the plainly false claim that every truth is actually known; say exactly which step of the derivation it blocks and why; respect the difference between "knowable" and "known"; explain why the knowability principle looked so plausible in the first place; and not contradict settled points like the factivity of knowledge or the distributivity of knowledge over conjunction unless it explicitly takes that bullet and says why. For each requirement, give the consideration behind it. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable constraints on any solution and which are just tastes of particular camps (intuitionists, restrictionists, actuality-operator people, whatever), and flag any subset of the list that looks jointly unsatisfiable — if the paradox shows that some combinations can't all be had, that's the part I most want spelled out. Skip the scorecard; I'll do that myself. And do please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff, rather than presenting one camp's view as the consensus.
