I teach an upper-level undergraduate philosophy of science seminar whose final unit covers the problem of induction and formal learning theory. I am setting the take-home final, and I want one essay question on the relationship between the no-free-lunch theorems in machine learning and optimization, associated with Wolpert and Macready, and Hume's problem of induction. The question should be answerable in about 900 words and must not be answerable by simply reciting Hume's argument or stating the theorems. It should force students to work out the exact logical relationship between the two: what a formal vindication of Hume's skeptical conclusion would actually have to show, what a no-free-lunch theorem really establishes, and where the analogy between the two is only partial. Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 900 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 marks. The marking scheme should name the specific moves an A answer must contain, the marks each move is worth, and the specific mistakes that should lose marks, with a note on which misunderstanding each mistake is catching. For example, a good answer should not treat the uniform distribution over all possible target functions as the absence of assumptions, and it should not read the theorems as showing that no learning method can ever succeed in practice. Finally, append a short note stating how confident you are in your characterization of both Hume's argument and the no-free-lunch theorems, and identify the single point you are least sure about.
