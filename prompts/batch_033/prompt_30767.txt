I've spent a while reading the consciousness literature and the rationalist-adjacent philosophy of mind stuff, and I've landed pretty firmly on the Dennett-style line about the color phi phenomenon and the timing of experience. For anyone who needs the setup: in the color phi experiments, subjects see a red dot flash at one spot and then a green dot at another spot, and they report a single dot moving between them that changes from red to green around the midpoint — which is weird, because the green dot hasn't actually appeared yet at the moment the color change is experienced. The standrad question is whether this is Orwellian revision (you consciously saw the real red-then-green sequence and your memory got rewritten after the fact) or Stalinesque revision (your conscious experience itself was delayed, so the green got inserted into the "show" before you ever became conscious of it). My view is the Dennett/Kinsbourne one: this is a false alternative. There's no Cartesian theater, no single place and time where "the" experience happens, so there's no fact of the matter about whether the revision happened before or after consciousness. Asking whether it's Orwellian or Stalinesque is like asking whether a novel was edited before or after publication when the edits happen across multiple drafts that are all simultaneously the novel.

Not just the objections I've already heard a million times (it's unfalsifiable, it's behaviorism in disguise), but a real map of its exposure. You're a philosopher of mind who actually knows the empirical timing-of-consciousness literature — backward masking, the flash-lag effect, the cutaneous rabbit, metacontrast masking, Libet-style readiness potentials, the whole mess. Start by brainstorming the strongest candidate defeaters, then sort them from decisive to merely awkward. For each one, say what would have to be true in the experiment or in the argument for the no-fact-of-the-matter view to actually lose, and say what a committed Dennettian would have to concede counts against the view rather than just explaining away. Also tell me what the no-fact-of-the-matter view predicts about other timing illusions that the Orwellian and Stalinesque stories would get wrong, and whether there's a version of the view that's been hedged so far that nothing could count against it — and if so, whether that's a bug or just what you'd expect from a claim about the nonexistence of a Cartesian theater.

Be as specific as you can about what an actual experiment would have to show: not just "neural evidence for one story over the other," but what kind of temporal resolution, what kind of readout, and which background assumption would have to fail for it to count. I genuinely want to know where this position is load-bearing and where it's just ruling out a picture.
