Spent the whole evening arguing with a mate about free speech, and I've come away unsure what principle I actually hold. Going on my gut, here's where I landed on cases. Defamation: fine to have laws against it. Doxxing: also fine. Hate speech: I can't bring myself to support criminalising it, even when the speech makes me sick. Holocaust denial: not a crime, but I don't think a students' union owes the guy a platform. Yelling fire in a crowded theatre: obviously punishable. Publishing a step-by-step guide to making a bomb: I'd ban it. The Charlie Hebdo cartoons: I'd defend the right to publish them. A colleague repeatedly and deliberately misgendering someone: I'd count that as harassment. A tabloid printing a knowing lie about a celebrity's private life: I'd let them sue for damages, but I wouldn't prosecute.

My mate says the whole thing is just Mill's harm principle: the only ground for restricting speech is preventing harm to others. I want to believe him, but I can't see how it fits banning doxxing (who exactly is harmed by the publication itself?) while refusing to criminalise hate speech (which does seem to harm people). So: is there a principle, or a small set of them, that actually captures my verdicts, and which of my verdicts would it force me to give up? Be precise about what "harm" would have to mean for the principle to do the work, and say what your account predicts for a case I haven't listed: my town letting a neo-Nazi group hold a rally, with counter-protesters promising to turn up.

And if the whole way I've framed this is confused — say, I'm smuggling in two seperate questions, what should be legal versus what a university should platform — tell me that straight rather than papering over it. Don't gerrymander a principle that fits all nine verdicts at the cost of saying nothing.
