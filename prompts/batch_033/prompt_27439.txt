I run procurement for a mid-size city government and I keep seeing projects turn into dollar auctions — a contractor underbids, we're locked in, change orders pile up, and by year three we're paying double because pulling out would mean paying someone else to redo the work from scratch. I need a procedure my team can use at the RFP stage to flag which procurements are structurally vulnerable to escalation traps, not just which bids look suspiciously low. It should be something we can run in an afternoon for a given RFP, it should distinguish between ordinary competitive pressure and genuine trap structure, and it should be honest about when the procedure says "don't structure the procurement this way" and my boss won't like the answer. Apply it to two cases: a custom software migration where the vendor will have all the institutional knowledge by month six, and a bridge repair where the contractor who does the initial assessment is also bidding for the work.
