Epistemic logic in the Hintikka tradition models knowledge as truth in all epistemically accessible worlds. This straightforwardly entails that agents know all logical truths and all logical consequences of whatever they know — the problem of logical omniscience. This isn't a minor edge case; it means the formalism attributes to agents cognitive capacities no actual agent possesses. I understand that there are patches (impossible worlds, awareness operators, syntactic approaches), but these seem to sacrifice the elegance that made the framework attractive in the first place. So here's what I'm stuck on: if the core semantics gets the most basic fact about real epistemic agents wrong, and the fixes amount to bolting on epicycles, why should we treat the possible-worlds approach as anything more than a historical curiosity? What ongoing value does it have that justifies its continued centrality in the field?
