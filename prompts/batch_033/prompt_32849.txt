My wife and I are going through IVF, and we've got five embryos that all came back chromosomally normal after PGT-A. Now our clinic offers this extra screening — polygenic risk scores — that gives you a predicted ranking of each embryo for traits like cognitive ability, educational attainment, and risk for various conditions like diabetes or depression later in life. My wife wants to use these scores to pick which embryo to transfer. Her reasoning is: we already chose to screen out aneuploid embryos, so we're already in the business of selecting; if we're going to pick one of these five anyway, why wouldnt we pick the one with the best projected life outcomes? She acknowledges the scores are probabilistic and the differences between embryos are small, but her argument is that even a small edge is better than none, and its weird to deliberately ignore information that could benefit our kid. I'm not religious, I don't think natural means good, and honestly I can't find a logical flaw in what she's saying. But the whole thing makes me feel viscerally wrong in a way I can't articulate. I find myself wanting to say "we shouldnt be treating our future child like a product we optimize" but I'm not even sure if that objection holds up under scrutiny. Is there a real philosophical problem here, or is this just old-fashioned discomfort with a new technology that I'll get over?
