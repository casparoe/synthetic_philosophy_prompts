we spent two weeks on hofstadter's "a person paper on purity in language" — the one from Metamagical Themas where he does the full gender-role swap, 'person' for 'man,' 'woperson' for waitress, 'herstory' for history, and by the end you viscerally feel what it's like when your language's default isn't you. the students got really into it, which is rare for a philosophy class, so i want to reward the ones who actually *got* it rather than the ones who just memorized the summary slide.

here's my problem: i want a question that can't be answered by just regurgitating the piece. the bright-ish students who can say "language shapes thought" and gesture at sapir-whorf should not automatically get an A. the whole mechanism of the person paper is that it works via emotional jiu-jitsu rather than by laying out premises and conclusions — the satire *shows* you something that an analytic argument would have to *argue for*, and that difference is itself philosophically interesting. i want a question that gets at that.
