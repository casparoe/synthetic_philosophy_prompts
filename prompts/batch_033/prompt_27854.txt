Ok so I've been turning this over in my head. The copyright office in the US (and a lot of other places do something similar) won't register an image that was made entirely by an AI image generator, because they say there's no human author. But if a person prompts the thing, picks from a bunch of outputs, tweaks the prompt, then does a little editing, they'll register it. So the line seems to be somewhere in how much the human did. What view of creativity or authorship is that practice actually presupposing? It can't just be that a human made choices, because photography is also mostly a machine doing the work and photos get copyright all the time. Is the implicit theory that the artist has to control the details of the output, or that creativity is a kind of intention, or something else? And is the practice consistant with the way the same office treats photographs, collages, or a piece of conceptual art where the artist never physically made the object? I want to know what theory of what makes a thing creative the practice quietly assumes, whether it actually needs that theory or if a few different theories would all allow the same rules, and what would have to change if you instead thought creativity was in the idea or in how people receive the work.
