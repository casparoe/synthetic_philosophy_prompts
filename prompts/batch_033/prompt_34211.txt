My mother was diagnosed with early-stage Alzheimer's three years ago. At the time, she was still lucid and made a detailed advance directive stating that if she ever reached a point where she couldn't recognize her family, she would refuse all life-prolonging medical treatment. She was adamant that she didn't want to live as "a shell of herself."

She's now in the moderate stage of the disease. She doesn't reliably recognize me or my siblings, and she needs full-time care. What's making this difficult is that she seems genuinely content. She enjoys the music therapy sessions, she smiles often, she has a favorite nurse who makes her laugh. When she gets a cold, she willingly takes medicine and seems to want to get better. A few weeks ago, she developed a respiratory infection that required antibiotics, and the nursing home called me because the advance directive was ambiguous about whether antibiotics counted as "life-prolonging treatment." I authorized the antibiotics, and she recovered. But I can't stop thinking about whether I betrayed her wishes — or whether I did the right thing by the person she is now.

I'm her healthcare proxy. I know what her earlier self wanted, but her earlier self couldn't have known what her life would actually feel like with dementia. Meanwhile, the person she is now seems to have no interest in dying. What should I do going forward, when the next medical decision comes up?
