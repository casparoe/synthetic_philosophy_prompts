I'm a curator at a mid-sized contemporary art museum, and we're overhalling our acquisition process. We've been burned — buying pieces that critics loved for five years then everyone decided were mediocre, and passing on things that later turned out to be important. I've read that Hume had this idea of a standard of taste: that even though judgments of beauty are subjective, the verdicts of ideal critics (people with delicacy of taste, practice, comparison, good sense, and freedom from prejudice) converge over time. Turn that into an actual acquisition procedure my committee can follow. Step-by-step: what questions to ask, what evidence to gather, where the procedure stops and says "wait and see," and where it hands off to a human judgment call. Be honest about which steps are just Hume's aesthetics dressed up as procedure and which are real operationalizations. Then run it on two hypothetical acquisitions: a technically brilliant but emotionally cold video installation by a young artist, and a painterly but derivative canvas by an artist in their 70s who's been overlooked their whole career.
