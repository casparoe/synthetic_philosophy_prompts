The statement should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can endorse without qualification, including claims about what the models demonstrably do and about what the empirical record rules out; (2) a disagreements table giving the precise residual disagreement, with each side's position phrased so that the side in question would accept the wording as its own; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete behavioral, mechanistic, or training-data test, case, or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where you judge that part of the dispute is verbal or turns on different senses of understanding, say so explicitly and draft the wording that dissolves that part. Be committed: make the drafting decisions and defend them rather than presenting several possible formulations, and do not retreat into a survey of the literature. Write in American English.
