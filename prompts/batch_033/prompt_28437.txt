I am setting the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on philosophical logic and metaphysics. We have spent three weeks on the problem of future contingents, working through Aristotle's De Interpretatione 9, the medieval debate over necessity per accidens, and the contemporary revival of the issue in work on branching time and the Thin Red Line. Students have seen the standard taxonomy of responses: reject bivalence, reject the inference from truth to necessity, reject the fixity of the past, accept the conclusion and embrace compatibilism.

For the exam, I want one essay question that tests whether students can reconstruct the fatalist argument with enough precision to see that it can be formulated in two distinct ways—one that depends on the Principle of Bivalence and one that depends only on the Law of Excluded Middle—and whether they can explain why this difference matters for evaluating the Aristotelian response. The question should not reward a student who simply lists four responses and summarizes each.

Please write the question, a model answer of approximately 800 words, and a marking scheme on a 25-point scale. The marking scheme should distinguish an A (22–25), a B (17–21), and a C (12–16) by specifying the analytical moves each band must contain and the characteristic errors that separate one band from the next.
