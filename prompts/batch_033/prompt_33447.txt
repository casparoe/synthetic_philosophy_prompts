Construct a thought experiment to the following specifications.

The case should be a decision problem in which causal decision theory and evidential decision theory recommend the same action, but the style of reasoning that supports paying in a counterfactual mugging—reasoning that evaluates a decision procedure by its performance across logically correlated counterfactuals rather than by the expected utility of the act at the decision node—recommends a different action. That is, CDT and EDT align; the counterfactual-mugging logic dissents.

Constraints. No perfect predictors: any predictor in the story may be no better than a shrewd human. No Omega, no Laplace's demon, no exotic physics. The scenario should be set in an ordinary domain—a business negotiation, a personal relationship, an institutional decision, or the like—and should be no longer than a paragraph. The structure must be transparent enough that the divergence can be demonstrated step by step without appeal to formalism the reader does not already possess.

After presenting the scenario, provide the following.

(a) The CDT reasoning. Show the agent's causal expected utility calculation and state which action it recommends.

(b) The EDT reasoning. Show the evidential expected utility calculation and explain why it agrees with CDT in this case.

(c) The counterfactual-mugging-style reasoning. Show how an agent who evaluates the output of their decision function across the relevant counterfactual scenarios would assess the choice, and state which action that reasoning recommends. Explain why it diverges from the CDT/EDT verdict even though those two theories agree.

(d) A diagnosis. Identify the single structural feature of the case that causes the policy-level or function-level reasoning to peel off from the act-level reasoning. What is it about the causal and evidential structure of the problem that makes CDT and EDT coincide while the counterfactual-mugging logic points elsewhere?

(e) A removal check. If that structural feature is eliminated, what does the case become? Does it collapse into a standard Newcomb problem, into a straightforward dominance argument, or into something else? Show what each of the three styles of reasoning says after the removal.

(f) A note on resistance. How would a defender of CDT or EDT push back against the counterfactual-mugging-style argument in this case? Would they deny that the case is decision-relevant, argue that the policy-level reasoning equivocates between acting and precommitting, or take some other line? Be specific to the case you constructed.

The point of the exercise is to isolate the logical feature that makes counterfactual mugging challenging for act-based decision theories, and to show that it can arise even when CDT and EDT happen to agree—so that the disagreement is not merely between CDT and EDT but between act-based theories and function-based theories more broadly.
