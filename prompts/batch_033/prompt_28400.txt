hey — I'm a CS PhD student working on mechanistic interpretability, specifically on how models compose features they've learned. I've been dipping into epistemology and keep running into this thing about Nozick's tracking theory and epistemic closure, and it sounds weirdly like problems we actually deal with.

Here's my rough understanding: Nozick says knowledge requires sensitivity — if p were false you wouldn't believe p. But sensitivity doesn't compose under known entailment. You can sensitively believe you have hands, and know that having-hands entails not-being-a-brain-in-a-vat, but your belief that you're not a BIV isn't sensitive (if you were a BIV you'd still think you weren't). So knowledge isn't closed under known entailment. Philosophers seem split on whether this is a bug or a feature.

In my world, I spend all day thinking about whether a model's latent representation of some feature F composes with its representation of G when F entails G. We find cases where a model reliably tracks "is a cat" but its "is an animal" representation fails under distribution shift where the cat representation still works. Or circuits that implement some invariant but the downstream circuit that should inherit that invariant doesn't. This feels like the same shape of problem.

So here's what I want: restate the whole Nozick-vs-closure debate in the vocabulary of mechanistic interpretability and world models — latent features, circuits, counterfactual robustness, out-of-distribution generalization, compositional generalization, the lot. Do it systematically and don't cheat: if something in the philosophical debate has no analogue in how we think about models, say so. Then the residue report: what translated cleanly, what you had to warp, and what had no counterpart at all. And then the real question: is the failure of epistemic closure under known entailment the *same* phenomenon as the failure of compositional generalization in neural nets, or is that a tempting but wrong analogy? If it's wrong, what's the disanalogy that matters?
