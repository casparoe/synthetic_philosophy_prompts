A friend and I spent most of yesterday evening arguing about two-dimensional semantics. I came away still holding a package of views that each seemed individually compelling, but the argument exposed enough friction among them that I no longer trust the set as a whole. Before I commit any of this to writing, I would like an audit of the package. My current commitments are as follows.

(1) I accept the Kripkean picture of natural kind terms: 'water' is a rigid designator, and the proposition that water is H2O is necessary a posteriori. The fact that I can conceive of water turning out not to be H2O does not show that water could have failed to be H2O.

(2) I accept a two-dimensional framework on which an expression has both a primary intension (a function from epistemically possible scenarios to extensions) and a secondary intension (a function from metaphysically possible worlds to extensions), with the primary intension capturing the epistemic or a priori dimension of meaning.

(3) I think the primary intension of a natural kind term such as 'water' is a priori associated with the term — something like the conditional that if the actual world is the watery-stuff world, then water is the watery stuff — and that this is what explains why the claim that water is H2O is informative despite being necessary.

(4) I remain an anti-descriptivist of the Kripkean sort: 'water' does not abbreviate a description such as 'the clear, drinkable liquid in the lakes and rivers,' and a speaker need not associate any such description with the term in order to be competent with it.

(5) I am attracted to Chalmers's modal rationalism: ideal primary conceivability entails primary possibility, and where primary and secondary intensions coincide — as he claims they do for phenomenal terms — primary conceivability is a guide to metaphysical possibility, which is why the conceivability of zombies is not idle.

(6) I hold that the secondary intension of 'water' is fixed by external facts about the actual world (its being H2O rather than anything in the head), along Putnam-style externalist lines.

Are these six jointly tenable? I can see several places where they might rub against one another, but I cannot tell which of these frictions are genuine, which are merely apparent, and which of the commitments are load-bearing. Please rank the tensions by severity, distinguish the apparent conflicts from the real ones, and identify the least costly revision if the package cannot stand as written.
