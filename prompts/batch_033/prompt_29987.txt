My younger brother was diagnosed with early-onset Parkinson's disease two months ago. He's 34. I've been reading philosophy to try to make sense of what this means for our relationship, and I recently came across Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment." I find myself troubled by what seems to follow from his view. Strawson argues that reactive attitudes like resentment and gratitude constitute the participant stance — the stance from which we relate to others as persons. He then says we suspend these attitudes and adopt the objective stance when someone is, through no fault of their own, incapable of the kind of reciprocal goodwill that makes them an appropriate target of such attitudes. As my brother's condition progresses and his capacities change, doesn't Strawson's framework entail that I will, and perhaps should, progressively withdraw from the participant stance with him — that I will come to see him more as an object of management than as a brother? That seems monstrous to me, but I cannot see where the reasoning goes wrong.
