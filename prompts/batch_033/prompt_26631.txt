Philosophers writing about the wrong kind of reasons problem often claim that there is a deep puzzle about cases like the demon who threatens to punish you unless you admire him. But I do not see what the puzzle is. We already have a perfectly clear distinction between reasons to bring about an attitude and reasons for the attitude itself. The demon gives you a reason to bring it about that you admire him—take a pill, undergo hypnosis, whatever—but plainly no reason to admire him. Once we mark that distinction, nothing remains. What exactly is supposed to resist this dissolution of the problem?
