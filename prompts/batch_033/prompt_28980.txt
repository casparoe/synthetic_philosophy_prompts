I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether W. E. B. Du Bois's 1926 essay "Criteria of Negro Art" can be turned into a workable funding criterion, and I came away convinced the question is more difficult than either of us had admitted. I now have to face a concrete version of it.

I sit on the grants committee of a foundation that funds early-career Black artists. Our stated criteria are "artistic excellence" and "contribution to Black cultural life," and we have deliberately declined to define either term more precisely. We have two finalists for one remaining fellowship. One is a formally adventurous abstract painter whose work has no explicit political content and who says in her statement that she refuses to make her identity or her politics the subject of the work. The other is a multimedia artist whose proposed project is openly propagandistic in Du Bois's sense: it is built to move viewers toward specific positions on housing justice and police accountability, and it subordinates formal experimentation to that aim. Half the committee argues that "Criteria of Negro Art" settles the matter, since Du Bois held that art is always propaganda whether or not it presents itself as such, and that Black art should serve the struggle, so we should fund the second applicant. The other half argues that Du Bois was writing in a particular moment, that his essay was aimed at white patrons and at Black artists who wanted to escape into an apolitical conception of art, and that using it to score grant applications would flatten both the art and Du Bois. I have been asked to draft the decision memo.

Please write the memo I will circulate before next week's meeting. It should recommend one of three options — fund the painter, fund the multimedia artist, or defer with a concrete plan for how to decide — and it should engage Du Bois's actual argument seriously rather than treating the claim that all art is propaganda as a slogan. In particular, address whether Du Bois's view leaves room for a distinction between art that serves Black life through formal innovation and art that serves it through direct persuasion, and whether a funding committee in our position is even the kind of body to which his argument applies. Give the strongest consideration on the losing side its due.

After the memo, append a JSON object with the following fields: "recommendation" (one of the three options), "conditions" (a list of any conditions attached to the recommendation), "strongest_argument_for_recommendation" (one sentence), "strongest_argument_against_recommendation" (one sentence), "du_bois_claim_doing_the_work" (the specific Du Boisian claim, if any, the memo relies on), and "future_decision_rule" (a one-sentence rule the committee could apply in later cycles). Write the entire response in American English.
