Draft an adversarial-collaboration summary between a Kierkegaardian and a Kantian on the question of the teleological suspension of the ethical, as presented in Kierkegaard's *Fear and Trembling* (1843, under the pseudonym Johannes de Silentio). The Kierkegaardian maintains that there can be a genuine religious duty to God that suspends or overrides universal ethical obligations, and that Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac exemplifies this in a way that is neither reducible to murder nor assimilable to a tragic hero's dilemma. The Kantian maintains that moral duties are unconditionally universal and that no appeal to a private relationship with God can justify setting them aside; whatever is genuinely obligatory must be formulable as a universal law, and a command that cannot be so formulated is not a moral reason at all.

The document should include the following sections, all written in British English:

1. **Agreed claims.** A numbered list of claims both parties can sign. These should cover at least: what the biblical narrative says; the structure of Johannes de Silentio's argument (including the distinction between the knight of infinite resignation and the knight of faith, and the contrast with the tragic hero); what each philosophical tradition affirms in its own terms; and what is at stake in the dispute. The claims should be substantive — neither vacuous nor slanted towards one side.

2. **Disagreements table.** At least four rows. For each row, state the point of disagreement neutrally, then give the Kierkegaardian's position and the Kantian's position, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording without flinching. The rows should capture the dispute at different levels: the interpretation of the Abraham story, the status of the ethical, the epistemology of purported divine commands, and the practical or existential implications of each view.

3. **Cruxes.** Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, describe a concrete case, argument, or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about it. At least one crux should address the problem of distinguishing the knight of faith from the fanatic or the deluded, and at least one should address whether the Kantian's insistence on universality already presupposes what the Kierkegaardian denies.

4. **Verbal disputes.** A brief note on whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal — that is, whether the two sides are using key terms (such as 'duty', 'ethical', or 'religious') in different senses such that some of their disagreement would dissolve under disambiguation. If so, specify which part and why; if not, explain why the disagreement is genuinely substantive throughout.
