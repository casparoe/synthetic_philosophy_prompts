I was on a jury last month. The trial lasted two weeks and I took notes the whole time — I really paid attention. By the end I was pretty convinced the defendant was guilty. The evidence seemed clear to me: the timeline fit, the forensics lined up, and the defendant's story had too many holes. But when we got to the deliberation room, every single other juror thought he was not guilty. All eleven of them. We talked it through for hours and I could see they weren't being lazy or sloppy — they'd noticed things I missed, and they had reasonable explanations for the stuff that looked bad. But I still couldn't shake the feeling that my original read was right. I ended up voting to acquit because I figured I should defer to the group, but honestly I'm still not sure I did the right thing. Was I being rational to go along with them? Or should I have stuck with my own judgment?
