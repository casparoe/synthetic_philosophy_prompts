I've been reading Émilie du Châtelet's Discourse on Happiness, and I'm stuck on her argument that the love of study is the only passion that can sustain us through old age. She claims that other passions — romantic love, ambition, the desire for glory — necessarily diminish with age because they depend on things external to us, while the love of study is self-sufficient and actually grows stronger. But I don't see why the love of study is supposed to be so different. If knowledge is limitless and always leaves us wanting more, doesn't that make it just another form of desire that could frustrate us as much as it fulfills us? And if the love of study depends on having the mental faculties to pursue it, isn't it also vulnerable to aging? I want to understand whether her argument is making a conceptual point about the nature of different passions, or a psychological claim that might or might not be true for different people.
