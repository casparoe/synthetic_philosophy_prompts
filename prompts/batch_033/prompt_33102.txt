I'm taking a philosophy of mind course this term, and we've spent the last few weeks on the zombie argument against physicalism. What's striking to me is how little the debate seems to move: the argument has been with us for decades, each side has a well-rehearsed reply to every move the other makes, and yet neither side gives ground. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not another survey of the arguments.

Here is how I understand the dialectic. The anti-physicalist asks us to conceive of a creature that is a physical duplicate of a conscious human being but lacks phenomenal consciousness entirely. The conceivability of such a zombie is then taken to show its metaphysical possibility, and that possibility is taken to refute physicalism, because if the physical facts are fixed while the phenomenal facts can still vary, the phenomenal facts cannot be grounded in the physical facts. Physicalists respond in several standard ways. Some deny that zombies are really conceivable, holding that we can describe them only because the description hides an unnoticed contradiction. Some grant conceivability but deny that conceivability entails possibility, treating the inference as fallible in this domain. Others grant the intuition but try to explain it away, for instance by saying that phenomenal concepts pick out experiences in a way that makes the absence of experience seem conceivable even though nothing in the world could correspond to it.

My working hypothesis is that the debate has stalled because the two sides are not answering one question under a shared set of standards. They seem to differ over what conceivability is evidence for, over how much weight our modal intuitions carry when the subject matter is consciousness, and over what an adequate explanation of the conceivability intuition would have to accomplish. But I can also imagine the disagreement being more substantive than that: perhaps it comes down to whether there is an explanatory gap at all, or to what physicalism itself is committed to.

So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it a verbal dispute, a clash of bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for, a disagreement about standards of evidence in modal epistemology, or a hidden factual or formal question? And given that diagnosis, what would count as progress — a sharper distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or the recognition that the argument can do no more than make an intuition vivid? Please give me a committed diagnosis: pick a definite account of the structure of the impasse and defend it, rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose.
