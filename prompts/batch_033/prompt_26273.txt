I'm giving a five-minute toast at the retirement party for my old PhD advisor. He spent forty years on judgment and decision-making, and he's basically the reason the "outside view" (Kahneman & Lovallo sense) got taken seriously in public policy circles — testified before Congress about reference-class forecasting, the whole thing. He's also the most stubbornly outside-view person I've ever met in daily life, which his wife and kids find alternatingly endearing and maddening.

The room will be about half academics (decision theorists, behavioral economists, a few psychologists) and half family and non-academic friends. I was his student and now I work in venture capital, which he considers roughly equivalent to professional base-rate denial. He knows I love him.

Here's what I want: the toast should be built around the inside view / outside view distinction itself — not just name-dropping it, but letting it do structural work in what the speech says and how it moves. The toast needs to honor him genuinely, but it also needs to acknowledge, affectionately, that the outside view isn't always the right lens for a human life. (His daughter once told me that when she was fifteen and heartbroken, he gave her the base rate for teenage relationships ending within six months. She's thirty-two now and still brings it up.)

Please write the toast, and then include a section called "Commentary" (about 300 words) where you explain the key choices you made — especially how you handled the mixed audience and where you placed the inside-view/outside-view tension.
