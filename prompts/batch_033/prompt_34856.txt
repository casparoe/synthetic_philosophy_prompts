I run an upper-level undergraduate seminar in moral and political philosophy — 16 students, one 75-minute session — and we are in the middle of the punishment unit. The students can rehearse the standard positions (deterrence, rehabilitation, desert, proportionality, the familiar counterexamples), but they still treat retributivism and consequentialism as two vocabularies you can attach to whatever sentence you already favor. I want a single session that makes them feel that these are genuinely different decision procedures that come apart under pressure, and that each one, followed honestly, leads somewhere its own adherents find hard to accept.

Design a runnable classroom simulation, role-play, or game for this group. The two frameworks should be operationalized as different scoring functions rather than as different rhetoric, so the conflict shows up as a concrete tension between two procedures. The theoretical distinction should surface from the rules and the cases before it is named: no lecture up front, and no labels until the debrief.
