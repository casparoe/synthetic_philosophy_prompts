Write a short story that takes the phenomenon of "conceptual slippage" — the idea, developed in the Copycat project and related work on fluid concepts, that human concepts are not rigid categories but can slide and deform under contextual pressure, so that under the right circumstances "abc" is to "abd" as "xyz" is to "wyz" rather than "xyd" — and makes it a central narrative element. The story might follow a character (human, artificial, or otherwise) for whom conceptual boundaries are unusually labile, or it might be set in a world where slippage between concepts operates differently than in ours. Whatever the premise, the story should explore what it is like to inhabit a mind where the border between two neighboring concepts — perhaps "same" and "different," "tool" and "weapon," "promise" and "threat" — has become unstable or permeable. Keep the story self-contained and under 1,500 words.
