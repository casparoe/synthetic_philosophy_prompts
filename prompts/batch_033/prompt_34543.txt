Van Fraassen's Reflection principle says that my current credence in a proposition should equal my expected future credence in it — I should, in effect, defer to my future self. But this seems to rule out the possibility of learning from experience. If I am about to run an experiment that I expect will teach me something, then I should anticipate that my credence will shift in a particular direction once I see the results, which would violate the equality. Doesn't the Reflection principle therefore make empirical inquiry rationally impossible?
