I am a PhD student in decision theory and serve as the graduate representative on my university's investment advisory committee. The committee has been offered a bespoke contract, which we have been calling a "Pasadena note." The terms are these: a fair coin is tossed until it first lands heads; if the first heads occurs on toss n, the university receives (if the amount is positive) or pays (if negative) (−1)^(n−1) · 2^n / n dollars. In exchange for our taking on this payoff, the counterparty will pay the university a fixed fee of $0.90 per note, and proposes a position of 10,000 notes, with settlement within 30 days.

The committee agrees on the mathematics. If one sums the expected contributions in their natural order, one obtains the alternating harmonic series, which converges to ln 2 ≈ 0.69; but because the positive and negative contributions each diverge when taken separately, the expectation does not exist in the standard sense. The disagreement is about what follows for our decision. One group argues that we should accept: the fee plus a natural-order value of ln 2 makes the position favorable. A second group argues that we should reject: with an undefined expectation there is no well-defined price, and a fiduciary has no basis for entering the contract. A third group argues that we should accept only if the counterparty agrees to a cap on the maximum payment in either direction, so that the expectation becomes well-defined.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action with a time frame, give reasons the committee can act on, and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than merely restating the definition of expected value.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on unbounded and undefined expectations, and do not present a contested view as the consensus; where you rely on a particular decision rule for undefined expectations, flag it and name the main alternative.

After the memo, append a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "recommendation" (the action and time frame in one sentence), "settled_claims" (an array of claims you treat as established), "contested_claims" (an array of claims you rely on that are genuinely disputed, each with a one-line note on the main alternative), "losing_side_strongest_consideration" (one sentence), and "conditions" (an array of amendments, caps, collateral, or monitoring you would require; empty if none).
