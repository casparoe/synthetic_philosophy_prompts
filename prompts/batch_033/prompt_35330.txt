a friend of mine is really into the illusionist view of phenomenal consciousness — the Dennett/Frankish line that qualia and the 'what it's like' are a kind of introspective illusion, not something real. his pet analogy is the computer desktop: the icons and folders are a useful fiction, there's no little trash can inside your laptop, just patterns of voltage that the interface presents in a simplified way. he says qualia are exactly like that — the brain's user interface for its own internal processes, and we mistake the representation for the thing itself. from this he concludes the hard problem is a confused question, not a genuine mystery. i'm not convinced. audit the analogy: what corresponds to what, where does the mapping break, what does the analogy smuggle in about the 'user' who's being fooled, and whether the hard-problem-dissolving conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table.
