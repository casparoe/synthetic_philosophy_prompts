I am a programming-language researcher. My native vocabulary consists of type systems, evaluation semantics, side effects, referential transparency, preconditions and postconditions, exception handling, and the distinction between expressions that denote values and statements that change state. My exposure to philosophy of language is thin. I gather that Austin and his successors held that many utterances are not descriptions of independently existing facts but acts: that uttering the words I promise under the right conditions is the promising, not a report of a mental act; that the initial distinction between constative and performative utterances turned out to be unstable and was replaced by a taxonomy of locutionary, illocutionary, and perlocutionary acts; and that a speech act can go wrong not by being false but by being infelicitous — the wrong person, the wrong procedure, a defective proposition, insincere intent, or a failure of uptake by the hearer, with the conditions standardly sorted into propositional content, preparatory, sincerity, and essential conditions. I have also heard that there is a live dispute between conventionalist accounts, on which the force of a speech act comes from social rules or institutions (for example, a constitutive rule that a certain utterance counts as a promise in a certain context), and intention-based accounts, on which it comes from the speaker's communicative intention and the hearer's recognition of it.

Restate this body of thought systematically in my vocabulary. What, if anything, corresponds to an illocutionary act: a function with a side effect, a change to some shared context, a transaction, an assertion in a proof system? What corresponds to felicity conditions: preconditions, type well-formedness, exception cases, invariants? What corresponds to uptake: a return value, an acknowledgement message, a state change visible to another process? I want the translation to be honest: say explicitly where the mapping is exact, where you had to stretch either the philosophy or my vocabulary, and where something in the speech-act picture simply has no counterpart in how I think about programs. Then give me the residue report. Is what fails to translate a symptom of an impoverished framework on my side, or of a less precise concept on the philosophy side? In particular, I want to know whether illocutionary force is anything over and above a program's effect on state plus a type, and whether uptake is a genuine ingredient or merely a runtime condition that a programming-language view can safely ignore.
