I'm giving a talk at a workshop called "Consequentialism Under Pressure." My thesis: the problem of cluelessness is not just an epistemic inconvenience for consequentialism but a structural defect in its claim to guide action — and none of the standard replies (heuristics, symmetry arguments, sophisticated expected-value reasoning) succeed. The audience is mostly consequentialist moral philosophers who know the literature on cluelessness well, plus a few epistemologists working on decision theory and two critics from the virtue ethics tradition. Give me exactly five objections I'll get in the Q&A, ordered by how damaging they are, each phrased as this audience would put it, in a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Include the one I'm hoping nobody asks.
