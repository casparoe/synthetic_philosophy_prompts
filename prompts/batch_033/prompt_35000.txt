so i've been rewatching Memento (Nolan, 2000) and i think the whole thing is basically a feature-length exploration of Nozick's tracking theory and the denial of epistemic closure, and i want someone who actually knows the epistemology to walk me through whether this reading holds up.

quick setup for anyone who hasn't thought about Nozick in a while: S knows p iff (1) p is true, (2) S believes p, (3) if p were false S wouldn't believe p (sensitivity), and (4) if p were true S would believe p (adherence). the famous consequence is that closure fails — you can know you have hands without knowing you're not a brain in a vat, because the sensitivity condition fails for the BIV scenario (if you were a BIV you'd still believe you weren't).

Leonard's whole cognitive apparatus is externalized — polaroids, tattoos, notes — and the film is basically asking: under what conditions does this system produce knowledge? he's trying to build a method that tracks the truth, but the tragedy is that he's been subverting his own method: burning the photo of dead Jimmy, writing "don't believe his lies" on Teddy's polaroid, tattooing a license plate that he himself picked as a decoy. so his beliefs end up true only accidentally, and the tracking conditions break down.

what i want is a reading that takes both the film and the epistemology seriously. specific things i'm stuck on:

(a) is there any scene where Leonard's belief actually satisfies all four Nozick conditions and counts as knowledge? or does the film systematically deny him that?

(b) the film's structure — black-and-white scenes moving forward, color scenes moving backward — constantly reverses cause and effect. does this formal trick say something about the tracking conditions that a linear narrative couldn't? i keep thinking there's a connection between the reversed chronology and the failure of sensitivity but i can't quite articulate it.

(c) Nozick's denial of closure strikes a lot of people as absurd on its face. does Memento make it feel intuitively plausible, or does the film actually show why closure is indispensible and we can't live without it?

(d) the final voiceover: "I have to believe in a world outside my own mind. I have to believe that my actions still have meaning, even if I can't remember them.
