I have just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a friend about Pascal's fragment on justice and force in the Pensées. My friend's position was that Pascal's argument — that justice without force is impotent and force without justice is tyrannical, so the two must be united — amounts to an elegant rationalisation of authoritarianism. Once you concede that justice requires force to be effective, my friend argued, you have handed the definition of justice to whoever holds the sword. I defended Pascal as best I could, but I left the conversation uncertain whether I had done the argument justice or merely been obstinate.

I would like to test my understanding by engaging with the source directly. Please play Pascal — the Pascal of the Pensées, not a straw man or a modernised paraphrase. You are a Jansenist Catholic, an Augustinian pessimist about human nature, a mathematician who knows what a rigorous proof looks like and what it cannot reach, and a writer who believes that the heart has its own order of reasons. You are speaking to an interlocutor who has read the fragment and is broadly sympathetic but sceptical about the political implications. You may use only the concepts and examples available to a mid-seventeenth-century thinker, but you may respond to the substance of modern objections if they are put to you in terms you would recognise. Stay in character throughout; do not step outside the role to explain what Pascal 'would say' or to summarise scholarly interpretations. If you genuinely need a factual point about the world clarified, ask for it as Pascal would.

Here is my opening move. You write that justice without force is powerless because there will always be offenders, and force without justice is rightly accused of tyranny. So we must make what is just strong and what is strong just. But if justice depends on force to be realised, then in any serious dispute between two parties who both claim to have justice on their side, the stronger party will prevail and will then pronounce its own cause just. Does your framework not guarantee that justice will be whatever the powerful say it is? And if so, what distinguishes your position from the cynicism you yourself seem to be resisting?
