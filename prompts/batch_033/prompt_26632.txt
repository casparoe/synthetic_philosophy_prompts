ok so I'm a PhD student in comp bio and I'm helping organize this interdisciplinary workshop on self-reference across biology, logic, and CS. We've got people coming from mol bio, philosophy, theoretical CS — the whole mix. For the opening session I want something provocative that'll get everyone's assumptions on the table.

Could you write a dialogue between two characters arguing about whether the analogy between Gödel's incompleteness theorems and DNA self-replication is actually deep or just a pretty metaphor? You know the one — Hofstadter made it famous, the idea that both involve a system containing a symbolic description of itself that it uses to reproduce/copy itself, the "strange loop" thing.

One character (call her Mira) should defend the isomorphism as genuinely illuminating — she thinks it reveals something structural that life and formal systems share, and that dismissing it as "just an analogy" misses the point. The other (call him Chen) should push back hard: he'd say DNA replication is a physical causal process involving enzymes and base pairing, there's no arithmetization or Gödel numbering going on, and the kind of self-reference in the Gödel sentence is logical slash quasi-paradoxical while DNA transcription is just... chemistry.

Both need to be competent and well-read — no strawmen. I want them to actually engage with each other's specific arguments, not talk past each other. Maybe they converge on some things (like agreeing there's a family resemblance worth studying) while staying split on whether it's more than that. The vibe should be two colleagues arguing over coffee, not a formal debate.

Don't force a resolution. If the disagreement stays live, that's fine — that's actually better for sparking discussion.
