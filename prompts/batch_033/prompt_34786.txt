I've been reading Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* for a philosophy of language course, and the private language argument has me genuinely scratching my head. As I understand it, Wittgenstein is trying to prove that you can't have a language that only you understand — like a set of words for your own private sensations that no one else could ever access or learn. But this just seems empirically false? I get these weird headaches and I've literally named them: there's the "nail" one (feels like someone's hammering a tiny nail behind my left eye) and the "fog" one (just a dull pressure all over). I know exactly what I mean when I use these labels, and I can reliably tell the difference between them. No one else has access to my headache sensations, but I'm clearly using these words meaningfully. So isn't Wittgenstein just wrong? Like, the fact that I can do this seems to refute the whole argument. Or am I misunderstanding what he's even trying to show? My TA said something about "rule-following" and "criteria of correctness" but I don't really see how that changes things — if I can consistently identify which headache is which, doesn't that count as following a rule, even if I'm the only one who can do it?
