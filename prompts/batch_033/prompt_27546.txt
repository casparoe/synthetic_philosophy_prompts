I am a software engineer with no formal background in academic philosophy. I have been reading Douglas Hofstadter’s Gödel, Escher, Bach (GEB) and want to evaluate the central analogy before I go further: the idea that the kind of self-reference found in Gödel’s incompleteness theorem—where a formal system can construct a sentence that says something about itself and thereby escapes the system’s own proof machinery—is the right model, or at least a necessary ingredient, for explaining how a mind has a self. I am not asking for a summary of GEB, and I am not asking for a reading list.

Write the desiderata, the requirements specification, for any theory that takes the Gödelian analogy seriously. In particular, give me:

- the cases and phenomena an adequate version of the analogy must get right, with the reason each is a requirement rather than an optional extra;
- the distinctions the theory cannot collapse, such as provability within a system versus truth from outside, syntactic self-reference versus mental self-representation, or a system being able to refer to itself versus a system being aware of itself;
- the constraints imposed by Gödel’s actual results, including the conditions under which incompleteness arises and the fact that the theorem is about formal systems with enough arithmetic;
- the engineering-adjacent cases the theory should handle, such as quines, self-referential programs, self-modifying code, and systems that reason about their own outputs, and why these are relevant analogues for the mind case;
- any rules from biology, cognitive science, or information processing the theory must not contradict, or at least must explain why they are not decisive.

For each desideratum, separate what is non-negotiable from what is merely a preference of one camp. Then flag any pair of desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain the tension. Finally, say which single requirement does the most work in ruling out common readings of the analogy. I am most interested in the criteria themselves and the pressures behind them, not in a scorecard of named positions.
