I'm on the value assessment committee for a regional health system, and we meet Thursday to vote on adding a new drug for a chronic condition (it slows progression but has real side effects). The manufacturer's filing estimates the quality-of-life weights from a stated-preference study: patients were asked to choose between hypothetical scenarios trading off survival against it's side effects. Our own analysts redid the model with revealed-preference weights instead — they looked at how patients with the condition actually choose among the existing treatments, which differ in survival and side-effect burden, and inferred the utility weights that would make those choices come out as optimizing. The two sets of weights disagree enough that the cost per QALY comes in just under our threshold on one and well over on the other. I need to send around a short memo before the meeting recommending what we should do: adopt one set of weights, approve with conditions, or reject — and give reasons the other side will recognize as fair. I suspect the real fight is over what a utility weight is even supposed to measure. Draft that memo for me. Please engage the revealed-preference camp's argument seriously, that only actual choices (not hypothetical survey answers) reveal what patients really value. And don't punt to 'more research': we need a decision we can vote on.
