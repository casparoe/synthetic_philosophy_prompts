I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I have been trying to determine whether Gödel's incompleteness theorems have direct consequences for computationalism. The argument I keep returning to runs as follows. The first incompleteness theorem shows that any consistent, recursively axiomatized formal system strong enough to express elementary arithmetic contains a true arithmetical sentence that the system cannot prove. Any AI, and any computational model of cognition, is just such a formal system, or can be treated as one. So for any candidate machine, we can identify its Gödel sentence, recognize that the sentence is true, and observe that the machine cannot prove it. Doesn't this show that no machine can match human mathematical competence, and therefore that computationalism about the mind is false? If the inference is too quick, where exactly does it fail, and what do the theorems actually establish about the relation between minds and machines?
