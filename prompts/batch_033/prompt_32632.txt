I'm a journalist who covers philosophy and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about a classic moral puzzle: you can rescue either one stranger or five strangers, but not both. I keep coming back to Taurek's view that the numbers don't matter here — that you should give each person an equal chance, maybe by flipping a coin, because each person's loss is total for them and there's nobody who experiences all five losses at once. The other side says that's absurd: five lives weigh more than one, and a coin flip shortchanges the five.

Could you write a dialogue between two people who hold these views — call them Dana (who thinks the numbers count) and Sam (who thinks they don't)? I want it to feel like two sharp people actually arguing, not a setup where one side get's all the good lines. They should respond to each other's actual points, press on the weak spots, and maybe shift or rephrase their view under pressure. It would be great if they get into the subtler stuff: whether harms can be added up across people the way they can within a single life, whether a coin flip gives the one person too much weight per capita, and what happens when the numbers get huge (one versus a million) or when the harms aren't equal. Don't wrap it up with a tidy agreement — its fine if they end by pinpointing exactly where they diverge.
