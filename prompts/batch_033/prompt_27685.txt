I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about using AI at work, going by my gut on cases. I keep going back and forth and I think there's some principle I'm tracking but I can't articulate it.

Here are my verdicts on a bunch of situations:

1. Using Copilot to autocomplete a repetitive getter/setter or a loop pattern I've written a thousand times. Fine, no problem.

2. Asking ChatGPT to explain a concept I'm fuzzy on (like the CAP theorem tradeoffs) and then using that understanding to make a design decision. Fine, this is basically like reading a really good StackOverflow answer.

3. Copy-pasting a regex from ChatGPT that I don't really understand, but I test it and it works. Leaning not fine, though I do it sometimes.

4. Using an AI coding assistant to write an entire feature, reviewing each chunk as I go, understanding each chunk, and shipping it. Fine.

5. Same as 4, but three months later I have to extend the feature and I realize I can't really reason about the system as a whole without the AI again. The original use was fine at the time, but now I feel like something went wrong.

6. I'm a senior dev supposed to be the expert on our payment module. I use AI to fix a bug in it, and the AI's explanation teaches me something I didn't know about our own code. Fine.

7. Same situation, but the AI's fix works, I don't fully understand why, and I approve the PR anyway. Not fine.

8. I'm learning Rust. I use AI heavily to get past the borrow checker on a personal project, and I tell myself I'm learning by reading the AI's explanations. Six months later, I still can't write Rust without AI assistance. I was fooling myself. Not fine.

9. I use AI to write all my Jira ticket updates and status reports. Fine, honestly. That's just formatting thoughts I already have.

10. I use AI to summarize a bunch of design docs I was supposed to read before an architecture review, and I go into the review relying on the summaries. Not fine.

11. A junior dev on my team uses AI for almost everything and ships code that works, but when I ask them to walk me through why they made a particular design choice, they can't. They're not fine, but I'm not sure if the problem is the AI use itself or something else.

The thing I keep getting stuck on: cases 4 and 5 have identical behavior at the time of use, but my verdict changes when I look back later. That makes me think my principle has to be about whether understanding is preserved over time, not just about the moment of use. But then case 9 (Jira updates) doesn't involve understanding at all and I'm fine with it, so it can't just be "any use that doesn't build understanding is bad."

Is there a principle that gets as many of these as possible, and which of my verdicts does it force me to overturn? Also, what does the principle say about a case I haven't listed: using AI to write unit tests for code I wrote myself?
