I'm on the review panel for a private foundation that funds interdisciplinary work at the intersection of philosophy and cognitive science, and we've got a grant proposal that's dividing the panel right down the middle. I need to draft the recommendation memo for our final meeting next week, and I'm genuinely stuck on what to recommend.

The proposal comes from a philosopher of mind and a sleep neuroscientist who want to run a series of studies testing what they call the "experiential status" of dreams. The setup: they'll use high-density EEG and fMRI to track neural activity during REM sleep, then wake participants at varying delays — some immediately at REM onset, some after several minutes of REM, some after REM ends but before the participant wakes naturally — and compare the richness, coherence, and temporal structure of dream reports against the neural signatures. Their argument is that if dream reports degrade systematically with longer wake-up delays in ways that don't match the underlying neural activity during the REM period itself, that's evidence for something like Dennett's view — that dreams aren't experiences we undergo in real time but narratives we construct or confabulate upon waking. If instead the reports track the neural activity tightly regardless of when you wake the person, that supports the standard view that dreams are genuine experiences occurring during sleep.

Half the panel thinks this is exactly the kind of philosophically sophisticated empirical work the foundation exists to support. The other half thinks its fundamentally confused. Their objection isn't that the methods are sloppy — it's that the whole question is either trivial (of *course* dreams are experiences, what else would they be?) or a conceptual question that no amount of brain data can settle (if Malcolm was right that there's no independent check on dream reports, then you're just measuring neural correlates of something whose existence you've already assumed by asking for a report). The neuroscientist on the panel actually said, "this is philosophers trying to prove we're all p-zombies again, and I'm not paying for it."

I need to write a short memo — maybe two pages — that recommends fund, reject, or fund with conditions. It needs to take the skeptical half of the panel seriously, engage their actual arguments rather than caricaturing them, and give reasons the full panel can actually deliberate about. Bottom line: what do we do with this proposal, and why?

Approach this as a philosopher who knows the Malcolm/Dennett debate inside out — not just the surface positions, but the underlying arguments about what would even count as evidence for or against dreams being experiences, and whether empirical work can bear on that question at all. Think carefully before you write.
