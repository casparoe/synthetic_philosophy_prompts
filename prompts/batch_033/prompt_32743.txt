People routinely pay to watch fiction whose main selling point is that it will make them cry, and streaming platforms institutionalize this demand by curating categories of sad films and tearjerkers. At the same time, these platforms often attach content warnings to emotionally distressing material, and the surrounding culture tends to treat persistent sadness as something to be managed or relieved. What view of emotion, pleasure, and value does this practice presuppose? In particular, does the practice strictly presuppose that viewers can enjoy an artwork in virtue of the very sadness it arouses, or only that the sadness is an acceptable cost paid for some other good? Reconstruct the implicit theory of tragic enjoyment, say which rival explanations of why people seek out sad art the practice is compatible with and which it quietly rules out, and explain what would have to change in how such content is classified, marketed, or warned about if the governing view were replaced by a straightforward hedonism on which felt pleasure is the only thing sought for its own sake.
