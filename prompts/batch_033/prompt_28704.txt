My husband froze sperm before chemo and I froze eggs, and now that we're on the other side of treatment we're deciding whether to make embryos now or wait two years. I keep asking myself: if we wait, is the kid we end up with the same kid we'd have if we didn't wait, or a different one? Same gametes either way, just a later conception. My sister-in-law Priya is a philosophy PhD student and the only person I know with actual views about where a person 'comes from,' so I've been quizzing her.

Her track record so far:

- If you'd been gestated by a surrogate instead of your biological mother, you'd still be you. (yes)
- If a different sperm from your father had fertilized the same egg, that child wouldn't be you. (no)
- If the same sperm had fertilized a different egg from your mother, that child wouldn't be you. (no)
- If the embryo you came from had been frozen for five years before transfer, you'd still be you. (yes)
- If someone grew a clone from one of your skin cells, that clone wouldn't be you. (no)
- If the oak tree in our yard had grown from a different acorn, it wouldn't be that same tree. (no)
- If our kitchen table had been made from a different block of wood, it wouldn't be that same table. (no)

The case I actually want to put to her this weekend, because it's basically our situation: suppose the very same sperm and egg that made you had been frozen and then fused two years later than they actually did. Same gametes, later conception. Is the resulting person you?

I honestly can't call it — I can't seperate the two rules in my head. One rule that fits everything she's said says yes: what matters is which sperm and egg you came from, so same gametes means you, whenever they fuse. Another rule that fits just as well says no: you come from a particular zygote, a particular conception event, and a zygote that forms two years later is a different beginning even from the same materials. I think there's also a genome rule that fits some of her verdicts but is already dead after the clone one, and a same-parents rule that's dead after the different-sperm and different-egg ones.

So: predict what Priya will say, lay out the candidate rules that fit her track record (including the dead ones), say wich rule you'd bet she's actually running and why, and give me one more case to ask her that would settle it — especially if her answer to the first one still leaves two rules standing, like whether it's the particular sperm and egg as continuing things or just the matter they're made of. Also tell me how confident you are in the prediction and which step of your reasoning you're least sure about.
