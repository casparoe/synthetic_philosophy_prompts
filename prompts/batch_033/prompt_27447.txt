I'm a blockchain dev who's been deep in the weeds of consensus mechanisms for years. I keep hearing evolutionary biologists say that the handicap principle (Zahavi's thing, peacocks' tails, costly signaling) is basicaly the same idea as proof-of-work — that wasting resources proves you're not faking it. I want you to restate the whole costly signaling framework in the vocabulary of crypto and distributed systems: miners, hashpower, difficulty adjustment, Sybil resistance, honest majority, block rewards, forks, nothing-at-stake, the whole lot. Do it systematically.

Then give me the residue report. What maps cleanly? What did you have to fudge? And what in the biologists' theory has no counterpart at all in how I think about PoW?

I'm especially interested in one thing. In PoW, every miner faces the same cost per hash — the asymmetry is between the attacker and the honest majority, not between "high-quality" and "low-quality" signalers. But in the handicap principle, the whole point is that the signal has to be differentially costly: it has to hurt a low-quality individual more than a high-quality one, otherwise it doesn't carry information. Does that difference survive the translation, or does it collapse? And if it doesn't survive, is that because my vocabulary is missing something real, or because the biologists' claim is vaguer than it looks?
