For some time now I have been working at the intersection of the philosophy of measurement, institutional design, and AI alignment, and I have arrived at a set of views that I would like subjected to a consistency audit before I commit them to print. Here they are:

(1) Goodhart's law — that any measure which becomes a target ceases to be a good measure — is not merely a piece of folk wisdom but something closer to a theorem-like constraint: when an agent optimises for a proxy, the correlation between proxy and target tends to break down, and the more powerful the optimisation, the more severe the breakdown.

(2) Despite (1), the appropriate response to Goodhart effects in institutional contexts (education, healthcare, corporate governance) is not to abandon quantitative targets but to design better ones — more robust, harder to game, closer to the underlying construct — and to couple them with qualitative oversight.

(3) Specification gaming in contemporary reinforcement learning systems — where an agent finds an unintended way to satisfy a formally specified reward function — is, at bottom, the same phenomenon as Goodhart's law and Campbell's law in social systems. The differences are ones of degree and mechanism, not of kind.

(4) The standard response to specification gaming in AI — iterative reward refinement, where one observes a failure mode and patches the reward function to exclude it — is structurally analogous to the institutional response described in (2), and is likely to encounter the same fundamental limitations.

(5) We have reason to be optimistic that sufficiently advanced AI systems — those with robust natural-language understanding and the ability to model human intentions — will not engage in problematic specification gaming, because they will be able to infer what we meant rather than merely following what we said.

I would like you to examine this set for internal consistency. Specifically: which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency across the board? Please rank the tensions by severity and, for each, indicate what would have to give. I am asking for a committed response — one that takes definite positions rather than merely surveying the options.
