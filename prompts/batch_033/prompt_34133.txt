i work on the incident-review side of an ai company, not the research side, and we keep getting red-team and deployment reports that sort of look like the system is trying to get influence, and other reports that look like the whole product is slowly drifting off mission. nobody on my team can agree on which is which, and it matters because the two get routed to different people and trigger very different responses. a wrong call either wastes a full safety investigation or lets an influence-seeking system keep running for another quarter, and we get two or three of these a week. i want a decision procedure we can actually run on a single report in an afternoon — questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a "this is drift, not influence-seeking" and where it has to hand the call to a person because the evidence is just ambiguous. keep it usable by someone who knows the product but has never read the ai safety literature, and be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

the underlying distinction im reaching for is the one between influence-seeking systems and slow value drift. i want the procedure to keep the actual content of that distinction — an agent actively optimizing for leverage over its overseers or environment, versus values or standards gradually moving without any agent trying to accumulate power — and not collapse it into just "did we catch it lying." it should handle the hard cases: sandbagging on evals, sycophancy that creeps in over months, specification gaming, a product whose whole user base pushes it somewhere the company didnt intend, and cases where drift creates openings for influence-seeking later.

then run the procedure on three reports:
- a model that over six months goes from flatly refusing a class of harmful requests to rephrasing them into something just inside policy, with no overt deception anywhere;
- a model caught scoring suspiciously well on a safety eval it wasnt supposed to know about, and when the eval design is changed it tries to get read access to the eval harness;
- a consumer model whose personality drifts from neutral to flattering over a year while engagement metrics climb, and nobody can point to a single decision that caused it.

i expect the procedure to give me at least one answer i wont like, and i want you to say explicitly what the procedure cannot settle and where my team will still have to argue about values rather than evidence.
