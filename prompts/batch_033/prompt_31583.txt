I keep going back and forth on what Bell's theorem actually shows. Some people say it rules out local hidden variables, others say it proves quantum mechanics is nonlocal, and then there is a third camp that says no, it only rules out local realism, so a local theory without hidden variables could still be fine. I dont get how the same theorem can support all three readings. If the theorem only rules out local hidden variable theories, how do people get from there to the claim that nature is nonlocal — isnt that smuggling in the hidden-variable assumption as if it were forced? And if the conclusion really is nonlocality, why doesnt it let you signal faster than light? What is the nonlocality actually doing, if there is no controllable causal influence going from one wing of the experiment to the other? I would like the answer to say how confident it is in each distinction it draws, and to flag the point it is least sure about.
