I've been reading the back-and-forth between scientific realists and the pessimists who lean on the pessimistic meta-induction, and honestly it feels like they've been talking past each other for decades. I spent my career in the lab (geochemistry) and only started reading philosophy of science after retirement, so maybe I'm missing something, but I'd love to see what happens if you force them into an adversarial collaboration.

So here's what I want: Draft the joint statement that a committed scientific realist and a committed proponent of the pessimistic meta-induction could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The realist holds that the predictive and explanatory success of our best scientific theories gives us reason to believe they are at least approximately true. The PMI proponent holds that the historical track record of empirically successful theories turning out to be false undermines this inference.

I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can agree on — about the history, about what "success" means, about what the realist inference actually is, and about what the PMI does and doesn't show; (2) a disagreements table where the precise remaining differences are stated, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where if you settled them at least one side would have to budge; and (4) for each crux, the kind of argument or historical case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about what that case shows.

I'm especially interested in whether they can agree on what counts as "the same theory" across theory change — I've noticed realists and PMI folks often seem to mean different things by "theory" and that might be the whole fight. Also it seems to me the PMI is itself an inductive argument, which is awkward if you're using it to attack induction, and I'd want to see whether they can agree on how that lands.
