I serve on the committee of a small housing co-operative, and we must decide how to allocate next year's maintenance budget. At present, the larger flats receive more spending per resident than the smaller flats. One proposal would equalise spending per resident, but because the total budget is fixed this could only be done by reducing what is spent on the larger flats; the smaller flats would receive no more than they do now. Several committee members support the proposal on the grounds that the resulting allocation would be fairer. I share their wish for fairness, yet I am uneasy about a change that would make some residents worse off while making no one better off, and I cannot articulate the source of this unease. Are their grounds sound, and what principle should guide our decision?
