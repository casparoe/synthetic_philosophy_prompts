I'm a philosophy professor, but I've been co-teaching this grad seminar that crosses over into psychophysics -- signal detection theory, Weber's law, just-noticeable differences, all that -- and I cannot shake the feeling that Williamson's anti-luminosity argument (the one that's supposed to sink the KK principle) is just psychophysics reheated as epistemology.

Here's the picture. Williamson says: take a condition that changes gradually across cases, like feeling cold, where adjacent cases are too close to discriminate reliably. If the condition were luminous (whenever it obtains, you're in a position to know it obtains), you'd have to detect the exact boundary where it stops, which you can't. So nothing non-trivial is luminous, and KK fails.

But signal detection theory has known forever that any detector near threshold has a region of uncertainty. You've got your criterion, your sensitivity (d-prime), and when d-prime is small for adjacent stimulus levels, you get a region where you can't say confidently whether the signal is present. That's just the psychometric function being sigmoidal -- the hit rate and false-alarm rate smear into each other.

So here's what I want. Restate the anti-luminosity argument in the vocabulary of signal detection theory: criterion, sensitivity, d-prime, the ROC curve, the whole nine yards. What in the argument maps cleanly onto the fact that finite sensitivity implies a region of uncertainty? What do you have to distort to make it fit? And what in Williamson's argument has no counterpart in signal detection theory at all? Then give me the residue report. Is the anti-luminosity argument just rediscovering that psychometric functions aren't step functions, or is there something about the normativity of knowledge that can't be captured by d-prime and hit rates? In particular: does the signal detection translation reveal that the argument works against any detection system, not specifically against knowledge, or does it miss something about the first-person character of knowing that no amount of signal detection theory can model?
