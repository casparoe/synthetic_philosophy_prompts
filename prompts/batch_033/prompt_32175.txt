My new job is to evaluate proposals for alignment techniques that come from our researchers, and to recommend which ones should get engineering resources allocated to them. The proposals range from new reward-modeling schemes to interpretability tools to adversarial training curricula. The problem I keep running into is that most techniques look fine on our current models, but I have no principled way to judge whether they will hold up when the models get more capable — which is the whole point.

I need a decision procedure I can run on a proposal: a set of questions to ask in order, tests to apply, and red flags to check for, grounded in what we actually know about how alignment techniques can fail to scale. The procedure should be usable by someone with an ML engineering background but no specialized alignment theory, it should take no more than an afternoon per proposal, and it should flag the points where the answer genuinely depends on a judgment call rather than an engineering fact. Where the procedure can't settle the question, it should tell me what additional information would.

Please then run the procedure on two proposals: (1) a scheme that trains a model to produce critiques of its own outputs and uses those critiques as an auxiliary reward signal, and (2) a technique that uses sparse autoencoders to find features corresponding to undesirable behaviors and then applies a fine-tuning penalty to those features.

Please begin with the procedure itself, with no preface or commentary.
