So I've been down this rabbit hole lately — I was reading some old LessWrong posts about calibration and Aumann agreement, and that led me to the actual philosophy of probability literature, which is how I ended up spending a weekend trying to figure out whether Van Fraassen's Reflection principle is actually a rationality constraint or just, like, a useful heuristic that happens to hold most of the time. The basic idea: a rational agent's current credence in P should equal their expected future credence in P. You should treat your future self as an expert. Except there are all these counterexamples — if you know you're going to take a sleeping pill that makes you irrationally certain of things, your current credence shouldn't defer to your drugged-up future credence. So then the debate becomes: is Reflection a genuine norm of rationality, or is it just a rule of thumb that holds when you don't expect your epistemic abilities to degrade?
