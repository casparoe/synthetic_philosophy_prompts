You are an expert in decision theory with a deep knowledge of expected utility theory, the Allais paradox, and the independence axiom. Please work through the following problem carefully and systematically, starting with some initial brainstorming before arriving at your final analysis.

A colleague of mine in behavioral economics has, over many years, kept a record of her own choices on a variety of decision problems under risk. She treats these as a kind of personal dataset and has occasionally wondered aloud what principle she is implicitly following. She has not told me what she thinks the principle is. Below are four pairs of gambles with her recorded choice for each. Assume all outcomes are in dollars, there is no background wealth to worry about, and she treats each pair in isolation.

Pair 1:
A: $3,000 for certain.
B: 80% chance of $4,000; 20% chance of $0.
She chose A.

Pair 2:
C: 25% chance of $3,000; 75% chance of $0.
D: 20% chance of $4,000; 80% chance of $0.
She chose D.

Pair 3:
E: $3,000 for certain.
F: 90% chance of $3,500; 10% chance of $0.
She chose E.

Pair 4:
G: 10% chance of $3,000; 90% chance of $0.
H: 9% chance of $3,500; 91% chance of $0.
She chose H.

Now, I would like to ask her about a fifth pair, which she has not yet seen:

Pair 5:
I: 99% chance of $3,000; 1% chance of $0.
J: 89.1% chance of $3,500; 10.9% chance of $0.

My questions for you:

1. Identify at least two distinct principles that are jointly consistent with her four recorded choices. State each principle precisely enough that it makes a definite prediction about Pair 5.

2. For each principle, what does it predict she will choose in Pair 5, and why?

3. Based on the four choices alone, which principle do you think is the better fit, and what is your best prediction for her choice in Pair 5?

4. If you could present her with just one additional pair of gambles — beyond the five above — to settle which principle she is really following, what would that pair be? Explain how the two principles would diverge on it and why.

Please show your reasoning at each step, and be explicit about any structural features of the gambles — common-consequence structure, ratio preservation, expected values, and so on — on which your analysis depends.
