I'm taking a philosophy of mind course this semester and we just finished a unit on Dennett's *Consciousness Explained*. I'm working on a short paper about the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque distinction in the color phi phenomenon, and I'd love some feedback on my core argument before I flesh it out into a full draft.

Here's the basic idea I'm running with: Dennett claims there's no fact of the matter about whether the brain revises the memory of the experience (Orwellian) or constructs the experience itself after the fact (Stalinesque), because both accounts posit the same functional processes just located at different points in the stream. But I'm not sure his argument actually works for the color phi case specifically. In the standard setup, a red dot flashes at position A, then a green dot flashes at position B, and subjects report seeing a single dot move from A to B and change from red to green roughly midway. The puzzle is: how could the brain "know" the second dot was going to be green before it actually appeared, in order to construct the illusory color change? Dennett's answer is that consciousness isn't a single point in time — the brain edits the narrative retrospectively, and by the time you report the experience the whole sequence has already been processed. So the "midway color change" is just a post-hoc story the brain tells itself.

My worry is this: even if we accept the multiple drafts model, there still seems to be a real empirical question about where in the processing stream the "editing" happens. If it happens before the content reaches whatever systems are responsible for verbal report and deliberate action, that's Stalinesque — the experience itself was tampered with. If it happens after those systems have already "received" a version of events, that's Orwellian — the memory was tampered with. And these seem like they could have different downstream consequences. For instance, if you hooked someone up to a lie detector or measured their reaction times during the phi phenomenon, wouldn't Orwellian and Stalinesque accounts predict different physiological signatures? The Orwellian story suggests there might be some residual trace of the "unedited" version (like a brief startle at the actual sequence), while the Stalinesque story says the fabrication goes all the way down. I feel like Dennett kinda glosses over this by saying it's all just "narrative" and there's no fact of the matter, but that seems like it would make the whole distinction empirically empty in a way that's maybe too quick.

I guess my question is: am I missing something in Dennett's argument that makes this objection not land? Or is this actually a live issue in the literature? I'd also appreciate any thoughts on whether the essay would be more interesting if I brought in some of the empirical work on apparent motion and backward masking, or if that would just muddy the philosophical point.
