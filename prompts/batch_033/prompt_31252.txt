I'm a novelist, not an academic, but I've been offered a six-month research residency at a philosophy institute (they take artists), and the application needs a short proposal. The novel I'm working on is set maybe forty years out: a company runs uploaded employees, digital reconstructions of people, and the plot turns on whether shutting one down is murder, property damage, or something else entirely. I've read enough of the moral-status debate to feel like its stuck, and my half-formed hunch is that it's because people keep running together three different questions: (1) whether a digital mind could be conscious at all, (2) whether it could suffer or have a welfare, and (3) whether we can owe obligations to a thing even when the first two are unsettled. I suspect the third question is doing more of the real work than the literature admits, and that separating the three would let the debate move.

Turn this into a one-page proposal for the residency: a precise statement of the question, why the existing positions leave it open, the method (what I'd actually do, month by month), and what would count as the project failing. Dont worry about citations; I'll add whatever the institute wants later.
