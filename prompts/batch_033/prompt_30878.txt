We're doing a unit on hedonism in my value theory module and I've been trying to get my own view straight before I have to present it in seminar. The problem is that whenever I write it all down it looks like it's about to fall apart, but I can't see exactly where the cracks are. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a hedonist about well-being: the only thing that's good for a person in itself is pleasure, and the only thing bad in itself is pain; everything else is good only instrumentally. (2) I think pleasure is a feeling — a distinctive qualitative character an experience has, not a belief, a desire, or a judgement. (3) But I'm also fairly convinced by the heterogeneity point: when I introspect the pleasure of getting into a warm bed, the pleasure of finally understanding a proof, the pleasure of being praised, and the pleasure of a cigarette, I can't find any felt quality they all share. (4) I take introspection to be authoritative about wether two of my experiences share a qualitative character, so I can't just wave that finding away. (5) And I do think hedonic bookkeeping makes sense: you can compare two lives by how much pleasure they contain, add up intensities and durations across different kinds of pleasure, and say one is better for the person living it than another by a determinate amount.

Could you go through these five commitments and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look as if they do? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, say what the cheapest fix would be for each real tension, and tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set hang together.
