(1) i know a bunch of ordinary things -- that i have hands, that my car is parked outside, that i ate oatmeal this morning. i'm not just pretty confident; i know them.

(2) knowledge is factive. if i know p, then p is true. (i'm not willing to give this up.)

(3) if i know p, then i'm in a position to know that any counterevidence to p would have to be misleading -- because p is true, and misleading evidence just is evidence that points away from the truth. and if i know the counterevidence is misleading before i even look at it, it seems like i'm rationally entitled to dismiss it without examining it.

(4) but it's obviously epistemically irresponsible to blow off counterevidence without looking at it. like, if someone says "hey, i think your car got towed, here's why," and i just go "nope, i know it's there, not interested," that's bad epistemic practice. that's not how a responsible knower behaves.

(5) i'm a fallibilist: i think you can know p even if your evidence doesn't guarantee p. (so i can know my car is outside even though it's possible it got towed.)

so that's five things i find myself wanting to endorse. but (3) and (4) look like they're in direct conflict, and (5) seems to make the conflict worse by opening the door to the possibility that i'm wrong even when i know. meanwhile (1) and (2) are the non-negotiables that got me into this mess.
