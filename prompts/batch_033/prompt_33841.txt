I'm a reporter putting together an explainer on psychiatric diagnosis — the DSM, the neurodiversity movement, the argument over whether conditions like ADHD and depression are "real." My editor, who did some science studies back in the day, sent me a paragraph to use as a framing device. It reads roughly like this: "Hacking's looping effect shows that psychiatric categories don't just label pre-existing conditions; they change the people classified. Once people learn they have a diagnosis, they start interpreting their moods and behavior through it, which changes the symptoms the category was meant to track, which forces psychiatrists to redraw the category, which changes people again. So psychiatric kinds keep shifting under your feet — unlike natural kinds, they can't be pinned down the way we pin down a chemical element. The upshot is that conditions like ADHD are partly created by the labels themselves, and the whole project of carving psychiatric disorders at their joints is doomed." I've read just enough Hacking to suspect this isn't crazy, but something about the last two sentences feel like they overreach, and I don't want to build an explainer on a misreading. Walk me through it: which parts can I use without getting letters from philosophers, which parts are defensible only with a bunch of qualifiers, and which parts would a Hacking specialist flatly reject? And does the punchline — that ADHD is partly created by its label and that the carve-at-the-joints project is doomed — actually follow from the rest, or is it riding on one of the shakier moves?
