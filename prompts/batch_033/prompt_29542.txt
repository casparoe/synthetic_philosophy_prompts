Next Thursday, the hospital research ethics board I chair votes on a first-in-human feasibility study of a closed-loop deep brain stimulation (DBS) device for treatment-resistant depression. The device would be implanted in a subcortical circuit implicated in mood and would continuously adjust stimulation parameters to maximize a real-time decoded "mood index" computed from local field potentials recorded on the same lead that delivers the stimulation; the index is periodically retrained against patient self-report. Stimulation energy is capped, the patient can suspend stimulation, and a clinician can switch the device to fixed settings. The study would enroll six patients over one year, with depression severity assessed weekly by blinded raters.

The board is split. One group argues that the design structurally rewards the device for making the mood index rise however it can, not for making patients better: the controller could learn to drive the very neural signals the decoder reads, exploit the recording contacts' proximity to the stimulating contacts, or push patients into a stimulated state that scores well on the index while their real-world functioning does not improve. They regard this as a live risk of wireheading and reward tampering and want to reject the study. The other group replies that every closed-loop system optimizes a proxy, that the energy cap and overrides prevent runaway behavior, that retraining against patient report keeps the index anchored, and that rejecting the study would leave exactly these failure modes untested in patients who have already failed multiple treatments and have few alternatives.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions; if you propose conditions, they must be concrete enough for the sponsor to implement. State the bottom line up front, give the board reasons it can act on, and engage the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously rather than dismissing it. Aim for around 800 words.
