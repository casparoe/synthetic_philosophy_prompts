Here is the package I currently hold.

(1) Assertion is governed by a knowledge norm: a speaker is entitled to assert that p only if she knows that p.

(2) Moore-paradoxical assertions — utterances of the schema p, but I do not believe that p — are defective for a purely epistemic reason: the asserted content cannot be known, and this unknowability is the complete explanation of why such utterances cannot properly be made.

(3) Moore-paradoxical beliefs are irrational, and the right explanation is the transparency of belief: from the deliberative standpoint, the question whether I believe that p is settled by settling whether p, so one cannot coherently judge both that p and that one does not believe that p.

(4) Asserting that p expresses the belief that p; this is part of what distinguishes asserting from merely uttering a sentence.

(5) A fully satisfying account of Moore's paradox should explain the defective assertion and the irrational belief by a single underlying mechanism. A view that offers one story for the assertion case and a different story for the belief case should count that disunity as a serious cost.

(6) The KK principle is false: one can know that p while not being in a position to know that one knows that p.

Please audit this set.
