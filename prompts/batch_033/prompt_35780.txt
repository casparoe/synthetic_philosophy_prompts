I'm preparing an episode of my decision-theory podcast with a researcher who argues that acausal trade — cooperation between agents that cannot causally interact but can predict one another's reasoning — should be given serious weight in choices about long-term priorities. Because I recently had to make a grantmaking decision in which this kind of reasoning was urged on me, I want questions that force him to say precisely what the view commits him to, rather than questions that let him restate its appeal. Write a set of questions that press on the machinery of the view: how an agent selects a counterparty when the space of possible agents is astronomically large; whether acausal trade adds anything to what evidential decision theory already says about Newcomb-like cases; and why the framework does not simply license extortion by any agent able to threaten to simulate us. For each of these, include the follow-up question that takes the reply he is most likely to give as its premise and pushes one step further.
