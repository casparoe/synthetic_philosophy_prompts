My working views:

(1) Speaker meaning is a matter of the speaker's communicative intentions — roughly, meaning something by an utterance is intending the audience to recognize the intention behind the utterance.

(2) What is said is fixed by the conventional meaning of the sentence, plus only the context needed to assign reference and resolve ambiguity. No extra pragmatic enrichment sneaks into what is said.

(3) Conversational implicatures have to be calculable from something like the Cooperative Principle and the maxims, and they have to be cancellable.

(4) Generalized conversational implicatures — like the move from some of the students passed to not all of them did — are genuine implicatures rather than part of what is said; they just arise by default rather than from a special context.

(5) Irony and metaphor are cases where the speaker only goes through the motions of saying the literal content and means something else, so they come out as implicatures, not as part of what is said.

(6) Modified Occam's razor: don't multiply senses beyond necessity — if a pragmatic explanation handles the data, don't posit a lexical ambiguity.
