I serve on my university's religious accomodation committee. A group of students from several faith traditions has petitioned to make the mandatory 'Respect and Consent' workshop optional — they say it promotes a conception of sexuality and the good life that conflicts with their religious doctrines, and that a public university shouldn't compel participation in moral education that contradicts their comprehensive views. The committee is split. One faction wants to deny the request, citing Rawls: public reason requires that institutional decisions be justified by reasons all reasonable citizens can accept, and the students' objection rests on comprehensive religious doctrines that fail this test. The other faction says this overextends Rawls — his public reason requirement was meant for constitutional essentials and matters of basic justice, not for university workshop policies, and Rawls himself argued that religious citizens may properly introduce their comprehensive views in the broader 'background culture.' I need to draft the decision memo for our meeting next week. It should recommend a concrete policy with a rationale, engage both sides' use of Rawls seriously, and give the losing position it's strongest argument fairly stated. Please write in British English.
