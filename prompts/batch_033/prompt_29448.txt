My half-formed idea concerns the Jain doctrines of anekāntavāda and syādvāda: the view that reality is irreducibly many-sided, and that every assertion about a thing holds only under a qualification — the particle syāt, roughly 'in some respect' — with the full range of truths about the thing captured by the sevenfold scheme of predication (saptabhaṅgī). I would like you to turn this into a proposal built around a precise question: what kind of thesis is anekāntavāda, and which reconstruction — metaphysical, epistemic, semantic, or some hybrid — best withstands the two standard objections that (i) the doctrine is self-refuting, because the claim that all assertions hold only qualifiedly is itself asserted unqualifiedly, and (ii) the doctrine is trivial, because the qualifier syāt can be attached to any claim so as to make it true in some respect? The proposal should state the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the existing reconstructions leave it open; set out a method — what I would actually do month by month across the twelve months, working conceptually from existing English translations and the secondary literature rather than from Sanskrit — and say why that approach is preferable to the obvious alternatives, such as a purely doxographical survey or a straight defence of one traditional reading. It should also describe what a successful answer would look like, what would count as the project failing, and give a realistic assessment of scope. Please do not include a bibliography or citations; I will supply those myself.
