I've been assembling the published case-verdicts of a metaphysician who works on the special composition question, the question of when some things compose a further thing. She is a restrictivist: she holds that composition happens in some cases and not in others, and she has gone on record about the following cases. The molecules arranged dog-wise in a living, healthy dog compose an object, she says; so do the cells arranged oak-wise in a living tree, and the macromolecules arranged bacterium-wise in a living bacterium. But the grains of sand in a dune do not compose an object; nor do the unmortared bricks in a builder's pile, two people shaking hands, a flock of starlings in flight, or the cards in a deck on a table.

Next month a visitor is going to press her on three cases she has never ruled on: a granite boulder, a functioning wristwatch, and a dog's hind leg that has been surgically removed and is being kept alive by a perfusion machine. Predict her verdict on each, and say what rule she seems to be running. I want more than the bare predictions: I want the candidate rules that fit her published verdicts equally well (I can see at least two), what each candidate predicts for the three new cases, which of the three new cases would do the most work in telling the candidates apart, and which candidate you would bet on. Then give me one further case, not on her record and not among the three, that would settle the matter most cleanly, and say what each candidate rule predicts for it.
