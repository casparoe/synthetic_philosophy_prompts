I run a small discussion group at my local library and we've been going back and forth for weeks on why religion exists — like, is it basically just something brains do, a kind of glitch or byproduct, or is there something more going on? People have really different instincts about this but they struggle to pin down what they actually think, so I want to make a quiz that sorts them by their real views instead of by what label sounds nice.

Can you write me a "what's your actual position on religion as a natural phenomenon?" quiz? Here's what I'm picturing:

Eight to ten questions. Each one should be a concrete case or scenario, not a abstract statement like "religion is reducible to psychology." Give a specific situation and three or four answer options that force a choice about what's really going on. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto roughly five positions:

1. Religion is entirely a natural phenomenon — evolution, cognitive biases, social bonding, the whole thing is explained without any supernatural ingredient.
2. Religion has natural origins and functions, but that doesn't rule out the possibility that some of it connects to something real beyond the natural.
3. We can study religion's natural dimensions just fine without ever taking a position on whether the beliefs are true — the two projects are separate.
4. Natural explanations capture surface-level stuff (rituals, group dynamics) but miss the actual supernatural core that makes religion what it is.
5. Natural explanations are basically a category mistake — religion is a response to genuine divine reality and trying to reduce it to biology or psychology misses the point entirely.

The trick is: don't ask people which of those they identify with! The questions should be about cases where these views genuinely come apart. Like, if someone reads about a study showing religious experiences light up the same brain regions as psychedelics, does their mind go to "well there you go, that's what it is" or "sure, that's the mechanism — so what?" That kind of thing. Or a case where a specific prayer seems to get answered in a striking way, or a case about children raised in different faiths ending up with similar moral intuitions, or a case about near-death experiences across cultures.

Also: if there's a answer combination that's logically possible but no real position seems to occupy it, flag that and say why you think it's empty — is it incoherent? just really unattractive? or maybe nobody's thought to defend it yet? And if two of the five positions can't really be separated by your questions, be honest about that rather than pretending the quiz distinguishes them.

Please start with the quiz itself — no preface, no commentary, just the questions and then the scoring key. And at the end, tell me how confident you are in the mapping from answers to positions, and single out whichever part of the scoring you're least sure about.
