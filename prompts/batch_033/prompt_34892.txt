Construct a thought experiment in which an agent faces a single decision problem that has the structure of a counterfactual mugging nested inside an XOR blackmail, such that the policy that handles counterfactual muggings (paying up when the counterfactual condition is satisfied) makes the agent vulnerable to exploitation in the XOR structure, and vice versa. Constraints: no perfect predictors, no exotic physics, no agents with logically inconsistent preferences — a situation a professional might face in a negotiation, a legal settlement, or an insurance decision, at most one paragraph. The agent faces a one-shot choice. After the scenario, walk through what causal decision theory and updateless decision theory each recommend, and explain exactly where they come apart. Then identify the structural feature of the combined case that does the work — the feature you would remove to collapse it back into a standard counterfactual mugging or a standard XOR blackmail. Finish by saying what a defender of whichever theory comes out worse would say to resist the case, and whether that response is ad hoc or principled.
