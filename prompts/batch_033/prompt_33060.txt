My epistemology professor has been giving us verdicts on knowledge cases all semester and I've been keeping a list. Here's what she's said so far:

- Low-stakes bank (DeRose): you remember the bank was open Saturday a few weeks ago, you have no urgent need to deposit, lines are long. She says you know the bank is open Saturday.
- High-stakes bank: exact same evidence, but you've written a large check that'll bounce if you don't deposit by Saturday. She says you don't know.
- Airport (Cohen): Mary and John both have the same itinerary showing their flight stops in Chicago. Mary has a tight connection to her best friend's wedding; John is just picking someone up with no time pressure. She says Mary doesn't know the flight stops in Chicago but John does.
- Lottery: one ticket in a fair million-ticket draw, before the drawing. She says you don't know your ticket will lose.
- Ordinary perception: you look at a tree in good light, normal eyesight. She says you know there's a tree in front of you.
- Stopped clock (Gettier-style): you glance at a clock that stopped exactly 24 hours ago, it happens to read the right time at that moment. She says you don't know the time.

Next week we're doing a new case and I want to predict her: Alex is a journalist covering a corruption trial. He's read all the evidence, it's genuinely strong, the defendant is in fact guilty, and Alex's belief is safe and sensitive. But Alex's editor has told him that if he gets the verdict wrong in his article he loses his job; if he's right, he gets a promotion. So a lot is riding on it for Alex. The question: does Alex know the defendant is guilty, before the jury comes back?

I can see at least two theories that fit her verdicts so far — epistemic contextualism and subject-sensitive invariantism — and they might come apart on Alex. Which one do you think she's running, what's your best guess for her verdict on Alex, and what's the single best further case to nail down which theory she holds? If there's a third theory I'm missing that fits her track record, flag it.
