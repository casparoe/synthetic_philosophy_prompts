Write a dialogue between two philosophers stuck on a long train ride: one defends the zombie conceivability argument against physicalism (roughly Chalmers-style), and the other is a type-B materialist who thinks zombies are conceivable but metaphysically impossible. The type-B materialist should deploy something like the phenomenal concepts strategy — the idea that we have special, conceptually isolated ways of thinking about our own experiences that create an illusion of a metaphysical gap where there isn't one. The zombie defender should push back: why think phenomenal concepts work that way, and doesn't that just relocate the mystery rather than solving it? Make sure they genuinely respond to each other. Neither should be a strawman.
