Amateur here, reading around in philosophy of mind on my own. I keep noticing a pattern in arguments about how mental states get their content, and I don't have a word for it. Someone proposes that a state means X because the mechanism behind it was selected — evolutionarily, or by reinforcement — to do X; the stock case is a frog whose snap response means "fly" because it was selected to catch flies. Then someone else points out that the very same mechanism was also selected to detect small dark moving things, or to catch fly-sized food items, and those descriptions pick out different contents, so the content starts to slide around even after selection has been brought in. I've seen the same shape of reply against learned catagories: whatever got reinforced got reinforced under a description, and more than one description fits. Is there an established name for this specific problem? I know it's adjacent to the thing where a state is caused by two different things and you ask why it means one rather than the other (the disjunction problem, if I've got that right), and adjacent to Quine's indeterminacy stuff, but it seems different: the trouble isn't the causes, it's the grain of the function. If there's no name of its own, what would you call it, and where exactly is the boundary between it and those neighbors? I'm less interested in the label than in the carve-up.
