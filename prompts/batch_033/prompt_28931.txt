Over the past year I have been reading about utilitarianism on my own, and there is one point where I keep losing my footing. The view's defenders say that pleasure is the only thing that is good in itself, and they speak of it as though it names one thing. But the items they place under that heading do not seem to share any felt character. The relief when a pain stops, the satisfaction of finishing a difficult puzzle, the comfort of warmth after being cold, and the enjoyment of a good meal all feel entirely unlike one another; even two bodily ones, such as scratching an itch and drinking when thirsty, do not feel alike. What they appear to share is only that I want them or am glad to be having them. Does this difficulty have an established name, and how is it best formulated? I am not after a bare label: I want to know what exactly the problem is, how the main ways of responding to it differ from one another, and where the remaining disagreement lies.
