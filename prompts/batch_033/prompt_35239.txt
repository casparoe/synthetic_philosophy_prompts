I'm a novelist, working on a near-future political novel where a country switches to sortition — selecting its legislature by lottery. While researching, I've been reading real-world proposals and case studies of citizens' assemblies, and I keep noticing a pattern that's driving me nuts. It goes like this: someone proposes giving a randomly-selected citizens' assembly real legislative power. The response is almost always, "Interesting idea, but let's start with a purely advisory pilot and see how it does." Then, if the pilot works well, the conclusion is "Great, it's working as an advisory body, let's keep it that way." If the pilot falters — often precisely because it has no real power and people treat it as a performative exercise — the conclusion is "See, sortition doesn't scale." Either way, the pilot never graduates to actual power. It feels like a trap, but a trap that isn't just generic institutional inertia, because the pilot itself is used as the justification for never moving beyond it. Is there a name for this specific dynamic? If not, can you give it one and distinguish it from related things like regulatory capture, the overton window, or plain old foot-dragging?
