Construct a thought experiment in which the control theory of privacy and the limited-access theory of privacy come apart in the less familiar direction: the control theory (privacy consists in control over information about oneself) says a person lacks privacy, while the limited-access theory (privacy consists in others' actually lacking access to information about one, whatever the source of that limitation) says the person has privacy. The scenario must be realistic — a workplace, hospital, apartment building, or similar — and no more than one paragraph. The person must genuinely lack control over whether others access the information, and the fact that others do not access it must be an ordinary consequence of the social arrangement, not a lucky accident or a broken device. The verdicts should fall out of what each theory takes control and access to amount to, not from stipulating that the person feels exposed or that the information is trivial. After the scenario, state what the information is, who could access it, why the person cannot stop them, and why they in fact do not access it. Then give the verdict each theory delivers and why, identify the feature of the case doing the work, and say what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed. Finish with the strongest reply available to the control theorist, and with a note on where your own reasoning is most likely to fail: which consideration, if supplied, would lead you to change your mind about whether the case really drives the two theories apart.
