I want your credence that money pump arguments succeed in establishing that cyclic preferences are irrational. By "succeed" I mean: the argument shows that an agent with cyclic preferences (A ≻ B ≻ C ≻ A) over a fixed set of options makes a mistake of rationality, not merely that such an agent would lose money if exploited by a clever trader. Please decompose your credence into at least the following sub-questions:

(a) Whether the possibility of being money-pumped is a symptom of an underlying irrationality rather than an independent argument that establishes it — that is, whether the pump merely dramatizes a problem that must already be there for the pump to get traction.

(b) Whether a sophisticated agent who foresees the sequence of trades and refuses to engage at the outset thereby avoids the charge of irrationality, or whether the foresight itself requires a kind of preference revision that concedes the point.

(c) Whether the normative force of diachronic consistency requirements — for instance, that your preferences at t₁ should cohere with your preferences at t₂ in the absence of new information — can be grounded without already presupposing the very principle, like transitivity, that the money pump is meant to vindicate.

(d) Whether the argument overgenerates by condemning preference structures that seem rationally permissible, such as non-transitive preferences arising from the interaction of multiple reasonable criteria (e.g., choosing between jobs on the basis of salary, location, and intellectual interest, where pairwise comparisons yield cycles because no single criterion dominates).

For each sub-question, give a probability and a one- or two-sentence justification. Then explain how these probabilities combine to yield your overall credence — in particular, state whether you treat them as independent, and if not, what dependencies you model. Identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to: if you were to revise that one sub-question's probability, which way would your overall credence move and by roughly how much? Finally, tell me what evidence or argument would most shift your view on that pivotal sub-question.
