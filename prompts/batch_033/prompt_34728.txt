i'm giving the opening talk at a two-day workshop on AI and democratic institutions, and the organizers want the whole event anchored in Dennett's "counterfeit people" worry from that Atlantic piece a couple years back. the room is genuinely split: half are people who can talk shop about RLHF and interpretability and red-teaming, and the other half are political theorists and philosophers who've never trained a model in their life. i get about twenty minutes.

here's what the talk has to do. i want to convince both halves that Dennett's counterfeit-people framing is importantly different from the standard x-risk story, and that the difference changes what we actually do. the x-risk people worry the AI gets too smart and kills us; Dennett's point was that the AI doesnt need to be smart at all, it just needs to be convincing enough to corrode the epistemic environment democracy runs on — and that's not a future threat, it's already happening. the hard part is that if you take the diagnosis seriously, the obvious solutions look pretty weak, because Dennett himself spent decades arguing that we can't help taking the intentional stance toward anything that talks sensibly with us. so "teach people to spot the fakes" is asking people to do something his own account says their almost incapable of doing reliably. i want the talk to sit in that tension rather than resolve it cleanly. end on something constructive but modest — no policy wishlist, no call to build, just something the room can actually hold onto.
