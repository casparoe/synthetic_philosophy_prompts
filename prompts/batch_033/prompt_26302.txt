Two colleagues in my department got into it at a reception last week about Hacking's looping effects, and I've been turning it over ever since. Miriam (philosophy of psychiatry) said looping effects show that psychiatric categories can't be treated as natural kinds — they're interactive kinds, and the whole DSM project of carving nature at the joints is fundamentally misguided for mental disorders. David (philosophy of biology) pushed back hard: he said plenty of medical categories have feedback loops too (a cancer diagnosis changes your behavior, stress levels, immune function), and the fact that a category has looping effects doesn't mean there's no stable underlying biology. Miriam replied that the mechanism is different: in psychiatry, people consciously incorporate the label into their self-conception and shape their behavior to match it — that's not what happens with a tumor. She brought up the classic example of multiple personality disorder: once the diagnosis entered the broader culture, the number of alters patients reported shot up dramatically. David conceded that's a real phenomenon but said (a) it's an empirical question how much looping any given category exhibits, and you can't rule out a priori that some psychiatric categories have minimal looping and stable biological correlates, and (b) even where looping is strong, it doesn't mean there's no underlying pathology — it just means the expression of the pathology is socially mediated. Miriam said she wasn't ruling anything out a priori, but the history of psychiatry gives strong inductive reason to expect looping across the board, and more importantly, looping changes what it means to validate a category — you can't just do it the way you'd validate a mineral kind. David said that sounded right but he wasn't sure it amounted to "fundamentally misguided" rather than "needs to be done more carefully." They had to break it off there.

I'd like you to go through their exchange move by move and say who has the better of each point, and then give me your overall assessment: does looping make the DSM project fundamentally misguided, or just harder? And if Miriam's right that validation has to work differently for interactive kinds, what does that different validation look like?
