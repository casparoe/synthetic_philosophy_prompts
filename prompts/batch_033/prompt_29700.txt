I'm preparing a statement of my views on perceptual experience for a seminar and would like an internal consistency check before I circulate it. My commitments are these: (1) naive realism about veridical perception: a veridical experience is a direct relation to a mind-independent object, and that object partly constitutes the experience. (2) Disjunctivism: veridical perception and hallucination are fundamentally different kinds of mental state, sharing no common fundamental kind. (3) Introspective indistinguishability is the best available test for sameness of phenomenal character: if two experiences cannot be told apart from the inside, the default conclusion should be that their phenomenal character is the same. (4) Total hallucinations can occur and can be introspectively indistinguishable from veridical perceptions. (5) The causal version of the argument from illusion is unsound, because sameness of proximal stimulation does not by itself establish sameness of perceptual state. Please audit this package: identify which commitments are in genuine tension, rank the tensions by severity, distinguish them from any that are merely apparent, and say what the least costly revision would be and what it would sacrifice. Also indicate how confident you are in each diagnosis and single out the point of your assessment you are least sure of.
