I've been running a faculty reading group on American pragmatism for about six months now, and we've gotten stuck in a way that's starting to feel less like productive disagreement and more like a structural problem with the whole conversation. We read Peirce's "How to Make Our Ideas Clear" and "The Fixation of Belief," then James's Pragmatism lectures, and ever since we've been circling the same argument: half the group thinks James basically distorted Peirce's account of truth into something almost opposite, and the other half thinks James was just drawing out the practical implications Peirce himself was too cautious to embrace.

Both sides have read the same texts, and everyone agrees on the basic facts of what each philosopher said. Peirce says truth is the opinion fated to be agreed upon by all who investigate — it's tied to the ideal limit of inquiry, what the community of inquirers would converge on in the long run. James says truth is what works, what's expedient in the way of belief, what has cash value in experience — truth happens to an idea, it's something that proves itself useful. The disagreement in our group isn't about whether these are different formulations; it's about whether they're different theories. One camp says James abandoned the normativity in Peirce — truth becomes whatever satisfies an individual's momentary needs, not what a community would converge on given enough investigation. The other camp says James is just operationalizing Peirce: if you can't cash out "what the community would converge on" in practical terms, you're just waving your hands at an abstraction, and James is the one actually doing the work Peirce called for.

I've started to suspect this isn't really a disagreement about the texts at all. It feels like a deeper disagreement about what a theory of truth is supposed to *do* — whether it's supposed to describe an ideal epistemic condition or give us a live criterion for when we're warranted in calling something true right now. But even that diagnosis might be too neat, and I've been wrong about these things before. I want to know what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal — two groups using the word truth to mean different things, and if we disambiguated they'd find they don't conflict? Is it substantive — a real disagreement about what matters for a belief to be true, and one side is just wrong? Is it a disagreement about the explanatory desiderata for a theory of truth, in which case neither side can be refuted by the other's lights because they don't share standards of success? Or is it something else entirely — maybe a disagreement about what Peirce *meant* that's genuinely underdetermined by the texts?

And given your diagnosis: is there anything that would actually move this debate, or is it the kind of thing where the best we can hope for is clarity about why we're stuck? I don't need a survey of the secondary literature — I want the structural diagnosis.
