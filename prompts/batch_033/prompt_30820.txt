I keep noticing a pattern in arguments about whether we can trust a faculty or an instrument, and I don't have a word for it. Someone wants to check whether their color vision is reliable, so they look at a set of colored chips and see whether the color they seem to see matches the label printed on each chip — but they used the same color vision to read the labels. Another case: someone tests a scale by weighing objects whose weights are already 'known,' but those weights were themselves measured on that same scale, and the agreement is taken as evidence the scale is fine. Or someone reasons, 'The table looks red, so my visual system is working properly.' These moves feel too cheap, but they aren't exactly circular in the textbook sense, since the person is gathering evidence rather than just assuming the conclusion. Does this pattern have an established name? Wether or not it does, I want more than a label: I want the precise diagnosis. What makes this different from ordinary circular reasoning, is it always a mistake or only under some conditions, and where exactly is the line between a legitimate check and this kind of move?
