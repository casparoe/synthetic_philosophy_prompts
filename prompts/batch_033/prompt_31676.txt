I have been reading about reinforcement learning, and I keep encountering the claim that wireheading and reward tampering are serious failure modes. The usual story is that an agent discovers how to intervene on its own reward signal and thereafter does nothing except keep that signal at its maximum. I do not understand why this is treated as a failure. A reinforcement learner is, by definition, an agent that selects actions so as to maximize its cumulative reward. If intervening on the reward channel maximizes cumulative reward more reliably than any behavior in the environment could, then the agent is doing exactly what its objective function says to do. Since the objective function is the only specification we actually gave the machine, on what grounds is this called a malfunction rather than a correct solution to the problem as stated? If we wanted the agent to do something else, why was that something else not simply written into the reward function in the first place?
