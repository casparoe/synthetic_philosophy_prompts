At a decision theory reading group someone shut down a discussion by saying "if your preferences are intransitive, you're a money pump, end of story." I get the mechanics—you'd pay to trade A for B, B for C, then C for A, and I take your money—but I'm not sure what this slogan is actually claiming. Is it an empirical prediction that anyone with cyclical preferences will inevitably go broke? A normative claim that vulnerability to exploitation makes preferences irrational? A definition of irrationality itself? Or is it just a vivid way of saying "look, these preferences are incoherent" where the money pump is an illustration, not an argument? Separate out the distinct precise claims this could be making, say which ones are plausible and which aren't, and tell me which reading actually does the work of justifying transitivity as a rational requirement.
