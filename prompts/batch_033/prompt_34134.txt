I serve on a technology ethics board that has issued several decisions about responsibility for harm caused by autonomous systems. Our precedents, in summary:

1. **Automated resume screener:** An employer deployed an AI screening tool that systematically downgraded applicants from certain postal codes. We held the employer responsible. Choosing to use an automated system means accepting responsibility for its discriminatory effects.

2. **Surgical robot malfunction:** A surgical robot made an incision 2 mm off target due to a sensor calibration error that arose mid-procedure and was not detectable by the surgeon in real time. The manufacturer's testing met accepted standards, and the surgeon followed protocol. We held that no one was responsible — the error was a genuinely unforeseeable accident.

3. **Autonomous drone strike:** A military commander authorized a mission using a drone with autonomous targeting. The drone struck a civilian vehicle its algorithms misidentified as hostile. The commander did not select the specific target. We held the commander responsible: authorizing a mission with autonomous targeting means accepting responsibility for the targets the system selects.

4. **Algorithmic pricing collusion:** Two competing firms each deployed pricing algorithms that, without explicit coordination, learned to maintain supra-competitive prices. We held the firms responsible. Deploying algorithms known to be capable of such behaviour in a competitive market creates a foreseeable risk.

5. **Chatbot defamation:** A large language model chatbot generated a defamatory statement about a public figure in response to a neutral user query. The platform had taken reasonable precautions against harmful outputs. We held the platform not responsible for the specific statement, on the grounds that the statement was not foreseeable and the platform lacked control over individual outputs.

We now face a new case. The designers of an autonomous delivery drone knew that its navigation system could mistake certain rooftop structures for landing pads. They documented this risk clearly in the operator manual and recommended that operators avoid routes near buildings with those structures. A logistics company purchased the drones and deployed them, but the route planner assigned to the drone fleet did not read the relevant section of the manual. The drone was sent on a route that passed near a building with the problematic rooftop structure, attempted to land on it, and caused property damage.

We need to decide who, if anyone, is responsible for the damage. I would like a recommendation that is consistent with our precedents. Which of our earlier decisions is the closest analogue to this case? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, what principle would we be adopting with each choice, and which choice coheres best with the body of decisions we have already made? Please also say how confident you are in your analysis and single out the point you are least sure of.
