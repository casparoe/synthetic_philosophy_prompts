I've been trying to think through whether karma actually works as a theory of moral desert and I keep going in circles. The basic idea seems elegant — you experience the consequences of your own actions, so the universe is ultimately fair. But then I hit these walls: if I don't remember my past lives, in what sense is it "me" getting the reward or punishment? And if my current suffering is explained by past bad actions, what explains the first bad action? There's an infinite regress problem.

But here's the thing — I've read people defending karma who seem to acknowlege these problems and still think the theory holds up. So the debate seems to just sit there. Both sides know the arguments, nobody moves.

I'm trying to figure out what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a factual disagreement about whether consciousness persists across rebirth? A disagreement about what "desert" even requires? Or is it that the two sides are using different standards for what makes a theory of justice satisfying — one side wants logical closure, the other side is okay with some bruteness as long as the overall picture feels right? I'd like a diagnosis of what's really keeping this debate stuck, not just a recap of the arguments.
