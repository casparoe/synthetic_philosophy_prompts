I keep going back and forth on what "socially constructed" is supposed to mean, because my gut says different things about different categories and I can't find the line that sorts them. Here's where I've landed, mostly:

- Money: constructed. A dollar bill is money because we treat it as money; if everyone stopped, it'd just be paper.
- Race in the US (black/white etc.): constructed, but real in its consequences. I don't think there's a biological joint there, but the categories structure housing, policing, health outcomes. So "constructed" can't mean "doesn't matter."
- Sex (male/female): not constructed. Gametes and chromosomes are doing real work. Though the binary part — how we handle intersex people, where we draw the line — feels more like us than nature, and I don't know how much that should move my verdict.
- Gender: constructed, obviously.
- Species: not constructed. There are real reproductive lineages out there. Although taxonomists do lump and split, and the boundary between species is often a judgment call, which makes me a bit uneasy about how confident I am here.
- Gold: not constructed as a natural kind — it's atomic number 79. But gold's role as a store of value is constructed.
- Planets: this one bugs me. The objects are out there, but whether Pluto counts is literally a vote. I want to say the category "planet" is constructed by stipulation.
- ADHD: constructed-ish. There's definately something real about attentional differences, but the diagnostic threshold and the category boundaries feel partly social.
- Weeds: constructed. A weed is just a plant where we don't want it.
- Disability: constructed in the sense that the problem is stairs and attitudes rather than the impairment, but the underlying impairment is real.

My first pass at a principle: a kind is socially constructed iff its unity or boundaries are fixed by human practices and attitudes rather than by mind-independent properties of the things themselves. If there's a mind-independent core but we draw the exact line, that's merely conventional demarcation, not construction.

But that principle seems to make "planet" merely conventional (real objects, line drawn by vote), which conflicts with my gut call that planet is constructed; and it makes "sex" merely conventional too (real core, binary line drawn by us), which maybe is fine but then I'm not sure sex and species come apart the way I want. It also says nothing about why race is real in its effects while money isn't real in the same way, even though both come out constructed.

So: test this principle against my verdicts, tell me where it breaks, and give me a repaired version — or tell me which of my verdicts I have to give up. If I need two different senses of "constructed" to save all of them, say so. And please say what the repaired principle says about a case I didn't list, like the difference between a psychiatric diagnosis and a purely stipulative category (say, "jumbo shrimp" as a menu category), because I want to know how far the label stretches.
