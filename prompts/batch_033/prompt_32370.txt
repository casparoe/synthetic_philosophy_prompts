I'm presenting a paper at a workshop next month where I argue that prioritarianism doesn't actually escape the leveling-down objection — or at least, not cleanly. The short version: the standard prioritarian move is to say we don't care about equality per se, we just give extra weight to benefits for the worse off. But when you ask why we give that extra weight, the most natural answers either collapse back into egalitarianism (and inherit the leveling-down problem) or rest on claims about diminishing marginal utility that don't do the normative work prioritarians need them to do. If Im right, prioritarianism is either egalitarianism in disguise or it lacks the resources to explain why the worse off matter more.

The audience is about twenty moral and political philosophers at a workshop on distributive justice. The other papers are on luck egalitarianism and sufficientarianism — I'm the only prioritarianism-focused paper. There are two people in the room who are known prioritarians (one wrote a book defending the view), a couple of committed telic egalitarians who think the leveling-down objection isnt actually fatal to their view anyway, and a bunch of people who work on adjacent topics and are sympathetic to welfarist approaches broadly. The telic egalitarians will be friendly to my conclusion but probably not to my argument, since I'm not defending egalitarianism — I'm just saying prioritarianism doesn't have the advantage it claims.
