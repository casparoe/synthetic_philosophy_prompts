In my science-and-religion seminar we've been on Galileo for two weeks, and whenever the Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina comes up someone quotes the line "the Bible tells us how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go" and acts like that just ends the discussion. Last seminar it was used to wave off the question of whether Galileo's position is actually compatible with taking scripture seriously, or whether he's just carving out a separate sphere where the Church has no say. I'm pretty sure that one sentence is doing way more work than people admit. Could you pull apart the different precise claims it might be making—about what scripture is for, about whether science and faith can conflict, about who gets to interpret the Bible—and say for each one whether it's a platitude, a defensible but substantive thesis, or something much stronger than Galileo's actual position supports? Then tell me which reading is doing the real work when someone uses it to end the argument. I want to bring a list to office hours so I don't just sit there annoyed.
