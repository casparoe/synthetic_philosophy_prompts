My friend has pretty strong views about separating the art from the artist, but she's not consistent in the way you'd expect — she doesn't have a blanket rule either way. Here are her verdicts on some cases we've argued about:

- A landscape painter who abused his wife: she says the paintings are beautiful and knowing about the abuse doesn't change how she sees them. "They're landscapes, they don't have anything to do with his marriage."

- An abstract expressionist who was a vocal racist: same thing — the canvases are just color and form, the racism isn't in them, she can appreciate the work fully.

- A novelist who wrote celebrated books about moral integrity and justice, but it came out that he'd plagiarized sections of his early novels and lied about it for decades: she says this one actually bothers her. The books are about integrity and he was a fraud — "the moral authority of the work feels hollow now," even though she admits the prose is still good.

- A singer-songwriter whose whole thing is raw emotional honesty, confessional love songs, and it turns out he was serially unfaithful and manipulative in his relationships, basically living the opposite of what he sang: she says she can't listen anymore, the persona is ruined, the songs feel like a con.

- A film director who made critically adored movies but was notorious for verbally abusing actors and crew on set, just a complete tyrant: she says the films are still great, what happened on set doesn't show up on screen, and anyway film is collaborative — hundreds of other people made those films too.

I'm trying to figure out what rule she's actually running. I can see at least a couple different principles that would fit all five verdicts. Then there's a new case I want to predict her on: a nature poet who wrote these gorgeous, precise, widely loved poems about birds and forests and rivers — not preachy environmental stuff, just really close observation of the natural world — and it turns out he owned a factory that was secretly dumping toxic waste into the local river for years, killing a lot of the wildlife he wrote about. What would she say about the poems? And if her existing verdicts don't settle it, what further case would force the issue?
