I am preparing a statement of my epistemological commitments for a workshop on formal epistemology and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My views are as follows:

(1) Bayesianism is the correct framework for rational belief: credences are probabilities, and learning proceeds by some form of conditionalization.

(2) Jeffrey conditionalization is the right generalization of conditionalization for cases of uncertain evidence. When an experience shifts your credences over a partition {E_i} without making any element certain, you update by holding the conditional probabilities P(H | E_i) fixed and setting your new credence in H to the average of those conditional probabilities weighted by the new credences P'(E_i).

(3) The inputs to a Jeffrey update — the new credences P'(E_i) — are not themselves subject to rational norms. They arise from perception or other extra-rational sources, and rationality's role is exhausted by ensuring that the rest of the credence function adjusts coherently.

(4) The Uniqueness Thesis is correct: given a body of total evidence, exactly one credence function is rationally permissible. Permissivism is false.

(5) Commutativity is a non-negotiable constraint on any adequate update rule: the order in which evidence arrives should make no difference to an agent's final credences.

(6) The problem of old evidence — where a proposition already known with certainty (P(E) = 1) comes to confirm a hypothesis H through a new theoretical or explanatory insight — is a genuine difficulty for Bayesianism, not a pseudoproblem that can be dissolved by clarifying the nature of confirmation.

Please go through these six commitments and identify which pairs are in tension. For each tension, say whether it is a genuine incompatibility or merely apparent, and explain why. Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, identify which commitment would be the least costly to revise and what the revision would sacrifice.
