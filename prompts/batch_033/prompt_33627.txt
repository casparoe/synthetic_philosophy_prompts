I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've noticed something in my own motivations that I don't have a name for, and I'd like to know whether there is an established one. I maintain a small open-source library in my spare time, and the work is often tedious. I've realized that what makes it feel worth doing has almost nothing to do with anything I will ever experience. If I learned with certainty that human extinction would occur shortly after my own death—so that I would live out my natural lifespan, and so would everyone now alive, but there would be no future people—the project would immediately come to seem pointless to me, and I suspect I would stop working on it that same day. Yet nothing about my own life would be different: no one would die earlier, I would not be around to witness the end, and I would suffer no loss I could feel. Whatever is bad about that situation seems to be located entirely in the fact that there will be no human future.

But the effect is not uniform. The pleasure I take in cooking with my partner, or in understanding a difficult piece of code for its own sake, does not seem to depend on that future in the same way. So I am trying to understand: is there an established name for this phenomenon—the way some present activities and values depend for their point on a human future I will never experience—and is there a principled line between the values that depend on it and the values that do not? I am less interested in a label than in the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges are. If the nearest established concept does not quite match what I have described, I would like to know what it is and how my description differs from it.
