I'm editing a collection of philosophical short fiction for a small press, and I'm looking for a story that dramatizes the concept of acausal trade. For context: in decision theory, acausal trade refers to a situation where two agents cannot causally interact — they are outside each other's light cones, or in separate branches of a branching multiverse, or otherwise separated by a barrier that no signal can cross — yet each can still benefit the other by taking an action that is costly to herself but beneficial to the other, on the reasoning that the other, being sufficiently similar in her decision procedure, will take the complementary action. The trade works not through any causal channel but through the logical correlation between the two agents' reasoning processes: each thinks, in effect, "if I do this for her, then she — being the kind of reasoner I am — will have done that for me." The puzzle is whether this is genuinely rational, or whether it is a kind of magical thinking dressed up in decision-theoretic language.

I'd like you to write a short story of 1,000 to 2,500 words that makes this idea vivid and emotionally resonant. Do not write an expository essay or a philosophical dialogue that merely explains the concept. Instead, give me characters and a situation that embody the dilemma. Some possibilities: two prisoners in separate, sealed cells who have never met and never will, but who each have an opportunity to leave a message that will help the other at a cost to themselves. Or two civilizations that detect each other's existence at opposite ends of a universe expanding too fast for any signal to cross, and each must decide whether to devote resources to a project that only benefits the other. Or two copies of the same person, uploaded and then separated into non-interacting simulations, each facing a parallel choice. The story should capture the vertigo of acting on a reason that seems to have no causal force — the feeling of reaching across a void that you know cannot be crossed, and betting that someone on the other side is reaching back.

Please write the story in continuous prose, as you would for a literary magazine. No headings, no bullet points, no bold or italic text, and no tables. Just the narrative.
