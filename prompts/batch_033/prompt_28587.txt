I have just become chair of a three-member tenure committee. Our rules treat the two stated conditions as jointly necessary and sufficient: a candidate is granted tenure exactly when the committee finds both adequate research and adequate teaching. In every case we take separate votes on research, teaching, and the final tenure decision, and each member's tenure vote is constrained by that rule. I have the committee's last three decisions, recorded member by member in the order research, teaching, tenure:

Candidate A: Alvarez yes–yes–yes; Bhandari yes–yes–yes; Chen no–no–no. Recorded committee findings: research yes, teaching yes, tenure yes.
Candidate B: Alvarez yes–no–no; Bhandari yes–no–no; Chen yes–yes–yes. Recorded committee findings: research yes, teaching no, tenure no.
Candidate C: Alvarez yes–yes–yes; Bhandari no–yes–no; Chen no–yes–no. Recorded committee findings: research no, teaching yes, tenure no.

Next week we decide Candidate D, and the members have already indicated their votes: Alvarez yes–yes–yes; Bhandari yes–no–no; Chen no–yes–no.

I want the committee to be consistent with its own record. What should we decide about Candidate D, which earlier case is the closest analog, and what principle would we be adopting with each possible decision? Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Your five items should cover: the candidate rules that fit our precedents, what each rule requires for Candidate D, the closest earlier case, your recommended decision and the principle it commits us to, and the principle the opposite decision would commit us to together with a further case that would best distinguish the competing rules.
