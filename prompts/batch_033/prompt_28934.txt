I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on metaontology, and we are about to spend two weeks on the thought that some apparently substantive ontological disputes — over holes, statues and lumps of clay, or composite objects — may be merely verbal. In previous years I lectured on quantifier variance and translation arguments, and the students could restate the formal point but never really felt its force; the discussion stayed flat and slightly incredulous. This year I want to stage the idea instead of presenting it, so that the class runs into the problem themselves before anyone names it.

The class has 14 students and we have 90 minutes. Please design a structured role-play or constrained deliberation in which two groups are given rival but equally coherent "counting languages" or definitional policies and are then forced to settle a shared practical question — an inventory, an insurance claim, a museum label, a repair budget, or something similar of your choosing. The rules, incentives, or roles should be arranged so that the two sides talk past each other for a while, the deadlock feels substantive, and the resolution appears only when an explicit translation between their languages is produced. I want the students to leave seeing both the appeal and the limits of the "merely verbal" diagnosis: that a dispute can be real as a choice of language even when there is no factual disagreement, and that not every stalled debate is thereby deflated. Include the setup and materials, the roles and rules, the exact constraints or stakes that create the pressure (no real money needed), a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the exercise onto quantifier variance and the verbal-dispute literature without having named the view up front. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be candid about which feature of the real philosophical debate the exercise cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself in the closing discussion.
