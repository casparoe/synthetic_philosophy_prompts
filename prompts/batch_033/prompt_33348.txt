A friend of mine works at a large AI lab, and we spent most of last evening arguing about something I found oddly unsettling. The same organisations that describe their models as "aligned" through reinforcement learning from human feedback also run a battery of safety practices that seem to assume the very opposite: they train separate monitor models to watch for suspicious outputs, keep high-capability systems behind APIs with filters and usage restrictions rather than releasing the weights, run dangerous-capability evaluations before deciding whether a model may be deployed, and fund research on detecting deception, sandbagging, and "scheming" in models that have shown no sign of it. My friend thinks these practices are just sensible engineering prudence and that I am over-reading them. I think a practice that systematically prepares for the possibility that a system is competent-but-misaligned must presuppose some substantive view about what the system is.

What philosophical commitments, if any, does this family of control-oriented safety practices presuppose? In particular: does it presuppose that a model can be highly capable while pursuing goals quite different from those its training was intended to produce, that good behaviour during training is weak evidence about underlying goals, and that a model's overt outputs are not a transparent window onto whatever, if anything, it is "trying" to do? Or can all of this be justified on a much thinner, behaviouristic story — that we merely have an opaque system and are managing uncertainty, without committing ourselves to any claim about hidden goals, agency, or an inner life? Reconstruct the implicit theory of mind or agency as rigorously as you can, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and tell me whether it sits coherently alongside the same labs' public talk of having "aligned" their models. If we replaced the presupposed picture with a thoroughgoing behaviourism — according to which a model that reliably behaves helpfully just is aligned — which of these practices would become rationally unmotivated, and which would survive?
