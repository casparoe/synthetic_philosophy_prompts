You are a professional philosopher specializing in modal metaphysics, with particular expertise in the debate between counterpart theory and transworld identity theories of de re modality. Please think carefully and work through the following task step by step.

Construct a thought experiment in which counterpart theory and a standard transworld identity theory (of the kind developed by Kripke and Plantinga) agree on the truth-value of a de re modal claim about an ordinary object, but disagree on what proposition that claim expresses. The scenario must satisfy the following constraints:

1. The object must be an ordinary material thing — a person, an artifact, a natural object — not an abstractum or a merely possible individual.
2. The modal claim must be a simple "could have been otherwise" claim (not iterated modality, not a counterfactual conditional), and it must be one that ordinary speakers would unreflectively assent to.
3. The scenario must be described in no more than two paragraphs.
4. You may not stipulate the verdicts; you must derive them from the structure of each theory.

After presenting the scenario, provide the following:

(a) State the modal claim precisely and explain why each theory judges it true. Show your reasoning for each theory, being explicit about which possible worlds and which individuals (or counterparts) are involved.

(b) Explain how the two theories disagree about what proposition is expressed, even though they agree on the truth-value. Specify the proposition according to each theory — what each theory says the claim is about.

(c) Identify the structural feature of the theories that does the work — the single point of theoretical divergence that generates the disagreement about content while permitting agreement about truth-value. Explain what would happen if that feature were removed: would the two theories collapse into each other, would the agreement on truth-value break, or both?

(d) Anticipate one objection from a defender of transworld identity: how would they argue that the counterpart theorist's account of the proposition expressed is not genuinely about the original object, and that the counterpart theorist has therefore changed the subject? Then explain how the counterpart theorist would reply.

Throughout, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists in modal metaphysics. Where a step in your reasoning depends on a contested reading of one of the theories — for example, a particular way of spelling out what counterpart theory takes the semantic content of a de re modal claim to be — flag it explicitly and note the alternative reading.
