I'm a PhD student in philosophy presenting a paper at my department's colloquium next month. The paper defends a version of the evolutionary debunking argument against moral realism. My core claim is that the best evolutionary explanations of our moral intuitions do not reference mind-independent moral truths, and that this creates an epistemic defeater for moral realism: if moral realism were true, it would be a striking coincidence that our evolved intuitions track the moral facts, and third-factor explanations—such as the appeal to a pre-established harmony or to the claim that truth-conducive cognitive capacities were fitness-enhancing—either beg the question or fail to establish the right kind of explanatory connection.

The audience will be my department's faculty and graduate students. The department is roughly 40% moral realists (including two faculty who have published defenses of non-naturalist realism and are sympathetic to third-factor replies), 30% constructivists and expressivists who are skeptical of realism but also skeptical that evolutionary arguments carry much dialectical weight, 20% epistemologists and philosophers of science with no settled metaethical views but strong opinions about what counts as an epistemic defeater, and 10% philosophers of biology who know the evolutionary literature better than I do.

Please prepare a Q&A prep sheet for me: the eight or so objections I am most likely to get from this audience, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased roughly as a member of this audience would put it, with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
