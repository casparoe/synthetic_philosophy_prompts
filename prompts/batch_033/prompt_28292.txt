Two friends of mine, Alex and Sam, got into an hours-long argument about stake sizing and neither would budge, and I've been chewing on it since. The setup they agreed on: a bet that pays even money, a genuine 60% chance of winning each round, independant rounds, and you can play as many rounds as you like. Alex's opening move was that each dollar staked has an expected profit of twenty cents, so if you're not staking as much as possible you're leaving money on the table. Sam said that's how you go broke: the Kelly criterion says bet 20% of your bankroll per round, because what compounds is the geometric mean, not the arithmetic mean, and betting everything means a 40% chance of ruin every single round. Alex came back that Kelly is a theorem about maximizing expected log wealth over the long run, not a rule for how much to bet; for any finite number of rounds — or a single round — a risk-neutral person should maximize expected wealth, and Sam hasn't shown anyone is risk-averse. Sam said "risk-neutral" is doing all the work: over many rounds, the strategy that maximizes expected wealth goes bust almost surely, and its expected value is being driven by a tiny probability of astronomically large wealth. Alex said that's just the long-run point again, and it's Sam who keeps smuggling in the long run; for one favorable bet, betting small isn't more rational than betting big, it just has a different risk profile. Sam replied that you never actually face exactly one bet — rationality is about policies over a life — and a policy of betting everything on favorable gambles is ruinous. Alex said a policy can be re-evaluated after each round, so "policy" doesn't rescue the log rule either; if I only care about expected final wealth at some horizon, dynamic consistency tells me to bet everything every round. Sam then said that in real life your 60% is an estimate, and betting full Kelly on an estimated edge is already overbetting; Alex said that's moving the goalposts, since they'd stipulated the 60% was known. They asked me to settle it and I honestly couldn't.

Can you adjudicate the exchange move by move? Rate each of the claims from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say who is right about which specific point, and say what, if anything, both of them are missing. I'd rather you take a definite position on each move and on who is more right overall than give me a survey of possible views.
