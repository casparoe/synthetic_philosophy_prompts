I teach an upper-level undergraduate metaphysics seminar with 16–20 students. We have one 75-minute session on the metaphysics of time, specifically the presentism–eternalism debate. I want an in-class activity — not a lecture — that makes students feel the explanatory pressure each view faces, rather than just hearing about it. The activity should force them to confront what it actually costs to say that only the present moment and its contents exist (presentism) versus saying that past, present, and future entities all exist equally (eternalism). In particular, I want them to experience the problem of cross-temporal relations: if only the present exists, what makes it true that Caesar crossed the Rubicon? And if the future already exists, what room is left for genuine contingency or for the sense that the present is uniquely real?

Design a structured role-play or deliberation game for this session. The activity should have clear rules, assigned roles, and a concrete task that forces participants to make decisions using only the ontological resources their role permits. The tension between presentism and eternalism should emerge from the gameplay itself — they should feel the pull of each side and the strain of its commitments before anyone names the positions. No lecture up front.
