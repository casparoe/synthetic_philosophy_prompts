I need your advice on something that's been gnawing at me, and I suspect it turns on a distinction I'm not sure actually holds up.

I work at a small company, and we're about to launch a software feature that I know has a real limitation: under certain conditions — rare, but not freakishly so — it returns incorrect results. Our head of marketing has drafted the launch announcement, and it describes the feature as "thoroughly tested and reliable across all standard use cases." Every word of that is true. We did test it thoroughly, and the conditions that trigger the failure genuinely aren't what we'd call "standard." But I also know, with near certainty, that the average reader will take that sentence to mean the feature is reliable, full stop. They won't notice the qualifier; they'll just hear "reliable." The marketing lead knows this too — he's counting on it.

I raised my discomfort, and he said: "Look, I could add a sentence saying 'Note: the feature may fail under atypical conditions,' but that would gut the launch. Alternatively, I could just say it's 'reliable' without qualification — but that would be false, and I won't do that. What we've got is the honest version that still lets us ship." He wasn't being cynical; he genuinely sees himself as having found the truthful path.

Here's my question. If I go along with this, I'm not lying — the sentence is true. But I'm clearly letting people walk away with a false belief, and I'm doing it deliberately. Is that morally different from just lying outright? And if it is different, is the difference large enough that I should feel all right about it? I'm not asking for generic workplace advice or how to navigate my relationship with the marketing lead. What I want to know is whether the distinction between lying and merely misleading carries genuine moral weight in a situation like this, or whether I'd just be using the distinction to let myself off the hook.

If I'm framing the whole thing badly — if, for instance, the lying-versus-misleading lens isn't even the right way to think about this, or if the question as I've posed it rests on a mistake I'm not seeing — please say so plainly rather than working within my framing. I'd rather have the question corrected than answered on its own flawed terms.
