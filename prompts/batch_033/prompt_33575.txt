I have been teaching the split-brain material in a philosophy of mind course, and I keep finding myself defending a position I would like to have pressure-tested rather than merely rehearsed. The view is this: the split-brain cases are best read as showing that consciousness is not the single, unified field introspection presents, but a set of dissociable streams that are ordinarily bound together by ongoing interhemispheric traffic and can come apart when that traffic is cut. On this reading, the canonical findings — the left hand selecting an object while the speaking hemisphere confabulates an explanation for the choice, the failure to compare stimuli across the two hemifields, episodes of intermanual conflict in which the hands work at cross purposes — are not quirks of experimental design and are not the behavior of a disconnected but unconscious module. They are evidence of two (or more) centers of experience and agency sharing one body, with the speaking hemisphere's confabulation being exactly what one stream of consciousness does when it is denied access to the causes of the body's behavior.

I want to know what would count against this view. Which empirical findings, alternative interpretations of the data, or more general considerations about the nature of consciousness should actually move me away from it? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and mark which of them a defender of the two-streams reading would themselves concede as counting against the view, as opposed to objections they would dismiss as question-begging or as artifacts of the testing situation. In particular: what would the switch model — on which the patient is a single subject who alternates between hemispheres — or the view that only the speaking, typically left hemisphere is genuinely conscious have to get right for my reading to fail? What does my view predict that those rivals do not, and are any of those predictions testable in partial callosotomy patients, hemispherectomy patients, or ordinary subjects under conditions that disrupt interhemispheric coordination? Finally, I want an honest assessment of whether the position has been insulated by its willingness to treat the introspective datum of unity as negotiable — whether it now risks nothing empirically — and, if so, whether that insulation is a defect for a view of this kind.
