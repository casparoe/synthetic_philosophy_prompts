A friend of mine, Dana, and her roommate, Leo, have been going back and forth in our group chat about what it takes for a belief to actually be justified, and I got dragged in as referee even though I honestly dont know this stuff. It started when Dana said every belief needs a reason, but the reason is another belief, so the chain of reasons either goes on forever, loops in a circle, or stops at something that's just accepted. She said only the last option makes sense: there have to be "basic" beliefs that don't get their justification from other beliefs. (She called this foundationalism.) Leo immediately said that's just giving a belief justification for free — if nothing supports it, calling it "basic" is either arbitrary or a circle of size one.

Dana came back that basic beliefs aren't arbitrary because some are about your own immediate experience, like how things seem to you right now; you can't be wrong about how things seem to you, so those don't need support, they're their own evidence. Leo said those "seems" beliefs are too thin to build anything on — you can't get from "I seem to see a table" to justified beliefs about tables, other people, or science — and besides, even "I seem to see red" uses concepts you could be misapplying. He said his view (coherentism, he called it) was better: beliefs justify each other in a web, and justification comes from how the whole thing hangs together, not from a few special floor-stones. Dana replied that a web of mutually supporting beliefs is just circular reasoning with more steps: a perfectly coherent story could be totally false, so coherence by itself can't connect you to reality. Leo said coherence isn't just consistency; it includes how well the web explains and predicts your experience, and that's what anchors it, at many points rather than one privileged layer.

Dana then said she'd read about a third option, infinitism, where the chain of reasons just goes on forever, but that can't work because a finite person can't hold an infinite chain in their head, so no belief would ever actually get justified. Leo agreed infinitism fails, but said it fails for the same reason her view fails: if every reason needs a reason, you never finish, and if some beliefs don't need reasons, then "every belief needs a reason" was never really the rule. He said only his web view survives, because it drops the idea that justification is a chain at all. Dana's last message was that dropping the chain doesn't help: a web still needs some connection to the world or it's just a floating story, and "anchored by experience" just smuggles her basic beliefs back in under a different name.

That's where it stands. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, what either of them is missing, and who you think is more right overall?
