I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I've been reading about the Hart–Fuller debate in my spare time and I'm struck by how it seems to go in circles. As I understand it, H. L. A. Hart argued that law and morality are conceptually separable — a rule can be a valid law even if it is morally abhorrent — while Lon Fuller argued that law has an inner morality, and that a system of rules that fails certain procedural principles (generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, and the like) is not properly a legal system at all. The debate has generated a vast literature, but I often find it hard to tell whether the two sides are disagreeing about facts, about the meaning of words, or about something deeper.

I would like to see what an adversarial collaboration between a committed Hart-style legal positivist and a committed Fuller-style natural law theorist would produce. Please draft the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith effort to find common ground. The document should include the following:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can affirm — about the nature of legal systems, about the relationship between law and morality, about what actually happens in legal practice, and about what each side is *not* claiming.

2. A disagreements table, structured as follows: for each remaining point of disagreement, state the issue, give the positivist's formulation of their own position (in wording they would accept), give the natural law theorist's formulation of their own position (in wording they would accept), and identify the crux — the sub-question that, if settled, would resolve that disagreement.

3. For each crux, describe a concrete case or piece of evidence that both sides would agree bears on it, and record what each side would predict about that case.

I am particularly interested in whether any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal (for example, two different uses of the word 'law') and whether the two sides can agree on what would actually count as evidence against their own view.
