The paradox of tragedy is that we seek out and seem to enjoy artworks that make us sad, even though we normally avoid sadness. Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated philosophers of art (not necessarily historical figures) who disagree about how to resolve it. The first defends a conversion view: the formal and representational features of the artwork transform painful emotions into pleasurable ones, so the sadness we savor is no longer, strictly speaking, distressing. The second defends a constitutive view: the negative emotions remain genuinely painful, and the value of the experience lies in the understanding, emotional clarity, or exercise of affective capacities that the painful parts make possible; the whole is worth having, but not because the pain has been converted into pleasure. Have each character respond to the other's actual arguments, press the strongest version of the opposing case rather than a strawman, and concede where the other has scored a point. By the end, each should identify the consideration or example that would most move them toward the other view.
