I'm working on an short essay on eliminative materialism and I'd like comments on the argument before I sink more time into it. The rough idea: most of the debate treats eliminativism about beliefs and desires as one thesis about all of folk psychology at once, and then fights about whether the whole thing stands or falls. I think it's the wrong grain. Folk psychology does a bunch of different jobs—predicting what people will do, explaining why they did it, letting us self-ascribe reasons, and underpinning practices like praise, blame, and promising. Eliminativist arguments (from the track record of folk theories, from neuroscience's failure to find anything belief-shaped, etc.) mostly target the predictive-explanatory job. But it's much less clear they touch the normative/attributive job: holding someone responsible might not require 'belief' and 'desire' to refer to real internal states, any more than talk of legal obligations requires obligations to be physical things. So the upshot is a piecemeal eliminativism: we can be eliminativists about beliefs and desires as explanatory posits while keeping the practices that seem hardest to give up, because those practices were never really in the business of positing inner causes. My worry is that this either collapses into plain instrumentalism about the mental (belief-talk as useful fiction), or that it concedes the main point to the eliminativist by admitting that in the only domain where 'belief' was meant to refer, it fails. Could you give me comments on whether the distinction between the explanatory role and the attributive/normative role is actually load-bearing, whether the essays conclusion would be interesting rather than just a relabeling of existing positions, and any place where the argument sounds wrong or needs an example?
