I've been using AI coding tools pretty heavily for the last year and I've noticed something unsettling. I find myself accepting completions and explanations without really chewing on them, and when I go back to write something from scratch I'm slower than I used to be and less confident. I've basically come to the view that epistemic dependence on AI — outsourcing your thinking to a model, even when it's correct — erodes genuine understanding, and that this is a real loss even if your throughput goes up. Like, the understanding was the point, or at least a big part of it.

I want to know what would count against this view. Not just people saying "but productivity!" — I mean what evidence or arguments should actually move me off this position. What would the world have to look like for this erosion story to be wrong, or overblown, or missing something important? Are there ways the view could be qualified into unfalsifiability (e.g. "any visible improvement is shallow, the real loss is invisible") and if so is that a problem for a view of this kind? I'd want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd especially want to know which ones a defender of the erosion view would themselves concede count against them.
