I've been asking a friend about a set of "what if" cases concerning where a person comes from, and she answers without hesitating. She has no background in philosophy; she just says she has thought about this a lot since she first learned how IVF works. I'd like help working out whether her answers really hang together, and what she would say about a case I haven't put to her yet.

Here are the cases and her answers so far:

1. If you had been switched at birth and raised by different parents, would you still have been you? — Yes. You would be the same person, just with a different upbringing.
2. If you had grown from the same embryo but been carried by a surrogate, would you still have been you? — Yes. The womb doesn't matter.
3. If your parents had used a donor embryo made from a stranger's egg and a stranger's sperm, so that you had no biological connection to either of them, would that child have been you? — No. That would have been a different person.
4. If the embryo you came from had been frozen for ten years and then implanted, would you still have been you? — Yes. The delay doesn't matter.
5. If a different man had been your biological father, would that child have been you? — No.
6. If a different woman had been your biological mother, would that child have been you? — No.

The new case is this. Suppose your parents had done IVF and ended up with three embryos at the same time. You came from one of them. Could you have existed if you had come from a different one of those embryos instead — same two parents, but a different egg and a different sperm?

I can see at least two rules that fit everything she has said so far. One says that the exact egg and sperm are what fix you, so any change there means a different person. Another says that only the biological parents matter, so any egg and sperm from those same two people would still have been you. I can also imagine a middle rule that treats the mother's egg as the thing that fixes you while allowing any sperm from the same father.

I'm less interested in the bare yes or no than in the rule she seems to be running. Are there other rules that fit her verdicts just as well? Which rule best explains what she has said so far, and what should I predict she will say about the new case? Finally, what further case could I ask her about that would cleanly separate whichever rules are still in the running?
