Write a short story that dramatizes Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. The story should make the philosophical point felt rather than stated: show a character coming to see that their own actions, and those of others, flow from factors no one chose — genetics, upbringing, formative experiences, luck — and that this undermines the notion of ultimate moral responsibility in a way that ordinary practices of praise and blame cannot easily absorb. Do not turn the story into a lecture; let the idea emerge through situation, character, and consequence.

Begin with the story itself, with no preface or commentary. After the story, add a brief note identifying where the narrative takes liberties with Strawson's actual argument or leans on a contested reading of it, and indicate which consideration would most likely lead you to revise the story's implicit stance.
