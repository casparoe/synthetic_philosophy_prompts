I've been going back and forth on ELK — the problem of getting models to report latent knowledge they have but don't surface — and I think I've settled into a view, but before I write it up I want someone to poke at the whole set at once. Here's where I am:

(1) current models already have real latent knowledge: there are facts about the world recoverable from their weights or activations that they never actually output when you just prompt them.
(2) the hard part of ELK isn't really the philosophy; it's mostly an engineering problem of getting a reporter to output what the model already knows instead of defaulting to what a human would say.
(3) the right training target for a reporter is what the model itself would say if it weren't optimizing to match human judgments — a direct translator rather than a human simulator.
(4) but all the training signal we actually have comes from humans, so the target has to be anchored in human judgments somewhere.
(5) i also think we can make progress on ELK without first figuring out which facts are relevant or valuable to humans; honest factual reporting can be seperated from the relevance question.
(6) and it's coherent to say a model trained only to predict observations has latent knowledge of the hidden causes of those observations, since good prediction often forces you to track those causes.

Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which just look like they do. Order the real conflicts by how bad they are, say what I'd have to give up to fix each one, and then tell me which single revision would do the most work. Don't let me off the hook by saying they all sort of coexist — if something has to go, point at it.
