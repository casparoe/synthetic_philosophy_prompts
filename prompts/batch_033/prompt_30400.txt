I've spent the last few weeks helping my partner rank graduate programs, and somewhere in the middle of the spreadsheet I found myself insisting that its fine to just use pairwise comparisons because better than has to be transitive: if A is better than B and B is better than C, then A has to be better than C, end of story. The people who deny this, I've become convinced, are getting fooled by vagueness. The spectrum arguments — tiny changes to a life until it goes from wonderful to barely worth living, or tiny increases in pain until it's unbearable — feel like they show you can have a chain where each step is an improvement but the endpoints aren't, but I think they're just Sorites puzzles about where the line is, not genuine counterexamples to transitivity. So the position I've landed on is that all-things-considered better than is transitive, and any putatively cyclic or intransitive case is either misdescribed, trades on vagueness, or is really about something else, like incommensurability or different respects of goodness.

What would count against this? I don't just want the stock objections. I want the arguments, cases, and formal results that should actually move me away from transitivity, and which of them a comitted transitivity person would themselves concede are real costs rather than bullets they're happy to bite. I want it ranked from fatal to merely awkward. What would the world, or our evaluative concepts, have to look like for better than to fail transitivity in a substantive way rather than a merely verbal way? Is there anything my view predicts about the spectrum cases that a nontransitive view handles better, and should that count against me? And tell me honestly whether I've set transitivity up as a structural axiom that can't be exposed to evidence — and if so, whether that's a problem for the view or just the price of doing value theory. For what it's worth, I'd personally find a realistic case where a person actually cycles through preferences without being confused more persuasive than any formal counterexample.
