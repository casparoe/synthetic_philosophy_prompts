I've been trying to pin down something I keep noticing across creative fields, and I wonder whether there is an established concept for it. The pattern is this: a genuine innovation often turns out, on closer inspection, to be less like the invention of something from nothing and more like the result of taking an existing form and turning one of its parameters further than anyone had thought to turn it. A jazz musician keeps the harmonic skeleton of a standard but stretches the rate at which the chords change until the tune stops behaving like a song and starts behaving like a landscape; a translator keeps the literal meaning of a poem but drops the original meter, and the resulting decisions about line and breath produce a kind of verse that the source language never had; a programmer takes an existing language and changes a single evaluation rule, and the language that emerges makes a whole style of programming possible that no one had designed for. In each case, what seems to do the creative work is not the novelty of the ingredients but the judgment about which parameter of which inherited structure was worth turning, and then the willingness to treat the result not as a one-off variant but as a new base from which further variants can be made.

I am not looking for a vague slogan, such as remixing or standing on the shoulders of giants. I know there are adjacent ideas — stylistic influence, memetic evolution, Darwinian variation plus selection — but none of them quite captures what interests me, which is that the pivotal move is the choice of a single parameter on an existing structure, and that the result gets treated as a new structure rather than as a variant of the old one. I want to know whether there is a worked-out model of creativity that places this mechanism at the center, and if so, what it is called and how exactly it is supposed to work. In particular: how does the model distinguish a variation from a genuinely new theme? Can it explain the origin of the very first theme, or does it only account for novelty once a theme already exists? Where is the line between this kind of creative variation and mere imitation? If there is an established name, tell me whose model it is and how it handles these boundary questions; if there is not, propose a name and define the concept precisely, with clear edge cases. Please answer in American English.
