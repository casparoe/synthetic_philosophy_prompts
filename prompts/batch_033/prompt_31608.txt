I've been teaching the prioritarianism-versus-egalitarianism debate in my grad seminar for years, and every time we hit the leveling-down objection I come away thinking we're still missing something — or maybe several somethings. I'm at the point where I need to decide what to work on next myself, and I'd rather not spend three years on a question that turns out to be downstream of something else or just terminological.

So here's what I want: suppose you've got a philosopher who knows the literature cold — Parfit, Temkin, Broome, Otsuka, Hirose, the whole back-and-forth — and has maybe five years to spend on this cluster. Which open questions around prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection are most worth that time? I'm not asking for a survey of the debate. I want a ranked list with real defense: which questions are upstream of others (so settling them reshapes the rest of the landscape), which are tractable with the tools we actually have (rather than requiring a whole new theory of value), and which ones, if answered one way rather than another, would actually change what policies or institutional designs we endorse. 

A few things I'm particularly curious about. One is whether the whole dispute between prioritarians and egalitarians is partly verbal — whether there's a way of formulating the view that makes the leveling-down objection dissolve without residue, or whether that's wishful thinking. Another is how much work the "person-affecting" restriction is really doing here and whether that restriction itself can be motivated without begging the question against impersonal goods. And a third is whether the leveling-down objection actually has any bite against views that aren't pure telic egalitarianism — like, if you're a pluralist who cares about both equality and absolute wellbeing, does the objection just collapse into a weighting problem?

But those are just my hunches. I want to know what someone who's thought hard about this thinks the real pressure points are, and which ones are dead ends. And if your answer is that the most important thing is actually a question I haven't mentioned, all the better — just tell me why it's more fundamental than the ones I've got in my head.
