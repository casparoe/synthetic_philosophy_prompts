But it seems to me the problem is deeper than that. Even if you're a Bayesian, you still face the question of which reference class to use when you're forming a prior about a particular case (like, what's the probability this patient has the disease, given that they're in class A, class B, class C, etc.). And the problem shows up in legal reasoning about base rates, in medical diagnosis, in all kinds of places where nobody is doing metaphysics.

So I want the question to force them to confront whether the problem really is specific to frequentism, and what it would even mean to solve it. Could you write me: (1) the essay question, (2) a model answer of about 700 words, and (3) a marking scheme that separates A, B, and C answers? For the marking scheme, an A answer should say something specific about why the problem isn't just a frequentist issue, and should engage with the question of what a solution would require. A C answer is the one that just describes the problem and names Venn and Reichenbach and calls it a day.
