I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about comparing wellbeing across people, going by my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts:

If Alice and Bob both stub their toe, same force, same toe, I think they suffer about the same amount. That one's easy.

If Carol has high pain tolerance and Dave has low pain tolerance, and they both get the same injection, I think Dave suffers more — the physical stimulus is identical but the experience isn't.

Now here's where I get tangled. If Erin is going through a bad breakup and Frank is going through a bad breakup, and Erin's relationship was longer and deeper, I want to say Erin suffers more. But I also know people who seem to bounce back in a week from things that would wreck me for months. Am I supposed to say they suffer less, or that they suffer the same amount but cope better? Those feel like different claims and I'm not sure which one my gut is making.

If Grace has clinical depression and reports feeling "a little better today," while Helen without depression says the same thing after recovering from a cold, I think Helen's improvement is larger in absolute terms. Grace's baseline is lower, so the same self-report corresponds to a smaller actual shift. But I'm queasy about telling someone with depression that their felt improvement is "smaller" than someone else's.

If I have to prevent a mild headache for a thousand people or prevent a migraine for one person, I pick the thousand. But if it's a thousand mild headaches versus saving one person from an hour of torture, I pick the one. So there's some threshold where quality swamps quantity, but I can't locate it precisely.

And here's the one that really bothers me: I think the pleasure a sadist gets from inflicting pain shouldn't count at all in the comparison. But I can't say why without smuggling in a moral judgment that's supposed to be downstream of the utility comparison, not baked into it.

Is there a coherent principle of interpersonal utility comparison that captures most of these, and which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? Also, what does the principle say about comparing human and non-human animal wellbeing — say, a dog's distress at being left alone for a day versus a human's mild loneliness for a day?
