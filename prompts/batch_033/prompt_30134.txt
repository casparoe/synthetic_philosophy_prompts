Construct a thought experiment that isolates a way in which a present project's value can depend on humanity's having a future, even when no actual future person's welfare is at stake. The scenario should be this: an agent is already engaged in a long-term project; the agent learns that the current generation will be the last — no one alive now will die prematurely, but no further people will be born; and the project comes to seem pointless to the agent, although nothing about its effects on anyone who will ever exist has changed. Constraints: no one is harmed or killed in the scenario; the project must not be one whose stated aim is to benefit future generations or to save humanity; and the loss of value must not be explainable as grief, depression, or anxiety about the agent's own death. After the scenario, show why the agent's reaction is intelligible and why it cannot be reduced to concern for the welfare of future people, and explain what the case establishes about the afterlife conjecture. Finish by identifying which feature of the case does the work and how someone who holds that extinction is bad only because of the deaths it involves would resist the conclusion.
