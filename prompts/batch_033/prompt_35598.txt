I keep noticing a move in discussions of thermodynamics and I can't tell whether its a recognized thing or just a tic people have.

Background: I'm an electrical engineer who reads about the foundations of physics for fun, so my vocabulary is probably a bit off. Every time someone proposes a gadget that seems to extract useful work from a single temperature bath — a trapdoor that only lets fast molecules through, a ratchet that only turns one way, a partition you slide in and out of a box of gas — the reply always has the same shape. First, someone finds a mechanical flaw in the gadget. The proposer patches it. Then the discussion reaches a point where the objection stops being about springs and pawls and becomes something like: whatever the gadget does, it has to measure the molecules, or store a record of which side they're on, and that information-processing step has a thermodynamic cost, so the second law is safe. Everyone treats that as decisive, even when nobody has actually worked out the cost for this specific machine.

I'm not asking whether the second law is true, and I'm not asking what the little-being-who-sorts-molecules thought experiment is called. I'm asking about the shape of the reply: your machine is secretly an information-processor, and information has to pay its own way thermodynamically. Does this move have an established name? If it's a principle, what does it actually assert, and what does it presuppose about the relationship between information and physics — is information being treated as something physical in a strong sense, or is this just a bookkeeping convention that can always be made to come out right? How is the move different from simply re-asserting the second law and challenging the other person to find a loophole? And are there known cases where the move has been pushed too far, or where the measurement and the erasure costs need to be kept seperate and people run them together?

Don't just hand me a label. I want to know what the move is, what it isn't, and where its edges are — the nearest named ideas, how what I'm describing differs from each, and which one fits best (or whether none does and it needs a name of its own).

Please think it through like a philosopher of physics or of information would: start by laying out the candidate concepts, then narrow down. Write in American English. And at the end, tell me how confident you are in the main claims and single out the one point you're least sure about.
