Two people in my department have been arguing for months about whether extreme suffering can ever be outweighed by enough mild pleasure. One is a lexical threshold negative utilitarian: she thinks there's a threshold of suffering so bad that no amount of mild pleasure can compensate for it, and that preventing such suffering has strict priority over promoting pleasure. The other is a commensurabilist utilitarian: he thinks pleasure and pain are quantities on a single scale, so any finite amount of pain can in principal be outweighed by a sufficiently large amount of pleasure, even if the exchange rate is astronomically steep. They've agreed to write an adversarial-collaboration document instead of another email thread. Draft it for them.

It should include: (1) a numbered list of claims about suffering, pleasure, and trade-offs that both can sign; (2) a disagreements table or section stating their residual differences precisely, with each side's view phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, at least one case, argument, or empirical finding both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect two of the cruxes are whether "lexical" means a genuine discontinuity in value or just a steep but finite exchange rate, and whether the threshold can be specified without falling into a sorites problem as pain intensity ramps up continuously. Don't adjudicate; produce the document they could both co-sign. American English, please.
