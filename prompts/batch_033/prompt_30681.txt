They all know the money pump argument as the standard reason you can't have intransitive preferences: if you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, someone can keep cycling you through trades and drain your wallet, so rationality requires transitive preferences, and by extension consistent preferences over time. My talk is going to defend the opposite-ish claim: the money pump doesn't actually show that intransitive or time-inconsistent preferences are irrational. At best it shows that if you want to avoid being exploited you should adopt a consistent policy, which is a pragmatic point about not leaking money, not a normative one about what counts as rational. The pump only looks like a refutation because it quietly assumes the agent is one diachronic thing whose later losses count against its earlier choices — but that's the very norm of diachronic coherence the argument is supposed to be earning. I can sketch the argument but I'm honestly unsure which joints of it hold up against smart non-philosophers.
