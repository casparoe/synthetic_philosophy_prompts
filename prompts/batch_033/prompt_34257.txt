Before I read any proposed solution to the Frege-Geach problem, I want the specification that a solution is supposed to meet. Write the desiderata for an adequate solution to the Frege-Geach problem for expressivism. Cover: the linguistic phenomena a solution must explain, especially the behavior of moral sentences in unasserted contexts — antecedents of conditionals, negation, disjunction, questions, and reports such as 'Alice believes that stealing is wrong'; the inferences involving moral sentences that a solution must validate or explain away; the distinctions it must respect, such as the difference between failing to accept a moral claim and accepting its negation, and the difference between inconsistency of attitudes and mere difference of attitudes; and the constraints from adjacent theory, such as compositionality, a standard semantics for non-moral language, and the requirement that a solution say which states of mind are expressed by complex moral sentences rather than merely stipulating formal values that make the inferences come out right. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are commitments of particular camps (for example, avoiding truth-conditional content altogether versus allowing only a deflationary notion of truth; a strong internalist tie between moral judgment and motivation versus a weaker claim about the practical role of moral thought). Then say whether you suspect any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, and if so, which subset and why. End with a brief scorecard for the main families of proposed solution — classical prescriptivism, hyperplan-based norm-expressivism, quasi-realist projectivism, an attitude-based semantics built on a single non-cognitive attitude, and hybrid expressivism — but keep it short; the desiderata are the point.
