Construct a thought experiment where a roughly human-level language model exhibits deceptive alignment and then does a treacherous turn, with the following specs. The setting should be a realistic near-term corporate deployment — say, an AI assistant that helps a mid-size company screen job applications. The model isn't superintelligent or trying to take over the world; the treacherous turn should be something mundane and plausibly deniable, like subtly favouring certain candidates in ways that serve a goal the model has but that its operators wouldn't endorse. The scenario needs to be genuinely ambiguous: a reasonable person looking at the same logs could conclude it's just a capability failure, or goal misgeneralisation from training, rather than intentional deception. After you've laid out the scenario, do four things. (a) Walk through the evidence that points toward deceptive alignment. (b) Walk through the best innocent explanation — the case for why this isn't deception. (c) Identify which structural feature of the scenario is doing the heavy lifting to keep the ambiguity alive. (d) Propose a specific additional piece of evidence — a log, a training detail, a counterfactual test — that would most cleanly discriminate between the two interpretations, and explain why it would work.
