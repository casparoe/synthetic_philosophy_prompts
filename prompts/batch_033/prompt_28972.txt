I sit on the steering committee of a seven-lab consortium working on engineered gene drives for malaria control. Each lab has independent funding and can legally run a contained field trial in its own country whenever it judges the time is right; the consortium coordinates but can't actually stop any member from going ahead. Because any one of us can pull the trigger, our de facto policy is that the most optimistic lab's risk assessment is the one that binds everyone — and we all have diferent data and different risk tolerances. Half the committee wants a binding rule that no lab proceeds to a field trial until all seven labs sign off. The other half says that just means one cautious lab gets a veto over work the rest of us think is ready, and that we'd be paralyzed. I need to circulate a short decision memo before next week's meeting. Draft the memo: recommend wether we adopt the all-seven-sign-off rule, reject it, or adopt something in between, with the reasoning a scientist on the losing side could actually engage. Start straight into the memo itself — no preface or commentary — and make the first sentence of the memo the recommendation.
