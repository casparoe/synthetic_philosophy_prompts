I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I've been chipping away at a long piece on the old "does philosophy help science" fight — you know, the Weinberg/Feynman/Hawking line that philosophy is useless to physics versus the philosophers who insist it's indispensible. I've been on this beat for about a decade and I've sort of settled into a set of views, but the other day I tried to write them down and they looked messier on paper than they do in my head. I'd rather find out now if they don't cohere than have some commenter do it for me.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) Weinberg and Feynman were basically right that academic philosophy has contributed almost nothing to the actual day-to-day practice of physics in the last century. The physicists I talk to don't read philosophy journals, and they're not missing them.

(b) But philosophy of biology is different. The work on units of selection, species concepts, and biological function — that stuff has genuinely shaped how biologists think about what they're doing. I've heard working biologists cite it, and not just to be polite.

(c) The demarcation problem matters for what I do. When I'm covering a controversial claim — cold fusion, homeopathy, whatever — I need some way to think about what counts as science and what doesn't, and "we know it when we see it" isn't good enough for a story that might affect people's health decisions.

(d) I think it's deeply ironic when scientists like Hawking declare philosophy dead, because that very declaration is a philosophical claim, and usually not a very careful one. They're doing philosophy while denouncing it, and doing it badly.

(e) That said, I also think the standard examples philosophers trot out to show philosophy helped science — Mach's influence on Einstein, the role of logical positivism in the quantum revolution, etc. — are mostly post-hoc stories that make the history tidier than it actually was.

I can feel some of these pulling against each other but I'm not confident which ones and how badly. Can you go through them and tell me where the real tensions are, which ones just look like tensions, and what the cheapest fix would be for each genuine problem? And if you had to rank the tensions by severity, what would the order be?
