I'm a working novelist who teaches an advanced fiction workshop: sixteen undergraduates, one two-hour session. I want a single runnable activity that makes them feel the force of the question whether an author's intention should govern the meaning of a text, and leave with a precise sense of what a writer can and cannot control about what a story means. The stakes should be practical rather than scholarly. The problem should emerge from the rules rather than from any framing: have participants produce or interpret a short passage under constraints, commit to an interpretation before learning the author's stated intention, and only then face the question of whether that intention changes what the text means. No lecture up front; the theoretical labels should appear only in the debrief. Give me the rules, materials, and a minute-by-minute timeline, then a debrief that maps the moments of the activity onto the main positions — that the text means what the author intended, that it means something independent of intention, and that the reader constructs the meaning — without requiring any prior reading. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which feature of the debate the activity cannot reproduce and I will have to supply myself.
