I've been reading rationalist blog and forum threads on Solomonoff induction and Occam's razor for a long time, and the debate seems to have settled into a loop: both sides accept the same formal facts, yet neither moves. One side argues that Solomonoff induction gives Occam's razor a rigorous foundation. It assigns prior probability that decreases exponentially in program length, so among computable hypotheses the shorter ones are favored from the start; the invariance theorem says the choice of universal reference machine changes program lengths only by an additive constant, which washes out in the limit; and Solomonoff's prior multiplicatively dominates every computable prior, so its predictions converge on any computable distribution. On this view the razor is not one heuristic among many but the prior that works. The other side replies that the reference machine is exactly where all the arbitrariness went. The additive constant is only bounded in the limit; with an adversarially chosen machine it can be made larger than any finite amount of evidence can overcome, so for any actual, finite dataset the simplicity ordering can be reversed. The prior is also uncomputable, and the step from a scientific hypothesis to a program computing a data stream is itself a substantive modeling choice. So, the skeptics conclude, Solomonoff induction does not justify the razor in any sense that matters for real inference; it proves something asymptotic and then treats the finite case as if it followed.

My working hypothesis is that this is not one disagreement but several, tangled together: a verbal disagreement about what it means to justify Occam's razor (a convergence theorem about priors versus a practical license to prefer shorter scientific theories on finite data), a substantive disagreement about whether a prior that is objective only relative to an arbitrary choice of reference machine can ground a rational requirement, and a running together of at least three different razors — algorithmic simplicity, resistance to overfitting, and something like metaphysical parsimony — that Solomonoff induction engages to very different degrees.

Diagnose this debate: which parts are verbal, which are substantive, and which are different questions sharing a name? Engage with what each side actually says, not just the steelmanned version. Be direct about which pieces the formal results actually settle and which they leave open. Then, given that diagnosis, say what would count as progress: a theorem about how the additive constant behaves across natural reference machines, a bound on how much data is needed before it stops mattering, a sharper separation of the three razors, or a replacement question. I want the structure of the disagreement and the conditions for movement, not a survey of Solomonoff induction.
