I'm a journalist who covers economics and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether 18th-century monetary thinkers have anything useful to say to the crypto and MMT crowds. I keep coming back to Hume because his actual view is weirder than the usual "he was pro-commerce" summary. As I understand it, from the essays he published in the 1750s on money, interest, and the balance of trade: he thought money is basically a convention and a token for labour and goods, so that in the long run the absolute quantity of it doesn't make a nation richer — a sudden influx of gold would just raise prices once it spread through the economy, though in the short run, before prices adjust, it can stimulate production. He also thought a trade deficit was self-correcting, because gold flowing out would lower prices in the deficit country and make its exports cheaper, until things reversed. So he thought the mercantilists were chasing the wrong thing by hoarding bullion.

Here's the thought experiment I want to run by you. Suppose we could sit Hume down in 2025 and brief him, carefully, on two things. First, modern fiat money: no gold backing, a central bank that creates and destroys money to hit an inflation target. Second, Bitcoin: a money with no sovereign issuer, a fixed supply cap, and "mining" that doesn't actually dig anything out of the ground. What would he make of each? Would he call Bitcoin a feature or a bug given his view that the quantity of money is ultimately irrelevant to real wealth? Would he see inflation targeting as a vindication of his point that money is a human institution, or as a dangerous pretense that we can fine-tune the thing he said only works through short-run surprise? I'd like the answer to be a genuine extrapolation from what he actually argued, not a generic "Hume was a wise man" answer — and where his framework breaks down (Bitcoin doesn't flow between countries the way gold did, fiat money has no non-monetary use, central banks are public institutions rather than private hoards), say so. Also tell me where you're on solid ground vs where your guessing, because I don't want to put words in his mouth in the piece.
