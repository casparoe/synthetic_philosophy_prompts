I am a PhD student in social epistemology preparing for my dissertation prospectus defense, and I would like to check whether my core commitments form a coherent package before I build an argument on top of them. My work is on epistemic injustice, and over the past two years I have settled into the following set of views:

(1) Testimonial injustice is a sui generis epistemic wrong. It cannot be reduced to distributive injustice, to ordinary epistemic error, or to generic moral wrongs such as disrespect.

(2) The distinctive wrong of testimonial injustice is best explained by the harm it does to the speaker in their capacity as a knower — specifically, it undermines their epistemic agency. Downstream practical harms are not what make it an epistemic injustice.

(3) Structural and institutional forms of epistemic injustice are more fundamental than the individualistic, agential forms foregrounded in Fricker's early work. The best explanation of why individual hearers deploy prejudicial credibility deficits is itself structural, and the most serious epistemic injustices are structural rather than episodic.

(4) Credibility excess is not itself a form of testimonial injustice. It may be implicated in other kinds of wrong — for example, it may contribute to epistemic exploitation or to the background conditions for hermeneutical injustice — but it is not an epistemic injustice in its own right.

(5) Hermeneutical injustice is a genuinely distinct kind of wrong from testimonial injustice. It is not merely a different manifestation of the same underlying phenomenon, and it is not reducible to testimonial injustice plus some background condition.

(6) Epistemic injustice can, in principle, be suffered by members of socially dominant groups. It is not restricted to marginalized groups, provided the wrong is systematic rather than merely incidental and is tied to identity prejudice.

I want to know whether these six commitments are jointly tenable. Specifically: which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension but dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency if any tensions are genuine? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious. And if your verdict is that the set is fully coherent, I would like you to say which argument or consideration would be most likely to lead you to reverse that verdict — that is, where your reasoning is most vulnerable and what kind of counterargument would change your mind.
