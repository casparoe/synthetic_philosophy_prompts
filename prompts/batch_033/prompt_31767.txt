Construct a thought experiment in which an autonomous system causes a serious harm, and each of the four standard candidate loci of moral responsibility — the system's designer, its deployer, its human operator or user, and the system itself — has a genuinely plausible case for being exculpated, yet the case as a whole does not feel like a mere accident: intuitively, someone should be responsible.

Constraints: The scenario must be realistic (near-future technology, no science fiction), no longer than one paragraph, and each exculpation must be grounded in an independently plausible principle of moral responsibility — not a stipulation, not a legal technicality. In particular, the designer must be exculpated not by ignorance of the system's workings but by something like the system having learned or adapted in a way the designer could not reasonably have foreseen; the deployer must be exculpated not by negligence but by having taken all reasonable precautions; the user or operator must be exculpated not by coercion but by the system's behaviour being genuinely non-transparent in the moment; and the system itself must be exculpated not by a simple denial of agency but by a more interesting argument — for example, that it lacks the right kind of reasons-responsiveness even though it is highly competent.

After the scenario, do the following: (i) Walk through each of the four candidates and show why, under the most plausible theory of responsibility applicable to that candidate, they are exculpated — making clear which theory you are applying. (ii) Identify the structural feature of the case that creates the gap: what is it about this scenario that prevents responsibility from attaching to any of the four loci? (iii) Explain how a defender of closing the gap would attack the case — which candidate they would try to pin responsibility on, and what they would have to argue to make it stick. (iv) Say which of the four exculpations you think is weakest, and why.

Keep the response to 800 words or fewer. Also, state how confident you are in your analysis and single out the point you are least sure of.
