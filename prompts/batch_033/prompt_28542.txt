I keep noticing the same snag in work that tries to explain what a mental state is about in terms of what the state was selected to do, and I cannot find a name for it. The difficulty is that one and the same selection history can be described at several different levels, and the level one chooses changes the content assigned to the state. Take a frog that snaps at flies. The history can be told as 'the snapping response was selected for detecting flies,' or 'for detecting small, dark, moving things,' or 'for detecting food,' or 'for detecting nutrients that happen to arrive in fly-sized packages.' Each description fits the same events, yet each assigns the internal state a different content: fly, small dark moving thing, food, nutrient. The difference is not merely verbal. When the frog snaps at a shadow or a slow-moving bee, one description counts that as a misrepresentation, while another counts it as a well-functioning response. I see the same thing with a bacterium that orients along magnetic field lines to reach low-oxygen water: is the state it uses about magnetic north, about low-oxygen water, or about 'that direction'? The obvious way to break the tie is to appeal to what the organism is really trying to achieve, or to which description best explains its success, but that seems to smuggle back in the very notion of content the account was supposed to naturalize. I know this is adjacent to the disjunction problem — the worry that a state would end up meaning 'fly-or-bee-or-shadow' because every cause gets included — but my question is narrower. The disjunction problem concerns too many causes entering the content; this concerns the fact that the selective facts themselves can be carved at many levels, and nothing in the history fixes one. Does this narrower problem have an established name of its own, and how exactly does it relate to the disjunction problem and to the problem of misrepresentation? If it has no name, what would you call it, and what would a solution have to do to count as a solution rather than a redescription? I am not after a bare label; I am trying to work out whether this is a distinct difficulty or just the disjunction problem in another guise.
