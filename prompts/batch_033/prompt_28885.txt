I hold that Fitch's argument decisively refutes the principle that all truths are knowable, and therefore that any broadly verificationist or anti-realist semantics that commits to that principle is untenable. The proof is short, seems to require only modest logical resources, and yields the absurd conclusion that all truths are actually known. I want to know what would count against this verdict — not just what critics have said, but what a defender of the knowability principle would have to show for their position to survive, and what the honest dialectical cost of each escape route is.

Specifically, I want the replies mapped from most to least threatening to the Fitch argument, with attention to the following distinctions: replies that challenge the formalization of the knowability principle itself (e.g., by restricting it to certain classes of propositions, or by reformulating it to avoid the substitution that generates the paradox); replies that challenge the logical principles the proof employs (the factivity of knowledge, its distribution over conjunction, or the classical logic in which the proof is carried out); and replies that accept the proof but deny that the conclusion is absurd (e.g., by embracing the claim that all truths are in fact known, perhaps by an ideal or divine knower). For each, I want to know whether it preserves the spirit of the original verificationist motivation, or whether it saves the letter at the cost of the motivation — and if the latter, whether that hollowing-out is itself a problem for the position, or whether the position can be reformulated with a different motivation that survives the repair. I also want to know whether any of these replies have been shown to fail in turn, or whether some remain live, and if the live ones collectively suggest that the Fitch argument, while powerful, does not quite close the door on all forms of knowability. Finally, I would like an honest assessment of whether the Fitch argument itself has been qualified or hedged in ways that insulate it from counter-reply, and whether that is a problem for an argument of this kind.
