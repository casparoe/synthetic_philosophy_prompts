i'm putting together a handout for an undergrad political philosophy class on the liberalism-communitarianism debate, and i want a taxonomy of the positions as a tree where each branch is labeled by the claim that splits it. the idea is that students can locate themselves in the logical space before they know any of the names — they answer the questions and then see where they land.

the tree should branch on something like these questions (you can reorder, add, or refine them):

- whether the state may legitimately promote a particular conception of the good life, or must remain neutral between conceptions
- whether the self is prior to its ends (we choose our values) or constituted by them (we discover our values through community)
- whether minority cultural groups can claim exemptions from general laws even when those exemptions limit individual members' rights
- whether liberal neutrality itself is a disguised comprehensive doctrine rather than a genuinely neutral framework

the leaves should include at least: rawlsian political liberalism, raz-style perfectionist liberalism, sandel's communitarianism, taylor's expressivist communitarianism, and kymlicka-style liberal nationalism. aim for six to eight leaves total. for any unoccupied combination of answers, note whether it's empty because the view is incoherent or just because nobody's defended it. flag any two leaves the tree can't cleanly seperate.

the tree should be built from the logical space rather than from the list of names: first the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual positions in that space. each question should be phrased as a concrete case or claim a student could answer without knowing the labels — so no 'do you think the self is encumbered or unencumbered?', but instead something like 'should a religious community be allowed to restrict its children's access to secular education?' or 'is the french state justified in banning headscarves in public schools as a defense of laïcité?'
