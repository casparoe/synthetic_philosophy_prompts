I got into a long argument with a friend last night about whether the language you speak changes how you think, and I've been turning it over since. She kept insisting that people who speak different languages literally think differently, and I kept pushing back, because I can obviously have the same thought in either of the languages I speak. But then she brought up something I can't quite dismiss, and now I want to know if theres a name for it.

I speak a language where you basically can't report something that happened without marking how you know it — whether you saw it yourself, heard it from someone, or are inferring it. In English I can just say it rained and nobody has to know where I got that from. I noticed that when I'm speaking that other language I actually do keep closer track of where my information came from, and when I tell a story I automatically include those details. When I tell the same story in English I leave them out. It's not that I stop knowing them, and it's not that I can't think them in English — it's more that the grammar keeps poking me to notice them, and after a while it becomes a habit. I've seen something similar with a bilingual friend who tells the same anecdote differently depending on which language he's in: same facts, but different details bubble up, because the language he's speaking pushes certain distinctions to the front. I also noticed that in one language I tend to pack the way someone moved into the verb and add where separately, while in the other the where is the verb and the way is an extra I often skip, so when I retell the same little scene I end up mentioning different things even though I saw the same event.

My friend thinks this prooves her strong point, but it feels to me like a much weaker thing. It's not that the language puts a fence around what I can think; it's that at the moment of speaking it makes some distinctions obligatory and others optional, and that shapes what I end up noticing and saying. Is there an established term for that specifically — the speaking-forces-attention thing, as opposed to the language-caps-what-you-can-think thing? And if there is, I want to know its exact edges: does it only apply while I'm speaking, or does it leak into how I perceive things when I'm not talking, and does the habit outlast the language? I don't just want the label; I want to know how the weaker claim and the strong claim get carved apart, and whether the weaker one is even a version of the strong one or something else entirely.
