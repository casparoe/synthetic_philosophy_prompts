I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I've ended up down a rabbit hole about virtue ethics and the situationist critique because it bears on something I think about constantly: whether people have anything like stable character, and whether a novel that builds a character out of consistent moral dispositions is describing something real or just flattering the reader's biases. So I read the situationist stuff, the Milgram obedience results, the Good Samaritan seminary students, the phone-booth dime study, the conformity experiments, and then I read the virtue-ethicist replies, and what strikes me is that this argument has been running for decades and the whole thing hasnt moved an inch. Both sides roughly agree about what the experiments show. Yet the situationists still say virtue ethics rests on a false psychology, and the virtue ethicists still say the critique attacks a strawman. I've watched smart people go around this same loop in person and in print, and I cant tell what kind of disagreement it is anymore.

My working hypothesis, for what it's worth, is that the two sides are doing different things: the situationists are making a descriptive claim about what people are like, and the virtue ethicists are making a normative claim about what a good life consists in, so the empirical data can't refute the normative claim because it was never aimed at it. But I'm not sure that's right, because the situationists have a reply, something like: if the virtues are so rare that almost nobody has them, then a theory organized around them is asking the impossible, and the "ought implies can" principle should bite. And then the virtue ethicists come back with the point that a virtue isn't a fixed behavioral disposition that has to show up the same way across every situation anyway; a virtuous person responds to reasons, so different situations can pull different behavior out of the same underlying trait. At which point I start to suspect the word "trait" or "virtue" is doing too much work for everyone involved.

So here's what I want: a diagnosis of the structure of this standoff, not a verdict about who's right. Is this a verbal disagreement about what "character trait" means? Is it a disagreement about how much descriptive plausibility a normative theory needs? Is there a hidden empirical question, say, how much cross-situational consistency there actually is, or whether local traits can do the work global traits can't, that's keeping it alive? Are they answering different questions under one heading, or is the question itself ill-posed? And then, given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a conceptual distinction, an experiment, a change of question, or should nobody expect movement? I'd rather you not just survey both positions; I've read those. I want the disagreement itself dissected.
