I keep seeing factored cognition and HCH treated as if they give a free idealization of what careful deliberation is: decompose the question, answer the pieces, recombine them. Before I accept or reject that picture, I want the full specification of what a theory in this area has to get right. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of HCH-style factored cognition as an idealization of deliberation. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it: the simple question that needs no decomposition; the synthesis task where decomposition genuinely helps; the subquestion that is harder to evaluate than the parent question; the case where subanswers can be optimized against the overseer's judgment; the case where the parent question demands a global consistency check across subanswers; the case where the overseer has false beliefs or limited competence; and the fact that real deliberation revises its questions as it goes rather than traversing a fixed tree. I want the distinctions such a theory cannot collapse — decomposability of the task versus decomposability of the evaluation, the overseer's competence versus the overseer's alignment, the idealization as a normative reconstruction versus a literal training recipe — and the formal or near-formal results about the limits of iterated amplification it must stay consistent with, stated as requirements rather than as a literature summary. Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only preferences of particular camps (for example, whether evaluation must be adversarial, whether the base units must be humans). Tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude naive versions of the view, and whether any pair of requirements is plausibly jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect the faithfulness-of-decomposition and boundedness-of-the-overseer pair is, but I want the argument either way. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing proposals. Finally, say how confident you are in the list as a whole and single out the point you are least sure of.
