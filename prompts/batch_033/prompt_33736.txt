I am currently taking a philosophy of language course, and we have just finished a unit on vagueness and the sorites paradox. For our final seminar I would like to contribute a villanelle that captures the structure of the paradox itself. The two refrains should carry the philosophical weight: one should voice the inductive premise — that no single grain can make the difference between a heap and not-a-heap — and the other should voice the intolerable conclusion the paradox seems to force, namely that there are no heaps at all. The villanelle form, with its two refrains alternating and finally meeting in the closing couplet, should do the work of showing why the inductive premise and the conclusion keep circling one another and why the paradox ultimately compels them together. Someone who knows the sorites should be able to identify which refrain is which and explain why the form forces them to end side by side. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of language would not wince, and it should succeed as a poem in its own right. Please write the answer in British English.
