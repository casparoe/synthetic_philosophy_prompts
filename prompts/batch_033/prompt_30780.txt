So I've ended up fairly convinced that truthlikeness is a real, objective feature of theories — that some false theories are genuinely closer to the truth than others — and that the right way to cash it out is the similarity approach: a theory's truthlikeness is how similar it is to the complete true theory, measured across some space of possibilities or constituents. I know Popper's original definition blew up (the Miller/Tichý results showed, roughly, that on his account false theories can't be ranked against each other), but I read that as its a bug in the definition rather than a reason to abandon the whole notion. I also tend to think the language-dependence worry is overblown: sure, you can gerrymander a weird predicate and flip an ordering, but for any reasonable scientific language the ranking is stable enough, and anyway we get to choose the metric.
