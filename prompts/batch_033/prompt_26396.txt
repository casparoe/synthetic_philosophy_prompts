For background: Lewis showed that if the probability of an indicative conditional equals the corresponding conditional probability — the Equation — then under very weak assumptions (roughly, that probabilities update by conditionalisation and that the class of propositions forms a Boolean algebra) the probability function can take at most four distinct values, which is absurd for any realistic belief state. The standard moral is that indicative conditionals do not express propositions with truth conditions; they are devices for expressing conditional beliefs. But there are escape routes: deny the Equation, deny that conditionals embed under the probability operator in the way Lewis assumes, or restrict the applicability of conditionalisation.
