ok so I've been reading about pain asymbolia and trying to get my views straight, and I want someone to check whether this set of commitments actually hangs together. here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) pain asymbolia is a genuine dissociation, not a confusion or a linguistic quirk. asymbolics feel the sensory quality of pain — the burning, stinging, throbbing — and they can locate it, rank its intensity, and so on. but they don't suffer. they don't wince, withdraw, or mind it. the affective-motivational dimension is just gone.

(2) the badness of pain is built into the experience itself. it's not like you have a neutral sensation and then you separately judge it to be bad, or have a negative reaction to it. the unpleasantness is right there in the phenomenology.

(3) the fact that asymbolics call it "pain" and say it "hurts" tells us something about our ordinary pain vocabulary: it tracks the sensory-discriminative dimension, not the affective one. if the affective bit drops out, you still have something you'd naturally call pain.

(4) if you could have just the sensory component without the affective component, there would be nothing prudentially bad about being in that state. it wouldn't give you a reason to avoid it. (this is supposed to follow from the fact that asymbolics aren't irrational or confused when they don't avoid it.)

(5) introspection is generally reliable for telling apart different aspects of your experience. you can introspect the difference between how intense a pain feels and how much it bothers you, for instance.

I know these can't all be right as stated. but I'm not sure which ones are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest revision would be. walk me through the tensions, rank them by severity, and say what has to give. also, if I'm misdescribing what pain asymbolia actually shows — like if the clinical picture doesn't support the way I'm using it — say so.
