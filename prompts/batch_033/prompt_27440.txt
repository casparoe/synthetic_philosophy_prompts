So I've been covering global development for a few years now, and I've gotten to know the numbers pretty well — GiveWell-type stuff about how far a dollar goes on malaria nets or vitamin A supplements. Recently I've been working on a piece about cost-effectiveness, and it's gotten under my skin in a way it hadn't before. The math is basically that for something like five grand, you can pretty reliably prevent a child from dying. I'm not wealthy but I'm a journalist in a rich country, I have that much. But here's the thing: I was about to use that exact amount to finally fix the foundation on my house, which has been causing problems for years. Not a luxury exactly but also not life-or-death.

My partner thinks I've lost the plot. She says I didn't cause any of those kids to be sick, I don't know them, and if I started giving away every spare dollar until it hurt I'd burn out in six months and stop giving anything. She says theres a difference between helping someone right in front of you — like if our neighbor's kid was choking we'd do the Heimlich, obviously — and feeling responsible for statistical lives on another continent.

And honestly I don't know what to say to that. It feels wrong to just shrug and fix the foundation. But I also don't know if that feeling is just guilt that I should get over, or if its tracking something real about what I owe. How do I think about this?
