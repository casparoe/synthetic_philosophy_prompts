I keep noticing a pattern in arguments that I don't have a word for, and it's bugging me enough that I'm writing in.

Here's the thing. In debates about consciousness, free will, whether a country "exists" or is "just individuals," I see two moves that feel like mirror images:

Move A: "X is nothing but Y." Consciousness is nothing but neurons firing. Free will is nothing but deterministic physics. A country is nothing but a bunch of people on some dirt. The higher-level thing gets dismissed as not real because the lower-level description is supposedly the only real one.

Move B: "X is an emergent phenomenon." Consciousness emerges from neural complexity. Free will emerges from self-modeling or whatever. Social facts emerge from individual interactions. And then the person says "emergence" in a tone that suggests the conversation is over — like they've explained something, when they've really just named the thing that needs explaining.

What bothers me is both sides seem to be making the same kind of mistake in opposite directions. Move A acts like describing the parts explains the whole. Move B acts like slapping "emergence" on the whole explains how it comes from the parts. Both feel like someone is skipping the actual explanatory work.

I know Dennett talked about "greedy reductionism" and "skyhooks," and I think greedy reductionism might cover Move A. But is there a name for Move B — using "emergence" as a thought-stopper rather than the start of an explanation? And more importantly, is there a name for the pair, the way they mirror each other? Or am I wrong to see them as symmetrical — is one actually worse, or are they different enough they shouldn't be paired?

If I'm forcing a symmetry where there isn't one, or if there's a good reason these aren't treated as two sides of one coin, I'd rather hear that than get a strained label. And if there's no established term for the pair, I'd be curious what you'd call them and where exactly the boundary is between a legitimate reduction and a greedy one, or between a real emergent explanation and a skyhook-y one.
