I've been teaching the liberty debates for years now — Berlin, MacCallum, Pettit, Skinner, the whole crew — and I've started to suspect the negative-liberty and republican-liberty camps are talking past each other more than they need to be. My colleague down the hall, who's firmly in the republican camp, and I have been batting this back and forth over coffee for months, and we've decided to actually try to write something together rather than keep arguing in circles.

What I'd like you to do is draft the joint statement we might produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a Berlin-style negative-liberty theorist and a Pettit-style republican-liberty theorist. I want the document to have two parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign — about the concept of liberty, about what makes interference or domination objectionable, about what the paradigm cases are that any theory has to handle, and about what the other camp gets right. Second, a disagreements table where each row identifies a precise crux, states the negative-liberty view and the republican view in wording each side would accept for itself, and — where possible — specifies a test case or argument both sides would agree bears on that crux, along with each side's prediction about how it comes out.

The hard part, I think, is figuring out whether the disagreement is really about the concept of liberty itself, or about the value of liberty relative to other goods, or about something else entirely. Some people say the whole dispute is just about whether we should build certain protections into the definition of "freedom" or treat them as seperate political values. If that's right, I want that stated plainly. And if there are points where the two sides just have irreducibly different intuitions with no shared arbiter, I want that flagged too, rather than papered over. Also, I'd like you to indicate how confident you are about each part of the document and single out the claim you're least sure about — I suspect some of the cruxes are less clear-cut than the literature makes them sound.
