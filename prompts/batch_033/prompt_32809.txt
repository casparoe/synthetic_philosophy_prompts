Please argue that behavioral dissociations in split-brain patients do not warrant the conclusion that there are two distinct subjects of consciousness occupying a single skull. Present your argument as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Each point should advance a distinct consideration against the two-subjects inference — for example, by identifying a hidden assumption, offering an alternative explanation of the same data, or exposing a conceptual cost of the two-subjects view that is not incurred by a one-subject account. Do not rest the argument on empirical claims that would require citation; work at the level of what the competing interpretations must commit to and what each can explain.
