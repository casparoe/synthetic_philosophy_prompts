I've come around to Lewis-style counterpart theory for de re modality: when I say "I could have been a plumber," there isn't one me existing in many worlds with different properties; instead, other worlds contain counterparts of me (things similar enough to me in contextually weighted ways), and my modal properties are really facts about those counterparts. I know the stock objections — Humphrey's complaint that this makes my modal properties about someone else rather than about me, worries about inconstancy of counterpart relations, the fact that you need advanced modalizing to avoid collapsing the view back into a box-diamond apparatus — and I don't find them decisive. So I'm not asking for a textbook defense or attack.

What I want is a systematic map of what would count against the view, in the sense of actually bearing on whether it's true (or at least whether it's the right theory of de re modality). Please seperate:

(a) defeaters a committed counterpart theorist would themselves concede count against the view, even if they think the view survives them;
(b) things only critics count as defeaters, and whether a defender is being unreasonable to shrug them off;
(c) developments — a possible case, an intuition, a result in neighboring areas like persistence over time, essentialism, or the logic of "actually" — that would be decisive or close to it.

Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say what the view is committed to that makes it vulnerable there. I especially want to know which commitments counterpart theory cannot give up without quietly becoming transworld identity (or some other rival), and what the view predicts that its rivals don't. Finally, be honest about whether the view has been insulated by qualifications — context-sensitive counterpart relations, multiple counterpart relations, similarity being vague and interest-relative — and whether that insulation is a vice or just how a theory of modality should work. If you think the view risks nothing, say so plainly.

You're an expert in modal metaphysics. Think carefully and brainstorm candidate defeaters before organizing your answer. Then give me the final answer in at most 1,000 words: a ranked list with a sentence or two of reasoning per item, plus a closing paragraph on whether counterpart theory is exposed or insulated, and whether that's a problem.
