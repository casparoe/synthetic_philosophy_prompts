Got into a long argument with a friend last night about lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, and I need to turn it into a two-page proposal for a small research grant. Her complaint was that the threshold is just a patch: below it the view treats pleasure and pain as commensurable — a mild pain can be offset by enough pleasure — but above it no amount of pleasure offsets one intense pain, and you cant have it both ways unless you can say what the threshold is and why the currency changes there. My half-formed idea is that the view has never actually said what commensurability between pleasure and pain amounts to, and the threshold is quietly doing two jobs: marking a discontinuity in a single scale of badness, and switching to a different, non-compensatory mode of comparison. I suspect you can't defend the view without separating those jobs. So the proposal question is something like: can a lexical threshold view give a principled account of where and why compensation stops, without collapsing into full commensurability (threshold arbitrary) or full incommensurability (below-threshold tradeoffs become mysterious)? Write the proposal: precise question, why the existing positions leave it open, the approach and why not the obvious alternatives, what a result looks like, and what would count as failure. Keep it scoped to six months, no empirical work. Don't worry about citations, I'll add them. And please flag what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff — don't present a contested take as the consensus.
