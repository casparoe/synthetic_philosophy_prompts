ok so we're doing this unit on aesthetics in my philosophy class and we've been arguing about AI art for like three days straight. i've been trying to figure out what i actually think by going case by case, and now i have a bunch of verdicts but no idea what principle ties them together. here's what i got:

- someone types "cute cat in a spacesuit" into midjourney and posts the first result as "my art." i don't think that's their art. like at all.
- a concept artist at a game studio uses AI to generate 50 thumbnails for a character design, picks the three most promising, combines elements from them, and then paints the final character by hand referencing the AI output. i think that's totally fine and the final character is their art.
- a photographer takes a photo, then uses AI generative fill to extend the edges of the frame because they wish they'd used a wider lens. that feels fine to me, it's still their photo.
- someone spends an entire weekend writing and rewriting prompts, generates like 400 images, curates down to one, and submits it to an art contest. honestly i have no idea. my gut says it's not the same as making art but i also can't point to why its different from the concept artist case.
- someone trains a LoRA on their own watercolor paintings and then uses it to generate new images in their style. i think that's their art. the training data is all theirs.
- someone uses a model that was clearly trained on a living artist's portfolio without permission, and generates images "in the style of" that artist. i think that's wrong because of the theft, but if i'm honest the output itself seems... creative? like the prompter still made choices. but it feels gross.

so my question is: is there a principle that actually makes sense of all these verdicts? and what does it say about the case i keep going back and forth on — the weekend-prompter who submits to a contest? i'm also wondering if i have to give up any of my verdicts because they can't all fit under one principle.
