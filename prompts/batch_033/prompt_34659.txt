I cover computing and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I keep noticing that complexity theorists and algorithm designers treat polynomial time as the line between feasible and infeasible, even though n^50 is polynomial and completely useless in practice while 2^(n/1000) might handle every input size anyone actually cares about. What theory of feasible computation does this practice presuppose? Is it strictly committed to polynomial time being the right cutoff, or is it more of a pragmatic convention that happens to track the problems people actually solve? And what would have to change in how textbooks, funding panels, and working engineers talk about hardness if the field took average-case complexity or parameterized complexity as the real measure rather than worst-case polynomial time?
