I've been down a rabbit hole lately reading about coordination problems and this idea people online call Moloch — the way a bunch of people can each do the smart thing given what everyone else is doing, and the whole group still ends up somewhere nobody wanted to be. My brother just calls these races to the bottom. Anyway, I rewatched Dr. Strangelove last weekend (hadn't seen it since highschool) and it hit me that the whole movie looks like one big multipolar trap: the Soviets build an automated doomsday machine because they can't afford to match the US in a conventional arms race, the US has its own hair-trigger retaliatory procedures because it's terrified of a first strike, and every move either side makes to feel safer just makes the other side more nervous, until one rogue general and a communications breakdown is enough to set the whole thing off. But here's whats bugging me. In the Moloch story, the tragedy is that everyone is being reasonable, and the trap is made of incentives. In the movie, a lot of what goes wrong is just stupidity, paranoia, and systems that don't let any human being call things off — plus that whole subtext about the generals being impotent and overcompensating. So is Dr. Strangelove actually a good dramatization of a multipolar trap, or does it smuggle in a different diagnosis of the cold war (that it's run by irrational, insecure men and runaway bureaucracy) and only look like a Moloch story on the surface? I'd like a reading that treats the film as a film and the trap idea as an idea, and is willing to say the fit only goes so far. Also, please write the answer in American English.
