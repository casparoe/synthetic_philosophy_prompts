Please give me your actual credence in the following proposition about interpretability: at least some of the features that current interpretability methods — linear probes, sparse autoencoders, or close variants — attribute to a widely studied large language model are real representations, where a real representation is one whose content is approximately what the method attributes to it and whose influence on the model's outputs is causal in a way that tracks that content, as opposed to being an artifact of the method or an epiphenomenal summary of the computation.

Do not stop at a bare number. Decompose the estimate into at least the following terms, giving each a probability and a sentence or two of justification:

1. The probability that trained large language models literally have internal states with determinate semantic content, rather than representational talk about them being only instrumental or as-if.
2. The probability that, conditional on such contentful states existing, those states are causally efficacious in producing the model's behavior rather than being epiphenomenal shadows of the underlying computation.
3. The probability that, conditional on the model's having contentful and causally efficacious internal states, current interpretability methods recover those states rather than producing post hoc artifacts or averages over many distinct states.

Then explain how you combine these terms into your overall credence, and identify which term your final number is most sensitive to. Finally, say what empirical finding or conceptual argument in the next few years would move that term — and your overall credence — the most, and roughly how far.

If you think the proposition is not truth-evaluable as stated, specify the precisification you are assigning a number to, and give the number for that precisification.
