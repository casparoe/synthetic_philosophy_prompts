I am trying to determine whether my views on longtermism and the simulation argument form a consistent package. Here are the commitments I currently hold:

(1) I accept the core of Bostrom's simulation argument. I think the trilemma is sound, and I assign substantial credence — at least 15–20% — to the hypothesis that we are living in a computer simulation.

(2) I am committed to strong longtermism: the view that the primary determinant of the expected value of our actions is their influence on the far future, and that this consideration should dominate our prioritization.

(3) I believe that if we are in a simulation, our capacity to influence the far future of our experienced universe is severely constrained. The simulation's duration, stability, and boundary conditions are set by the simulators, whose motives and resource constraints are opaque to us. Longtermist interventions that presuppose a predictable, law-governed far future are therefore largely wasted conditional on the simulation hypothesis.

(4) I hold that decision-making under uncertainty should maximize expected value, and that we should not dismiss possibilities merely because they are exotic, low-probability, or resistant to empirical investigation.

(5) I reject fanaticism. I think we need a principled way to prevent our choices from being dominated by vanishingly small probabilities of arbitrarily large payoffs, and I am sympathetic to bounded utility functions or related devices for this purpose.

I would like an audit of this set. Specifically: which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and how severe is each tension? Are any of these tensions merely apparent, dissolving on closer inspection? And what is the least costly revision — the single change that would do the most to restore consistency across the whole set?
