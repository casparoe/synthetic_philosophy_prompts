I'm writing a novel where a mathematician keeps getting pulled into arguments about infinite tasks, and I've been collecting the rulings of a friend of mine (a real mathematician, not the character) so the character doesn't come off as a strawman. Here's what he's said so far. Achilles and the tortoise: no problem, Achilles catches the tortoise; chopping the run into infinitely many half-distances doesn't stop anything. The lamp that gets toggled infinitely many times in the run-up to noon (one minute before, half a minute before, a quarter of a minute before, and so on): he says there's no fact of the matter about whether it's on or off at noon, the setup just doesn't determine an answer. The urn where, with the steps bunched up so they all finish at noon, you put in balls 1 through 10 and take out ball 1 at step 1, put in balls 11 through 20 and take out ball 2 at step 2, and so on: he says the urn is empty at noon, even though the number of balls in it grows at every finite step. The variant where you put in the same ten balls at each step but take out the ball whose number is ten times the step (ball 10 at step 1, ball 20 at step 2, and so on): he says infinitely many balls are left, namely all the balls not divisible by ten.

The case I actually care about for the novel is this. At each step you empty the urn completely and then drop in exactly one ball, alternating between ball 1 and ball 2: after step 1 the urn contains ball 1, after step 2 it contains ball 2, after step 3 ball 1 again, and so on, with the same bunched-up schedule finishing at noon. So at every finite step the urn has exactly one ball in it, but neither ball stays in for good. What would my friend say about this one if he's being consistent with his other verdicts? I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the rule he seems to be running — I can see at least two principles that fit everything he's said so far, and I suspect this new case is where they come apart. Which principle does his answer point to, what does each principle predict here, and is there a further case that would settle it even more cleanly? Also: do his earlier verdicts already commit him on this one, or is its genuinely open?
