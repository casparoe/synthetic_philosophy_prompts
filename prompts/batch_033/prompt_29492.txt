I work on a reinforcement learning team that is trying to make our agents more cautious about changing the world. We have been experimenting with a design pattern that adds an impact penalty to the task reward: the agent is penalized for reducing its ability to achieve a broad set of auxiliary objectives, relative to a baseline trajectory in which it stays idle. The motivating intuition is simple. A household robot told to fetch coffee should not smash a vase, lock the cat in a cupboard, or repaint the hallway on the way, even when none of those things lowers its task reward. So we measure something like how much the agent's actions shrink the set of future goals that would still be reachable, compared with doing nothing, and we subtract that from the reward.

But the more I implement this, the less sure I am about what we are presupposing. The penalty seems to treat the no-op baseline as value-neutral, as if the world as it would have been without the agent were the natural comparison point; yet inaction is not neutral, since an idle agent lets the house burn down or the patient deteriorate. The penalty also requires us to choose the auxiliary objectives over which future reachability is preserved, and that choice looks like a disguised choice about what matters, even if we claim we are staying agnostic about the true goal. Most troubling, the distinction between the intended change (fetching the coffee) and a side effect (the broken vase) appears to be doing all the work, but it is not supplied by the impact measure itself; it is inherited from the task reward. If that is right, the impact penalty either adds nothing beyond the reward function or quietly presupposes a fairly complete specification of value in the course of pretending to avoid one.

I would like to understand what theory of value, and of agency, this practice commits us to. Can avoiding side effects be defined without a full specification of what we actually want, or does an impact measure smuggle in a complete axiology under the cover of a neutral baseline? Please reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can, distinguish what the design strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, and tell me what would have to change in the design if the designer genuinely had only a partial specification of value.
