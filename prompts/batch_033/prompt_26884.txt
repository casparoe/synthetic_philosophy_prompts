For my school's astronomy and physics club, I am preparing a fifteen-minute talk in which I defend a definite position on the Fermi paradox. My claim is that the most probable resolution is rarity: the appearance of technological, tool-using intelligence is so improbable that Earth probably hosts the only such civilization in the galaxy, and perhaps in the observable universe. I am not claiming certainty, only that rarity currently explains the observed silence better than the two main rivals I expect my audience to favor: the view that technological civilizations inevitably destroy themselves, and the view that they exist but are deliberately keeping quiet.

The audience matters, because I have watched them argue. Most of the club are physics-track students; a few are from biology. Our physics teacher takes the Drake equation with fairly optimistic parameter estimates at face value and treats the silence as a problem of insufficient search depth and time, not a real absence. One biology student has read the rare-Earth line of argument and is half on my side, but worries I am overclaiming from a single data point. Two students are attached to the idea that advanced civilizations are hiding from us, whether out of a prime-directive-style noninterference principle or because a dangerous universe makes silence strategic. Another student thinks it is almost obvious that intelligence is self-destructive, and will point to nuclear weapons and climate change. I expect someone to press me on whether the rarity claim can be tested at all.

I am not asking you to write the talk itself. Please draft a Q&A preparation sheet: the eight or so objections I am most likely to get from this particular audience, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they would do, phrased the way these people would actually put them. For each, give me a reply I could deliver in about two minutes and a candid note on where that reply is weakest. Also tell me which objections I should partly concede up front, and include the one question I should most hope nobody asks.
