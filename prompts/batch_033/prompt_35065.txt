Donald Campbell died in 1996, so he never saw RLHF or reward hacking in modern ML. His law — the social-science cousin of Goodhart's — was about quantitative social indicators corrupting the social processes their meant to monitor. I'm trying to figure out how he'd react to specification gaming in deep RL and LLMs. Would he see a robot exploiting a misspecified reward function, or a model getting sycophantic because it's optimizing a learned reward model, as basically the same old story, or would he think there's something genuinely new — like the fact that the proxy is a learned model that keeps getting updated, or that the whole thing plays out in a sandbox rather than a bureaucracy? I'm less interested in a yes/no than in which features of the modern cases he'd find familiar and which would have surprised him, and whether he'd think the standard fixes (better reward models, process rewards, debate) actually address the corruption mechanism he cared about or just relocate it.
