I'm a program manager at a large tech company overseeing a portfolio of about twenty projects ranging from six-month experiments to multi-year platform builds. The thing I struggle with most is knowing when to kill a project that's gone sideways. I've read about the dollar auction and escalation of commitment, and I recognize the pattern: we've sunk $X into something, it's not working, but pulling the plug feels like admitting $X was wasted, so we throw in more. I want a decision procedure I can actually use in quarterly reviews — a checklist or sequence of questions I run through for each amber/red project, that takes maybe 15 minutes, and that's designed specifically to catch the escalation dynamic. It should flag where I'm likely to be fooling myself (e.g. 'the next milestone will be different' when it never is), and it should be honest about the fact that the procedure itself can be gamed by the same psychology its meant to check. Then run it on two cases: (1) an internal tooling project that was supposed to ship in 9 months, is now at month 20, has a working but unloved MVP, and the team lead wants 6 more months and 2 more engineers to 'get it right'; (2) a machine learning feature that was supposed to improve user retention by 5%, has delivered 1.2% in A/B tests after a year of tuning, and the data science lead argues the model is 'on the verge of a breakthrough' if we just double the training compute. For each, tell me what the procedure says and whether I should trust it.
