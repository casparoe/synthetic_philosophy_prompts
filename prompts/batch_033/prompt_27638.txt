I am taking a course on Buddhist ethics, and our readings have divided into two interpretations of the tradition's normative structure. One camp reads Buddhist ethics as consequentialist: the bodhisattva's compassion is the paradigm, and acts, rules, and practices are justified by their contribution to the welfare of sentient beings. The other reads it as a virtue ethic: the tradition aims primarily at the cultivation of character, and the precepts, the brahmavihāras, and the perfections are excellences and practices of self-transformation rather than instruments for maximizing outcomes.

I would like the document these two camps could both sign if they ran a good-faith adversarial collaboration instead of trading objections. Please draft it in three parts. First, a numbered list of claims both readings can accept — about the texts, about what each camp is and is not committed to, and about which parts of their dispute are merely verbal. Second, a disagreements table in which each residual dispute is stated exactly, with each side's position worded so that side would accept it. Third, the cruxes: the specific sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one camp. For each crux, identify a concrete textual case or argument from the tradition that both camps would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts it will show.

The cruxes should include at least these: whether the bodhisattva's apparent transgressions of the precepts are justified by their consequences or are expressions of an already-cultivated character; whether karmic consequences are best modeled as agent-relative or agent-neutral; and whether the brahmavihāras are attitudes whose scope should be extended without limit toward all sentient beings or dispositions to be cultivated and stabilized. Finally, say how confident you are in the readings and attributions you have given, and single out the point you are least sure of.
