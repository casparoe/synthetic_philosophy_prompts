Write a sestina about the charge that statistical machine translation is shallow: that a system which selects words by statistical fit over a corpus can produce fluent, grammatical output without understanding what it is translating. Choose the six end-words so that they are words a statistical translation engine would be tempted to flatten into a single target-language term, or to swap on the basis of surface frequency alone, but that a careful human translator keeps distinct — words whose correct rendering turns on gender agreement, register, idiom, or the particular sense carried by context. The form should carry the argument rather than merely decorate it. The sestina's fixed permutation of the same six tokens across the stanzas should enact the engine's mechanical recombination of surface forms, while the changing sense those tokens are forced to bear should enact the reply: meaning does not reside in the token but in the understanding that selects it. In the envoi, bring all six words together into a statement that is syntactically clean but semantically hollow, so that the poem closes by performing what fluent-but-empty sounds like. A reader who knows Hofstadter's critique should recognize its target in the poem's argument; a reader who does not should still come away understanding why fluency is not comprehension.
