The eliminative materialism debate about beliefs and desires has been running since the 80s, and honestly it feels like it's been treading water for most of that time. Both sides agree that folk psychology is predictively useful, both agree that neuroscience hasn't yet provided a mature alternative vocabulary, and both agree that some past folk theories (phlogiston, caloric) got eliminated. So why are the Churchlands and their opponents still at loggerheads? My hunch is that the real disagreement is about what counts as evidence *for* a folk theory: eliminativists treat folk psychology's predictive success as a low bar that any good-enough heuristic could clear, while realists treat it as exactly the kind of success that makes a theory likely to be tracking something real. But I'm not sure that's the whole story, and maybe the impasse is deeper — maybe it's a clash about whether the whole enterprise of explaining behaviour in terms of reasons is non-negotiable, in which case no amount of neuroscience could settle it. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? And what would count as progress?
