I keep seeing papers that propose a solution to the Frege–Geach problem and then the next paper points out it breaks on some embedding or other, and I've realised I don't actually have a clear list of what a solution even has to do. Before I read another expressivist semantics, lay out the desiderata for me: what must any adequate expressivist account of the semantics of moral language deliver in order to count as having solved the Frege–Geach problem? I want the full spec — every kind of embedding it has to handle (conditionals, negation, attitude reports, quantifiers, whatever else), every logical property it has to preserve (modus ponens, consistency, logical validity more generally), and anything about the attitude assigned to a moral predicate that has to stay the same whether the sentence is asserted or embedded. For each requirement, give me the motivating case: the actual sentence or inference pattern that forces it, so I can see why it's not negotiable. And flag which requirement has killed the most proposals — I want to know where the graveyard is. If there's a pair of requirements that might be jointly unsatisfiable, say so; that's the part I most want to see.
