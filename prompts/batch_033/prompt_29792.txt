I'm giving a seminar paper next month at a workshop on Plato's political psychology, defending the claim that Plato's position on drunkenness is neither prohibitionist nor permissive: across the Symposium and the Laws, he treats wine as a controlled instrument of moral self-knowledge, valuable only because it deliberately courts the shamelessness and loss of self-control that it is meant to train against. My argument runs: in Laws 1–2 the Athenian Stranger defends the symposium as a controlled test of the soul's disposition, explicitly parallel to the way courage is trained through controlled exposure to fear; the institution's value is instrumental and self-limiting, since the point is the practice of losing and regaining self-control rather than any celebration of intoxication; and the Symposium fits this reading, because its drinking is moderated from the outset and Socrates' endurance is set against Alcibiades' drunken arrival, wine revealing character in both cases.

The audience: mostly specialists in Plato's late dialogues, including two Laws scholars who think the Athenian Stranger's defense of drinking is partly ironic or dialectically tailored to his Spartan and Cretan interlocutors, a Symposium scholar who reads the Alcibiades scene as the dialogue's cautionary demonstration that wine destroys the very self-knowledge it is supposed to reveal, and a historian of Greek sympotic culture who suspects Plato is rationalizing an institution he in fact found morally dangerous. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I will actually get, in roughly the order they will come and ordered by how much damage they would do, each phrased the way this audience would put it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft; what I should concede up front and what I should hold; and the question I am hoping nobody asks. Finally, flag where your own verdict is most likely to fail: which objection's seriousness or place in the ordering a different reading of Laws 1–2 would most plausibly overturn, and what consideration would lead you to reorder the list.
