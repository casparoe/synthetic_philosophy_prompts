I was listening to a philosophy podcast and the host summarized the Epicurean argument about death like this: "Epicurus said death can't be bad for you because you won't be around to experience it — there's no subject to suffer the harm. He backed this up with the symmetry argument: you didn't exist before you were born and that doesn't bother you, so not existing after death shouldn't bother you either. The whole thing is clever but obviously wrong, because death deprives you of years you would have lived, and that's a real loss even if you never feel it. Case closed."

I've heard people reference this argument before but I've never read the original texts. Something about the host's confidence makes me suspicious — like he's flattening a few different moves into one and then knocking down the easiest version. Is this a fair summary? What would someone who actually defends something like the Epicurean position say in response, not just to the deprivation objection but to the way the host sets up the structure of the argument itself?
