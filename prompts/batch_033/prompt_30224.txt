I am a PhD student working on the philosophy of sleep and dreaming, and I am trying to pin down my advisor's position on a question that runs through the Malcolm–Dennett debate: under what conditions does a sleeping subject have an experience? She has given verdicts on a series of cases, and I want help predicting how she will rule on a new one.

Her verdicts so far:

1. Waking visual perception: an experience.
2. A lucid dream in which the subject signals with pre-arranged eye movements while still in REM sleep that she is aware she is dreaming: an experience; she stressed that the signal was produced during the episode, not merely recalled afterward.
3. An ordinary non-lucid dream with a detailed narrative reported immediately on waking: an experience.
4. A sleepwalking episode with complex behaviour but no later recall and no signs of conscious mentation: not an experience.
5. A night terror with screaming and autonomic arousal but no recall and no reportable content: not an experience.
6. Uneventful slow-wave sleep from which the subject wakes with no report and no behavioural sign of mentation: not an experience.
7. Hypnagogic imagery at sleep onset, which the subject can report and was aware of at the time: an experience.

The new case is this: a subject is awakened from an unambiguous REM episode, with dense rapid eye movements, sawtooth-wave EEG, and muscle atonia, of the kind that would ordinarily be expected to yield a dream report, but the subject reports no dream and can describe no mental content. My advisor has not commented on this kind of case.

I am less interested in the bare yes or no than in the rule she seems to be running. What candidate rules fit her verdicts equally well? What does each predict about the new case? Which verdict would you bet on, and why? Finally, which further case would best discriminate between the surviving rules? Candidates I am considering: a report of a vivid dream from stable slow-wave sleep with no REM and none of the usual autonomic signs of dreaming; a dream report that can be shown to incorporate sounds played in the room only after REM had ended; and a subject who, after repeated prompting, can produce only a vague and uncertain report. If some other case would do better, say what it is.
