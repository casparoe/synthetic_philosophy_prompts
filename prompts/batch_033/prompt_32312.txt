I am a novelist at work on a collection of stories about the hidden lives of ideas, and I would like to commission a piece of short fiction from you — say, 1,500 to 2,500 words — that takes seriously the Darwinian theory of culture: the view that cultural units (melodies, slogans, fashions, rituals, jokes, political tropes, fragments of ideology) replicate, mutate, and undergo selection in ways that parallel biological evolution, largely without the awareness or consent of the human minds that serve as their hosts.

The story should treat a single meme not as a metaphor but as something like a protagonist or a parasite with its own career. Track it across a population as it moves from host to host, mutating as it goes, outcompeting rival variants, exploiting features of human psychology it was never designed to understand, and either persisting or dying out according to pressures that feel, from the human point of view, like nothing more than ordinary social life — a conversation, a shared laugh, a moment of persuasion, a half-remembered tune.

What I am after is not allegory. I do not want the meme to be personified as a scheming villain or a whispering devil. I want the story to make the Darwinian logic feel concrete and narratively vivid: the sheer indifference of the selection process, the way fidelity and fecundity and longevity matter and truth and beauty do not, the way a meme can be wildly successful while being false or banal or destructive, the way it can shape human lives as a side effect of its own replication. If you can make this feel unsettling rather than merely clever, so much the better.

You have considerable freedom regarding setting, tone, and narrative voice. The story might be told from the meme's perspective — a kind of alien phenomenology — or from a roving third-person view that flits between hosts, or from the viewpoint of a character who slowly becomes aware of what is happening to her. The meme you choose might be a snatch of melody, a political slogan, a conspiracy theory, a religious formula, a gesture, a fashion, a joke, or something else. The only firm constraint is that the story should genuinely engage with the Darwinian theory of culture as a way of seeing, not merely gesture at it from a distance.
