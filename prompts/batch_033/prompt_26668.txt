so my buddy and i just spent like three hours going back and forth about teleosemantics and whether it can actually handle misrepresentation, and i realized neither of us could keep the positions straight. he'd start out sounding like a straight indicator semanticist, then when i pushed him on the disjunction problem he'd pivot to consumer-based stuff, then when i brought up swampman he'd retreat to something else entirely. by the end i had no idea what he actually believed and honestly im not sure i know what *i* believe either.

so heres what i want: a decision tree that takes someone from the starting commitment "mental states have representational content, and this can be explained in purely naturalistic terms (no primitive intentionality, no unreduced normativity)" and sorts them into a named position. the tree should be built around the hard choices that actually divide people working on this — the disjunction problem, the role of learning history versus current causal relations, whether misrepresentation is the crux or a sideshow, whether teleology is necessary or a red herring, that kind of thing.

each branch node should be a concrete question someone could answer without already knowing which philosopher theyre siding with. like, dont ask "do you accept etiological teleosemantics?" — ask something like "if a neural circuit in a frogs brain was caused by a cosmic-ray accident to fire for small dark moving things in a way that happens to reliably indicate flies, does that circuit mean FLY?" or whatever actually separates the views.

the leaves should include at least: indicator/causal semantics (early Dretske), consumer-based teleosemantics (Millikan, Neander), asymmetric dependence (Fodor), and whatever youd call the view that representational content is a matter of structural isomorphism or mapping relations rather than causal or teleological ones. if there are unoccupied regions of logical space — combinations of answers nobody defends — flag them and say whether you think theyre empty because the position is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored.

also, if there are pairs of positions your tree cant pull apart, say so instead of pretending a distinction where there isnt one.

and look, if the whole framing is off — if "naturalizing content" is a bad way to carve the space, or the disjunction problem isnt actually the right diagnostic — tell me that instead of building a tree on a confused premise. id rather know im asking the wrong question than get a clean answer to a broken one.
