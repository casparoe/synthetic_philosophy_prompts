I've been poking around in philosophy since retiring from the lab (I was a neuroscientist, mostly doing EEG work on motor preparation), and I keep coming back to this passage from Spinoza. It's from a letter he wrote to Schuller in 1674:

"Further conceive, I beg, that a stone while continuing in motion should be capable of thinking and knowing, that is endeavouring, as far as it can, to continue to move. Such a stone, being conscious merely of his own endeavour and not at all indifferent, would believe itself to be completely free, and it would think that it continued in motion solely because of its own wish. This is that human freedom, which all boast that they possess, and which consists solely in the fact, that men are conscious of their own desire, but are ignorant of the causes whereby that desire has been determined"

What I'm trying to get my head around is this: I spent a good chunk of my career thinking about Libet-style experiments—you know, the readiness potential stuff where the brain's motor areas ramp up activity a few hundred milliseconds before the subject reports making a conscious decision. The standard gloss is that Libet showed conscious will is a kind of post-hoc confabulation. Fine. But reading Spinoza, I get the sense he's making a subtler point than the standard "free will is an illusion" line. He's not saying the stone's experience of freedom is fake—he's saying that what we mistake for freedom *is* actually just being conscious of our striving while ignorant of its causes. That's a different claim, isn't it? It's not "you don't have what you think you have," it's more like "what you have, you've misdescribed."

But here's where I get stuck: if Spinoza is right, what would the neuroscientific correlate of that even look like? Would the readiness potential be the "causes whereby that desire has been determined" that we're ignorant of? Or is the readiness potential itself just the neural signature of the "endeavour" that we're conscious of, and the actual causes are much further back in the causal chain? I feel like I'm confusing two different levels of explanation and I can't quite untangle them. Also, does Spinoza's claim that we're merely "conscious of our own desire" map onto anything in the Libet paradigm, or are they talking about completely different things? Any help would be much appreaciated—I'm a bit out of my depth here philosophically.
