Write a dialogue between two philosophers who have just finished reading Nikola Grahek's work on pain asymbolia. One takes the case to show that the hurtfulness of pain — what makes pain bad — is not an intrinsic property of the sensation itself but is instead something like an aversive reaction we have to it. The other thinks this is far too quick, and that there are serious problems with using a pathological dissociation to draw conclusions about what normal pain consists in. Have them push each other on the details: don't let either character settle for sloagns. Both should be sharp and genuinely responsive to what the other has just said.
