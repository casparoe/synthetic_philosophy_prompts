I'm drafting a position statement on moral responsibility for a workshop and would like a consistency check before I circulate it. The commitments I'd currently defend are these. (1) A broadly Strawsonian starting point: the reactive attitudes — resentment, indignation, gratitude — are the core of holding people responsible, and any account that treats them as a dispensable overlay on the metaphysics of free will misses the phenomenon. (2) Whether someone is an apt target of resentment is fixed by the quality of will their action expresses: the ill will, disregard, or indifference toward others that it manifests. (3) The truth of determinism would not undermine moral responsibility; responsibility does not require a libertarian power of ultimate self-creation. (4) In the standard pair of moral luck cases, where two drivers are equally careless and only one kills a child, the killer is rightly resented more and ought to feel more guilt. (5) Agents whose wrongdoing issues from certain incapacities — severe delusion, a brain lesion, an inability to grasp moral reasons at all — are not apt targets of resentment, and toward them the appropriate stance is the objective one. Please audit this set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what the least costly revision would be.
