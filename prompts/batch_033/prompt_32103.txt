Construct a thought experiment in a realistic, non-geometric domain — policy, medicine, finance, everyday reasoning — where the principle of indifference generates at least two different probability estimates for the same question, purely because the unknown is described in two equally natural but different ways. Both parameterizations must be genuinely defensible, not one obviously correct and the other contrived. Keep the scenario to roughly one paragraph.

After the scenario: (1) walk through the indifference-based reasoning for each parameterization and show the arithmetic that yields each probability; (2) isolate the feature of the case that makes the answers diverge — what is the mathematical relationship between the two parameterizations, and why does it break the indifference principle? (3) what happens if you remove that feature — do the two answers collapse into one? (4) give the best defense a proponent of the principle of indifference could offer to escape the paradox in this specific case — could they privilege one parameterization, or appeal to a maximum-entropy prior? Offer the strongest version of that defense, then your honest assessment of whether it succeeds.
