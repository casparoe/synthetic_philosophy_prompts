I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind and I've been going deep on Integrated Information Theory lately — Tononi's framework where consciousness just is the amount of integrated information (Φ) in a system. I get the basic machinery: the postulates, the Φ calculation, the idea that even a photodiode has a tiny sliver of consciousness. What I'm trying to do is figure out how much work IIT can actually do when you take it out of the thought-experiment sandbox and apply it to a real, messy case.

The case I keep coming back to is Terri Schiavo. You probably know the outlines: she collapsed in 1990, was diagnosed as being in a persistent vegetative state, and then for fifteen years there was this brutal legal and political battle between her husband (who wanted the feeding tube removed, saying she wouldn't have wanted to live like that) and her parents (who insisted she was responsive, conscious, being starved to death). The case went through Florida courts, the state legislature, Congress, even the White House got involved. Eventually the feeding tube was removed and she died in 2005; the autopsy confirmed massive and irreversible brain damage.

Here's what I want to do: analyze the Schiavo case through the lens of IIT. What does the theory say about what was actually going on with her, and what does it say about what should have been done? And then run the analysis the other way: does the case expose any cracks in IIT — places where the theory's distinctions (like the exact threshold of Φ that matters for moral status, or the relationship between Φ and the capacity for future recovery) get strained by the realities of a clinical case that also had legal, political, and religious dimensions? I'm especially interested in the fact that IIT gives a graded answer (some Φ, not zero, probably) but the practical and legal questions demand a binary one (pull the plug or don't), and in whether IIT's proponents have a principled way to bridge that gap or whether they're just helping themselves to ethical assumptions the theory itself doesn't supply. I'd like a committed take — not a survey of possible views, but a definite position on what IIT can and can't handle here, and what the case teaches us about the theory's limits.
