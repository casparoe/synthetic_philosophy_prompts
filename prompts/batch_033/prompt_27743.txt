I'm trying to work out what I actually think about moral worth — when an action reflects well on the agent in the distinctive way that makes us praise the person, not just the deed. Here are my verdicts on a set of cases.

1. The prudent shopkeeper gives correct change and charges fair prices, but only because honesty is good for business. I don't think his honesty has any moral worth.

2. The devoted friend visits her sick friend every day because she loves her and can't stand to see her suffer. I think this is morally worthy — as worthy as anything.

3. The reluctant duty-doer also visits the sick friend, but out of a sense that this particular person's need and their history together make it something he owes her; he finds it exhausting and would rather not go. I think this is morally worthy too.

4. The self-promoter stays late to help a colleague finish a report purely so the boss will see her as a team player. I don't think this is morally worthy.

5. The mistaken helper helps someone escape an unjust punishment because he cares about the person, even though he genuinely believes he is doing something wrong. I think this is morally worthy.

6. The lucky prescriber prescribes a drug because she believes it will cure her patient, and it does cure, and curing is exactly what makes the prescription right — but her belief rests on a misread chart, so she is right only by luck. I don't think this is morally worthy, or at most much less worthy than the same prescription made on sound medical judgment.

7. The rule-follower helps a neighbor move because it is the right thing to do, full stop; he has no particular concern for this neighbor's needs and, asked what makes helping right, he can only say that it is his duty. I think this is morally worthy, though it leaves me cold.

The case I keep going back and forth on: someone returns a lost wallet both because it is the right thing to do and because there is a reward; he is confident he would return it even without the reward, and the rightness genuinely moves him, but the reward is also genuinely part of his motivation.

Is there a single principle that captures my verdicts on 1–7, or close? If not, which principle gets the most of them with the least strain, which verdict does it force me to drop, and what does it say about the wallet case?

Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five candidate principles, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should be one or two sentences: state the principle precisely and say which of my verdicts (1–7) it cannot accommodate. Make the fifth item the principle you think I should keep, together with the verdict it forces me to overturn (if any) and its verdict on the wallet case.
