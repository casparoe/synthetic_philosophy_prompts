I'm part of a reading group that's been working through the moral worth literature for the past year — we've read the classic Kant, some Arpaly, Markovits, a bit of the recent back-and-forth. Two members of the group have gotten into this really productive but intense disagreement and they've agreed to try an adversarial collaboration.

One of them (let's call her the duty-first view) holds that an action has moral worth only when it's done from the motive of duty: you do the right thing because you recognise it as the right thing, full stop. If you help someone because you feel sympathy and it just so happens to be the right thing, your action might be correct but it doesn't have moral worth — it's not praiseworthy in the distinctive way that matters. The other one (the reasons-responsiveness view) thinks this is way too restrictive. On his account, what matters is whether you're responding to the reasons that make the action right in the first place. If compassion is what makes helping the right thing to do, then acting from compassion gives your action moral worth, even if you never form the thought "this is my duty." You don't need the concept of duty to be praiseworthy.

They've been at this for months and I suspect there partly talking past each other — maybe they mean different things by "moral worth," maybe they're using different cases to pump different intuitions. They want to produce a joint document that nails down what they actually agree on, where exactly they diverge, what the cruxes are, and for each crux a concrete case both accept as relevant with each person's prediction written down in advance. The idea is that if we could run a study or at least get clear on what would move each of them, we'd make progress instead of just rehashing the same moves.

Can you draft that document? Format it as: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, (2) a disagreements table with columns for the point of disagreement stated neutrally, each side's preferred formulation, the crux sub-question, and a case or test both agree bears on it plus their predictions. If you think any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, flag that — they'd genuinely want to know.
