I'm on my school's student council and there's this pattern I keep noticing that I don't have a word for. Last month we were deciding whether to hold the spring dance outside. Our rule was that we'd do it outside only if two conditions were met: the forecast had to be good, and the school had to give permission. We voted on the conditions seperately. "Is the forecast good?" passed 2–1. "Do we have permission?" also passed 2–1. Then we voted on the actual proposal, "hold the dance outside," and it failed 2–1. Nobody changed their mind between votes, and when I checked everyone's answers were totally consistent on their own. The two people who voted no on the proposal each supported one of the conditions but not the other, and the one person who supported both conditions got outvoted on the proposal. So the group said yes to both parts and no to the whole thing, even though every individual was being logical.

The same thing happened in environmental club: we agreed to buy a compost bin only if we had the funds and the cafeteria agreed to use it. Both parts had majority support, but the actual purchase got voted down, again with nobody changing their mind. I know about the Condorcet paradox where majority preferences can cycle, but this dosen't feel like that, since nobody's ranking options—it's just yes/no votes on conditions and then on the thing that follows from them. Is there a name for this? And I don't just want the label: I want to know what exactly is going on, how it's different from ordinary majority-rule weirdness, and whether it means voting on conditions separately is a bad idea.
