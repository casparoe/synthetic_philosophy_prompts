I am a novelist assembling a collection of short stories about people who live, often uneasily, inside the concepts of decision theory. I would like you to write the title story, and I want it built around two thought experiments rather than merely mentioning them: counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail. Here is the setting I have in mind. In a near-future city, a small but reputable industry of predictive agencies actually delivers what the thought experiments promise; their predictions are certified accurate, their counterfactual contracts are enforceable, and their letters arrive with the quiet authority of a court summons. My protagonist, Maris, runs a two-person inheritance-arbitrage firm. One morning she receives a letter from one of these agencies: a coin has already been flipped; it came up tails, so the agency is now asking her for a modest fee. Had it come up heads, it would have paid her a life-changing sum — but only because it predicted that, in the tails branch, she would have paid the fee. The fee is due now, in the branch where the benefit never arrives. That same week, her business partner receives a different letter, telling him it was sent if and only if exactly one of two things is true: either his house is infested with termites, or he will pay a thousand dollars. Everyone in the industry insists this second letter is not blackmail, though it is hard to explain to a layperson why not.

Please write the story. I want the plot to turn on what these characters owe to versions of themselves in branches of the world that never happened, and on the difference between being moved by evidence and being moved by causes. Give it a proper ending, not a debate in dialogue. After the story, add a brief author's note — a single paragraph — in which you flag where the story takes liberties with the underlying decision theories or leans on a contested reading of them; in particular, tell me which objection to the story's implicit verdict would most tempt you to rewrite it, and where a causal decision theorist would think Maris has simply been swindled. Please write in American English.
