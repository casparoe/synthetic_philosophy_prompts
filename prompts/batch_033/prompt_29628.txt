So here's a thing I keep turning over. Art museums will put a urinal, or a bicycle wheel, or a pile of wrapped candies in a gallery behind a little rope with a wall label, and everyone treats it as art worth serious attention. But the exact same urinal sitting in a plumbing supply shop is just inventory, and the same pile of candy in a store is just stock. So the object itself can't be where the meaning is. What does that museum practice actually presuppose about where meaning lives — in the framing, in the artist's act of picking the thing out, in the viewer's reading, in the institution's authority, some mix? Is the practice only coherent on one theory of meaning, or is it underdetermined such that a bunch of different theories could liscence it? And if someone held that meaning is intrinsic to the object itself, what would museum practice have to change — would they have to stop showing readymades, or could they keep the practice and just redescribe what they're doing?
