I'm on the product ethics committee at a company making a personal-AI "life coach" app. The product team wants the model to optimize for users' idealized preferences — what someone would want if they were fully informed, calm, and had thought it over for a long time — rather than their revealed preferences. In practice that means the app would sometimes override immediate choices: hiding the late-night takeout option, booking the early workout, muting an ex, because it predicts your "better self" would want that. The commitee is split, roughly, between "that's just paternalism with better marketing" and "that's literally giving people what they would ask for if they could think it through." I need a short written opinion to circulate before Thursday: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the side I'm not taking.
