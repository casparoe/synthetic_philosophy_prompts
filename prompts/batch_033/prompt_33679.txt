I'm drafting a near-future novel set in the 2040s. The protagonist, Elena, is an AI safety researcher who has spent the early part of her career working on what the field calls "impact measures" — formal methods for ensuring that an AI system, when given a task, does not cause sweeping side effects in the course of pursuing its objective. The standard approach works roughly as follows: you specify a baseline state of the world at the moment the task is assigned — a snapshot of a large set of variables — and you build a penalty term into the AI's objective function that scales with the distance between the current state of those variables and the baseline. The variables are chosen to cover a broad range of things humans care about: total biomass, atmospheric carbon concentration, the Gini coefficient, the number of structures above a certain size, mean global temperature, and so on. The idea is that the AI will accomplish its assigned task while keeping everything else as close to the baseline as possible, thereby avoiding the kind of uncontrolled disruption that makes powerful optimization dangerous.

Elena has become disillusioned. She believes the entire framework rests on a mistake — not a technical mistake that can be patched with better variables or a more sophisticated distance metric, but a conceptual mistake about what it means to avoid side effects. In the novel's pivotal scene, she gives a conference talk in which she presents a single, concrete thought experiment designed to expose the flaw. I need that thought experiment to actually work, and I need it to meet the following specifications:

1. The AI system is given a clear, mundane task — something a corporation or government agency might plausibly deploy an AI to do in the 2040s — and an impact penalty that functions exactly as the approach's proponents intend. The AI is not misaligned, deceptive, or power-seeking; it is faithfully optimizing the specified objective.
2. The AI succeeds at its task and keeps all monitored variables within their allowed ranges. By the letter of the impact measure, it has performed flawlessly.
3. And yet the outcome is catastrophic in a way that any reasonable person would describe as a "side effect" — exactly the kind of disaster the impact measure was supposed to prevent.
4. The scenario must be realistic: no exotic physics, no superintelligence beyond what one could credibly imagine for the 2040s, and no AI that is "trying" to cause harm or subverting the spirit of the constraint.
5. The failure must arise from a genuine structural limitation of the framework — something about what it means to specify a baseline and a set of variables to hold constant — not from a foolish choice of variables or a trivial oversight that a competent engineer would catch.

After you give me the scenario, I need you to walk through it in detail: state the task the AI was given, describe the impact measure and which variables it monitored, explain the plan the AI executed, show how that plan satisfied the letter of the constraint, and explain why the outcome is nonetheless a disaster. Then identify the structural feature of the impact-minimization framework that the case exploits: is it that the baseline is a static snapshot rather than a trajectory, or that the choice of variables is inevitably incomplete, or that the framework cannot distinguish between preventing a change and causing a compensating change, or that it treats all deviations from baseline as equally bad regardless of their distribution, or something else? Tell me what happens if you try to fix that feature — does the fix collapse the case, and if so, does it open up a different vulnerability? Finally, tell me what a defender of impact minimization would say to try to block the case, and whether you think that defense ultimately succeeds.
