I'm a journalist on the science and ideas beat and I've been going down a rabbit hole on decision theory and rationality for a feature I'm working on. Along the way I've picked up a set of commitments and I want to know if they actually hang together before I start putting anything in print.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) In Parfit's hitchhiker — you're stranded in the desert, a driver stops and offers to rescue you but only if you promise to pay him a thousand bucks once you're safe, and you both know that once you're home you'll have zero self-interested reason to pay and the driver won't be able to find you — I think you should form a genuine intention to pay and then actually follow through. Not fake the intention; really intend it and really pay.

(2) I think it can be perfectly rational to cultivate dispositions — like being the sort of person who keeps promises, or who retaliates even at a cost — when having those dispositions secures benefits (cooperation, trust, deterrence) you couldn't get otherwise. This feels almost obvious to me.

(3) But I also think akrasia — acting against your own best judgment at the moment of action — is basically the paradigm case of a rational failure. If you judge that A is what you should do, all things considered, and then you do B, you've messed up, full stop.

(4) I'm drawn to the view that an action's rational status depends on the reasons you have at the time you act — not on promises you made or intentions you formed in the past, except insofar as those show up as present reasons in the moment.

(5) The fact that your life would go worse overall if you lacked a certain disposition doesn't, by itself, give you a reason to follow through on what that disposition demands in a particular case where doing so is costly and the other party can't retaliate.

I can feel some of these pulling against each other but I'm having trouble sorting out which pairs are genuinely in tension, which ones only seem like they are, and how severe the real conflicts are. What I'd love is an audit: rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to give to restore consistency, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
