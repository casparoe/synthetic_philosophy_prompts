I've been reading around in the Humeanism vs anti-Humeanism debate about laws of nature and honestly I'm getting frustated. It feels like the same moves have been getting made for decades and nobody's changed their mind.

Quick sketch of the terrain: Humeans (Lewis-style Best Systems Account, but also the newer wave with stuff like the Better Best System Account) say that laws of nature just *are* the axioms of the deductive system that best balances simplicity and informativeness in describing the Humean mosaic — the total distribution of local, non-modal facts. Laws describe regularities; they don't produce them. Anti-Humeans (Armstrong's necessitation relations between universals, Maudlin's primitivism about laws, the dispositional essentialists) say no — laws are something over and above the regularities. They govern, they constrain, they have genuine modal oomph.

Both camps agree on the physics. They agree on what actually happens. The disagreement is about what makes it the case that those are the laws, and what the laws *are*.

The standard arguments are also well-rehearsed. Humeans say: we don't need spooky governing relations, parsimony favors us, and the anti-Humean's extra ontology does no explanatory work. Anti-Humeans say: Humeanism can't distinguish laws from accidental regularities in a principled way, it gets the counterfactuals wrong, and it can't explain why induction is reasonable. Then the Humeans reply with stuff about how the Best Systems Account *does* distinguish laws from accidents because laws are the axioms of the system that would win the competition for best balance, and that's not an accidental feature. And back and forth it goes.

My working theory is that this is a disagreement about what an explanation is supposed to do. The Humean wants to explain the modal (laws, counterfactuals, dispositions) in terms of the non-modal (the mosaic). The anti-Humean thinks that's exactly backwards — you can't get modality out of non-modal ingredients, so modality has to be somewhere in the fundamental furniture. If that's the diagnosis, then the debate is really about explanatory priorities or bedrock metaphysical taste, and their's no fact of the matter to be discovered.

But I could be wrong. Maybe there's a formal result lurking somewhere (about the expressive power of Best Systems, or about whether governing laws do any extra work in a completed physics) that could actually settle this. Or maybe the two sides are answering different questions under the same label — one asking "what's the most parsimonious account of laws consistent with the physics?" and the other asking "what grounds the modal structure of reality?"

So diagnose this thing. What kind of disagreement is it really? Is it substantive, verbal, a clash of bedrock intuitions? And what would progress look like — or should we just admit it's a philosophical taste war and move on?
