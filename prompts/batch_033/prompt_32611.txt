I've been reading Le Ton beau de Marot and I'm trying to get clear on what a theory of translation under formal constraint actually has to account for. Not interested in yet another taxonomy of translation types (domesticating vs. foreignizing, etc.) — I want the desiderata: what any adequate theory of how to translate a formally constrained text (rhyme, meter, fixed form, puns, sound patterning) must get right. Hofstadter's book is basically a 600-page worked example with the little Marot poem (the "Ma mignonne" one), so use that as the running case. I want the requirments list — the phenomena it has to explain, the distinctions it can't collapse, the cases it must handle — with the reason each is a requirement. Flag any that are jointly unsatisfiable (my suspiscion is that semantic fidelity and formal fidelity can't both be fully preserved, but maybe that's too crude). Also mark which desiderata are negotiable (preferences of one school of translation) and which are non-negotiable. If you think some of the standard demands are actually red herrings — things people think a theory must do but that turn out not to be genuine requirements — say so and why. Keep the weight on the criteria, not on scoring contenders, though a sentence or two on how well the main camps (formal-equivalence, dynamic-equivalence, functionalist, etc.) fare against the hardest requirement is fine.
