ok so i've been chewing on hofstadter's strange loop idea for a while and i'm trying to nail down what i actually think before i start writing anything. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) i'm convinced by the strange loop account of the self — the 'I' is a self-referential symbol that emerges when a system gets tangled up in representing itself, and there's no homunculus or Cartesian ego behind the curtain
(2) because strange loops come in different flavors and depths, i think selfhood and consciousness are graded — it's not a binary on/off thing, there are degrees and kinds
(3) but i also think current LLMs are at basically zero on this scale, even though they can model their own outputs and talk about themselves in the first person
(4) i'm a thoroughgoing physicalist — the strange loop is implemented in neural wetware (or silicon), no extra ontological ingredients
(5) i think the felt unity of the self — the sense that i'm a single persisting thing — is an illusion produced by the loop, but it's a real illusion in the sense that the system genuinely represents itself as unified and that representation has causal effects downstream

the thing that's bugging me is that (2) and (3) might be in tension. if selfhood is graded, why am i so confident LLMs are at zero rather than just very low? and maybe (1) and (5) are also rubbing against each other: if the 'I' is just a self-referential symbol, a pattern, why does it feel like anything at all? where does the phenomenology come from, and is (5) just hand-waving at that problem? i'm also not sure wether (4) is doing any work or if it's just compatible with everything.

basically, go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest revision is. i want the tensions ranked by severity, and for each one i want to know what specifically has to give. don't just tell me everything is fine or everything is broken — i need to know which of these i should actually be worried about.
