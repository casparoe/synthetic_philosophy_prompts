I'm taking a philosophy of probability course this semester and we just spent two weeks on Bertrand's paradox and the principle of indifference. I'm honestly a bit obsessed with it. For our final project we can do something creative, and I had this idea: I want to make a "which response to Bertrand's paradox do you actually hold?" quiz — like one of those BuzzFeed-style things but actually rigorous. The idea is to sort people by what they *do* when they encounter the paradox, not by which label they've heard of.

Here's what I'm picturing: maybe 7 or 8 questions, each presenting a variant of the paradox or a related scenario (like the wine/water paradox, or the cube factory problem, or just a new case where indifference gives different answers depending on how you parameterize). Each question would have 2-3 answer options describing what someone would actually say or do. At the end, a scoring key maps answer patterns onto positions. The positions I want as leaves are: (a) the Jaynes-style invariance approach — indifference is fine if you apply it to the right partition using symmetry constraints, (b) the "indifference is just broken" view — the paradox is a refutation and we should abandon the principle, (c) the subjectivist view — the paradox just shows that probability is relative to background knowledge and the problem is underspecified, (d) the frequentist view — the paradox dissolves once you specify a physical chance mechanism, and (e) maybe a "geometric probability is just a mess" view that thinks the whole project of assigning probabilities to continuous spaces without a natural measure is misguided.

I want the questions to actually force people to show their hand. No question should just ask "do you think the principle of indifference is valid?" — instead, give them a case and see what they *do*. And if there are combinations of answers that no real position occupies, I want those noted in the key (like "if you answered this way, you might be inconsistent" or "nobody really defends this combination because...").

Also, we read van Fraassen's discussion of the cube factory problem, so I'd love at least one question that uses that case. And could you flag any positions your tree can't cleanly separate from each other?
