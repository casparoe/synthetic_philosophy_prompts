Write a joke about Gödel's modal ontological argument. The joke should be funny specifically to someone who has actually worked through the proof, and its punchline should turn on a precise technical feature of the argument rather than on the vague idea that philosophers define God into existence. Suitable features include the S5 principle that possible necessary existence entails necessary existence, the treatment of positive properties as closed under entailment, the definition of God-likeness as possession of all positive properties, or the axiom that necessary existence is itself a positive property. Whichever feature you choose, the joke's internal logic must respect how the argument actually works: a reader who knows the proof should recognize that the punchline exploits a real step or commitment of the proof, not merely its reputation. Write the joke in continuous prose, as in a letter, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
