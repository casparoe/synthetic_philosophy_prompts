It's about the regress problem for justification. Here's what it says (paraphrasing from memory, but I think I've got it right):

"Here's the problem: a belief is only justified if you have a reason for it. But then that reason is another belief, which also needs a reason, and so on. So you get a chain of reasons that has to go somewhere. There are really only three options. One, the chain goes on forever — but nobody takes that seriously, because an infinite chain of reasons could never actually justify anything. Two, it stops at some foundational beliefs that are self-evident or incorrigible, like 'I exist' or '2+2=4.' That's foundationalism, and it's been the standard view since Descartes. Three, the chain loops around so that beliefs support each other in a circle. That's coherentism, but it's basically circular reasoning, so it's a nonstarter. Philosophers these days mostly argue about what the foundations are, not whether foundationalism is right."
