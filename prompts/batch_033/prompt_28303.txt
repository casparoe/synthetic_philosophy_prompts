I've been noticing something in discussions about skepticism and I can't figure out if there's a name for it. It's about a certain kind of belief that doesn't behave like normal beliefs. Take something like "the world wasn't created five minutes ago with the appearance of age." I can't prove that's false — any evidence I point to (fossils, memories, historical records) already assumes it's false. But at the same time, I can't seriously doubt it either. It's not that I have overwhelming evidence for it; it's more that the whole activity of weighing evidence seems to depend on it being true. If I tried to genuinely doubt it, I wouldn't know how to go about investigating anything else either — it's like the doubt would eat the whole framework.

I've noticed there are quite a few beliefs like this: "other people have minds," "my memory is broadly reliable," "I have two hands." They're not immune to revision in principle — I can imagine discovering I'd lost a hand — but the more fundamental ones seem to be in a different category. You can't argue for them without using them, and you can't argue against them without using them either. They seem to function as a kind of background that makes inquiry possible rather than as conclusions of inquiry. I keep tripping over this in epistemology discussions because people treat them as if they're just very confident empirical beliefs, but that feels like a category mistake.

Is there an established term for beliefs of this sort, and for the epistemological picture that treats them as special? I've come across "basic beliefs" in foundationalism and "properly basic beliefs" in Reformed epistemology, but those seem to be about beliefs that are justified without inference — whereas what I'm describing are beliefs that seem to resist the whole justification game, not because they're self-justifying but because they're presupposed by the practice of justifying anything. I'm less interested in the label than in whether there's a worked-out account of what makes these different from ordinary basic beliefs, where exactly the line is between one of these framework beliefs and a merely very-well-entrenched belief, and whether the account has anything to say about what we should do when people apparently don't share our framework beliefs.
