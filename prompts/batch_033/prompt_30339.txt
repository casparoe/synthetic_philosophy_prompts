As a programmer I keep seeing the Maxwell's demon thing explained as: the demon can't actually violate the second law because eventually it has to erase its memory, and Landauer says erasing a bit costs a minimum amount of energy. But I don't get why erasing a bit should cost anything thermodynamically. In software, setting a bit to zero is just a state change, the old value doesn't have to go anywhere, and information feels abstract rather than physical. So what if the demon just never erases anything and keeps an ever-growing log of every mesurement it makes? Then there's no erasure cost, and it looks like you get a perpetual motion machine that runs on information. Is the standard resolution just sneaking in the assumption that the demon has finite memory? What exactly breaks if the demon has an unbounded log, and is the erasure cost a real physical thing or more like a bookkeeping convention?
