The literature on the distribution of consciousness across the animal kingdom has struck me lately as a debate that has largely stopped moving. On one side are those who hold that phenomenal consciousness depends on something like the integrated, recurrent processing found in mammalian and avian brains, and who therefore read the behavioral sophistication of fish, cephalopods, and insects as the operation of complex but unconscious mechanisms. On the other side are those who take broadly the same findings — fish that show nociceptive responses, learn to avoid noxious stimuli, and alter their behavior after analgesics; bees that generalize across contexts and adjust to novel contingencies; octopuses that solve multi-step problems with a nervous system distributed through their arms — as evidence that a line has been drawn far too high, and that the burden of proof has been quietly shifted so that creatures must earn the attribution of consciousness rather than being granted it by default.

That the disagreement persists despite decades of comparative neuroanatomy, behavioral ecology, and neurophysiology suggests to me that it is not, at bottom, an ordinary empirical dispute that further data would settle. Both sides seem to agree on a great deal of the data; they disagree about what the data show. My working hypothesis is that the real disagreement is about what should count as evidence for consciousness in a non-linguistic creature, and beneath that, about whether 'conscious' is a threshold concept — something a species either has or lacks — or a graded, multidimensional set of capacities that different taxa possess in different combinations and to different degrees.

I would like a diagnosis of the structure of this disagreement rather than a verdict on who is right. Is it a factual disagreement wearing a conceptual disguise, a methodological disagreement about standards of evidence, a conceptual disagreement about the target notion, or some combination? Ground the diagnosis in what each side actually argues, and then say what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a distinction, a class of experiment, a formal framework, a changed question — or whether the impasse indicates that the question itself is ill-posed.
