1. Community A: Speaker says "gavagai" whenever a rabbit scoots by. M says the word means rabbit, the whole animal—not undetached rabbit parts, not temporal rabbit-stages. Standard Quinean move, no surprises.

2. Community B: Speaker says what translates literally as "the mountain is angry, that's why it erupted." M doesn't think they're attributing emotions to geology. She renders "angry" as something closer to "unstable" or "volatile." So she's reinterpreting rather than attributing a false belief about mountain psychology.

3. Community C: Speaker seems to assert both "the ancestors demand a sacrifice" and "the ancestors don't demand a sacrifice," in different ritual contexts. M says there's no contradiction: "ancestors" in the first context means literal deceased kin, in the second it means something like malevolent nature spirits—two distinct categories our word "ancestors" would collapse.

4. Community D: Counting system is "one, two, many." M says this isn't defective; it's a different conceptual scheme, and speakers are perfectly rational within it. "Many" doesn't map onto any integer.

5. Community E: Speaker says "witches cause illness." M says nope, this one's just a false belief. "Witch" means roughly what we'd mean, "cause" means cause, and the speaker is wrong about disease etiology. No reinterpretation.

Now she's doing fieldwork with Community F, and I want to predict her call on this: a speaker says, in contexts that look like straightforward assertion, "when a person dies, their soul leaves the body and enters a nearby animal."

Here's what I'm after. First, predict her verdict: is this a Community B situation (reinterpret "soul" or "leaves" or "enters" so the claim isn't straightfowardly false), a Community E situation (same meanings, false belief, move on), or a Community D situation (different conceptual scheme entirely, not even truth-evaluable by our lights)? Second, what's the rule she's running that sorts these five cases—is it "maximize truth unless the speaker would be incoherent," or "preserve literal meaning unless the belief is a category mistake," or something else entirely? And third, what's the one further case I should bring up to settle which rule it actually is?
