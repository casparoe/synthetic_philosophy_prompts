Write a dialogue between two moral philosophers who disagree about the procreation asymmetry. The first defends the asymmetry: the absence of pain in a person who is never created counts in favour of not creating that person, whereas the absence of pleasure in a person who is never created does not count against not creating that person. The second rejects this, maintaining that the two cases must be treated alike and that the alleged asymmetry therefore lacks a principled basis. Each character should be equally competent and should respond to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past one another. The dialogue should engage with the apparent datum that we may have reason not to create a person whose life would be full of suffering but no corresponding reason to create a person whose life would be full of happiness, and with the question of whether a merely possible person can be harmed or benefited by being brought into or kept out of existence. Do not make either character a strawman. Write the dialogue in British English.
