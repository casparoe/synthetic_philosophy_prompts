I've been thinking about Shubik's dollar auction game and its value as a model for real-world escalation of commitment. The setup, for anyone who hasn't encountered it: an auctioneer sells a dollar bill to the highest bidder, but the second-highest bidder also pays their bid and gets nothing. Once two players have entered, the runner-up faces a choice between bidding higher to avoid losing their sunk cost, or dropping out and eating the loss. The game famously escalates well past the dollar's face value, with both players ending up worse off than if they had never started.

I've seen the dollar auction used as a loose analogy for the Vietnam War, but I want to go deeper than the usual hand-waving. Take the U.S. escalation from the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution in 1964 through Nixon's "peace with honor" strategy. I want a careful mapping: who are the players in the auction — is it just the U.S. and North Vietnam, or does the game break down when you try to fit the Viet Cong, South Vietnam, China, and the USSR into it? What exactly is the dollar being bid for — credibility, South Vietnam, stopping the dominoes? And what do the bids correspond to — troop deployments, bombing campaigns, domestic political capital? Walk me through the escalation step by step and show me, at each key decision point, where the dollar-auction logic explains what happened and where it fails to.

Then I want the other direction: what does this episode reveal about the dollar auction as a model? The Pentagon Papers showed that decision-makers privately doubted the war was winnable while publicly escalating — that's not supposed to happen in the standard game, where both players at least think the prize has positive value. The U.S. also had the option to simply declare victory and leave, which isn't a move in the auction. And the "credibility" argument — that the U.S. had to keep fighting to maintain its reputation with allies and adversaries — introduces a payoff structure the dollar auction doesn't capture: the prize isn't just the object of the auction but the signal it sends to third parties. Does the model survive these mismatches, or do they break it? If you had to design a variant of the dollar auction that better captures the dynamics of Vietnam, what would you change?
