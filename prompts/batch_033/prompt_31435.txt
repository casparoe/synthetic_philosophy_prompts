I'm in a philosophy of language seminar and one of the grad students keeps deploying the slogan "to say that p is true is just to say that p" as if it's a conversation-ender. Last week someone asked whether mathematical truths and empirical truths are true in the same sense, and he shot it down with "that question doesn't even arise — to say a mathematical claim is true is just to assert the claim, same as for any other claim." I'm pretty sure that slogan can be cashed out in a bunch of different ways, from something so trivial no one would dispute it to something that would radically reshape how we think about truth, and only some of those readings actually support his dismissal. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan might be making, say which are boringly true, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false, and tell me which ones would actually license brushing off the question about mathematical vs empirical truth? I want a menu of readings I can bring to the next session.
