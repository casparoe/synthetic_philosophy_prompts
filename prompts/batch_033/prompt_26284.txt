I teach a graduate seminar on Rawls and his critics. For the final take-home exam, I need one essay question on the role of religious reasons in public justification. I want a question that cannot be answered by reciting the ideal of public reason or the proviso. Instead, it should force students to confront the asymmetry objection: why must citizens who hold religious comprehensive doctrines translate their views into public reasons, when citizens who hold secular comprehensive doctrines — utilitarianism, Kantian deontology, Marxism — face no parallel requirement, since their views already speak in the vocabulary of public values? A student who has only memorized Rawls's taxonomy will not see why this objection is harder to answer than it looks. Please write the question, a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme that assigns 20 marks across five must‑hit points. For each point, include a one‑line note on the characteristic mistake candidates make when they miss it.
