I'm a novelist working on a book where the central character is trying to decide whether to have a child, and the whole question of the procreation asymmetry keeps coming up in my research. I've read enough to know there are people who defend it and people who think it's a mistake, but I can't tell how much of the fight is real and how much is people talking past each other. So here's what I want: draft the joint statement that a defender of the procreation asymmetry and a critic of it would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The procreation asymmetry, as I understand it, is roughly: you have no obligation to bring a happy person into existence, but you do have an obligation not to bring a miserable person into existence. The defender says this captures a deep moral intuition; the critic says it's inconsistent or leads to weird conclusions. I've also seen it framed in terms of reasons — that the absence of a happy person isn't bad, but the presence of a suffering person is — and honestly I'm not sure if the obligation version and the reasons version are even the same claim.

I want the document to have: a numbered list of claims both sides can sign (about the intuitions, about what follows from them, about what existing arguments have shown and haven't shown); then a table of disagreements where each side's position is stated in wording they'd accept; then the cruxes — the sub-questions that, if resolved, would actually move someone off their position; and for each crux, what kind of case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and what each side predicts about it.

I'm especially interested in whether the dispute turns out to be partly verbal — like, are they using "obligation" or "reason" or "bad" differently — and if so, which parts of it. The character I'm writing is someone who feels the intuitive pull of the asymetry but can't tell if it stands up to scrutiny, and I want the adversarial-collaboration document to be the thing she'd read to figure out where she actually stands.
