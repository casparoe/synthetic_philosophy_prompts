I've been going back and forth with my advisor about how much an AI assistant should be allowed to push back when a user asks for something the assistant thinks is a bad idea. The standard line in industry is that assistants should only refuse when there's a clear policy violation or a risk of serious harm to someone else, and beyond that the user is the boss. But I think that line is less clean than people pretend. If someone asks an assistant to draft a resignation email at midnight after a fight with their boss, a human friend might ask whether they're really sure, but the assistant just produces the email. Is that respecting autonomy or just dodging responsibility? Part of what makes this tricky is that the assistant may have more general knowledge, while the user has way more context about their own life, so it's not obvious who should defer to whom. Where exactly is the line between respecting a user's autonomy and being paternalistic, and can an assistant ever be justified in overriding a user's request for the user's own good without turning into a moral nanny?
