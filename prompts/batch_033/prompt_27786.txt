I was an entomologist for about 35 years — mostly social insects, some neurobiology. Retired now and finally have time to read philosophy. Here's my problem.

There are something like 10^18 insects alive at any given moment. If I assign even a tiny probability to individual insects having valenced experience — actual suffering or pleasure — the expected quantity of insect suffering becomes cosmically large, easily swamping everything else we might care about. So my credence on insect sentience really matters for what I think about, well, basically everything ethically.

But I find myself totally stuck on what number to put on it. I've read enough to know the arguments cut both ways: insects show surprisingly complex learning (honeybees can learn abstract concepts, fruit flies show something that looks a lot like attention), but their brains are orders of magnitude smaller than ours and organized completely differently; some theories of consciousness require thalamocortical loops or similar structures they just don't have, others are much more permissive. I can talk myself into 0.01 and I can talk myself into 0.8 depending on what I read last.

So: what's your credence that at least some insects (let's say hymenoptera and diptera as the strongest candidates) have conscious states with a positive or negative valence — that there's something it's like to be them and it can go well or badly? Give me a number and decompose it. What sub-questions does this hinge on — which theory of consciousness are you leaning on, how much weight are you putting on it being correct, how much rides on behavioral evidence vs. structural arguments vs. evolutionary considerations? Assign rough probabilities to each piece, show how you combine them, and tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to. Where would new evidence move you most?
