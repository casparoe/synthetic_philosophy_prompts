I keep coming back to the doomsday argument — roughly, the claim that, if we treat our own birth rank as random among all humans who will ever live, we should expect to find ourselves roughly in the middle of the distribution, which means we should expect humanity not to last very much longer, since it would be a massive coincidence to be among the earliest few percent of all humans who will ever be born. The thing is, every time I read a defense or a critique, I feel like the real action is in the choice between the self-sampling assumption and the self-indication assumption, and the papers I've read tend to talk past each other on this point rather than really engaging. SSA says you should reason as if you are randomly selected from the set of all observers in your reference class; SIA says you should reason as if your existence is more likely under hypotheses that predict more observers, which — because it favors large total populations — tends to cancel the doomsday update.
