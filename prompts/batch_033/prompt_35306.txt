Ok so in my rational choice class we went over Kavka's toxin puzzle and I ended up building this argument that now I can't get out of my head because it seems to prove that basically no forward-looking intentions are rational. Here it is:

(1) It is rational for you to form an intention to φ at t only if you have reason to believe that, at t, φ-ing will be the rational thing to do (given your beliefs and desires at t).

(2) In the toxin case: at midnight, you know that tomorrow morning, having already received the million dollars, drinking the toxin will give you a day of misery with no compensating benefit. So at midnight you know that tomorrow morning, drinking the toxin will not be rational.

(3) So by (1), you cannot rationally intend at midnight to drink the toxin tomorrow.

(4) But now consider promising. You promise a friend you'll help them move on Saturday. Saturday comes, you're tired, the friend already relied on your promise and arranged things accordingly — the benefit to them is already locked in. Helping them move will just cost you a day of exhaustion. By the same reasoning as (1)–(3), you cannot rationally intend on Friday to help them move on Saturday, because you know that on Saturday, helping will be against your interests and the promise is already "spent" in terms of its effects.

(5) Same for threats, resolutions, and basically any commitment where the incentive to perform the action depends on the commitment itself having already had its effect. You can never rationally intend to follow through.

(6) But this is absurd. People do form and carry out these intentions, and we think they're rational to do so — sometimes paradigmatically rational.

(7) So (1) must be false. But if (1) is false, then what does rational intention-formation depend on? And more worryingly, if (1) is false, doesn't that open the door to the toxin case actually working — you CAN rationally intend to drink the toxin, and then you get the million, and then... you just don't drink it, which seems like a kind of bootstrapping?

I feel like the argument equivocates somewhere, maybe between "rational to φ" and "rational to intend to φ," but I can't nail down exactly how without giving up something I want to keep. Which step is the problem and what do I have to pay to reject it?
