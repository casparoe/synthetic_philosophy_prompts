I'm trying to get clear on what I actually think about originals and forgeries, going by gut reactions to cases. A molecule-for-molecule copy of a painting, made by a machine that lays down the same pigments in the same arrangement: I can't tell it apart from the original, but I still value the original more. A damaged old master painting that's 90% repainted by restorers: I still call that the original and value it more than the molecule-for-molecule copy, which feels inconsistent even to me. A bit-identical copy of a digital photo: I value the two files exactly the same. A "newly discovered Vermeer" that turns out to be a modern forgery: I think the object itself becomes less valuable once I learn that, not just my opinion of it. But my grandmother's ring, which I find out was accidentally swapped years ago for an identical replica: I keep going back and forth on whether that matters. Is there a principle that captures all or most of these, and what does it say about the ring? Tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, and when you answer please separate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested, rather than presenting one contested view as the consensus.
