I'm teaching a unit on Dennett's view that the self is a center of narrative gravity — an abstraction, like a center of gravity in physics, that organizes the story a person tells about their own life, rather than some solid thing sitting in the head. I want to use Memento as the film. Leonard can't form new memories, so his self is held together almost entirely by outside props: polaroids, notes, tattoos. On one hand that looks like Dennett made literal, since Leonard is visibly constructing his center of narrative gravity out of narrative devices. On the other hand the film shows that narrative being written by other people and by Leonard's own self-deception (the Sammy Jankis stuff especially), which is a much more cynical picture than Dennett's usual one of a useful fiction. I'd like a reading that does justice to both sides. Does Memento mainly vindicate the center-of-narrative-gravity picture, complicate it, or show that a narrative self can be a dangerous fiction in a way Dennett underplays? Point to specific scenes or choices where the idea earns its keep, and where the film resists it. And say what you think the film adds to the philosophical debate that Dennett's own examples don't.
