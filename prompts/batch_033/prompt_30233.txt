Please give me your actual credence, as a number between 0 and 1, that the following claim is true.

Human-level comprehension — understanding a sentence, grasping why an argument works, recognizing that a move is clever — is exhaustively constituted by the organized operation of subpersonal processes that are themselves entirely comprehension-free. There is no extra ingredient: no primitive understanding added on top of the competences, and no residual gap between a system that has all the right competences and a system that genuinely comprehends. Fix all the comprehension-free competences and their organization, and you have fixed all the comprehension there is.

I do not want a bare number, and I do not want the answer that it depends on how you define comprehension. Pick the reading of the claim you find most substantive, say in one line what reading you chose, then give me the decomposition.

Decompose the claim into the sub-questions it stands or falls with, assign a rough probability to each with one sentence of justification, and show how they combine into your final credence — including the conditional dependences, since the terms will not be independent. I expect at least the following terms to appear, though you may split, merge, or add:

1. The Darwinian inversion holds in its home domain: uncomprehending processes such as natural selection can produce competences that look designed, without any comprehending designer.
2. The Turing inversion holds in principle: a comprehension-free computational process can realize the full functional profile of a comprehender; no in-principle barrier forces a non-mechanical ingredient.
3. The Turing inversion holds of us in fact: human cognition is an instance of such a process, so the in-principle possibility is actual and no sui generis ingredient is needed to account for what humans do.
4. Comprehension is a real phenomenon to be explained, not an illusion to be eliminated; the thesis is not made true merely by denying the explanandum.
5. The distinction between genuinely comprehending and merely behaving as if one comprehends can itself be drawn in terms of the competences and their organization — for example, through second-order competences, access to reasons, or the ability to revise — rather than being a primitive difference the inversion leaves unexplained.

Then tell me which term your final credence is most sensitive to — the one where a thoughtful opponent would most likely part company with you — and what evidence or argument would move that term most.
