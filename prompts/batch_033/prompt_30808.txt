I spent most of my career in a neurophysiology lab, and only after retiring did I start reading philosophy of mind seriously, so forgive me if I use the terminology loosely. Something that's been nagging me from the old days: my university's animal care and use commitee drew a line that never made sense to me. If you wanted to record from a mouse, you filled out a long protocol — justify the animal numbers, describe anesthesia, pain management, endpoints, all of it. If you wanted to dissect a fruit fly or a nematode, there was no protocol at all; the animal simply didn't count. A colleague who worked on octopus had to go through a full review, even though an octopus is an invertebrate and far more distant from us than a mouse. And I later learned that Europe's research rules explicitly list cephalopods as the one invertebrate that counts, which only deepens the puzzle. Meanwhile, a postdoc down the hall was running big simulations of pain processing in a mouse spinal cord, and nobody thought of that as an animal-use question, because there was no animal.

I can't make the underlying principle cohere. It isn't "vertebrates get protected," because the octopus breaks that. It isn't "anything that can suffer," because a honeybee can learn to avoid shock and a fruit fly has nociceptors, and neither one gets anything. And it isn't "anything with a nervous system," because then the fly would count, and so might the simulation if what matters is the pattern of activity rather than the substrate. What theory of sentience — or of moral standing — does this whole patchwork of rules actually presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view as best you can, say whether the parts of the practice presuppose consistent things, and separate what the practice strictly commits to from what's just historical accident or administrative convenience. Then tell me what would have to change in how research is regulated if we took seriously the idea that certain computations matter morally regardless of what runs them.

And please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my question.
