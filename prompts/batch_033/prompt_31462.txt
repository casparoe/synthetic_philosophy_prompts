"In a few paragraphs, state Harsanyi's aggregation theorem and explain what it does and does not establish about utilitarian social welfare functions. Be precise about the assumptions."

And here's the student's answer:

"Harsanyi's (1955) aggregation theorem starts with a society of n individuals whose preferences over lotteries obey the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, so each person i can be represented by a cardinal utility function u_i. If the social preference relation also obeys the vNM axioms and satisfies the Pareto condition — that whenever every individual is indifferent between two lotteries, society is indifferent between them — then there are weights a_1, ..., a_n such that the social utility function is W = a_1 u_1 + ... + a_n u_n. The weights are all positive, and they can be rescaled by rescaling the individual utilities, so we are free to set them all equal to one. This means the theorem shows the only acceptable social welfare function is the utilitarian sum of individual utilities, and it does so from individual rationality plus Pareto alone, with no extra moral assumptions. One caveat is that the result presupposes interpersonal comparisons of utility, because we are adding different people's utilities together, but that's unproblematic since the vNM axioms already give us cardinal, interpersonally comparable utilities."
