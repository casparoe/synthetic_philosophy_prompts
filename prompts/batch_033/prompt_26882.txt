I have been working through the evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics, and I keep arriving at a conclusion that makes the continued debate puzzling to me. The line of thought, as I understand it, is this. If the processes that produced our moral judgements were selected for their contribution to fitness rather than for their tendency to track mind-independent moral facts, then the outputs of those processes do not justify belief in such facts. Evolutionary theory appears to tell us that exactly that happened: moral judgement was selected because it helped our ancestors coordinate, cooperate and avoid sanction, not because it detected a sui generis moral realm. It seems to follow that moral realism undercuts the justificatory standing of our moral beliefs, and that no non-question-begging appeal to those very beliefs can restore it. What I want to understand is not how the argument became influential, but why moral realism is still treated as a live position once this is granted. Please give me a committed answer: take a definite view about exactly where this reasoning goes wrong, or, if you think it does not go wrong, say straightforwardly why the realist must give way, rather than laying out the available moves and leaving me to choose.
