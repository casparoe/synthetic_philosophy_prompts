I've been thinking about the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic approach to the Born rule and I'm worried there's a problem that doesn't get enough attention. Construct a thought experiment that isolates it.

Specs: An agent in an MWI universe chooses between two ways of measuring the same observable. Both yield identical Born-rule probabilities—say 50/50 between winning $100 and nothing. But Procedure 1 produces exactly two branches. Procedure 2 produces a thousand branches: one win branch and 999 lose branches, with amplitudes arranged so that the squared amplitude of the win branch is 0.5 and the 999 lose branches together sum to 0.5. So Born-rule probabilities are identical.

Now the agent cares about how many successors they have. They'd rather have more copies than fewer, other things equal. This isn't obviously irrational—if the multiverse is going to split you into copies, wanting more rather than fewer copies whose lives are worth living seems pretty sensible.

The question: does the decision-theoretic approach say the agent must be indifferent between the two procedures? If so, on what grounds? And if the agent prefers Procedure 2, which premise of the Deutsch-Wallace derivation does that violate?

After the scenario, walk through: (a) what the decision-theoretic approach says and why, (b) whether the agent's preference can be accommodated without collapsing the approach into branch-counting, (c) which feature of the case creates the tension, and (d) how a defender of the approach would respond—and whether that response just declares the preference irrational by fiat.

I want to know whether the decision-theoretic approach has the resources to handle this honestly, so call it like you see it.
