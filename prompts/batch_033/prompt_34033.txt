I teach a mid-level undergraduate module on Descartes and early modern rationalism. I'm setting the final exam and want one essay question that tests whether students genuinly understand what the cogito does and doesn't establish. Too many of them just write "Descartes proves he exists because even if he doubts everything, he can't doubt that he's doubting" and leave it there. I want a question that forces them to confront: what kind of thing is proved to exist? For how long? And does the move from "I think" to "I am a thinking thing" require additional steps that Descartes might not be entitled to? Something that brings in Lichtenberg's objection would be good — you know, the point that all Descartes is really entitled to is "there is thinking going on," not "I exist as a thinker."

Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme that distinguishes a First (A), Upper Second (B), and Lower Second (C) answer by naming the specific moves each must contain and the specific mistakes that should cost marks.
