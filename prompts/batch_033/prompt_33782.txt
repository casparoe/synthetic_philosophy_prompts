I'm teaching a graduate seminar on existentialism this semester, and we've just finished two weeks on the chapter on bad faith in *Being and Nothingness*. My students have been particularly animated by Sartre's examples — the café waiter who is too eagerly a waiter, the woman on the date who lets her hand rest inertly in her companion's as though the gesture had no sexual meaning, the man who insists he is not reducible to his homosexuality. The students divide roughly into two camps: those who find Sartre's descriptions phenomenologically acute and recognize something of themselves in these portraits of self-deception, and those who think Sartre is being uncharitable — that the waiter is simply doing his job well, that the woman is navigating an ambiguous social situation with reasonable caution, that the man's refusal of a label is not self-deception but a legitimate insistence on the complexity of personhood.

I would like to give them a piece of philosophical fiction to discuss in our final session on this material. Please write a dialogue between two characters — call them Claire and Julian — who represent these two readings of Sartre. Claire defends Sartre's account of bad faith as capturing a genuine structure of self-deception in which one disowns one's own radical freedom and treats oneself as a mere object or fixed essence. Julian argues that Sartre's examples are tendentious and that what he diagnoses as bad faith is better understood as the ordinary, non-pathological process of inhabiting social roles and responding to uncertainty. The dialogue should have genuine back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's actual points, neither should be a strawman, and the exchange should deepen rather than merely recapitulate the initial disagreement. If one character ultimately has the stronger argument, let that emerge from the dialectic rather than be stipulated in advance.

Please keep the dialogue to no more than 1,000 words. Format it as a play script with character names and brief stage directions where they aid understanding.
