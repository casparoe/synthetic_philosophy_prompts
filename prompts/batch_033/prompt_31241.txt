I'm a journalist who covers philosophy and ideas, and I've been following the debate about hypocrisy and the standing to blame for awhile now. The basic phenomenon is familiar: when a hypocrite blames you — the serial adulterer who calls you out for a white lie, the tax cheat who lectures you about jaywalking — you feel like telling them to get lost, even if what they're saying is true. Philosophers have been trying to explain what exactly goes wrong in these cases for something like twenty years, and from where I'm sitting the debate has completely stalled. You've got the moral standing camp (you forfeit the right to blame when you do the thing yourself), the commitment camp (the hypocrite isn't genuinely committed to the norm they're invoking), the epistemic camp (the hypocrite lacks the relevant knowledge because they haven't grappled with the difficulty of complying), and the conversational camp (hypocritical blame is a speech act that misfires because the speaker's history undermines the felicity conditions). Everyone agrees on the paradigm cases. Everyone agrees the tax cheat's blame is defective. The disagreement is purely about why. And yet nobody moves. I'm starting to suspect this isn't really one debate — that the different camps are answering different questions under the same heading, or that "standing" is doing different work in each account. Is that right? And if so, is there a way to reframe the question that would actually make progress possible, or is this just one of those disputes where the intuitions run out before the arguments do?
