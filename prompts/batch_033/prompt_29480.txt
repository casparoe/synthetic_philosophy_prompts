For my philosophy club's unit on responses to skepticism, I want to start from the standards before we evaluate any actual arguments. Write out the desiderata for a transcendental argument: what such an argument has to deliver to count as a genuine answer to the skeptic rather than a restatement of the problem — for example, whether it must establish that some framework is a condition of the possibility of experience, or only that we cannot coherently doubt it; whether its conclusion may concern only how we must think, or must reach all the way to how things are; what kind of necessity it must establish; and which stock objections it must survive, such as the charge that it shows only that we cannot help believing something, not that the belief is true. For each requirement, give the consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are only one tradition's preferences. Then say which single requirement most cleanly separates a genuine transcendental argument from one that is merely psychological or merely regressive.
