(1) the special sciences (biology, psychology, economics) are autonomous — you can't derive their laws or explanations from physics, even in principle, and you wouldn't want to. they have their own explanatory vocabulary that earns its keep.

(2) physicalism is true: everything that exists is composed of fundamental physical stuff, and the physical domain is causally closed (every physical event has a sufficient physical cause).

(3) some higher-level properties — like being a recession, or being in pain — are genuinely real and novel, not just useful fictions or epistemic shortcuts. they're out there in the world, not just in our heads.

(4) there's no downward causation. all the causal work is done at the fundamental physical level. higher-level properties don't push around the physical stuff.

(5) multiple realizability is a big deal: the same mental or biological state can be realized by wildly different physical substrates, which is a core reason why (1) holds.
