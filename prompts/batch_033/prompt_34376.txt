**Question:** In *Kinds of Minds* (1996), Daniel Dennett lays out a hierarchy of four kinds of creatures: Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian. Explain what cognitive capacity each kind adds over the previous one, and then address the following: is the Gregorian step best understood as a qualitatively new kind of mind, or merely as an elaboration of capacities already present in Popperian creatures? Defend your answer.

**Student's Answer:**

Dennett's hierarchy describes four increasingly sophisticated ways that creatures can generate adaptive behavior.

Darwinian creatures are the baseline. They don't learn at all during their lifetimes. Their behaviors are entirely fixed by their genetic endowment, shaped by natural selection over evolutionary time. If the environment changes within a generation, a Darwinian creature cannot adjust — only the species can adapt, across generations, through differential survival and reproduction.

Skinnerian creatures add the capacity for operant conditioning. They can learn from the consequences of their own actions within a single lifetime. They try out behaviors more or less randomly, and those that produce positive outcomes are reinforced while those that produce negative outcomes are extinguished. This is still a kind of trial-and-error, but the error doesn't have to be fatal in the way it is for Darwinian creatures — the selection now happens within the individual's nervous system rather than only in the gene pool.

Popperian creatures add the capacity for internal representation and foresight. They can, as Dennett puts it, let their hypotheses die in their stead. Rather than physically trying out actions and suffering the consequences of bad ones, a Popperian creature can simulate possible actions in an inner environment and select the best one before acting. This massively reduces the cost of error. Dennett suggests that many mammals and some birds are Popperian creatures.

Gregorian creatures add the capacity for language. Language allows them to formulate hypotheses more precisely and to run more sophisticated internal simulations. With language, a Gregorian creature can label and categorize its internal representations, which makes the simulation process more efficient. Humans are the paradigmatic Gregorian creatures. The Gregorian step is essentially a refinement of the Popperian capacity — it uses the same underlying mechanism of internal simulation but makes it more powerful through linguistic representation. So while it is an important development, it is not a qualitatively new kind of mind; it is better understood as a Popperian mind augmented with language.
