I'm a novelist. I've spent twenty years thinking about time in terms of narrative — tense, point of view, flashbacks, foreshadowing, the narrator's temporal distance from the story, whether a scene is in present or past tense, the way a reader's experience of time gets shaped by the order chapters arrive in. I have zero philosophical training.

Here's what I want. Take the debate between presentism and eternalism in the philosophy of time and restate it in my vocabulary — the vocabulary of narrative craft. Not as a loose analogy, but as a systematic translation. What does presentism look like when you express it in terms of narrative choices? What does eternalism look like? What's actually at stake between them?

Then I want the residue report. Tell me what translated cleanly, what you had to fudge to make it fit, and what has no counterpart at all in how novelists think about time. And here's the part I really care about: when something doesn't translate, is that because my framework is missing a concept it ought to have, or is it because the philosophers' dispute is less clear than it looks and the untranslateable part is the part that was never really coherent?
