In my English class we were debating whether a novel can still count as a great work of literature when its author did terrible things, and a classmate ended the argument by insisting that you have to separate the art from the artist. I suspect that one sentence hides several different claims — about what the work is in itself, about what counts as a good reason for judging it, and about whether we may keep reading, teaching, or enjoying it. Give me exactly five distinct things the slogan could be asserting, ordered from the most trivial to the most radical. For each, in a sentence or two, say whether the claim is plausible and whether it actually supports my classmate's conclusion that a morally awful person can still produce good art. Use a numbered list with exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
