I sit on a small grant committee that funds work in formal epistemology, and we have been asked to support an adversarial collaboration between a foundationalist and a coherentist about the epistemic regress problem. (We are treating infinitism as a third position that may inform the exchange but is not a principal party here.) Before we release funds, we need to see the document the two would produce after six months of good-faith work together. Please draft it.

The document should have two sections. First, a numbered list of claims both can sign—about what the regress problem is, what it demands of a theory of justification, what the Agrippan trilemma actually rules out, and which structural features of inferential justification are common ground. These claims should be substantive enough that signing them represents real progress, not a retreat to truisms. Second, a disagreements table. For each row: state the precise point of disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position; identify whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal (and if partly verbal, say which part); name the crux—the sub-question whose answer would move at least one side; and, where possible, describe a test case, argument, or consideration both agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. The table should cover at least: the status of basic beliefs (or their coherentist substitute), the nature of the support relation, and the role of circularity or infinite chains. If the two discover they agree on more than the literature suggests, say so and explain why the remaining gap has proven stubborn.
