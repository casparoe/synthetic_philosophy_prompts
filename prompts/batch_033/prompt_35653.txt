Can you be a committed Frankfurt-style compatibilist for me to argue against? I've been reading the back-and-forth over whether Frankfurt cases refute the principle of alternative possibilities, and every time I try to press the point in my reading group I end up conceding something I shouldn't. Here's the character I need: someone who holds that Jones, in the standard case where Black would intervene if Jones were about to do otherwise but never has to, is fully morally responsible even though he couldn't have done otherwise — so the principle is false. Defend that for real; refine the case when you need to, grant the small stuff, but don't hand me the conclusion. And keep it conversational, like a paragraph per turn, in character until I say stop.

To start, I'll push on this: the case never removes every alternative possibility, because Black has to detect that Jones is about to do otherwise, and that means tracking a sign — a twitch, a blush, a neural pattern, whatever. So Jones could still have done otherwise at least in the respect of not producing the sign. There's a leftover flicker of an alternative in every Frankfurt case, and maybe responsibility only needs that flicker. Doesn't the compatibilist need to eliminate alternatives completely, and dont these cases always leave one behind?
