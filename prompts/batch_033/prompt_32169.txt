I was arguing about the Knobe effect with a friend in a philosophy of mind class, and we ended up talking past each other, so I'd like an essay to help me sort it out. The basic setup, in case you havent seen it: a company chairman is told that a new policy will raise profits and also harm the environment. He says he doesn't care at all about the environment and only wants to make as much money as possible; the policy goes ahead, profits rise, and the environment is harmed. A large majority of people say he harmed the environment intentionally. But when the same chairman is told the policy will raise profits and help the environment, and he says the exact same thing — he doesn't care about the environment, he only wants profit — only a minority say he helped the environment intentionally. So the same state of mind (knowing the side effect will happen, not caring either way, acting anyway) gets labelled intentional when the side effect is bad and not intentional when it's good.

The interesting fight is over what this shows. One camp says it shows that our ordinary concept of intentional action is already moralized — that we can't apply the concept without, in effect, sorting outcomes into things the agent deserves blame or credit for. Another camp says it's a deflationary artifact: maybe the harm case involves accepting a cost (the harm) in exchange for a benefit (the profit), while the help case involves no such trade-off, so people aren't really tracking morality at all, just whether the side effect counted against the agent's aims. A third view says the blame judgment comes first and the "intentional" answer is mostly a post-hoc way of justifying that blame — or that the answer tracks whether the side effect reveals something about the agent's deep self, his values and what he really cares about. And then there's the possibility that the effect is just a performance slip: people are using the word sloppily and would revise their answer if they thought about it harder.

Write an essay on this controversy. What does the Knobe effect actually show about the concept of intentional action — is the concept genuinely moralized, or is the effect better explained by one of the deflationary stories? Weigh the main positions, but dont end on a non-committal note: defend a verdict about which reading is most plausible. Structure the response with these sections: "The asymmetry" (~150 words), "The moralized reading" (~300 words), "Deflationary readings" (~350 words), "My verdict" (~300 words). Don't worry about citing the literature — just argue it out.
