ok so ive been reading cliffords the ethics of belief and jamess the will to believe and i want to think through a real case using both. take the titanic disaster — the night of april 14 1912, when the ship hit an iceberg and sank with about 1500 people dead. heres what i know: the ship had a reputation as practically unsinkable, it was going full speed (~22 knots) through an ice field on a moonless calm night, the lookouts didnt have binoculars, multiple ice warnings had come in from other ships but the wireless operator was swamped with passenger messages and didnt relay them all to the bridge, and captain smith (and bruce ismay, the white star chairman, who was on board) basically kept the speed up. the sea was flat calm so no waves breaking at the base of icebergs, making them nearly invisble until too late. there werent enough lifeboats for everyone — thats a diferent issue maybe.

what i want is a careful mapping of this episode onto both cliffords and jamess frameworks. for clifford: who was believing what on insufficient evidence, and were those beliefs culpable? the shipowner example from his essay is basically on the nose here — but the details are different (the shipowner knew the ship was dodgy; here the ship was actually new and state-of-the-art, the belief was about ice risk not structural integrity). for james: was the decision to maintain speed a genuine option — live forced momentous? if so does jamess will-to-believe justify the crews apparent belief that theyd spot ice in time or that the risk was manageable? or does jamess framework break when the risk is borne by passengers who didnt consent to the gamble?

and then i want the reverse: what does the titanic case show about each position? does it expose a blind spot in james (that his passional justifications only work for first-person existential choices, not fiduciary decisions where youre gambling with other peoples lives)? does it expose a problem for clifford (that his evidentialist standard is impossible under time pressure and uncertainty — you cant just suspend judgment when a decision is forced)? or does the episode actually vindicate clifford and show exactly why jamess permissiveness is dangerous?

basically i want the theory to illuminate the case and the case to test the theory, honestly, not just scoring points for one side.
