I've been thinking about causation and responsibility, and I've worked myself into a corner. I'd like you to tell me where the argument goes wrong.

(1) Suppose I promise to water your plants while you're away, I forget, and they die. It seems right to say my omission caused their death.

(2) But now consider all the other people who also didn't water your plants: your neighbor, a stranger in another country, someone who died a century ago. In each case, it's true that if that person had watered your plants, they wouldn't have died. The counterfactual holds for all of them.

(3) If my omission caused the death, what distinguishes it from the neighbor's omission, or the stranger's? It can't be the counterfactual structure, because that's the same across all of them. It can't be physical proximity, because we can imagine a case where I promised to water them but was out of town and arranged for a hose timer that failed — I'm not physically near the plants, but we'd still say my omission caused their death.

(4) The natural answer is that I had a duty or was expected to act, and the others weren't. But this makes causation — which seems like a factual, worldly relation — depend on normative facts about what someone ought to have done. That looks backwards: we ordinarily establish what caused what first, and only then assign responsibility.

(5) So we seem to face a trilemma: (a) deny that omissions are causes at all, and say my failure to water the plants didn't cause their death; (b) accept that omissions are causes but that every omission satisfying the counterfactual counts, which means the stranger and the long-dead person also caused the plants to die; or (c) accept that causation by omission is selective, but the selection depends on normative facts, making causation itself partly normative.

I find (a) and (b) both hard to accept, and (c) makes me uneasy because it blurs the line between what happened and what should have happened. I want to know: is there a way out I'm not seeing? If I have to reject one of the steps, which one should it be, and what does rejecting it cost?
