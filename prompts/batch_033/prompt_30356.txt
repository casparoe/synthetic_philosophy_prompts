hey, could you write me a villanelle about Russell's paradox? I want the two refrains to be the two directions of the contradiction — one says the set is a member of itself only if it isn't, the other says it isn't only if it is — and I want the form to carry the point: the refrains keep returning and swapping places the way the paradox keeps reasserting itself, and the final couplet should put them side by side so there's no way out. For the setup: naive comprehension says that for any property there's a set of exactly the things with that property; take the property of not being a member of itself and call the resulting set R, so R is a member of R just in case R is not a member of R. The tercets can build that machinery — the promise of a set for every predicate, the construction of R — and then the refrains take over. And please, this is not the barber paradox: the barber version dissolves because no such barber has to exist, but naive comprehension says the set has to exist, which is exactly why the contradiction bites. A logician should be able to read it without wincing, but it should also just be a decent poem, with the rhymes and repeated lines doing real work rather than padding. dont force the rhymes so hard that the logic goes wobbly.
