I keep running into accounts of slurs built around a single phenomenon—the truth-conditional difference from a neutral counterpart, or in-group non-derogation, or the way the force survives negation—and I want the full specification before I evaluate any of them. Write the desiderata for a theory of slurs: everything an adequate account of how a slur means, derogates, and is taken up in ordinary talk must deliver, with the case or contrast that makes each item a requirement. Include at least the following, and add anything I've missed: what separates a slur from an ordinary insult and from its neutral counterpart; how derogatory force behaves under embedding (negation, conditionals, questions, attitude reports, quotation, and mention) and which of those behaviors are fixed data rather than theory-optional; what the account must say about in-group use, reclamation, and the asymmetry between speakers in what a term does; how it must accommodate variation across communities and over time without making the category dissolve; and the distinctions it cannot collapse—causing offense versus derogating, mention versus use, speaker identity versus term content, being taboo versus being false—along with the contrast between a slur uttered without contempt that still lands and a contemptuous non-slur that does not. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the demands of a particular camp. Finally, flag any pair of desiderata you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to eliminate the leading candidate theories. Keep the criteria the focus; any scorecard of views should be brief.
