I've been turning something over for a while and I'd like a proper diagnosis rather than a survey. In undergrad I read Dawkins on memes and it felt like a revelation: tunes, catchphrases, fashions, bits of technology all spreading and mutating and getting selected more or less like genes. Then I got into the more serious literature and found that almost nobody in the actual sciences of culture uses "meme" anymore, and the people who do serious cultural evolution (Boyd and Richerson, Henrich, and so on) mostly treat memetics as a wrong turn. But when I read the back-and-forth from the 90s and 2000s, it doesn't look like anyone actually lost an argument. Dawkins and Dennett say memes are real units of cultural inheritance and selection; Sperber and others say culture mostly isn't copied the way genes are, it's reconstructed and transformed by each mind, so the unit dissolves; and the cultural evolution people say you can do Darwinian population thinking about culture without discrete, high-fidelity replicators. All three sides seem to accept the same basic facts about how culture spreads, yet they've been talking past each other for decades. My working hypothesis is that the debate was never really about whether culture evolves — everyone grants that in some loose sense — but about what an explanation of culture has to do: whether it has to bottom out in entities that get transmitted, or can stop at changing frequencies of traits in a population. But I'm not sure that's right; maybe its a straightforward empirical question about copying fidelity that just wasn't settled at the time, or maybe "meme" was doing two jobs at once (a scientific construct and a catchy word) and people argued past each other because of that. I'd like a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, what would have counted as progress in, say, 2005, and whether anything in cultural evolution research since then could actually settle it or whether the question is just badly posed. I know the lay of the land, so don't bother with citations or a reading list; I want the structure of the disagreement laid bare.
