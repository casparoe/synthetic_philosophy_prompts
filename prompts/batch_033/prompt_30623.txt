So me and my friend got into it last night about Langdon Winner's essay "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" and I still cant figure out what the thesis is even supposed to be. She kept bringing up the Robert Moses thing—the low overpasses on Long Island that supposedly kept buses, and therefore poor and Black people, off the beaches. She said the bridge is racist, full stop, and that once a technology has a politics built into it, that politics just plays itself out no matter who uses it or what the surrounding society looks like. But then later she also said the same artifact can have different politics in different contexts, like a wall meaning something different depending on who builds it and where. I don't see how she can have it both ways.

If an artifact's politics is really in the artifact, then it should be a stable property of the thing itself, and we should be able to read it off the design—including the designers' intentions. If the politics depends on context, then it sounds like we're just talking about how people use or interpret the thing, and the artifact itself isn't doing any political work. So which is it? Is "artifacts have politics" a claim about intentions being baked into the object, or a claim about context? And if it's somehow both, what exactly is the claim supposed to be?
