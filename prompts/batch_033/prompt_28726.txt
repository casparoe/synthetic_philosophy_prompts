A friend and I have been arguing for weeks about whether the decision to have a child can be a rational one; it started when his sister told us she could not have known from the outside what she was choosing, and it has since drifted into questions about what a decision even is when the person making it will be changed by the outcome. Last week he ended the discussion by saying "you can't know what it's like until you've been through it," and he clearly took this to settle more than the modest point that my predictions would be imperfect; he took it to show that expected-value reasoning is the wrong tool before the fact. I have read enough around transformative experience to suspect that one sentence is compressing several distinct claims, and I would rather pull them apart than either concede to or argue against an unanalyzed blur.

Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, ordered from the most trivial to the most radical. For each reading, say whether it is obviously true, plausibly true, or probably false, and — this is the part I care about — whether that reading actually supports the conclusion that a person cannot rationally decide to undergo the experience, as opposed to merely supporting weaker conclusions such as that the decision must be made under unusual uncertainty or that the later self has some special authority. Where it helps, taboo the words "know" and "rational" — restate the readings without using those terms — since I suspect the disagreement lives inside them. Finally, tell me which reading my friend's conclusion actually needs, which reading he is most likely asserting, and which argument or piece of evidence would most cleanly distinguish the two.
