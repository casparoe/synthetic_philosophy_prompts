So I've gotten pretty convinced by the moral luck argument — the idea that since so much of what we do, who we are, and how things turn out depends on factors beyond our control, we can't really deserve blame or praise in the way we ordinarily think. I'm not just talking about resultant luck (who gets charged with murder vs. attempted murder based on whether the bullet hits) but also constitutive luck (your temperament, your upbringing) and circumstantial luck (whether you ever face the temptation in the first place). My question is: what would count against this view? I want a map of the strongest challenges, from "this is fatal" down to "this is annoying but survivable." I'm especialy interested in whether there's a version of the view that's been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that's a problem for a view of this kind. Also, what would a defender of the moral luck challenge have to honestly concede, and what can they just dismiss?
