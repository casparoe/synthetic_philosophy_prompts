ok so i've been going back and forth on the whole buridan's ass thing. the standard line in rationalist circles is that it's a fake problem — just use a tiebreaker, or notice that real agents have stochastic decision procedures, or whatever. but i've started to think there's actually something genuinely puzzling here that the usual dismissals don't touch. can you make the case that buridan's ass scenarios reveal a real problem for theories of rational choice? and i don't mean the superficial "how do you break ties" version — i mean the deeper issue about whether reasons can ever fully determine action, and what that means for the picture of agency where choices are supposed to be guided by reasons all the way down. argue that the paradox isn't just a curiosity but points to a structural limitation in how we think about rational decision-making.
