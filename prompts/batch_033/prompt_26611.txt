Draft the joint statement that a "logical-learning Bayesian" and a "deflationary Bayesian" could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the problem of old evidence.

Quick background in case you need it: the problem of old evidence (Glymour) is that if evidence E is already known before a theory T is proposed, then P(E) = 1, so P(E|T) = P(E) = 1, and Bayesian conditionalization says E can't confirm T. But intuitively old evidence does confirm theories — general relativity and the perihelion of Mercury being the classic example. The logical-learning camp (roughly Garber, Jeffrey) says we need to enrich Bayesianism so agents can be uncertain about logical relations between T and E. The deflationary camp (roughly Howson, maybe Earman) says the problem dissolves if you're careful: confirmation isn't about the historical order in which evidence was gathered, and the right counterfactual or subjective probability assignment handles it fine.

What I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign — about the formal setup, about what the problem is and isn't, about which cases are genuinely puzzling; (2) a disagreements table where each row states the point of divergence in wording each side would accept for their own view; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that if you settled them, at least one side would have to budge; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

If you think some of the fight is verbal — like they're using "confirmation" differently — flag that and say which part, but don't let that swallow the whole thing. I want the residual substantive disagreement made as precise as possible.
