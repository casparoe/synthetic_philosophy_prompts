I've been trying to nail down what I actually think emotions are, and I went through a bunch of cases just going by gut. Here's what I've got:

When I nearly step into traffic and jerk back, that flash of fear doesn't feel like a thought — it's just there, visceral, before I've judged anything. Ditto for the jolt of disgust when I smell spoiled milk. Those feel like pure bodily reactions.

But then I read about some political scandal and feel this hot indignation, and it's hard to seperate that from the judgment that someone did something wrong. If I didn't think it was wrong, the indignation wouldn't make sense. Same with feeling guilty about skipping a friend's wedding — guilt seems to *contain* the judgment that I shouldn't have done that.

Then there's my phobia of flying. I know flying is safe. I've looked at the numbers. I judge it's fine. But I still white-knuckle the armrest. So the emotion is completely out of step with my judgment. That makes the judgment theory look wrong.

But wait — sometimes I'm grumpy and snappish and only realize hours later that I was actually angry about something specific from that morning. So I had the emotion, but I wasn't aware of what it was about. That's weird. If emotions are judgments, how can I have one without knowing its content? That feels more like a perception — like seeing something out of the corner of your eye and not being sure what it was.

And then there's the feeling of being watched in an empty house. It's exactly like a perception — it presents the world as being a certain way, it's involuntary, and it persists even when I know it's false. That really makes me lean toward emotions being perceptions of value or danger or whatever.

But then what about a lingering sadness months after a breakup that I know was the right call? I judge the breakup was good, I don't perceive anything as wrong, but the sadness is still there. It doesn't seem to be about anything at all at that point — it's just a mood, a feeling. So maybe some emotions are perceptions, some are judgments, and some are just raw feelings?

Is there a single theory of what emotions are that makes sense of all these cases, or close to it? And which of my verdicts would I have to give up? Also, what does the best theory say about emotions in very young infants — like a newborn's distress cry — who can't form judgments or perceive much of anything?
