Hey, I'm sketching out an essay on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility and I'd like some comments on my notes before I write it up. Here's the idea.

The argument as I understand it: you do what you do because of the way you are — your beliefs, desires, values, temperament, and so on. So in order to be truly responsible for what you do, you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, at least in the respects that actually produce the action. But you can only be responsible for the way you are if you intentionally brought it about that you are that way. And to intentionally bring that about, you'd need to have already had some earlier nature in light of which you chose to make yourself into the later one. The same question then arises about that earlier nature, and off we go. Since nothing can be causa sui, the regress never bottoms out, so ultimate responsibility is impossible.

The angle I want to take: the argument is sound but less revisionary than it looks. It only rules out a very strong, self-creationist notion of responsibility, where you're the ultimate source of yourself. But most of what we actually care about in ordinary blame and praise is weaker than that — acting without coercion, being responsive to reasons, having the capacity to do otherwise, that sort of thing. If that's right, Strawson has proved something true about an impossibly strong target, but he hasn't touched the notion that does the day-to-day work. My worry is that this might be the first thing everyone says about the argument, so maybe its a boring essay.

Can you comment on: (i) whether my reconstruction gets the "intentionally brought it about" step right — does Strawson's version actually require a choice, or is the regress supposed to run even if you just ended up with your nature through non-agential causes? (ii) whether the "changing the subject" reply is fair, or whether Strawson has a way of insisting that the strong notion just is the one that matters. (iii) Is the angle too obvious, and if so what would be a more interesting way in? I don't need references, just judgment.
