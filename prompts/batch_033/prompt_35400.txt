so here's something that's been bugging me. When a news outlet covers a controversial topic—like climate change or vaccine safety—there's this norm that they should give 'both sides' a hearing, present the mainstream view and then the dissenting view, and let the audience decide. This is treated as basic journalistic integrity. But then the same outlet might have a policy of not platforming, say, flat-earthers or holocaust deniers, on the grounds that giving them airtime legitimizes something that's just false. And platforms like facebook or twitter have moved toward labeling or removing certain kinds of misinformation rather than just letting the 'marketplace of ideas' sort it out.

So what I want to know is: what does the 'both sides' norm actually presuppose about how people form beliefs and how they end up with false ones? It seems like it's assuming that the main problem is that people just haven't *heard* the other side—that if you expose them to the evidence and arguments on both sides, they'll figure it out. But isn't that kind of a specific picture of what's going wrong? Like, it seems to rule out the idea that the real problem is that people are in enviroments where contrary evidence isn't just absent but is actively discredited by sources they trust. And if that's the real problem, then platforming the other side doesn't fix anything—it might even make things worse, because the person has already been trained to dismiss that source.

So: reconstruct the implicit epistemology of the 'both sides' norm. What view of belief formation, and of what goes wrong when people form false beliefs, does it presuppose? Is it strictly committed to the view that the problem is that people lack exposure to contrary evidence, or is it compatible with the view that the deeper problem is that contrary evidence gets discredited by trusted sources? And is there a tension between this norm and the practice of deplatforming or labeling misinformation—do they presuppose inconsistent things about how people's minds work, or is there a coherent way to hold both?
