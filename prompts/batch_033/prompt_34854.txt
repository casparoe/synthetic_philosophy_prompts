I've been following the Sleeping Beauty debate for years now — you know, the one where Beauty gets put to sleep Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and if it's heads she's woken Monday and that's it, but if it's tails she's woken Monday, given amnesia, and woken again Tuesday. Every time she wakes up they ask her what her credence is that the coin came up heads. Halfers say 1/2, thirders say 1/3. And as far as I can tell, this thing has been going since the 80s (or arguably since the 50s if you count the earlier variants) and nobody has budged. The arguments are all on the table, everyone's seen everyone else's thought experiments, and the two camps just keep talking past each other.

I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but this problem gets under my skin because it's basically about what a character should believe when they don't know which instance of themselves they are — which is exactly the kind of thing I think about when I'm writing fiction with unreliable narrators, or amnesia plots, or characters who are copies or simulations of each other. And the fact that extremely smart people can't resolve this makes me wonder whether I'm missing something deep about self-location and identity that I should be grappling with in my work.

So here's what I want: a diagnosis of why this debate is stuck. Not a verdict on who's right. I want to know what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a verbal dispute about the word "credence"? Is it two camps answering different questions — one about centered worlds and one about possible worlds — under the same label? Is there a hidden disagreement about what counts as evidence when you're inside the world rather than looking at it from outside? Are halfers and thirders just working from different starting intuitions about indifference principles that neither side can argue for? Or is the problem itself somehow ill-posed in a way that reveals a crack in how we think about probability and self-location?

I have a hunch, for what it's worth: I suspect the real disagreement is about whether Beauty gains new evidence when she wakes up. The halfers seem to think waking up tells her nothing she didn't already know (she knew she'd be woken at least once regardless), while the thirders think waking up is evidence because there are more wake-up moments in the tails world and she should treat herself as randomly sampled from those moments. But maybe that's too simple, or maybe that framing just restates the problem rather than diagnosing it.

Whatever your diagnosis is, I want it grounded in what each side actually argues — the centered-worlds stuff, the betting arguments, the Dutch books, the analogy to the many-minds interpretation, all of it. And then tell me: given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress? A new formal result? A conceptual distinction everyone can agree on? A reframing of the question? Or is this just one of those things where we should accept that our concept of probability doesn't have a single right answer when self-locating uncertainty is involved?

Oh, and at the very end, give me a one-sentence summary of your diagnosis as the last line.
