I'm putting together a reading group on AI safety, and we're planning a session on the difference between alignment and control. The way I've been framing it to myself is: alignment work tries to make the model want what we want; control work assumes the model might not want what we want and tries to prevent it from doing harm anyway. The control literature seems to have a lot of clever proposals — trusted monitoring, untrusted monitoring with paraphrasing, capability elicitation bounds, honeypots, boxing, and so on — but I don't yet have a clear sense of what would count as a satisfactory solution to the control problem. Before we dive into individual papers, I want to get the evaluative framework right.

What I'm after is a list of desiderata: the requirements that any adequate theory of AI control (under the assumption that the model may be strategically misaligned) must meet. For each requirement, I want the case or scenario that motivates it — why can't we drop it? — and a judgement about whether it's non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular camp. I'd also like you to flag any pairs of desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, or any single requirement that seems to rule out large swathes of the existing proposal space. If you think some widely demanded criterion is actually a red herring, say so and explain why. The weight should be on the criteria themselves and their motivation, rather than on scoring specific papers; but a brief note on which requirement does the most to exclude popular approaches would be helpful.

Please write the answer in British English.
