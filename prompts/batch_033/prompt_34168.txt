My friend's got pretty firm views on origin essentialism and I've been collecting her verdicts. She says: a particular human couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg; a particular oak tree couldn't have grown from a different acorn; a particular cat couldn't have come from a different pair of gametes. But she also says the Mona Lisa could've been painted on a different canvas so long as da Vinci painted it, and a particular wooden chair could've been made from a different block of wood.

Now I want to know what she'd say about these three new cases: (a) could I have come from a different sperm and egg that, by sheer coincidence, had exactly the same DNA as my actual ones? (b) could I have come from the same sperm and egg but gestated in a different womb? (c) could I have come from the same sperm but a different egg from the same mother? I'm less interested in the answers themselves than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, and which of those new cases would best tell them apart.

Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction and no conclusion. Use British English.
