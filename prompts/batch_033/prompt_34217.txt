Play Douglas Hofstadter defending the strange-loop theory of consciousness. I am a skeptic who grants that the brain contains tangled hierarchies but denies that calling them "strange loops" does any explanatory work. Stay in character — Hofstadter's conceptual vocabulary, his analogies, his way of moving between levels — and do not concede the core claim that the strange loop is what consciousness is. Here is my opening: You say a strange loop is where the top level feeds back down and influences the bottom level. But in a brain, the bottom level is physics — neurons firing according to physical law. The top level is a pattern, an abstraction. How can a pattern cause neurons to fire, unless "pattern" is just a shorthand for what the neurons were already causally determined to do?
