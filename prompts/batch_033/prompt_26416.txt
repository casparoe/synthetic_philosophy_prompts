Exam question:

"Explain van Inwagen's Special Composition Question. What are the main families of answers? Which answer does van Inwagen himself defend, and what does he hold about whether tables and chairs exist?"

Student answer:

"The Special Composition Question asks when two or more objects compose a further object. Van Inwagen distinguishes it from the General Composition Question, which asks what composition itself is. The main answers fall into three families: mereological nihilism says composition never occurs, so there are no composite objects — only simples exist; mereological universalism says composition always occurs, so any two or more things, no matter how scattered, compose something; and moderate views say composition occurs only under certain conditions. Examples of moderate views include contact (objects compose when they touch), fastening (when they are joined), and fusion (when they are melted together). Van Inwagen rejects the moderate views through counterexamples — for instance, two people shaking hands are in contact but do not seem to compose a new object. His own answer is that composition occurs exactly when the activity of the parts constitutes a life, so the only composite objects are living organisms. Because tables and chairs are not alive, they do not exist; strictly speaking there are only simples arranged tablewise and chairwise. So van Inwagen's view winds up being a kind of nihilism about ordinary objects."
