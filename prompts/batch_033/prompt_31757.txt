I've got two colleagues in philosophy of medicine who've been arguing for years about what makes something a disease, and they've finally agreed to do an adverserial collaboration instead of just talking past each other. One of them — call her the naturalist — holds that disease is fundamentally a matter of biological dysfunction: some part or process failing to do what it's biologically meant to do, where that's anchored in evolution, homeostasis, or normal species functioning. The other — call him the normativist — holds that the biology can't do the job by itself, because what makes a condition a disease is that it's a bad thing for the person: harmful, disvalued, something we'd rightly want to prevent or fix. On his view dysfunction matters only instrumentally, as one common source of the badness.

They want to produce one joint statement both can sign, and I want you to draft it. Include: (1) a numbered list of claims they both accept — about particualr cases (hypertension, type 2 diabetes, pregnancy, ordinary grief, infertility, ADHD, or whichever cases you think actually put pressure on them), about what an account of disease has to explain, and about where the easy cases lie; (2) a disagreements table giving their residual disagreement exactly, with each side's view phrased so that side would sign it — no strawmen; (3) the cruxes — the two or three subquestions such that settling them would genuinely move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, plus what each side would predict about it. If you think part of their fight is merely verbal, say which part and why, and keep it out of the disagreements table. The agreed-claims section shouldn't be vacuous, and the residual disagreement should be stated precisely enough that a third party could tell what would settle it.
