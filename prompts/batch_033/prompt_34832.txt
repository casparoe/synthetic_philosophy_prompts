I'm a novelist at work on a book about political language — not a dystopia, but a realist novel set in the present, in which the degradation of public speech is one of the forces my characters have to navigate. I've been rereading Orwell, especially "Politics and the English Language" and the Newspeak passages in 1984, and I'm trying to separate what still cuts from what has hardened into conventional wisdom.

I'd like you to help me set an agenda. Among Orwell's central claims about language and political thought — that unclear language enables political dishonesty; that dead metaphors and ready-made phrases substitute for thinking; that if you reduce a language's vocabulary you can reduce the range of thought; that, as he puts it, "political speech and writing are largely the defence of the indefensible"; that language can be an instrument of self-deception as much as of deception; and so on — which two or three are most worth a novelist's attention now, and which have been overstated or apply more to mid-century bureaucratic prose than to the political speech people actually encounter today?

I'm not asking for a survey of Orwell's views. I want a defended ranking. For each idea you highlight, tell me what makes it more important or more neglected than the others, how it might play out differently in 2025 than in 1946, and — this is the part I really need — where the novel form has an advantage over the essay. What can fiction do with Orwell's insights that polemic can't, and where does fiction risk being a worse vehicle for them?

I'd also like to hear which of Orwell's ideas about language and politics you would deprioritize for a novelist, and why. If there are developments in the relationship between language and political power that have become salient since Orwell's time and that he didn't anticipate, I'd like to know which of those you'd rank above or below his own concerns.

Please write your response in continuous prose, the way you would in a letter — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
