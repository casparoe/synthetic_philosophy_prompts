Consider the following passage from Charles Darwin, *On the Origin of Species* (1859), Chapter V, “Laws of Variation,” in the section headed “Correlation of Growth”:

“I mean by this expression that the whole organisation is so tied together during its growth and development, that when slight variations in any one part occur, and are accumulated through natural selection, other parts become modified. This is a very important subject, most imperfectly understood.”

Explain what Darwin means by “correlation of growth” in this passage, and why he describes the whole organisation as being tied together during growth and development. Then discuss how this idea complicates a strongly adaptationist reading of natural selection, in which each part of an organism is explained by a distinct selective advantage. Finally, evaluate whether Darwin’s treatment of correlated modification is best understood as a mere qualification to adaptationism, or as an earlier recognition of the kind of constraint and nonadaptive by-product later emphasized by Gould and Lewontin’s spandrels critique.
