I'm giving a short speech at my uncle's retirement party. He was a criminal court judge for thirty years, and about five years ago he read Galen Strawson and became quietly convinced by the Basic Argument — that nobody is ultimately morally responsible for what they do. He kept sentencing people anyway, because what else are you going to do, but it changed how he talked from the bench. Half the room will be judges and lawyers who think this is all soft-on-crime nonsense, and the other half is family who just want to hear nice things about him. I need a five-minute speech that honors his career, acknowledges this shift honestly, and doesn't make either half of the room angry. It should actually use the structure of the Basic Argument — the infinite regress of self-creation — to say something true about him as a person, not just name-drop it.
