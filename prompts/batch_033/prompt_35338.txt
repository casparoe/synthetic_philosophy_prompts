David Hume argued that the self is a fiction — when we look inward we find only a bundle of perceptions, and the idea of a persisting "I" is a trick of the imagination, rather like mistaking a succession of related things for a single enduring substance. Douglas Hofstadter, in *I Am a Strange Loop* (2007), argues that the self is a strange loop: a self-referential symbol that emerges from the tangled hierarchy of the brain's symbol-processing, and that this "I" is perfectly real at the level of patterns, even if it isn't a thing you can point to. I'm writing a piece on theories of the self across intellectual history, and I want to stage a dialogue across the centuries. If Hume could read Hofstadter, where would he find the argument surprisingly congenial, and where would he dig in his heels hardest? Please give me exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
