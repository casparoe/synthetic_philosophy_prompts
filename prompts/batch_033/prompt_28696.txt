I'm a software engineer who's been following the AI alignment discussion for a few years, and I've noticed that two broad camps seem to talk past each other on the question of whether "prosaic" alignment methods — roughly, the family of techniques we have today (RLHF, constitutional AI, debate, scalable oversight, process-based supervision, and so on) — can be extended to align systems substantially more capable than current ones, without requiring fundamentally new conceptual breakthroughs.

I'd like you to draft the joint statement that a thoughtful representative of each camp could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The two positions are:

- **Prosaic sufficiency**: Current alignment methods, iterated and scaled, can in principle produce systems that are sufficiently aligned (they robustly do what we intend and don't cause catastrophic harm) even as capabilities increase substantially beyond today's frontier — no qualitatively new insight about values, agency, or alignment is required.
- **Prosaic insufficiency**: At some capability level short of transformative AI, current methods will break down in ways that scaling alone won't fix; we will need at least one genuinely new conceptual breakthrough (about, e.g., how to specify objectives that survive distributional shift, or how to detect and correct misaligned internal cognition) to achieve sufficient alignment.

The document should include:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign — these should be substantive, not just truisms. What do they agree on about how current methods work, what the failure modes are, what "sufficient alignment" means, and what the empirical track record shows so far?
2. A disagreements table: for each point of disagreement, state it precisely, then give the wording each side would accept for its own position (not a caricature the other side wrote). The table should separate disagreements that are genuinely about the world from ones that might be partly verbal (e.g., about what counts as a "new insight").
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that if we could answer them, at least one side would substantially update. For each crux, describe a concrete empirical result, experiment, or observation that both sides would agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts.
4. An honest assessment of whether the disagreement is currently resolvable — are there experiments we could run today, or does resolution require capabilities we don't yet have? If the latter, does that mean both sides should be more uncertain than they typically are?

Finally, I'd like you to tell me how confident you are in your own rendering of each side's position — in particular, whether you think either side would object that you've mischaracterized them — and single out the point in the document you are least sure about.
