ok so for a paper i'm writing on genetic information i need to figure out if my views actually hang together before i commit to them. here's roughly where i am:

(a) DNA genuinely carries information — it's not just a handy metaphor. genes literally encode phenotypic traits.

(b) the right way to cash out "information" in biology is something close to shannon's sense: a signal carries information about a source if there's a systematic correlation that reduces uncertainty.

(c) the genetic code is a real code, roughly in the way morse code is — it's a mapping from codons to amino acids, and the mapping is arbitrary in the sense that other mappings were possible and some were actually selected over others.

(d) we don't need an intentional sender or receiver for genetic information to be meaningful. no homunculus, no designer.

(e) "genetic program" and "blueprint" talk is misleading and should be dropped, but "genetic information" talk is fine and should stay.

the paper is me arguing against people who want to throw out all information talk in biology, and i want to make sure i'm not smuggling in a contradiction. which of these are in tension, which tensions are real vs just terminological, and what's the cheapest fix? rank them by severity rather than just listing everything as equally problematic.
