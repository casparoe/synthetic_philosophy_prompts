ok so i've been reading a bunch about pascal's wager for a philosophy club thing and i've ended up with this set of views and honestly i can't tell if they actually fit together. here's where i'm at:

(1) i think expected value reasoning is basically the right way to make decisions when you're uncertain. you figure out the probabilities, multiply by the value of each outcome, and go with the biggest number. i don't see what else you could mean by "rational choice" when you don't know what's gonna happen.

(2) given (1), pascal's wager seems basically right to me. infinite reward times any nonzero probability is still infinite, and the cost is finite. so the math checks out.

(3) but i also think you literally cannot choose to believe something. i can't wake up tomorrow and go "ok i believe in god now." beliefs aren't under direct voluntary control. so i don't see how the wager actually tells me to do anything.

(4) i think the many-gods objection is kind of overrated. you can assign different probabilities to different gods — like, a god that wants me to sacrifice goats has a much lower prior than a god that just wants me to be decent, you know? so you're not stuck with equal infinite expected values for all religions.

(5) at the same time, i think using infinity in expected value calculations causes all sorts of problems. like, if there's any nonzero probability of infinite payoff, everything you do has infinite expected value and the whole framework collapses into nonsense. but pascal's wager needs the infinity to work.

(6) and practically, even if the wager is technically correct, i don't think it means you should give away everything and become a monk. like, presumbly god doesn't want you to be a miserable fanatic, and that fact itself should show up in the expected value calculation somehow.

so yeah, that's the cluster. i want to know: which of these are in actual tension, which just look like they're in tension but aren't, and what's the cheapest fix? rank the tensions by how serious they are. and please take a side — don't just lay out the options, tell me what you think i should give up and why.
