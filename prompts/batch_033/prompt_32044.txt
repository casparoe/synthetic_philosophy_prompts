I spent my career in molecular biology, and since retiring I've been working through the philosophy of mind literature. I've found Global Workspace Theory particularly congenial — it's satisfying to see consciousness explained as a matter of information being globally broadcast across specialized modules in the brain, rather than some mysterious extra ingredient. Dehaene's experimental work showing that conscious perception coincides with ignition in the global workspace seems to nail this down empirically. Here's what I don't understand: if GWT gives us a complete mechanistic account of how sensory information becomes conscious, why do philosophers like Chalmers still insist there's a separate hard problem that hasn't been touched? Once we've explained the global broadcasting, haven't we explained the experience?
