I'm in a film club with some mates and we've been having the same argument on loop for months now. Every time we pick a film by someone problematic — Polanski, Allen, whoever — someone says "you've got to separate the art from the artist" and half the room nods like that's settled it. But I honestly can't work out what they're actually claiming. Are they saying the moral qualities of the artist are irrelevant to the aesthetic qualities of the work? That we shouldn't let our knowledge of the artist affect our experience? That we have some kind of duty to evaluate the work independently? That consuming the art doesn't make you complicit in anything? These feel like really different claims and I'm not sure which one is supposed to be doing the work.

I tried to push back last week by saying that knowing what Polanski did does change how I experience his films — I can't just un-know it — and someone accused me of "collapsing ethics into aesthetics." I don't even know what that means. What I need is a proper breakdown: what are the distinct things "separate the art from the artist" could mean, which of them are plausible, which are nonsense, and which of them would actually tell me I'm wrong to feel icky watching Chinatown? If the slogan is just saying "a bad person can make a good film," then fine, obviously — but it feels like people are using it to say something much stronger, and I want to know what that stronger thing is and whether it holds up.
