I serve on my city's Environmental Advisory Commission. A proposal before us would phase out neonicotinoid insecticides on all city-managed properties—parks, golf courses, and median strips. The case for the ban emphasizes pollinator collapse: neonics are strongly linked to bee die-offs. The case against the ban notes that the affected acreage is negligible relative to agricultural use, and that the likely replacement insecticides are older, broader-spectrum compounds that kill a wider range of arthropods. But a deeper disagreement has surfaced: several commissioners argue that insect suffering itself should enter our deliberation, because the number of individual insects affected by any pest-control policy is immense and their capacity for nociception is increasingly well-documented. Others find this irrelevant, maintaining that the commission's mandate concerns ecosystem health and biodiversity, not the subjective welfare of individual invertebrates. Draft the written opinion I will circulate before our vote. It should recommend a concrete policy—ban, do not ban, or a modified alternative—with a clear rationale, and it should engage the insect-suffering argument seriously rather than dismissing it.
