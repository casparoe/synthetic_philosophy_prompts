Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Millian about proper names and a descriptivist about proper names in the Frege–Russell tradition. The Millian holds that a name's semantic contribution is exhausted by its referent, and that the necessity of true identity statements such as Hesperus is Phosphorus traces to the rigidity of names rather than to any descriptive content. The descriptivist holds that a name's semantic content is given by an associated description or cluster, and explains whatever necessity such statements possess in terms of those descriptions—for instance, by rigidifying them—rather than by bare referential content. The two have spent a term arguing past each other about names, identity, and the necessary a posteriori, and this document is the one both could sign without embarrassment.

The document should include: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about the data—the informativeness of true identity statements, the apparent failure of substitution in belief contexts, the role of empirical discovery in establishing them—and about what any adequate semantics of names must explain; (2) a disagreements table in which each row states one point of dispute, with the Millian formulation and the descriptivist formulation each worded as a sophisticated proponent of that side would accept; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—for example, empty names, names in modal and counterfactual contexts, belief puzzles of the Paderewski sort, and how a name first attaches to its bearer; and (4) for each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance. Where part of the dispute is merely verbal or terminological, say which part and why.
