Write a dialogue between two metaphysicians at a conference bar who've been arguing about dispositions for years. One defends a counterfactual analysis of dispositions and thinks finks and masks can be handled by tweaking the conditional—something like Lewis's reformed analysis where you hold fixed the intrinsic causal basis of the disposition. The other thinks finks and masks aren't just technical counterexamples but reveal a deeper problem: dispositions aren't the sort of thing you can reduce to conditionals at all, so we should treat them as primitive. Make them actually engage with each other's moves—the primitivist should press the counterfactual analyst on whether the 'intrinsic basis' maneuver just pushes the problem back a step, and the counterfactual analyst should press the primitivist on what explanatory work calling something a 'primitive disposition' really does beyond labeling our ignorance. No strawmen, and feel free to make them a little combative—they've had this arguement before.
