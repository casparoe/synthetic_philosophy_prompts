Draft the joint statement that a committed Malcolmian (who holds that dreams are not experiences occurring during sleep but are instead dispositions to produce narratives upon waking, the telling being criterial for having dreamt) and a committed Dennettian (who holds that dreams are experiences, though their temporal properties and relation to memory may be quite unlike what introspection suggests) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should include the following sections.

First, a numbered list of claims both sides can accept: about the empirical facts of REM sleep and dream reporting, about the fallibility of memory and the reality of confabulation, and about which naïve or extreme views both reject.

Second, a disagreements table giving the precise formulations of where they part ways — each entry phrased so that its own side would accept the wording, and with no straw-manning. At minimum, the table should address the status of dream reports as memories of prior events, the conditions under which a mental event can be said to occur at a given time, and whether the temporal content of a report settles the timing of what is reported.

Third, a list of cruxes: the sub-questions on which the broader dispute turns, such that settling them would move at least one side. I expect these to include questions about what work the concept of "experience" is doing in each account, whether concurrent cognitive capacities (judgment, concept-application) are necessary for undergoing an experience, and what principle links the time at which a subject can first report a content to the time at which that content was first entertained.

Fourth, for each crux, a concrete case or experimental paradigm both sides would agree bears on it — for instance, cases involving dream reports that are elicited only after a delay, or cases where narrative coherence emerges gradually — with each side's prediction about the outcome recorded in advance.

If you conclude that any part of their dispute is merely verbal or rests on equivocation over "experience," say which part and why. Format the document with the agreed-claims section numbered, followed by the disagreements table, followed by the cruxes with their associated tests and predictions.
