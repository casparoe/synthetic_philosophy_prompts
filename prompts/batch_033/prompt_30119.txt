I've somehow ended up co-chairing an adversarial collaboration at our safety org after a long, increasingly unproductive Slack argument about iterated amplification and distillation, and I'm worried the final writeup will either paper over the disagreement or just restate both sides' opening positions. One camp -- call them the compositionalists -- holds that if you decompose a hard task into subtasks a human overseer can actually supervise, run the amplification step to produce a better policy, and then distill it back down to a fast model, you can iterate this without loosing the original goal, because each step only ever optimizes against a human-checkable signal. The other camp -- the leakage skeptics -- thinks decomposition is exactly where alignment leaks: the overseer can only grade subtasks they can already check, distillation copies input-output behavior rather than the reasons behind it, and the small per-step gaps aren't noise that washes out; they get selected for and compound across iterations.

Draft the joint statement this collaboration should produce. I'd like a numbered agreed-claims section both camps can sign, followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be substantive, not just definitions. The disagreements should be stated precisely enough that each side would accept the wording of its own row. Then give me the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side -- and for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I half-suspect one crux is whether the overseer's ability to check a subtask and the system's competence at that subtask come apart, and another is whether the distillation step preserves alignment or just input-output behavior on the training distribution; use those if the camps would actually accept them, otherwise find better ones. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Don't adjudicate, and don't include a bibliography or citations -- the document has to be useful to the two camps themselves, not to an outside reader.
