I'm taking a philosophy of religion elective this semester, and we recently spent a couple of classes on J. L. Schellenberg's argument from divine hiddenness. I've been trying to work through it on my own, and I keep getting stuck on what feels like a slippery slope problem with the reasoning.

As I understand it, Schellenberg's central claim is that a perfectly loving God would ensure that anyone capable of a personal relationship with God, and not actively resisting one, would at least believe that God exists—because you can't have a relationship with someone you don't think is real. Since there seem to be sincere seekers who don't believe, the argument concludes that a perfectly loving God doesn't exist. So far so good. But here's what bothers me: if we accept that a loving God would remove epistemic obstacles to relationship, why would God stop at mere belief in His existence? Wouldn't a perfectly loving God also ensure that sincere, non-resistant people believe the right things about God's nature—for instance, that God is a Trinity, or that salvation comes through faith rather than works, or whatever the correct theology happens to be? After all, holding wildly false beliefs about someone also seems like an obstacle to a genuine personal relationship with them. If I believed my mother was a stranger who wished me harm, I wouldn't really have a relationship with my mother, even if I believed she existed.

It seems like the logic of the hiddenness argument, if sound, would commit us to saying that a perfectly loving God would prevent not just non-belief but also serious theological error among non-resistant people. And since serious theological error is rampant—devout people across different religions hold mutually incompatible beliefs about God—this would make the argument even stronger, maybe too strong. But Schellenberg doesn't seem to go there, and I'm not sure why. Is there a principled distinction between the obstacle of non-belief and the obstacle of false belief that I'm missing? Or does the argument actually have the broader implication I'm describing, and if so, does that make it more plausible or less?
