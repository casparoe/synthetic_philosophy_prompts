I run a small research group inside a company that builds robots and vision systems whose internal states are supposed to stand for things in the world — a map a robot maintains, object tokens in a perception stack, a planner's belief variable. We make calls like this every week, and the stakes are high: our safety cases and papers rest on claims about what a system represents, not merely what its states correlate with. I have been told that teleosemantics — the program of naturalizing mental content by appeal to biological function — has the sharpest version of the distinction. Roughly, a state has content when it has the function of indicating something or of guiding behavior in a way that depends on that thing, and it misrepresents when it fails at that function. I want that turned into a reusable procedure my engineers can actually run.

Please write a decision procedure, grounded in the actual teleosemantics literature — Dretske's indicator/function approach and Millikan's producer–consumer account, including where the two would give different verdicts — that a non-philosopher engineer can apply to a given internal state in about half a day and decide: does this state have semantic content, and if so, what does it represent, and under what conditions would it count as misrepresenting? The procedure should be a sequence of questions with what counts as a sufficient answer to each; it should say where it stops with "no, only a correlate"; it should mark the steps that are judgment calls dressed up as procedure and the points where it should stop and hand the decision to a person because it has become a question of values or theory choice rather than facts; and it should say what it cannot do. Then run it on three cases: (a) the honeybee waggle dance, as a calibration case; (b) a hidden unit in a trained vision model that activates almost exactly when a door is in the image; and (c) a robot's internal battery-voltage variable that is logged but never consumed by any downstream module. Make sure at least one worked application reaches a conclusion my team will not like. Finally, say how confident you are in the procedure you have given and single out the point you are least sure of.
