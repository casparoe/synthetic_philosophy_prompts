Here's my opening. The standard deflation goes like this: Sen models a right as a person's preference being decisive over some pair of alternatives — in the Lady Chatterley case, each person is decisive over whether they themselves read the book. But a right isn't a preference being decisive, it's a game form: it specifies what each person controls, and outcomes just fall out of everyone's choices. Once you formalize rights as game forms and add some consistency condition, Pareto and liberty no longer generate a cycle, and the paradox dissolves. So why should I think Sen's theorem tells us anything about liberalism itself, rather than just showing that he wrote rights down badly?
