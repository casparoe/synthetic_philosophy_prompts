A friend of mine has been reading about the metaphysics of dispositions and sent me the following summary, which he presents as a fair statement of where the debate landed. I have a feeling it overreaches in a couple of places, but I don't know the literature well enough to say exactly where.

"Philosophers used to analyze a fragile glass as simply one that would shatter if struck. Then came finkish dispositions: a glass could be fragile, but a guardian spirit might be poised to render it shatterproof an instant before any impact, so the glass would not shatter if struck. Since the glass is plainly still fragile, the simple conditional analysis must be wrong. There are also masks: a fragile glass packed in shock-absorbing foam would not shatter if the crate were struck, yet it remains fragile. The standard fix is to say that a disposition is really a matter of the object's internal structure — its causal basis — which would produce the characteristic manifestation in the absence of interference; finks and masks are just two forms of interference. This shows that dispositions are intrinsic properties of objects, and it also shows that two objects with the same disposition must share the same internal structure."

How fair is this summary? Go through it claim by claim and tell me what a philosopher working on dispositions would accept, what they would qualify, and what they would reject. I'm especially suspicious of the last two sentences: does either the intrinsicality claim or the shared-structure claim actually follow from the fink and mask cases? And I'd like you to separate what is settled in this area from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, rather than presenting one side of a live dispute as the consensus.
