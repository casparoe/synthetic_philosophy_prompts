I'm a PhD student in philosophy of psychiatry, and in three weeks I'm presenting a paper at my university's psychiatry department grand rounds. The paper argues that Ian Hacking's concept of looping effects — the process by which classifying people changes them in ways that alter the classification itself — has been underappreciated in DSM revision debates, and that taking looping effects seriously would change how we evaluate diagnostic categories: not just by their reliability and clinical utility, but by tracking whether a category is stable under classification, whether it is drifting because classified people are responding to it, and whether that drift is benign or pathological. I'm not arguing we should abolish diagnosis or that mental disorders aren't real; I'm arguing that the reality they have is partly constituted by the classifications we apply, and that this matters for revision.

I've spent years reading rationalist blogs and I'm trying hard to avoid the standard failure mode where a philosopher gives a talk that sounds subtle to other philosophers but makes practitioners roll their eyes. I want this to land as something clinically relevant, not as an exercise in epistemic hygiene for its own sake.

The audience will be mostly clinical psychiatrists and psychiatry residents, plus a handful of psychologists and social workers. The department is research-oriented but not philosophically inclined — they care about treatment outcomes, diagnostic reliability, and not having their clinical judgment second-guessed by outsiders. There will also be one or two faculty who know the social construction literature and are hostile to anything that sounds like it. I've been told the Q&A at grand rounds here is sharp and sometimes adversarial. I need a prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this audience, ordered roughly by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way a skeptical clinician would phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is softest. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
