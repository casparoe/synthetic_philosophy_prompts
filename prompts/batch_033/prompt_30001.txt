I am a third-year undergraduate currently taking a course in social epistemology, and I have been trying to think clearly about a situation with my older brother. Over the past two years he has come to get nearly all of his information about politics and current events from a single, very tight-knit online community. It is not simply that he reads different sources from me. The community has its own trusted figures, its own running interpretations of events, and, most strikingly, a well-practiced way of handling outside information. When I send him a report from a mainstream newspaper or an academic study, he does not dispute the details. He says that the outlet or the researchers work for institutions the community has already exposed as compromised, that their disagreement is itself evidence that the community's picture is correct, and that my willingness to trust those sources shows I have not yet seen through the system. He describes himself as doing his own research and says that I am the one in a closed information loop.

I do not want to argue politics with him, and I am not asking which of us has better beliefs. What I want is a diagnosis. Is there something epistemically defective about his situation over and above the fact that he and I draw on different sources? And does the answer bear on how I should respond — in particular, on whether continuing to present him with contrary evidence is likely to do anything, or whether his position is structured so that such evidence can be absorbed without changing his mind? I would rather understand the structure of the problem before deciding whether to keep engaging.
