Write the joint statement that a counterfactual analyst of dispositions and a primitivist about dispositions could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By "counterfactual analyst" I mean someone who thinks a disposition ascription like "fragile" can be analyzed as something like: x would manifest M if given stimulus S, absent interfering factors — they've spent years patching the analysis to handle finks and masks. By "primitivist" I mean someone who thinks dispositions (powers, capacities) are basic modal properties, and that the counterfactuals are only rough guides to them. Both sides agree on the standard data: the glass packed in styrofoam is still fragile even though it wouldn't break if struck (a mask), and the wire hooked to a safety device that makes it go dead the moment before a conductor touches it, so it wouldn't shock if touched, is still live now (a fink), so the crude conditional analysis is false. Where they split is over whether a repaired conditional analysis with a "no interference" clause can be made non-circular and adequate, or whether the whole project of analyzing dispositions into conditionals has to be given up.

Please draft the document they'd produce: a numbered list of claims both can sign; then a table of the precise residual disagreements, with each side's own wording of its view; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and for each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Try to make the agreed claims substantive, not just "there are finks and masks," and try to catch any part of the dispute that's really about words.
