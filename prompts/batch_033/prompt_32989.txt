I was discussing philosophy of science with a friend who had just finished a popular book on Thomas Kuhn, and he offered me the following summary of what he took away from it:

"Kuhn basically showed that the idea of scientific progress is a myth. Science doesn't gradually converge on the truth — it just lurches from one 'paradigm' to another, and because these paradigms are incommensurable, there's no neutral standard you can use to say one is better than the last. Scientists don't switch paradigms because of evidence; they switch because of social pressure, professional incentives, and the charisma of the new theory's advocates. That's why Kuhn says a new paradigm only wins when the old generation of scientists literally dies off. So in the end, science is just a series of revolutions with no rational direction — it's politics by other means."

I've read some of Kuhn myself — enough to feel that this is a caricature — but I'm not enough of a specialist to pin down exactly where it goes wrong. I know Kuhn did say provocative things about paradigm change not being governed by algorithmic rules, and I know he used the word "incommensurable," which sounds on its face like it means "can't be compared." But I also recall that he wrote a fair amount of later clarification pushing back against what he regarded as misreadings of his work, which makes me suspect my friend's summary is collapsing several distinct claims into one dramatic thesis.

I'd like you to go through this summary claim by claim and tell me what a careful reader of Kuhn would accept, what they'd push back on, and what they'd say is simply wrong. I'm especially interested in whether the final slide — science as "politics by other means" — actually follows from anything Kuhn argued, or whether that's a move my friend is making on his own. Please give me a committed answer: don't just lay out the range of interpretive options and leave me to choose. Tell me which reading you think is best supported by the text and why, and flag any places where the popular book my friend read is probably smoothing over distinctions that matter.
