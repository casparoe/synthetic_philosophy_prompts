In a discussion group on language acquisition, someone dismissed a recent computational model that learns syntactic patterns from child-directed speech with the remark that "the poverty of the stimulus" already rules it out. I think that phrase is being asked to do several different jobs at once, and I'd like to be precise before I respond. Lay out the distinct claims that "the poverty of the stimulus" could be asserting, ordered from the weakest and least controversial to the strongest, and for each say whether it is roughly trivial, plausible but contested, or likely false, and what kind of evidence would be needed to settle it. In particular, separate claims about what the child's input does or does not contain, claims about how many grammars are compatible with that input, and claims about what a domain-general learner could in principle extract from it. Then say which of these readings would actually rule out the statistical learning model in question, and whether the person's dismissal depends on a reading stronger than the evidence typically offered for the slogan.
