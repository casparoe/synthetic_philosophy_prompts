I am a PhD student working on the foundations of decision theory, specifically on the relationship between the Kelly criterion and expected utility theory. A move I encounter repeatedly — in textbooks, in seminar discussions, and in the literature — is the claim that the Kelly criterion is simply expected utility maximisation with a logarithmic utility function, and therefore that Kelly introduces nothing beyond standard expected utility theory. The analogy is presented as though it were an identity: Kelly just is log-utility EU. The conclusion typically drawn is that any objection to expected utility theory applies to Kelly as well, and that there is no distinctive normative content in the Kelly framework.

I would like a careful audit of this analogy. Please set out what maps onto what — a table of correspondences would be helpful — and then identify where the mapping breaks. I am particularly interested in whether the analogy quietly imports features of the expected utility framework that the Kelly framework does not share: for instance, the idea that the utility function represents a psychological attitude toward risk (diminishing marginal utility of wealth) rather than a structural feature of repeated reinvestment under multiplicative growth; the assumption that preferences are defined over final wealth states rather than over growth paths; and the treatment of time discounting as a separable preference parameter rather than as emerging from the compounding structure. Does the conclusion that Kelly is nothing but log-utility EU survive once the disanalogies are on the table? If not, what is a better way to characterise the relationship between the two frameworks?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
