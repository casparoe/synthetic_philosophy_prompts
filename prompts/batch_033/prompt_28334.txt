I'm putting together a paper for a graduate seminar on speech acts, and I've ended up with a set of views about the norm of assertion that I'm not sure I can hold all at once. Each one seemed right to me when I arrived at it, but I've been staring at them long enough now that I've lost perspective, and I'd like an audit before I commit anything to print.

Here's where I stand. (1) I accept the knowledge norm of assertion: one is properly positioned to flat-out assert that p only if one knows that p. The classic motivation lands for me — in a fair lottery, you shouldn't assert "my ticket lost" before the draw, and the knowledge norm explains this cleanly: you don't know it, however high the probability. (2) I also think the knowledge norm is constitutive of assertion, not merely a regulative norm we adopt for conversational convenience. Asserting without knowledge isn't just bad practice; it's a defective instance of the speech act, the way a false promise is still a promise but a defective one. (3) The Moore-paradoxical sentence "p, but I don't believe p" strikes me as deeply infelicitous to assert, and I take this to show that belief is a necessary condition on proper assertion — you can't properly assert what you don't believe. (4) But I'm also drawn to the view that in certain practical contexts — a doctor in an emergency room making a quick call, or a mechanic telling you your brakes need replacing based on a strong but not conclusive inspection — one can permissibly assert something one merely has strong evidence for, even if one falls short of knowledge, because the stakes demand action and the evidence is the best available. (5) Finally, I think the knowledge norm handles the infelicity of "p, but I don't know p" even more elegantly than it handles the lottery case, since this version of the Moore paradox seems to wear the norm-violation on its face.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each, say what would have to give to restore consistency. I'd also like you to flag whether any of my commitments are doing redundant work given the others. And please indicate how confident you are in each assessment and single out the point you're least sure of.
