I'm on the technical advisory board of a regional flood-control authority, and we're about to vote Thursday on whether to keep relying on a streamflow forecasting model that everyone agrees is built on assumptions we know are false. It treats the whole catchment as if it were a uniform sponge, assumes flows are laminar when they plainly aren't, and collapses a bunch of distinct infiltration processes into a single fitted parameter. The awkward part is that its predictions are genuinely good—better then the two "realistic" competitor models that try to represent the actual physics, which keep producing worse flood forecasts.

Half the board wants to replace it anyway, on the grounds that you can't base flood-control decisions on a model whose assumptions are false; the other half keeps invoking the old Friedman line that unrealistic assumptions don't matter as long as the model predicts well. I've been asked to circulate a short written opinion before the vote. Please draft it: it should recommend a concrete course of action (keep the model, replace it, or keep it with conditions such as an annual audit of the assumptions), give reasons the other side can actually engage with, and honestly deal with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than just restating the slogan.
