Ok so I'm trying to nail down my philosophy of math views before I have to defend them in a seminar and I realised I've never actually checked whether all these things I believe can live together. Here's what I'd say if you asked me:

(1) I'm a platonist. Numbers, sets, functions — they're real, they're abstract, they don't depend on us thinking about them, and they'd exist even if there were no minds at all.

(2) My main reason for (1) is the indispensability argument. Our best scientific theories quantify over mathematical objects in a way that's ineliminable, and we should be ontologically committed to whatever our best theories need. That's basically the Quine–Putnam line.

(3) I also think reference has to be grounded causally somehow. Not in some crude "you have to bump into the thing" way, but if there's genuinely no causal connection at all between a term and what it's supposed to pick out, I don't see how the term manages to refer to that thing rather than something else or nothing. This seems right to me for pretty much any domain.

(4) Mathematical knowledge is real knowledge. We know that 2+2=4, we know there are infinitely many primes, and this isn't just warranted assertibility within a game — it's truth-tracking, fact-stating knowledge.

(5) I'm a naturalist about epistemology. The methods that produce knowledge are the kind of thing we can investigate empirically, and epistemology shouldn't float free of how we actually form beliefs as creatures in the world.

So my question: does this set hang together, or am I tacitly helping myself to something that has to go? I can feel that there's at least one tension in here but I'm not sure which pairs are the real problem and which only look like they are. If you could go through these and flag the genuine conflicts, rank them by how serious they are, and tell me what the cheapest fix would be for each, I'd appreciate it. And if any pair that looks bad actually isn't, say why.
