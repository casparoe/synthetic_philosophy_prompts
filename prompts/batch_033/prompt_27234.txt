I recently read a popular philosophy book that summarizes Galen Strawson's Basic Argument like this:

"Strawson proves that free will is impossible. His argument is simple: you are only responsible for your actions if you are responsible for the kind of person you are — your character, desires, and dispositions. But you didn't choose your character; you just find yourself with it. And even if you did try to change your character, you'd have to rely on character traits you already had to do so, which you also didn't choose, and so on backward indefinitely. Since the regress never bottoms out in a self-created self, nobody is ever truly responsible for anything they do. The practical conclusion is that our practices of blame, punishment, and moral condemnation rest on a mistake and we should work to eliminate them."

I'm not sure whether this is a fair rendering. It sounds plausible to me, but I've heard that Strawson's view is more nuanced than the summary suggests. Could you go through this summary and tell me what a philosopher familiar with Strawson's work would accept, what they would push back on, and whether the practical conclusion actually follows from the argument as Strawson presents it?
