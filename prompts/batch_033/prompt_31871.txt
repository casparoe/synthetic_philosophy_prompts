Okay so my friend and I just spent like two hours arguing about Arrival — the Villeneuve film, though we also talked about the Chiang story it's based on. The premise, for anyone who hasn't seen it: linguist Louise Banks is tasked with deciphering the language of alien visitors (the heptapods), and as she becomes fluent in their written language — which is non-linear, with no directionality, all the semantic relationships in a sentence laid out simultaneously — her perception of time shifts. She starts experiencing her own life non-linearly, seeing her future (including her daughter's death) before it happens. The film basically dramatizes a strong Sapir-Whorf / linguistic determinism: learn a language, restructure your cognition.

My friend was arguing this is a beautiful illustation of how language shapes thought. But I kept pushing back from the language of thought hypothesis side — Fodor's idea that thinking happens in a species-wide, innate mentalese, and that natural languages are just peripheral systems for encoding and decoding thoughts that are already structured in mentalese. Under LOTH, learning a new natural language shouldn't restructure your basic cognition; you're just learning a new mapping from mentalese to external symbols. The heptapods' language might be cool, but it wouldn't literally let you see the future. My friend countered that I was being boring and missing the point of the film entirely.

But then I started wondering if I'm being too uncharitable. Maybe the film actually shows something LOTH misses, or maybe LOTH and the film's metaphysics can be reconciled if you think about the heptapod language not as a natural language but as a direct externalization of a different mentalese — like, the heptapods literally think in a non-linear mentalese, and Louise isn't just learning a surface grammar but rewiring her own mentalese through immersion. Or maybe the film is actually a reductio of linguistic determinism, and I'm supposed to find it implausible? I'm not sure anymore.

I'd love a careful reading of Arrival through the language of thought hypothesis: where the film challenges LOTH, where LOTH gives us reason to be skeptical of the film's physics and cognition, and whether there's a version of the story that respects both.
