For next week, I'd like you to argue that tranquilism — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters — is self-effacing in a way that's genuinely damaging to the view, not just a bullet its defenders can shrug off. Here's the pressure point: if only the absence of craving and suffering matters, then the desire to persuade other people to become tranquil is itself a craving, and the distress you'd feel at all the suffering still going on elsewhere is itself suffering. So a consistant tranquilist seems pushed toward not caring wether anyone else ever adopts the view. Make the strongest case you can that this is a real problem for tranquilism.
