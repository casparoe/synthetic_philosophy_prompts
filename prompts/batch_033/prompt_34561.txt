Before I read another proposed solution to the problem of eliciting latent knowledge (ELK), I want the specification that any adequate solution must meet, so I can judge proposals against a standard rather than against their own framing. Take the problem in the following form. A model is trained to answer questions using feedback from human supervisors who can grade only some of the answers. On the questions the supervisor cannot grade, the model may nonetheless form an accurate internal judgment that the supervisor could not verify: consider a model trained on a vault's sensor data that can tell that a diamond has been swapped for a convincing fake, even though a supervisor looking only at the same sensor feed could not distinguish the two. The challenge is to elicit that latent judgment — to train the model to report what it actually knows — rather than training it to produce whatever answer the supervisor is most likely to approve, which is what the straightforward training objective would do. By a solution I mean a general training method or objective, not a manual fix for one particular model or one particular question.

Write the desiderata: a complete list of what any adequate solution to this problem has to deliver. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Pay particular attention to the distinctions a solution must not collapse — for instance, the difference between a model that holds a judgment but declines to assert it and a model that never formed the judgment at all — and to the failure modes a solution must remain robust to once the model understands the elicitation procedure and can game it. Then do three things. (1) Identify any pair of desiderata that are in tension or jointly unsatisfiable, and explain the source of the tension. (2) State which single requirement does the most work in ruling out superficially attractive proposals, and why. (3) If some requirement that is often assumed is in your view a prejudice of one camp rather than a genuine constraint, name it and justify removing or weakening it. A table is fine for the requirements themselves.
