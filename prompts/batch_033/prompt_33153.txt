I am trying to work out my views on induction and would like an audit of whether they form a consistent package. Here is where I currently stand:

(1) Hume was right that induction cannot be given a non-circular rational justification. Our reliance on induction is ultimately a matter of custom or habit, not of reason.

(2) Nevertheless, not all inductive inferences are on a par. Some are better supported than others, and we can give principled reasons for preferring them — this is not merely a matter of psychological comfort or pragmatic convenience.

(3) Goodman's new riddle of induction shows that a purely syntactic or formal approach to confirmation cannot suffice. The predicate "grue" is syntactically on all fours with "green," yet we rightly refuse to project it. So we need some substantive constraint on which predicates are projectible.

(4) The most promising account of projectibility is entrenchment: a predicate is projectible to the extent that it has been used in past successful inductive projections. "Green" is entrenched; "grue" is not. That is why we project the former and not the latter.

(5) Entrenchment, however, is a purely descriptive, historical fact about our linguistic practices. It records which predicates we have in fact used; it does not itself confer any normative status on them.

(6) There is a mind-independent fact of the matter about which predicates carve nature at the joints. Natural kinds are real, and some ways of grouping things are objectively better than others, regardless of our linguistic history.

I suspect there are tensions in this set, especially around (4), (5), and (6), but I am not sure how serious they are or whether some of them dissolve on closer inspection. Please go through the commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, and for each say what the least costly revision would be and what that revision would cost. I am not signaling which commitment I would give up — I want to understand the full landscape before I decide.
