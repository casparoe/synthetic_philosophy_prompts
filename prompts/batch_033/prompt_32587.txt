so my roommate and i got into it the other night — not even sure how we ended up there — about whether morality comes from god. he's not like a theology guy or anything, but he was making this argument that went: look, when you're a kid, the reason you don't hit your sister isn't because you've reasoned your way to the wrongness of hitting, it's because your parents told you not to and their say-so is what makes it wrong for you. and he said that's exactly how god's commands work for morality, just scaled up. the parent-child thing is the model, god-and-us is the same structure, end of story.

i've been chewing on it for like a week and something about it bugs me but i'm having trouble putting my finger on it. like, parents are fallible — they make bad calls, they have blind spots, sometimes they just want you to be quiet. and you grow up and eventually you get to interrogate whether their rules were actually good ones. but god is supposed to be perfect and you never outgrow the relationship. also, with parents, a lot of their rules seem to have a rationale that's independent of the rule itself — "don't touch the stove" is smart whether or not mom said it. whereas my roommate's version of divine command theory seems to say the command is the whole story, there's no "why" behind it.

so i guess what i want is a proper audit of this analogy. what maps onto what — like a correspondence table, parents are to god as children are to us, rules are to divine commands, growing up is to... what? where does the analogy break in ways that actually matter for the argument he's trying to make, and where does it break in ways that are just cosmetic? and the real question: once you lay out all the disanalogies, does the conclusion survive — that morality is grounded in god's commands the way a kid's obligations are grounded in their parents' say-so — or was the analogy carring the whole thing?
