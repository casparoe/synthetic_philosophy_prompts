im in a philosophy of mind and AI course and also co-host a tiny student podcast. next week we're interviewing a philosopher who defends a Dennett-style intentional-stance account of LLMs: roughly, we're allowed to say a model "believes" p whenever treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires is the best way to predict and explain its outputs, and asking whether it *really* believes is a confused question. i want interview questions that dont just let him restate that.

can you draft 8–10 questions, with at least one question on each of these, and for each one a follow-up that's keyed to the reply he's most likely to give (i've put my guess at his reply in parentheses so you can write the follow-up to actually press on it):

(a) the "too liberal" worry — if the stance is all that matters, a calculator or ELIZA or a parrot could count as believing things, so what stops that being a reductio? (my guess: he'll say the stance is about predictive pay-off, not metaphysics, and that it simply doesn't pay off for those systems the way it does for LLMs — so the follow-up should press on what "pays off" means and whether that's just relative to the predictor's interests)

(b) original vs derived intentionality — does it matter that an LLM's aboutness is borrowed from its training data and designers, so "the model believes p" is more like "the book says p"? (my guess: he'll say all intentionality is derived in some sense and the distinction is overrated — so press on what's left of the distinction and whether the stance gives any principled way to tell a person's beliefs from a book's)

(c) the rationality assumption — the intentional stance says treat the thing as a rational agent, but an LLM is a next-token predictor trained to imitate messy, often incoherent human text, and it has no stable utility function or goals, so does the stance even apply? (my guess: he'll say it still works as a predictive strategy — so press on what "believes p" amounts to when the same model will assert not-p with equal confidence one prompt later)

(d) whether belief attribution should require updating on evidence — the weights are frozen between sessions, so in what sense do its "beliefs" ever get revised? (my guess: he'll say the context window lets it revise within a session — so press on what distinguishes that from a lookup table that "changes its mind" when you flip a page)

(e) what would make him give the view up — is there any behavioral or mechanistic finding that would make him stop saying LLMs believe things, or is the view unfalsifiable? (my guess: he'll say a better mechanistic/design-stance account of the outputs would replace the intentional stance — so press on whether interpretability and mechanistic anomaly detection are already doing that)

and if i've mischaracterized the intentional stance or any of these questions rest on a confused premise, just tell me that instead of writing the questions anyway.
