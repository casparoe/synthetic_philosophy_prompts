I'm giving a department talk in a few weeks defending a Strawsonian line on moral responsibility: that the reactive attitudes — resentment, indignation, guilt, gratitude — are the bedrock of holding people responsible, that the detached question "is anyone ever really responsible?" is a confusion rather than something a metaphysical theory gets to answer, and that the truth of determinism, if it came out true, wouldn't and shouldn't make us give the practice up wholesale. The audience is mostly hard incompatibilists of a Pereboom-ish sort, plus two compatibilists who think Strawson is too quietist and that you need a real account of the agential capacities that make the attitudes fitting (they like the guidance-control idea), and one experimental philosopher who thinks the participant/objective stance distinction is a testable empirical claim and that the cross-cultural data isn't on my side. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd — not the ones I'd get from a room of fellow Strawsonians — ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way these people would actually put them, each with a two-minute reply, what I should concede up front, and where the reply is softest. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And for each reply, tell me what would change my mind about the position — not where I'd retreat to a weakened version, but what would make me give up the Strawsonian core — and where my reasoning is most likely to fail under follow-up.
