I've been working through a version of negative utilitarianism that posits a lexical threshold between severe suffering and mild pleasure, and a colleague has handed me an argument that seems to show the view collapses into absurdity. I cannot locate the misstep, and I would like a precise diagnosis.

(1) If the disvalue of pain is commensurable with the value of pleasure, then for any pain, however severe, there is some finite aggregate of mild pleasures whose value outweighs it.

(2) The lexical-threshold view denies exactly this at the severe end: no amount of mild pleasure can justify deliberately inflicting torture-level pain on an unwilling person. That is the commitment I want to keep.

(3) But lexical priority cannot be a matter of degree. If whether a pain was lexically prior to pleasure depended on how intense the pain was, then intensity and value would be varying together along a single scale — and a single scale just is commensurability, which by (1) means finite trade-offs are available at every point. So a degree-dependent "lexical priority" would not be lexical priority at all.

(4) Therefore, if any pain is lexically prior to pleasure, that priority cannot attach to some intensities and not others; it must attach to pain as such.

(5) Hence every pain, however mild, is lexically prior to every amount of pleasure.

(6) That is absurd. It implies that a momentary twinge is a graver matter than any quantity of happiness, that it would be wrong to cause someone a fleeting headache no matter how much good resulted, and that no addition of pleasure can improve a world containing one mild pain.

(7) So the view faces a dilemma: either give up the torture intuition in (2), or accept (5). Since (5) is unacceptable, the lexical-threshold view fails.

I am not willing to accept (5), and I do not think (2) is where the error lies. But I cannot identify a step I am entitled to reject, and I want to know what the least costly rejection commits me to. My standing worry is that any repair will leave me having to specify where on the intensity continuum the threshold falls, and that this just reopens the arbitrariness objection: why that point rather than a nearly identical one? Where exactly does the argument go wrong, if it does, and what is the price of fixing it?
