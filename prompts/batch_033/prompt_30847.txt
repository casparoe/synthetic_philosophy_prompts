I just had a long argument with a friend about what Alan Turing would have made of today's AI alignment problem. We both know his 1950 paper and his later remarks about building a child machine and educating it rather than trying to program adult-level intelligence directly. My friend argued that Turing's whole framework — treating machine intelligence as something cultivated through a kind of upbringing — commits him to seeing alignment as a problem of education and socialization, not of specifying goals or constraints in advance. I countered that Turing also explicitly considered the possibility that machines would eventually outstrip human control, and that his educational analogy might break down precisely when the machine becomes more capable than the teacher. We couldn't settle it. What would Turing's views on learning machines and education actually commit him to, if he were confronted with the modern alignment problem as we now understand it? Would he see it as one unified challenge or as several distinct problems? And would his child-machine framing turn out to be a helpful guide or a misleading metaphor?
