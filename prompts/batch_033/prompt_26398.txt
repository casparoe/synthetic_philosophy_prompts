Everettians have been arguing about the Born rule for decades and, as far as I can tell, the debate has completely stalled. One camp — roughly the decision-theoretic line — says that once you accept the branching structure, a rational agent deciding between acts should weight her successor branches exactly the way the Born rule says, because those are the only weights that make her preferences coherent. The other camp says this is circular: the theory says every branch happens, so there are no probabilities left to derive, and any "rationality" argument is just smuggling the Born rule back in as a norm about which branches to care about. Both sides accept the same formalism and the same experimental results, and nobody moves. My hypothesis is that the disagreement is really about what "probability" has to mean in a deterministic branching world, not about the physics at all.
