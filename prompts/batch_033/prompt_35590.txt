I've been trying to think through whether harms that happen in virtual spaces — like losing something valuable in an online game, or having your digital work destroyed — are real harms in the same sense that physical harms are. I can see arguments on both sides, and I want to work through a carefully designed case to make progress.

Construct a thought experiment that meets the following specifications:

- The scenario involves two harms: one virtual, one physical. The harms should be matched for the amount of psychological distress they cause — the victims are equally upset by what happened to them.
- Despite the match in distress, the virtual harm should be clearly the more serious moral wrong. The point is to test whether the "virtualness" of a harm reduces its moral significance independently of its psychological impact.
- The asymmetry must not come from downstream physical consequences (e.g., the virtual harm leading to job loss, illness, or physical danger). It should be about the harm itself.
- Use only technology that exists today — no brain-computer interfaces, no simulated realities, no uploading.
- Keep the scenario to one paragraph.
- The harms should be of kinds that ordinary people could experience in their daily lives.

After presenting the scenario, do the following:

(a) Explain why the virtual harm is the more serious wrong in this case, and identify which feature of the case is doing the work.

(b) Say what someone who holds that virtual harms are not real harms — or are always less serious than physical harms — would have to claim about the case to resist the conclusion. Where exactly would they push back, and would that pushback force them into any positions they might not want to accept?
