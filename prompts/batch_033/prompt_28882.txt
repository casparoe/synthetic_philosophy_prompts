Analyze the 2018 Uber self-driving car fatality in Tempe through the lens of the frame problem. Here are the facts you can rely on: the car was in autonomous mode at night when it struck Elaine Herzberg, who was crossing a road outside a crosswalk with a bicycle. The system's sensors detected her about six seconds before impact, but the software could only classify an object as a pedestrian if it was near a crosswalk — so for the next five seconds it kept reclassifying her as a vehicle, a bicycle, or an unknown object. Each time the classification flipped, the system treated her as a brand-new object and lost her trajectory, so it never calculated that a collision was likely. The Volvo's built-in emergency braking had been disabled, and a feature called action suppression blocked braking for a full second even after the system realized a crash was unavoidable, handing control to the safety driver — who was watching a TV show on her phone and didn't brake until after impact.

I want you to use this case to work through the frame problem, not just summarize it. First, seperate the two things people call the frame problem: the narrow logical problem of representing what doesn't change after an action (McCarthy and Hayes), and the broader epistemological problem of how a finite agent determines what's relevant without running down an infinite regress of side effects (Dennett). Then map the Uber system's behavior onto each: which version of the problem does the crash illustrate, and does the distinction hold up under the example? What would a frame-problem-aware control system have to do differently — and is there any way of doing it that doesn't just push the relevance problem to a different module? Finally, what does this episode do to the standard deflationary line that the frame problem is just an engineering difficulty rather than a genuinely philosophical one — does the crash support that view, or does it show that the engineering fix was always hostage to a prior relevance judgment that no amount of code can settle?
