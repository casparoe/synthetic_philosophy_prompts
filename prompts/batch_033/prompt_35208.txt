I'm organizing a workshop on the philosophy of medicine, and one of the sessions will be devoted to the long-running debate between naturalism and normativism about the concept of disease. Rather than have another round of papers each defending one side, I want to try something different: I'm asking two senior participants — one who has defended a broadly naturalist account of disease (on which disease is a matter of biological dysfunction, specifiable in value-free terms) and one who has defended a broadly normativist account (on which disease is ineliminably value-laden, because it essentially involves harm or undesirability) — to engage in an adversarial collaboration and produce a joint document. The idea is that they spend the months before the workshop working together to identify what they actually agree on, formulate their disagreements precisely in terms each side would accept, and isolate the cruxes — the sub-questions that, if answered, would genuinely move at least one of them.

The deliverable should be a single document with two main sections. The first section is a numbered list of claims that both participants can sign: these might include claims about paradigm cases they agree are diseases (or are not), claims about what a good account of disease should explain, and claims about which features of the world are not in dispute between them. The second section is a disagreements table, with columns for: (i) the precise statement of the disagreement, in wording each side would accept for its own position; (ii) the crux or cruxes the disagreement turns on; and (iii) for each crux, a concrete case, piece of evidence, or argument that both agree bears on it, along with each side's prediction about what that case or argument will show. I also want them to flag, in a brief note at the end, any part of their long-running dispute that they now believe was merely verbal.

A few pressures I would like the document to address. One is whether the debate is really about the meaning of the word 'disease' or about the nature of the phenomenon itself — and if it is about word meaning, what turns on the answer. Another is the status of controversial cases: is infertility a disease? What about aging in non-human organisms? What about conditions that are statistically normal in a reference class but harmful, or statistically abnormal but not harmful? Both sides need to say what they predict about these cases and why. A third is the meta-question of whether settling the disease concept is a scientific project, a conceptual analysis project, or a normative project — and whether the two sides might actually agree on that meta-question more than they think. Finally, I want the document to address whether either side's view implies that the boundary between disease and health is sharp or vague, and whether they agree about what makes it one or the other, since that bears on how much precision we should expect from an account of disease in the first place.
