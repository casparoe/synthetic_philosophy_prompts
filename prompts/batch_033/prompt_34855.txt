I'd like your credence that eternalism is the correct metaphysical account of time — the view that past, present, and future objects and events exist equally, and that "now" functions as an indexical (like "here") rather than picking out an objective, metaphysically privileged moment. The alternative I have in mind is presentism: only present entities exist, and the apparent reality of past and future is to be explained entirely in terms of present-tense facts — present records, present memories, present laws, and so on. I am setting aside the growing block theory as a distinct view for now, though you may bring it in if it affects your reasoning.

I don't want a survey of the literature or a "reasonable people disagree." I want a number between 0 and 1, and I want you to decompose it. In particular:

1. List the sub-questions your credence most depends on. These might include: whether special relativity forces a B-theoretic, eternalist picture, or whether a Lorentz-invariant formulation of presentism is viable; whether the phenomenology of temporal passage favors presentism and how heavily to weight that kind of evidence; whether tensed language requires a tensed metaphysics or can be given an adequate tenseless semantics; whether the truthmaker problem for past- and future-tensed claims — what makes "Caesar crossed the Rubicon" true if the event no longer exists — decisively favors eternalism; whether the growing block theory is a stable intermediate position or collapses into one of the poles under pressure; and any other sub-questions you think carry weight.

2. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question (conditional on the relevant background), with one or two sentences of justification per term.

3. Show how these combine to yield your overall credence. If the dependencies are not straightforwardly independent, say how you are handling that.

4. Identify which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to — the one where a shift in your probability would produce the largest change in the bottom line.

5. Finally, tell me what evidence or argument would move that most-sensitive sub-question the most, and by how much you think your overall credence could realistically shift if you encountered it.
