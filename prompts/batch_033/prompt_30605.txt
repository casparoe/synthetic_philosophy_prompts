Write a sestina on the theme of competence without comprehension, the two strange inversions of reasoning associated with Darwin and Turing. Before Darwin, the design-like adaptedness of living things was naturally explained by a designer who understood what he was making; Darwin inverted the order of explanation, showing that blind variation and natural selection can produce that design-like order without foresight, purpose, or understanding. Before Turing, intelligent performance was naturally explained by an inner agent who understood what it was doing; Turing inverted that explanation, showing that rule-governed symbol manipulation can produce intelligent-seeming performance without any part of the system comprehending the task. The poem should render these inversions accurately rather than merely name them: a reader who knows the view should recognize it in the imagery and in the turns of the stanzas, and a reader who does not should still find a coherent poem about order that no one intended.

Use the standard sestina form, and let the form do philosophical work. The six end-words are design, mind, rule, chance, order, machine. The fixed permutation by which a sestina rotates its end-words is itself an instance of the thesis, since a complex and apparently inevitable pattern arises from the mechanical iteration of a rule rather than from a semantic plan; the poem should submit to the constraint and let the sense be discovered inside it. Across the six stanzas, move from the commonsense picture in which design and mind come first, through Darwin's inversion, and then to Turing's, allowing each return of an end-word to accrete new meaning. In the three-line envoy, use all six end-words, two per line, and turn the poem's own machinery back on the reader, who is another case of comprehension built up out of uncomprehending competences.
