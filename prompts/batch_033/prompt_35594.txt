Write the joint statement that a commited defender of the self-sampling assumption and a committed defender of the self-indication assumption could both sign after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration on anthropic reasoning. I don't want a transcript of their arguments or a ruling on who's right; I want the document they would actually produce together.

It should include:

- a numbered list of claims they both accept, covering what they agree on about the God's coin toss case, the doomsday argument, and the presumptuous philosopher: the setup of each case, what the case is supposed to isolate, and anything they both think people get wrong about it;
- a disagreements table. For each row, give the neutral statement of the issue, then the SSA-side wording of their view and the SIA-side wording of their view, each phrased so its own side would sign it rather than a caricature;
- the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions that, if settled, would actually move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is whether your own existence is evidence that there are more observers, and another is whether the presumptuous philosopher's conclusion is a reductio or just a bullet they have to bite, but don't take my word for it;
- for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance (for example, what each would say about a coin-toss variant with a much bigger number of observers on tails, or an incubator variant where you wake up and can see which room you're in);
- if you think part of their dispute is verbal or dissolves under a sharper formulation, say which part and why.

The point is to get formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted, and to separate the real disagreement about how to reason from any talking-past-each-other about the cases themselves.

Oh, and write the whole thing in American English.
