ok so i've been noticing this thing that happens in group projects and clubs and honestly just anywhere people make decisions together. here's the pattern: we're a group trying to decide something, and there's some option that most of us think is a bad idea. but one person disagrees and thinks it's fine, and instead of convincing the rest of us they just go ahead and do it. like they send the email, or make the purchase, or post the thing. and now we're all stuck with it because walking it back would be super awkward or literally impossible. the weird part is this keeps happening even when everyone knows it might happen — like we can see the dynamic coming and still can't stop it.

is there a name for this? i know about stuff like the bystander effect and groupthink but those seem different — those are about people not acting when they should, this is about one person acting when the group would've said no. i'm wondering if philosophers or game theory people or whoever have a term for it. and i don't just want a label, i want to understand what the exact conditions are that make it possible. like does the action have to be irreversible, or is it enough that reversing it is costly or embarrasing? and is it the same thing when the person knows they're going against the group vs when they just didn't check first?
