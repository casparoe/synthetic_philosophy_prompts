I'm in a course on social dynamics and rational choice this term, and we just did informational cascades — the sequential-choice setup where you get a private signal but also see what earlier people picked. A blog post I read summarized the idea like this: "In an information cascade, people rationally infer that the crowd knows more than they do, so they copy the people ahead of them even when their own private signal points the other way. After two or three people have made the same choice, everyone else rationally ignores their own signal and follows along. Since each person is being rational, the whole group can still end up locked into the wrong option. Cascades are fragile, though: one well-informed person can break one, which is why they don't last long in the real world." It sounds mostly right to me, but something about the "crowd knows more than they do" line and the "one well-informed person can break one" part feels of, and I don't trust my instincts here. Could you go through it and tell me which parts a careful presentation of the model would accept, which parts are subtly misleading, and whether the last sentence follows from anything true in the summary?
