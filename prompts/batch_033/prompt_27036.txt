I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I have been reading papers across systematics, microbiology, and conservation biology. I keep noticing a practice I do not have a word for. Within a single paper, "species" gets used in what look like two or three different ways without any flagging: one section treats species as interbreeding populations, another as diagnosable lineages, and a third as units for conservation counting. I have seen the same two populations treated as one species in a section on gene flow and as two species in a section on conservation priorities. The shifts are not sloppiness — in each section the choice does real work, and forcing the paper to settle on one definition would make the argument worse. I know this is somewhere near "species pluralism," but that is usually a philosophical thesis about how many species concepts there are, not about the practice of deliberately moving among incompatible species concepts inside a single piece of scientific reasoning. Is there an established name for that practice? If not, tell me what the nearest concepts are, how exactly the practice differs from each of them, and propose a term with a definition and its boundary cases.
