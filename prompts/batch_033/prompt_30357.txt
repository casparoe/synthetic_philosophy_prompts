A friend and I argued at length about verse translation. He maintained that any translation that preserves rhyme and meter must distort the literal sense, so the only honest translation is a plain prose paraphrase; a version that keeps the form is an adaptation or imitation, not a translation. I could not refute him, yet I was left with the strong intuition that the prose paraphrase loses the poem. If form is nothing but a constraint that forces departures from meaning, why should losing it matter? Is the prose version actually the more faithful translation, and is our preference for verse just a sentimental attachment to sound?
