Every few years the IQ heritability fight flares up and then stalls in the same place. Both sides accept roughly the same numbers — adult IQ heritability in developed countries is maybe .5 to .8, it rises with age, shared environment effects fade — but one side comes away saying the trait is "largely genetic" and the other says heritability is being misread as destiny or as a limit on what environments can do. My working theory is that the real disagreement isnt about the numbers but about what heritability licenses you to infer: whether it's a fact about individuals or about populations, whether within-group variance components tell you anything about between-group differences, whether a high estimate means the trait cant be changed by intervention, and whether an estimate computed in one range of environments even travels to another. But I don't think that's the whole story, since plenty of people on both sides know the technical definition and still land in different places. What kind of disagreement is this, actually — verbal, substantive, about what counts as the relevant environment or the relevant question, or just a proxy for a political fight? And given your diagnosis, is there any move that could actually unstick it?
