I spent my career as a natural scientist and have only recently begun reading philosophy of biology seriously. Please make an argument for the view that viruses are best understood as organisms with a two-stage life cycle: the virion as a dispersal stage, functionally akin to a spore, and the infected cell as the metabolically active stage. Take seriously the strongest objection to this view—namely, that in the intracellular stage there is no separately bounded viral individual, only a host cell reprogrammed by viral genes, so what is called the virus is at best a temporary state of the host. Then state explicitly what your position commits you to about plasmids and other mobile genetic elements, and whether that consequence is one you accept.
