My jurisprudence tutor's been pretty consistant across the term on where the criminal law should stop. So far she's said she'd:

- Uphold the ban on selling human organs, even between consenting adults
- Strike down laws criminalising homosexual acts between consenting adults
- Uphold mandatory seatbelt legislation
- Strike down bans on same-sex marriage
- Uphold the criminalisation of heroin possession for personal use
- Strike down laws prohibiting the sale of sex toys

Next week's seminar is on blasphemy. The question is whether she'd vote to uphold or strike down a law that criminalises public blasphemy — not incitement to religious hatred, just causing grave offence to religious sensibilities. (The actual blasphemy laws in England and Wales were abolished in 2008, but we're treating it as a live proposal.)

I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the principle she seems to be running, what other principles fit her track record equally well, and which of them come apart on the blasphemy case. If you had to bet, what would she say, which of her earlier cases is the closest analogue, and what further case would cleanly settle which principle she's actually using?
