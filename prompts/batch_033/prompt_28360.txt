ok so I'm in a philosophy of probability reading group and we're supposed to write up our own views before next week. here's where I've landed — tell me if this hangs together or if I'm papering over a crack somewhere:

(a) for repeated, well-defined setups like dice and cards, I'm a frequentist. probability = long-run relative frequency in a reference class. this seems obviously right for games of chance.

(b) for scientific theories and everyday reasoning, I'm a subjectivist. there's no ensemble of universes to ground a frequency for "dark matter exists" or "it'll rain tomorrow," so probability here is just rational degree of belief, obeying the axioms and updating by conditionalization.

(c) for single-case quantum events — a particular radium atom decaying in the next hour — I think propensities are the right interpretation. there's an objective tendency that's neither a frequency (it's a one-off) nor anyone's belief.

(d) I endorse the Principal Principle: when you know the objective chance of an event, your credence should match it.

(e) I'm a pluralist about all this. I don't see why we have to pick one interpretation for everything. different jobs, different tools.

(f) in practice, when more than one interpretation might apply to the same case, they don't contradict each other — they give compatible numbers.

where's the problem? be specific — which pair is in actual tension and which only looks that way, and what's the cheapest thing I'd have to give up to fix it.
