I've been running a reading group on formal epistemology for a couple of years now, and we spent the last six weeks on the Sleeping Beauty problem. You know the setup: Beauty goes to sleep Sunday night, a fair coin is tossed, if heads she's woken Monday and the experiment ends, if tails she's woken Monday, has her memory erased, and is woken again Tuesday. Each time she's woken she's asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. The thirder says 1/3, the halfer says 1/2.

What's driving me a bit mad is that after reading Elga, Lewis, and a bunch of the back-and-forth since, I don't think anyone in the group has actually changed their mind. And it's not because we don't understand each other's arguments — we can all reconstruct the thirder's betting argument and the halfer's reply about double-counting, we all get the centred-worlds formalism, we've walked through the technicolour-beauty and the sailor's-child variants. It feels like we've reached a point where the disagreement isn't about any particular step in anyone's reasoning but about something deeper that neither side can argue the other into.

So here's what I want: a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement the Sleeping Beauty debate actually is. Is it a substantive disagreement about probability? Is it a verbal dispute about what 'credence' means in self-locating contexts? Is it two camps answering different questions — one about what Beauty should bet, one about what her evidence supports — under the same heading? Or is it a case where the question itself is somehow ill-posed, and the fact that smart people keep disagreeing is evidence of that? I have a hunch it might be about whether self-locating beliefs are fundamentally the same kind of thing as ordinary propositional beliefs — that the halfers are treating 'today' as indexical in a way that dissolves into a reference-class problem, and the thirders are treating it as a genuine uncertainty that credences should range over — but I'm not sure that's right and I'd like someone to push on that.

Don't give me a survey of the arguments — I've already read those. I want the diagnosis: what the structure of the impasse is, and given that diagnosis, what (if anything) would actually count as progress. A theorem? A new thought experiment? A conceptual distinction everyone's been missing? Or should we just accept that the dispute isn't going anywhere and stop expecting it to?

Oh, and could you end with a one-sentence summary of your diagnosis as the very last line? Something I can quote back to the group.
