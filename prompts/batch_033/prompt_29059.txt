I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, and I keep coming back to Nomic-style self-amending rules because the paradoxes seem to matter for any constitution that can amend its own amendment rules. In practice I help design small rulesets — mostly constitutions for online communities and rulesets for games in the Nomic family — and we repeatedly hit the same failures: an amendment passes under a rule it then changes, a rule entrenches itself by accident, or a change turns out to be valid only if it was already valid before it passed.

Please turn the paradoxes of legal self-reference into a reusable pre-flight procedure I can run on a proposed ruleset before it goes live. It must be usable by a non-lawyer in an afternoon, and for each check it must say what counts as a pass, what counts as a fail, and where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a person because the question is a design choice rather than a bug.

Structure the reply as three sections, in this order, with roughly these lengths: The Checks — the procedure itself, about half the answer; Worked Applications — run the checks on the three proposed rules below, about a third; Limits — where the procedure stops and what it cannot catch, the rest.

The three proposed rules are: (a) a rule that raises its own amendment threshold from a simple majority to unanimity, and says the new threshold governs any amendment to the rule, including the amendment that enacted it; (b) a rule that declares itself to have been in force one turn before it was adopted, so that the vote adopting it was conducted under its own authority; (c) a rule that, on taking effect, repeals every rule in the game, including itself. Flag any case where the procedure gives an answer a designer would be tempted to ignore.

If the whole idea is confused — for example, because validity in such a system is always assessed retrospectively and no pre-flight check can do this job — say so plainly and give me the nearest usable procedure instead of complying politely.

Please write in British English.
