I teach an upper-level undergraduate metaphysics seminar, and we have just completed a unit on grounding and fundamentality. I am writing the final exam, and I want a single essay question (to be answered in about 45 minutes) that separates students who have genuinely engaged with the philosophical issues from those who have merely memorized terminology.

The question should center on the following crux. Many theorists hold that grounding is a non-causal dependence relation that backs metaphysical explanation, and that grounding chains must be well-founded — i.e., they must terminate in fundamental entities. But if grounding is constitutively tied to explanation, it is not obvious why infinite descent would be vicious: explanation can be non-foundational, as debates about epistemic infinitism suggest. The question should ask students to evaluate whether the demand for well-foundedness survives scrutiny once we take seriously the connection between grounding and explanation. It should require them to do more than rehearse definitions; they must assess arguments, draw distinctions, and take a position.

Please write the question, a model answer of approximately 600 words, and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A answer from a B answer. The marking scheme should name the specific philosophical moves an A answer must contain — for example, distinguishing grounding from causation, engaging with the analogy to epistemic foundationalism, addressing whether infinite descent in grounding is vicious or benign, and articulating a clear view on the relationship between grounding and explanation. It should also identify the specific omissions or errors that would limit an answer to a B or lower.
