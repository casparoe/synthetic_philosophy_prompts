For a dissertation chapter on the epistemology of machine learning, please argue that the Wolpert–Macready no-free-lunch theorems should not be read as a formal vindication of Humean skepticism about induction. Defend the position that the theorems' uniform average over all possible target functions (or all possible data sequences) is not a neutral, prior-free baseline but a highly specific meta-inductive assumption, and that the correct conclusion is therefore domain-relative: there is no universally justified inductive policy, but this does not impugn the possibility of justified induction once a domain structure is assumed. Address the strongest objection to this line, namely that it merely relocates the problem of induction into the choice of prior or inductive bias, and explain what the theorems nonetheless contribute to our understanding of why induction succeeds when it does.
