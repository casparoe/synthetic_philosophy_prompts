I sit on the welfare-standards committee for a mid-size aquaculture certification body, and we are in the middle of revising our code for invertebrate handling. Our current standard covers fish and basically ignores anything without a backbone, but two of our biggest retail buyers now want octopus and cuttlefish covered, and one producer group is threatening to walk if we extend anything to crabs or lobsters, which they insist are basically reflex machines with no one home. The commitee is split and nobody on it has the background to settle the question, so I've been told to come back with a defensible proceedure rather than a position. We face a version of this every few months when a species gets added to the standard or a producer challenges a listing. The decisions matter: a code provision changes how animals are stunned, chilled, transported and slaughtered, and it gets audited, so the stakes are commercial and legal as well as ethical.

What I want is a decision procedure my committee could actually run, grounded in the distribution of consciousness across animals as philosophers actually debate it. I don't need a literature survey — I need the working distinctions: the difference between nociception and felt pain, whether consciousness is binary or graded, whether self-awareness matters for welfare or only valenced experience, how to weigh behavioral markers against neuroanatomy, and what a precautionary principle should do when the evidence is thin. The procedure has to be usable by someone who has read maybe one popular article on animal minds, has about two hours per species, and has to survive a board meeting with a shrimp farmer on one side and a cephalopod researcher on the other before the board signs off. It should say which questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, where it says insufficient evidence, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is a values call rather than a factual one.

Then run the procedure on three cases: an octopus, a blue crab, and a Pacific oyster. I want to see the path each case takes through the steps and the resulting tier (full protection, limited protection, no protection, or insufficient-evidence-with-precautionary-handling). Make sure at least one of the outcomes is one my board will hate, and be explicit about what the procedure cannot settle and where a smart person could reasonably reverse the call.

Format: first give the procedure itself as a table with columns: Step, Question to ask, What counts as a yes, What counts as a no, Action if yes, Action if no or insufficient evidence, and Judgment call? (where the last column says whether that step is a factual test or a value decision). Then, after the three worked cases, give me a JSON object with fields: species, evidence_inputs (the specific facts the case turned on), path_through_steps (the step numbers and outcomes), resulting_tier, confidence, and residual_uncertainty. Make the JSON valid with no comments.
