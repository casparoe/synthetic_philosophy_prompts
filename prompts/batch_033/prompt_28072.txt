I'm teaching an intro metaphysics unit and I want to use Christopher Nolan's The Prestige to get students into the identity of indiscernibles. If you haven't seen it in a while: Angier gets Tesla to build him a machine that duplicates whatever's placed inside it. Every night he steps in, a duplicate appears some distance away, and the original (or is it the original?) falls through a trapdoor into a locked water tank and drowns. He says he never knows whether he'll be the prestige or the man in the box. The duplicates share all intrinsic properties at the moment of creation — same body, same memories, same desperate obsession with Borden — but they're numerically distinct.

This feels like it's staging Black's two-spheres thought experiment in narrative form: two objects indiscernible in their intrinsic properties, yet two, not one. But I'm not sure the analogy holds up under pressure. In Black's case the spheres are in a perfectly symmetric universe so they share relational properties too — that's what makes it the hardest case for the Leibnizian. In the film the duplicates start diverging almost immediately (one drowns, one takes a bow), so their relational properties differ. But right at the instant of duplication, before the trapdoor triggers, are they in a Black-spheres situation? And does the film give us something the thought experiment can't — the first-person experience of being one of a pair of indiscernibles?

I want to walk my students through what the film gets right about the metaphysics and where it smuggles in issues about personal identity that aren't really about indiscernibility at all. Like, Angier's horror at not knowing "which one" he'll be — is that a coherent worry if the duplicates are genuinely indiscernible? Would a Leibnizian say there's no fact of the matter about which one he is, because there's no property that distinguishes them? What scenes would you point the class to, and what would you flag as the film cheating by leaning on psychological continuity rather than pure metaphysics? I'm trying to make sure they don't just remember the cool twist and forget the philosophy, you know?
