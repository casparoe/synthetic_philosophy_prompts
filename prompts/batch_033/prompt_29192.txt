I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on free will and moral responsibility — sixteen students, one ninety-minute session. I want an activity that makes them feel the force of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument before they read it, rather than just nodding along with a reconstruction on the board.

Design a structured group exercise I'll call a "Responsibility Audit." Here is the constraint I want to build into the rules: a participant may only claim that an agent is morally responsible for some action or outcome if they can also claim the agent is responsible for whatever feature of the agent's psychology, character, or decision-making produced it. If they cannot ground responsibility for that prior feature, the chain stops and the claim of responsibility for the original action is, by the rules of the exercise, unsupported.

Give me:

- A concrete scenario (or two) of a wrongful act rich enough to generate several plausible "responsibility chains" — something students will initially feel confident about attributing blame for.
- The exact rules and sequence of the exercise: how groups are formed, what they produce, how long each phase takes, and how the results are collected and displayed.
- A debrief script that maps what happened in the exercise onto the steps of the Basic Argument: the regress, the impossibility of causa sui, and the conclusion that ultimate moral responsibility cannot get started. The mapping should use specific moments from the students' own chains.
- An honest note on what the exercise inevitably stacks the deck toward. The rules as stated make compatibilist stopping points look like failures to follow the rules. Tell me how to handle that in the debrief — what the compatibilist would reject in the setup itself, and how to present that as a live option rather than as special pleading.
- A prediction of what will go wrong the first time I run it: where students will try to short-circuit the regress, what they will say, and how I should respond in the moment without giving away the argument prematurely.
