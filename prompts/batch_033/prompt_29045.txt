I've been following the debate about aesthetic testimony for a couple of years now, and I'm starting to suspect that the two sides aren't really disagreeing about the same thing, even though they appear to be. On one side, the pessimists argue that you cannot legitimately form an aesthetic judgment on the basis of someone else's say-so: if I tell you that a novel is beautifully constructed, you aren't entitled to go around asserting that it's beautiful unless you've read it yourself. The intuition is that there's something second-rate, something epistemically deficient, about a belief like "the film is sublime" when it's held purely on the word of a trusted critic. The optimists reply that this makes aesthetic judgment uniquely epistemically isolated in a way that looks irrational — we rely on testimony for scientific beliefs, moral beliefs, and just about everything else, so why carve out an exception for the aesthetic? And they point out that in practice we do, in fact, defer to others' aesthetic judgments all the time when choosing what to read or watch, so the pessimist's position seems to conflict with ordinary practice. Both sides have been refining versions of these arguments for decades, and as far as I can tell, neither has budged.

What I want to understand is what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a verbal dispute about what counts as an "aesthetic judgment" — where pessimists are really talking about a kind of first-personal appreciation or acquaintance and optimists are talking about propositional knowledge that such-and-such is beautiful? Is it a normative disagreement about the proper role of autonomy in aesthetic life, with both sides agreeing on the descriptive facts but differing on what we should value or aspire to? Or is there a hidden empirical question — about how aesthetic judgments are actually formed, or about the psychology of aesthetic experience — that neither side has properly isolated and that, if settled, would break the impasse? I'm not asking for a verdict on who is right. I want a diagnosis of the structure of the impasse, supported by what each side characteristically says when pressed, and an honest answer about what, if anything, would count as making progress — a distinction that could dissolve it, an experiment that could settle it, or an argument that no progress should be expected and why.
