In William Satire's (Douglas Hofstadter's) satire "A Person Paper on Purity in Language," the narrator presents "white" as the natural, generic term for all people and ridicules proposals to replace it with "person" as artificial special pleading. But since "white" has never actually functioned as a generic term in English, whereas "man" genuinely has, doesn't the satire backfire? That is, doesn't it show only that we should reject invented, unnatural linguistic reforms, while leaving long-established generic terms such as "man" and "he" untouched?
