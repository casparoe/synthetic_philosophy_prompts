I was reading a popular econ blog post that summarized the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem like this:

"Gibbard and Satterthwaite proved that every possible voting system is vulnerable to strategic voting. As long as there are at least three candidates, no matter how you design the ballot or count the votes, someone will always have an incentive to lie about their preferences. The only system that is completely immune to tactical voting is dictatorship, where a single voter's top choice wins. So the theorem shows that all democratic elections are fundamentally broken: since any voting rule can be gamed, there's no meaningful sense in which an election outcome reflects the will of the voters, and trying to design a fair voting system is a waste of time."

I know enough to suspect the last bit is overblown, but I dont know exactly where the summary stops being accurate. Which parts would someone who actually works on social choice accept, which would they want to qualify, and does the pessimistic conclusion follow from the accurate parts?
