I'm trying to sort out the substantivalism/relationism debate in the philosophy of spacetime, and I keep seeing people evaluate proposals one consideration at a time — the bucket, the globes, the hole argument — without ever laying down what the theory is actually being asked to do. Before I read another defense of either side, I want the spec. Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of spacetime (substantivalist, relationist, or something weirder) has to deliver, where "adequate" means adequate as a story about what spacetime is, not just as a device for predicting relative motions. Include the cases it has to get right (absolute rotation and Newton's bucket, the two globes, Leibniz's shift, the hole argument, maybe Mach's rotating shell), the distinctions it can't collapse (kinematic possibility vs dynamic possibility, empirical equivalence vs theoretical equivalence, determinism vs underdetermination, observability vs reality), and whatever it has to say about why inertial structure exists at all. For each requirement, give the consideration that forces it. Then flag which desiderata are non-negociable and which are just the prejudices of one camp — I suspect "no unobservable spacetime points" is negotiable and "recover the observable symmetries" isn't, but I want that argued, not asserted. And if some subset of the list can't be jointly satisfied, given the hole argument or anything else, say so plainly: which combinations are live, and which are ruled out. Tell me which single requirement does the most work excluding the standard views, and whether any desideratum that both camps take for granted is actually up for grabs. Keep the scorecard short or skip it — the list is what I'm after.
