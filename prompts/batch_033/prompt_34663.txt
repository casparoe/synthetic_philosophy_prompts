I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about moral testimony — the practice of forming a moral view on the basis of what someone else tells me, rather than by working the moral question through myself. I've collected my gut reactions to a set of cases, and I'd like to know whether there is a single principle behind them, or whether some of them have to go. When a friend who has looked into it tells me that a certain café pays below the legal minimum wage, and I stop going there, I take that to be fine, since I am accepting her word only about the wage facts and drawing the moral conclusion myself. When a colleague announces, without offering any argument, that tipping is exploitative, and I find myself agreeing simply because I trust his judgment, that feels wrong, as if I have outsourced a moral view. When my partner tells me I was unfair in a family dispute, and I do not see it but accept it because she knows the people and the history better than I do, I think that is fine, even admirable. When I was a child and accepted my parents' view that cruelty to animals is wrong, that also seems fine, and not merely excusable on grounds of immaturity. When a junior doctor accepts a senior physician's judgment that continuing a treatment would be unethical, and withholds it, I think that is fine. When I am on a hiring committee, unsure whether a remark in an interview was sexist, and the rest of the committee, whose judgment I respect, all conclude that it was, I go along with them; I think this is fine, though it leaves me uneasy. When I read an essay defending the wrongness of the death penalty, cannot follow the argument, but still adopt the conclusion because the author is an eminent philosopher, that feels wrong. And when I discover that nearly everyone I admire favors a heavy tax on inherited wealth, and I shift my view to match theirs without having thought through the arguments, that also feels wrong, perhaps worse than the colleague case.

My tentative principle is something like this: it is acceptable to take over the non-moral facts on which a moral judgment rests, and acceptable to defer within relationships or roles in which the other person has better access to the relevant particulars, but it is not acceptable to adopt a moral verdict wholesale from someone I am treating as a moral expert. I can already see this wobbling, since the partner case and the doctor case look like wholesale deference, while the philosopher case looks like a refusal of the very expertise I allow in the doctor case. Is there a principle that gets all eight verdicts, or nearly all of them, and which of my verdicts does it force me to revise? And what does the resulting principle say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a close friend tells me that a mutual acquaintance did something unforgivable at a party I did not attend, and I accept that verdict on her say-so?
