Before I read any more in the philosophy of religion, I want the specification that the literature usually assumes rather than states: what would a theory of divine hiddenness actually have to get right? By 'divine hiddenness' I mean the phenomenon pressed by Schellenberg's argument — the existence of nonresistant nonbelief, that is, people who do not believe that God exists though they are not culpably refusing, suppressing, or distorting what they have been given — together with the felt absence of God reported by some believers. Write the desiderata for any adequate account of why a perfectly loving God would permit this.

For each desideratum, state it as a requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than an optional preference, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a commitment of a particular camp. The list must respect at least these distinctions rather than collapsing them: nonbelief versus culpable resistance; absence of propositional belief versus absence of a felt relationship; a de jure objection to the rationality of theistic belief versus a de facto argument against God's existence; and hiddenness as a general human condition versus the felt absence reported by committed believers. It must also be responsive to a range of cases, including at least the reflective lifelong nonbeliever, the apostate, the person who never encounters the relevant theistic teaching, and the committed believer in a period of felt abandonment; do not let any one case stand in for the rest.

Then flag any pairs of desiderata that you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and say why that matters. Finally, give a brief scorecard of the three main strategies — the Schellenbergian strategy, which takes the desiderata to be jointly unsatisfiable and concludes that theism is false; the freedom-based defence; and the soul-making or pedagogical defence — against your list. Keep the weight on the desiderata and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard.

Please write the answer in British English.
