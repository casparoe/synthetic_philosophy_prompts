I'm an epistemology PhD student and I've been following the norms of assertion debate for a couple years now, and honestly I'm starting to think its just not going anywhere. You've got the knowledge norm people (Williamson and co.) saying you should only assert p if you know p, the truth norm people (Weiner) saying nah it's just truth, and then Lackey and others pushing reasonable belief norms. And there are like ten other proposals too. Everyone has their counterexample cases — the lottery proposition cases, the Gettier cases, the Moorean paradox stuff — and each side has a story about why the other side's counterexamples don't actually land. It feels like we've been trading the same moves since the early 2000s and nobody's budged.

What I want is a diagnosis. Not a verdict on who's right — I want to know what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a verbal dispute about what we mean by "norm"? Like, are the knowledge-norm people and the truth-norm people just using "norm" differently (constitutive vs. regulative, or something like that) and if we cleared that up there'd be nothing left to fight about? Or is there a hidden empirical question in here — maybe it turns on how ordinary speakers actually judge assertability in weird cases, and we just don't have the right experimental philosophy data yet? Or is it that everyone's trying to answer different questions under the same heading — some people are asking what makes an assertion correct qua assertion, others are asking what makes an assertion epistemically responsible, and those are just different projects that happen to share a vocabulary?

I've got my own hypothesis but I want to hear yours first. And whatever diagnosis you give, I want to know what would actually count as progress. If it's a verbal dispute, what distinction would dissolve it? If it's empirical, what experiment? If the sides are answering different questions, how do we rephrase things so people stop talking past each other? Or — and I'm open to this — is the whole thing just ill-posed and we should walk away?

Oh and please write in American English. And don't give me a survey of the literature — I already know the positions, I want the diagnosis.
