I'm trying to get my views on Moore's paradox straight and would appreciate a consistency audit. Here's the package I've arrived at:

(1) Moore-paradoxical assertions — saying "p, but I don't believe that p" — are always irrational to make, even though the proposition asserted can be true. (I might, after all, believe p without being aware that I do.)

(2) What explains (1) is the knowledge norm of assertion: one should assert p only if one knows p. Since I cannot know "p and I don't believe p" — believing the conjunction would falsify the second conjunct — the norm blocks the assertion.

(3) Moore-paradoxical beliefs, by contrast, are possible and can be rationally held. Someone could have good evidence that it's raining while also having good but misleading psychological evidence that they don't believe it's raining. The resulting belief would be false, since believing the first conjunct entails that they do believe it's raining, but it need not be irrational.

(4) Self-knowledge of one's beliefs is fallible and sometimes inferential: we can be mistaken about what we believe, and figuring it out can require the same kind of evidence we use to figure out what others believe.

(5) An occurrent Moore-paradoxical thought — consciously thinking "p, but I don't believe that p" right now — is always irrational. The occurrent awareness makes the inconsistency manifest in a way that the thinker cannot rationally ignore, even if a merely dispositional version of the same belief could be rational.

(6) The irrationality in (1) and in (5) should have a unified explanation. I don't want one story about why the assertion is impermissible and a completely different story about why the occurrent thought is irrational.

I'm worried that (2) and (3) might be in tension, and that (4) might undermine (5). But I'm not confident I've located the real problems. Please go through these and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which single revision would resolve the most. I want a committed verdict on each — not a menu of options.
