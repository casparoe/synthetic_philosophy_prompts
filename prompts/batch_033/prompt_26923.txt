(2) For a belief to be about phenomenal consciousness, phenomenal consciousness has to be what fixes the belief's content — otherwise the belief isn't really about phenomenology at all, it's about whatever physical or functional property actually triggers it.

(5) Illusionism is itself a claim about phenomenal consciousness — namely, that it's an illusion.

(6) If no one has ever had a belief about phenomenal consciousness, then no one can genuinely believe illusionism.

(7) So illusionism, if true, can't be believed.

He says this isn't just a cheap "you can't believe your own view" trick — he thinks premise (2) is doing real work and that denying it forces the illusionist to say something weird about intentionality.
