I'm taking a seminar on rational choice and we've spent the last three weeks on transformative experience. I'm trying to pin down where I actually stand before I write my final paper, and I'd like an audit of whether my commitments are jointly tenable.

Here's where I've landed:

(1) I accept the core claim that there are experiences that are epistemically transformative in a principled way: you cannot know what it is like to undergo them without actually doing so, and this is not merely a contingent limitation of imagination or available information.

(2) I think this poses a genuine problem for expected utility theory. If you cannot assign utilities to the possible outcomes of a decision, then the standard maximizing framework simply does not apply, and it is not obvious what should replace it.

(3) I think people nonetheless make decisions about transformative experiences rationally all the time — deciding to have a child, to emigrate, to change careers — and it would be a reductio of any view that classified all such decisions as arational.

(4) I think the primary way people navigate these decisions rationally is by relying on the testimony of those who have undergone the experience: their reports of satisfaction, regret, and flourishing. Even though this testimony cannot convey the phenomenal character of the experience, it can convey whether people relevantly like them tended to be glad they did it.

(5) I also think there is a distinct class of transformative experiences — call them autonomy-expanding — that are rational to choose even in the absence of useful testimony, because undergoing them increases your future range of options or your capacity to make informed choices about further transformative experiences.

(6) I think the problem identified in this literature is real but not as deep as it is sometimes presented: it reveals a limitation of standard decision theory, but not a limitation of rationality as such. Rationality is broader than expected utility maximization, and the fact that a decision falls outside the latter's scope does not make it irrational.

I want to know: does this set hang together? Which commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the cheapest revision that would restore consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally problematic, and for each genuine tension, specify which commitment would have to give and what it would cost.
