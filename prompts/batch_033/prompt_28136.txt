Three students come from a Kantian or deontological background and will want to hear about rights and constraints, not just welfare trade-offs. One postdoc works on global priorities research and is friendly to suffering-focused views but worries about their rhetorical and epistemological robustness. Draft me a Q&A prep sheet: what objections am I going to get from this audience, phrased the way they would put them, ordered by a combination of how likely and how damaging they are, each with a reply I can deliver in roughly two minutes and a candid assessment of where the reply is weak.
