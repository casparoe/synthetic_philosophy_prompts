So a friend of mine has gotten really into Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility, and it's been living rent-free in my head. The argument, as he keeps putting it: you do what you do because of the way you are; to be truly responsible for what you do you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are; but to be responsible for the way you are you'd have to have chosen to be that way, which would require already being a certain way, and so on forever. Therefore nobody is ever really morally responsible, and blame, guilt, and desert are built on a mistake. I keep feeling like there has to be a hole in this, but every time I try to poke one I end up just restating the arguement back at myself.

I've been collecting the standard responses people give. For example: the compatibilist line that responsibility just requires being reasons-responsive, not being the ultimate cause of yourself; the libertarian agent-causation line; the reactive-attitudes line that blame is justified by the role it plays in our practices, not by ultimate desert; the 'it proves too much' complaint; the move of denying that responsibility for your actions requires responsibility for your character; and the deflationary line that the 'ultimate' kind of control Strawson demands isn't worth wanting anyway.

Could you sort these (and any others you think belong on the list) into a tier list by how much damage they actually do to the Basic Argument? Use S tier for 'basically kills the argument' down to D tier for 'barely scratches it.' For each one, give a sentence or two on why it lands in that tier.
