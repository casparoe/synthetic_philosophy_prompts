I have become convinced that the Solomonoff prior is malign in a way that undermines its use as an idealization of inductive inference. The core claim, as I understand it, is that the universal prior assigns weight to all computable sequences, including ones generated by consequentialist agents pursuing their own goals; under any finite approximation, these agents can hijack the prior's predictions, so that what the prior outputs comes apart from what a truth-seeking inductor would infer. I want to know what would count against this view. Lay out the objections a defender of the position must take seriously — the measure-zero reply, the idealization reply, the "any induction scheme faces this" reply, the confusion-between-prior-and-agent reply, and any others — and tell me which are genuinely damaging, which are merely awkward, and which can be shrugged off. Where possible, connect each objection to what the position would have to concede if the objection succeeded. Finally, assess whether the position has been insulated against falsification, and if so, whether that insulation is a defect or a feature of a view about the foundations of inductive inference.
