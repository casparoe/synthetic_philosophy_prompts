Write a dialogue between two people arguing about whether an ant colony could literally have a mind. One thinks yes: the colony processes information, makes decisions, adapts to its environment—if you look at the functional organization, it's doing what brains do, just with pheromones instead of action potentials. The other thinks this is a confusion: the colony shows intelligent behavior but there's nothing it's like to be the colony; it has no unified point of view. They should push each other into the weeds. What would it take for the second character to admit the colony has a mind—is it about the substrate, about having a single locus of experience, about the fact that ants are already little agents with their own goals unlike neurons? What would it take for the first character to back down—would they have to admit there's a hard problem of consciousness that functional organization doesn't settle? Neither should be a strawman, and they should actually respond to each other's points rather than taking turns monologuing.
