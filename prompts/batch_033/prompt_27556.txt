Construct a thought experiment in which a higher-level scientific generalization satisfies the conditions for Nagelian reduction to a lower-level theory but is also multiply realizable in a way that makes the higher-level taxonomy explanatorily autonomous. The case must be drawn from a real science—not a toy example—must fit in one paragraph, and must not appeal to exotic physics or speculative neuroscience. After presenting the scenario, provide the following sections:

**The Nagelian case for reduction** (roughly 150 words): State the higher-level generalization, the lower-level theory, and the bridge principles that connect them. Show that the derivation goes through on Nagel's criteria. Be explicit about which feature of the case makes the derivation possible.

**The non-reductive physicalist case for autonomy** (roughly 150 words): Explain why, despite the derivation, the higher-level taxonomy remains indispensable for explanation. Identify the pattern of multiple realizability in the case and show why it blocks explanatory reduction even if it permits derivational reduction.

**What drives the disagreement** (roughly 100 words): Identify the precise feature of the case that allows both sides to claim it as a victory. Is the disagreement about what counts as reduction, about the relationship between derivation and explanation, or about something else?

**How to break the symmetry** (roughly 100 words): Describe a small change to the case that would force one side to concede. Which side concedes, and why does the change break the symmetry?
