I'm in a grad seminar on Bayesian epistemology and we've been going back and forth on the problem of old evidence for like three weeks now. You know the setup: if E is already known (so P(E)=1), then by the standard Bayesian formula E can't confirm any theory T because P(E|T) = P(E) = 1 trivially. But obviously old evidence does confirm theories sometimes — the classic case is the precession of Mercury's perihelion, which was known for decades before general relativity came along and explained it, and everyone treats that as a major confirmation of GR. So something's gotta give.

We've read Glymour's original presentation, Howson's counterfactual move (where you conditionalize while pretending you don't know E), and a bit of Garber on logical learning. The seminar is split down the middle: half think Howson basically fixed it, the other half thinks the whole thing exposes a deep crack in Bayesianism that no patch job can really seal. My advisor keeps saying "it depends what you mean by 'solved'" and I'm getting tired of that move.
