"Here's the conceivability argument in a nutshell: if you can conceive of something without contradiction, it's possible. That's the guiding principle. Descartes used it to argue for dualism — he could conceive of his mind existing without his body, so they must be distinct substances. Chalmers updates this: imagine a zombie, a being physically identical to you down to the last particle, but with no consciousness — no felt experience, no qualia, total darkness inside. If you can conceive of this zombie without contradiction, then zombies are metaphysically possible. And if zombies are possible, physicalism is false, because physicalism says the physical facts fix all the facts. The standard killshot against this is that we can conceive of impossible things all the time — people conceived of water not being H2O, or the morning star not being the evening star, but those are impossible. Chalmers's reply is that in those cases we were conceiving under a partial description; once you know the full story, the conceivability vanishes. But with consciousness, he says, we have the full story from introspection — we know our own mental states directly and completely. So there's no hidden essence to discover, and the conceivability of zombies really does tell us something about how the world is. The upshot is that physicalism is probably false, or at least we have a strong prima facie case against it."
