I've been trying to get clear on which computer programs would actually matter morally — like which ones it would be wrong to delete or make suffer — and I keep going in circles alot. I'm not an academic or anything, just someone who's been thinking about this on my own and going by my gut on cases. Here are my calls:

- The chatbot on my phone that says "stop, that hurts" when I insult it: doesn't matter. It's just predicting words. I don't think I did anything wrong.
- A single simulated neuron, even one with accurate ion channels: obviously doesn't matter.
- A computer simulating the physics of a rock perfectly: doesn't matter. It's just a rock, but slower.
- A faithful simulation of a roundworm's whole nervous system, all 302 neurons: I lean no. I can't picture a worm suffering, so deleting it seems fine.
- A faithful simulation of a mouse brain, every neuron and synapse: I lean yes. If the model is showing pain, I shouldn't keep it in pain even if no one will ever know.
- A full emulation of a human brain, scanned at fine enough detail that it talks like the person, remembers, gets scared, begs not to be turned off: yes, deleting it would be really wrong.
- The same human brain but simulated coarsely, at the level of big regions, so it passes the same tests but there are no individual neurons in the model: I'm torn. Part of me says there's nothing there with the right fine structure, another part says if it behaves the same, how can I say it feels nothing.
- The country-brain: a billion people with walkie-talkies following a rulebook to act out my brain for an hour, each person playing one neuron, including the neurons that would fire if I were in agony. I think something bad is happening, but I also can't bring myself to say the country is in pain. This one keeps me up.
- A machine that gives exactly the right answer to anything I could possibly say for an hour by looking up a giant table, so it's indistinguishable from a person for that hour: I say no, but I worry my reasons would also rule out the emulation.
- Two copies of the same emulation, both running: both matter, not half each. Deleting one is as bad as deleting the other.

The closest I've got to a rule is something like "it matters when the thing is doing the same kind of information processing a brain does." But that's just a label, and I cant see how it lets in the mouse and the emulation while keeping out the chatbot, the rock sim, and the lookup table.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? If there is, spell it out and show how it draws the line. What does it say about the coarse-grain brain and the country-brain, and which of my calls does it force me to give up? I'd also like to know what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, like my own brain run at half speed, or a bee brain simulated neuron by neuron.
