Write a villanelle about pragmatic encroachment where the two refrains are the knowledge claim from a low-stakes bank case and its retraction from the high-stakes version—something like "the bank is open Saturday, I know" and "but do I really know?" The refrains should alternate so the reader feels how the same proposition shifts from known to not-known as the stakes rise, and when they finally meet in the closing couplet the poem should capture the weirdness of both claims being true in their own contexts. Don't name-drop the view or lecture about it; let the form do the philosophical work. Someone who knows the DeRose bank cases should recognise them, and someone who doesn't should still feel the conceptual vertigo.
