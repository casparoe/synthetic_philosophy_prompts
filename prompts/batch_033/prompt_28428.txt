I keep running into "the will of the people" used as a conversation-ender, most recently in an argument about whether a referendum result should override a court ruling — the other side just said we have to respect the will of the people and acted like that settled it. But I honestly can't tell what the phrase is supposed to pick out. Somtimes it sounds like a factual claim about what a majority currently wants; sometimes like a normative claim that whatever the majority wants should win; sometimes like something almost mystical, where "the people" is a single agent with a single will that only populist leaders can hear. What are the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, from the trivial to the radical, and which of those readings actually do the work when someone uses it to end a policy argument? I'd like a menu of readings with verdicts — which are defensible, which are just confused, and which would actually support treating the phrase as settling anything — so I can push back next time instead of just getting annoyed.
