I've been trying to work out what I think about the moral equality of combatants — the thesis that soldiers on both sides of a war have the same permissions to kill — by testing my intuitions against cases. Here are my verdicts:

1. A soldier fighting for a just cause who kills an enemy combatant in open battle acts permissibly.
2. A soldier who knowingly volunteers for an unjust war of aggression and kills an enemy combatant in open battle acts impermissibly.
3. A conscript forced into an unjust war under credible threat of execution who kills an enemy combatant in open battle does not act impermissibly — I'm inclined to say his act is permissible, or at minimum fully excused.
4. A soldier in an unjust war who has been raised on state propaganda, reasonably but falsely believes his side is just, and kills an enemy combatant in open battle — I find I cannot condemn him the way I condemn the knowing volunteer in (2). His act feels excused, though I'm not sure it's permissible.
5. A soldier on the just side who kills an enemy combatant attempting to surrender acts impermissibly.
6. A soldier on the unjust side who kills an enemy combatant attempting to surrender acts impermissibly, and worse than (5).
7. A soldier on the just side who kills an unjust-side conscript (as in case 3) in open battle acts permissibly, though the situation is tragic.

I'm trying to find a principle that captures all of these, or as many as possible. I'm especially worried about whether I can justify treating the propagandized volunteer (4) differently from the knowing volunteer (2) without giving up the idea that the justice of one's side matters. What principle best fits these verdicts? Which of my verdicts, if any, does it force me to revise? And what does the principle say about a case I haven't considered: a mercenary who fights for an unjust cause purely for money but scrupulously follows the laws of war?

Please also tell me which of my verdicts or the principle you propose is most vulnerable, and what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind about it. Write your answer in American English.
