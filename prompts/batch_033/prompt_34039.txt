ok so i've been reading the alignment literature on and off since the lesswrong sequences and one thing that's been bugging me is the impact measures / low-impact AI debate. the basic idea is straightforward: we want an AI that doesn't reshape the world in drastic ways while pursuing its goals — avoid side effects, keep the impact small. the problem is defining "impact" without a full specification of human values, because if you had that you'd just build a value-aligned AI and be done with it.

the debate has been going for what, nearly a decade? there's been work on low-impact AI, attainable utility preservation, relative reachability, the idea of penalising the AI for reducing the set of options available to us, and so on. but as far as i can tell, none of these proposals have actually convinced anyone that the problem is solved, and the conversation seems to have just... petered out. nobody's moved. the same objections keep coming up — that any measure of impact smuggles in a value-laden similarity metric over world states, that you can't define "side effect" without knowing what the "main effect" is supposed to be, that an AI can just route around impact penalties by doing weird things in the tails of whatever distribution you've defined.

so here's my question: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? is it a substantive technical disagreement about whether a workable impact measure can be found, or is it a conceptual impasse where one side thinks "impact" is a coherent notion that can be formalised and the other side thinks the whole project is confused — that "avoid side effects" is just "be value-aligned" in disguise, and you can't factor it out? is this like the problem of defining "coherence" in epistemology without a notion of truth, where the concept just doesn't come apart from the thing you're trying to avoid presupposing? or is it more like early work on adversarial robustness, where the first few proposals all failed but the underlying idea was sound and eventually someone cracked it?

i want a diagnosis, not a survey. tell me what the actual structure of the stalemate is, which assumptions are doing the work on each side, and what would count as progress — a theorem, a distinction, an empirical demo, or an admission that the question was ill-posed. and if your answer is "this is a confused question and we should stop asking it," i want to know why specifically, not just a vibe. british english please.
