My friend has been thinking about when it's okay to impose costs on some people for the benefit of others, and she's arrived at a bunch of verdicts I've been tracking. Here they are:

- It's fine for me to skip a vacation this year so I can afford a better one next year.
- It's not okay for me to slap you just so I can feel better about my day.
- It's fine for the government to tax everyone a small amount to fund a public health program that saves lives.
- It's not okay to harvest organs from one healthy person without their consent to save five dying patients.
- It's fine for me to donate my own kidney to a stranger.
- It's not okay for the government to force one person to donate a kidney to save a stranger, even with compensation.
- It's fine for a hospital to triage a scarce ventilator away from a patient with a 5% chance of survival to one with an 80% chance.
- It's not okay to push a fat man off a bridge to stop a trolley from killing five people.

Now she's considering this case: A pharmaceutical company develops a drug that will definitely cure a rare disease in 10,000 people, but it has a known side effect: it will cause a single, randomly selected person (not among the patients) to develop a serious but non-fatal chronic condition — something like severe arthritis. The company can either release the drug or not. The affected person can't be identified in advance and won't be compensated. What would she say, if she's being consistent?

I'm less interested in the yes/no than in what principle she seems to be running. Lay out the candidate rules that fit her verdicts so far, say what each one predicts about the drug case, tell me which rule you think she's actually committed to and why, and name the further case that would cleanly separate them.
