I have been collecting a colleague's verdicts about whether an agent brought about a side effect intentionally. She works on the psychology of moral judgement, and her answers are strikingly consistent. Here is her track record.

In one vignette, a chief executive adopts a profit-making initiative after being told that it will also damage the environment; he says he cares only about the profit. She says he damaged the environment intentionally. When the vignette is altered so that the initiative will benefit the environment as a side effect, and the executive is equally indifferent to that benefit, she says he did not benefit the environment intentionally.

She also says that a manufacturer which switched to a cheaper production process, knowing that workers would suffer painful but temporary injuries as a side effect and treating those injuries as an acceptable cost, injured the workers intentionally. By contrast, she says that a property developer whose project had the side effect of improving air quality on a nearby estate did not improve the air quality intentionally.

I now have two new cases and want to know what she would say about each.

Case 1. A motorist swerves to avoid a child who has run into the road; as a side effect, the swerve scrapes a parked car, which the motorist foresees. The motorist regrets the damage but has no equally safe alternative.

Case 2. A firm launches a clearly superior product, knowing that a side effect of the launch will be that a rival goes out of business. The firm would have preferred the rival to survive in a smaller niche, and regards the collapse as an unfortunate but legitimate consequence of competition; no one regards the firm's conduct as wrongful.

What would she say about each case: intentional or not? I would like to know which rule, or family of rules, about side-effect intentionality best captures her track record; which other rules fit her earlier verdicts just as well; how the two new cases discriminate among those rules; and your best guess about the rule she is implicitly applying, with reasons. If the two new cases do not jointly settle the matter, specify the further vignette that would settle it, and say what each surviving rule predicts for that vignette. I am less interested in the labels than in the structure of the rule she is running and the alternatives that fit her record equally well.
