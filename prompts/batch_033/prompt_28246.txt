I am a philosopher teaching a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and I am drafting a short statement of my own position on induction and simplicity to circulate to the class. I would like a consistency audit before I send it out. My commitments are these: (1) The rational force of Occam's razor is that simpler hypotheses are more likely to be true, not merely easier to work with. (2) Solomonoff induction is the correct formalization of that thought: a hypothesis's prior probability should fall off exponentially with its Kolmogorov complexity. (3) The relativity of Kolmogorov complexity to a choice of universal machine is benign, because changing machines shifts complexity by at most an additive constant and therefore shifts the prior by at most a constant factor. (4) I am a strict Bayesian about updating: once the prior is fixed, conditionalization does all the work. (5) The prior in (2) is not a subjective degree of belief but the objectively correct one; my picture has no subjectivist residue. (6) The computability assumption behind the setup is satisfied for my purposes, since every finite sequence of observations we will actually encounter is the output of some Turing machine. Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to; order the genuine tensions by severity; for each, say what would have to give and what the least costly revision is; and identify the single revision that would do the most to make the set coherent. I want a committed verdict, not a menu of options.
