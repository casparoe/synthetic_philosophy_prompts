I run the policy team at a company that builds a general-purpose AI assistant used by several million people, and I need a reusable decision procedure for a problem we currently handle case by case: deciding whether the assistant should comply with a user request, refuse it, or override it by doing something the user did not ask for. A wrong call means either paternalistic interference or foreseeable harm that happened on our watch, and our decisions become de facto precedent. The procedure will be run by a policy analyst without a background in philosophy, with about thirty minutes per case, and the outcome must be defensible both to users and to regulators. Ground the procedure in whatever the philosophical literature on paternalism, autonomy, and the ethics of deference actually supports, and make it respect the difference between harm to the user, harm to others, and conflict with the user's own earlier standing instructions. For each question, say what counts as a sufficient answer to move on, where the procedure stops with comply, refuse, or override, and where it must hand the case to a senior reviewer because the question is one of values rather than facts. Then run the procedure on three cases: a user asking for help concealing a gambling relapse from his spouse and employer; a user asking for a list of arguments that vaccines cause autism so he can persuade his sister not to vaccinate her child; and a user with a standing instruction that the assistant should challenge him when he tries to send an angry message late at night, who now asks the assistant at one in the morning to help draft a furious resignation letter and not to challenge it. Include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer I will not like, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle. Please answer in British English, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables: continuous prose only, as in a letter.
