I'm working on a paper about the philosophical frame problem — the problem of how a cognitive system determines which of the indefinitely many logical consequences of an action or belief are relevant to its reasoning. I want to construct a thought experiment that isolates a specific tension between two strategies for handling it:

- **Frame-based irrelevance**: the system pre-compiles explicit markers that certain facts or consequences are irrelevant to certain actions or contexts, and those markers are not revisited unless something explicitly triggers them.

- **On-demand relevance checking**: the system determines relevance at query time by examining the current context and reasoning backward from its goals, without relying on pre-compiled irrelevance judgments.

Your task is to construct a thought experiment that meets all of the following specifications.

**Base scenario.** A simple, ordinary situation in a kitchen where an agent is reasoning about the consequences of a mundane action — moving an object, opening a drawer, pouring a liquid, or the like. The scenario should involve a set of background facts that are genuinely irrelevant to predicting what happens next.

**Agreement in the base scenario.** Both the frame-based system and the on-demand relevance checker must reach the same conclusion about what will happen and why. Both must correctly ignore the irrelevant background facts.

**The modification.** Introduce exactly one change to the base scenario: add a single new object to the kitchen. This new object must be causally inert with respect to the original action and its relevant consequences — it does not interact with, obstruct, or otherwise affect the action or its outcomes.

**The divergence.** In the modified scenario, the two systems must diverge in their predictions. Crucially, the **frame-based system must get the prediction right** and the on-demand system must get it wrong. This is the reverse of the direction typically claimed by advocates of flexible, context-sensitive reasoning, who argue that frame-based systems are brittle and on-demand systems are more robust.

**Constraints.**

- The domain must remain an ordinary kitchen; no exotic physics, no perfect predictors, no demons, no science-fictional elements.
- The on-demand system's error must not arise from a missing fact in its knowledge base. The error must come from the structure of on-demand relevance checking itself — specifically, from the fact that adding an inert object causes the system to reconsider previously settled relevance judgments in a way that opens the door to a spurious connection.
- The frame-based system's success must be attributable to its pre-compiled irrelevance markers, not to luck, a richer knowledge base, or an ad hoc patch.

**What to deliver.**

First, write the thought experiment as continuous prose: describe the base scenario, state the prediction each system makes and why, introduce the modification, and then walk through each system's revised reasoning step by step, showing exactly where they diverge and why the on-demand system goes wrong.

After the prose, provide a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields:

- `baseScenario`: a one-paragraph description of the base scenario, including the action, the relevant consequences, and the genuinely irrelevant background facts.
- `modification`: one sentence describing the inert object that is added.
- `frameSystemReasoning`: the frame-based system's complete reasoning chain in the modified scenario, as an array of steps, each step a string.
- `onDemandSystemReasoning`: the on-demand system's complete reasoning chain in the modified scenario, as an array of steps, each step a string.
- `divergencePoint`: a string identifying (a) the exact step number in each chain where the two part ways and (b) the structural feature of on-demand relevance checking that causes the error.
- `diagnosis`: a string answering these two questions: Is this error a quirk of the specific scenario, or does it point to a general difficulty for on-demand relevance checking? And if it is general, what would a defender of on-demand checking need to add to the architecture to block the error without collapsing back into frame-based pre-compilation?
