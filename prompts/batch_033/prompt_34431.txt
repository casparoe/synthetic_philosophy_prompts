I'm a retired physicist — quantum optics, mostly — and I've been spending my retirement reading philosophy of physics. Wallace, Greaves, Albert, Maudlin, the usual suspects. I'm trying to get my views straight on the Born rule problem in many-worlds before I write something up for a little discussion group I'm in. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm an Everettian. No collapse, decoherence explains the appearance of collapse, and all branches are equally real. Not budging on this.

(2) The Born rule gives the right probabilities. We observe the right statistics, and any interpretation that doesn't recover the Born rule is a nonstarter. Also not budging.

(3) I'm attracted to the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic approach. The idea that the Born rule can be derived from rational choice principles under branching, without adding probability as a separate postulate, strikes me as elegant. I think they're on the right track.

(4) But I also think probability in fundamental physics is about objective chance — not just rational credence. The Born rule isn't advice about what to bet; it's a law of nature that tells us something about how the world actually is.

(5) I think the "incoherence" worry (what can probability even mean if every outcome actually happens?) is overblown. Self-locating uncertainty handles it fine — I just don't know which branch I'll end up in, so I can assign credences.

(6) That said, I'm suspicous of branch-counting arguments for the Born rule. Counting branches needs a preferred basis, and you can't get the right numerical weights that way anyway.

I'd like someone to go through these and tell me which ones are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix is. I susppect something's off in here but I can't quite put my finger on where. Be specific about which commitment has to give and how bad the damage is.
