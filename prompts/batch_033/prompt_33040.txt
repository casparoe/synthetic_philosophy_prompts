I've been trying to get Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes straight, and I keep getting stuck on the hard core. He says the hard core is protected by the negative heuristic—you're not supposed to direct falsifications at it, and when a prediction fails you tinker with the protective belt instead. But doesn't that mean the central commitments of a programme are just shielded from evidence? Like, a flat-earther could declare 'the earth is flat' part of their hard core, blame every failed prediction on some auxiliary assumption, and then by Lakatos's own rules they'd never have to give up the core. What stops every research programme from being unfalsifiable in the bad sense? I dont see how 'protected by the negative heuristic' is any different from unfalsifiable dogma.
