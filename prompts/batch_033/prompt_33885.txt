Write a dialogue between two AI alignment researchers who disagree about whether "the alignment problem" is one problem or several. One holds that the field's apparent subproblems—outer and inner alignment, corrigibility, specification gaming, interpretability, eliciting latent knowledge, multi-agent dynamics—are facets of a single underlying problem: getting a system to robustly do what we intend rather than what we literally specified, with the fragmentation into subproblems being mostly an artifact of not yet having a sharp statement of the core problem. The other holds that alignment is a cluster of related but distinct problems with different failure modes and success criteria, and that treating them as one problem produces conflation errors—for example, a system that avoids specification gaming can still be deceptively aligned, and a corrigible system can still be inner-misaligned. Early in the dialogue, have each character try to state what the alignment problem is with enough precision that it becomes clear how much of their disagreement is substantive rather than terminological, and have them return to that question when one accuses the other of changing the subject. Ensure neither character is a strawman, and that each responds to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past them. After the dialogue, add a brief note separating the points that are broadly settled among people who work on the topic from the positions that are genuinely contested, and flag any place where a character leans on a contested reading of a term.
