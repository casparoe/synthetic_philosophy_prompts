Exam question:
Kripke's Wittgenstein gives a "skeptical solution" to the rule-following paradox. Explain (i) the skeptical paradox, (ii) why the most obvious "straight" solutions fail, and (iii) how the skeptical solution is supposed to work. Then evaluate whether the skeptical solution answers the original challenge or just changes the subject.

Student answer:
The skeptical paradox starts from the observation that any finite amount of past use of '+' is compatible with infinitely many functions. I might have meant quus all along, where quus is just like plus as long as the numbers involved are smaller than 57, and gives 5 otherwise. The challenge is to find some fact about me that determines that I meant plus rather than quus. Kripke considers and rejects the obvious candidates: my past behavior (it's finite and could be read either way), my dispositions (they're finite, they include mistakes, and they can't explain what I should say, only what I would say), and any mental image or feeling I had while using the sign (because an image or feeling would itself need to be interpreted). The skeptical conclusion is that there is no fact of the matter about what I meant; meaning facts don't exist.

Kripke's skeptical solution accepts that conclusion but says meaning ascriptions can still be legitimate. Instead of looking for truth conditions for sentences like 'I meant plus', we should look for their assertability conditions. A speaker is justified in asserting that she meant plus just in case her present and past use matches the way the community uses '+'. Since the community agrees in calling it plus, the ascription is legitimate. This shows that meaning is constituted by communal agreement: to mean plus just is to be disposed to use '+' in the way the community does. That is the fact that answers the original skeptic.

I think the solution succeeds because it replaces an unanswerable metaphysical question with a social one. The community supplies a standard of correctness that the isolated individual lacked, and once we have that standard we can say that someone meant plus rather than quus.
