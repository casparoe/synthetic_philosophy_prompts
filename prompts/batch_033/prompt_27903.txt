A concept that surfaces repeatedly in rationalist discussions of free will, AI, and agency is "sphexishness," drawn from Hofstadter and Dennett's account of the Sphex wasp. The wasp follows a fixed provisioning routine: it leaves a paralyzed cricket at the burrow entrance, goes inside to inspect, returns to retrieve the cricket, and drags it in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp will drag the cricket back to the entrance and repeat the inspection — and will do so indefinitely, never "realizing" it could skip the inspection and proceed. The concept is meant to capture a specific kind of rigidity: not mere behavioral inflexibility, but inflexibility that betrays the absence of any capacity to step back from one's own procedures and notice that they have become pointless. The unsettling thought is that human flexibility might be sphexishness all the way down — more elaborate subroutines, but subroutines nonetheless.

I want a thought experiment that tests whether "sphexish" and "behaviorally rigid" actually pick out the same thing. Construct a case in which a human agent displays behavior that is, on its face, just as rigid and repetitive as the wasp's — the agent performs the same sequence every time, fails to adapt to changes that would make adaptation obviously beneficial, and would continue indefinitely if the situation persisted — but where there is a clear, structural reason to deny that the rigidity is sphexish. The case should force the reader to either abandon the equation of sphexishness with surface rigidity or else accept that the concept of sphexishness does no work beyond "inflexible."

Constraints: the agent must be an ordinary human in an ordinary situation — no brain lesions, no exotic psychology, no science fiction; the rigidity must be genuine and striking, not a trivial case like someone following explicit instructions or acting under physical constraint; and the reason for denying sphexishness must be statable in functional or structural terms, without appealing to consciousness, qualia, or introspection. The scenario should fit in one paragraph.

After presenting the scenario, do the following:

(a) Specify exactly how the behavior is rigid in the way the wasp's behavior is rigid. What pattern repeats? What disruption does the agent fail to adapt to? Why would a naive observer call it sphexish?

(b) Identify the structural feature of the case that makes it not an instance of sphexishness. What kind of agency, flexibility, or responsiveness does this feature preserve despite the surface rigidity? Why does this feature matter for the broader debate — that is, what does it tell us that the wasp case alone does not?

(c) Pinpoint which element of the scenario does the work in driving a wedge between behavioral rigidity and sphexishness. If you removed that element, would the case collapse into a straightforward instance of sphexishness, or would some other feature take over the work?

(d) A committed sphexishness skeptic — someone who holds that all human behavior, no matter how apparently flexible, is sphexish at bottom — will attempt to redescribe your case so that it falls back under the sphexishness umbrella. Give their strongest response, and then evaluate whether it succeeds or whether the case forces them to refine their position in a way that concedes the distinction you set out to draw.
