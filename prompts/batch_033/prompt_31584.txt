I help run the giving side of a family foundation that makes around thirty grants a year, mostly in the $50k–$500k range, and I've been asked to fix a recurring fight. Our program staff genuinely disagree about what matters: a few of us are basically neartermist global-health people, two are animal-welfare focused, one is a longtermist who wants everything going to pandemic and AI risk, and our board chair keeps invoking side-constraints (she'll block anything that smells like instrumentalizing people or violating someones rights even if the expected lives saved is huge). We're not going to resolve these disagreements, and we don't want the loudest person in the room to win.

I know there's a philosophical literature on decision-making under moral uncertainty — maximizing expected choiceworthiness, 'my favorite theory' as a rival rule, intertheoretic comparability, fanaticism, cluelessness, and so on — but I've only ever seen it as theory. Please turn it into an actual procedure we could run in a two-hour grant meeting with staff who have never read a philosophy paper. The procedure should take a proposed grant and a spreadsheet of what each staff member thinks about it under the moral views they hold (including 'I can't put this on my view's scale'), and output either a decision or an explicit 'this is a values call for the board.' It has to say how to get from messy staff judgments to the weight given to each moral view, what to do when a view says the stakes are infinite or the comparisons make no sense, what to do when someone says there might be a moral view we haven't thought of yet that would dominate, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands off to a human. It should be something a program officer can fill out in twenty minutes before the meeting, and it should flag which steps are genuinely mechanical and which are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

Then run it on three proposals: a deworming program in a low-income country, a corporate-campaign grant to improve farmed fish welfare, and a grant to a biosecurity org working on engineered pandemics. Try to make one of the worked examples come out against your own sympathies, and be honest about what the procedure will still get wrong.
