I'm in a reading group that's been stuck on the ant colony question from the Aunt Hillary dialogue in Hofstadter for about a month now, and two of us have been arguing past each other so long that the rest of the group has asked us to write an adversarial collaboration instead. One of us holds that the colony can literally have a mind (or at least a unified cognitive system that isn't just a shorthand for what the ants are doing); the other holds that the colony has no mind at all, and that colony-level talk is metaphor or a convenient summary of thousands of individual ant behaviours. We actually agree on a fair amount already: individual ants are probably not conscious in any rich sense, the colony does remarkable things (nest building, division of labour, foraging routes), and signal-threshold models capture a lot of it.

The document we've been asked to produce should have a numbered list of claims we can both sign, followed by a table of our remaining disagreements, each phrased so that the relevant side would accept its own formulation. Then we need the cruxes: the sub-questions such that, if they were settled, at least one of us would change our mind. For each crux, give a concrete observation, experiment, or thought-experiment we both agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts we'd find. The person chairing the group is strict about not smuggling conclusions into the 'agreed claims' section and about seperating real disagreements from ones that are just about words, so please be careful about that. Also, we don't want a literature review or citations, just the argument itself. British English please.
