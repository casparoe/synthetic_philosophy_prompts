I'm helping to set the research agenda for a small foundation that funds conceptual work on AI safety, with a budget that can support about four postdoctoral researchers for two years. We've been particularly interested in the distinction between approval-directed and goal-directed agency—roughly, the difference between an agent whose behaviour is shaped by seeking human approval or endorsement, and one whose behaviour is driven by the pursuit of some fixed objective. The distinction matters for alignment because it bears on whether we should aim to build systems that are corrigible and responsive to human judgement, or systems that robustly pursue the right goals once we've specified them.

But I'm finding that the conceptual landscape here is messier than it first appears. For one thing, it's unclear whether the distinction is exhaustive: there might be forms of agency that are neither straightforwardly approval-directed nor goal-directed in the usual sense. For another, it's not obvious that 'approval-directed' is a single thing—it could encompass everything from literal reward-maximisation over a human-feedback signal to more subtle forms of deference, like acting on principles one believes others would endorse upon reflection. And then there's the question of whether approval-directed agents are stable under reflection and self-improvement, or whether they tend to collapse into goal-directed agents over time. There are also cross-cutting questions about how these categories interact with other distinctions in the literature—between process-based and outcome-based evaluation, between myopic and far-sighted optimisation, and between agents that model their overseers and those that merely respond to a training signal.

What I'd like from you is a prioritised list of the open conceptual questions in this area that are most worth funding, with the reasoning behind the ordering. For each question, I want to know: why it's upstream of other work (what depends on getting it clear first), how tractable it is for philosophers and conceptually oriented researchers working with current tools, and what would count as real progress on it within a two-year postdoc. I'm less interested in a survey of the literature than in a defended view about where limited funding can make the most difference. Please write your answer in British English.
