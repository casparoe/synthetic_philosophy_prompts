ok so i just had this like three-hour argument with my friend about whether what you know depends on what's at stake and i think i've figured out what i believe but i'm worried it doesn't all hang together. here's where i'm at:

(1) pragmatic encroachment: whether you know p depends partly on what's practically at stake for you if p turns out false. higher stakes = harder to know, roughly.

(2) but i'm a semantic invariantist about "knows" — it's not like "tall" where the word itself shifts meaning across contexts. "knows" always picks out the same relation.

(3) i have the standard bank case intuitions: keith knows the bank is open saturday in the low-stakes version but not in the high-stakes version.

(4) knowledge is the norm of assertion — you shouldn't flat-out assert p unless you know p.

(5) i also think the only things that shift epistemic standards are genuinely practical factors (like what's actually at stake), not merely conversational factors like what possibilities happen to get mentioned. this is why i'm not a contextualist — contextualists let any old salient possibility raise the bar.

(6) here's one i'm less sure about: if you know p, then you're epistemically in the clear to act on p no matter what the stakes are. like, once you've cleared the bar for knowledge, you're good for any practical purpose. higher stakes can't retroactively make your epistemic position insufficient.

(7) oh and i think knowledge is intimately tied to practical reasoning: what it IS to know p just is to be in the epistemic position that entitles you to treat p as a premise in practical reasoning. there's no gap between "knowing" and "knowing for practical purposes."

my friend kept insisting that (2) and (3) together force me to be a contextualist, and that (1) and (6) obviously contradict each other. i pushed back at the time but now i'm not so sure. can you go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which just look like they are, and what the cheapest fix is? please rank the tensions by severity — i don't want a list where everything is equally bad.
