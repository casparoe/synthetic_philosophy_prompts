I've tracked our team's past calls across several pairs of options, and I want to figure out what decision rule we're implicitly running so I can apply it consistently to a new case we're debating. Here are the team's verdicts on past pairs (each option shows projected revenue and its probability):

Case 1: Option A ($1M guaranteed) vs. Option B (10% chance of $5M, 89% chance of $1M, 1% chance of $0). The team chose A.

Case 2: Option C (11% chance of $1M, 89% chance of $0) vs. Option D (10% chance of $5M, 90% chance of $0). The team chose D.

Case 3: Option E ($500K guaranteed) vs. Option F (50% chance of $1.2M, 50% chance of $0). The team chose E.

Case 4: Option G (50% chance of $500K, 50% chance of $0) vs. Option H (25% chance of $1.2M, 75% chance of $0). The team chose H.

Now we face a new case: Option X ($2M guaranteed) vs. Option Y (80% chance of $3M, 20% chance of $0).

Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The items should: (1) predict the team's verdict on the new case, (2) identify the implicit decision rule that best explains the past verdicts, (3) name at least one other rule consistent with the same past verdicts, (4) state what each candidate rule predicts for the new case, and (5) describe a further case whose outcome would cleanly distinguish the candidate rules. Write in American English.
