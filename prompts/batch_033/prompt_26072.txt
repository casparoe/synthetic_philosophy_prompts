so ive been reading about pain asymbolia — where people say they feel the pain, can locate it and describe its quality, but just dont find it bothersome or unpleasant. the way its usually presented is like a fire alarm: the sensor (the sensory-discriminative part) still works fine, but the actual alarm bell (the affective-motivational part) is disconnected. and from this people conclude that the badness of pain isnt built into the sensation itself, its a separate thing the brain adds on — which has implications for how we think about suffering, about pain treatment, maybe even about whether a creature with only the sensory part is really worse off.

but i keep wondering whether the fire alarm picture is doing more work than people admit. can you audit the analogy? what maps onto what, where does it break, and does the conclusion — that the badness of pain is a separable module — survive once the disanalogies are on the table? im especially interested in whether the analogy sneaks in a picture where the "alarm" is obviously optional equipment you could just unplug, when maybe in a real nervous system the two arent so cleanly separable. and if the fire alarm is misleading, is there a better analogy?
