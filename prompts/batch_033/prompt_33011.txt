In the color phi phenomenon, two briefly flashed dots of different colors at nearby locations are reported as a single moving dot that changes color in the middle of its apparent trajectory, even though the later color is available only at the second flash. Philosophical discussions of this phenomenon often contrast Orwellian revisions of experience, in which the brain retroactively edits an earlier experience before it is reported, with Stalinesque revisions, in which the brain delays experience and only then presents an already-edited version.
