ok so i saw that forgery exhibit at the met a couple weeks ago — the one where they put the real paintings next to the fakes and you're supposed to try to tell which is which — and it's honestly messed up my whole view of this stuff. i've been trying to write down what i actually think and i keep going in circles. here's where i've landed and i need someone to tell me if these five things can all be true at the same time:

(1) aesthetic value is experiential: if two objects give you the same perceptual experience, they have the same aesthetic value, full stop. a perfect forgery of a vermeer is just as good, aesthetically, as the vermeer it copies.

(2) but i also think that when someone learns their prized painting is a fake, the sense of loss is completely appropriate — they lost something real, not just resale value. it's not like finding out your designer bag is a knockoff where the only thing hurt is your wallet.

(3) part of what we value in art is the achievement — what the artist pulled off given the materials, the tradition, the constraints of their time. a van meegeren painted in the 1930s with modern pigments and three centuries of hindsight just isn't the same kind of achievement as a vermeer painted in delft in the 1660s.

(4) but i also want to say that for something to count as an aesthetic property of the work, it has to be perceptible in principle. if you need a chemical analysis or a provenance document to detect it, it's not an aesthetic property — it's a fact about the object's history, not about the object as an object of aesthetic attention.

(5) and finally, i think the art market's obsession with authenticity is mostly status signaling and a kind of magical thinking about auratic contact with the artist, and it's philosophically pretty indefensible. the fact that a painting sold for 30 million when it was "a vermeer" and for 30 thousand when it was "a van meegeren" tells you something about rich people, not about art.

so that's the set. i can feel that some of these are pulling against each other but i can't figure out wich pairs are genuinely incompatible and which just look like they are. go through these and tell me: which commitments are actually in tension, rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be. and if some of these only seem to conflict but don't really, say why. i'm not attached to any particular one of these — i just want the most coherent package i can get without gutting the intuitions behind them.
