I want your credence on the following claim: Over the next thirty years, the widespread use of AI systems for intellectually demanding tasks—writing, analysis, coding, decision-support—will produce a net measurable decline in genuine human understanding in the domains where those systems are most heavily adopted, relative to a counterfactual in which AI assistance is absent or far more limited.

By "measurable decline" I mean a decline that would show up in longitudinal studies of practitioners' ability to perform the relevant cognitive work without AI assistance, their grasp of the conceptual structure of their domain, or their capacity to detect errors, reason through novel cases, and transfer skills to adjacent problems—not merely a shift in the division of labor where humans do different things.

I want a number, but much more importantly I want the decomposition. Break the question into its main sub-questions, assign a rough probability to each, and show how they combine to yield your overall credence. The sub-questions should include at least the following (and any others you think belong):

(a) What constitutes "genuine understanding" such that we could in principle recognize its erosion? In particular, does understanding require that the knower could regenerate the relevant cognitive product without external aid, or is it compatible with a kind of knowing-how-to-use-tools that presupposes some residual competence?

(b) Does cognitive offloading to external tools typically erode the offloaded capacities? What does the historical record—writing, printed books, calculators, internet search—actually support here, and how confident should we be in extrapolating it?

(c) Does AI differ from earlier cognitive tools in a way that makes erosion of understanding more likely? Consider, for instance, that AI systems can produce outputs whose internal rationale the user cannot inspect, that they can substitute for integration and synthesis rather than merely for rote operations, and that they may be deployed at a scale and speed that outpaces the development of complementary skills.

(d) Are institutions, professional norms, educational practices, or other counter-pressures likely to arise that preserve or actively cultivate understanding even under heavy AI use? Or do the incentives—productivity, convenience, competitive pressure—point the other way?

For each sub-question, give a rough probability and a one- or two-sentence justification. Then combine them and give your overall credence. Finally, tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to—the one where a shift in your probability would move the overall number the most—and what evidence over the next decade or so would most likely shift that sub-question for you.
