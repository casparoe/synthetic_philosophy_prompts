I run a tiny podcast on the foundations of physics, and I've got an upcoming episode with someone who defends the standard Landauer-Bennett resolution of Maxwell's demon — the view that the demon has to erase information to make room for new measurements, and its that erasure (not the measurement itself) that costs entropy and saves the Second Law.

I want to push past the textbook version everyone already knows. Can you draft me eight or nine questions that get into the weeds? In particular:

- At least one question on whether the demon could simply avoid erasure by having an infinite memory tape — and if the objection is just that infinite memory is unphysical, what that says about the status of the resolution (is it a physical argument or an argument about idealization?)
- A question about the measurement step: the standard line is that measurement can be done reversibly in principle (e.g. by coupling to a system that starts in a known state), but there's been recent-ish work arguing this isn't quite right. I want a question that presses on whether measurement really is thermodynamically innocent, and a follow-up that anticipates the "but you can make the measurement cost arbitrarily small" reply.
- A question about the difference between thermodynamic entropy and Shannon information: does the resolution actually require us to identify them, or is it a weaker claim? And a follow-up on whether treating them as identical papers over something important.
- A question linking this to the broader issue of whether the Second Law is a fundamental law or a statistical regularity — if it's "just" statistical, does the demon paradox even need a deep resolution beyond "fluctuations happen but are astronomically improbable"?
- And a closing question that asks: granting that Landauer-Bennett works as a consistency proof for the Second Law, does it actually explain anything, or is it more like showing that if you try to violate the Second Law you find the entropy cost hidden somewhere else by construction?

I want these to feel like a real conversation where you're anticipating the guest's likely comebacks and have the next move ready.
