I'm a microeconomist, not a philosopher — utility functions, budget sets, endowments, feasible sets, envy-freeness, mechanism design, that's where I live. I've picked up that political philosophy has this long fight about what equality is supposed to be equality of — welfare, resources, capabilities — but I've never actually worked through it. I want you to restate the whole debate in my vocabulary, position by position, as faithfully as you can. Translate the welfare view into equalizing utility or preference satisfaction, with the expensive-tastes and adaptive-preferences objections. Translate the Dworkin-style resource view into equal endowments plus a hypothetical insurance market that compensates brute luck but not option luck, with the ambition-sensitivity / endowment-sensitivity distinction. Translate the Sen/Nussbaum capability view into a person-specific conversion function from resources into a feasible set of functionings, and the functionings-vs-capabilities distinction. For each one, tell me what maps cleanly, what you had to fudge to force it to fit, and what has no counterpart in anything I normally model.

Then give me the residue report. The thing I actually want to know: is a 'capability' just a budget set with a nonlinear, person-specific production function, or does something drop out in translation? If something drops out — the intrinsic value of unchosen options, adaptive preferences, the brute-luck/option-luck line, dignity or participation as non-market functionings — tell me whether that's a real gap in the economist's vocabulary or a sign that the philosophers' distinction was less sharp than it sounds once you demand a measurable structure. Dont give me citations or a reading list; I want the translation and the residue, not a bibliography.
