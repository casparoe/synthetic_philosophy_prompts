In preparing a graduate seminar on the ethics of scarcity, I have found myself returning to Garrett Hardin's lifeboat ethics: the proposal that wealthy nations should be understood as a fleet of crowded lifeboats surrounded by swimmers, and that the norms appropriate aboard a lifeboat — above all, the refusal to take on additional passengers — differ sharply from the norms of sharing that apply on land. I want to press that model against a concrete case rather than let it remain a free-floating metaphor. Consider the voyage of the MS St. Louis in 1939. The liner left Hamburg carrying more than nine hundred Jewish refugees. After Cuban authorities revoked most of the passengers' landing permits, the ship was refused permission to disembark in Havana. It then waited off the coast of Florida while appeals were made to the United States, and Canada likewise declined to admit the passengers. The ship returned to Europe, where several countries eventually accepted the refugees; a substantial number of those passengers were later murdered in the Holocaust.

Analyze this episode through the lens of lifeboat ethics. I know the standard outlines of both the episode and the argument, so I am not looking for a summary of either. What I want is a careful, two-directional analysis. Be disciplined about the mapping: identify the lifeboat-holders and the swimmers, specify the options and payoffs as the model would represent them, and say what the model prescribes at each juncture. Then assess which features of the episode the model genuinely illuminates and which it distorts or omits — consider, for example, whether the receiving states' carrying capacity was really the binding constraint, what the refusals actually purchased for the United States and Canada, and what the eventual acceptance of the passengers by European countries does to the model's predictions. Finally, say what the episode shows about lifeboat ethics itself: does the case confirm the model, is it an anomaly the model cannot absorb, or does it reveal that the lifeboat metaphor was doing argumentative work the explicit theory never defended?
