After a long career in statistical mechanics and nonlinear dynamics, I have only recently begun reading metaphysics, and I find myself reflexively translating everything into the vocabulary I am fluent in: phase spaces, state variables, trajectories, ensembles, coarse-graining, and the distinction between a system's instantaneous state and its full history. The debate I keep returning to is between endurantists, who hold that a persisting object is wholly present at each moment at which it exists and remains strictly one and the same object through change, and perdurantists, who hold that a persisting object is extended through time and has distinct temporal parts at different moments. I would like you to restate each side of this debate in that dynamical-systems vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, without assuming in advance that either side is right. In particular, tell me what the endurantist's claim of being wholly present at each moment becomes when you are only allowed to talk about states and trajectories, and what the perdurantist's temporal parts become.

One complication matters to me. In the physics I know, a macroscopic object is not a fundamental item in the description; it is a coarse-grained pattern picked out by our interests and measurement scales, and in many-body systems the identity of individual constituents is often not well defined. I would like the translation to be honest about whether either side of the metaphysical debate presupposes a level of description that the formalism has to construct rather than find. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which can be expressed only by distortion or stipulation, and which have no counterpart at all in this vocabulary. And when something fails to translate, I want your judgment on whether that failure exposes a limitation of the physicist's formalism, or whether it suggests that the untranslatable claim was less substantive than it looked. My working suspicion is that perdurantism simply is the claim that an object is its trajectory through the appropriate state space, while endurantism is an attempt to keep referring to an entity the formalism has no independent need for; I want to know whether that suspicion is fair or whether the translation quietly discards something that matters.
