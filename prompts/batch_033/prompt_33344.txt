A couple of people in my reading group got stuck on Kavka's toxin puzzle and I'd like someone to sort through their arguments. The puzzle is this: a billionaire tells you he will deposit a million dollars into your account tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you genuinely intend to drink a toxin tomorrow afternoon. The toxin will make you miserably sick for twenty-four hours but has no lasting effects. The money arrives before the deadline to drink—you don't actually have to go through with it. You just need the intention.

Here is roughly how their exchange went:

(1) Elena said the puzzle reveals a paradox internal to instrumental rationality: it is rational to want the money, rational to know that forming the intention gets you the money, and yet you cannot form the intention because you know you will not drink—so rationality seems to demand something psychologically impossible.

(2) Marcus replied that she is smuggling in the assumption that you know you will not drink. You can intend to drink and then, when the time comes, simply change your mind. People intend to do difficult things and fail to follow through all the time.

(3) Elena answered that this is not like failing to follow through. When someone intends to quit smoking, they at least regard success as possible. In the toxin case, you know ex ante that after the money is in your account, drinking is pure cost with no benefit and every reason to avoid it. So at midnight you cannot sincerely intend to drink; you would merely be pretending.

(4) Marcus said that is exactly why you would use a precommitment device: hire someone to force you to drink if you back out, or publicly announce your intention so that failing to follow through damages your reputation. The puzzle nowhere forbids external aids.

(5) Elena said precommitment changes the scenario. The puzzle is about whether intention alone can be rationally formed. If you alter the payoffs by adding a reputation cost or a coercive mechanism, you are no longer doing the puzzle—you are solving a different problem.

(6) Marcus said that is question-begging. The puzzle simply says "intend to drink the toxin." It does not say "without external aids." If precommitment helps you form the intention, then the rational thing to do is precommit. Elena is treating the puzzle as if it is about pure willpower, but that restriction is not in the setup.

They went around on this for a while. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the six moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, and then say what the puzzle is actually meant to show—what is the philosophical point it is driving at?
