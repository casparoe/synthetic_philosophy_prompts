As a journalist covering debates in formal epistemology, I'm working on a piece about the Sleeping Beauty problem and the persistent halfer–thirder divide. I've heard that some philosophers have proposed an adversarial collaboration to pin down exactly where they agree and disagree. I want you to draft the joint statement that a committed halfer and a committed thirder could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The document should have the following structure:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — things both sides accept about the problem setup, the formalism, the relevance of betting arguments, the role of centered worlds, and what a resolution would require. These should be substantive, not just restatements of the problem.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should state a precise point of disagreement, with the halfer's position and the thirder's position each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. Avoid caricature.

3. A cruxes section: identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, describe a concrete case, argument, or test that both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it.

4. If you conclude that any part of the dispute is merely verbal — that the two sides use terms like "credence" or "incremental confirmation" differently in ways that dissolve once made explicit — say which part and why.

Present the document directly, with no preface or commentary. After the document, include a brief editorial note stating how confident you are in the formulations you've given and singling out the point or passage you are least sure of.
