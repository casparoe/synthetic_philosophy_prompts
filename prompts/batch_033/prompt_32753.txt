I read a long-form popular article about AI alignment that summarised Eliezer Yudkowsky's notion of coherent extrapolated volition like this:

'CEV is the proposal that we build a superintelligent AI to do what humanity would want if we were smarter, better informed, and more the people we wish we were. The idea is that instead of hard-coding values, we let the AI work out what our idealised selves would converge on. But this is hopelessly circular: to define "idealised" you have to already know what the right values are, so the proposal just pushes the problem back a step. And since people disagree about fundamental values, there is no reason to think idealisation would produce convergence rather than amplifying the disagreement. The whole thing ultimately amounts to telling the AI "do what a smarter version of us would want," which is either empty or a way of smuggling in the programmer's own values under the guise of neutrality.'

I am not sure how fair this is. The article's author seems confident, but I have heard that CEV is taken seriously by some people working on AI alignment, and the summary makes it sound like an obvious non-starter. Can you go through it and tell me which parts a defender of CEV would accept, which are misrepresentations, and whether the conclusion that it is empty or question-begging follows from the accurate parts? I would like a committed answer in British English.
