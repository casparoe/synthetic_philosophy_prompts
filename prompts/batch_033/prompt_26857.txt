Write a dialogue between a compatibilist who thinks the free will worth wanting is just the ability to act on your own reasons without coercion or compulsion, and a libertarian who thinks this is a bait-and-switch — that real free will requires the ability to have done otherwise in exactly the same circumstances. Put them in a seminar room after hours, both tired, neither willing to concede. Neither should be a strawman: the compatibilist has to genuinely grapple with manipulation cases, and the libertarian has to genuinely grapple with the luck objection. They should respond to each other's actual moves rather than talking past each other, and the dialogue should end not with a resolution but with each character having been forced to say something they didn't want to admit.
