I've been going in circles on this sphex wasp thing for months now. You know the one — the wasp that drags a cricket to its burrow, goes in to check the burrow, comes back out, and if some meddling biologist moved the cricket two inches while it was inside, it'll drag it back to the threshold and check the burrow *again*, and you can loop it forty times and it never catches on. Dennett and Hofstadter use it to talk about how what looks like intelligent behavior can just be rigid programming, and then they ask: are we just sphex wasps with fancier subroutines?

And here's where I'm stuck. The debate between people who say "yes, free will is an illusion, you're just a more complicated automaton and the feeling of choosing is part of the program" and people who say "no, there's a qualitative difference — humans can notice their own patterns and break them, that's what makes it different in kind not just degree" — this debate seems totally frozen. Both sides agree on the neuroscience (as far as I can tell), both sides agree the wasp is rigid and humans are more flexible, and then they just... stop. One side says the flexibility is still deterministic underneath so it's the same thing, the other side says the flexibility *is* the difference and asking about what's "underneath" is missing the point.

What kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a factual disagreement where we're waiting on some neuroscience result that would settle it? Is it just two differnt definitions of "free" talking past each other? Or is it one of those things where both sides have different bedrock intuitions about what counts as an explanation and neither can argue for their starting point?
