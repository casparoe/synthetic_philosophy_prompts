I sit on the ethics commitee of a fertility clinic, and we've realized we've been deciding embryo-selection cases one at a time with no obvious rule, so now we're trying to reverse-engineer what principle we've actually been running. I'm not a philosopher and neither are most of the members; our chair keeps muttering about procreative beneficence and liberal eugenics but never explains what they mean. Here is our track record. We approved PGD to select embryos that don't carry the mutation for a severe, fatal childhood metabolic disorder. We approved PGD with tissue typing to have a baby who is an HLA match for an older sibling with a serious blood disease, on the condition that the baby itself is expected to be healthy. We approved PGD to avoid a mutation that gives a very high lifetime risk of an aggressive adult-onset cancer. And we rejected a request for sex selection for "family balancing," where there was no sex-linked disease involved, on the grounds that there was no medical indication.

Now we have a new request on the table: a couple wants PGD to select an embryo with a polygenic score associated with higher cognitive ability, where the effect is small, uncertain, and has no known health downside. Half the committee says it's just like the cancer case (improving the child's prospects); the other half says it's like the sex-selection case (a preference, not medicine). Please work out what our own precedents commit us to: identify the rule or rules that fit the four past decisions, say what each rule would do with this request, and give me your honest best guess about which decision is most consistent with the track record, with some real confidence levels. Tell me which past case is the closest analog and whether that actually settles it. If the precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, say what principle we'd be adopting with each choice, and what future case would best force the issue. And if you think the whole exercise is confused—for instance, our past verdicts are just inconsistent, or "consistent with ourselves" isn't a real constraint here—say that plainly instead of humoring me.
