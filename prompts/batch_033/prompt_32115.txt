I've been reading through old debates about AI capability and IQ research on LessWrong and the EA Forum, and one phrase keeps surfacing as a supposed insight-stopper: "intelligence is whatever intelligence tests measure." I've seen it used in at least three different ways: to dismiss the project of defining intelligence as essentialist hand-waving, to dismiss the tests themselves as circular, and to claim that the only thing uniting the various things we call intelligent is a family resemblance picked out by our measurement practices — nothing deeper.

The sentence is so compressed that it's compatible with all of these and more, and I suspect its rhetorical force comes from that very ambiguity. I want to get clear on what distinct precise claims this slogan could be expressing, from the most deflationary and trivial reading to the most substantive and controversial one. For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a defensible claim, or just false. And crucially: tell me which reading someone is implicitly relying on when they use the slogan to argue that "general intelligence" is a meaningless concept and we should stop trying to define it. I want a taxonomy I can reference the next time this line gets dropped.
