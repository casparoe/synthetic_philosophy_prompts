I'm drafting a position paper in the scientific realism debate and want an independent consistency check on the view I've settled into. Here are my current commitments:

(1) The no-miracles argument is the strongest positive case for realism: the predictive and instrumental success of our best theories would be a miracle unless those theories are at least approximately true.
(2) The pessimistic meta-induction is also compelling: the history of science is full of empirically successful theories that were later rejected as false, so success alone cannot underwrite a theory's truth.
(3) The correct reconciliation is selective realism: when a successful theory was overturned, the parts that had been doing the predictive work were typically retained in the successor theory or were approximately true, while the idle parts were discarded.
(4) But I doubt that the working/idle distinction can be drawn in a principled, forward-looking way; it looks retrospective, imposed only after we see which parts survived.
(5) Nonetheless, I am confident that central posits of current theories, such as electrons and genes, will not be abandoned the way phlogiston, caloric, and the luminiferous ether were.

I'm not sure whether this set hangs together, and if it doesn't, I'm not sure where the problem is. Please audit it rather than lecture me on the general debate: identify every pair of commitments that pulls against another, say whether each tension is genuine or merely apparent, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each specify what would have to be given up, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision costs. Don't treat any commitment as more negotiable than the others unless the argument forces it.

Return the audit as a JSON object with this schema:
- "tensions": an array of objects, ordered from most to least severe, each with:
  - "commitments": array of the relevant numbers;
  - "type": "genuine" or "apparent";
  - "severity": "high", "medium", or "low" (null for apparent tensions);
  - "explanation": string;
  - "cheapest_revision": string stating which commitment to revise and how;
  - "cost": string stating what is lost by that revision.
- "overall_assessment": a string of at most four sentences saying whether the five commitments are jointly tenable as stated, and if not, which single revision does the most to restore coherence.
