Please argue that the simulation argument, when taken seriously, substantially dampens fanaticism about the far future. Structure your argument as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Grant the longtermist premise that the far future could contain enormous amounts of value and that we should be willing to make significant sacrifices now for tiny improvements in its expected trajectory. Your task is to show why the simulation argument undercuts the inference from that premise to fanatical levels of sacrifice — the kind that would, for instance, divert nearly all resources away from present beneficiaries. Address at least one natural objection along the way, and do not rely on the claim that we are certainly or almost certainly in a simulation; the argument should work even if the probability of being simulated is merely non-negligible.
