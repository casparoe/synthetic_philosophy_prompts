For the last meeting of a graduate seminar on well-being, I want to end the unit on hedonism with a poem rather than another paper. The topic is the heterogeneity problem: the objection that the pleasures of, say, a warm bath, understanding a proof, Schadenfreude, hearing a favorite piece of music, relief from pain, and a lover's touch share no single felt quality, so that a hedonism built on 'pleasure' as a homogeneous sensation equivocates from case to case. Please write a sestina — six six-line stanzas with six rotating end-words, closed by a three-line envoi — in the voice of a monist about pleasure: someone who holds that there is one hedonic tone common to every pleasure. Choose six end-words that name six pleasures of the sort just listed, each chosen to resist the monist's unifying description. The philosophical work should be done by the form: as the same six words are forced back in each stanza in new grammatical and emotional guises, the poem should show the monist's single 'tone' stretching thinner with each return, and the envoi should bring in the word 'pleasant' as the would-be common term and let it fail to unify what the six words have already shown to be heterogeneous. The result should be accurate enough that someone working on hedonism would recognize the dialectic, and good enough as a poem that someone who has never heard of the problem would still feel the strain of the search for a common note.
