I've been trying to pin down my views on normativity as it bears on AI alignment, and I'd like a consistency check before I commit to writing any of this up. The question I keep circling is: what should an AI actually optimize for, given that our stated preferences are messy, myopic, and often inconsistent? I've ended up with a cluster of commitments that feel individually right but whose joint implications I'm less sure about. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) Moral anti-realism: there are no stance-independent normative facts. "Ought" claims do not refer to anything out there in the world.

(2) Indirect normativity: the right thing for an AI to aim at isn't what we currently say we want, but what we would want after an appropriate process of idealization — becoming more informed, more logically coherent, less subject to akrasia and bias, and having more time to reflect on what we care about.

(3) The idealization in (2) should be understood as a causal-psychological process: it is about what we would converge on through reflection, not about tracking external normative truths. This makes me a constructivist about normativity rather than a realist.

(4) Coherent Extrapolated Volition, as described in the rationalist literature, is the right broad framework for operationalizing (2) for AI alignment. We want an AI that does what humanity would collectively want if we knew more, thought faster, and were more the kind of people we wish we were.

(5) There is no reason to expect idealization to converge to a unique endpoint. Different starting points, different orders of reflection, or different framings of the question could lead to different outcomes. Value is plausibly underdetermined by our current values plus idealized reflection.

(6) Despite (5), I think a bounded-but-substantial idealization process — say, "what we would want after a very long period of reflection under favorable conditions" — is still the right target. Non-convergence is a practical problem for engineering the idealization process, not a philosophical reason to abandon the framework.

(7) Epistemic humility: we are sufficiently far from the idealization endpoint that any particular person's vision of what idealized values will look like should be treated as almost certainly wrong. The practical upshot is that we should build AIs that maintain uncertainty about what we would want upon reflection and that aim to learn more, rather than locking in anyone's current guess.
