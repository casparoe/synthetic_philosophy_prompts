I am a postgraduate tutor, and on Thursday I have to lead a fifty-minute seminar on Moore's proof of an external world for first-year undergraduates who have read the First Meditation but have not read Moore. Do not explain the proof to me; plan the session. I need from you: (1) the order in which the ideas must be introduced and why each depends on the previous one; (2) the single example that should carry the whole session — I am inclined to use Moore's gesture with his own hands, but tell me whether that is right and what a carrying example must have and must lack for this particular proof; (3) the misconceptions the students will predictably bring or form, including, I suspect, that Moore simply begs the question and that he has confused proving that he has hands with proving that there is an external world, and the exact point in the sequence at which each should be headed off; (4) what I should explicitly set aside or refuse to settle at this level, and why saying so matters; and (5) one final question whose answer would show me whether they have understood what the proof is doing rather than merely finding it quaint. Make definite choices and defend the order; do not give me a menu of options.
