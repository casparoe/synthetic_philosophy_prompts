ok so I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on decision theory and diachronic rationality, and I've been trying to get my views straight on money pump arguments before I commit to a disseration chapter. I'm worried I'm holding a set of comittments that don't quite hang together, and I'd rather find out now than three months into writing. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) I think money pump arguments are genuinely normative — they show that cyclic preferences are a form of irrationality, not just that they'd be pragmatically costly. The fact that you'd be exploited doesn't just make it a bad idea; it reveales a structural defect in your preferences.

(b) I think the "time-slice agency" response to money pumps doesn't work. The idea that you can evaluate each choice at each moment as locally rational and thereby escape the charge of irrationality — I think this misses the point. An agent-over-time who walks away poorer after a series of trades, each of which they preferred at the time, has done something wrong qua agent-over-time.

(c) I think preferences can rationally change when you acquire new information, without needing any special story about higher-order endorsement or reflective stability. If you learn something that shifts your values or your beliefs about what's good, the new preferences are just your rational preferences now.

(d) I think that if you know you're going to face a predictably irrational preference shift — like a hyperbolic discounter who knows they'll cave in tomorrow — you should take steps now to constrain your future choices. Sophisticated choice is rationally required in those cases.

(e) I think the "no-better-than" money pump is more philosophically interesting than the standard one. The standard pump requires strict preference cycles, which are already suspicious just on the face of it. But the negative one only needs a series of "at least as good" relations that loop, and you can end up strictly worse off without ever making a strictly bad trade. That seems like the deeper problem to me.
