I've been making little letter-string puzzels for a game night — the kind where you say 'abc goes to abd, so what does ijk go to?' — and I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a word for. When the rule is simple, like 'replace the last letter with the next letter in the alphabet,' people just do it: abc becomes abd, ijk becomes ijl, pqrs becomes pqrt. But the moment the rule doesn't apply cleanly, people don't give up; they quietly start bending what the rule was even built out of. If I give them aabc goes to aabd and then ask about ijkk, a striking number of people answer ijll rather than ijkl. It's as if 'the rightmost letter' has turned into 'the rightmost chunk of repeated letters,' and 'change that letter to its successor' has turned into 'change that whole chunk to its successor.' Once I started looking, I saw it everywhere in these puzzles: 'rightmost' can swap with 'leftmost' when the string runs the other way, 'successor' can flip to 'predecessor,' and people do this without noticing they've changed the rule they would have said they were following.

Is there an established name for this — the way the ingredients a rule is made of (letter, rightmost, successor) get stretched or swapped under pressure from the particular case, rather than the person abandoning the rule or picking a different one? I know it's somewhere near analogy, metaphor, and context-dependence, but those all feel too broad, and I'm not just after a label; I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. If there is no established name, what would you call it, and how do you distinguish it from ordinary analogy or from simply choosing a different rule?
