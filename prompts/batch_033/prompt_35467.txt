I'm a novelist. My current project follows two metaphysicians — one who holds that the Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles is a necessary truth, and one who holds that Black's two-sphere universe is a genuine counterexample that refutes it — who are compelled by their department to produce a signed adversarial-collaboration document before either will be permitted to teach the graduate seminar they both want. I need the document itself, which I will quote in the novel. Please draft it with the following structure: a numbered list of claims both parties can sign (about what the formalism requires, about what Black's thought experiment stipulates, about what would settle the dispute); then a table of disagreements, each stated in wording the relevant party would accept for its own side; then a list of cruxes — sub-questions on which at least one side's view turns — and for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction or verdict recorded. The document should distinguish genuine metaphysical disagreements from merely verbal ones (e.g., whether "property" is being used the same way by both sides, or whether one side's "qualitative" is the other's "non-relational"). I want the residual disagreement to be sharp and honest, not a diplomatic blur.
