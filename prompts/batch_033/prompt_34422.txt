so my friend and i have been having this running argument about what actually counts as "doing something together" versus just a bunch of people doing things at the same time. she's got pretty strong opinions about these cases:

- two people carrying a heavy couch up a flight of stairs: together, obviously
- a flash mob where everyone learns the dance from a youtube video and shows up at the same time and place: together
- a packed subway car where everyone shifts their weight when the train turns: not together, that's just physics
- an orchestra playing a symphony: together
- a group of strangers who all spontaneously push a stuck car out of an intersection without anyone saying a word: together
- two devs committing to the same repo but working on totally separate features and never communicating: not together
- a stock market with thousands of traders buying and selling: not together

now here's the new case. an open source project on github where dozens of contributors submit pull requests, sometimes review each other's code, and share a rough sense of the project's goals, but most of them have never interacted directly and there's no formal coordination or roadmap. people just kinda gravitate toward whatever needs doing.

what would she say about the open source case? and more importantly, what rule or rules is she implicitly running? i can think of at least two different principles that fit her verdicts so far, and i'm pretty sure they'd disagree about the open source case. which further cases would best tell those principles apart?
