I'm a retired physicist — spent my career in condensed matter — and I've been making my way through moral philosophy the last couple years. The stuff on promises has really grabbed me, and I've been trying to figure out where I stand. I've got a set of views I seem to hold, but I'm not sure they all fit together. Here's what I've got:

(1) I think the obligation to keep a promise is grounded in the expectations you create in the promisee. When you say "I promise," you're deliberately leading someone to form a belief about what you'll do, and the wrongness of breaking a promise is fundamentaly the wrongness of letting someone down after you've set them up to rely on you.

(2) I don't think promises made under duress or through outright deception bind. If someone threatens you into promising, or if you promise to do X while secretly planning to do Y, the fact that the promisee might still form expectations doesn't seem to generate a real obligation.

(3) I think a deathbed promise to a friend still binds even after the friend dies. If I promise my dying friend I'll look after their kid, that obligation doesn't evaporate the moment they're gone.

(4) I don't think the strength of a promissory obligation depends on how much the promisee actually ends up relying on it. If I promise to help you move and you immediately forget I ever said it, I still ought to show up — the obligation is the same as if you'd rearranged your whole schedule around it.

(5) I think promissory obligations can be overridden by sufficiently weighty consequentialist considerations — if keeping a lunch date would mean failing to save someone's life, you break the lunch date. But I don't think promises are just a heuristic for maximizing expected utility; they have a kind of robustness that purely consequentialist reasoning doesn't capture.

So those are my five commitments. I'd like a proper audit: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and for the genuine tensions, how severe are they and what's the cheapest fix? Rank them by severity rather than just listing everything as equally problematic.
