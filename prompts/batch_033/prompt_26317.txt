I work on community design at a large online platform. My team regularly proposes new behavioral norms we want to see emerge organically — things like "credit the creator when you repost," "use content warnings for certain topics," or "don't reply to a new user's first post with sarcasm." We promote these through interface nudges, community manager messaging, and public endorsement, but we don't enforce them through the moderation system. Some take hold and become self-sustaining; others fizzle out or require constant prodding. We can't reliably predict which.

I've been told that David Lewis's account of convention — where a convention is a self-sustaining solution to a recurring coordination problem, supported by mutual expectations rather than enforcement — offers a way to think about this. I want you to turn that account into a procedure my team can actually use, before we invest in promoting a norm, to assess whether it has the structure to become a genuine convention.

Please structure the response as follows:

1. **The procedure** (~500 words). A step-by-step checklist, each step a yes/no question. A "no" should either stop the procedure (the norm won't become a convention) or flag a specific obstacle we would need to address before proceeding. The steps should operationalize the core Lewisian conditions: that the situation is a recurring coordination problem, that there is at least one viable alternative regularity, that each person prefers to follow the regularity when they expect others to, and that these expectations can become common knowledge. For each step, specify what counts as sufficient evidence for a "yes" — what we should look for in user behavior, platform data, or the structure of the interaction. Be honest about which steps require judgment rather than data.

2. **Worked example: a norm that should work** (~300 words). Run the procedure on a proposed norm that passes. Show the reasoning at each step, including what the evidence would look like and what the alternative regularity is.

3. **Worked example: a norm that should fail** (~300 words). Run the procedure on a norm that fails — ideally one my team would find intuitively appealing and would want to work — at an early step. Explain what kind of intervention would be needed instead (enforcement, incentive redesign, changing the environment) and whether the Lewisian framework tells us anything about why that intervention is necessary.

4. **Where the procedure breaks** (~200 words). Identify cases the procedure cannot settle: norms that aren't coordination problems at all (norms against exploitation, norms encoding status hierarchies, norms where people disagree about what outcome is good), norms where the Lewisian conditions are met but the norm still doesn't take hold, and points where the procedure substitutes the designer's judgment for evidence about what users actually prefer. Say what the Lewisian framework is structurally blind to.
