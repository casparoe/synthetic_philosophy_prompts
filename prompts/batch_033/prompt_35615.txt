Please argue that the looping effects characteristic of psychiatric classification give us good reason to reject the thesis that the central psychiatric categories are natural kinds. Your argument should do the following. First, formulate the target thesis precisely enough that a defender of natural kinds about psychiatry could accept your formulation, and identify the specific feature of natural kinds — stability, mind-independence, projectibility, or some other candidate — that looping effects are supposed to threaten. Second, explain what a looping effect is by working through one concrete psychiatric example, not merely by giving a general definition. Third, show that looping effects threaten that feature at the metaphysical or kind-constituting level, rather than merely showing that psychiatric classification is ethically fraught or practically unstable. Fourth, address the strongest reply available to a defender of natural kinds — for example, that looping effects change only the presentation or self-understanding of the individuals classified while leaving the underlying kind intact, or that interactive kinds can still count as natural kinds in the sense that matters — and explain why that reply fails, or what its proponent would have to concede for it to succeed.
