I keep going back and forth on this and it's driving me nuts. The standard argument says voting is irrational — your chance of actually deciding an election is basically zero, so the expected value of your vote is nothing, and any cost at all (time, gas, standing in line) makes it a bad bet. Mathematically that checks out. But then I look around and I see political scientists treating voter turnout as a serious thing to study, philosophers writing about civic duties, and plenty of people who definitely get the expected-value argument still showing up at the polls. So is the economic argument just wrong, or are all these people systematically irrational about this one thing? What am I missing? Keep it to one paragraph.
