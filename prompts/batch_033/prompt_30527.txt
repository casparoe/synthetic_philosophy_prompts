I've been kicking around an essay idea about debate as an alignment scheme and I'd like comments before I sink a weekend into writing it. Working title: "Debate doesn't fail because debaters lie; it fails because judges update." The setup is the usual one: the hope behind AI safety via debate is that if a human judge can reliably pick the better of two arguments, then two superhuman models can argue it out and the judge's verdict will track the truth. The standard worry is that the dishonest model just lies, or both models collude. My essay would set those aside and argue the protocol still fails, because even an honest debater—one that only asserts true things—gets to choose which true things to assert. The judge's posterior is a function of the judge's priors plus whatever evidence the judge actually processes, so a capable debater can move the judge toward a false conclusion by selecting true statements the judge is predisposed to overweight. On this picture the obfuscated-arguments problem isn't lying; it's the gap between persuasive force and logical force, and that gap grows as the judge's ability to independently verify the argument shrinks. So debate buys you at most "the judge would assign high probability to the winner's position after hearing these arguments," not "the winner's position is true." The obvious replies are to make the judge better or to apply debate recursively, but I dont think either gets you to ground truth—the first just relocates the bottleneck and the second needs an external standard for who checks the checker, and I want to say that's the actual crux rather than honesty. Before I write it up, I'd like comments on: (a) whether this is attacking a steelman of debate or a strawman; (b) whether the selection-of-truths problem is actually distinct from the boring problem of biased evidence in normal epistemics; (c) whether there's an obvious patch I'm missing, like forcing debaters to precommit to a claim list or requiring only verifiable steps. Also tell me if the whole thing is just relabeling the standard "unreliable judge" critique and not worth a post.
