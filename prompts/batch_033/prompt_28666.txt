Here is my opening. Futarchy's decisive comparison is between two conditional markets: one that prices the metric if the policy is adopted, and one that prices it if the policy is not. Yet after the decision only the branch that actually occurs is ever settled; the losing branch's contracts are voided. That means the price on the losing branch — the price that can block a policy — is a forecast of a counterfactual that is never scored. A trader can push that price around without ever having to pay up, because that branch never settles. Doesn't this place the pivotal information in a futarchy in exactly the market position that has no real money at risk?
