A passage in my introductory philosophy textbook glosses the multiple realizability argument for functionalism like this: "The brain is just hardware, and the mind is the software running on it. Pain, for example, cannot be identical to any particular kind of neural state, because humans, octopuses, and hypothetical Martians can all be in pain even though their nervous systems are built completely differently. Since the same mental state can be realized in endlessly many different physical structures, it cannot be a physical state at all. What all these realizers share is not their physical composition but their causal role — being produced by tissue damage and producing avoidance behavior — so pain is nothing over and above that causal role. That is why functionalists conclude that any system whose internal states play the right causal roles, whatever it is made of, has the very same mental states we do." I have a nagging sense that this moves too quickly from "different brains can realize pain" to "pain just is a causal role," but I don't know whether that is a real gap or just the compression you expect in a textbook. In particular, does the final claim about any system with the right causal roles follow from the parts of the summary that are accurate, or does it depend on one of the questionable steps?
