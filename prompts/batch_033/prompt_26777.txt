I'm a backend engineer who works mostly on event-driven distributed systems — Kafka, event sourcing, CQRS, exactly-once semantics, that kind of thing. I recently stumbled across a description of Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism in a long-form blog post about medieval Islamic philosophy, and something about it has been nagging at me ever since. As I understand the position, Al-Ghazālī denies that created things have any genuine causal powers. God is the sole and direct cause of every event. What we call causation — fire burning cotton, food producing satiety — is just the pattern of God's successive acts: He creates the fire, and then He creates the burning, and He does so with such regularity that we mistake the sequence for a necessary connection. But there is no necessity. The cotton does not burn because of the fire; God simply creates the burning at the moment of contact, and He could have done otherwise. The world is, in effect, recreated from scratch at each instant, and the apparent continuity of nature is just God's habit, not a constraint He is subject to.

What I can't shake is that this sounds structurally a lot like how I think about my own systems. In an event-sourced architecture, you have a sequence of discrete, immutable events in a log, and the "state" of the world is a projection built by replaying that sequence. Nothing intrinsic to one event compels the next — the event store simply records what happened. The "causal" relationships are really just ordering and correlation in the log. If you squint, the event log looks like a record of divine decrees, the projection is the state of the world at each moment, and the fact that event A is consistently followed by event B is a pattern the system exhibits, not a law it enforces. The producer can, at any time, write an event that breaks the pattern — and the system will faithfully project it, just as Al-Ghazālī's God can produce a miracle by departing from His habit.

So here's what I'd like. Restate Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism as precisely as you can in the vocabulary I work with: event logs, projections, message brokers, producers and consumers, idempotency, exactly-once semantics, causal consistency, the happens-before relation, and whatever else from the distributed-systems toolkit seems relevant. I want a systematic translation, not a loose analogy. Then give me the residue report. Which claims of occasionalism translate cleanly, which ones require distortion, and which ones have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary? In particular, I want to know whether the occasionalist is saying something genuinely different from what I'd say about my systems, or whether my vocabulary is smuggling in assumptions — about persistence, about the nature of the producer, about what it means for an event to "happen" — that the occasionalist would reject. Is Al-Ghazālī's God more like an event producer, an event store, a transaction coordinator, or something my vocabulary simply cannot capture? And if the latter, is that a limitation of my framework or a sign that the occasionalist's claim is less clear than it looks?
