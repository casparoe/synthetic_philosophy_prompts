I teach jurisprudence and I've been preparing a lecture on the logical structure of legal systems, and I keep running into a pattern that I don't have a satisfactory name for. I've been collecting examples, and I'd like to know whether this specific configuration has been given a label in the literature—and if it hasn't, what the nearest concepts are and how it should be precisely distinguished from them.

The pattern arises whenever a system of rules contains a rule about how the rules themselves can be changed, and someone then attempts to use that very rule to alter the rule-changing rule itself. Here are three instances I've been using to illustrate it. First, a constitution requires a two-thirds supermajority for amendments. A duly passed amendment, meeting that threshold, then declares that all future amendments shall require only a simple majority. Is that amendment valid? If it is, then the simple-majority rule was enacted under the old supermajority procedure, and future amendments can proceed under the new, lower threshold. But suppose someone now tries to amend the constitution by simple majority, and a challenge is raised: the amendment that established the simple-majority rule was itself invalid, because it purported to change the very procedure that authorized it. Second, a legislative body passes a statute whose sole content is "this statute is hereby repealed." The statute was enacted following the proper procedure, but its content is its own annulment—so what is the legal status of the repeal? Third, in a game whose rules include a procedure for changing the rules, a player proposes a rule change that says "this rule may not be changed by any future vote." If the change passes, the rule prohibiting further changes was itself the product of a change; is the new rule binding against a later attempt to change it, and if so, under what authority?

I'm aware of neighboring ideas—self-reference in law, the paradoxes one finds in discussions of Kelsen's basic norm or Hart's rule of recognition, and the general literature on legal antinomies. But I'm not sure those quite capture the specific structure I'm describing: a rule-changing rule applied reflexively to itself, generating a question about the validity of the reflexive application and of everything downstream of it. Is there an established term for this exact configuration in jurisprudence or philosophical logic? If there is, what are its boundaries—does it cover all three of my cases, or only some, and why? If there isn't a single term, what are the closest concepts, how do they each fall short, and what would you propose as a name and definition?
