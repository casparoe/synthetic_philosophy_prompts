I'm teaching an epistemology graduate seminar this term — we've been doing closure, tracking, relevant alternatives, the whole thing. And one of my students, who is frankly too sharp for her own good, has been keeping a running list of every offhand verdict I've given on knowledge cases during discussion. Last week she handed me the list and said, "OK, you need to be consistent with yourself."

The list: I've said that fake barns aren't knowledge, stopped clocks aren't knowledge, ordinary perception of my coffee cup is knowledge, believing what a reliable friend tells me about the weather is knowledge, seeing a zebra at the zoo and believing it's a zebra is knowledge, but believing it's not a cleverly disguised mule is NOT knowledge (can't rule that out by looking), and neither is believing I'm not a brain in a vat. She pointed out, correctly, that these verdicts are consistent with both Nozick's tracking theory and Dretske's relevant alternatives — and she wants to know which one I'm actually running, or at least which one my pattern of verdicts commits me to.

So she gave me a new case: a person buys a single ticket in a fair state lottery with eight million tickets. The drawing has already happened, the ticket lost, and the person believes it lost based purely on the probabilties. Is this knowledge? The thing is, I can see that Nozick and Dretske come apart on this: Nozick's sensitivity condition fails (if the ticket had won, the person would still believe it lost), but Dretske can say the winning-ticket alternative isn't relevant in ordinary contexts given the odds. So my answer to this case effectively picks my theory.

I'm curious what you'd predict I'll say, given that list of prior verdicts. Which theory do they best support — and are there other theories I'm not seeing that also fit the data? What does each candidate theory say about the lottery case? If the lottery doesn't cleanly settle it, tell me what further case would. And here's what I really want: tell me what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind about which theory I'm running. Where's your reasoning most vulnerable here?
