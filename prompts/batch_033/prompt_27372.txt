so ive been trying to get my story straight on vagueness for a talk and i want someone to poke holes in it before i present. my commitments, as best i can state them:

(1) vagueness lives in language, not in the world: there arent vague objects or fuzzy boundaries out there, its just that our predicates leave some cases unsettled.
(2) classical logic is basically fine. in particular excluded middle holds, so "either this patch is red or it isnt" is true even for a borderline patch.
(3) but bivalence has to go: for a genuinely borderline patch, "this patch is red" is neither true nor false.
(4) the precisification story is the right way to spell that out: a borderline case is one where our use leaves open a range of acceptable sharpenings of the word, and the sentence comes out true on some sharpenings and false on others.
(5) theres higher-order vagueness too: no sharp line between the clear cases and the borderline cases, and no sharp line between the definitely-borderline cases and the not-definitely-borderline ones either.
(6) the sorites premise—for every n, if n grains is a heap then n-1 grains is a heap—is false, but there is no particular n where the conditional fails.

i honestly cant tell if this is a neat package or a quiet mess. go through and tell me which pairs genuinely conflict, which only seem to, and how bad each real conflict is; then say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. and please keep track of what people in this area mostly agree on versus what is still genuinely contested—dont make a contested take sound like the settled view.
