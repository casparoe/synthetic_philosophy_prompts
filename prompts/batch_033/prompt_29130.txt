I've been trying to figure out what I think about when conscious experience happens relative to the brain events that produce it, and I keep going back and forth depending on the case. Here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of temporal illusions — I want to know if there's a coherent view behind them or if I need to give some up.

Color phi: a red dot flashes on the left, then a green dot flashes on the right. I see a single dot moving across and changing from red to green roughly halfway. It feels like my brain must have constructed the motion and the color-change on the fly before I became conscious of any of it. That is, the "editing" happened pre-consciously — Stalinesque.

Cutaneous rabbit: someone taps my wrist three times, then near my elbow three times, then up near my shoulder. I feel taps hopping up my arm at evenly spaced intervals, not clustered at the three actual sites. Same verdict: the brain filled in the intermediate taps before the experience hit consciousness. Stalinesque.

But then: the stopped-clock illusion. I look at a clock, and the first second I see seems to last way longer than a second. This one feels different — it's like my brain retroactively stretches the experience backward in time to cover the gap during the saccade. That's Orwellian: the experience happened, but my memory of its timing got revised after the fact.

Flash-lag: a bar moves across the screen and a second bar flashes exactly aligned with it. I see the flashed bar lagging behind the moving one. I honestly don't know which way this cuts. It could be that the brain predicts the moving bar's position forward (Stalinesque, the prediction is built into the experience), or it could be that I consciously see both at their true positions and then the moving one's position gets updated in memory (Orwellian). My gut says Stalinesque though.

Backward masking: a shape flashes for 30ms, then a pattern mask appers. I don't see the shape at all. Is the shape blocked from ever reaching consciousness, or did I briefly see it and then the mask wiped it from awareness so fast I can't report it? I lean Stalinesque — I think the mask prevents the shape from ever becoming conscious. But I'm not confident.

Libet-style cases: I decide to flex my wrist "whenever I feel like it," and the readiness potential shows up in my brain a few hundred milliseconds before I report the conscious decision. This one I'm tempted to read as Orwellian — the conscious decision feels like it happened earlier than it actually did, or the timing judgment itself is a retrospective construction. But I also worry this whole paradigm is measuring something else entirely.

So my pattern seems to be: I lean Stalinesque for most perceptual illusions but Orwellian for the stopped-clock and maybe for volition. Is there a single principle about the timing of conscious experience that makes sense of all these, or at least most of them? And if I have to give up a verdict or two, which ones go first? Also, what does your preferred account say about a case I haven't mentioned: plain apparent motion without a color change — two dots flashing in alternation, and I see smooth motion. Is that the same as color phi in the relevant way, or does it pull in a different direction?
