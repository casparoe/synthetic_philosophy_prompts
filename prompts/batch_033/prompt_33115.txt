I'm a postdoc in comparative philosophy with two years of funding and a mostly blank slate, and I need to pick a research project within Confucian role ethics that won't just end up being commentary on commentary. Assume I'm already convinced the Ames/Rosemont-style picture — roles and relations are ethically basic, not derived from individual virtues or principles — is at least worth taking dead seriously as a reading of the Analects, so I don't need to be sold on the frame or handed a reading list. What I want is a defended agenda: which two or three open problems in role ethics should I actually spend my time on, and in what order?

To make it concrete, the candidates I keep circling are:

- The conflict problem: if my obligations come from the roles I occupy, what happens when roles pull in opposite directions (say, son versus subject — the old "father stole a sheep" problem), and does the view have the resources to say anything principled, or does it bottom out in "do what the exemplary person would do"?
- The ground problem: is there something under or behind the roles — ren, for instance — that explains why roles are normative, and if so, isn't role ethics just virtue ethics in disguise?
- The scope problem: what does the view say about beings I stand in no thick role-relation with — strangers, future generations, animals? Does it have to stretch "role" until the concept is doing no work, or is the honest move to say the theory is incomplete?
- The interpretive problem: how much of the early Confucian material actually resists the role-ethics reading, and at what point does the reading stop being an interpretation and become a reconstruction?

I care about the ordering, not just the list. For each candidate: why it's upstream of the others (if you settled it, what else would get easier), how tractible it is for someone trained in comparative philosophy with some analytic moral philosophy, and what evidence over the next eighteen months would make you move it up or drop it. Also say which one you'd deprioritize because settling it wouldn't change much downstream. And please keep straight what's actually settled among people who work on this versus what's still genuinely contested — don't present a contested reading, including the role-ethics reading itself, as the consensus.
