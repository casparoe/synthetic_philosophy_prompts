Construct a thought experiment in which two agents apply the self-sampling assumption behind the doomsday argument to the same process and the same evidence, and reach opposite conclusions about whether the process's total size is small or large, solely because they adopt different reference classes. The case is meant to isolate the role of reference-class choice in the doomsday argument. Constraints: the process must be simple enough that the posteriors can be computed by hand and each reference class is well-defined under every hypothesis considered; the two agents must share the same prior over the possible total sizes; and each reference class must be independently defensible, with neither obviously gerrymandered to produce the result. After the scenario, show each computation, identify the single feature of reference-class choice that drives the divergence, say what happens to the divergence if that feature is removed, and state how a defender of the doomsday argument would argue that the case does not undermine the argument. Finally, indicate how confident you are in your diagnosis and which point you are least sure of.
