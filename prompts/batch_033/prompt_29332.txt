Don't explain the theorem to me; I need a plan for the session's explanatory arc.

Identify the one carrying example that should do most of the work, and say what it must have and what it must lack; I suspect the standard lemons/used-car example is the wrong vehicle here, and I want your judgment on whether to use it, adapt it, or replace it. End with one question or small task for the final ten minutes that would show whether they have internalized the theorem's logic rather than merely learned to state it. Also tell me what to leave out at this level and to say so explicitly — the state-space proof, p-belief systems, the relationship to Aumann's agreement theorem, or whatever you think should be postponed. Begin your response with the bottom line in the first sentence: state the single structural decision around which the whole session should be organized, then give the reasoning behind the plan, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
