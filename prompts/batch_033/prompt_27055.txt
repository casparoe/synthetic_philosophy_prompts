I have taught a graduate seminar in formal epistemology and the anthropic reasoning literature long enough to have watched a peculiar impasse develop between proponents of the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and the self-indication assumption (SIA). The two camps agree on the formal tools: they assign prior probabilities, conditionalize on the same evidence, and accept the same descriptions of the doomsday argument, the presumptuous philosopher scenario, and a family of cases about reference classes and observer selection. What they disagree about is what evidence a theorist's own existence provides. The SSA camp treats an agent as a random sample from a suitable reference class of observers, so that learning one's place among the observers can shift probability toward hypotheses with fewer observers in the relevant class. The SIA camp treats the agent's existence as more likely under hypotheses that contain more observers, and weights hypotheses accordingly. The result is that the same body of empirical evidence leads the two camps to systematically different credences, and in the presumptuous philosopher case SIA appears to license high confidence in theories that simply posit vast numbers of observers with no independent empirical support. Year after year, the seminar divides along predictable lines, and the final session ends with each side insisting the other has begged the question.

My working hypothesis is that this is not a dispute inside probability theory at all, but a disagreement about something prior to the formalism: what facts, if any, self-locating or indexical information is evidence for. But I am not confident in that diagnosis, and I have heard colleagues argue that the real issue is the unprincipled choice of a reference class, or that the two camps are answering different questions under the heading of anthropic reasoning. I would like a structural diagnosis of the disagreement rather than an adjudication of it. What would have to be true for one side rather than the other to be correct? And given your diagnosis, is there any distinction, theorem, or class of cases that could in principle break the impasse — or should I expect two internally coherent packages that will never be brought into convergence?
