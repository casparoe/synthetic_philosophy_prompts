im a novelist, and my current book turns on a character who has to choose between two lives that she sees as not measurable against each other. ive ended up with the following set of views about hard choices, and i want a consistency check before i put any version of them in her mouth. here they are:

(a) some options are genuinely incommensurable: neither is better than the other, and they're not equally good either.
(b) this isn't just uncertainty or vagueness about which is better.
(c) a person can still choose rationally between such options, and the choice needn't be arbitrary.
(d) what makes the choice rational is that the person commits to one of the options, and that commitment creates reasons for it that were not there before.
(e) i also think rational choice has to answer to features of the options themselves: an action is only rational if something about the chosen option favours it, not merely something about the chooser's act of will.
(f) i'm wary of saying there's a fourth comparative relation like 'parity'; it strikes me as a label for the problem rather than an account of it.
(g) values are plural, so theres no single scale on which everything can be ranked.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look that way, and rank the tensions by severity. for each real tension say what has to give and what the cheapest fix would be. i want a definite verdict, not a menu: tell me which single revision would leave the set most coherent, and why. and please write the answer in british english.
