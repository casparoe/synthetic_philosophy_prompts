Van Fraassen's Reflection principle holds that a rational agent's current credence in a proposition, conditional on the supposition that their future credence will be r, ought itself to be r. The principle appears plausible in cases where an agent expects to gain relevant evidence, but it faces well-known counterexamples in which the agent can anticipate that their future credences will be epistemically degraded—for instance, because they are about to take a drug that induces memory loss or irrational certainty. What are the most important implications of the Reflection principle and the debate surrounding it for our understanding of rationality over time? In particular, does the principle capture a genuine normative requirement that survives refinement, or do the counterexamples show that there is no interesting sense in which we are rationally required to defer to our future selves?
