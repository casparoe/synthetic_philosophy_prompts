Rorty died before the current wave of LLMs showed up, so he never had to have a view on them. But his whole later thing was that we should stop asking whether our words or theories represent reality and instead ask whether they help us cope with the world and with each other. So imagine he were around now and someone made him spend an afternoon with a decent chatbot. What would he have made of the usual objection that an LLM doesnt really understand language, it just does next-token prediction? On the one hand I can see him saying the word really is doing all the work, and that this is just the old representationalist worry about being in touch with reality dressed up in new clothes. On the other hand, an LLM doesnt have projects, audiences, or a life its trying to cope with in his sense, so maybe hed think calling it a new vocabulary, or even a conversational partner, is a category mistake. Which way do you think hed lean, and why? Would he want to drop the question does it understand? entirely, or would he keep a version of it for beings that share our practices? Dont just give me his greatest hits; reason it out from what he actually cared about.
