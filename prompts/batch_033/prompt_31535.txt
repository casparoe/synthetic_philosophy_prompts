(1) whether a distribution is just is a historical matter — it depends on how people came to hold what they hold, not on whether the current distribution fits some pattern like equality or need.
(2) I think the Wilt Chamberlain example is basically decisive against strict patterned principles: if people freely pay to watch him play, the extra inequality that results isn't unjust, even though it wrecks whatever pattern held before.
(3) I'm a self-ownership person: people own their bodies and their labor, and forcing someone to work for someone else's benefit is unjust.
(4) a transfer only transmits justice if the thing transferred was justly held to begin with; you can't get clean title from a thief.
(5) the actual world is shot through with massive historical wrongs — colonial land theft, slavery, dispossession — so many present-day holdings trace back to defective titles and can't just be presumed legitimate.
(6) but I also think a just society taxes people to guarantee everyone's basic needs (housing, food, healthcare), and that this is a demand of justice, not charity.
(7) and I accept something like the Lockean proviso on original acquisition: you can only appropriate unowned stuff if you don't leave others worse off than they would have been.
