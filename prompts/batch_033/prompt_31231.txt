In my seminar on the philosophy of music, students regularly invoke Schopenhauer's line that music is "the direct copy of the will itself" as if it settled several questions at once: whether pure instrumental music has representational content, why music affects us more immediately than the other arts, and what a composer is doing in writing a melody. I want to treat the line as a live philosophical claim rather than an authority. What are the distinct precise theses that sentence could be expressing, from relatively sober claims about music's expressive character to strong metaphysical claims about music revealing the thing-in-itself? For each reading, indicate whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or likely false, and say which of those readings would actually do the work my students enlist the slogan for. Lay the readings out as a menu, with a brief verdict on each entry.
