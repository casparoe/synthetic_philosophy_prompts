Just got done arguing with a friend about fraud detection and I'm still annoyed about it. My bank blocked a charge I made on a trip — different city, different kind of store than I've ever shopped at — and my friend kept saying the block made sense because the charge was "generated by a different mechanism" than my normal spending. But that feels circular to me: all the system sees is that the transaction is unlikely given my purchase history, and unlikely doesnt tell you whether it came from a different mechanism (someone with my card) or the same mechanism running in an unusual situation (me, traveling). The bank's whole practice seems to treat those as basically the same thing, like a low-probability point under the baseline distribution is automatically evidence of a different generating process. So what view of what counts as "the same mechanism" does a practice like that presuppose? Lay out the implicit theory, say whether it actually hangs together, and tell me what the system would have to change if you carved mechanisms up by underlying causal structure instead of by the shape of the output data. Also, is the practice really committed to that view, or could it be run the same way under a few different theories of mechanism identity?
