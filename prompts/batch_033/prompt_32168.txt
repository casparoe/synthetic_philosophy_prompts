The goal: participants should experience firsthand what it's like to be trapped inside a formal system, genuinely trying to solve a puzzle by grinding the rules, and then have the moment where they realize that the only way to make progress is to step outside the system and reason about its invariants. I don't want them to have read about the MU-puzzle before — the activity should work for people encountering it cold.

Design the activity for me. I need: the exact rules and materials, how to structure the time (how long they grind on the puzzle before any hint, what the hinting looks like if any, how to stage the transition to meta-level reasoning), and a detailed debrief that maps what happened onto the broader concept of JOOTSing — why this matters for understanding formal systems, Gödelian incompleteness, and the limits of working inside a framework. Include a note on what predictably goes wrong (e.g., someone in the group already knows the puzzle, or nobody makes the jump without a heavy hint) and how to handle it. Also be frank about what the activity can't capture: the MU-puzzle is a toy system, and the jump is small — what aspects of the real phenomenon does this leave out that I should address in discussion?

Give me a committed design: make definite choices about timing, group configuration, and the debrief structure. Don't just lay out options for me to pick from.
