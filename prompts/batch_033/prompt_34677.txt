OK so I'm a philosophy PhD student working on personal identity and I've hit a wall with an argument I can't shake. It's about Parfit's claim that what matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, not identity. The argument seems to show that if Parfit is right, then moral responsibility and prudential reasoning both come apart in ways that are completely untenable. But the premises each look solid to me. Here it is:

(1) According to Parfit, what matters in survival is Relation R — psychological continuity and connectedness — not numerical identity.

(2) Relation R is a matter of degree. You can be more or less psychologically continuous with your past self depending on how many memories you've retained, how much your values have changed, and so on.

(3) If what matters in survival is a matter of degree, then survival itself is a matter of degree.

(4) If survival is a matter of degree, then moral responsibility is a matter of degree — a person at t2 is only partially responsible for the actions of the person at t1 to the extent that the person at t2 survives the person at t1.

(5) If moral responsibility is a matter of degree in this way, then a reformed criminal who has genuinely transformed their values, character, and priorities is literally less the same person as the one who commited the crime, and is therefore less responsible for it. Not just less blameworthy in the ordinary sense — less the subject of the attribution.

(6) And by the same logic, if I expect to undergo a radical psychological change (say, a religious conversion, or joining a cult that will reshape my values), I now have less reason to save for my own retirement or avoid future harms to myself, because my future self won't be fully me.

(7) But (5) and (6) are absurd. Courts don't and shouldn't reduce sentences based on degree of psychological continuity, and it's irrational to neglect my own future just because I'll be different.

(8) So Relation R can't be what matters in survival.

I feel like the slide from (3) to (4) is where the trouble is — maybe survival being a matter of degree doesn't entail that responsibility is a matter of degree in the same sense. But I can't articulate why without it looking like I'm just insisting that responsibility is special. Which step do you reject, and what do you have to give up to reject it?
