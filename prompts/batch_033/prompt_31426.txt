Last month I sat on a jury for an aggravated assault case, and once we got to sentencing I couldn't stop noticing how much of it just assumed the defendant could of done otherwise and also that he deserved to suffer in a way that wasn't only about protecting people or deterring others. Since then I've gone down a rabbit hole reading the Dennett/Caruso exchange, and I've landed pretty firmly on Caruso's side: basic desert is indefensible, retributivism should go, and punishment should be rebuilt as a public-health quarantine model justified only by its consequences. I don't want to be the kind of person who adopts a big revisionary view and can't say what would change their mind. So: what would count against it? I want the best defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward — the strongest versions of the retributive intuition, the worry that the quarantine model quietly sneaks desert back in when it sets how long someone is detained or how institutions talk to victims, and anywhere Caruso's own arguments depend on a picture of agency he and Dennett might actually share. Please also flag what's settled versus what's genuinely contested among people working on this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
