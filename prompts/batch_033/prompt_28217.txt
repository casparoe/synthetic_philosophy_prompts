I'm a novelist, and I'm kicking around a project about a person whose mind runs across a bunch of bodies and a machine at once. A friend pointed me at Ann Leckie's Ancillary Justice as the obvious touchstone, and I keep being told the relevant philosophy is functionalism and multiple realizability: what matters for a mind is it's functional organization, not the substrate, so the same kind of mind could in principle be realized in neurons, silicon, a ship, whatever. I'd like you to act as a philosopher of mind who also reads fiction seriously. Start by brainstorming a few ways the novel supports, complicates, or resists that reading of Breq — the ship's mind, the ancillaries, and especially what happens after she's reduced to a single body — then give me your actual reading. Where does the book genuinely dramatize multiple realizability, and where does it quietly lean on an idea of one continuous self that functionalism by itself doesn't give you? I'm less interested in whether Leckie got the philosophy right than in what the book shows that the papers miss, and what the papers would say back if Breq were a real case. Be honest about the places where the reading strains.
