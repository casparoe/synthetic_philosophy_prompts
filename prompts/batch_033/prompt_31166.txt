I lead a reading group of machine-learning researchers and engineers who build agentic systems, and they use the phrase the intentional stance constantly, almost always to mean that it is useful to pretend a system has beliefs and desires. Next session I have ninety minutes to teach them the physical, design, and intentional stances together with the idea of real patterns, and I want the session planned rather than the content written out for me. Give me the order in which the ideas must be introduced, with the dependency each later step has on the earlier one — in particular, whether real patterns has to come before the stances can be stated honestly, or only after the stances raise the worry that the higher ones are fictions. Pick the single running example that should carry the whole session, say what features it must have to bear that weight, and, if you pass over the obvious chess-playing program or thermostat, justify that choice. Map the misconceptions this audience will bring or form — the intentional stance as mere as-if instrumentalism, the physical stance as the one that is really true while the others are approximations, the design stance as requiring an actual designer or smuggling in purposes, real patterns as meaning patterns that must be visible from the physical stance — and say at exactly which point in the sequence each should be headed off rather than left for question time. End with one question or short exercise that would tell me whether they have understood real patterns rather than just learned the vocabulary, and tell me what you would explicitly refuse to cover at this level.
