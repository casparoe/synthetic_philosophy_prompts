I keep running into the phrase "beauty is in the eye of the beholder," most recently in an argument about whether a certain building is ugly, where it was used to mean that there is no fact of the matter and that the argument was pointless. But the same people who say this will also insist that some works are genuinely better than others and will defer to people they regard as having good taste. I am not asking whether I am entitled to my own likes; I know I am. I want to know what the sentence could actually be claiming about whether there is anything to get right or wrong. Please lay out the distinct precise theses it might express, from the ones that are obviously true to the ones that are radical and probably false, and say for each whether it is plausible and what it commits a person to. Then tell me which reading, if any, would actually support ending an argument about whether a building is ugly, and what a person who accepts that reading is committed to about the judgments of more experienced viewers, agreement among people who have looked carefully, and the possibility of ever being wrong about beauty.
