I spent my career in the natural sciences, where we treated mathematical objects as tools — one uses them, one does not ask what they are. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mathematics, and I have become preoccupied with Skolem's paradox. The position I find myself drawn to is this: the paradox is not merely a technical artifact of first-order logic but reveals that cardinality notions — countable, uncountable, and so on — are not absolute properties of sets but are relative to the model in which they are ascribed. There is no model-transcendent fact about whether a given set is countable; the same set can be countable from one perspective and uncountable from another, and neither perspective is privileged.

I want to know what would count against this view. I am not looking for a bibliography or a summary of the standard replies — I have read the introductory literature. What I want is a structured assessment of the view's vulnerability. In particular:

1. What would it take to show that the view is false, not merely incomplete? Which arguments, if they succeeded, would establish that there is an absolute, perspective-independent fact about countability after all?

2. What would make the view less plausible without refuting it — for example, developments in mathematical practice that treat cardinality as robustly absolute, or results in neighboring areas that undercut the significance of first-order formulations?

3. What else does the view commit me to? For instance, does it force a particular stance on second-order logic, on the determinacy of reference, or on what it means to say that a mathematical statement is true? Are any of these commitments independently problematic?

4. Has the view been qualified in ways that make it unfalsifiable — and if so, is that a defect, or is it simply the right response to the fact that the view is a piece of philosophical interpretation rather than an empirical claim? In my former life I would have regarded unfalsifiability as fatal; I am no longer sure that standard carries over.

I would also welcome an honest assessment of whether the position as I have stated it is even coherent, or whether it needs refinement before it can be evaluated at all.
