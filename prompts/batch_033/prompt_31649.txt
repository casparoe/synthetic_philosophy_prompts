I am trying to locate my uncertainty about the nature of vagueness, and I would like your help in the form of a credence decomposition.

Consider a paradigmatic sorites series: a sequence of color patches running from clearly red to clearly orange, with each adjacent pair indiscriminable. The question is whether there is a particular patch that marks the last red one — a sharp boundary between the red and the non-red patches.

The main positions are:

(a) Epistemicism: there is a sharp boundary, but we are necessarily ignorant of where it is.
(b) Semantic indecision (e.g., supervaluationism): there is no sharp boundary, because the predicate "red" simply does not settle the status of every patch; it is true only of those patches that count as red on every admissible precisification, and false only of those that count as red on none.
(c) Ontic vagueness: the world itself lacks sharp boundaries; there is no fact of the matter about the last red patch because reality is indeterminate at the margins.

Please give me your credence that (a) epistemicism is correct. I want the full decomposition: what are the main sub-questions your credence rides on? For each, assign a rough probability and a one-sentence justification. Then show how they combine to yield your overall credence. Finally, identify the sub-question you are least confident about and tell me what evidence or argument would most shift that term.
