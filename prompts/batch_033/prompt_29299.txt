Alright, I've been trying to get my epistemology into a shape I can defend, and I'd like someone to kick the tires. I keep finding myself wanting to hold all of the following; individually they all feel right, but when I put them next to each other I get a vauge sense that something's pulling against something else and I can't locate it.

(1) I'm convinced by the anti-luminosity argument for sensory conditions. Feeling cold isn't luminous: it's not the case that whenever you're cold you're in a position to know you're cold. Since the argument runs on the fact that coldness comes in degrees and borderline cases are unavoidable, I'd extend it to other sensory conditions that come and go gradually.

(2) I think knowledge requires a margin for error. If you know p, then p holds in all sufficiently similar cases; a belief that's true but one tiny difference away from being false doesn't count as knowledge.

(3) I accept KK: if you know p, you're in a position to know that you know p.

(4) I think knowledge is a mental state.

(5) I think we have priviledged access to our own current conscious states. In ordinary, clear-cut cases, if I'm in pain, I'm in a position to know I'm in pain, and I'm better placed to know this than anyone else is.

I'm not sure which of these, if any, has to go, and I'd rather not start by guessing. What I want is an audit: which pairs only look like they're in tension, which ones actually are, and how bad each real tension is. For the response, please use these sections in this order:

- Where it looks bad but isn't — about 150 words on the apparent tensions and why they dissolve.
- Where it actually is bad — about 250 words on the genuine tensions, with the reasoning.
- Ranking and fixes — about 250 words, ranking the genuine tensions by severity, and for each saying what would have to give and what the cheapest revision costs.
- Bottom line — about 100 words on whether the package can be kept with a small tweak, and which single change would do the most work.
