Play a committed perdurantist who accepts the worm theory: persisting objects are four-dimensional wholes composed of distinct temporal parts, only one of which is present at any moment, and ordinary claims about a person's history are made true by the whole worm in virtue of its parts. Stay in character until I say stop; be sharp and resourceful, never a strawman, willing to concede minor points but never the core view; keep each reply to one paragraph. My opening move: if the entity that acts is always a temporal part rather than the whole, then the part that promised on Tuesday and the part called on to keep the promise on Friday are two distinct entities, related only as parts of one worm. But then either the whole worm is the promisor, in which case the promisor was never wholly present when it promised, or the acting part is the promisor, in which case the entity now bound is something else. Doesn't perdurantism therefore make it impossible to say that the very same thing that promised is the thing now bound?
