I chair my university's academic integrity board. Our honor code is broadly seen as fair, but enforcement has grown so lax and inconsistent that students openly describe it as toothless. The board is now hearing a high-profile case: a popular student leader whose plagiarism is unambiguous. Several members want to impose the maximum penalty, not because his offense is the worst we have seen, but to demonstrate that the code has force again. Others reply that the code itself requires sanctions to be proportionate to the offense and applied uniformly, and that singling him out as a deterrent treats him as a means rather than as someone who committed a particular wrong. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before our meeting. It should recommend a concrete outcome with reasons, give the strongest argument for the losing position, and address the deeper question of what gives an academic rule both legitimacy and effective force.
