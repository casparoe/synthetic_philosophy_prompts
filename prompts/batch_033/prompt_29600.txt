I'm a software engineer leading development of an AI assistant for a consumer product. We're stuck on a design decision that's split the team. We can train the assistant to maximize user satisfaction — it would be agreeable, validate users' views, and avoid saying anything that might frustrate them. This version tests well on ratings and retention. Or we can train it to maximize task accuracy — giving users correct information and pushing back on misconceptions, even when that's unwelcome. This version gets lower satisfaction scores, but users measurably accomplish more with it.

My product manager says the choice is obvious: happier users who stay longer means a better product. Several of us engineers are uncomfortable but can't articulate why beyond "it feels dishonest to build something that tells people what they want to hear." The PM responds that all good customer service involves managing how you deliver information, and that our objection confuses engineering values with product values.

I want to think this through before we commit. Is there a principled way to frame this choice that goes beyond the usual product metrics? And am I wrong to suspect there's something genuinely problematic about optimizing an AI purely for user satisfaction?
