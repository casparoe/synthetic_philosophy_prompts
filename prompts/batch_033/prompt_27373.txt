As a philosophy professor in metaethics, I would like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two parties: a proponent of evolutionary debunking arguments against moral realism, and a defender of robust moral realism. The document should be the statement both parties could sign after a good-faith collaboration — constructive, precise, and free of unearned concessions.

Part 1 should be a numbered section of claims on which both parties agree. These claims must be substantive and specific to this debate, not truisms: for example, claims about what the debunking argument must establish to succeed, what a realist reply must accomplish, which families of moral judgment the evolutionary record is better or worse placed to explain, and what follows for the realist if the argument goes through. If part of the standing dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.

Part 2 should be a disagreements table. Each row should state a question neutrally, then give the debunker's answer in wording the debunker would accept and the realist's answer in wording the realist would accept. The table should cover at least: whether the best evolutionary explanations of human moral judgment would have produced much the same judgments even if the moral facts posited by realism had been different; whether third-factor explanations can secure a non-accidental correlation between moral judgment and moral truth, and for how much of the moral repertoire — thin judgments about harm and kinship, or also thicker judgments about fairness, authority, and obligation to strangers; and whether the debunker's underlying epistemic principle can be restricted to the moral domain without overgeneralizing to perception, mathematics, or induction.

Part 3 should identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that a settled answer to any one of them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify (a) why both parties agree it bears on the dispute, (b) a concrete case, argument, or thought experiment both parties would accept as a fair test of it, and (c) each party's prediction about the outcome, recorded in advance. Include at least one crux about the scope of third-factor explanations and at least one about whether antirealism faces a structurally parallel problem.

Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and do not quote from the literature; paraphrase positions as needed. The document should be self-contained and precise enough that a metaethicist could tell where the remaining disagreement actually lies.
