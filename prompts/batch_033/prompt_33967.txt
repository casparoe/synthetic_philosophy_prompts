I am a graduate student in a seminar on confirmation theory, and I am trying to reconstruct the principle behind my instructor's judgments about old evidence before I have to anticipate her next case. Across the term she has been strikingly consistent, and she treats each verdict as though it follows from a single rule, though she has never stated the rule outright. She has said that the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion, recorded long before general relativity was formulated and not used in constructing the theory, confirms general relativity substantially; that the in-sample points used to fit a curve do not confirm the fitted curve, even though the curve entails them; that the discovery of Neptune, predicted from Newtonian mechanics before it was observed, strongly confirms Newtonian mechanics; that a known datum which a new theory makes more probable than its rivals do, without entailing it, and which was not used in constructing the theory, confirms the theory, though less strongly than a successful novel prediction would; and that a hypothesis deliberately constructed to entail a piece of evidence that was already known, and which has no independent predictions yet, is not confirmed by that evidence. I know the standard Bayesian difficulty: if the evidence is already known, its probability is one, so conditioning on it cannot change anything. She plainly does not regard that as the last word, but I cannot tell which resolution of the problem she is implicitly running.

The case she will ask about next week is this. A hypothesis was deliberately constructed to entail a known anomaly E, so E was used in building it; the hypothesis also made a novel prediction P, which has since been observed. Does E now confirm the hypothesis? I want more than a bare yes or no: I want to know which principle or principles are consistent with all of her earlier verdicts, what each of those principles says about this new case, which verdict you think she would give and why, and what further case would best discriminate among the remaining candidates. I would also like to know how the rule you attribute to her sits within the standard options for responding to the old-evidence problem.
