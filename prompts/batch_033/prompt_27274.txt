I'm finishing a graduate seminar on the philosophy of psychiatry, and our final meeting is on Ian Hacking's looping effects — the thought that classifying people is not like classifying electrons, because a person who is classified can become aware of the kind, take it up as a self-description, resist it, or bend their life around it, so that the kind itself shifts; the experts then revise the kind, it acts on people again, and the loop turns. I want to end the seminar with a short poem instead of a closing summary, something that makes the mechanism felt rather than explained.

Please write a pantoum on the looping effect, using the fixed form in which the second and fourth lines of each quatrain become the first and third lines of the next, and the final quatrain turns back to the poem's opening lines. The form should carry the argument: each recurrence of a line should arrive altered by its new context, the way a diagnostic category returns with a different meaning after the people it names have lived inside it and changed it. The poem should be recognizable to a reader who knows Hacking as being about dynamic nominalism and human kinds rather than merely about labels affecting people. Keep it concrete rather than didactic — no jargon doing the work the repetition should do — and aim for five or six quatrains.
