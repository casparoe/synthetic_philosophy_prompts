I retired from physical chemistry a few years ago and have been reading philosophy of physics on my own since then, so bear with me if my terminology is off. The position I've landed on is that the direction of time just is the thermodynamic arrow. The underlying microdynamics are time-symmetric, so the whole story has to come from a low-entropy boundary condition at one end, plus the fact that entropy increases toward the other end, with coarse-graining doing the rest. I take the psychological arrow, the causal arrow, and the arrow of records and knowledge to all be downstream of that. I know the standard objections in a hand-wavy way: Loschmidt's reversibility objection, Poincare recurrence, the worry that the low-entropy past is just put in by hand. But I want a real map of what would count against the view. What would the world have to be like, or what would we have to find out, for me to give it up or seriously weaken it? I mean observations, arguments, formal results, thought experiments. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones a defender of the thermodynamic-arrow view would themselves concede as counting against it, and which they'd dismiss. I also want to know whether the view has been qualified to the point that it risks nothing anymore, and if so whether that's actually a problem for a view like this. No bibliography please, I want the reasoning not a reading list. I'm most interested in where the view is genuinely exposed, not the parts everyone already rehearses.
