I'd like a decision tree that takes a student from their reaction to specific puzzle cases to a named position in the dispositions debate. Each branch node should be a concrete case — a finkish disposition, a mask or antidote, a disposition that appears to lack a distinct categorical basis, a case where a disposition's manifestation occurs via an atypical causal path — phrased as a question the student can answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Build the tree from the logical space: identify the key claims that divide the positions, construct the branches from those, and then locate the actual positions as leaves. Flag any logically possible combination of answers that no one defends, and say briefly why (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). Also flag any pair of positions your tree cannot separate and explain what additional case would split them.
