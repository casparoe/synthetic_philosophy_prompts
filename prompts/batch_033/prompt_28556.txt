So I keep hearing about the Judy Benjamin problem in epistemology circles — it's this thought experiment where a paratrooper gets a radio message saying something like "if you're in the red zone, the odds you're in the northern sector are 3:1 instead of 1:1." And supposedly this creates a paradox for Bayesian updating because there's no unique way to revise your probabilities when the new information is conditional. But... isn't that just, like, obviously true? If I tell you "if it rains tomorrow, the probability the picnic is ruined is higher than you thought," that doesn't tell you anything about whether it will rain. So of course your overall probabilities are underdetermined. I feel like I'm missing what makes this a "problem" rather than just a trivial observation about partial information. Is there actually a deep puzzle here or is this one of those things philosophers make a fuss about that statisticians figured out decades ago?
