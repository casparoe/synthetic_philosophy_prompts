I am a retired research chemist who has come to philosophy late, and I keep returning to a claim I find both alarming and hard to dismiss: the view, associated with some forms of negative utilitarianism, that there is a threshold of suffering so severe that no amount of pleasure can outweigh it, so that a single instance of torture-level pain could not be compensated by any quantity of happiness distributed among any number of people. I would like an essay that weighs the case for and against this lexical-threshold view. In particular, please address whether it is more defensible than the standard utilitarian assumption that pleasure and pain are commensurable on a single scale, and whether the threshold can be drawn without arbitrariness. Do not end on a non-committal note: defend a verdict. Please also say which argument or consideration would most likely change your verdict, or where your reasoning is most vulnerable.
