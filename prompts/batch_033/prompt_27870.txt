I'm a PhD student working on Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities, and I need a case built to a precise specification for a section of a paper on the flicker-of-freedom strategy. Construct a Frankfurt-style case in which an agent is intuitively morally responsible for an action even though the agent could not have done otherwise, and in which the case is engineered so that the only alternatives left to the agent are flickers too trivial to ground responsibility. Constraints: the counterfactual intervener must be an ordinary person, no better than a shrewd and observant human, with no special equipment and no neurosurgery, implants, drugs, demons, or science-fictional devices; the intervention mechanism must be something an ordinary person could actually carry out, such as a credible threat or blackmail, and it must remain purely counterfactual in the actual sequence; the trigger on which the intervener would act must be an involuntary state or process of the agent, something the agent cannot produce or suppress at will, rather than a voluntary action, so that the flicker theorist cannot point to an action the agent chose to perform; and the scenario itself must be a single realistic paragraph set in ordinary life. After the scenario, show that the case meets each specification in exactly five numbered items of one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario; (2) the verdict the principle of alternative possibilities gives and why; (3) the Frankfurt-style verdict and why; (4) the strongest attack a flicker theorist can mount against the case and whether the case survives it; (5) which single feature of the case is doing the work in blunting the flicker strategy, and what happens to the case if that feature is removed. The case is for an argumentative paper, so the verdicts must fall out of the scenario rather than being stipulated, and it must not simply relabel the textbook neurosurgeon case with the technology changed.
