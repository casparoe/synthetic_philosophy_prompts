Okay so we covered costly signaling theory in my evo psych class last week and the prof kept using this analogy: a peacock's tail is like a designer handbag or a Rolex. The idea is that in both cases you're spending a ton of resources on something that seems useless or even harmful to survival, but the very wastefulness is what makes it an honest signal — if you can afford to blow resources on that, you must be doing well. The prof then used this to argue that a lot of human status displays (expensive degrees, fancy weddings, conspicuous charity) are basically the same thing, and that the analogy shows they're "honest signals" of underlying quality.

But I'm not sure the analogy holds up as well as he made it sound. Like, a peacock can't decide not to grow a tail — it's genetic and the cost is mandatory. Someone can definately decide to buy a fake Rolex or take out loans for a wedding they can't afford. And the peacock isn't trying to signal to other peacocks that it's rich — it's signaling fitness to peahens in a mating context, which seems pretty different from signaling to your whole social group. Plus the tail is a single trait that directly correlates with health, whereas human status symbols are culturally constructed and can shift over time.

And the real question: does the conclusion that human status displays are "honest signals" survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing too much work?
