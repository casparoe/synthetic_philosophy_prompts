I spent my career in the life sciences, and I've been reading philosophy of science lately — Kitcher, Longino, some of the social epistemology literature. One thing I keep coming back to is the practice of conflict-of-interest disclosure in scientific journals.

Here is the practice as I have lived it: when you submit a paper, you fill out a form listing your funding sources, any paid consultancies, any patents or equity that might bear on the work. The journal prints this at the end of the article. The explicit rationale is that financial interests can bias one's judgment, and that readers deserve to know about them so they can weigh the findings accordingly.

But here is what puzzles me. The same journals do not ask me to disclose my theoretical commitments, my career ambitions, my personal rivalries, my desire to see a cherished hypothesis vindicated, or the fact that my next grant application depends on the result coming out a certain way. Yet the social epistemology literature suggests that these are at least as powerful as financial interests in shaping what questions we ask, which data we trust, and how we interpret ambiguous results. So the practice singles out one kind of influence — money — and treats it as epistemically corrupting in a way that other influences apparently are not, or at least not in a way that requires disclosure.

What I want to understand is: what theory of manipulation — or of legitimate persuasion, or of epistemic corruption — does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory as carefully as you can. In particular, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with. Is there a coherent view under which financial interests are a special case, or is the practice philosophically underdetermined — something several different theories of epistemic corruption would license, and we happen to have settled on one out of inertia? And if we replaced the presupposed theory with, say, a view on which all biasing influences are on a par and transparency is the remedy for all of them, what would the disclosure form have to look like?
