I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about personal identity—what makes someone the same person over time. Here are my verdicts on a range of cases:

Teletransportation, destructive. My body is scanned and destroyed while an exact replica is assembled on Mars from new matter, with all my memories and psychological traits intact. I think the person on Mars is me.

Teletransportation, branching. Same as above, but the scanning does not destroy the original body. Now there are two people with my memories and psychology. I think the original on Earth is me, and the replica is a distinct person—even though he is psychologically identical to me.

Total amnesia with personality change. I suffer complete retrograde amnesia, and my personality, values, and preferences all change dramatically. I still think it is me—the same human being, just different.

Brain transplant. My brain is transplanted into a different body. The resulting person has my memories and personality. I go with the brain: that person is me.

Hemispherectomy and transplant. Each hemisphere of my brain is transplanted into a different body, and both resulting people have my memories and personality. I do not think either one is me—or perhaps both are, but I cannot accept that identity can branch.

Permanent vegetative state. I enter a PVS with no psychological activity and no prospect of recovery. I still think the body in the bed is me.

The Buddhist practitioner. A monk practices meditation for decades and eventually reports, with every sign of sincerity and clarity, that the sense of being a separate, enduring self has vanished—that what remains is a stream of causally connected mental and physical events with no owner. By all ordinary criteria (same body, continuous memories, same social role), he is the same person who entered the monastery. But he himself denies that there is a self that persists. I find I cannot simply dismiss his report as false—but if I take it seriously, it seems to undermine the very question I am asking.

Is there a principle of personal identity that captures all or most of these verdicts? If not, which verdicts should I overturn? And in particular, does the Buddhist practitioner's report force me to reconsider whether the concept of personal identity I am trying to analyze tracks anything real at all, or can I accommodate it within a standard theory?
