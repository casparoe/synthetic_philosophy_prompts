Compare the concepts of an echo chamber and an epistemic bubble as they are distinguished in recent work in social epistemology. Your comparison should address the following:

(a) What structural features differentiate an echo chamber from an epistemic bubble? In particular, what role does the active cultivation of distrust toward outside sources play in the former, and why is this feature typically absent from the latter?

(b) Why does the distinction matter for the design of epistemic interventions? Consider why an epistemic bubble can often be addressed by simply exposing a person to missing information or alternative viewpoints, whereas an echo chamber tends to resist such remedies. What is it about the internal structure of an echo chamber that makes it more resilient to straightforward informational correctives?

(c) Does the distinction suggest that one of the two phenomena poses a deeper challenge to the possibility of rational persuasion in a democratic society? Some have argued that echo chambers are the more insidious problem because they undercut the very capacity to trust external evidence; others contend that epistemic bubbles, when they operate at scale across entire media ecosystems, can be equally damaging. Evaluate these competing claims and defend your own position.
