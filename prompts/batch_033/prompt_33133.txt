My dad was diagnosed with early-onset Alzheimer's about eight months ago. It's still relatively mild — he has good days and bad days, but he's still recognisably himself, still knows who we all are, still enjoys his garden and his books. About five years ago, long before any symptoms, he drew up an advance directive. In it he said that if he ever developed dementia, he would refuse antibiotics for life-threatening infections — pneumonia, specifically. He was very clear about it. He'd watched his own mother die slowly of Alzheimer's and he was adament he didn't want to go that way.

Now here's the thing. Last week he came down with a chest infection. The GP said it's early-stage pneumonia and he needs antibiotics. My dad — the man sitting in front of me, who seems perfectly content with his life right now — wants to take them. He says he feels fine, he's not ready to go, and he doesn't remember writing anything that says otherwise. The advance directive is legally binding where we live, but I'm the one who has to decide whether to enforce it, because I hold his medical power of attorney.

I've been reading the directive over and over. The man who wrote it was sharp, clear-eyed, and terrified of becoming a burden. The man in front of me is gentler, more forgetful, but also — happy? He's not suffering the way his mother did. Or at least not yet. I don't know whether I'm supposed to honour the wishes of the person who wrote the document, or the person who's here now asking me to help him. They're the same person, legally, but they don't feel like the same person. He'd be furious with me if I let him die — but the earlier version of him would be furious if I didn't.

I'm not asking for legal advice. I know what the law says. I'm asking what the right thing to do is, philosophically. Which version of my dad has the stronger claim? And on what basis? I want a straight answer, not a survey of options.
