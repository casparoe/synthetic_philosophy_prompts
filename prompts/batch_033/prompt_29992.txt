I'm organizing a semester-long working group on the units of selection debate, and after watching two of our most committed members talk past each other for weeks, we've asked them to run an adversarial collaboration and produce a document they could both sign. One holds the gene's-eye view: the gene is the unit that persists, replicates, and accumulates adaptations, and talk of group selection is at best a convenient redescription of gene-frequency change. The other holds a multilevel view: selection genuinely acts at several levels at once, and in at least some cases the group-level process does explanatory work that a purely gene-level account would miss. We want the document they would actually put their names to, not a diplomatic compromise.

Please draft it. It should contain the substantive claims both can sign, stated precisely enough that neither is signing something empty; the exact points of disagreement, each side's position worded so its advocate would accept it as fair; the cruxes, understood as the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and, for each crux, the empirical or mathematical case both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction about the outcome recorded in advance. In our group the recurring sticking points have been whether the between-group term in the Price equation picks out a real causal force or is only a statistical partition, and whether there is any trait whose evolution the gene's-eye account fails to predict or explain while the multilevel account succeeds; I'd like those addressed, but don't restrict yourself to them. Please also say explicitly which disagreements you judge to be genuine and which are terminological, for instance turning on what unit of selection is supposed to mean.

If you think the two positions cannot be brought under one signed document without distortion, say so plainly and explain the obstacle rather than forcing a reconciliation. And please write it as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write a letter to the group, even though a document like this might naturally invite them.
