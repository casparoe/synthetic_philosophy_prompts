My friend is doing a PhD on religion as a natural phenomenon — she's interested in what kind of thing religion is, not whether any of it is true. As a party trick she sorts things into "religion" and "not religion," and she's been pretty consistant about it. So far:

- Christianity: religion.
- Theravada Buddhism: religion (she's adamant you don't need a creator god).
- Confucianism: religion (ancestor rites and Heaven count, she says).
- Scientology: religion (being recent, secretive, and a bit rediculous doesn't disqualify it).
- a Melanesian cargo cult: religion.
- a football supporters' club with chants, scarves, match-day rituals, and real collective emotion: not religion.
- a model railway society that meets weekly and has its own jargon and traditions: not religion.

I want to know what she'd say about two new cases. First: a small revolutionary political group, decades after losing power, whose members meet weekly, venerate the movement's founders almost as saints, treat certain texts and dates as inviolable, and say the movement is what gives their lives meaning — but they explicitly reject anything supernatural. Second: a solitary person with elaborate private beliefs and daily practices directed at spirits and an afterlife, but no community, no shared texts, and no rituals anyone else participates in.

More important than the verdicts: what rule or rules is she actually running? I can think of at least two rules that fit everything she's said so far, and I want them laid out, with what each predicts for the two new cases, your best guess at her verdicts, and the one further case you'd want to run by her to settle which rule she's using.
