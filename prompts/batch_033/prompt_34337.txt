I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I want to see the combination problem for panpsychism argued at the level of its strongest current forms, not surveyed. Please write a dialogue between a bottom-up constitutive panpsychist and a priority cosmopsychist. The panpsychist holds that the consciousness of human beings and other macro-level subjects is grounded in the consciousness of micro-level entities standing in some constitutive relation. The cosmopsychist holds instead that there is exactly one fundamental subject—the cosmos—and that finite subjects are derivative from or grounded in it. The dialogue should focus on whether reversing the grounding relation in this way actually dissolves the combination problem or merely relocates it as a decombination problem: how one cosmic subject yields many apparently bounded, mutually inaccessible finite subjects. Have the two engage each other's actual arguments, respond to what the other says rather than talking past each other, and each make at least one genuine concession.
