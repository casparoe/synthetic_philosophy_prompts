I keep coming back to the sphex wasp example that Hofstadter and Dennett both use — the digger wasp that drags a paralyzed cricket to the burrow entrance, goes inside to inspect, comes back out, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches it just runs the whole sequence again from scratch, over and over. It looks deliberate, but it's a fixed program. The obvious anti-determinist move is to say we're different because we can notice our own routines and revise them. But "noticing and revising" is itself just a routine we run, at a higher level.
