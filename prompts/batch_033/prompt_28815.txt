I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether justification ever bottoms out. He is a foundationalist — he insists there must be basic beliefs, self-justifying or non-inferentially justified, that halt the regress. I kept asking what makes those beliefs justified if nothing supports them, and he kept telling me I was asking a question that makes sense only inside the very framework I was challenging. We went in circles for two hours, and by the end neither of us had moved an inch. But the argument had a shape — a kind of back-and-forth rhythm that felt almost formal, like a dance where each step calls forth its opposite. I want to capture that shape in a poem.

Please write a villanelle about the epistemic regress problem — the trilemma among foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. The two refrains should embody two of the three positions (I leave it to you which two, and how the third enters the poem). Someone who knows the debate should be able to identify which refrain belongs to which position and why they keep circling each other. The form should do philosophical work: the way villanelle refrains alternate through the tercets and finally meet in the closing couplet should track something structural about how the positions relate — whether they pass each other by, collapse into one another, or resolve into the third position. The poem should be accurate enough that an epistemologist would recognize the views rather than merely spot name-dropped jargon; the ideas need to be right even if the language stays poetic.

Please deliver the poem first, then a section titled "Commentary" of roughly 300–400 words explaining the structural choices: which refrain represents which position, how the tercets develop the argument, whether and where a volta occurs, what the closing couplet achieves, and how the villanelle form itself mirrors the philosophical structure of the regress problem. Use American English.
