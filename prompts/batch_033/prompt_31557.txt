After forty years in atmospheric chemistry I retired and, on something of a whim, started working through moral philosophy with a small reading group. The question I keep getting stuck on is why promises bind. I've come to see two live camps, and I'm not sure how far apart they really are. On one side, the practice (or convention) view: promising is a social institution, and the obligation to keep a promise comes from the rules of that institution — breaking a promise is wrong in roughly the way that cheating within a rule-governed practice, or free-riding on a mutually beneficial arrangement, is wrong, so the obligation is somehow conditional on there being a going practice and on the practice being the kind of thing worth supporting. On the other side, the assurance (or expectation) view: what does the moral work is the individual act of voluntarily leading another person to expect and rely on your performance; the obligation is owed to the promisee, and the social practice of promising is just the usual vehicle for giving assurance, not the source of the obligation. I cant tell whether the two camps are genuinely disagreeing or mostly using "practice" and "obligation" differently, and the group has gone back and forth without resolution.

What I'd like is the document that would come out of a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a committed practice/convention theorist and a committed assurance/expectation theorist — the document both could actually sign, with the residual disagreement made exact. Please draft it as: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, substantive rather than just terminological; (2) a disagreements table giving the precise remaining disagreement(s), each side's view worded so that side would accept it; (3) a short list of cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about the case stated in advance. I suspect one crux is whether anything like full promissory obligation could arise between two people who share no practice of promising at all, and another is whether the fundamental wrong in breaking a promise is a wrong against the promisee or against the other participants in the practice. Also flag any part of the dispute you think is merely verbal. Hard limit: 900 words; if it doesn't fit, cut the least important crux rather than the agreed claims.
