so I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I've been reading around in the Strawson reactive-attitudes stuff because my next book is basically about blame — a guy does something unforgivable and the people around him have to figure out whether and how to keep relating to him. I've ended up with a loose set of commitments and I want to know if they actually hang together before I build a whole novel on them:

1. I'm a compatibilist. I think determinism, if it's true, dosen't touch moral responsibility, and I'm pretty convinced by Strawson that resentment, gratitude, blame, forgiveness — the reactive attitudes — aren't a theory we adopt after checking the metaphysics. They're just what it is to be in ordinary human relationships. The question 'is blame justified if determinism is true?' feels close to a category mistake.

2. I think a novel only works if the writer takes what Strawson calls the participant stance toward their characters. I resent mine, I forgive them, I get genuinely hurt by them. I've stopped reading books where the author stood outside their people like a case worker.

3. But I also think there are real people — and I've written a couple — whose whole way of being is so plainly the deposit of what was done to them, abuse, addiction, a brain that never gave them a chance, that resenting them feels like resenting a landslide. For those people the objective stance — seeing them as something to be understood, managed, helped — feels more truthful and more humane.

4. I don't think the objective stance has to be cold. I think it can be a kind of emphathy, maybe the deepest kind, because it stops asking the person to be someone else.

5. And I think 'he was shaped by his circumstances' is an excusing consideration of the same broad kind as 'he didn't know' — a fact about him that, once you really take it in, silences blame.

Is this a consistent package? I genuinely can't tell where the strain is. Go through the pairs, tell me which of these are actually in tension and which only look like they are, rank the real tensions by severity, and say what has to give in each case. Then tell me which single revision would do the least damage to the rest — and to the novel.
