I keep seeing this line on the forum whenever someone's arguing about evidence and induction: "a white shoe confirms that all ravens are black." I want to get clear on what this slogan is actually saying. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making — from the trivial logical point about contraposition to the strong epistemological claim that observing my socks genuinely supports an ornithological hypothesis — and tell me, for each, whether it's true, false, or genuinely contested? And crucially: which of these readings would actually licence the move from "confirmation has some counterintuitive features" to "therefore your pre-theoretic judgments about evidence are systematically unreliable"?
