I've been down a rabbit hole on the knowing-how vs. knowing-that thing — started with Ryle, then the Stanley & Williamson (2001) paper that tries to collapse knowing-how into knowing-that (you know how to ride a bike iff you know, under a practical mode of presentation, that some way w is a way for you to ride a bike), and then all the back-and-forth since. I think I get the moves but I have trouble holding the whole dialectic in my head at once.

So here's what I'd love: write a dialogue between an intellectualist and a neo-Rylean who run into each other at the pub after a conference panel on this exact topic. They're both sharp, they both know the literature cold, and neither is a pushover. They should actually engage with each other's points rather than talking past each other.

I want the dialogue to cover: the regress argument (if knowing-how is knowing-that, don't you need further knowing-how to apply it?), the gradability problem (you can know how to swim better or worse, but you don't know that Paris is the capital of France better or worse — or do you?), and the question of what a "practical mode of presentation" even cashes out to. If the intellectualist's practical modes of presentation just are knowing-how in disguise, then the reduction is circular. If they're not, then what are they?

The dialogue should end with them identifying where their disagreement actually bottoms out — like, what fact about cognition or metaphysics would settle it one way or the other. I don't want a fake reconciliation, but I do want the sense that they've clarified what they're really fighting about.
