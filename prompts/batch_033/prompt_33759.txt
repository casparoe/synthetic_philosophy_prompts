so I've been going back and forth on wrong-kind reasons for a while and I want a consistency check on where I've landed before I put any of this in a post. Here's roughly the package:

(1) In the demon case — the one where a demon will punish you unless you admire him — you have a genuine reason to admire the demon. Not just a reason to want to admire him or to cause yourself to admire him: a reason to admire him, full stop. If there were no such reason the case wouldn't have the pull it does.

(2) But this reason is the wrong kind of reason. It doesn't make the demon admirable, and it has nothing to do with what he's like. Its a reason of a different kind from the reasons that bear on whether admiration fits its object.

(3) I also hold the fitting-attitude account of value, roughly: X is good just in case X merits a pro-attitude, where merit is fixed by X's features and is primitive. I don't want to analyze merit in terms of reasons, and I don't think the demon's threat makes him merit anything.

(4) Reasons transmit across means: if you have a reason to bring about E, and M-ing is a means to bringing about E, then you have a reason to M. I'm not giving this up.

(5) When the demon's threat gives you a reason to admire him, what it actually rationalizes is taking steps to cause yourself to admire him — focusing on whatever's least unappealing about him, taking a pill if there were one, that sort of thing. It doesn't rationalize the admiration itself, and anyway admiration isn't under direct voluntary control.

(6) But I also think you can't have a reason to bring about an attitude unless the attitude itself is good to be in, in some respect. Reasons for means have to be transmitted from something, and in this case the only candidate something would be the state of admiring the demon.

I can't tell whether the real problem here is substantive or just a verbal dispute about what "a reason to admire" means, and I keep changing my mind about which of these is load-bearing. Go through the pairs and tell me which are actually in tension, which only look like they are, rank the tensions by severity, and tell me what the cheapest single revision is and what it costs. I'd rather have an audit than a literature review.
