I'm trying to figure out the principle behind moral progress as expanding the circle, going by cases. Here are my verdicts:

- Extending moral concern from one's tribe to all humans: clear moral progress.
- Extending concern to non-human animals: progress, though I'm unsure if it's the same kind of progress.
- Extending concern to future generations: progress, but this feels different because future people don't exist yet.
- Extending concern to ecosystems or species (not just the individual animals): I want to say this is progress but I'm not sure it's expanding the circle in the same sense.
- A white supremacist who extends concern to black people but withdraws concern from Jewish people: not progress, even though the circle expanded in one direction.
- A person who extends concern to their political opponents after years of dehumanizing them: progress, maybe even paradigm progress.
- Extending concern to AI systems that pass the Turing test: I have no intuition here, which itself worries me.
- A vegan who starts eating mussels on the grounds that they're probably not sentient: I don't think this is moral regress, but it does shrink the circle.

I'm trying to see if there's a single principle that captures when expanding the circle counts as moral progress. Is it just "include more sentient beings"? That handles the animals case but not ecosystems. Is it "include more entities with interests"? That might handle ecosystems but then what about the AI case? And the white supremacist case suggests that expansion isn't sufficient if it's accompanied by contraction elsewhere. But then does all moral progress require simultaneous expansion in all directions? That seems too demanding.

What principle best captures my verdicts, and which of my verdicts would I have to drop to make it work?
