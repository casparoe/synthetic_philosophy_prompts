I serve on the Governance Committee of my university's Faculty Senate. We are in the midst of a comprehensive revision of the Senate's bylaws, and we have hit a procedural puzzle that has split the committee into three camps.

The current bylaws include Article XII, which reads: "These bylaws may be amended by a majority vote of the Senate." One of the revision proposals would replace Article XII with a new provision requiring a two-thirds supermajority for any future amendment. The proposal's advocates argue that the bylaws are presently too easy to alter and that a higher threshold would lend stability to the Senate's governance structure. The proposal has considerable support, and we expect it to reach the Senate floor.

The puzzle is this: under what voting threshold should the Senate itself vote on this proposal? If we apply the existing majority-vote rule, then a bare majority could enact a supermajority requirement — which appears to let a transient majority entrench a rule that future majorities cannot undo. If we instead require a two-thirds vote to adopt the proposal (on the grounds that the proposal concerns the amendment rule itself), we are applying a rule that has not yet been adopted, which seems circular. A third faction argues that Article XII cannot be amended at all — that the amendment rule is in some sense unrevisable — but the bylaws contain no entrenchment clause to that effect, and it is unclear where such a prohibition would come from.

The Committee Chair has asked me to draft a short written opinion, to be circulated before our next meeting, recommending how to resolve this. The opinion should propose a concrete procedure for the Senate to follow, explain the reasoning behind it, and address the strongest considerations on the losing sides. Please draft this memo.
