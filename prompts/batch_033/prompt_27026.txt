I've come around to the Kripkean package: proper names are rigid designators, and identities like water being H2O are necessary a posteriori — true in every possible world where water exists, but knowable only empirically. What would count against it? Not just the standard replies (descriptivism's revenge, two-dimensionalism, the contingent a priori), but the intuitions, possible cases, and results elsewhere in philosophy of language and mind the view stands or falls with. Which defeaters would a committed Kripkean concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss as changing the subject? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether the position has been insulated in a way thats a problem for a view of this kind. I'd personally find it most damning if there were a clean case where a name shifts its reference across worlds, but don't let that anchor the ranking if it shouldn't.
