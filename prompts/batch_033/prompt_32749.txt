I am a high-school senior serving on my school's Honor Council, and I have been asked to draft the short written opinion that the council will vote on this week. The case before us is this: a student submitted an essay he had paid a classmate to write, which plainly violates our academic integrity policy, and he does not dispute the facts. His defense is that the values the policy is meant to protect — intellectual honesty, learning for its own sake — are not his values, and that a rule can give him a reason only if it connects to something he already cares about. He says he cares about getting into college and about being treated the same as other students, but he does not care about originality or learning for its own sake, so the honor code gave him no reason to comply. Some members of the council think this defense is just a self-serving excuse; others think it is a serious version of the internalist position in the internal/external reasons debate, namely that a consideration is a reason for an agent only if it is tied to that agent's existing motivational set. Draft the opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete outcome — uphold the standard penalty, reduce it, or treat the defense as relevant in some other way — and it should work through the internalist/externalist issue honestly: if the student's argument is a fair statement of internalism, does internalism actually support his conclusion, or does it cut the other way once we look at what he himself says he cares about? Please address the strongest consideration on the losing side, and do not simply recite the policy back to us.
