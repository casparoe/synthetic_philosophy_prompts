In world history we spent one day on the School of Names, and the only thing that stuck with me is the sentence the teacher wrote on the board: "a white horse is not a horse." He basically said it was a famous ancient Chinese puzzle and moved on, so nobody in class actually knows what it means. My friend has turned it into his personal trump card. Anytime somebody says one thing is a kind of another thing — a hot dog is a sandwich, a graphic novel is a novel, an electric scooter is a vehicle — he goes "white horse is not a horse" and acts like the argument is over. Last week I said a veggie burger is still a burger and he told me I was making the exact mistake the white horse thing is about, but when I asked him what the sentence actually means he couldn't really explain.

I want to push back, but I don't know what I'd be pushing back on. Can you lay out the different precise claims that sentence could be making, from the boring ones to the radical ones, and mark wich are trivially true, which are interesting and maybe right, and which are just false? And then tell me which reading my friend would actually need for his move to work — like, does "a white horse is not a horse" actually support saying "an X Y is not a Y" whenever someone sticks an adjective in front? I'd like something I can bring to him as a list.
