For my philosophy of mind seminar we've been deep in Dennett's "Real Patterns" paper and his stuff on the three stances, and there's this one question I keep going in circles on. So Dennett says the intentional stance picks out real patterns — they're real because they're predictive, they compress information, you'd have to do way more computational work to get the same predictions from the physical stance. But then he also says things like the intentional stance is a stance you can take or not, as you wish, and that beliefs and desires are abstracta like centers of gravity. And I can't tell if that makes them genuinely real or just, like, instrumentally useful fictions that we project onto systems when it's convenient.

Could you write a dialogue between two people who disagree about exactly this? One of them should be on board with Dennett's mild realism about intentional patterns — they'd say something like, look, if a pattern lets you predict behavior you couldn't otherwise predict without insane amounts of computation, and the pattern is there whether you take the stance or not, then it's real in the only sense that matters. The other should push back and say no, the fact that you can adopt or drop the stance at will shows these aren't real properties of the system — they're artifacts of your interpretive framework, and the only thing that's genuinely real is what the physical stance describes.

I want them to really dig into this, not just state positions. Like, have them go after each other's analogies, press on what "real" even means here, and maybe get into whether the design stance is in the same boat or whether it's different. Ideally the dialogue should surface the tension in Dennett's own view rather than just having one person be right. Dont make either of them a strawman — both should have genuinely good points.
