Before I read more accounts of races to the bottom and multipolar traps (the phenomenon sometimes called Moloch), I want the specification first. Tell me what any adequate account of these situations has to deliver: the structural features that make something a multipolar trap rather than a bare conflict of interest or a zero-sum game; the cases it must sort correctly (arms races, positional competition, commons overuse, jurisdictional regulatory competition, and at least one case that looks like a trap but isn't); the distinctions it can't collapse (local versus global optimum, individually rational versus collectively self-defeating, traps that cheap talk or side payments would dissolve versus those that only a change in payoffs would, races that are real versus ones that are only perceived); and the empirical regularities about institutions, preference formation, and strategic interaction it must respect. Mark which of these are fixed constraints and which are parochial to one modeling tradition, and say whether any subset can't be jointly satisfied — that finding would be worth more than the list. Then, in a few lines each, show how the standard game-theoretic, evolutionary, and institutional treatments fare against it. If the request is itself misposed — say, because "multipolar trap" is a grab-bag rather than a unified kind, or the real disagreements are normative rather than structural — say so and tell me what I should be asking instead; don't pad the list to make the task look doable.
