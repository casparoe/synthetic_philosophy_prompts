So I've been going back and forth with some friends about whether social media algorithms are "neutral" or whether they have values baked into them, and my one buddy keeps shutting the whole thing down with "guns don't kill people, people kill people." His point is that the algorithm is just a tool, and if it's causing polarization or whatever that's on us for how we use it, not on the tool itself. He says this like it's obviously right and the coversation is over.

But the more I think about it the more I feel like there's something slippery going on. Like, okay, a gun is designed for exactly one thing — putting holes in things at a distance — and that does seem different from, say, a kitchen knife which can at least plausibly be used for non-violent stuff. And then there's stuff like speed bumps, which literally force you to slow down regardless of what you "intend." So "guns don't kill people" might be saying something true about guns but false about speed bumps, or it might be saying something true in a trivial way but false in the way he's using it to talk about recommender systems.

What I want is someone to lay out, clearly, the different precise claims that "guns don't kill people, people kill people" could actually be making. Like, what are the distinct theses hiding in that one sentence? Which ones are basically obvious (like "inanimate objects don't have intentions"), which ones are substantive and worth arguing about, and which ones are just false? And crucially: which reading does my friend need to be true for his argument about algorithms to actually work, and is that reading defensible?
