I'm working on a novel and the protagonist is a neuroscientist, so I've been reading a bunch of pop-neuroscience to get the voice right. One thing that keeps coming up is this idea that consciousness is a "user illusion" — like the desktop on your computer. The icons and folders aren't really there, they're just a convenient interface for the actual machine code. And supposedly conscious experience is the same deal: we feel like we have this rich inner life with direct access to our thoughts and perceptions, but it's all a simplified readout the brain generates after the fact. The real work is unconscious.

Here's what's been bugging me enought that I can't write. If consciousness is literally an illusion — and the scientists seem pretty settled on this, it's not just some stoned undergrad thing — then my characters don't have inner lives either. They're just biological machines with a fake dashboard. Which means the entire tradition of the psychological novel, interior monologue, free indirect discourse, all of it, is describing something that doesn't exist. Woolf, Proust, Knausgaard — they're all just making up a fantasy and calling it realism.

Am I missing something here, or should novelists who buy this just stop pretending we can represent consciousness? And please answer in British English if you don't mind, the book's set in London.
