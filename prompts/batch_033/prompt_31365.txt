I've been thinking on my own about how much other people's reports should count when it comes to beauty and art. I'd like a "which position on aesthetic testimony do you actually hold?" quiz — that is, a quiz on how much we can learn about beauty from other people's reports — for myself and a few friends. Make it eight questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options; none of the questions should just ask which label we accept. The fault line I most want to test is the gap between forming a true belief that a work is beautiful and actually experiencing its beauty, so several questions should probe that. Please work these cases in somewhere: a friend whose taste you respect calls a film you haven't seen a masterpiece; a wine critic's note names flavors you haven't yet tasted; someone tells you a paragraph is beautiful before you read it; and people whose judgment you trust say a novel you loved is sentimental. Add a scoring key that maps answer patterns to pessimism about aesthetic testimony (others' reports cannot justify or transmit aesthetic judgment), optimism (they can, in the right conditions, give full aesthetic knowledge), and at least one intermediate view that allows justified aesthetic belief from others while denying that it can replace first-hand experience. Flag any two positions the quiz cannot cleanly separate. Give the quiz and the scoring key directly, with no preface or commentary.
