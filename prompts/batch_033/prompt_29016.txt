I'm teaching a third-year undergrad metaphysics course and we're about to hit the sea battle / future contingents stuff — Aristotle, De Interpretatione 9, the whole thing. Last year I just lectured through the positions and by the end everyone nodded along but I'm pretty sure nobody actually knew which view they held or why, they just sort of picked the one that sounded least weird. So this year I want to start the unit with a handout: a decision tree that takes a student from "I have ordinary beliefs about the future" to a named position on whether future contingent statements have truth-values now.

The tree should be built from the logical space rather than from a list of names. The key branching questions I can think of are: whether statements about the future have truth-values at all before the fact (bivalence), whether having a truth-value implies necessity (the modal bridge principle), whether the future is genuinely open or merely epistemically open, and whether God's foreknowledge changes anything if you want to go there. But I'm sure I'm missing some distinctions and I'd love to see what the full space looks like. I want leaves for at least: logical fatalism, Aristotle's own view, Ockhamism, Peirceanism, supervaluationism, and the thin red line view. If their are logical positions with no defenders, flag them and say why nobody lives there — is the view incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored?

For the response format: please give me (1) The Decision Tree itself, as a series of branching questions with paths to leaves, maybe 300–500 words. Phrase the questions so a student can answer without already knowing the labels — I want them sorted by what they believe, not by which position sounds coolest. (2) A Mapping section, maybe 200 words, that lists each leaf, names the position, and gives one sentence on its most awkward commitment. (3) A Separability Audit, maybe 150 words, flagging any pairs of positions the tree can't actually pull apart and why. (4) A "What Would Change My Mind" note, maybe 100 words, on which branching question you're least confident about placing and what argument would make you restructure the tree.
