I'm a machine learning researcher working on mechanistic interpretability. My day-to-day involves running ablation experiments on transformer components, probing residual stream directions, and trying to answer questions like "what is this attention head for?" But I've realized that the word "for" is doing a lot of work I don't understand. When I say a head is "for" copying information from the previous token, I might mean that removing it breaks that behavior — a causal role in the forward pass. Or I might mean that it was shaped by gradient descent to perform that function — something about its training history. Or I might mean something else entirely. A philosopher friend tells me that philosophy of biology has been arguing about exactly this distinction for decades, under the names "etiological account" and "causal-role account" of biological function, and that I'm essentially rediscovering their problem in a new domain.

Restate the core disagreement between the etiological (selected-effects) account and the causal-role account of biological function in the vocabulary I actually work with: loss functions, gradient descent, forward passes, computational graphs, credit assignment via backpropagation, training versus inference, ablation studies, and the distinction between what a component was optimized to do and what it happens to do in the trained model. Be as faithful as you can to the philosophical positions — don't let the fact that I know the training history exactly, while biologists have to infer it from comparative and paleontological evidence, quietly settle the debate in favor of one side. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which ones you had to distort, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I especially want to know whether anything in the philosophers' debate sheds light on a problem I actually face — like how to think about a component whose causal role shifts between training and deployment, or what "malfunction" could even mean for a learned representation — or whether the residue mostly runs the other way, with my framework having concepts (the loss function itself, the training distribution, distribution shift) that the biological debate lacks and might actually need.
