I'm a member of my high school's philosophy and economics club, and we recently spent a few sessions on Bayesian epistemology and its intersection with game theory. One idea that generated a lot of debate was the "common prior assumption" (CPA) — the claim that any difference in beliefs between two rational agents ought to be explainable entirely by a difference in the information they have received. If two people start from the same prior probability distribution and each conditionalizes on their own evidence, their resulting beliefs are as they should be, and any remaining disagreement reflects only different evidence. But if they start from different priors, then even with identical evidence they could rationally disagree forever. The CPA shows up in a lot of places: Harsanyi argued for it as a methodological principle in game theory, and Aumann's famous agreement theorem shows that if two agents share a common prior and have common knowledge of each other's posterior probabilities, their posteriors must actually be equal. But the CPA is controversial. Some people think it is a straightforward requirement of rationality — without it, you cannot explain why rational inquiry should ever lead to convergence. Others think it is wildly implausible as a normative standard, since there are indefinitely many internally coherent prior distributions and no purely a priori way to single one out as uniquely correct.

Our club could not reach a consensus, so I thought it would be helpful to work through the disagreement by writing a fictional dialogue between two characters who each hold one of these positions and are genuinely trying to persuade each other. Could you write that dialogue for me? Both characters should be equally competent and should engage with each other's actual arguments — no strawmen, no talking past each other. The dialogue should dig into the real philosophical substance: the implications of Aumann's theorem, the problem of the priors, the distinction between ideal rationality and the kinds of agents we actually are, and whatever else you think is relevant. I would like it to feel like a real exchange where both sides have to refine their views in response to the other's objections.
