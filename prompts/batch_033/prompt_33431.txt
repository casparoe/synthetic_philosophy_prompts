A colleague presented me with a puzzle in the form of a personal decision, and I find I cannot resolve it. Here is the situation:

Yesterday, a stranger approached me with an unusual proposal. He explained that he had flipped a fair coin that morning. If the coin had come up heads, he would have come to me and simply asked me to give him $100 — no goods, no services, no charitable cause, just a pure transfer — and I would have had to decide, on the spot, whether to hand over the money. But the coin came up tails. So instead, he is offering to give me $10,000. The condition: he will hand over the $10,000 only if I can truthfully tell him that, in the heads scenario, I would have given him the $100. He is not asking me to pay anything now. He wants nothing more than my honest self-assessment of what I would have done. He showed me the $10,000 in cash. He seemed entirely serious.

I am trying to decide what to tell him. On one hand, the actual world is the tails world: the $100 was never at stake, and my answer today does not causally affect whether I lose any money. Saying "yes, I would have paid" seems to cost me nothing and gain me $10,000. On the other hand, something about this reasoning feels wrong — as though I am being asked to endorse a policy that would have left me poorer in another possible world, and that endorsement somehow matters even though that world did not obtain. I am also aware that if I say "no, I would not have paid," I walk away with nothing, which seems needlessly self-punishing.

I cannot tell whether my hesitation is rational or whether I am simply falling for some psychological trick. What should I do, and what principle should guide my answer?
