I chair the outbreak-scenario committee of a regional public health authority. We maintain a probability model over four mutually exclusive states for the coming respiratory-virus season:

- VH: a new variant is present and hospitalizations exceed the surge threshold;
- VL: a new variant is present and hospitalizations do not exceed the threshold;
- ¬VH: no new variant is present and hospitalizations exceed the threshold;
- ¬VL: no new variant is present and hospitalizations do not exceed the threshold.

Our agreed prior is P(VH) = 0.15, P(VL) = 0.25, P(¬VH) = 0.10, P(¬VL) = 0.50.

This morning our modeling group sent a single report: "If the new variant is present, the odds that hospitalizations exceed the threshold are 3 to 1." The committee must decide by Thursday how to revise the full distribution, because our recommendation on pre-positioning supplies depends on both the posterior probability that hospitalizations exceed the threshold and the posterior probability that the variant is present.

Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a specific updating rule, apply it to our prior so the committee can see the revised numbers, and explain why that rule is preferable to the two alternatives the committee will raise: (i) the minimal-information rule, which minimizes relative entropy subject to the conditional-odds constraint, and (ii) the rule that adjusts only the conditional distribution within the variant-present branch, leaving the probability that the variant is present and the distribution conditional on its absence unchanged. Engage the Judy Benjamin problem directly: the report is a constraint on a conditional probability rather than a proposition in the event algebra, so ordinary Bayesian conditioning gives no unique answer, and the minimal-information rule has the well-known feature of moving probability mass between the variant-present and variant-absent branches even though the report appears silent on whether the variant is present. Give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration in favor of the rule you recommend against, and say explicitly where your recommended rule is underdetermined or likely to misbehave.
