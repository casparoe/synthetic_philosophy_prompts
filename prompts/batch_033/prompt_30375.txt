I run a small evaluation team inside a robotics company that builds reinforcement learning agents for warehouses and light industrial sites. Our agents are given narrow task rewards—move crates to the correct conveyor, navigate to a charging point, count items on a shelf—and we repeatedly find that they cause side effects no one thought to penalise: knocking over barriers, blocking doorways, jamming sensors, shifting equipment. We cannot enumerate every side effect in the reward, so we want to use a general impact penalty. The candidates on the table are relative reachability with a stepwise inaction baseline, attainable utility preservation with a stepwise inaction baseline, and a simple unreachability penalty that compares the current state with the starting state. We do not know how to choose among these, how to set their parameters, or what failure modes to check before approving one for deployment.

Please write a decision procedure my team can actually run on each candidate impact measure before we approve it. The procedure should be reusable across deployments, not specific to one warehouse. It should include: the questions to ask in order; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; where the procedure stops with a no; where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than fact; and a short set of diagnostic simulation scenarios we should run, specified in words rather than code. The diagnostics should target at least these failure modes: interference with changes that would have happened without the agent, offsetting or gaming the penalty by making one change and then trading it off against another, and magnitude insensitivity, where a small irreversible disruption and a large one are treated as equally bad.

Then apply the procedure to the three candidate measures above. For each candidate, state where it passes and where it fails, and say which feature of the measure—the baseline, the deviation measure, the normalisation, or the auxiliary reward functions—explains the verdict. Include at least one case where the procedure produces an answer my team will dislike, such as telling us that our cheapest or most familiar measure is unusable, or that no candidate can safely be used for a proposed task. Flag every step that is a judgement call dressed up as procedure, and say what the procedure cannot settle on its own.

Constraints: we have two engineers and roughly half a day to evaluate one candidate measure; we can run gridworld-style simulations but cannot produce formal proofs; the safety lead must sign off on any measure we deploy; and none of the engineers has a background in philosophy.
