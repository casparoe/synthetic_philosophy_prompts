im a novelist and i need a poem to use as an epigraph for a chapter where a game theorist gets sucked into a dollar auction. i want it to be a pantoum — the form where the second and fourth lines of each stanza become the first and third lines of the next stanza, and the final stanza loops back so its second and fourth lines are the first stanza's first and third lines. i want the repeated lines to be the bidder's justifications for raising one more time, and each time a line comes back it should land a little differently because the price has climbed. so the form is doing the conceptual work: the reader should feel how, at every step, raising is the locally rational move and how that exact same reasoning is what traps you. the setup is the standard dollar auction: an auctioneer sells a one-dollar bill to the highest bidder, but the second-highest bidder also has to pay their bid and gets nothing. once two people have bid, the runner-up is always better off raising a little than dropping out, and that stays true even after the bids pass a dollar. the poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the dollar auction and escalation of commitment recognizes the trap, not just a generic poem about greed or stubbornness. aim for five or six quatrains, and please write it in American English.
