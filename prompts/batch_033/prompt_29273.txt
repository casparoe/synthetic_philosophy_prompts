I am a novelist researching the continental drift controversy for a book about scientific conviction and delayed vindication, and I want a disciplined Lakatosian analysis of the episode from Wegener's early twentieth-century proposal through the plate tectonics revolution of the 1960s. Map the drift programme as it stood by about 1930: its hard core, its protective belt, what the negative heuristic forbade and the positive heuristic directed, and which adjustments — for instance, the proposed mechanisms for how continents move — functioned as ad hoc belt changes rather than novel predictions; then say whether the programme was degenerating by Lakatos's criteria or merely stalled for lack of a mechanism. Map plate tectonics in the same terms: its hard core, how seafloor spreading and the magnetic-stripe data worked as novel predictions, and whether its consolidation should be counted as a new research programme or a progressive revival of Wegener's. Finally, turn the episode back on the theory: what does this case show about Lakatos's distinction between a degenerating programme and a temporarily dormant one, and can his criteria make that distinction before the fact rather than only in hindsight? Where the theory is silent, say so plainly rather than forcing it to speak.
