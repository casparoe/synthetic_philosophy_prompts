ok so i'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, but I've been going down the AI-safety/LessWrong rabbit hole about Moloch and multipolar traps and races to the bottom, and its started to bleed into how I think about my actual job (I work on systems where a bunch of teams or companies are optimizing against each other). I tried to write down where I've landed and I'd like someone to tell me if it's internally coherent before I bring it to my reading group. Here's the list:

(1) Moloch-type dynamics are real: a bunch of individually reasonable decisions can lock a whole system into an outcome basically everyone involved hates, and nobody chose it on purpose.

(2) The people inside those traps usually aren't being stupid or evil. Given the incentives they actually face, they're doing the locally rational thing; the problem is the structure, not the individuals.

(3) You can't fix that kind of situation by getting people to be more virtuous or more aware of the problem. You have to change the incentive structure — coordination mechanisms, regulation, mechanism design, whatever works.

(4) In my day job I'm pretty pro-competition and pretty suspicious of regulation. Most of the best software I've used exists because companies were forced to compete, and a lot of the worst stuff I've seen came from cozy monopolies.

(5) When I design a system, I assume the agents in it will act on their incentives, not their stated values or good intentions.

(6) I also think that when a situation is systematically bad for everyone in it, and the people involved are smart and can see the problem clearly, they'll eventually find a way to coordinate out of it — maybe slowly, but they do.

Are these actually compatible with each other? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and which single change would fix the most with the least damage? Please rank the tensions by how bad they are instead of just saying everything is equally problematic, because I honestly can't tell where the weak point is.
