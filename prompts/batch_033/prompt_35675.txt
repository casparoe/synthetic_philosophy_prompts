Write a dialogue between two philosophers who disagree about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism about value. One character holds that every pleasant experience is valuable for the same reason, because pleasure is a single unified phenomenon and apparent differences among pleasures are differences in their objects, causes, or intensity rather than in the pleasure itself. The other character holds that what we call pleasure is a motley collection of experiences with no common felt character and perhaps no common evaluative ground, so that a value theory built on pleasure rests on an unsupported generalisation. The dialogue should work through concrete cases together: the pleasure of a warm bath, the pleasure of solving a proof after days of effort, relief at escaping danger, malicious glee at a rival's failure, an addict's fix, and aesthetic pleasure. Each character should restate the other's view accurately before objecting to it, each should press the other at the point where their position is weakest, and each should make at least one genuine concession. Write the dialogue as continuous prose in the manner of a short story or a letter, using quotation marks and speech tags rather than script formatting, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and write in British English.
