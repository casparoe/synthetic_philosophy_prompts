Draft the joint statement that an actual intentionalist and an anti-intentionalist about literary meaning could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The actual intentionalist holds that the meaning of a literary work is constituted by what its author intended to communicate in writing it; the anti-intentionalist holds that the meaning is constituted by what the text itself conveys, independent of authorial intention, and that appeal to intention in settling meaning commits the intentional fallacy.

I want the following:

(1) A numbered list of claims both sides accept. These should include claims about what is not in dispute (e.g., that authors have intentions, that texts have linguistic meanings, that some interpretations are plainly wrong), claims about the role of public linguistic conventions, and claims about paradigm cases where both sides reach the same verdict even if their routes differ. Do not let the agreed claims collapse into vacuity—they should be substantive enough that signing them represents real progress.

(2) A disagreements table. Each row should state a single point of dispute in neutral framing, followed by the intentionalist's formulation of their own position and the anti-intentionalist's formulation of theirs, each in wording its own side would accept as fair. Points of dispute should include at least: the status of unintended yet textually supported meanings, the relevance of an author's later statements about their own work, the treatment of irony and unreliable narration, and the question of whether meaning can change after the work is written.

(3) The cruxes: the sub-questions such that if they were settled, at least one side would have to revise its view. For each crux, provide a concrete test case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction about what that case shows.

(4) If any part of the dispute is merely verbal—a disagreement about the word 'meaning' rather than about the underlying facts—identify it and explain why.

End with a one-sentence summary of the collaboration's principal finding, enclosed as \answer{...}.
