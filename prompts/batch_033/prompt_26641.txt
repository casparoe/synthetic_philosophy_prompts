(2) Ben said that's the calculator argument reheated. People said calculators would ruin math education, and they didn't — they just changed what skills we value. No one hand-weaves textiles anymore either, and that's fine. Offloading cognitive tasks to tools is how civilization works.

(3) Ana said calculators are a bad analogy because arithmetic is a narrow mechanical skill, whereas writing and reasoning are constitutive of understanding itself. You can't offload the thing you're trying to build without undermining the whole project.

(6) Ben said that's a fine line in theory but in practice people do develop domain models even when using AI heavily. (7) Ana said programming might be special because code is self-testing in a way that an essay or a historical analysis isn't — you can run code and see if it works, which gives you feedback that builds understanding. For most other domains, the AI output *feels* right without the user ever developing the ability to check it.
