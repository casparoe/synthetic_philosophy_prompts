I'm a retired chemist who has spent the last couple of years working through moral philosophy, and I've been trying to get clear on Bernard Williams's critique of consequentialism. I've arrived at the following set of views, and I'd like to know whether they hang together:

(1) The "one thought too many" objection is not merely about the psychology of deliberation — it is an objection to the structure of consequentialist justification. The point is that the fact that someone is my wife should figure directly in my reasons for saving her, not be mediated by an impartial principle.

(2) Railton's sophisticated consequentialism successfully answers the "one thought too many" objection. By distinguishing the criterion of rightness from the decision procedure, it allows that the good consequentialist agent may be motivated directly by personal attachments without invoking the criterion in deliberation.

(3) The integrity objection is a distinct objection from "one thought too many," and it is not answered by the sophisticated consequentialist's move. The integrity objection concerns the way consequentialism requires the agent to regard their own deepest projects as having value only insofar as they contribute to the overall good.

(4) The integrity objection applies to all forms of consequentialism — direct, indirect, rule-consequentialist, and sophisticated — because at the level of justification they all make the agent's projects derivative on the impersonal good.

(5) Despite these objections, I remain drawn to consequentialism because it seems to me the only moral theory that provides a clear, non-question-begging answer to the question why one should be moral at all: namely, that morality is a matter of making the world go as well as possible.

I am not sure where the tension is, if there is one, but I have a nagging sense that something does not fit. I would like an audit: which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad.
