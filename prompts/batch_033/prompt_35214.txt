I'm a senior taking a philosophy of mind elective this year, and we recently spent a few weeks on Daniel Dennett's 1992 essay "The Self as a Center of Narrative Gravity." The central analogy has really stayed with me: just as a center of gravity is not a little glowing dot hidden somewhere inside a physical object but is nevertheless a perfectly real and indispensable abstraction for predicting how the object will behave, the self is not a Cartesian theater or a homunculus inside the brain but an abstraction we posit — a kind of "theorist's fiction" — to make sense of the running narrative each of us generates about our own life. So far, I think I follow. But here is what I cannot get past, and what I keep turning over in my head.

If the self really is an abstraction — something we construct, a narrative posit rather than a concrete entity — then in what sense am I the same person who will go to college next fall, or who will have to live with the consequences of decisions I make this semester? It seems to me that Dennett's view, taken seriously, should lead me to regard my future self the way I regard a character in a novel I am writing: I can tell stories about her, I can even care about what happens to her in an aesthetic or sympathetic way, but she is not *me* in any robust sense that grounds the special kind of concern we call prudence. And yet Dennett plainly does not think we should stop treating ourselves and others as responsible agents with genuine future interests — he is not arguing for some kind of nihilism about personal identity. I feel as though I am missing a step, and I cannot tell whether the problem is with Dennett's view or with my understanding of it. What exactly is the difference between saying the self is an abstraction that is "real in its effects" and saying it is a fiction we have merely practical reasons to treat as if it were real? And if the latter is closer to the truth, why shouldn't I conclude that prudence — caring specially about my own future well-being — is just a kind of useful make-believe?
