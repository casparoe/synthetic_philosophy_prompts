I'm trying to get clear on what I actually think probability is, and I figured I'd start with my gut reactions to a bunch of cases and see if there's an interpretation that fits them — or if I need to abandon some of my reactions.

Here are the cases and my verdicts:

Coin flips. A fair coin has probability 1/2 of heads. This just means that if you flipped it forever, the proportion would converge to 1/2. Frequentism is obviously right here.

One-off events. "Probability it'll rain in Chicago next Thursday is 30%." I think this is a perfectly meaningful claim, and it's not just a report of the meteorologist's feelings — it's tracking something objective about the atmosphere. But there's no referenece class of identical Thursdays. So frequentism is already in trouble.

Scientific discoveries. When physicists announced the Higgs with "5 sigma," I treated that as a probability claim about a one-off fact — the particle either exists or it doesn't — and I was fine with it being objective. But it's not a frequency (there's no ensemble of universes where the Higgs does or doesn't exist), and it's not a subjective degree of belief either, because the whole point was that the evidence compelled the conclusion regardless of what anyone believed.

Fair lottery. One ticket in a million. Probability 1/million. Everyone agrees on this one.

Historical claims. Caesar crossing the Rubicon — I'm about as confident as I am about anything in history, but I don't want to say "probability 0.999." Assigning a number to a past event we have ordinary documentary evidence for feels like a category mistake. And yet I was fine doing it for the Higgs. What's the difference?

Quantum mechanics. The Born rule says a particle in a superposition has probability |ψ|² of being detected here. This feels like it's about something in the world — genuine chanciness, not just my ignorance. Propensity or something in the neighborhood.

My own credences. "I'm 70% sure I left my keys on the counter." This is clearly about me and my evidence, not about the world. Subjectivism nails this one.

So my verdicts pull me toward frequentism, subjectivism, propensity, and something like logical probability, all at once. Is there one interpretation that can stretch to cover most of these, and which verdicts do I have to give up? If the answer is "you're a pluralist, probability means different things in different contexts," is that a respectable position or is it a cop-out? And what does your preferred interpretation say about a case I haven't mentioned: the probability that a mathematical conjecture is true, before it's been proved?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning — no preamble, no restating the question.
