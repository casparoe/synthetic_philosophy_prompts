Construct a thought experiment in which a Skinnerian creature systematically outcompetes a Popperian creature — the opposite of the usual direction in Dennett's tower of generate-and-test, where Popperian creatures improve on Skinnerian ones by preselecting candidate behaviors in an inner environment instead of trying them against the world. Constraints: the scenario must be set in a realistic ecology with no exotic cognitive abilities; the Popperian creature's inner model must be as accurate as the history of its environment allows, so the loss must not come from the creature being foolish or from an obviously bad model; and the outcome must be generated by the difference between online and offline generate-and-test itself, not stipulated or bolted on as an extra handicap. After the scenario, do three things. (1) Walk through what the Skinnerian creature and the Popperian creature each do at each stage, and show exactly where their verdicts diverge. (2) Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, and say what would happen if it were removed or reversed. (3) Give the strongest reply a defender of the Popperian architecture could make, and then say whether that reply dissolves the inversion or merely shows that a more sophisticated Popperian creature can recover the lost ground.
