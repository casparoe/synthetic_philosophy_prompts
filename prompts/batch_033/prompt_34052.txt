I've been in a metaethics reading group for a couple years now and the Frege–Geach problem just will not die. The basic setup: expressivists say moral claims express attitudes (approval, disapproval, plans, whatever) rather than beliefs, so "murder is wrong" is more like "boo murder!" than "murder has the property of wrongness." Frege and Geach pointed out that this falls apart when you embed moral claims in conditionals or negations: "if murder is wrong, then getting your brother to murder is wrong" doesn't seem to express disapproval of murder in the antecedent, and yet the sentence is perfectly meaningful and logically valid. So expressivists need to explain how moral language gets its compositional semantics without treating moral predicates as picking out properties.

Here's the thing: expressivists have been answering this for like forty years. Blackburn has his commitment-theoretic account, Gibbard has norm expressivism and then the plan-laden thought framework, Schroeder has the being-for relation, Charlow, Ridge, Silk, etc. And every few years someone (Schroeder himself, or Unwin, or Dreier) points out a new embedding that the latest version can't handle, or shows that the expressivist is secretly smuggling in truth-conditional semantics, or that the solution only works at the cost of making expressivism indistinguishable from realism. Then the expressivists patch it, and the cycle repeats.

My working hypothesis is that the debate isn't really about whether expressivists *can* solve the embedding problem. It's about what counts as a solution. The critics want a compositional semantics that assigns the right logical properties using only attitude-expressing resources, without any representational cheat. The expressivists keep producing accounts that work, but the critics keep saying "that's not *really* expressivism anymore" or "you've just reinvented truth conditions under another name." So it feels like the real disagreement is about the individuation conditions for semantic theories — what makes a theory genuinely expressivist vs. a truth-conditional theory in disguise. And that's a methodological disagreement, not a technical one.

Am I right about this? Is the Frege–Geach dialectic actually a substantive dispute that could be settled, or is it a clash of background commitments about what semantics is supposed to do, where neither side has acknowledged that they're arguing past each other? And if I'm right, what would actually move things — a formal result about the equivalence of expressivist and truth-conditional frameworks under certain conditions, or just an explicit agreement about what the adequacy conditions on a solution even are?
