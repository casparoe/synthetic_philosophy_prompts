I'm a software architect who designs event-driven distributed systems, and I recently had a long conversation with a philosopher friend that left me unsettled. She was describing a debate about "free-floating rationales" — the idea that there might be reasons for action that don't belong to any particular agent, reasons that are simply there in the world, waiting to be recognized, rather than always being reasons-for-someone grounded in that person's existing desires or projects. She traced the idea through Nagel's *The View from Nowhere* and the notion of agent-neutral reasons, and contrasted it with the view that reasons are essentially relational: a reason is always a reason for some agent, and it gets its normative force from connecting to something that agent already cares about. I tried to map this onto my own mental furniture and found myself thinking: isn't this just a debate about whether a message can be valid without a handler? In my world, an event gets emitted by a producer, it conforms to a schema, and if no consumer is subscribed, it sits in a dead-letter queue or evaporates. The event is real, but its significance — whether it prompts action — depends entirely on whether there's a consumer configured to care about it. That sounds like the internalist side. But my friend kept pushing: what if the event itself creates the obligation to handle it, and a system that ignores it is simply a badly designed system? That sounds like the externalist side, and I don't have a natural way to say it in my vocabulary.

So here's what I want. Restate the debate between the free-floating-reasons view and the reasons-are-always-relational view in the vocabulary of event-driven distributed systems: events, messages, schemas, producers, consumers, subscriptions, delivery semantics, dead-letter queues, idempotency, and so on. Don't just give me a metaphor — give me a systematic translation of each side's core claims and arguments. Then give me the residue report: which arguments map cleanly onto my concepts, which ones you had to stretch or distort to make fit, and which ones have no counterpart at all. I want to know whether the bits that don't translate are pointing to something my vocabulary is blind to, or whether the philosophical debate is partly sustained by a vocabulary that hasn't made distinctions my vocabulary already forces.
