For a working group on decision theory, write the desiderata that any adequate theory of acausal trade has to satisfy, where the purpose of the theory is to tell an agent whether, when, and on what terms it should cooperate or exchange benefits with another agent it cannot causally affect and may never communicate with. By acausal trade I mean cases where the only reason to expect your action to be matched or reciprocated is that the other party's decision procedure is correlated with, or can reason its way to, yours — standard examples being copies or near-copies of yourself in other branches of a multiverse, or a distant civilization that wants something cheap from you and can provide something you want, with no signal passing either way.

I want the full specification before I read any more proposals. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it. The list must at least speak to the following: the symmetric-copy prisoner's dilemma; an ordinary one-shot prisoner's dilemma with no such correlation; acausal blackmail, in which a powerful agent threatens harm unless you comply even though your compliance cannot causally affect whether the threat is carried out; asymmetric trades, where one side's cost or benefit is much larger, or where one side cannot verify the other's performance; trades with near-copies whose algorithms match yours only imperfectly; cases where you are uncertain whether the would-be partner exists or is running your algorithm at all; and the problem that if you may trade with every sufficiently similar agent there may be indefinitely many such agents, so the theory must not demand unlimited sacrifice or double-count the same gain. The theory should also say what an acausal agreement consists in, given that there is no message, contract, or enforcement, and it should distinguish acausal trade from mere acausal coordination on a common interest and from extortion — and say which of those differences are load-bearing.

Then mark which of your desiderata are non-negotiable and which are really commitments of a particular camp: a causal decision theorist may reject the practice outright, an evidential decision theorist may permit it for different reasons than a functional or updateless decision theorist, and so on. I especially want the tensions: identify any subsets of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, such as acting only on causal consequences while cooperating with your copy, or avoiding infinite sacrifice while cooperating with every sufficiently similar agent. Say which single requirement does the most work in excluding existing approaches.

Close with a brief scorecard of how causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and functional or updateless decision theory fare against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than the scoring.

Two standing requests. First, lead with the bottom line in the first sentence — the core of the specification and your verdict on which requirements are non-negotiable or jointly unsatisfiable — then give the full list and reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Second, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
