Two friends of mine got into it at a dinner party over whether beauty is objective. Sam said it clearly isn't: when people disagree about whether a painting is good, there's no measurement you can make to settle it, the way you could settle a disagreement about how much the painting weighs. Priya said thats too quick — taste is subjective at the level of individuals, but there's still a standard: the verdicts of experienced critics who've seen a lot, compared a lot, and aren't biased by envy or fashion. Sam came back that "experienced critic" is doing all the work: you identify good critics as the ones who rate the acknowledged masterpieces highly, but those works became acknowledged because good critics rated them highly, so the whole thing runs in a circle and the "standard" just describes which opinions happened to win. Priya said the circle isn't vicious — it's like calibrating a bunch of instruments against each other until the readings stabilize, and the stabilized reading genuinely tells you something even though no single instrument is authoritative. Sam said the instrument analogy sneaks in the assumption that there's a real quantity being measured; in art there's no independent beauty for the instruments to converge on, so convergence is just people imitating each other. Priya replied that the test of time is the independent thing: works survive because they keep rewarding careful attention, not because critics voted on them once. Sam said "keeps rewarding careful attention" is just another way of saying "people keep liking it," which is the consensus again. They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Please go through the exchange and say who's right about what at each step, what both of them are missing, and where exactly the circularity worry bites.
