I've been going through a rough patch with my partner and we've been doing couples therapy, and the therapist keeps pointing out ways I'm "deceiving myself" about my own motivations. At first I just shrugged it off as therapy-speak, but then I actually started reading some philosophy on self-deception and now I'm genuinely confused about whether the whole thing is even coherent.

Here's the thing: deception requires a deceiver and a deceived. If I'm both of them, then I have to believe some falsehood (to be deceived) while also knowing the truth (to be the deceiver). But that's just a straight-up contradiction — I can't simultaneously believe p and not-p, can I? The standard move I've seen is to say that one belief is "unconscious" or that the mind is partitioned, but that feels like a dodge. If it's unconscious, in what sense is it *my* belief? And if I don't really hold the true belief at all, then I'm not deceiving myself, I'm just mistaken about something.

So it seems like either self-deception is literally impossible, and the therapist is just describing ordinary bias or wishful thinking with a fancier name, or the whole concept is built on a contradiction and we should just abandon it. What am I missing? People talk about self-deception all the time like it's a real thing — is there actually a way to make sense of it that doesn't collapse into paradox?
