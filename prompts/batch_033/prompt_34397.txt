I was at a dinner after a conference last month and two people I know — both sharp, both in tech — got into a long argument about whether one of them should quit his job to start a company. I took notes because it felt like they were replaying the whole inside-view/outside-view debate from Kahneman and Tetlock in real time. Here's roughly how it went:

Dan said he was thinking of leaving his Big Tech job to build a fintech startup, and he had a specific thesis about an underserved market. Maya asked what the base rate is for fintech startups. Dan said he didn't know exactly but probably low. Maya said that's the number that should dominate his thinking — the outside view says you start with the class of ventures you belong to and adjust from there. Dan said that's fine for someone with no information, but he has a decade of domain expertise and a concrete product hypothesis, and the outside view deliberately ignores all that. Maya said that's exactly the trap: everyone with a startup idea thinks their inside information is special, which is why the base rate is so low, and the whole point of the outside view is to correct for that predictable overconfidence. Dan said then by that logic no one should ever start a company, because the base rate will always be terrible. Maya said that's not quite right — you can have inside information that's genuinely diagnostic, like you've already built a prototype and users are pulling it out of your hands, but Dan hasn't done that. Dan pushed back: what's the principle that separates diagnostic inside information from the kind he thinks he has? He said her position seems to be "inside information counts when it's so obvious any idiot could see it." Maya said that's a caricature, but the burden of proof is on the person who thinks they're an exception, and the default should be the outside view. Dan said the default is just another word for a prior, and priors get updated — refusing to update on your own experience is epistemic self-sabotage.

They went back and forth like this for a while and I couldn't tell who was winning. Can you go through their exchange and adjudicate it move by move — who's right about which point, and what's the right way to think about the inside/outside view distinction here?
