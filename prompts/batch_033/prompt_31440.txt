I'm a PhD student working on the epistemology of cognitive extension, and I keep noticing a pattern in my own intellectual life for which I am struggling to find the right conceptual vocabulary. The pattern goes like this. I used to be able to do something — say, reconstruct the dialectical structure of a paper I had read, or design a survey experiment with proper counterbalancing — without any external aid. Then I began using an AI tool to assist with it. At first, the tool was genuinely an auxiliary: I still understood what I was doing and could have done it myself, just more slowly. But over time something shifted. I now find that when I try to perform the task without the tool, I genuinely cannot. I have lost the ability. Yet because the tool is always available and my output remains good — arguably better than before — I had no occasion to notice the loss until I was forced to work without it. The deterioration was invisible to me precisely because the tool compensated for it so seamlessly, and the very feedback that would ordinarily signal a decaying competence — deteriorating performance — was absent.

I do not think this is simply the familiar phenomenon of deskilling, because what is being lost is not a manual or procedural skill but something more like understanding — the kind of grasp that lets you notice when the tool's output is subtly wrong, or that lets you adapt an approach to a genuinely novel case. Nor is it quite the same as the 'Google effect' studied in the transactive memory literature, where people forget facts they know they can look up; here I am not storing facts externally, I am outsourcing the very capacity to reason through a domain. And it is not straightforward epistemic dependence either, because in standard cases of epistemic dependence — relying on a colleague's expertise, or on a published finding — I can typically articulate what I am relying on and why, and I retain the meta-level understanding of the domain's structure that lets me assess the dependence rationally. What I am describing is a kind of dependence that progressively erodes the very understanding that would make the dependence rationally assessable in the first place.

I suspect this phenomenon matters well beyond my own case, especially as AI tools become embedded in professional and educational settings, but I have not been able to find an established term for it. The nearest I have encountered is Shannon Vallor's discussion of moral deskilling in the context of AI, but that concerns moral capacities specifically, and what I am tracking is epistemic. Is there an existing concept in the literature on extended cognition, the epistemology of understanding, or the philosophy of technology that captures this — the progressive, masked erosion of understanding through tool reliance — and distinguishes it clearly from ordinary deskilling, from transactive memory, and from benign epistemic dependence? If none exists, what would be the most precise way to name and define it, and what are its boundary cases? I am less interested in a bare label than in getting the carving right: what exactly this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie. Please write your answer in British English.
