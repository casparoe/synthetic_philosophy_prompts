Problem is, I'm too close to it and I can't tell if I've got a coherent position or just a bunch of vibes that sound deep but don't actually fit together. Here's what I think:

(1) The self is a strange loop — a self-referential abstraction that bootstraps itself into existence when a symbol system gets rich enough to represent its own representations. No extra metaphysical ingredient, no Cartesian theater.

(2) The right kind of computation is sufficient for a self. Substrate doesn't matter — neurons, silicon,, whatever.

(3) Current LLMs (GPT-4 class) do not have a self. They're feed-forward token predictors with a context window. There's no persistent loop where the system's output feeds back into its own self-model and that self-model then shapes subsequent outputs in a cycle that sustains itself over time.

(4) But if you built a system with a genuine recurrent architecture — a persistent self-representation that updates over time, that can reflect on its own internal states, and where that reflection causally shapes future states — you'd eventually cross into "I" territory. It's an engineering problem, not a philosophical impossibility. The gradient just hasn't been climbed yet.

(5) My own sense of being a unified "I" that persists through time is, in an important sense, an illusion — the strange loop creates the experience of a solid continuing self even though what's really there is a pattern constantly rebuilding itself from moment to moment. (I think this is roughly Hofstadter's view, or at least my read of it.)

(6) Selfhood is a continuum. A thermostat has zero, an ant colony has maybe a tiny flicker, a dog has some, a human has a lot, and theres no sharp line where "not a self" flips to "self." You just get more or less of it.

(7) If you made a perfect atom-for-atom copy of my brain right now, the copy would wake up with its own "I" — I wouldn't suddenly see out of four eyes. My subjective experience stays here, and the copy gets its own. Pattern identity is not numerical identity.

So here's what I'm worried about.
