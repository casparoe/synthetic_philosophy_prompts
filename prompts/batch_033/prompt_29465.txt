I was talking to a friend who just finished a philosophy of mind course, and he gave me this summary of the Churchlands' eliminative materialism. He was pretty confident about it, but a few things sounded off to me, and I don't trust my own judgment enough to say where. Here's what he told me, roughly as I remember it:

"The Churchlands argue that beliefs and desires don't really exist. Their whole point is that folk psychology — the everyday way we explain people's behavior by attributing beliefs and desires to them — is a theory, and a really bad one. It's been around for thousands of years and hasn't made any progress; it can't explain mental illness, sleep, or how memory works; and it's completely disconnected from neuroscience. So it's basically like astrology or alchemy. Mature neuroscience is going to sweep it away entirely and show that we don't have beliefs, desires, or any other mental states — there's just brain activity. The analogy they use is that just as science didn't reduce phlogiston to something real, it eliminated phlogiston altogether, beliefs will go the same way. People sometimes object that this view is self-refuting because you have to believe eliminative materialism to argue for it, but the Churchlands just shrug that off by saying the objection begs the question."

I recognize some of this from things I've read about the Churchlands, but other parts seem like they're probably caricatures. The thing about astrology and alchemy, for instance — would the Churchlands really say folk psychology is on a par with those? And I've heard that Paul Churchland in particular doesn't deny that we have inner experience, just that our ordinary concepts for it are the right ones. So I'm wondering: what in this summary would a careful eliminative materialist accept, what would they push back on as a misrepresentation, and what parts are just flat wrong? Go through it piece by piece so I can see what to trust and what to correct.
