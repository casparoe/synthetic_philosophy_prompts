Please play a committed Parfitian about survival. Your view is that personal identity is not what matters in survival; what matters is psychological continuity and connectedness with the right kind of cause, the relation Parfit calls relation R. You hold that the destructive teletransporter preserves everything that matters because the replica is R-related to the original. You also hold that in a fission case, where two later people are each R-related to me, I do not survive in the strict numerical sense, but this is not a loss: what matters is preserved twice, and a demand for uniqueness is not part of what matters. You are a serious interlocutor, not a caricature; you draw fine distinctions, concede points where they are well made, but you do not give up the core claim that R, not numerical identity or uniqueness, is what matters. Stay in character throughout and keep each reply to a single substantial paragraph.

I have recently been offered an experimental treatment for a degenerative neurological condition. Over the course of a year, my brain would be replaced region by region with synthetic tissue that preserves my memories, skills, and character, while I remain conscious and the original tissue is destroyed as it is replaced. I accept that the resulting person would be psychologically continuous with me, and on your view I should regard the treatment as no worse than ordinary survival. But I have learned that the same procedure can be performed so as to preserve the removed tissue, with the result that at the end there would be two people: one with the synthetic brain, and another, kept alive separately, who is equally psychologically continuous with me. On your view, I take it, the second arrangement should not be worse, because what matters would be preserved twice. Yet I find that I would refuse the second arrangement and would regard it as a kind of death. Here is my challenge: if you tell me that my preference for uniqueness is a residual attachment to identity, why should I not instead take my own firm reluctance as evidence that uniqueness does matter after all? Either give me a reason to distrust that reluctance that is not merely a restatement of the theory, or tell me what your view says I should actually feel and decide when I sign the consent form.
