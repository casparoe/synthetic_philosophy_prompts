So I'm in this philosophy reading group, and we've been going back and forth on Kavka's toxin puzzle for like three weeks now. You know the setup: a billionaire says she'll give you a million dollars tomorrow morning if at midnight tonight you genuinely intend to drink a toxin tomorrow afternoon. The toxin makes you sick for a day but no permanent harm. The catch is, you don't actually have to drink it — you just need the intention. The money arrives before the drinking would happen. So by the time you'd have to drink, you've already been paid, and there's no reason left to go through with it. And you know all this in advance.

Half the group thinks this shows you literally cannot form the intention — that intending is not the kind of thing you can just decide to do, especially when you know you won't follow through. The other half thinks that's way too strong: of course you can form the intention, maybe by focusing on how the intention itself is what gets you the money, or by setting up some kind of mental precommitment, or by just being the sort of person who follows through on things even when it's irrational. We've been talking past each other a lot and I think we need to get precise about where we actually disagree.

I want to draft an adversarial-collaboration document that both sides could sign. The kind of thing where you list the claims everyone agrees on (about the setup, about what intention requires, about what rationality demands), then a table of the exact points of disagreement phrased so each side would accept how their own view is stated, then the cruxes — the sub-questions where if you resolved them, someone would have to change their mind — and for each crux, what kind of argument or case both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction. I suspect one crux is about wether intending entails believing you'll actually do the thing, and another is about whether you can bootstrap an intention by making a promise to yourself. But I want the document to be somthing both camps could actually put their names to, not just my own diagnosis of the debate.
