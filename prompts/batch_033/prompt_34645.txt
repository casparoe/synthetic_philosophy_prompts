I am a senior distributed systems engineer. The vocabulary I work in daily includes message buses and publish/subscribe architectures, broadcast and multicast, contention and arbitration, backpressure, replication and leader election, observability and logging, and the distinction between a system's externally visible behavior and its internal state. I have no background in philosophy of mind.

In the philosophy of mind, a distinction is drawn between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, and a prominent theory, global workspace theory, accounts for consciousness in terms of a workspace in which information is broadcast globally to many specialized subsystems. Restate that distinction and that theory in my vocabulary as faithfully and systematically as you can. I do not want an analogy; I want a translation. For each central claim of the distinction and the theory, tell me which construct in my vocabulary carries it, whether the mapping is clean or requires distortion, and where it breaks down.

Then provide a residue report. Specifically: (1) Does the distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness survive the translation, or does one side collapse into the other? (2) Once translated, does global workspace theory turn out to be a theory of access, that is, of global availability for report and control, rather than a theory of phenomenal experience, and what in the translation decides that? (3) Is the untranslatable residue a sign that my vocabulary is missing a construct it needs, or a sign that the notion of phenomenal consciousness is less clear than it looks? Defend a verdict rather than ending in equipoise.
