I teach a second-year philosophy of mind module, and I'm putting together the final essay paper. I'd like one question on the original/derived intentionality distinction, and I want it to force students to actually engage with Dennett's two-bitser rather than rehearse the standard definitions. My worry, after marking last years scripts, is that students can all write that original intentionality is intrinsic to minds while derived intentionality is borrowed from interpretation, and they can summarise Searle's Chinese room, but they fall apart when asked what the two-bitser is actually suppose to show: that even a paradigm case of derived intentionality, a coin-detecting vending machine, has no determinate content until someone takes an interpretive stance toward it, and if that is true of all derived intentionality then the contrast with original intentionality starts to look as if it is doing no real work.

So please write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 25. The question should be answerable in 90 minutes by a prepared student, and it should not be answerable by listing definitions. The marking scheme should separate a first from an upper second by naming the specific moves each must contain: for instance, whether the student sees that Searle can concede the two-bitser's content is stance-relative while denying the same is true of brain states, and whether the student then asks what independent grounds Searle has for that asymmetry beyond simply insisting on it.
