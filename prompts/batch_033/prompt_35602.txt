I keep bumping into the same puzzle when I try to reason about uncertain beliefs, and I'm wondering whether it has a name and a literature.

The situation goes like this. I've got probabilities over a bunch of mutually exclusive possibilities — could be hypotheses, could be locations, doesn't matter. Then I learn something new. But the new thing isn't a plain proposition like "A is true." It's a conditional claim: "if B holds, then the chance of C is such-and-such." Or sometimes it's a correlation, or an inequality constraint. And I realize there's no single obvious way to fold this into my existing beliefs. If I try to generalize Bayesian updating in the most natural-seeming way, I get one answer; if I try a different natural-seeming way, I get a different answer. And I can't tell which one is "correct."

Here's a stripped-down example. I'm trying to guess which city my friend flew to. I start with equal credence on Paris, London, and Berlin — 1/3 each. Then I get a message from her saying: "if I'm in a European capital, the probability I'm in France is 80%." Well, all three are European capitals, so the antecedent covers the whole space. The message tells me the conditional probability of Paris given European-capital is 0.8. But it says nothing, directly, about how my London and Berlin credences should relate to each other afterwards. Should they stay equal? Should they shift proportional to something else? I can write down two different updating rules that both respect the new conditional and both seem reasonable a priori, and they give different ratios for London vs. Berlin. Which one is right, and why?

I've run into versions of this when trying to update on things like "X and Y are positively correlated" without being told the exact correlation, or "the probability of disease given symptom is at least 0.6," or even just learning a conditional probability where the antecedent isn't the whole space. Each time it feels like the same underlying problem: standard conditionalization works great when you learn a proposition, but the minute you learn something that constrains your probabilities without being a proposition about which possible world you're in, the machinery goes wobbly.

So my questions: does this general problem have a standard name? And more importantly, what are the main candidate solutions — the different updating rules people have proposed — and where exactly do they disagree? I'm not just after a label; I want to understand the shape of the debate and whether any approach has more or less won.
