I'm in a reading group working through the evolutionary debunking literature in metaethics, and one of the standard realist replies has been bothering me. The reply goes like this: our perceptual faculties were also shaped by natural selection, but nobody thinks that fact alone gives us reason to doubt that perception tracks the physical world. So the mere fact that our moral intuitions have an evolutionary origin shouldn't give us reason to doubt that they track moral truths. If you're not a skeptic about perception, you shouldn't be a skeptic about morality on evolutionary grounds either. The analogy is meant to shift the burden: the debunker needs to show what's special about the moral case, because the bare appeal to evolutionary origins would overgeneralize and take down perception too.

I can feel the force of the move, but I'm not sure how much work the analogy can actually do once you press on it. For instance, in the perceptual case we have an independent story about why getting things right was adaptive — predators and cliffs really exist and really matter for survival — whereas in the moral case, the debunker's whole point is that we can explain the content of our moral intuitions without invoking moral facts at all. So the analogy seems to assume the very thing at issue. But maybe I'm missing something, and there's a more careful version of the analogy that doesn't beg the question. I've also noticed that realists who deploy this analogy often concede that some moral intuitions (about incest avoidance, say, or in-group loyalty) are debunked by their evolutionary pedigree while insisting others survive — but nobody carves up perception that way, which makes me suspect the analogy is being applied selectively when it suits.

I'd like you to do a full audit of this analogy: what corresponds to what across the two domains, where the mapping is tight and where it comes apart, which disanalogies matter for the dialectical work the analogy is being asked to do, and whether the realist's conclusion — that the evolutionary debunking challenge is no more threatening than evolutionary skepticism about perception — survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If there's a better analogy the realist should be using instead, I'd like to hear that too. And please flag where you're reporting something that's broadly agreed upon in the literature versus where you're taking a side in a live dispute; I don't want a confident-sounding answer that papers over genuine disagreement.
