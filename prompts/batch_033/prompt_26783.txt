I'm writing an essay for my English class on Aldous Huxley's *Brave New World*, and I want to read the novel through the philosophical lens of wireheading and reward tampering. The novel's soma — a drug that delivers instant, hangover-free happiness — seems to be a perfect fictional case of wireheading: it short-circuits the brain's reward system so that people feel satisfied without achieving anything or forming real relationships. I'm especially interested in the contrast between John the Savage, who insists that unhappiness and struggle have value, and Mustapha Mond, who argues that people are happier without those things and that the trade-off is worth it. My question is: does the novel actually take a position on whether wireheading via soma is bad, or does it stack the deck by making the World State also totalitarian, anti-intellectual, and emotionally shallow in ways that are separable from the wireheading itself? If a society could offer universal wireheading without the eugenics, conditioning, and loss of high culture, would the novel's critique still hold? I'd like a reading that takes both the novel and the philosophical debate seriously — where the novel dramatizes something the philosophy misses, and where the novel might be guilty of bundling wireheading with other dystopian features to make it look worse than it is.
