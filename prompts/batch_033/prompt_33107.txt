Construct a thought experiment in which a suffering-focused negative utilitarian and a classical utilitarian agree on whether to fund a public health intervention, but for structurally different reasons — and where a modest change to the severity of the condition being treated causes their verdicts to diverge. Constraints: the scenario should be drawn from public health policy, one paragraph, with realistic magnitudes, and the divergence must not come from an obvious cheat like making the suffering trivial or the pleasure cost enormous; it should turn on the feature of negative utilitarianism that treats the reduction of suffering and the promotion of happiness as inhabiting different moral categories rather than as merely two directions on a shared scale. After the scenario, (a) show the reasoning for each theory in the base case where they agree, (b) vary the severity parameter and walk through why they now disagree, (c) identify which structural feature of the value functions is doing the work, and (d) say how a classical utilitarian would argue that the negative utilitarian's verdict in the divergent case reveals a deeper problem with the view. Begin with the thought experiment itself, with no preface or commentary, then proceed to the analysis.
