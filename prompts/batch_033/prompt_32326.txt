"Schrödinger was convinced that quantum mechanics proved the ancient Vedantic teaching that all consciousness is one. He showed that quantum entanglement means particles remain connected across any distance, and he argued that human minds work the same way — beneath the illusion of separate selves, there's a single cosmic consciousness linking all beings. The reason you feel like a distinct person is maya, a kind of perceptual trick, and Schrödinger believed that quantum physics was finally catching up to what the Upanishads had known for millennia: that Atman is Brahman, that your innermost self is identical with the ground of all reality."

I also vaguely remember that his interest in Vedanta came through Schopenhauer rather than from him trying to retroactively justify physics with mysticism. Can you go through this summary and tell me what's genuinely Schrödinger, what's the author projecting onto him, and what's just garbled?
