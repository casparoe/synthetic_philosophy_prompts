I serve on the steering committee of a nonpartisan organization that runs citizens' assemblies on contentious policy questions. We are designing the ground rules for an upcoming assembly on land-use reform, and one committee member has proposed that we formally exclude anyone who holds office in a political party or has run for partisan office in the past five years. Their argument draws on Simone Weil's claim that political parties are inherently hostile to truth-seeking: Weil argued that a party exists to advance its own power, that collective passion replaces individual conscience within a party structure, and that genuine deliberation about justice requires freedom from party discipline. The member contends that allowing party actors into a citizens' assembly would reproduce the very dynamics the assembly is meant to escape, and that even if we do not exclude them, we should adopt rules that prevent participants from caucusing or voting as blocs. Others on the committee find this illiberal and worry it would skew the assembly's demographic legitimacy. Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our next meeting: it should recommend a concrete policy on participant eligibility and internal rules, engage Weil's argument seriously rather than dismissing it, and address the strongest objection to the recommendation.
