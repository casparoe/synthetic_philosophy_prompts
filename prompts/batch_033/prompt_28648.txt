I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but my current project has two characters who are both philosophy postdocs and they get into it about evolutionary debunking in metaethics. I need to write the scene where they really go at it — one of them is basically a Street-style debunker, the other's a moral realist who thinks the whole debunking project is overblown. I want the dialogue to feel alive and actually move, not just be two people monologuing at each other.

The debunker's core move is the Darwinian dilemma: either our moral judgments track mind-independent moral truths via some crazy coincidence or spooky mechanism, or they don't track them at all, in which case we should doubt them. The realist needs to push back hard — maybe the "third factor" response, or the idea that if you debunk morality this way you also debunk math and perception, or something about how the debunker's own normative commitments survive thier own argument. I don't want either side to be a punching bag. Real back-and-forth, they interrupt each other, they concede points, they pivot.

And please don't let it resolve neatly. I want the reader to feel why someone would find the debunking argument genuinely unsettling and also why a realist wouldn't just fold. If you could work in something about what's at stake for each of them personally — like, the debunker used to be a realist and misses it, or the realist has a kid and feels the weight of actually needing morality to be real — that would be great, but only if it feels organic.
