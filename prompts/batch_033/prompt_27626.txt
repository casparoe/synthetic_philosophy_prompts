I run a safety team at a mid-sized AI lab. We're deploying a new generation of models and I need a reusable procedure for a question that comes up repeatedly: given a model and its deployment context, should we invest primarily in control measures (sandboxing, monitoring, capability restrictions, output filtering) or in alignment measures (training for corrigibility, honest reasoning, value learning, interpretability-based assurance)? Both approaches are imperfect, resources are finite, and the two have characteristically different failure modes. I want a decision procedure my team can run in roughly an hour. It should specify: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a clear recommendation, where it hands off to human judgment, and where it prescribes investing in both. Then apply it to two cases: (a) a coding assistant deployed in an IDE with file-system and network access, and (b) a model that generates and executes its own research plans on a cluster, including writing novel code and browsing the web. Begin with the procedure itself, with no preface or commentary.
