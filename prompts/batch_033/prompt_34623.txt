I attended a physics colloquium on the foundations of thermodynamics where the speaker, discussing Maxwell's demon, kept invoking the slogan "Information is physical" as though it by itself closed the case. The line of argument was: the demon cannot decrease the entropy of the gas for free because the information it acquires and uses is physical, and therefore subject to the same thermodynamic constraints as everything else. But I am not sure what work the slogan is actually doing — it seems like it could be gesturing at several very different claims, from the trivial to the quite radical. Lay out the distinct precise theses that "Information is physical" could be asserting, say which are platitudes, which are substantive but defensible, and which are false, and — most importantly — identify which of them actually do the work needed to close the loophole Maxwell's demon seemed to open, and which merely sound like they do. I want to know which reading, if any, genuinely blocks the demon without smuggling in the conclusion.
