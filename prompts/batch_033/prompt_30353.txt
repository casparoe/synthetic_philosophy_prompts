My friend and I went back and forth on this for an hour last night and I still can't tell if he has a point. I'm on the board of a small housing co-op and we're rewriting the bylaws. I proposed that our rule keeping rents permanently below market can only be changed by a two-thirds vote plus a six-month cooling-off period. He says that's just undemocratic: the current board tying the hands of future members who might know better, and no generation should get to lock in its values. I say the whole point of having bylaws at all is to stop ourselves from making predictable mistakes under preassure. The co-op has nearly voted to sell twice when money was tight, and both times people regretted it after. Who's right, and is there a principled way to tell which committments should be this hard to undo?
