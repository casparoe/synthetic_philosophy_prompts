I am a novelist working on a book set in the contemporary art world. Two of my characters—a painter and an art critic—have been close friends for many years, but their friendship has come under strain because of a philosophical disagreement they have never quite resolved. The painter holds that one cannot genuinely acquire aesthetic knowledge through the testimony of others: to know that a painting is beautiful, or powerful, or profound, one must experience it for oneself. The critic finds this view baffling and rather precious; she maintains that we routinely and unproblematically form justified aesthetic beliefs on the basis of reviews, descriptions, and the reports of trusted friends, and that to insist otherwise is to treat aesthetic judgement as something occult. I would like to write a scene in which they finally have this argument out in full. Please write the dialogue. Both characters should be intelligent and articulate; neither should be a straw man. The exchange should involve genuine back-and-forth, with each character responding to the other's actual points rather than talking past each other. The dialogue should be written in British English, and the register should be serious but not academic—these are people who live with art, not philosophers by trade.
