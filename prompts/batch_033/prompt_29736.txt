I'm preparing a short statement of my views on moral responsibility for a graduate seminar, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the position I have arrived at, stated as a set of commitments I currently hold all of.

First, I accept the Basic Argument in roughly Galen Strawson's version: you do what you do, at least in significant part, because of the way you are; to be truly morally responsible for what you do, you would have to be truly responsible for the way you are in the relevant mental respects; and being truly responsible for the way you are would require having caused yourself to be that way, which is impossible. So no one is truly or ultimately morally responsible. Second, I take this conclusion to be independent of determinism: adding indeterminism to the causal story could only insert randomness or luck, and luck cannot ground responsibility any more than determination can. Third, I think the Basic Argument refutes compatibilism, not merely libertarian incompatibilism — at least insofar as compatibilism claims to preserve a notion of free will rich enough to underwrite desert-entailing praise and blame. Fourth, I continue to hold that the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, guilt, and the rest — are rationally warranted, and that the project of excising them from our emotional lives would be both futile and ethically misguided. Fifth, I hold that legal and social punishment is justified only on forward-looking grounds such as deterrence, incapacitation, and reform, and that retributivism is false. Sixth, I am also committed to the view that blameworthiness tracks quality of will: whether someone is blameworthy for an action depends on whether it expressed malice, negligence, or ill will, not on how they came to have that quality of will.

I would like you to go through these commitments and tell me, for each pair that actually pulls against another, how severe the tension is and what the cheapest repair would be — which commitment has to give, how exactly it should be revised, and what the revision costs. I would also like you to identify any pair that merely seems to be in tension but is not, so that I can preempt the obvious objections. Please be definite: I am asking for your own verdict on whether the package can be made consistent, not a survey of possible moves.
