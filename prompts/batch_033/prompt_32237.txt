So I got into this huge argument last night with my friend who's doing a philosophy PhD, and he kept bringing up Bradley's regress to make some point about how facts can't be the kind of structured things most metaphysicians think they are. I pushed back and he eventualy gave me this summary of what Bradley's regress actually is. Here's roughly what he said:

"Bradley noticed that when you try to explain what makes a proposition true, you end up with a bunch of constituents — an object, a property, and a relation like instantiation that's supposed to glue them together. But instantiation is just another constituent, so you need a further relation to glue instantiation to the object and property, and then another relation to glue that relation, and so on forever. Bradley took this to show that all relations are illusions and that reality is ultimately one undifferentiated whole — the Absolute. The standard response nowadays is that instantiation is a 'non-relational tie' that doesn't need further glue, but that's just naming the problem, not solving it."

I'm not sure if he was being fair to Bradley or to the contemporary responses. Which parts of this are accurate, which are oversimplifications, and is his dismissal of the non-relational tie response as strong as he made it sound? I want to go back to him with something more informed.
