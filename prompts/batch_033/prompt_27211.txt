I'm in a metaethics reading group where the robust moral realists and the quasi-realist expressivists have been talking past each other for a year, and we've finally made them do the thing they should have done from the start: write an adversarial-collaboration document. Could you draft the joint statement a committed robust moral realist and a committed quasi-realist expressivist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration? It should open with a numbered "Agreed claims" section — not the easy first-order stuff both sides already accept (nobody needs another sentence about how wrong torturing for fun is), but the substantive concessions: what the realist grants about expressivism's ability to earn the right to truth-talk and belief-talk, what the expressivist grants about the realist's explanatory ambitions, and what both accept about what moral disagreement and moral motivation do and dont show. Then include a disagreements table; for each row give the exact formulation of the dispute, phrased so each side would accept the wording of it's own column. Then list the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side — and for each crux give a concrete argument or case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I'm less interested in which side you think wins and more in whether you can state the residual disagreement precisely without turning either side into a strawman.

After the table, add a short note on where the drafting itself is most likely to fail: which claim or formulation one side would probably refuse to sign, and what argument or consideration would make you reword it. End with a single-sentence summary of the deepest remaining crux, on its own line beginning "Crux summary:".
