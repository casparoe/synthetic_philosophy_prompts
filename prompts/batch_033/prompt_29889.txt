My friend has quite specific views about when you can permissibly form an aesthetic belief on the basis of someone else's say-so. Here's where she's landed on a bunch of cases:

- A novelist you admire tells you a debut novel is "stunningly good." You haven't read it and it's not in your local library, but you could order it. She says: yes, you can justifiably believe it's stunningly good.
- A stranger on Goodreads calls a film "visually gorgeous." She says: no.
- A musicologist tells you a rare recording in an archive is "extraordinary." The archive is in another country and you'll probably never visit. She says: yes.
- Your brother, who reads a lot but isn't any kind of critic, tells you a book is "the best he's read in years." She says: yes.
- A sommelier tells you the wine already poured in your glass has "superb structure." You haven't tasted it yet. She says: no, taste it yourself.
- An art critic describes a mural in a private home as "sublime." You'll never be invited to see it. She says: yes.

Now she's asked about a new case: a colleague at work, who sees tons of theatre and whose taste she generally trusts, tells her a play currently running is "unmissable." Tickets are available and affordable, but she hadn't planned to go. Can she justifiably believe the play is unmissable without seeing it?

I'm less interested in what you personally think than in what rule she seems to be implicitly running, what other rules fit her verdicts just as well, which of those rules would say yes to the theatre case and which would say no, and which further case would best tell the competing rules apart. Also, could you write the whole thing in British English? Cheers.
