I'm giving a colloquium paper next month at a department whose ethics group is dominated by consequentialists — several act-consequentialists, one rule-consequentialist, two Kantians, and one virtue ethicist. The consequentialists by and large believe the demandingness objection was settled decades ago and will arrive expecting a revival of a dead debate; the Kantians are sympathetic to my conclusion but suspicious of my framing; the virtue ethicist thinks any criterion-based moral theory is the real problem. My thesis is that the demandingness objection has been systematically mislocated. It is not, I argue, an objection to how much act-consequentialism asks of us — the standard replies that distinguish the criterion of rightness from a decision procedure, or that appeal to blame, satisficing, or rule-consequentialism, can all handle that reading. The real objection is to the standing of the agent's own projects and attachments within the theory: act-consequentialism can treat an agent's giving non-instrumental weight to her own commitments only as a bias to be eliminated. My argument has three steps: (1) each standard reply concedes this point, because each leaves the criterion of rightness with no room for an agent to treat her own projects as a source of non-instrumental reasons; (2) the only way to create such room is to add an agent-relative prerogative to the criterion itself; (3) adding that prerogative is either unmotivated from within consequentialism or turns the view into something no longer recognizably consequentialist. So the objection refutes act-consequentialism, or at least forces a choice between two unappealing options. Please write me a Q&A prep sheet: the six to eight objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by how much damage they would do and flagged by how likely each is, each phrased the way the relevant member of the audience would put it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks, and say which step of my argument it targets. Separate what is settled in the literature from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
