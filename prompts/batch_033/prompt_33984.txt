Here is my opening challenge. In *Discipline and Punish* you argue that disciplinary techniques produce docile bodies and that power operates directly on the body, shaping it, training it, marking it. But as a biologist, I cannot help noticing that the body you describe appears to have almost no biological constraints—it presents itself as a surface upon which power writes at will. Real bodies have metabolic limits, circadian rhythms, genetic variation, and evolutionary histories that resist certain kinds of regimentation. The factory time-discipline you analyze, for instance, runs up against the fact that human attention and physical performance degrade predictably after a certain number of hours regardless of the disciplinary regime. My question is this: does your account of disciplinary power have the resources to acknowledge these biological limits as genuine constraints that are not themselves products of power, or must it treat them as just another discourse that power produces? And if the latter, how do you account for the fact that these constraints operated before there were discourses about them?
