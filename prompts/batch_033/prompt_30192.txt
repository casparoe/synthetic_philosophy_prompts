ok so im in this philosophy club and we've been doing population ethics and i volunteered to put my actual views in a shared doc, and i want to check they hang together before everyone picks at them. here's the list:

(1) total view: one outcome is better than another iff it has more total welbeing, so adding a happy person to an otherwise unchanged world makes the world better.
(2) the repugnant conclusion is true and honestly not that repugnant: a huge enough population of lives barely worth living is better than a small population of very good lives.
(3) procreation asymmetry: there's no reason to create a happy person, but there is a strong reason not to create a miserable person.
(4) person-affecting constraint: an act can't be wrong unless it makes some particular person worse off than they would otherwise have been.
(5) in the nonidentity case (conceiving now gives this child a life barely worth living, waiting would give a different child a much better life), the child isn't made worse off, but i still think conceiving now is wrong.
(6) if one outcome is better than another, then other things equal there's at least some reason to bring it about.

which of these are actually in tension with each other and which just look like it? rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be.
