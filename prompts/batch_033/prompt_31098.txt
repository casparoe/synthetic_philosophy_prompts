I've been collecting my honest reactions to a bunch of animal cases to see where I stand on speciesism, and they don't add up the way I expected. I'd like you to help me find the principle, if there is one.

Here are the verdicts:

- Killing a healthy adult dog for no particular reason: wrong.
- Trapping and killing mice that are eating the grain in my pantry: fine, I do it without much thought and I don't think it's wrong.
- Raising pigs on a factory farm and killing them for pork when I could easily eat plants instead: wrong, but I'm genuinely unsure whether the killing is the problem or just the suffering. I'd probably eat pork from a pig that lived well and died instantly and painlessly.
- Doing painful medical experiments on macaques to develop a drug that might save hundreds of children: permissable, though it makes me queasy and I wouldn't want to watch.
- Doing the same experiments on severely cognitively disabled human children who score below the macaques on every test: not permissable, full stop.
- Killing and eating a genetically engineered pig with the mental and emotional life of a typical four-year-old human: wrong, and I'd say as wrong as killing the four-year-old.
- Withdrawing a feeding tube from a human in a permanent vegetative state: fine.
- Killing that same PVS patient to harvest organs for five other people: also fine if the family consents, but it bothers me in a way the withdrawal doesn't.
- A human infant born anencephalic, who will never be conscious: letting it die is fine, and I don't think actively ending its life is any worse, but I'd still balk at saying it's the same as killing a pig.

I keep wanting to say the principle is something like 'what matters is the capacity for welfare, not species membership' — the engineered pig and the disabled children push in opposite directions on that. But then the macaque experiment and the disabled children seem to smuggle species back in, and I can't tell whether my pig verdict is about killing or only about suffering.

So: is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? State it (or a small set of candidates) precisely, say which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and what I'd have to give up to keep the rest, and tell me what it predicts about a case I haven't listed: a human with a severe brain injury who permanently functions at the level of a healthy dog.
