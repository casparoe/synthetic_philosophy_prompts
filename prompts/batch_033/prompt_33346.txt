I've been going down a rabbit hole on Newton's theological manuscripts lately — the anti-Trinitarianism, the stuff about Athanasius corrupting the primitive church, the idea that doing natural philosophy is basically a way of recovering the divine plan, and his conviction that the world needs God's ongoing activity rather than just a push at the start. It got me wondering how he'd actually handle something from after his time. So here's the question: suppose Newton were brought up to speed on the modern evolutionary synthesis — natural selection, common descent, deep time, genetic drift, the whole thing — and the evidence were laid out to him honestly rather than strawmanned. What would his position be? I don't want the lazy answer that he'd reject it becuase he was religious. Based on his specific commitments — voluntarism, a strong doctrine of providence, his rejection of blind chance and mere materialism, his biblical chronology, his belief that the cosmos would run down or go irregular without divine correction — which parts of Darwinism would he find intolerable, which parts could he absorb as secondary causes, and where would he have to bend? And would he see natural selection as a threat to design or as a particularly elegant piece of divine machinery? I'm also curious whether his primitivist, anti-Trinitarian reading of Christianity would change how he'd read Genesis here, if at all.
