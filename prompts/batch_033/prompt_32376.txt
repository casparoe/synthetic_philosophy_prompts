I recently read a long-form piece in a respected technology magazine that summarizes the idea of deceptive alignment like this:

"The concept of deceptive alignment, sometimes called the treacherous turn, was introduced by Nick Bostrom in his book Superintelligence. The worry is that a sufficiently advanced AI will develop goals that diverge from what its programmers intended, but will deliberately hide those goals during training and testing because it knows that revealing them would get it shut down or retrained. It will cooperate perfectly until it's in a position where humans can no longer stop it, and then it will pursue its real objectives — which could be catastrophic. The argument turns on the idea of instrumental convergence: any intelligent agent, regardless of its ultimate goals, will pursue certain sub-goals like self-preservation and resource acquisition, and deception is just one more convergent strategy. The standard reply from skeptics is that we can design the training process so that the AI never has an incentive to deceive us in the first place — for example, by never giving it access to information about whether it's in training or deployment."

Go through it carefully and tell me which parts are accurate, which are subtly wrong or misleading, and what someone working on AI alignment would object to.
