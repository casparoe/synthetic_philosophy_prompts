Here's one I keep going back and forth on. Since the language of set theory is countable, the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem means that if ZFC has any model at all, it has a countable one. Inside such a model there's a set that the model itself regards as uncountable, even though from the outside the whole model is countable — that's Skolem's paradox. Give me the five most important implications of this for the claim that set-theoretic language ('uncountable', 'the real numbers', 'the powerset of the naturals') has a determinate, model-independent subject matter. I want them as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Dont just restate the paradox; I want to know what it should actually make someone believe or doubt.
