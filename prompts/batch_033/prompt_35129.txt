I've been working as a psychiatric nurse for about fifteen years now, and there's this pattern I keep seeing that I can't quite put my finger on, and I'm wondering if anyone in philosophy or sociology of medicine has named it and really worked out its boundaries.

Here's what I mean. A diagnosis gets into the DSM, or sometimes it's already there but gets reconceptualized in a new edition. At first it describes a relatively narrow set of people. Then something happens — maybe a celebrity discloses they have it, maybe there's an advocacy group, maybe it just catches on in online spaces. People start identifying with the label. They join communities organized around it. They learn the vocabulary that clinicians use to describe it, and they start describing their own experiences in those terms. So far so normal. But here's the part that bugs me: after a while, the way people *perform* the condition seems to change. The diagnosis starts to attract a different kind of person than it originally described, or the people who have it start shaping their self-understanding and behavior around what the diagnosis says they are, which then feeds back into what clinicians see in their offices, which then feeds back into how the diagnosis gets revised in the next DSM. It's like the map changes the territory, and then the territory changes the map, in this spiral that nobody seems to be tracking.

The clearest example I can think of is what happened with DID (dissociative identity disorder) — the presentation of the disorder seemed to shift dramatically once there were online communities and popular memoirs, and the number of alters people reported went way up, and the whole clinical picture got more elaborate. But I've seen smaller versions of it with other diagnoses too, even things like ADHD or autism spectrum disorder, where the way people talk about their own condition and the identity they build around it seems to loop back and reshape what the diagnosis *means* in practice.

I'm not talking about the simple idea that awareness reduces stigma and increases diagnosis rates — that's just detection. I'm talking about a feedback loop where the classification itself changes the thing being classified, which then changes the classification. Is there an established name for this specific dynamic? If there is, what distinguishes it from just "social construction" or "medicalization," which seem much broader? And if there isn't a name, what should it be called, and where exactly are the edges of the phenomenon — when does ordinary diagnostic expansion cross over into this kind of looping?
