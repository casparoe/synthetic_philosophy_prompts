Under the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, Article II states that "Outer space, including the moon and other celestial bodies, is not subject to national appropriation by claim of sovereignty, by means of use or occupation, or by any other means." Yet the Artemis Accords, signed by a growing number of nations since 2020, affirm that the extraction and utilisation of space resources can be conducted in a manner consistent with the Outer Space Treaty, and that such extraction does not constitute national appropriation. On its face, this looks like a tension: how can a private entity extract and sell lunar water ice without some underlying claim of exclusionary control that walks and talks like a property right? I want you to reconstruct the implicit theory of property and justice that the Artemis Accords presuppose—or must presuppose—to render this practice coherent. What philosophical view of original acquisition, ownership, and the commons is doing the work here? Is it Lockean, Grotian, or something else? Then tell me whether the practice is philosophically underdetermined—that is, whether several rival theories of property could license it—and what a policy built on a rival theory (say, a common heritage regime with mandatory benefit-sharing) would have to change in the Accords. Keep the answer in British English.
