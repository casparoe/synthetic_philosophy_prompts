I've been playing one of those games in which the players can vote to change the rules, and a change is adopted by following whatever procedure the current rules lay down. I keep noticing a configuration I can't name, and I'd like a precise account of it rather than just a label. Example: the rules say a rule change passes only if it receives a two-thirds majority. A player proposes, under that very rule, to lower the threshold to a simple majority, and the proposal passes with exactly two-thirds. Immediately afterward someone objects that the vote's authorization has been pulled out from under it: the two-thirds requirement was in force when the vote was held, but the adopted change abolishes that requirement, so in retrospect the act seems to destroy its own legal basis. The group splits three ways — the change stands, the change is void from the start, or both rules remain in force. Does this precise configuration, a rule change that alters or repeals the very rule under which it was made, have an established name? If so, how is it distinguished from an ordinary amendment of a rule of play, from a rule that invalidates past acts retroactively, and from a plain conflict between two rules that are valid at the same time? If no established term fits it exactly, I'd like a coined term with a definition and boundary cases. Please answer in American English.
