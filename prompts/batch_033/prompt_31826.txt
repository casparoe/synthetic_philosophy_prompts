So my roommate has been on this philosophy of action kick and he's got me as a captive audience because I'm trying to quit vaping and failing at it pretty spectacularly. He's been categorizing all my failures and everyone else's according to whether they count as genuine akrasia (he insists on the Greek) or something else. I've been keeping a mental list of his verdicts:

- Someone who knows they should be studying, thinks "I should really stop scrolling" while scrolling, and keeps scrolling — akrasia.
- A heroin addict who uses even though she knows it's destroying her life — he says no, that's compulsion, not akrasia. The addiction undermines the capacity to do otherwise.
- A guy who makes a New Year's resolution to go to the gym, goes for two weeks, and then just stops caring — not akrasia. He says that's just a resolution fading, not acting against your own judgment.
- Someone who, after months of deliberation, decides to leave their spouse but then stays, and keeps staying, even though their reasons haven't changed and they think about it every morning — akrasia.
- Someone who genuinely forgets they're on a diet when the dessert menu arrives — not akrasia, obviously.
- And he's weirdly insistent that Medea is the paradigm case, the whole I-know-the-better-and-approve-it-but-follow-the-worse thing.

Here's the thing. I tried this commitment device where I put money in an app that would fine me if I bought a vape, and I knew when I set it up that I'd want to weasel out of it — that was the whole point. Sure enough, a week later I found myself in the app settings, thinking about how the fine was kind of unfair actually, and I disabled the whole thing. I was fully aware of what I was doing. I even thought about his stupid akrasia definition while I was doing it.

So what's he going to say about my case? I want to know before I bring it up, cause if he calls it akrasia I'm going to feel like I have some kind of character flaw, and if he doesn't, I want to know why not.

More than the yes/no, I'm trying to figure out what rule he's actually running. I can think of at least two principles that fit all his verdicts so far, and I'm pretty sure they'd say different things about my vape-app situation. What are the candidate rules, which one has him calling me akratic, and what's a case I could ask him about that would force him to commit to one rule over the other?
