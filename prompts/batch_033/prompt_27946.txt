I've been thinking about Moore's paradox — you know, "It's raining, but I don't believe it" — and I feel like people undersell what it actually shows. The weirdness of that sentence doesn't depend on the weather at all; it's weird regardless. So isn't the real upshot that we have incorrigible access to our own beliefs? Like, if I can't even coherently entertain the thought that I might be wrong about whether I believe it's raining, doesn't that mean introspection is infallible? Why do epistemologists keep treating Moore's paradox as just a quirk of assertion instead of a proof that we can't be mistaken about our own mental states?
