I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so bear with me if I mangle the jargon. I'm writing a novel where one of the central characters is a philosopher who's spent her career on the nature of dreams — she's basically a Malcolmian: she thinks the whole idea that dreams are "experiences" we have during sleep is a conceptual mistake, that dream reports aren't reports of anything that actually happened in the night, they're just the kind of thing we're inclined to say when we wake up. She's sharp and she's been nursing this view for thirty years.

There's a scene I'm trying to write where she argues with a younger colleague who's more in the Dennett camp — he thinks the question is empirical, that the received view (dreams are experiences during sleep) is just one hypothesis and the cassette theory is a genuine rival, and it's not a conceptual confusion, it's something science could settle. They're both smart, neither is a strawman, and I want them to actually listen to each other.

Write me a dialogue between them. I want real back-and-forth where they respond to each other's actual points — not just two monologues with a line break. What I really want to see is the deeper fissure between them: is this a question science can even touch, or is it a conceptual muddle dressed up as an empirical one? What would make the Malcolmian genuinely uncomfortable? What would make the Dennettian pause and say "huh, that's a problem"? And can you show me where they might actually agree on something, even if it's just that the other guy's worst objection is the right one to worry about?

Don't resolve it neatly. The best arguments I've read in fiction are the ones where both sides land blows and the reader isn't sure who's winning.
