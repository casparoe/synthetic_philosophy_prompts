I'm a journalist working on a piece about whether it makes sense to say that LLMs "really" understand language or "really" have beliefs. I've been reading Dennett's stuff on real patterns and the three stances — physical, design, intentional. The way I understand it, the intentional stance is a predictive strategy: you treat the system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires, and if that reliably predicts its behavior, the patterns you're picking out are real patterns, even if there aren't literal belief-boxes inside the system. So what are the most important implications of this framework, if you take it seriously? I'm especially interested in whether it dissolves the question of whether LLMs "really" understand, or just reframes it in a way that the AI hype and skeptic camps are both missing. And seperately, what does the framework imply about how we should treat the outputs of systems like LLMs when they're making decisions that affect people — do we need to take the intentional stance toward them, or is that a category mistake?
