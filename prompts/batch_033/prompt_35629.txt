I've been arguing with a friend about what you should do when you're not sure which moral theory is right — not uncertainty about facts, but uncertainty between utilitarianism, deontology, and so on. The formal stuff I've seen, mostly secondhand, says to treat it like expected utility: assign each theory a credence and pick the act with the highest expected choiceworthiness. My friend thinks that basically settles it, but I keep getting stuck on the comparison step. A utilitarian might say an act produces 10 units of welfare, while a deontologist might just say the act is forbidden — theres no label on the deontologist's verdict saying how many negative utils a violation is worth. Any way you normalize the two theories so they can be weighed against each other seems like its already a substantive moral judgment dressed up as a technical detail. Could you give me a small, made-up toy example where this intertheoretic comparison problem really bites, in the sense that two different but reasonable ways of scaling the theories would flip the recommendation? I'm not after citations or real cases, just a concrete scenario that makes the conceptual issue vivid.
