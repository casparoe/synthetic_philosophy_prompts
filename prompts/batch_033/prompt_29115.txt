I am a novelist at work on a book about how philosophical thought experiments function as miniature narratives — and how their narrative architecture can illuminate or distort the arguments they are meant to serve. My thesis is that the most influential thought experiments in the analytic tradition derive much of their persuasive force from the same techniques novelists use: concrete sensory detail, the reader's identification with a protagonist, the temporal unfolding of a scenario, and the strategic management of what the reader is shown and what is withheld. A corollary is that some thought experiments mislead precisely because their narrative frame quietly does argumentative work that would be rejected if it were made explicit.

For a chapter that compares thought experiments along this dimension, I need a ranking of the following eight by how much of their persuasive power is attributable to narrative craft rather than to the underlying argument they are meant to illustrate. The operative question: if you stripped away the story and stated only the abstract argumentative structure, which would lose the most force?

The eight:

1. Judith Jarvis Thomson's violinist (the kidnapped dialysis patient)
2. Philippa Foot's trolley problem (the original switch case)
3. Hilary Putnam's Twin Earth
4. John Searle's Chinese Room
5. Frank Jackson's Mary the color scientist
6. Robert Nozick's experience machine
7. Derek Parfit's teletransporter (the Mars teleportation case)
8. Judith Jarvis Thomson's people-seeds

Please sort them into four tiers:

- Tier 1: The narrative does nearly all the work; the argument collapses without it.
- Tier 2: The narrative does heavy lifting, but a recognizable argument survives without it.
- Tier 3: The narrative is mostly illustration; the argument stands largely on its own.
- Tier 4: The narrative is mere decoration; the argument would be just as forceful stated abstractly.

For each thought experiment, give a one- or two-sentence justification for its tier placement that identifies the specific narrative technique — for instance, protagonist identification, temporal staging, sensory concreteness, information management, or defamiliarization — doing the persuasive work. In the lower tiers, explain why the narrative is not load-bearing.

Before you produce your final ranking, please adopt the perspective of a narratologist who also has a working knowledge of analytic philosophy. Think through what each thought experiment's argument would look like if reduced to its abstract logical skeleton, without any of the scenario's narrative particulars. Only then produce your tier assignments.

Your response should lead with the tier assignments in the very first sentence, formatted as: "Tier 1: [items]; Tier 2: [items]; Tier 3: [items]; Tier 4: [items]." Then provide your justifications and reasoning. No preamble, no restating of the question.
