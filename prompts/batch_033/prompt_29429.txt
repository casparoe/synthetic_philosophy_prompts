I'm trying to work out what I actually believe about moral responsibility, and I'd like you to help me find the principle behind my verdicts on cases. Here they are. A man with an ordinary, secure upbringing drifts over two decades through a long series of small self-serving choices, each apparently his own, until he has become a habitual embezzler; I hold him fully responsible and think blame is apt. A woman severely abused and neglected in childhood commits a nearly identical embezzlement; I hold her responsible in only a thin sense and find her far less blameworthy, because her background seems to excuse. A person whose sudden aggressiveness is traced to a recently developed brain tumor: not responsible at all. A person raised from infancy in an insular cult that teaches outsiders may be defrauded, who then defrauds outsiders at twenty-five: less responsible than the embezzler, more responsible than the brain-tumor case. A twenty-year-old who deliberately sets out to make himself cruel, reading violent material, practicing suppressing empathy, and seeking out situations that will harden him, and who commits a brutal assault at thirty: I blame him more than the drifter, because he cultivated the very character that produced the act. The pair I cannot reconcile is the drifter and the self-cultivator. In both, the act flows from character, and in both, the character was shaped by choices, but in the first the shaping feels like unreflective drift and in the second it is deliberate self-making; I cannot say why that difference should matter, yet it feels as though it does. The theoretical threat is Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: to be truly responsible for what you do, you would have to be responsible for the way you are, which would require being the cause of yourself, which is impossible, so nobody is truly responsible. I find the argument hard to answer, but I do not want to give up all my verdicts. What principle, or small set of candidate principles, best captures my verdicts? State it precisely, identify which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and say what it implies about a case I have not listed: an adult with no trauma, no indoctrination, and no prior pattern of wrongdoing, who commits a serious wrong on a sudden, entirely out-of-character impulse.
