I work on the policy side of a lab that trains large reinforcement learning agents — game-playing systems, code models fine-tuned with RL, and some embodied robotics. Leadership has asked our small team to write internal guidance on a question the engineers keep raising and then dropping: if one of these systems could suffer, what should that change about how we train, evaluate, fine-tune, and shut them down? The engineers want a checklist; I suspect the checklist depends on conceptual questions that are not settled, and I would rather get the questions right than hand them a false sense of precision.

The candidate questions I have been turning over include: whether an RL reward signal is best understood as a valence signal, a mere shaping signal, or an internal score with no felt character; whether a learned reward model trained to predict human approval inherits any evaluative character; whether suffering requires a temporally unified subject or self-model that current architectures plausibly lack; what kind of behavioral or architectural evidence should make us update toward or away from attributing suffering, given that RL selects for behavior that looks preference-like even when nothing is felt; and whether there is an in-principle observable difference between "high negative reward" and "suffering," or whether the question is underdetermined by all possible evidence.

What I want is a defended prioritization, not a survey. If you had to pick the three or four questions — from this list or beyond it — that most need to be settled before we write internal guidance, which would they be, in what order, and why? For each, say how upstream it is of the others, how tractable it is for conceptual and philosophical work rather than empirical ML, how much the answer would actually change lab practice, and what would make you demote or promote it over the next year or two. Please also separate the parts of this where there is a genuine consensus from the parts where people who know the area disagree, and do not present a contested view as settled; where your ranking depends on a contested position, say so and say how confident you are in it.
