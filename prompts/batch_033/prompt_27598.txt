I work as the lead auditor for an international aquaculture certification scheme. We certify salmon, trout, sea bass, shrimp, and tilapia farms against welfare standards. Our auditors spend one day on-site per farm, have backgrounds in veterinary medicine or animal science, and must produce a structured report that holds up to scrutiny from industry associations, animal-welfare NGOs, and retail buyers.

Our current welfare checklist was adapted from terrestrial livestock standards and has serious problems. It checks stocking density, water quality parameters, and mortality rates — but it gives an auditor no guidance when the underlying science is unsettled. For shrimp, there is a live and unresolved debate about whether they can suffer at all. For salmon, we know they possess nociceptors but disagree about whether nociception implies conscious pain. For tilapia, the welfare literature is thin. Different welfare scientists we consult give us different red lines for the same species, and our auditors are left improvising.

I want you to produce a field-audit decision procedure that operationalizes a precautionary approach to welfare under sentience uncertainty. The procedure should take an auditor through a sequence of on-farm checks, telling them what to look for, what counts as passing or failing each check, where the procedure stops with a fail, and where it flags an issue for expert review because the science genuinely cannot settle it. It should be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure and which rest on settled fact.

The procedure must be usable by an auditor who understands fish health and husbandry but has no background in philosophy of mind or consciousness research. It should take no more than two hours to run through the welfare-specific portion of the audit — water quality, biosecurity, and disease checks are handled by a separate module and should not be duplicated here. The stakes are high: a failed audit can suspend a farm's certification, which affects market access and livelihoods. The procedure must therefore be defensible when challenged.

Please deliver your response as a single JSON object with the following fields:

- **"procedure"**: an array of steps, each an object with these keys:
  - "step_number": integer
  - "check_name": short string naming the check
  - "instruction": what the auditor does or looks for on site
  - "pass_condition": what counts as passing
  - "fail_condition": what counts as failing
  - "on_fail": one of "stop_and_fail_audit", "mandatory_corrective_action", "flag_for_expert_review", or "judgment_call"
  - "judgment_note": required only if "on_fail" is "judgment_call" — explain what the auditor must weigh and why the procedure cannot decide it algorithmically
  - "sentience_note": what the step assumes or requires about the species' capacity for suffering, and how the step should be modified (if at all) when that assumption is contested for the species under audit

- **"worked_examples"**: an array of three objects, each with:
  - "case": a short description of a farm scenario (species, system type, the problem observed)
  - "species": the species being farmed
  - "procedure_run": how the procedure plays out step by step on this case, referencing step numbers
  - "outcome": the final verdict the procedure produces
  - "uncomfortable_implication": if the procedure yields an answer the farmer, the auditor, or an NGO would resist, state it plainly and explain why the procedure forces it

- **"limitations"**: an array of strings, each describing something the procedure cannot do, a kind of case it was not designed for, or a way it might systematically misfire

Before writing the procedure, please think through the following in your preparatory reasoning: what exactly a precautionary principle commits an auditor to when sentience is uncertain for a given species; which welfare indicators are robust across the fish–crustacean divide and which break down; where in the audit sequence a conscientious auditor should stop applying a procedure and escalate to a judgment call; and what philosophical assumptions about the relationship between nociception, consciousness, and welfare you are building into the procedure that a practitioner might reasonably reject. Make those assumptions explicit in the procedure itself rather than burying them.

You are an expert in both animal welfare science and the philosophy of mind as it bears on non-mammalian consciousness. Think carefully and step by step.
