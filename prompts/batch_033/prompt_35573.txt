I'm taking this political philosophy course and we've spent like three weeks on Mill's harm principle and I feel like we're going in circles. The class has split into two camps and nobody's budged. One camp says the harm principle is actually pretty straightforward — you can't restrict speech unless it causes direct, tangible harm to someone else, like inciting a riot or defrauding someone. The other camp says that's naive because "harm" is the whole battlefield: once you acknowledge that speech can cause psychological trauma, reinforce systemic oppression, or gradually erode the conditions for democratic deliberation, then basically anything can count as harm and the principle doesn't constrain anything. What's frustrating is that we all agree on the empirical stuff. Nobody denies that hate speech causes real psychological damage, and nobody denies that some speech contributes to structural inequality over time. The fight is over whether those things are "harm" in the sense Mill cared about, or whether calling them harm stretches the concept until it snaps.

My TA keeps saying we need to "clarify our terms," which makes me wonder if this is just a verbal dispute dressed up as a philosophical one. But I'm not sure thats right either, because when I push people on it, they seem to have different intuitions about what the state ought to be in the business of preventing — like, is the state supposed to protect people from physical violence only, or is it supposed to guarantee the conditions for equal citizenship? So maybe the real disagreement is normative and the word "harm" is just where it surfaces. Or maybe, and this is my pet theory, the two sides want different things from a principle: one side wants a crisp rule that judges can apply without smuggling in their own politics, and the other side wants a framework for weighing competing interests case by case, and they're basically answering different questions under the same heading. I'd love an actual diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is — verbal, normative, methodological, or something else — and whether there's a way forward or if we should just expect it to stay stuck forever.
