Ok so in my epistemology reading group we've been doing the norm of assertion literature for a couple months, and it feels like it's just... stuck. The same cases get trotted out: you can truthfully say that it is raining, but you can't comfortably say that it is raining and you don't believe it; you shouldn't flat-out assert that this lottery ticket is a loser just because it is overwhelmingly likely; if you assert something and someone asks how you know, it is bizarre to answer that you don't. The knowledge-norm people say these all point to knowledge. The truth-norm people say the data is contaminated because a lot of the cases are really about misleadingness or conversational implicature. The belief-norm and reasonable-to-believe-norm people carve the cases up differently again, and everyone seems to accept the same cases but not what they show. My suspicion is that this isn't one debate but a few stuck together: one about what the word assert picks out, one about what we're doing when we hold a speaker to a norm, and one about whether the evidence base is supposed to be ordinary intuitions or theoretical costs. Is that right? What kind of disagreement is this actually, and what would count as progress given your diagnosis—a better taxonomy, experimental data on ordinary judgments, a formal model of conversational score, or just admitting the question is badly posed? I want a diagnosis that engages the actual positions, not a survey of them.
