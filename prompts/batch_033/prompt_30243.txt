I am a PhD student in reinforcement learning, with only a passing acquaintance with the philosophical literature. I work on multi-agent settings, credit assignment, and inverse reinforcement learning, and I would like P.F. Strawson's account of the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation, guilt, and the rest — translated into that vocabulary. Please translate three things as faithfully as you can into agents and policies, reward functions, credit assignment, modeling another system as an intentional agent versus as a generative process, and shaping a policy through reward versus predicting a fixed system: (1) the distinction between the participant stance and the objective stance; (2) the excusing/exempting distinction, where an action is excused while the agent remains an apt target of the reactive attitudes, versus an agent being exempted from those attitudes altogether; and (3) the compatibilist claim that coming to believe determinism neither does nor should lead us to abandon the participant stance. Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly, what you had to distort, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary. I am especially interested in two features that strike me as hard to translate — the idea that reactive attitudes involve holding someone to a demand or expectation of good will, and the idea that the participant stance is not a modeling choice one can simply set down — and in your verdict on whether what fails to translate is a gap in the RL vocabulary or a sign that those parts of the view are doing less argumentative work than they appear.
