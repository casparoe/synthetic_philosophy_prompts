In 1969, the astrophysicist J. Richard Gott visited the Berlin Wall, which had then stood for eight years, and reasoned that, since there was nothing special about the moment of his visit, he was equally likely to be in any of the four quarters of the Wall's total lifespan. There was therefore a 50 percent chance that he was in the middle two quarters, which implied that the Wall's remaining duration would lie between one-third and three times its current age — between roughly two years and eight months and twenty-four years. The Wall fell in November 1989, about twenty years later, inside the predicted interval, and Gott went on to publish the reasoning as an application of the Copernican principle. The doomsday argument runs the same style of inference against the future of humanity: if your birth rank among all humans who will ever live is not special, you should not expect to find yourself among the first few percent of humans, and this shifts probability away from futures in which a vastly larger number of humans are still to come.

Analyze the Berlin Wall episode through the lens of the doomsday argument, with the mapping done carefully rather than by loose analogy. Specify what plays the role of the observer, the evidence, the reference class, and the prior in each case, and say exactly which step of the doomsday argument the Wall case does and does not instantiate. Then run the analysis in both directions: what doomsday-style reasoning would have licensed Gott to believe in 1969, and what the eventual outcome should do to our confidence in the doomsday argument itself. Is the Wall's success a genuine confirmation of the doomsday argument, a confirmation only of a weaker Copernican method that the doomsday argument cannot automatically inherit, or something else? Be explicit about where the two cases come apart: the Wall was a single observed object with a known age so far and a relatively clear reference class, whereas the doomsday argument reasons from inside a population before its total is known and with a contested choice of reference class. Also say where the method is silent about the episode: it delivered an interval but no mechanism, and it could not distinguish among the causes that actually ended the Wall. I want an honest account of what the case shows about the theory, not a verdict settled in advance.
