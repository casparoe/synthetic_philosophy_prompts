I'm giving a talk at an EA conference next month where I'm going to argue that the cluelessness objection bites just as hard on near-term cause prioritization as it does on longtermism, and that the confidence with which the community ranks global health above animal welfare (or vice versa) is mostly an artifact of stopping the causal chain too early. The audience will be roughly a third global health and development people, a third animal welfare people, a third longtermists, plus maybe five academic philosophers who work on population ethics or decision theory. My core move is: once you take seriously the downstream ripple effects of any intervention — economic, political, cultural, ecological — the sign of even a straightforward bednet distribution becomes uncertain over a few decades, and the standard reply that we can bound the effects relies on modeling choices that are themselves vulnerable to the same cluelessness worry. I'm not saying do nothing; I'm saying our error bars are way wider than the community talks as if they are, and that this has practical implications for how we allocate marginal dollars.
