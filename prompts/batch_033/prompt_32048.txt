I'm on the ethics advisory board of a fertility clinic network, and we're stuck on whether to start offering preimplantation genetic testing for a condition that causes moderate but chronic pain throughout life — not so severe that life isn't worth living, but enough that we'd all agree it's a harm if you have it. The board is split right down the middle. One camp says we have a clear duty to help parents avoid bringing a child into a life of suffering, even if that life is still net-positive overall. The other camp says that's incoherent: if the life is worth living, there's no harm in bringing it about, and we shouldn't be in the business of acting as if there is. The chair asked me to draft a memo laying out a concrete recommendation — approve the testing service, reject it, or approve with conditions — and she specifically wants me to engage with the philosophical puzzle underneath the disagreement, which she called the procreation asymmetry, or something like the asymmetry of harms and benefits in procreation. I'm not a philosopher, just an OB-GYN who got roped into this committee, so I need the memo to actually make the philosophy usable for a clinical policy decision. The strongest argument on the losing side has to be taken seriously, not just dismissed, and the bottom line should be something the board can vote on. I keep going back and forth myself — sometimes I think the asymmetry is obviously right and we'd be reckless to ignore it, and sometimes I think it collapses under its own weight the moment you try to operationalize it in a clinical guideline. Help me sort this out.
