Means-tested welfare systems in most developed countries draw a distinction that I find philosophically puzzling on close inspection. If you are poor because your employer went bankrupt, you get unemployment benefits; if you are poor because you quit without good cause, you face sanctions or disqualification. If you are born with a disability, you receive lifelong support; if your disability results from an injury sustained while driving drunk, your benefits may be reduced or denied. The pattern seems to be that the system cares whether your disadvantage is the product of bad luck or bad choices, and treats the two very differently.

But the practice also has features that don't fit this cleanly. Someone born into a poor family in a neighborhood with failing schools receives the same means-tested benefits as someone who made a series of reasonable career bets that all went wrong — even though one case looks like brute luck and the other like option luck in the luck-egalitarian taxonomy. Someone whose poverty traces to a genetic predisposition toward impulsivity sits in a gray area the system doesn't really know how to handle. And the system sometimes seems to care about whether the disadvantage was foreseeable, not just whether it was chosen — a different axis entirely.

I want to know what theory of responsibility and luck this practice presupposes — or whether it presupposes anything coherent at all. Reconstruct the implicit theory: what distinction between types of luck is the system actually tracking, and is it the luck-egalitarian distinction between brute luck and option luck, or something else? Where does the practice diverge from what a consistent luck egalitarianism would recommend, and are those divergences ad hoc compromises with political feasibility or signs that the system is really tracking a different underlying value (desert, incentives, compassion, something else)? And if we took luck egalitarianism seriously as a guide to welfare policy design, what specific features of the current system would have to change, and are those changes ones that luck egalitarians themselves would welcome, or would they expose tensions within luck egalitarianism that the abstract theory papers over?
