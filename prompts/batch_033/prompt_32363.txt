I've been trying to get clear on what I think about objects and change, and my gut verdicts on cases keep pulling in different directions. Heres the cases.

Monday a sculptor makes a clay statue. Tuesday she squashes it. I want to say the lump of clay is still there, but the statue is gone — so the statue and the lump weren't the same thing. But then two material objects were in exactly the same place at the same time, which I also find hard to believe.

Theseus's ship: every plank gets replaced one by one. I say the original ship survives — it's the ship with the new planks. But if someone collected the old planks and rebuilt them into a ship, I don't think the rebuilt one is the original, even though it has all the original material. So here identity seems to follow the form or the continuity, not the matter.

The tree in my yard: is the tree the same thing as the aggregate of cellulose molecules that makes it up? I go back and forth. If you put it through a woodchipper, the aggregate of molecules still exists (scattered), but the tree is gone, so they look distinct — but again, two things in the same place.

And Tibbles the cat: before he loses his tail, I'm fine saying Tibbles and the part of him that's everything but the tail are two different things, but only Tibbles is a cat. After he loses the tail, I don't want to say there are suddenly two cats in the same spot, but I also don't want to say Tibbles went out of existence just because a tail fell off.

Is there one principle about persistence (or about what objects there are) that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about the tree and about Tibbles, and about a further case I haven't thought to ask about? And which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Also please seperate what's settled in this area from what's genuinely contested among people who work on it — I don't want a contested view passed off as the consensus.
