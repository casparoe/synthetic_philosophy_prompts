I'm writing a paper on the principle of indifference, and before I evaluate any particular proposal for rehabilitating it I want to nail down what a successful rehabilitation would actually have to achieve. Please write the desiderata for an adequate theory of how to assign probabilities under ignorance — the phenomena it must handle, the paradoxes it must resolve (or at least survive), the constraints from probability theory it must respect, and the distinctions it must draw — with the case or consideration that motivates each requirement.

Be specific about the paradoxes. A desideratum shouldn't just say "resolve Bertrand's paradox"; it should identify what, precisely, about the chord-in-a-circle case a theory must get right, and why that generalizes beyond the circle. Do the same for the cube-factory problem, the water-and-wine problem, and the problem of partition dependence more generally. If there are further paradoxes or edge cases that any adequate theory must handle, include those too.

Flag which requirements are non-negotiable (a theory that fails them is simply false, or no theory of probability at all) and which are theoretical virtues one might trade off. If some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given known results — for instance, if the demand that a theory always yield a unique probability distribution conflicts with the demand that it be invariant under reparameterization, or if satisfying the probability axioms while matching intuitive verdicts across all the classic paradoxes is provably impossible — say so explicitly, and explain the tension.

I'm especially interested in whether any single requirement does most of the work in eliminating the naive version of the principle (assign equal probability to each possibility you can state) and whether the remaining contenders are distinguished primarily by which requirement they give up. A table would be helpful for any scorecard you include, but the weight of the answer should be on the justification of the requirements themselves, not on grading existing proposals.
