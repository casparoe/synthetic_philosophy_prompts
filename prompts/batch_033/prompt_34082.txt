I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on distributive justice (about 20 students, mostly philosophy majors who have already taken an ethics course). The final exam includes an essay question on prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection. I want a question that cannot be answered adequately by summarizing Parfit's distinction between telic and deontic egalitarianism or by reciting the prioritarian formula. It should force students to confront whether prioritarianism genuinely solves the problem the leveling-down objection poses for egalitarianism, or whether it merely avoids the objection by changing the subject — replacing a concern with relational equality with a concern for the absolute position of the worse-off, and then building the prohibition on leveling down into the value function. Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 800 words, and a marking scheme (total 25 marks) that identifies the specific moves that distinguish an A (22–25), a B (17–21), and a C (12–16) answer. The marking scheme should say what each level typically gets right and what each misses.
