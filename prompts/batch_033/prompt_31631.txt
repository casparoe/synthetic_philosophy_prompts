I keep coming back to a debate in the consciousness literature that seems completely stuck, and I've started to suspect the sticking point is a hidden disagreement about what the question even means. The set-up: in the color phi phenomenon, a red dot flashes in one place and a green dot flashes a little later somewhere else, and people report seeing a single dot glide from the first location to the second, turning green roughly halfway along. The weird part is that the motion seems to start before the second flash has happened, so the brain must somehow be using information about the later flash to shape an experience that feels earlier. Dennett and Kinsbourne gave this two classic readings. The Orwellian reading says you consciously saw the two separate flashes in order, but your memory got rewritten afterward, so your report of a moving dot is basically a false memory. The Stalinesque reading says consciousness itself was held back, edited in the brain, and only then shown to you as a seamless moving dot, so you never actually experienced the raw flashes at all. Both stories predict exactly the same reports, and as far as I can tell no experiment has ever seperated them.

I'm not really asking which side is right. I want a diagnosis of the shape of the debate, because my working theory is that the whole opposition is ill-posed: it seems to assume there is a determinate fact about the timing of your conscious experience that exists independently of what you can report or remember, and once you drop that — once you stop imagining a place in the brain where "you" watch the show — the Orwellian/Stalinesque question may not have an answer at all. But I might be overreaching, and I'd like someone to actually work through it. So: is this a substantive empirical disagreement that some future experiment could settle, a verbal dispute about the word 'experience', or a pseudo-question generated by a bad picture of consciousness? And depending on the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a new experimental paradigm, a conceptual distinction, or just changing the question?
