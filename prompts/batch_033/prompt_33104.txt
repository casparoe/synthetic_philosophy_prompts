I've been reading about the cognitive science of religion — the idea that belief in gods and supernatural agents arises from ordinary mental tools like agency detection, theory of mind, and our tendency to see patterns and intentions in the world. From what I understand, the evolutionary story is that these cognitive mechanisms were selected for entirely mundane reasons (spotting predators, navigating social life), and religious belief is essentially a byproduct. This seems to me like it wraps up the question of whether religious beliefs are true. If we can give a complete natural explanation for why people hold them, without ever needing to reference anything supernatural, then surely those beliefs are just a quirk of our psychology — no different from an optical illusion. So why do philosophers still treat theism as a live option? What am I missing?
