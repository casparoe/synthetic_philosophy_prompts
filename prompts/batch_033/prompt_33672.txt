I'm a game designer — I've been doing systems design for about 8 years, mostly on strategy games and sims. I recently got into an argument with a friend who's a philosophy grad student about whether game mechanics are "political." He mentioned this old debate about whether artifacts have politics — the idea that certain technologies just *are* authoritarian, or just *are* democratic, regardless of how they're used. His example was something about low bridges on Long Island that were supposedly designed to keep buses (and therefore poor people) out. I kept pushing back saying that's just a design choice, not politics — the bridge has a height, the height constrains what passes, that's just a constraint, and constraints are what design *is*. He said I was missing the point.

I want to take this seriously. Please restate the "artifacts have politics" thesis — the Langdon Winner version, and any major variants or counterarguments you think are worth including — entirely in the vocabulary of game design. Use terms like mechanics, dynamics, aesthetics, affordances, player agency, balancing, win conditions, dominant strategies, emergent behavior, the magic circle, etc. Translate it as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what you had to distort or fudge, and what parts of the philosophical debate have no expression at all in game design vocabulary. I especially want to know whether the stuff that doesn't translate is a sign that the philosophical debate is tracking something real that game design vocabulary is blind to, or whether it's a sign that the philosophers are reifying something that's better understood as just a design constraint.
