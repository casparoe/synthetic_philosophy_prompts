I've been following the debates about whether large language models and other AI systems have beliefs, intentions, or anything resembling a mind, and I keep noticing that people talk past each other because they haven't agreed on what the bar is. Some point to a language model saying "I believe Paris is the capital of France" and think that settles it; others insist the whole thing is next-token prediction and attributing beliefs is a category mistake. Before I read any more position papers, I want to get clear on what the criteria even are.

Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of belief attribution to an AI system must meet. What cases must it get right — the stochastic parrot that passes conversational tests but stores no coherent world model, the RL agent that builds a causal map of its environment during training, the language model that passes false-belief tasks, the chess engine that represents its opponent's likely replies? What distinctions must it respect — between having a belief and merely behaving as if one does, between the intentional stance as a predictive instrument and as a metaphysical commitment, between full-blown propositional attitudes and subpersonal representational states, between derived and original intentionality if that distinction survives scrutiny? What results from cognitive science and philosophy of mind must it be consistent with — the existence of blindsight and unconscious representation, the dissociation between access and phenomenal consciousness, the lesson of the Chinese Room if you take it to have one? For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and flag which are non-negotiable and which are parochial commitments of a particular camp (Dennettian instrumentalists, realists about propositional attitudes, functionalists, biological chauvinists). I especially want to know whether any pair of desiderata are in tension — for instance, whether the demand that a theory track folk-psychological practice and the demand that it be empirically tractable can be jointly satisfied, or whether taking the intentional stance as a predictive stance pulls against treating belief attributions as literally true. Finally, give me your own committed view on which single desideratum does the most to exclude the most popular positions, and defend it.
