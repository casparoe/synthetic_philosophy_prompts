Over the past year I have been making concrete decisions about where to direct my time and money, and I keep finding that expected value reasoning pushes me toward conclusions I used to find absurd. I have ended up accepting the position that, when probabilities and values are well-defined, a rational agent should maximize expected value even when this means acting on an extremely small probability of an astronomically large payoff, and that the resulting so-called fanatical conclusions — devoting nearly all of one's resources to a one-in-a-billion chance of preventing vast suffering, say — are a correct implication of the view rather than a reductio of it.

I would like you to tell me what would have to be true for this position to be false. Please lay out the considerations — arguments, formal results, case judgments, intuitive costs — that should most move someone who currently holds it, ranked from those that would be decisive to those that would be merely awkward. For each, I want to know whether it is a consideration the position's own defenders would concede as a genuine cost, or one they would dismiss as misdiagnosed or question-begging. Where relevant, say what the position predicts about rational behavior that its rivals would not predict, and whether that prediction exposes it in any way.

I also want your judgment about whether the position has been insulated by its own qualifications — for instance by restricting itself to cases where probabilities and utilities are well-defined, or by treating each apparently absurd implication as a bullet to bite rather than a counterexample — and whether that insulation, if it exists, is a defect or a legitimate feature of a theory of this kind. Be willing to conclude that the view is better exposed than I think, or better insulated, and to say why that matters.

For what it is worth, I would personally find a formal demonstration that the view violates some principle I already endorse more persuasive than an appeal to the absurdity of its conclusions; I say this only so you can calibrate which considerations are likely to move me.
