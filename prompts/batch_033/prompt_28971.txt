I am a high school student enrolled in a philosophy elective this semester, and I also spend a lot of my free time programming—I have been working with object-oriented languages for about four years now, so I am comfortable with the standard vocabulary: classes, instances, objects, properties, methods, inheritance, interfaces, state, persistence, garbage collection, and so on. For example, when someone says an NPC is "conscious" or "suffers," I translate that mentally as the object having a particular property or a method that returns a certain value; when someone says the moral status depends on what kind of thing the NPC is, I think about class membership and inheritance hierarchies; when someone says it matters whether the NPC's experiences are "real" or merely simulated, I think about the distinction between an object's internal state and whether that state corresponds to anything in the hardware or the outside world.

I would like you to do this translation systematically. And which claims seem to have no counterpart at all in the OOP framework?
