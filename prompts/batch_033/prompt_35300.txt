I've been trying to work through the Rietdijk–Putnam argument on my own — the one that says special relativity forces us into a block universe where the future is already just as real as the present. I think I have the pieces: relativity of simultaneity means there is no absolute "now" across space; for any future event spacelike-separated from me, there exists some reference frame in which that event is simultaneous with my here-and-now. But I keep getting stuck on what seems like a gap between the physics and the metaphysics. The argument appears to move from "event E is simultaneous with my here-and-now in some frame" to "therefore event E is real." Why should I accept that simultaneity-in-some-frame is the right criterion for reality? Couldn't I instead say that reality itself is frame-relative — that what exists is relative to a frame, so there is no frame-independent fact about what is real, and the argument never gets off the ground? Or is there some further step in the argument that blocks that move, something about transitivity or about what it means for an event to be determinate? I want you to explain the argument in a way that makes painfully clear exactly which premises are doing the philosophical work, which are just physics, and where someone who wants to resist the conclusion should dig in. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
