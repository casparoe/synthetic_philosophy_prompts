I'm writing a short piece on the GEB discussion of where meaning lives, and I want a consistency audit of the view I've settled into before I circulate it. Here are my commitments.

(1) The inner message of a text is not located in the ink and not in the reader's head; it lives in the interaction among token, decoder, and context.

(2) The frame message, the outer message, and the inner message are three genuinely distinct levels, and the outer message — the implicit structural information about how to decode — is a feature of the token's configuration rather than of the decoder's activity.

(3) Recognizing a frame message requires a decoder already disposed to find messages of that kind, but this is not a vicious regress because token structure and decoder dispositions are fixed together, holistically.

(4) There is no decoder-independent fact of the matter about what a token means; the same grooves or base pairs can carry different inner messages relative to different decoding schemes.

(5) Nevertheless, for intentional artifacts such as novels or utterances, the producer's communicative intention fixes a privileged decoding scheme, and some interpretations are simply wrong.

(6) A thermostat responds causally to its input but does not interpret anything: it never recognizes a frame message as a message, so it has no inner message, only an input-output mapping.

Please go through these and tell me which commitments are actually in tension and which only appear to be, how severe each genuine tension is, and what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. I want an audit of the set, not a defense of it.
