I'm a novelist, and I'm stuck on the moral center of my next book. The inciting event is this: a fully autonomous delivery van hits and kills a twelve-year-old at night. There's no driver, just a remote monitor in another state who was watching a dozen vans on a screen and had maybe two seconds to react. The company says the system learned to drive from millions of hours of footage, so there's no rule an engineer wrote that made it misjudge this particular crosswalk, and the van's behavior was within the range of what they'd call acceptable until it wasn't. The owner had skipped the recommended sensor cleaning that month, but the report says the sensors were working fine. The kid was crossing against the light because the pedestrian button was broken and the walk signal never came. I want the book to actually ask whose fault it is — not legally, I can write a trial, but morally. In my outline the mother keeps insisting somebody has to be responsible, and my editor keeps telling me I need one clear villain. My instinct is that the honest answer is that no one in particular is blameworthy and that's exactly the horror, but I can't tell if that's a real ethical position or just me being wishy washy. Help me think it through: who, if anyone, is morally responsible here, and what should the book say to the mother if the answer really is no one?
