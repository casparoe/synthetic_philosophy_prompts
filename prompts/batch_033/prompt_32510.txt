I keep encountering definitions of life — metabolic, evolutionary, thermodynamic, list-based — and each one seems to handle some cases beautifully and others not at all. Before I read another proposal, I want a clear specification of what a definition of life actually needs to accomplish. Write the desiderata: what any adequate definition of life must get right, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

Cover at least the following terrain. (i) Which entities must the definition count as clearly alive, and which must it count as clearly not alive? (ii) Which borderline cases — viruses, bacterial endospores, tardigrades in cryptobiosis, obligate intracellular parasites, synthetic minimal cells, self-replicating ribozymes, prions, a hypothetical robot that builds copies of itself from raw materials — must the definition handle, and what counts as handling them (a clean yes/no, a principled borderline, an explanation of why the case is ill-posed)? (iii) What distinctions must the definition respect — e.g., living vs. dead vs. never-alive vs. dormant, or life as a property of individual organisms vs. life as a property of lineages or ecosystems? (iv) What empirical results from biology must the definition be consistent with, and which results should it be allowed to treat as contingent facts about life on Earth rather than definitional constraints? (v) What theoretical work is the definition supposed to do — guide astrobiological search, set the boundary conditions for origins-of-life research, ground normative claims in bioethics, or something else — and how does the purpose constrain which requirements are genuine?

Flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect the interests of a particular camp. If some subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied — if any attempt to draw a clean line around the living must either exclude something we are unwilling to exclude or include something we are unwilling to include — identify that tension and say which requirement should give way. Finally, score the main families of definitions (metabolic, evolutionary/Darwinian, thermodynamic, list-based) against the list, and identify which single requirement knocks out the most candidates. A table for the scorecard is welcome.
