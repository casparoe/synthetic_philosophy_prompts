My friend and I have been going back and forth for weeks over a question our sixth-form philosophy club keeps returning to: whether a sufficiently large number of very small harms can ever outweigh a single very great harm. She has been strikingly consistent in her verdicts on the cases we have put to her. She is certain that one person being tortured for a year is worse than a thousand people each getting a single dust speck in the eye, and that one person suffering a severe week-long migraine is worse than five people each having a mild headache for an afternoon. She is equally certain that a hundred people each suffering a broken arm is worse than one person suffering a broken arm, and that one person with a broken leg is worse than ten people each stubbing a toe.

The case on which we now disagree is the classic one in its strongest form: one person being tortured for fifty years versus a googol people (10 to the power of 100) each receiving a single dust speck in the eye. I am less interested in the bare answer than in what her track record shows. What rule or rules would make sense of her earlier verdicts, what does each candidate rule predict about the new case, which rule would you guess she is running and why, and what further case would most cleanly separate the candidates that the new case leaves open?
