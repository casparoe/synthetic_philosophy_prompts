I'm working on a piece about the singleton hypothesis for a general interest magazine — the idea, mostly from Nick Bostrom, that Earth-originating intelligence will eventually be governed by a single decision-making entity. Could be a superintelligent AI, a world government, some kind of merged consciousness, whatever. I'm convinced it's at least plausible. But I'm trying to figure out which downstream implications actually deserve the ink, and which ones people in these debates overhype.

Here's what I mean. Some folks say: if a singleton is coming, great-power competition is a sideshow — it all gets resolved eventually. Others say the opposite: the transition period is the most dangerous part, so competition matters even more. Some argue democracy is basically irrelevant in a singleton world; others say the whole question is what values the singleton locks in, and how those get set, which is a deeply political question. Some treat the singleton as a reason to go all-in on AI alignment; others say the singleton hypothesis is self-defeating because if everyone believes it, you get a race to be the singleton.

I want you to rank the main implications people draw from the singleton hypothesis by how much they should actually change someone's thinking about politics, technology, or the long-term future. Give me tiers if that helps. Which ones genuinely follow and carry weight, which are speculative but worth taking seriously, and which are overblown or don't really follow? I'm not after a lit review — I want your own judgment about which implications earn their place and why.
