I run a small philosophy podcast, and in two weeks I'm interviewing a philosopher who's been arguing that the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism collapses once you accept a reductionist account of personal identity — the idea being that if the boundaries between persons are less deep than we think, then the objection that utilitarianism ignores those boundaries loses its grip. He's published a couple papers on this and I want to push him past the stuff he's said before. Write me eight to ten questions. For each of those, include a follow-up that anticipates his most likely move. A few of the remaining questions can be wildcards, but they should still cut against the view — no softballs about what drew him to philosophy.
