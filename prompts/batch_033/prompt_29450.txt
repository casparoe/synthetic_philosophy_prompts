Im on the committee for a small charitable fund, and one member keeps pushing us to put nearly everything into insect welfare. His argument goes like this: there are something like a hundred million insects for every person, and even if you think there's only a one or two percent chance that an insect can suffer, one percent of a number that big still dwarfs all the human suffering we could prevent. When I pushed back that the one percent is basically a guess, he said it doesn't matter—pick 0.01 percent, or 0.0001 percent, and the math still says insects win by a huge margin. I get the arithmetic, but something feels off: it seems like hes turned a wild guess into a solid number and then let that number do all the work. Is he reasoning badly, or am I just bad at appreciating numbers that big?
