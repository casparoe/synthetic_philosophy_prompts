"Kant's whole thing about moral worth is that an action is only praiseworthy if you do it solely from duty — meaning you can't have any sympathy or emotional involvement whatsoever. Like, if you help someone and you feel good about it, that actually makes the action less morally worthy, because your motivation is contaminated. That's why he says the only person whose action has genuine moral worth is the one who hates helping but does it anyway out of duty. This is obviously perverse, and basically everyone since has agreed that motives don't determine moral worth — what matters is whether the action is right, not why you did it."

I know this isnt quite right — the stuff about the grudging benefactor being the *only* one with moral worth seems like a misreading of what Kant is doing with that example — but I'd like a careful breakdown.
