I've spent the last few weeks arguing with my flatmates about whether the person I married is literally the same object as the person currently burning toast in our kitchen, or whether 'same person' is just a convenient way of talking about a long chain of person-stages. We've all read enough about endurantism and perdurantism to have picked a team, but I'm fairly sure most of us would switch teams if we had to answer about cases without the jargon attached. So I'd like you to write a 'which theory of persistence do you actually hold?' quiz for our reading group: eight to ten questions, each one a concrete case (the lump of clay shaped into a statue and then squashed, the ship whose planks are all replaced, an amoeba that splits in two, a time traveller meeting their younger self, getting a tattoo or a haircut, that sort of thing), with two or three answer options per question, and then a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto positions. The point is to sort people by what they actually say about the cases, not by which label they like, so no question should just ask whether someone thinks they are a four-dimensional worm.

I'd like the key to distinguish at least three positions: endurance (a persisting thing is wholly present at each moment), perdurance (a persisting thing is spread out through time and has temporal parts), and stage theory (only momentary stages exist, and talk of 'the same object over time' is really a counterpart relation). If a deflationary 'ordinary objects don't genuinely persist' option can be made to fit without forcing it, include it; if not, say why. Please flag any pair of positions the quiz can't cleanly seperate, and note which answer patterns land in empty corners of the logical space. Write in British English, and start directly with the quiz itself — no preface, no commentary. After the quiz, give the scoring key and a sentence or two on the most awkward commitment of each leaf.
