I was reading a pop philosophy book that summarized the Knobe effect like this: "Joshua Knobe discovered that people's moral judgments distort their factual judgments. In his classic experiment, subjects read about a CEO who adopts a program to boost profits, knowing full well it will harm the environment. Most subjects said the CEO intentionally harmed the environment. But when the scenario was flipped so the side effect helped the environment, most said he didn't intentionally help it. The upshot is that the folk concept of intentional action is hopelessly contaminated by moral evaluation — people can't keep their oughts and their ises apart, which undermines the whole project of using folk intuitions as evidence in philosophy."
