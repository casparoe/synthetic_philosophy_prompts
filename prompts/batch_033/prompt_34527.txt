So my university is going through accreditation review again and the whole thing has me thinking. We have to write these "student learning outcomes" for every course — like "by the end of this course, students will be able to analyze and evaluate arguments using formal logical methods" — and then we have to demonstrate, with assessment data, that students actually achieved them. The outcomes have to be "measurable" and "observable," so you can't say "students will appreciate the beauty of mathematics" or whatever. And this isn't just one department being weird about it — it's built into the whole accreditation system, across basically all universities in the US at this point, and it's spreading to other countries too.

What I'm wondering is: what view of knowledge, and of teaching, does this whole apparatus presuppose? It seems to assume that what matters about education is the production of specific, pre-specified behavioral capacities — that if you can't measure it, it didn't happen, or at least it doesn't count for accreditation purposes. And it also seems to presuppose something about power: the faculty are the ones being watched (by accreditors, by administrators, by the assessment office), and the watching is justified by the idea that transparency and measurement serve accountability. But the weird thing is that the same institution also says it values things like "critical thinking" and "creativity" and "lifelong learning" — things that seem hard to operationalize as measurable outcomes. So there's a tension.

I know Foucault wrote about this kind of thing — discipline, normalization, the exam as a site where power and knowledge become the same thing. But I want someone to reconstruct, concretely, what this specific practice (the learning outcomes and assessment regime) presupposes philosophically. Does it presuppose a behaviorist epistemology? A particular model of power as top-down surveillance? Does it rule out othere views of what education is, or is it compatible with them? And if you actually took Foucault's analysis of power/knowledge/discipline seriously, what would have to change in how accreditation works — or would the whole project of accreditation collapse?
