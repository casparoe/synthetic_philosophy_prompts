The debate in metaontology about whether certain ontological disputes are substantive or merely verbal has been running for over two decades now, and I'm starting to suspect that the impasse isn't just a matter of the question being hard. Here's the shape of it.

On one side, you have the quantifier variance view, associated most prominently with Eli Hirsch. The idea is that when a mereological nihilist says there are no tables and an ordinary person says there are tables, they aren't disagreeing about the world — they're using the existential quantifier with different meanings. The nihilist means something like there exist no composite objects while the ordinary person means something looser. Each side, if they adopted the other's quantifier meaning, would assent to the other's sentences. So the dispute is merely verbal: it's a disagreement about which language to use, not about how things are.

On the other side, you have the view — defended by Ted Sider and others — that quantifier meanings are not all on a par. Some quantifier meanings are more natural or joint-carving than others, and the question which quantifier meaning is the joint-carving one is a substantive metaphysical question. The nihilist and the ordinary person are disagreeing about which quantifier meaning carves nature at the joints, and that is a genuine factual disagreement even if each could, in some attenuated sense, adopt the other's language.

What confuses me is that the debate seems to have reached a point where both sides acknowledge the same data, agree about what the other side would say, and yet neither budges. Hirsch can point out that the two sides can interpret each other's sentences as true under their own quantifier; Sider can reply that this doesn't settle the question of which quantifier is joint-carving. Then Hirsch asks what it even means to say one quantifier is more natural than another in a way that isn't itself just a matter of linguistic choice. And around we go.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, not a verdict on who is right. Is this a substantive disagreement about a metaphysical fact that we don't yet know how to access, or is it a verbal dispute about the word substantive — with Sider using a metaphysically loaded notion of substantiveness and Hirsch using a deflationary one? Or is it something else entirely: a clash of methodological starting points about what metaphysics is supposed to do, where neither side can argue for its starting point without presupposing it? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a new formal result, a conceptual distinction that breaks the deadlock, or an honest acknowledgment that the question is ill-posed?
