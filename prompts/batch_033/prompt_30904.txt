I'm drafting the position section of a paper on sphexishness and free will, and I'd like a consistency audit of the view I've assembled before I present it. Here is the position, stated as a set of commitments:

1. The difference between the sphex wasp's fixed provisioning routine — drag the paralyzed cricket to the burrow entrance, go inside to inspect, and, if the cricket has been moved in the meantime, drag it back and inspect again rather than simply taking it in — and flexible human action is one of degree, not kind. There is no sharp line at which a creature stops being sphexish and starts being free.

2. Human freedom is nonetheless real and worth wanting. It consists in ordinary competences: self-control, responsiveness to reasons, the ability to revise an intention when circumstances change, and the capacity to notice and override one's own routines.

3. Human behavior, like the wasp's, is wholly mechanistic, and there is no additional self or agent above the control loops; the higher-order process that notices a routine is itself just another mechanistic process.

4. What underwrites human flexibility is higher-order control: we can represent our own goals and routines and modify them. A creature with flexible first-order routines but no such meta-representation would still be sphexish in the relevant sense.

5. I accept the Frankfurt-style point that someone can be morally responsible for what she does even if she could not have done otherwise at the moment of action.

6. But I also hold that responsibility requires that the action issued from capacities the agent could, in principle, have exercised differently or revised through reflection. Responsiveness to reasons is what grounds responsibility.

7. A person with severe checking compulsions, who represents her rechecking as excessive, struggles against it, but cannot stop, is not a counterexample to this picture: she is flexible precisely because she represents the routine as excessive and resists it, even though her first-order behavior is rigid.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious, and for each genuine tension say what the least costly revision would be and what it would cost. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please keep the whole audit to at most 500 words.
