I'm a PhD student in evolutionary biology, and I've been going down a rabbit hole that's honestly starting to mess with how I think about my own research. I work on speciation in a group of freshwater fish, and I keep running into cases where I can't give a straight answer to "are these two populations the same species or not?" — and its not because I don't know enough about the fish. It's because different ways of answering the question give different answers, and none of them feels obviously right.

Here's the pattern I keep noticing, and I'm wondering if it has a name. You start with a concept that seems perfectly clear in everyday use — "species" — and you try to make it rigorous so you can do science with it. But instead of converging on a single definition, the project of making it rigorous produces multiple definitions that are each reasonable on their own terms but incompatible with each other. The biological species concept says it's about reproductive isolation, but that falls apart for asexual organisms. The phylogenetic species concept says it's about monophyletic groups, but then every slightly divergent population becomes a species. The ecological concept says it's about niche, but then you get different answers depending on how fine-grained you slice the niche. And the weird thing is, the more we learn about actual organisms — ring species, horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, hybrid zones that have been stable for thousands of years — the more definitions we seem to need, not fewer. It's like the concept is splintering under pressure rather than sharpening.

I know this is "the species problem" in some broad sense, but what I'm after is more specific. Is there a term for this particular dynamic: where trying to precisify a folk concept for scientific use causes it to fragment into multiple incompatible precisifications, none of which fully captures what we originally meant, and the fragmentation gets worse the more empirical detail you add? Is this a recognized pattern in philosophy of science beyond just the species case — like, does it happen to other concepts and is there a literature on it? And if there isn't an established name, how would you characterize what's going on here in a way that distinguishes it from ordinary disagreement about definitions, or from the kind of thing Kuhn talked about with incommensurability?

I'd love it if you could approach this as a philosopher of biology or a philosopher of science — think through the phenomenon carefully, maybe brainstorm a few candidate concepts it might be related to (conceptual fragmentation? promiscuous realism? something from the literature on natural kinds?), and then tell me which fits best and why, or coin something if nothing quite captures it. I'm less interested in the label itself than in getting clear on what exactly this pattern is and what it isn't — like, is this a feature of the world, a feature of our concepts, or a feature of the process of trying to operationalize folk categories for scientific work?
