The AI governance board I serve on has to decide, before our next training run, whether to adopt a gating policy: we would run a pre-registered suite of capability evaluations and, if any threshold is crossed, halt training and convene external reviewers before further scaling or deployment. The board is split on takeoff speed. One camp argues that capabilities are likely to improve roughly continuously with compute and algorithmic progress, so eval-based gates are our best tripwire and would give meaningful warning. The other camp argues that if the run enables recursive self-improvement, progress could compress from years to weeks and the gates would trip too late to matter; on their view the policy is mostly false confidence and delay. I need a short decision memo to circulate before the vote. Please write it so that it recommends a concrete course of action—adopt the gating rule, reject it, or adopt a specific modification with specified thresholds and a review timeline—and so that it takes the strongest version of the opposing position seriously rather than dismissing it.
