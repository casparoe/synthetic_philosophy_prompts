ok so i'm teaching an intro epistemology module for the first time next semester and i realized while prepping that i've never actually sat down and made my own commitments about the regress problem explicit. i've been poking around the literature and here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) i take the regress argument seriously as a genuine trilemma. you can't just dissolve it by saying justification is externalist or that knowledge is unanalyzable or whatever. foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism are the menu and you have to order.

(2) i lean foundationalist. i think there are basic beliefs (perceptual beliefs, maybe some a priori intuitions) that are justified without recieving their justification from further beliefs.

(3) but i also think that for any belief whatsoever — including the supposedly basic ones — you can always intelligibly ask "what justifies that?" the question doesn't suddenly become a category mistake or meaningless just because the belief is basic.

(4) i think coherentism is onto something important. in actual reasoning, justification doesn't flow one-way from foundations to superstructure. we adjust beliefs in both directions, and mutual support among beliefs seems to genuinely contribute to justification, not just to discovery.

(5) i'm not a skeptic. we know lots of things, and any epistemology that ends up denying that has gone wrong.

can you go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are? rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be — what i'd have to give up or modify to make things consistent.
