I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about human enhancement vs therapy, becuase I keep running into edge cases that don't fit neatly. Here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases — I want to know if there's a principle that captures them, or most of them, and what I'd have to give up if there isn't.

- Getting LASIK so I don't need glasses: totally fine.
- Taking caffeine to focus at work: fine.
- A healthy student using Adderall they bought off a friend to study for finals: not fine.
- Prozac for someone with clinical depression: fine, obviously.
- Prozac for someone who isn't depressed but wants to be more outgoing and confident than they naturally are: this one bothers me but I can't articulate why.
- Beta blockers for a musician with performance anxiety: fine.
- Beta blockers for a programmer who just finds code review stressful but doesn't have an anxiety disorder: also fine, oddly — which bothers me because it seems like it should pattern with the Prozac one but I'm giving it the opposite verdict.
- An athlete using EPO: not fine.
- An athlete sleeping in an altitude tent to boost red blood cells naturally: fine. I know the effect is similar to EPO but it feels different.
- CRISPR to fix the Huntington's mutation in an embryo: fine, arguably obligatory.
- CRISPR to make an embryo 5cm taller than it would otherwise be: not fine.
- A deaf couple using embryo selection to have a deaf child: not fine, but I get a bit uncomfortable when hearing people say that because it feels like they're imposing their values on a community they're not part of.

Is there a coherent line here? Therapy vs enhancement, natural vs artificial, something about the stakes, something about what's normal — none of them seem to get every case right. What principle fits my verdicts best, which ones does it force me to overturn, and what does it say about some cases I havent thought of? Please write in British English btw.
