As a science journalist, I've been covering the Maxwell's demon problem for years, and I've noticed something odd: the debate refuses to die. Every generation seems to produce a new resolution, and yet the demon keeps reappearing in the literature — in quantum thermodynamics, in black hole physics, in the foundations of statistical mechanics. It has been over a century and a half since Maxwell introduced the thought experiment, and we now have Szilard's connection to information, Landauer's principle about the cost of erasure, and Bennett's argument that measurement can be done reversibly. By most accounts, the second law is safe and the demon is exorcised. And yet the arguments continue.

What I want to know is: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it a substantive disagreement about physics — about whether there is some loophole in the Landauer-Bennett resolution that no one has yet closed? Is it a disagreement about the meaning of terms — about what "information" and "entropy" refer to, and whether the physical entropy of statistical mechanics is the same thing as the information-theoretic entropy Shannon defined? Is it a disagreement about what counts as an explanation — about whether a resolution that appeals to the cost of erasing a memory genuinely explains why the demon fails, or merely redescribes the fact that it does? Or is it something else entirely — a symptom of a deeper, unresolved question about the relationship between the epistemic and the ontic in statistical mechanics, a question that the demon is simply the most vivid way to keep alive?

I want a committed diagnosis, not a survey of positions. Tell me what the disagreement is actually about, and tell me what — if anything — would count as settling it. If you think it has already been settled and the continuing debate is a confusion, say so and explain the confusion. If you think it cannot be settled because the question is ill-posed, say so and explain why. Whatever your answer, take a definite position and defend it.
