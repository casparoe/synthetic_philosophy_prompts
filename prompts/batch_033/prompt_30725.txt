After retiring from a long career in physical chemistry, I have recently begun attending an evening seminar on aesthetics, and a one-page handout from the most recent session has left me uncertain in a way I would like to resolve. The instructor described the handout as a fair map of the debate over whether immoral art can be good art, and it says roughly the following. The debate is a standoff between separatists, who hold that a work's moral and aesthetic value are independent, and moralists, who hold that a work's moral defects are always aesthetic defects as well. On the separatist view, one can admire the formal brilliance of a Nazi propaganda film while condemning what it says, because the cinematography and the ideology belong to different registers of assessment. On the moralist view, that is self-deception: a film that glorifies evil is worse as a film. The handout adds that the moderate version of moralism, associated with Noël Carroll and Berys Gaut, says that if a work is morally flawed, that flaw counts against it aesthetically, and that therefore the only way to defend such a film is to deny that it is morally flawed. It concludes that the real issue is whether we can separate the work from the artist, and that once we learn an artist has done terrible things, continuing to enjoy the work is a way of endorsing what the artist did, so a morally sensitive person ought to give the work up.

I have a scientist's suspicion that several steps in this summary run together things that ought to be kept apart, but I do not yet have the philosophical vocabulary to say exactly where. I would be grateful for a careful audit: which parts of the handout would a careful participant in this debate accept, which parts overstate or conflate, what a defender of the positions named would object to, and whether the final conclusion about giving the work up follows from anything accurate in the summary. Please do not treat this as a request for a reading list; I want to know how much of the summary I can rely on, not where to go next. Kindly keep the response to at most 650 words.
