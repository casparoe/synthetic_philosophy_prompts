In thinking about the possibility that the basic axioms of arithmetic might be inconsistent, I would like you to assess the evidence we actually have for the consistency of first-order Peano arithmetic. Please rank the following five sources of assurance from strongest to weakest as evidence for consistency. Ties are permitted, but each tier must be justified.

(a) The absence of any discovered contradiction despite more than a century of intensive mathematical use and formal scrutiny.
(b) The existence of an intended interpretation—the standard natural numbers—in which all the axioms of Peano arithmetic are true.
(c) The relative consistency proof obtained by constructing a model of Peano arithmetic within Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory.
(d) Gentzen's proof-theoretic consistency proof, which proceeds by transfinite induction up to the ordinal ε₀.
(e) The near-unanimous judgement of working mathematicians that Peano arithmetic is consistent.

For each item, give one or two sentences explaining its place in the ranking. Please answer in British English, and no references or citations are required.
