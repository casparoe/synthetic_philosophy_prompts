ok so ive been going down a rabbit hole about leibniz's identity of indiscernibles and black's spheres and i think i've found an argument that proves way too much but i can't figure out where the mistake is.

the setup: leibniz says if two things share all properties they're identical. black's two iron spheres in an empty universe seem to be a counterexample — they share every property (mass, temperature, being-two-feet-from-another-sphere, etc.) but are clearly two spheres not one.

now here's the argument that's bugging me:

(1) if a and b are distinct, there must be some property one has that the other doesn't. (this is just the contrapositive of the identity of indiscernibles.)

(2) properties are either qualitative (how a thing is in itself) or relational (how it stands to other things).

(3) relational properties can't be what makes two things distinct, because a relational property like "being two feet from b" already presupposes that b exists as a distinct thing. you can't use the relation to ground the distinctness without going in a circle.

(4) so if two things are distinct, they must differ in some qualitative property.

(5) but now take any two electrons. they have exactly the same mass, charge, spin, everything. by (4), they must be the same electron. which is nuts.

(6) so either: (a) there are no qualitatively identical distinct objects (false — electrons exist), (b) distinctness can be brute, with no property grounding it (but this violates the principle of sufficient reason and seems like magic), or (c) relational properties can ground distinctness without circularity.

i feel like (c) has to be the exit but i dont see how. if the relation depends on the relata, and the relata being distinct depends on the relation, isn't that textbook circularity? and if you go with (b), what's so bad about brute distinctness anyway? leibniz would hate it but maybe he was wrong.

which step is the bad one and what do you have to give up? im most attached to (5) being false somehow but i don't know how to make that work. please say how confident you are in your diagnosis and what you're least sure of. and no formatting, just write it out as a plain email.
