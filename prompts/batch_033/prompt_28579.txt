I'm taking a course this term on the ethics of democratic institutions, and we've spent the last few sessions on Simone Weil's essay on the abolition of political parties. The position she defends, as I reconstruct it, is roughly this: a party is not simply a coalition organized around a program, but a permanent organizational form whose first imperative is its own preservation and growth; because it must generate a line on every question to remain relevant, it disciplines its members into collective passions, subordinates independent judgment to the demands of taking a side, and carries a totalitarian tendency even when its professed ideology is democratic. On her account the defect lies in the permanent apparatus itself rather than in any particular ideology or leadership, so that a temporary association formed around a single question might be unobjectionable while the party that must pronounce on everything is corrupting. I find the diagnosis gripping, but I also worry that it is exactly the kind of sweeping structural claim that can be made to survive almost any apparent counterexample: a party that seems to foster deliberation gets redescribed as the exception, or as still operating under the deeper logic of the party form.

I'd like you to take the position seriously as a live thesis about political organization and tell me what would count against it. What observable features of a party's internal life, its relation to its members, or its effects on public reason should a defender of this Weil-style view acknowledge as genuine defeaters, and which would be merely awkward? Please sort the defeaters from fatal to merely troublesome, and be explicit about which ones a committed critic of parties would themselves accept as counting against the view, rather than ones only an opponent would press. I'd also like an honest assessment of whether the claim can be formulated so that it risks anything at all — whether it has been, or would need to be, insulated by qualifications, and whether that insulation is a problem for a thesis of this kind. Finally, tell me what a political party would have to look like in practice for the structural claim to be substantially wrong rather than merely in need of refinement: what single kind of evidence should move an honest assessor of the view most?
