**The Fermi Paradox and the Expansion Assumption**

the fermi paradox goes like this: the universe is old and huge, there are billions of earth-like planets, so even with really conservative estimates for the odds of intelligent life evolving there should be tons of alien civilizations out there. and given that the galaxy is only about 100,000 light years across, even at sub-light speeds a civilization could colonize the whole thing in a few million years — which is nothing compared to the age of the galaxy. so where is everyone? why dont we see any signs of them?

the standard responses are things like: maybe the great filter is ahead of us or behind us (rare earth, etc.), maybe theyre out there but we cant detect them, maybe theyre deliberately hiding (the dark forest thing), or maybe civilizations inevitably destroy themselves before they can expand.

but i think theres a deeper assumption that most of these responses dont question: the idea that an advanced civilization *would want* to expand across the galaxy in the first place.

think about it. the argument that they "should" be here relies on the premise that if even *one* civilization in the history of the galaxy decided to colonize, theyd fill the whole thing. but why would they decide to do that? we're projecting human expansionist behavior onto aliens. on earth, life expands to fill available niches because of natural selection — organisms that dont reproduce and spread get outcompeted by ones that do. but natural selection stops applying in the same way once you have a civilization that can consciously choose its goals. an advanced alien civilization might look at the galaxy and think "were fine here, actually." they might value stability, or contemplation, or local optimization, or something we cant even imagine.

and the "but it only takes one" counterargument doesnt work either. it assumes that the distribution of alien psychologies includes at least one expansionist civilization. but we have no idea what that distribution looks like. maybe the kind of intelligence that can build an interstellar civilization is also the kind of intelligence that recognizes expansion as pointless or destructive. maybe the same traits that let a species avoid self-destruction long enough to reach the stars also make them disinclined to galactic conquest. theres no reason to assume independence between "can build starships" and "wants to colonize the galaxy."

so the fermi paradox might not be a paradox at all. it might just be that the universe is full of intelligent life that stays home. the silence isnt mysterious — its what we should have expected.
