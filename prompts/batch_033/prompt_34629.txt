In software engineering we have duck typing: if an object walks like a duck and quacks like a duck, we treat it as a duck. The interface is all that matters; the underlying implementation is irrelevant to the type system. I have seen colleagues extend this idea to AI minds: if a language model passes the Turing test, or behaves indistinguishably from a human in conversation, then it is a mind — or at least we should treat it as one. The behavioral criterion is the only one that matters, just as in duck typing; worrying about what is "inside" is a category mistake.

I am uneasy about this move but I struggle to articulate why. Can you audit the analogy? Lay out what corresponds to what — what plays the role of the interface, the implementation, the type system — and then identify where the mapping breaks. In particular, tell me whether the conclusion (that passing the Turing test settles the question of mindedness) survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion the whole time. Where you can, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic.
