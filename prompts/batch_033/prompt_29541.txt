I have been arguing with a colleague in behavioural economics about the sunk cost fallacy, and I would like a sharper map of the territory than either of us has managed so far. She takes the textbook view: a cost that cannot be recovered is irrelevant to a forward-looking decision, so anyone who lets past expenditure influence whether to continue a project is committing an error, full stop. I suspect that conclusion is too quick, because continuing a failing or declining project can sometimes serve genuinely prospective purposes—protecting one's reputation, learning something, managing future temptation, or avoiding real present distress. The trouble is that some of these sound like respectable reasons and some sound like dressed-up rationalisation, and I would like them sorted.

Here are five candidate justifications for honouring sunk costs, stated as neutrally as I can. Please rank them from the one that does most to show that honouring sunk costs can be rational to the one that does least. For each, write one or two sentences saying how far the justification genuinely escapes the charge of irrationality, and name a condition under which it succeeds or collapses.

(1) The prospective cost of stopping: abandoning a long investment would itself cause regret, distress, or a sense of waste, and these are real future states that should be weighed like any other cost.
(2) Reputation and commitment to others: others have observed the investment, and abandoning it signals unreliability or damages the trust on which future cooperation depends.
(3) The evidential value of the investment: the time and money already spent may indicate that the project is closer to completion than one thinks, or that one's initial estimate of its value was poorly calibrated and should be revised.
(4) Self-binding: treating sunk costs as if they mattered can act as a commitment device that makes it easier to resist the temptation to abandon worthwhile but difficult projects in the future.
(5) Narrative integrity: a life in which one repeatedly abandons costly long-term projects is, in itself and apart from further consequences, a less coherent or less admirable life to have led.

Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, in your ranked order, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Write in British English.
