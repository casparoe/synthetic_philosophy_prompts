Write a sonnet about the inside view and the outside view in forecasting. The octave should be spoken in the voice of someone taking the inside view of a project they are about to begin: full of specific plans, concrete details, and the quiet conviction that this time it will be different. The volta should be the moment the outside view intrudes — the base rate of similar projects, the reference class the planner has been ignoring, the statistical fact that most such projects run late or fail. The sestet should explore the planner's reaction to this intrusion: perhaps resistance, perhaps a flicker of recognition, but not conversion. The closing couplet should not resolve the tension but should instead leave the reader with the uneasy feeling that knowing about the outside view is not the same as being able to use it on oneself. Someone who knows the forecasting literature should be able to identify, line by line, where the inside view gives way to the outside view and why the volta lands where it does; someone who does not should still find it a good poem.
