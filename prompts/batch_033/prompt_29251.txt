Alright, here's one. I want you to build me a thought experiment that puts pressure on the simple counterfactual theory of causation by omission. You know the view: an omission causes an outcome iff had the omitted event occurred, the outcome wouldn't have. The problem everyone points to is overgeneration — the Queen of England's failure to water my plant comes out as a cause of its death. But the standard move is to add some kind of normality or relevance constraint, and then the counterfactual theorist says "sure, fine, I'll build that in."

What I want is more specific. Construct a case with two omissions, both of which straightforwardly satisfy the counterfactual condition, but where one of them also satisfies a plausible normative constraint (the omitted act was required by a duty, a role, a reasonable expectation, somethig like that) and the other doesn't. The bare counterfactual theory should say both are causes. The norm-constrained theory should say only the normatively backed one is. And — here's the key — I want the norm-constrained theory's verdict to be the intuitive one, but not in a cheap way where the normative difference is just obviously morally loaded. The normative constraint should be thin: a professional role, a customary practice, a coordination scheme — not a moral obligation. The point is to test whether the "selection problem" for omissions really is about norms all the way down, or whether the counterfactual theorist can solve it with something thinner.

Constraints: the scenario should be ordinary — a workplace, a household, a school, something like that. No more than a paragraph. And don't let the two omissions differ in their spatial or temporal proximity to the outcome; I don't want the counterfactual theorist to say "well, one is just more proximate, that's all I need."

After the scenario, walk me through: what each theory says and why, which feature of the case is doing the work, and what the counterfactual theorist's best response is — can they earn the intuitive verdict without going normative?
