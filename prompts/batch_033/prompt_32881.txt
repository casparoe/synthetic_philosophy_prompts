The question should force students to do more than rehearse the standard description of the regress; it should make them confront what the regress is supposed to show about the difference between merely listing the constituents of a proposition and explaining how they combine into a truth-evaluable whole, and whether the regress is genuinely vicious or merely curious. Please provide the following in your response:

1. **The Question** (one paragraph): State the essay question clearly, with any preamble or context you think the students need.

2. **Model Answer** (approximately 800 words): Write a model answer in American English that an A-level student might produce. It should canvass at least two distinct responses to the regress (e.g., a Fregean strategy, a fact-ontology strategy, a primitivist strategy, or a Russellian strategy) and explain what each strategy commits one to about the metaphysics of relations. The answer should also address whether the regress is vicious, benign, or misdiagnosed.

3. **Marking Scheme** (structured as a rubric): Identify five to seven specific moves or insights that separate an A answer from a B answer, each worth a stated number of marks out of a total of 20.
