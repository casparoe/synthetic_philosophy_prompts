I teach an upper-level philosophy of law seminar and I'm setting the take-home final. The students have read Hart's "Positivism and the Separation of Law and Morals" and Fuller's "Positivism and Fidelity to Law—A Reply to Professor Hart," plus a bit of secondary material. The problem I keep seeing: they can summarize each side's position but they can't get inside the architecture of the debate. They don't see that Fuller's "internal morality of law" is doing double duty — it's supposed to be both a condition for something to count as law AND a moral standard — and they don't see where Hart's positivism might actually have the resources to condemn Nazi law without collapsing into natural law. They also treat the grudge informer cases as if they're a knockout argument for one side or the other, without asking what those cases actually test.

So I want one essay question that forces them to confront whether the debate really turns on a substantive disagreement about the relationship between law and morality, or on different concepts of "law" such that the two sides are partly talking past each other. Please write the question, a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. The marking scheme should also flag the most common mistake — which is just reciting what each side says without analyzing whether they're even disagreeing about the same thing. This is meant to be a three-hour take-home essay, so they should be able to do it in that time.

Also, in the model answer, please separate what's pretty settled in the literature from what's still genuniely contested among people who work on this. Don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
