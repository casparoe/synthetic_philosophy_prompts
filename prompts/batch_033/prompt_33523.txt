I've been tracking a colleague's views on the ethics of procreation across several cases discussed in our reading group. She's a careful, systematic thinker, and I suspect she's running a consistent principle, but I haven't yet pinned down exactly which one. Here are her verdicts so far:

1. A couple considers having a child whose life would be, on balance, not worth living — more suffering than well-being. She says: impermissible.

2. A couple considers having a child whose life would be barely worth living — a small net positive. She says: permissible but not required.

3. A couple considers having a child whose life would be extraordinarily good — rich in flourishing. She says: permissible but not required.

4. A woman is in early pregnancy. If she carries to term, the child would have a life barely worth living. She says: permissible to terminate.

5. A couple is choosing between having no children and having one child with a moderately good life. She says: either choice is permissible.

6. A couple already has one child and considers a second, whose life would be not worth living. She says: impermissible, just as it would be for a first child.

Next week we turn to a new case. A couple has decided to have exactly one child and must choose between two embryos after IVF. Embryo A would develop into a child with a moderately good life. Embryo B would develop into a child with an extraordinarily good life. All else is equal — same burdens on the parents, same number of children either way. The question is whether there is any moral reason to choose B over A.

What do you predict my colleague will say? Which principle or principles is she implicitly running? In particular, is she committed to a strong procreation asymmetry (the goodness of a life never counts in favor of creating it), a weak asymmetry (it counts but with less weight than the badness of a miserable life counts against), or something else? Which of her existing verdicts most constrains the answer, and what further case would best distinguish the remaining candidate principles?
