I need a bespoke thought experiment for a post I'm drafting. The topic is higher-order evidence and whether the enkratic principle (roughly: don't believe P while believing your evidence doesn't support P) always hangs together with just treating higher-order evidence as more evidence.

Specs: Construct a one-paragraph case where an agent's higher-order evidence is *asymmetric* — it gives them reason to think they're systematically overrating evidence *for* P specifically, but says nothing about their ability to evaluate evidence against P. The source of the asymmetry should be mundane (not a demon, drug, or brain lesion). Maybe something like: they know they're in a good mood, and there's solid psych literature that good moods make people overcount confirmatory evidence while leaving disconfirmatory sensitivity intact.

Then walk me through: (a) what the enkratic requirement says they should believe about P, all things considered; (b) what a straight Bayesian — treating the higher-order info as just another piece of evidence to conditionalize on — would end up with; (c) whether (a) and (b) diverge. If they diverge, what feature of the asymmetry is doing the work? If they don't, what would you need to add to the case to break them apart?

The angle I'm chasing is whether "adjust your credence in the direction you're probably biased" is always coherent advice, or whether it breaks when you don't know the direction of the bias. So if the case illuminates that, all the better.
