I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I have ended up with the following set of commitments about reasoning under uncertainty, and I would like a consistency check before I rely on them. They are drawn partly from reading about Bayesian reasoning and partly from everyday engineering practice: estimating delivery dates, interpreting A/B tests, and resolving technical disagreements. Each commitment seems right to me on its own, but I suspect the set may not hang together.

(1) Subjective priors. There is no uniquely rational prior probability distribution. A prior is a starting point, and two reasonable people can begin from different priors without either being irrational.

(2) Bayesian updating. Once a prior is fixed, rational agents update by conditionalisation, and their degrees of belief should obey the probability calculus.

(3) The common prior assumption. Differences between the beliefs of rational agents should ultimately be traceable to differences in information, not to differences in priors. In particular, if two agents share a prior and have common knowledge of each other's posterior beliefs, then they cannot rationally agree to disagree.

(4) Persistent disagreement is sometimes rational. After a full and honest exchange of arguments, two competent people can still rationally assign different probabilities to a proposition — for example, that a given project will ship on time — without either having made a mistake.

(5) Disagreement with a peer is not automatically decisive. When I learn that someone whose judgement I respect as much as my own disagrees with me, I should take it seriously, but I am not rationally required to split the difference or defer; my own reasoning can legitimately leave me where I was.

I would like an audit of this set: which commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs merely appear to be in tension, and how severe each genuine tension is. Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. I have no settled view about which commitment would go first, and I am not looking for a lecture on which view is correct; I want to know where the fault lines are and what would have to give.
