Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a halfer and a thirder on the Sleeping Beauty problem. The collaboration concerns the standard version: Beauty goes to sleep on Sunday; a fair coin is tossed; if Heads, she is awakened on Monday only; if Tails, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday; between Monday and Tuesday (when Tails) she is given a drug that erases her memory of Monday's awakening; upon each awakening she is asked for her credence that the coin landed Heads. The halfer holds that upon awakening, Beauty's credence in Heads should be 1/2; the thirder holds it should be 1/3.

The joint statement should include the following sections.

**Section 1: Agreed claims.** A numbered list of claims both parties accept. These should go beyond restating the setup and include substantive points about the epistemology of self-locating belief, the relationship between credence and fair betting odds, constraints on admissible solutions, and what Beauty knows on Sunday night that she does or does not know upon awakening. Avoid vacuous agreements; find claims that are genuinely informative yet acceptable to both.

**Section 2: Disagreements table.** A table with three columns: (a) the precise proposition they disagree on, stated neutrally; (b) the halfer's position, phrased so a halfer would endorse the wording; (c) the thirder's position, phrased so a thirder would endorse the wording. The table should distinguish the surface disagreement about the numerical answer from the deeper disagreements that generate it — for example, disagreements about whether the two awakenings under Tails should be treated as independent epistemic subjects, about what exactly Beauty learns upon awakening that she did not know on Sunday night, and about whether credences should track the proportion of awakenings across runs of the experiment.

**Section 3: Cruxes.** A list of sub-questions such that if they were settled, at least one side would acknowledge that its view is undermined. For each crux, state what each side predicts or asserts about it, and describe a test, argument, or variant case that both sides would accept as bearing on it. Possible cruxes include: the import of the fact that Beauty would be correct more often across awakenings if she answered 1/3; whether the thirder's reasoning yields unacceptable consequences when the number of Tails-awakenings is made very large or when awakenings are not subjectively indistinguishable; whether the halfer can account for Beauty's change in credence between Sunday night and Monday morning without positing that she has lost information; and whether the disagreement persists under different decision-theoretic frameworks for connecting credence to action.

**Section 4: Verbal dispute assessment.** An assessment of whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal — that is, whether the two sides might be using "credence" or "probability" in different senses that, once disambiguated, would dissolve the disagreement. If some part is verbal, specify which part and why; if the disagreement is wholly substantive, explain why the disambiguation strategy fails.

The document should be one that a representative halfer and a representative thirder could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration — not a compromise that papers over differences, but a precise map of where they agree, where they diverge, and what would change their minds.
