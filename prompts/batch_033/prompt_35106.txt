Ok so I'm in a philosophy of action seminar and we've spent the last two weeks on moral worth, and I've ended up fairly convinced by the right-reasons view: an action has moral worth when the agent does the right thing because of the features that actually make it right, not because they believe it's right or because duty demands it. The Kantian picture where the motive of duty is the source of worth feels too narrow to me — it seems to count the grudging benefactor who helps solely out of duty as worthy while downgrading someone who helps a friend out of un-self-conscious love, which strikes me as backwards. I also like that the right-reasons view can say Huck Finn's action has moral worth even though he thinks he's doing something wrong, since he's responding to the features of Jim that actually matter.

But I've probably only seen this view at its best, and before I keep defending it in seminar I want to know what would count against it. So here's the position I hold, roughly: moral worth is acting for the right reasons, where the right reasons are the features that genuinely make the action right. What would have to be true for me to have to give that up? I'd like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I especially want to know which ones a committed right-reasons theorist would themselves concede are real problems rather than just bullet-biting. Some pressure points I'm already chewing on: the person who acts for the right reasons but would of done the same thing anyway for bad reasons; cases where the right-making features are too abstract or hard to cite in deliberation; whether people's self-reports about why they acted can even track the difference, which feels like a moral-psychology worry; and whether the view collapses into just saying the person had decent motives and got lucky, so it stops being a theory of worth and becomes a story about causation. I'd also like to know what the view commits me to that the duty-motive view doesn't, so I can see where it's actually exposed — and tell me honestly whether the whole thing has been insulated by defining right reasons flexibly enough to absorb whatever case gets thrown at it, and whether that would be a problem.
