I got into an argument with my cousin over Thanksgiving about affirmative action. He's a smart guy, reads a lot, and he pulled out "race is a social construct" as his main point — like, if race is just something we made up, then using it as a category for college admissions is reifying a fiction. I felt like he was doing something slippery but I couldn't say exactly what. I've heard the same phrase used by people on the complete opposite side to argue for reparations, which makes me think it's doing a lot of different jobs depending on who's holding it.

Can you unpack "race is a social construct" into all the distinct claims it could be making? I want the full menu: the biological claim about there being no racial essences or clean genetic boundaries, the claim about racial categories varying across cultures and history, the claim about race being real but not biologically real (which I've seen people say and don't totally get), the claim that we should stop using racial categories, the claim that racial categories are purely ideological and serve power... I probably don't even know all the versions. For each one, I want to know: is it obviously true, plausibly true but debatable, or probably false? And which of them would actually support my cousin's argument about affirmative action, and which would cut the other way? 

I'm not looking for the one "correct" reading — I want the menu, because I think the phrase is powerfull precisly because it lets people slide between meanings without noticing.
