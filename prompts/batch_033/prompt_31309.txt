I teach an upper-level seminar in the philosophy of history and I am drafting the final exam. I want one essay question on the covering-law model versus narrative explanation that cannot be answered by reciting Hempel's deductive-nomological schema. The question should force students to confront three connected problems at once: why narrative's retrospective character resists subsumption under general laws; why the symmetry of explanation and prediction is the deeper point of pressure, especially once Danto's notion of a narrative sentence is on the table; and whether giving up the covering-law model forces one to give up causal explanation in history. The difficulty should come from the structure of the question rather than from obscurity, and a strong answer will need to distinguish at least two distinct objections to the covering-law model and say which cuts deeper. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme for a 20-point, 60-minute essay. The marking scheme should separate an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain, and should identify the misunderstanding behind each common error — for example, confusing the observation that historians rarely state laws with the claim that history is not law-governed, treating narrative as mere chronology, or inferring that retrospective explanation cannot be causal.
