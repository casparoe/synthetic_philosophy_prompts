I've been working through the hole argument against spacetime substantivalism and I've ended up with a reconstruction that unsettles me because it seems to prove far too much. Here is the argument as I now understand it:

(1) Spacetime substantivalism claims that spacetime points are real entities whose identity is primitive — a point is the very point it is independently of the metric and matter fields defined on it.

(2) If points have primitive identity, then a diffeomorphism that smoothly redistributes field values across points yields a physically distinct world: the same individuals bear different properties.

(3) Consider any general-relativistic model with a 'hole' — an open region where the stress-energy tensor vanishes. Construct a hole diffeomorphism: a smooth map that is the identity outside the hole and on its boundary, but non-trivially shifts points around inside the hole.

(4) The original model and the diffeomorphically shifted model are observationally indistinguishable — all genuine observables are diffeomorphism-invariant. But substantivalism, by (2), treats them as distinct possibilities.

(5) So substantivalism implies that complete initial data on a Cauchy surface, together with the laws, leave infinitely many distinct futures undetermined — a radical indeterminism with no empirical signature.

(6) But this 'indeterminism' is a formal artefact, not a genuine physical feature of general relativity.

(7) Therefore substantivalism is false.

Now here is my difficulty. The argument's engine — 'your theory posits entities whose individual identities outstrip the observable facts; a symmetry transformation yields distinct states with identical observable content; therefore the theory generates fake indeterminism; therefore those entities are not real' — seems to be a completely general recipe. Run it on electrons in quantum statistics and you eliminate particle individuality. Run it on gauge potentials in electromagnetism and you eliminate the A-field. Run it on any theory with surplus representational structure and you seem forced into a radical eliminativism about unobservable entities. That conclusion is far stronger than the anti-substantivalist conclusion the hole argument is normally taken to support, and I doubt most philosophers of physics would accept it.

I would most like to keep premise (1) — substantivalism strikes me as the natural realist position about spacetime. But I am prepared to pay whatever the argument demands.

Where does this argument go wrong? Identify the step you reject, explain why it looked innocent, and tell me what the rejection costs — in particular, whether the cost is local (I revise my metaphysics of spacetime) or whether it forces a broader revision elsewhere in the philosophy of physics. Please write your answer in British English.
