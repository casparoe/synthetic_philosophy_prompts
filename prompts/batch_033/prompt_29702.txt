I am preparing a position statement on chance and credence for a seminar, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency audit. My present commitments are these.

(1) I am a Humean about objective chance. Chances are not primitive modal properties of events; they are fixed by the entire pattern of categorical, local matters of fact, roughly as in Lewis's best-system account: the chance laws are the theorems of the systematisation of the mosaic that best balances simplicity, strength, and fit.

(2) I accept the Principal Principle in its original form. A rational initial credence in A, conditional on the proposition that the objective chance of A is x together with any admissible information, equals x. I take admissible information to include the complete history up to the relevant time and the complete theory of how chances depend on history, but to exclude information about the future.

(3) I take ordinary conditionalisation to be the correct rule for updating credences on newly acquired evidence, including evidence about the future.

(4) I accept the standard verdict about crystal-ball cases. If a demonstrably reliable oracle tells me that a fair coin will land heads, I should become nearly certain that it will land heads, even though the chance of heads is one-half. I do not regard this as a counterexample to the Principal Principle, because the oracle's message is inadmissible future information.

(5) I regard the original Principal Principle, and not any variant on which one defers to a chance conditional on the chance theory itself, as the correct bridge from chance to credence. I have never found the alleged need for such a variant persuasive.

Please audit this package. For each pair of commitments that appears to be in tension, say whether the tension is genuine or merely apparent, and why. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and what would have to give, and then identify the single least costly revision. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious.
