I'm drafting a novel in which one character attempts to persuade another to return to religious practice by repeatedly invoking some version of "What have you got to lose?" — the popular distillation of Pascal's wager. I need to understand what precise claims that phrase could be making, so I can decide which version my character actually holds and which my other character should dismantle in their reply. Could you lay out the distinct theses "What have you got to lose?" might stand for when offered as a reason for theistic belief — what decision matrix is being assumed, what is being presupposed about the voluntariness of belief, about the nature of divine judgment, and about the exclusivity of the relevant god-claim — and say for each reading whether it is defensible and whether it would survive the objections the other character might level? I would like the readings presented as a menu I can draw on while writing the argument scene.
