A friend of mine who's into the rationalist blogosphere tried to explain Roko's basilisk to me the other day, and I'm trying to figure out how much of what he said checks out. Here's roughly what he told me:

"It's this thought experiment from LessWrong where a future superintelligent AI decides it's going to punish anyone who knew about it but didn't help bring it into existence. The AI can't literally reach back in time, but it can create perfect simulations of past people and torture those simulations — and if you think a perfect simulation of you is conscious, then from your perspective right now, you have to worry that you might already be in one of those simulations. So just learning about the basilisk traps you: now you have a reason to help build the AI, to avoid being tortured by it. That's why LessWrong treats the basilisk as a legit AI risk scenario and why it's considered an information hazard — the knowledge itself harms you."

I've heard this idea is widely misunderstood and that even the people who originally discussed it have complicated feelings about it. What in this summary is accurate, what's garbled or wrong, and is the framing of it as an information hazard and a genuine AI risk scenario something the LessWrong community would actually endorse?
