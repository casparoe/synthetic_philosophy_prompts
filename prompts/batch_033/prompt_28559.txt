Consider a one-shot symmetric Prisoner's Dilemma between two players who know each other to be perfect logicians, with common knowledge of the payoff matrix, the symmetry of their situation, and each other's reasoning abilities. The following are candidate principles for deciding what to do in such a situation:

1. Causal Decision Theory (CDT): Since the other player's move is causally independent of yours, defection strictly dominates cooperation regardless of what the other does.
2. Evidential Decision Theory (EDT): Your cooperation is strong evidence that the other player cooperates (since you are in symmetric situations and both perfect logicians), so cooperating maximizes expected utility.
3. Superrationality (Hofstadter): You and the other player are relevantly identical — you will, by the nature of the situation, arrive at the same answer. Therefore the only stable outcomes are (C, C) and (D, D), and (C, C) is better, so you should cooperate.
4. Team Reasoning (Bacharach, Sugden): The question is not "what should I do?" but "what should we do?" Since (C, C) is the best collective outcome, each player should do their part in achieving it.
5. Kantian Universalization: Act only on a maxim you could will as a universal law. If everyone defected, cooperation would be impossible; therefore cooperate.

Please rank these five principles from most to least normatively compelling as a guide to action in the described situation, and assign each a score from 0 to 10. For each principle, give a one- or two-sentence justification of its placement and score that engages with at least one specific objection to it.
