Since the knowledge norm of assertion states that one is properly situated to assert that p only if one knows that p, and knowledge is factive, it seems to follow that asserting a false proposition always violates the norm of assertion. But consider a physician in 1850 who asserts, on the basis of the best medical evidence and methods then available, that miasma causes cholera. Her assertion is false, so she did not know it, and the knowledge norm therefore classifies her assertion as improper. Yet it seems counterintuitive to say she violated any epistemic norm: she was proceeding exactly as a responsible epistemic agent should. Does this kind of case show that the knowledge norm conflates the objective propriety of an assertion with the speaker's subjective justification, and that we should therefore adopt a weaker norm, such as justified belief or reasonable belief?
