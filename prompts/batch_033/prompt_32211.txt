Look, I'm in a department where the causation folks have split into two camps and they've been going at it for years — the Lewisians who think causation is basically counterfactual dependence, and the interventionists (Woodward, Pearl) who think you need to talk about interventions on variables and causal models. We want to do a proper adversarial collaboration instead of another round of the same seminar-room fights. Can you draft the document we'd produce? I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign — about what the formalism requires, about symmetries both approaches respect, about which cases are easy for both; (2) a disagreements table where each row is a precise claim one side affirms and the other denies, worded so each side would say "yeah, that's what I think"; (3) the cruxes you think are actually driving the disagreement. I suspect one crux is whether preemption cases show counterfactual dependence isn't necessary for causation, and another is whether causation is fundamentally world-level or always relative to a variable set. A third might be whether the interventionist is doing metaphysics at all or just giving us an epistemology of causal inference. If you think some of the fight is just about words, say which part.
