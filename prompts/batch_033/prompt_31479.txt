Alfred North Whitehead argued that education often goes wrong by filling students’ minds with inert ideas, while he also proposed that effective learning follows a rhythm of romance, precision, and generalisation. Compare these two aspects of his educational philosophy. In particular, evaluate whether the three-phase rhythm is best read as Whitehead’s positive solution to the problem of inert ideas, or whether the diagnosis of inert ideas and the account of the learning rhythm target different aspects of education. Does this comparison reveal any tension within Whitehead’s view about the relationship between the content of instruction and the temporal structure of learning? Argue for a specific interpretive position and show its implications for how a teacher should sequence material.
