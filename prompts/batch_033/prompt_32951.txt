I'm on the board of our housing co-op — about 40 units, a mix of young families and retirees, been around since the 80s. We spend half our meetings arguing about capital projects: roof or boiler this year, how much to put into the community garden, whether solar panels actually pay off. Everyone's got a take, and I've noticed that the person who speaks last and sounds most confident tends to swing the vote, which is a terrible way to make hundred-thousand-dollar decisions. A while back I stumbled on this idea called futarchy — the basic pitch is that instead of voting on policies, you vote on a metric you care about (like "average member satisfaction with the building two years from now"), and then you run a prediction market where people bet on which policy will score highest on that metric. The market picks the winner. It sounds kind of out there but also like it might actually cut through the noise.

I brought it up at last month's board meeting and got a mixed reaction: a few people were intrigued, a couple just laughed, and one guy said he didn't move into a co-op to turn it into a hedge fund. Fair enough. But we've got our annual general meeting in about two months and I want to formally propose that we pilot this — just for one decision, maybe the solar panel question, as a six-month experiment with real money on the line (small stakes, like each member puts in $20). I need to draft a memo to circulate to the board first and then to the full membership. It should explain futarchy in plain terms, recommend the pilot, and lay out the actual mechanics: how would we run a prediction market with forty people who aren't traders, what's the market actually resolving to, how do we measure the outcome metric so nobody can game it. It also needs to take the strongest objection seriously — I think that's the worry about turning neighbors into speculators and undermining the cooperative ethos — and actually engage with it, not just wave it away. Oh, and I want the memo to say how confident it is in its own recommendation and single out the one point it's least sure about. I don't want to oversell this and then have it blow up in everyone's face.
