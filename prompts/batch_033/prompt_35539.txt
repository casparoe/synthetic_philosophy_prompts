The Mary's room argument has been going since the early 80s and I honestly can't tell if we've made any progress. Everyone knows the setup: Mary the neuroscientist knows all the physical facts about color vision but has never seen red, and when she steps out she learns something new — what it's like to see red. The physicalists say she doesn't learn a new fact, just a new ability or a new way of representing old facts. The anti-physicalists say no, she learns a fact, so physicalism is false. And round and round we go.

What's weird to me is that both sides seem to agree on what would happen in the scenario. Nobody disputes that Mary would say "wow, so that's what red looks like." The fight is entirely about how to describe what just happened — is it fact-learning or ability-acquiring or acquaintance or what. So my working theory is that this isn't really a disagreement about the mind at all; it's a disagreement about what counts as a "fact" and what "physical" means, and those terms are doing different work for different people. If that's right, the debate is largely verbal and we should stop expecting it to resolve. But I'm not sure — maybe there's a hidden empirical claim in there somewhere, or a commitment one side has that the other doesn't share about what an explanation has to do.

Diagnose this for me. What kind of disagreement is this actually? And if you think it's substantive, what would move it? If you think it's verbal, is there a reformulation that would make it substantive?
