I'm a novelist writing a book set across several worlds in which recognisably the same mental life — grief, deliberation, amusement — is implemented in very different substrates: a human brain, a head-sized silicon network, a colonial organism whose members are individually mindless but whose colony as a whole seems to deliberate, and a creature whose nociception runs through hydraulic channels. Before I fix the book's metaphysics, I want the specification rather than a theory: what must an adequate account of the mind–body relation deliver, given that multiple realisability is the phenomenon it has to reckon with (whether as a datum to explain or as a claim that must be earned)? Give me the desiderata in list form — the cases it must get right or explain away, including the imagined system that passes every functional test yet lacks felt experience and the imagined system whose experiences are inverted; the distinctions it cannot collapse, including realisation versus identity, role versus occupant, type versus token, and the grain at which a realiser is individuated; and the constraints it must respect, such as not being so liberal that any input–output mimic counts as minded and not so chauvinist that only biological brains do — with the consideration behind each. Flag which are non-negotiable and which are merely one camp's preference. Then tell me which single desideratum does the most to exclude the obvious contenders, and whether any pair of desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. Finally, say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to demote one of the requirements, or where the list is most likely to fail.
