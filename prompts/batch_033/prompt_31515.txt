I'm working on a piece about the metaphysics of scientific laws, and I'd like your view in a specific format. What is your current credence — a number between 0 and 1 — that Humeanism about laws of nature is true, understood as the claim that the laws are not primitive, governing features of reality but rather the theorems of the best systematization of the entire mosaic of local, non-modal facts? Don't just give the number. Decompose the estimate into the sub-questions it actually turns on: for example, whether a best-system account can draw a principled line between laws and accidental regularities, whether a mere summary of the mosaic can underwrite counterfactuals and the explanatory force laws seem to have, whether appealing to perfectly natural properties quietly reintroduces non-Humean structure, and any other term you judge to carry weight. For each term, give a rough probability and a line of justification; state how you combine them; then say which single term your final credence is most sensitive to, and what argument or empirical finding would move that term the most.
