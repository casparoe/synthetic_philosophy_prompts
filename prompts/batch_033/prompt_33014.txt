I'm taking a graduate seminar on paradoxes and theories of truth, and last week we turned to Curry's paradox. I'd seen the Liar before, but Curry's feels different — it doesn't exploit negation, which means solutions that tinker with negation (paraconsistent logic, truth-value gaps, dialetheism) don't touch it. The argument seems to show that from three principles that look indispensable — the T-schema, conditional proof, and modus ponens — we can derive any arbitrary sentence whatsoever. Here's the argument as I've reconstructed it from our discussion:

(1) Let C abbreviate the sentence: "If C is true, then P," where P is any sentence you like (say, "the moon is made of cheese").
(2) Assume, for conditional proof, that C is true.
(3) From (2) and the T-schema, we get: if C is true, then P. (The T-schema tells us that C is true iff C, and C just is the sentence "if C is true, then P.")
(4) From (2) and (3), by modus ponens, we get P.
(5) Having discharged the assumption, we conclude: if C is true, then P. (Conditional proof.)
(6) But "if C is true, then P" is exactly what C says. So by the T-schema (the other direction this time), C is true.
(7) From (6) and (5), by modus ponens, we get P.
(8) Since P was arbitrary, we can derive anything.

I've been turning this over and I can't find the exit. The options seem to be: reject the T-schema (but then what does "true" mean, and what principle governs it?), reject conditional proof (but that's the basic move of mathematical reasoning — if you can derive B from assuming A, you can assert "if A then B"), reject modus ponens (unthinkable), or say that C doesn't express a proposition (but it's grammatically well-formed and its constituent words have their ordinary meanings — what else is required?). I'm most reluctant to give up conditional proof or modus ponens. I could live with restricting the T-schema, but I don't know how to do that in a principled way that doesn't just gerrymander around the paradox. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it force me to give up elsewhere?
