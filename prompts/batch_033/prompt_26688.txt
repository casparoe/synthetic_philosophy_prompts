So I've got this friend who's really into moral particularism — she's read her Dancy, she's always going on about how moral principles are a fantasy and every situation is just its own thing that you have to perceive correctly. She says anyone who tries to boil ethics down to rules is making a category mistake, the world doesn't work that way, etc.

Naturally I've been trying to catch her out by giving her cases and writing down her verdicts. Here's what I've collected so far:

- Lying to your spouse about an affair: wrong.
- Lying to the secret police about the dissidents hiding in your basement: not just okay, you'd be a monster not to.
- Blowing off a promise to help a friend move because you feel lazy: wrong.
- Blowing off the same promise because on the drive over you come across a car wreck and stop to pull someone from a burning vehicle: obviously fine.
- Stealing a candy bar from Walmart for the thrill of it: wrong.
- Stealing baby formula from Walmart because your kid is hungry, you're broke, and you've exhausted every other option: permissible.

She insists there's no rule unifying these, she's just "seeing what the situation calls for." But it sure looks to me like there's a pattern, and I'd bet money I can predict her next call before she makes it.

Here's the case I want to test her on next: you've been donating monthly to a local animal shelter — it's a personal commitment, no contract. After a while you look into the numbers and realize that the same amount of money donated to a malaria net charity would prevent something like fifty times as much suffering and death. Is it permissible to redirect the donation?

Before I actually spring this on her, I want to get clear on: what are the candidate rules that fit all six verdicts, which of them diverge on the charity case, and which one do I think she's actually running (even if she'd never admit it)? And if her existing verdicts genuinely underdetermine it — which I think they might — what's the one extra case I should ask her about that would settle it?
