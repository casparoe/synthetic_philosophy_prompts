ok so I'm a grad student putting together a term paper on diachronic norms for credences, and I'd like a consistency check on the package I've ended up with before I send it to my advisor. Here's roughly where I've landed.

(1) I'm a Bayesian in the usual sense: credences are probabilities, and when I genuinely gain new evidence I update by conditionalization. (2) I accept van Fraassen's Reflection principle: for any proposition P and any r, my current credence in P, conditional on my future self's credence in P being r, should itself be r—my future self is, in that limited sense, an expert I should defer to. (3) I think epistemic Ulysses contracts can be fully rational: if I predict that my future self will form or revise beliefs in a way I now regard as unreliable, I can rationally bind that future self now, precisely because I don't trust its judgment. For instance, I ask my partner not to discuss our relationship with me when I'm badly sleep-deprived, or I install app blockers because I know midnight-me draws wild conclusions from ambiguous texts. (4) I also think there are clear cases where I know in advance that my future credences will be worse than my current ones—fever, sleep deprivation, early dementia—and in those cases, if I somehow learned that tomorrow-me assigns 0.9 to P, I wouldn't move my current credence to 0.9; I'd treat it as noise. (5) I've been assuming that (1) and (2) are basically a package deal, that Reflection is just what conditionalization looks like from a diachronic angle, so anyone who accepts (1) is more or less comitted to (2) automatically.

Is this set jointly tenable? I want an audit, not a lecture: go through the pairs that are actually in tension, rank the tensions by severity, flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, and tell me which single revision would buy the most consistency at the lowest cost. I honestly don't know wether the whole thing is fine and the friction is just verbal, or whether one of these has to go—and if so, which.
