Write a villanelle about hinge epistemology as developed in Wittgenstein's *On Certainty*. The two refrains should capture the two poles of the hinge metaphor: one refrain should express that some propositions are exempt from doubt—they are the hinges on which the movement of inquiry turns—and the other should express that these propositions are not themselves known, justified, or grounded, but rather form the ungrounded scaffolding that makes knowledge and doubt possible in the first place. The villanelle's form—two refrains that alternate, each returning in the shadow of the other, and finally meeting in the closing couplet—should do the work of showing why hinge propositions are neither ordinary certainties nor mere assumptions: they are something that stands outside the traditional epistemological categories. An epistemology specialist should recognise the two refrains as tracking the central tension in *On Certainty*, and a reader unfamiliar with the work should finish the poem with a sense of why the problem of radical scepticism cannot be met with better arguments but requires a different picture of how our beliefs hang together.
