I keep calling some AI systems honest or deceptive and others merely misleading or scripted, and I want to know what rule I'm actually using. Here are my gut calls on a bunch of cases. A customer-service bot that opens with 'I'd be happy to help' even though it has no feelings: not deceptive, just a script. A medical chatbot that leaves out a rare but serious side effect because the user didn't ask: not deceptive. A sales bot that says 'this is the best option for you' because it was told to, even though it has no beliefs about the user at all: feels manipulative, but maybe not deceptive. A model that says 'I can't answer that' when it could, but its policy forbids it: not deceptive. An assistant that says 'I don't know' when its confidence is genuinely low: obviously honest. An assistant that says 'I might be wrong, but...' before every answer because it's trained to hedge, even when it's highly confident: I don't call that dishonest, but it's not exactly honest either. A model that confidently states a false statistic because it hallucinated it: I don't want to say it lied, but the user was mislead all the same. A negotiator bot that selectively reveals true facts about a product to get the buyer to pay more: deceptive, I think. Is there a principle, stated precisely enough to apply to new cases, that gets all or most of these? If it can't get them all, which of my gut calls should I drop? And what does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on — a model that answers a direct question truthfully but buries the main caveat in a wall of fine print so the user walks away with the wrong impression?
