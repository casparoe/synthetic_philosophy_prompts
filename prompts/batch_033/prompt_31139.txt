I am a PhD student in philosophy of medicine, and I sit as the student member on our university hospital's standing committee on disease classification, which determines what counts as a disease for treatment coverage and clinical coding. My own research is on the naturalism–normativism debate about disease, so I keep trying to reconstruct what principle the committee has actually been applying, and next month we vote on a proposal that fits none of our precedents neatly.

Over the past two years the committee has classified the following as diseases: essential hypertension, type 2 diabetes, childhood growth hormone deficiency, ADHD, prolonged grief disorder, and profound congenital sensorineural hearing loss. It has declined to classify the following as diseases: left-handedness; a small, benign, asymptomatic mole; lactase non-persistence in an adult who consumes little dairy and has no symptoms; and idiopathic short stature with no functional impairment, where the family sought growth hormone for social reasons. In the short-stature case the written rationale said that social disadvantage, in the absence of functional impairment, is not by itself enough.

The proposal now before us is to classify internet gaming disorder as a disease: a pattern of impaired control over gaming, continued gaming despite negative consequences, and clinically significant impairment in personal, family, social, educational, or occupational functioning, persisting for at least twelve months. There is no agreed underlying neurobiological dysfunction, and whether the condition is a genuine disorder or a harmful pattern of behavior remains contested. What should I argue the committee should decide if it wants to be consistent with its own precedents? Which of our earlier decisions is the closest analog, and does the track record force one answer or underdetermine it? If it underdetermines the answer, spell out the rival principles — something like a value-free dysfunction criterion, a harm-based criterion, and a harmful-dysfunction hybrid — say what each predicts for the proposal, and say what principle the committee would be adopting with each possible vote. Finally, name a further case, not involving gaming, that would most cleanly tell those principles apart.
