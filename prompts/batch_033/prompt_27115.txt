I've been going down a rabbit hole on this idea people call "tranquilism" and I keep running into summaries of it that don't quite line up with each other. The clearest one I heard was from a podcast, where the host laid it out like this:

"Tranquilism is the view that the only thing that ultimately matters is the absence of craving and suffering. It's basically Buddhism with the metaphysics stripped out: suffering comes from craving, so the best possible life is one with no desires at all. Emotions are the enemy here, because every emotion—hope, love, curiosity—is just a kind of wanting, and wanting is craving. And if you think a life with no desires sounds empty or miserable, that just shows how attached you are. The obvious problem is that the whole thing is self-defeating: you'd have to want to be tranquil in order to get there, which is itself a craving."

I've also seen a Buddhist forum where someone got annoyed at this kind of summary and said it conflates craving with wanting or intention, and that theres a whole distinction between tanha and chanda that the podcast is flattening. I'm not sure I trust the forum either, since they had their own axe to grind.

Could you go through the host's summary part by part and tell me what's fair, what's a caricature, and what a careful defender of tranquilism would actually say to each bit? In particular: does the "zero desires" conclusion follow from the real view, is the "every emotion is wanting" part as sloppy as it sounds, and is the self-defeat objection actually fatal or is it aimed at a strawman?
