I'm a retired entomologist — four decades working on stored-product beetles, mostly — and I've only recently started reading philosophy seriously. The thing I keep chewing on is the scale argument about insect sentience. As I understand it, the thought is: even if you'd only give it a small probability that insects have felt experience in a way that matters morally, the numbers are so enormous (somewhere in the neighborhood of a billion billion alive at any one time, give or take) that the expected amount of suffering at stake completely dwarfs a lot of the things people spend their moral energy on. I know enough physiology to know that a lot of what looks like pain behavior could be nociception without any felt badness; the argument seems designed to work even under that uncertainty. I've also seen people push back that the probabilities are to unsharp to do any real work, or that this is exactly the kind of fanatical expected-value reasoning we should refuse to engage in.
