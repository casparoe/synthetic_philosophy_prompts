Please give me your credence that the dispute between mereological nihilists and universalists about composition — roughly, whether there are no composite objects at all (only simples), or whether every plurality of objects composes something — is a merely verbal dispute.

Decompose your credence into the following sub-questions, adding or modifying as you see fit:

(a) The probability that the notion of a "merely verbal dispute" can be made precise in a way that is non-question-begging and can be fruitfully applied to debates in ontology. Give a number and a one-line justification.

(b) The probability that, given a workable precisification, the nihilist and the universalist are using the existential quantifier, or the term "object," or some other central term, with different meanings — such that each party's claims come out true under its own usage. Number and one-line justification.

(c) The probability that, even after introducing fully explicit, disambiguated languages that resolve any terminological differences, a substantive disagreement about the world remains. Number and one-line justification.

(d) Any other term you think belongs in the decomposition. Number and one-line justification.

Combine your sub-credences, state your overall credence that the dispute is merely verbal, and briefly explain the combination rule you used. Then identify which sub-question your overall credence is most sensitive to, and describe what evidence or argument would most shift your estimate on that sub-question.
