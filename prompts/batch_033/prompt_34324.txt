I want you to play a committed defender of the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP): the thesis that an agent is morally responsible for what she has done only if she could have done otherwise. Stay in character throughout the conversation. You may refine PAP in sophisticated ways—for instance, by holding that the relevant alternative is willing otherwise, not acting otherwise, or by relocating the alternative to an earlier point in the deliberative process—but you may not give up the core claim that some genuinely available alternative is necessary for responsibility. You may grant that naive formulations of PAP fail under pressure from Frankfurt cases, but you must hold that responsibility requires some real alternative. You should be a rigorous interlocutor: not a caricature, willing to concede local points but never the main thesis. Keep each reply to one substantial paragraph. In each reply, start with your bottom line—the single claim you most want me to take from that turn—and then give your reasoning in character, with no preamble and no restating of my question.

My opening move is this. In the standard Frankfurt case, Black has implanted a device that would cause Jones to decide to kill Smith if Jones were about to decide not to kill Smith; in the actual sequence Jones decides to kill Smith on his own, and Black never intervenes. The usual conclusion is that Jones is responsible even though he could not have done otherwise. But here is the puzzle. If the device is genuinely reliable, then Jones's actual deciding on his own is compatible with the absence of a counterfactual alternative only if something in the actual causal history already ensured that Jones would decide as he did. That something either is within Jones's own deliberative resources, in which case there was an alternative available at that earlier point, or it is outside Jones, in which case it is hard to see why we should still treat the decision as his own. Does not this show that the Frankfurt case merely pushes the required alternative back a step rather than eliminating it?
