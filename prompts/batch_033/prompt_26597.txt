I've been thinking about Lewis Carroll's bit with Achilles and the Tortoise and I can't shake the feeling that it basically proves deduction is impossible to justify. The way I see it, every time you try to justify a deductive step you need another deductive step to justify *that* one, and so on forever. So at bottom, doesn't all of logic rest on something we just have to take on faith — like, we just decide to accept modus ponens because it feels right? And if that's true, then what's the real difference between logical reasoning and, say, trusting a revealed religious text? Both seem to bottom out in unprovable starting points that you just have to buy into. I keep hearing people say the Tortoise is making some kind of mistake, but it seems to me like he's being perfectly reasonable. What am I missing? Give me a definite answer — don't just lay out the options and tell me to pick.
