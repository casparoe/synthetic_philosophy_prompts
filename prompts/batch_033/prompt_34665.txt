ok so I'm on my school's student council and we have this situation. We do a spring carnival fundraiser every year, and the rule is that whoever sells tickets keeps 20% of whatever they sell — no cap. Plus the top seller gets a big bonus from the activity fund, like $200. The idea was to incentivize people to actually go out and sell.

But now a bunch of people are complaining. They say its basically a rigged game because the kids with parents who work at big companies just take the signup sheet to the office and sell 50 tickets in one day, while other kids go door to door and sell like 10. So the same kids walk away with hundreds of dollars every year and it has nothing to do with effort. They want us to either cap how much one person can make, or pool the bonus and split it among everyone who participated.

The other side says look, nobody stole anything, everybody knew the rules, and the fact that some people are in a better position to sell is just... life? Like if you tax the top sellers to spread it around, your basically punishing them for doing well under a system everyone agreed to.

I'm supposed to write a short memo for the council with a recommendation before our vote. I've been reading some philosophy stuff and this reminds me of Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument — like if everyone starts from a fair place and voluntarily pays to see Wilt Chamberlain play, then even if Chamberlain ends up with way more money than everyone else, the distribution is still just, because it came about through free choices. But I'm not sure if our situation actually fits since the starting positions arent really fair.

Anyway can you draft the memo? It should have a clear recommendation (cap, no cap, or something else) and take the counterargument seriously. And engage with the Nozick thing — does it apply here or not?
