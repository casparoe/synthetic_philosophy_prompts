In most wealthy countries, prenatal screening for Down syndrome is routine and publicly funded, and the large majority of pregnancies that get a positive result are terminated. At the same time, these same countries loudly affirm the equal dignity and worth of people with Down syndrome who are already born, fund their support services, and would condemn any policy that harmed them. What theory of moral status or value does this combination of practices presuppose? Does it commit us to saying that a fetus and a born child have fundamentally different moral standing, or that it's fine to prevent disabled people from coming into existence while still valuing the ones who do, or something else? And is the combination actually coherent, or do the standard moves (the distinction between "this particular child" and "any child," the difference between acts and omissions, appeals to parental autonomy) just paper over a real tension?
