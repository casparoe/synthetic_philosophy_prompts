I have come to accept the view that the observed asymmetry of time — the difference between past and future in records, memories, causal efficacy, and control — is grounded in the thermodynamic arrow: the second law of thermodynamics together with a low-entropy boundary condition on the early universe. Tell me what would count against this. I want a map of the view's actual exposure, not just the familiar reversibility objection that the underlying microdynamics are time-symmetric: which observations, cosmological results, and formal results should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and which of them a committed defender of this account would concede count against it rather than mark off as open problems. In particular, I want to know whether the view makes any prediction that rival accounts do not — a fundamental temporal asymmetry in the laws, an arrow carried by radiation or cosmology rather than entropy, or the claim that the future arrow follows from typicality alone with no special boundary condition — and whether the low-entropy boundary condition does genuine explanatory work or only insulates the view from counterexample. Finally, say honestly whether the position, once its qualifications are made explicit, risks anything at all, and whether that matters for a view of this kind.
