Please argue that the concept of "jumping out of the system" (JOOTSing), as introduced by Douglas Hofstadter through the MU-puzzle in Gödel, Escher, Bach, provides a misleading picture of how genuine mathematical insight operates. Focus your argument on the following claim: the MU-puzzle works as an illustration precisely because there is a clean, surveyable isomorphism between the string-rewriting system and elementary number theory — specifically, properties of powers of two and divisibility by three — which allows the solver to step into a familiar meta-system and see the limitation from above. In cases of real mathematical difficulty, such as longstanding open problems, no such ready-made embedding into a familiar meta-system is available, and the metaphor of "jumping out" disguises the fact that one is simply relocating to a larger system whose own limitations and blind spots are just as opaque as those of the original. Conclude by considering whether JOOTSing, as a metaphor for insight, does more harm than good when exported beyond Hofstadter's carefully constructed pedagogical example.
