Write a dialogue between two metaphysicians leaving a workshop session on quantified modal logic. One, A, is a committed counterpart theorist of a Lewisian sort: no object exists in more than one possible world, so a claim like "this table might have had six legs" is made true by a counterpart of the table — a contextually selected, sufficiently similar table in another world — having six legs. The other, B, is an equally committed defender of literal transworld identity: the very same table exists in other worlds and has different properties in them, and what unites its appearances across worlds is an individual essence or haecceity rather than any similarity relation. Both characters should be sharp and equally resourceful; each may concede local points but must defend their central commitment, and neither should be a strawman.

Have them argue about whether de re modal predication is better understood through counterparts or through literal identity across worlds, with the six-legged table as their running example. Let B open by pressing the charge that the counterpart analysis turns a claim about this very table into a claim about some other table, and let A answer by defending the analysis and pressing the identity view about accidental intrinsics; after that, the exchanges should build on what has already been said rather than restarting.

Produce exactly five numbered exchanges. Each numbered item is one exchange, consisting of one speech from each character; each speech should be no more than two sentences. Give no introduction, no conclusion, and no narration or stage directions — only the five numbered exchanges.
