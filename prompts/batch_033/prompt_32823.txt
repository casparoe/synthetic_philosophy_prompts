I'm starting a PhD in social choice theory and I'm trying to figure out what to work on. The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem says any non-dictatorial voting rule for three or more candidates will be manipulable — someone can sometimes get a better outcome by lying about their preferences. So the classic result looks like a dead end for designing honest voting systems. But obviously people don't just give up and go home; there are all these escape routes people have explored — restricting the domain to single-peaked preferences, relaxing strategy-proofness to something like 'hard to manipulate' computationally, looking at what happens when voters are uncertain or when equilibria matter rather than dominant strategies. What I want to know is: which of these escape routes is actually most worth spending a dissertation on? I'm not looking for a survey; I want a defended ranking. Which open problems are most upstream of other work, which are tractable in three to four years, and which would change the most about how we think about real-world voting if they got resolved? And are there neglected lines of attack that aren't getting enough attention?
