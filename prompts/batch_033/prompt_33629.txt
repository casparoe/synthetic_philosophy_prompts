I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a friend about whether the concept of "Moloch" — as it circulates in certain rationalist and effective altruist circles — actually picks out anything that isn't already captured by the prisoner's dilemma or the tragedy of the commons. My friend's position was blunt: "It's just a rebranding. Individual rationality leads to collective disaster — that's the PD. Why do we need a spooky name for it?" I pushed back, pointing to examples like arms races, positional goods, and multipolar AI dynamics, where the failure mode doesn't seem to reduce to a simple binary choice between cooperating and defecting. In these cases, each agent takes a step that is locally rational given what everyone else is doing, but the resulting equilibrium is worse for everyone than the starting point — and the process unfolds gradually, without anyone needing to "defect" in the classic sense. My friend conceded that the dynamics might feel different, but insisted that the underlying logic is still just an n-player PD with continuous action spaces, and that the solution is the same in principle: binding coordination or regulation.

By the end of the argument, I found myself unable to articulate what, if anything, the Moloch framing genuinely adds. If these traps are just multiplayer games with perverse incentives, then the standard remedies — communication, iteration, enforceable contracts, institutional design — should apply, and calling the problem "Moloch" seems like mere atmospherics. But the people who write about Moloch and multipolar traps seem to think they are pointing at something more recalcitrant, something that standard coordination theory doesn't fully address. So here is what I'm left wondering: is there a structural feature of Moloch scenarios that distinguishes them from generic collective action problems, or is the concept doing rhetorical work rather than analytic work? And if there is a genuine distinction, what does it imply about the adequacy of our usual coordination mechanisms?
