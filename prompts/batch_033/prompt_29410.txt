"John Stuart Mill argued that the only justification for restricting anyone's speech or action is to prevent harm to others. If you're only harming yourself, the state has no business interfering — that's the 'self-regarding' sphere where liberty is absolute. And when it comes to speech in particular, Mill held that even offensive, hateful, or deeply wrongheaded views must be allowed, because the only alternative is giving the state the power to decide what's true, which is worse than any individual piece of bad speech. The harm principle therefore draws a bright line: speech can be banned only when it directly and demonstrably causes physical injury to someone else, like shouting 'fire' in a crowded theatre. Everything short of that — including hate speech, misinformation, and verbal abuse — falls on the protected side of the line."

Could you go through this summary and tell me which parts a careful Mill scholar would accept, which parts they'd say are misleading or conflate things Mill kept distinct, and whether the overall picture — that Mill's principle draws a bright line at physical harm and protects everything else — is a fair characterisation of what he actually argued?
