Since Sartre holds that freedom is inescapable — the for-itself is not determined by its past, its roles, or its situation, and we cannot not be free — I do not see how bad faith can be a failure of freedom. If the project of fleeing one's freedom is itself freely chosen, then the person in bad faith is exactly as free as the person who lucidly assumes their freedom; the two differ only in what they choose, and radical freedom seems to leave no standard by which to call one choice a failure and the other an achievement. Is bad faith, then, just Sartre's label for choices he disapproves of, with the machinery of transcendence and facticity doing no real work? If not, what exactly does the distinction between bad faith and authenticity mark, given that both are free projects?
