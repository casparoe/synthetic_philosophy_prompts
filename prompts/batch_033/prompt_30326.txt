I run a small methods unit inside a central bank, and I have one researcher's time for the next two years to spend on foundational work about the assumptions in our models. The economists I work with build and use models whose assumptions they know are unrealistic — frictionless markets, a representative agent, rational expectations — and they genuinely disagree about how much this matters. The debate keeps returning to Friedman's F-twist: the idea that unrealistic assumptions are no defect in a model as long as the model predicts well enough. I want the researcher's work to actually change how our staff and our policy committees think about this, not to survey the field. Give me a defended ordering of the open questions or lines of work in this area that the researcher should take up. For each candidate, explain why it is upstream of other debates, how tractable it is for someone with philosophical training rather than econometric training, and whether it is crowded or neglected. Then tell me what you would need to see over the next two years to move an item up or down the list. I want the reasoning behind the ranking, not just the list. Finally, flag the part of your ordering you are least confident in: which argument or finding would most change your ranking, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail?
