I've been trying to get my head around Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment" and honestly I'm not seeing what the big deal is. As far as I can tell, his whole argument is: look, we're just wired to feel resentment and gratitude and all that — we can't actually drop the reactive attitudes, even if we try, because they're baked into how we relate to each other. And from this he concludes that determinism isn't a threat to moral responsibility. But that seems like a total non sequitur? Like, the fact that I can't psychologically stop believing something doesn't make it true or justified. If I'm constitutionally incapable of not believing that people have astral essences, that doesn't mean astrology is suddenly a sound practice. So how is this supposed to be a contribution to the free will debate and not just a fancy way of changing the subject?
