I am a philosophy professor, and next week I must give one fifty-minute lecture to a first-year introductory ethics course on the question whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible. Do not write the lecture; give me a teaching plan. The students will arrive with only a rough distinction between right and wrong and some exposure to consequentialism and Kantianism. Plan the order in which the ideas must be introduced and say what depends on what: in particular, where the difference between a conflict of obligations and an all-things-considered verdict has to be drawn; where ought implies can and the agglomeration principle (if you ought to do A and ought to do B, then you ought to do A-and-B) have to enter so that the argument that dilemmas are impossible does not appear as a mere technicality; and how the argument from moral remainders — that a conflict can leave a residue of unmet obligation that a merely overridden consideration would not — should be built up before the deflationary replies to it are given. Choose one carrying example to run through the whole lecture, and say what it must have and must not have; if you think the obvious candidates, such as the trolley problem or Sophie's Choice, are unsuitable for this purpose, say why. Identify the misconceptions the students are most likely to bring or to form — I expect at least these, but correct me where I am wrong: that any hard choice is a dilemma; that not knowing what to do is a dilemma; that feeling guilty afterwards proves there was a genuine dilemma; and that if both options are obligatory then it does not matter which one is chosen. For each, say exactly where in the sequence it should be headed off rather than postponed. End with one question I can put to the class that would show whether they have grasped the difference between a tragic trade-off and a genuine dilemma, rather than merely learned the vocabulary. Finally, tell me what a rigorous treatment would include that I should deliberately set aside at this level, and say how I should announce that I am setting it aside.
