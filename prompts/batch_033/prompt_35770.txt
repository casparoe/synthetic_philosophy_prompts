I chair the review panel of a private foundation that funds behavioral research. Two proposals this cycle request support to study the same species' foraging decisions. The first proposes to model the animals from the intentional stance, attributing goals, preferences, and expectations and testing hypotheses about what the animals want and believe. The second proposes to model the same behavior from the physical and design stances, explaining it in terms of neural and physiological mechanisms and the functions those mechanisms serve, with no appeal to mental states. Panelists disagree sharply. Some argue that the intentional-stance model captures real, predictive patterns in the animals' behavior and deserves full funding; others argue that its mentalistic vocabulary is at best a useful heuristic and at worst a reification of fictions, and that only the physical/design model is scientifically defensible. Draft the panel's decision memo for this funding cycle, recommending whether to fund the first proposal, the second, both, or neither. The memo should state a clear bottom line, give reasons the foundation can act on, and engage the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly.
