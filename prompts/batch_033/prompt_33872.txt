I want a decision tree that sorts someone into a position on the nature of conscious experience, using the Cartesian theater debate as the trunk split. The tree should take a reader from the starting acknowledgment that they have conscious experiences to a named position, with each branch node being a question about a concrete case or an empirical claim they can answer without knowing the philosophical labels. The diagnostic work should be done by the cases, not by asking people which "-ism" they prefer.

The first split should be this: do you think there is a place in the brain, or a moment in time, where conscious experience comes together — where the outputs of disparate processing streams are integrated into a single, unified representation that is the conscious experience itself? If yes, the tree leads toward positions that retain something like a Cartesian theater. If no, it leads toward distributed, deflationary, or eliminativist views.

After that, I want the tree to use cases like the following (and others you think are diagnostic):

- The color phi phenomenon: two differently colored dots are flashed in quick succession at different locations. Subjects report seeing a single moving dot that changes color mid-trajectory — and they report the color change as occurring before the second dot actually appears. What does the timing of the reported experience imply about when and where the conscious percept is constructed?
- Binocular rivalry: incompatible images are presented to each eye, and conscious perception alternates between them, with intermediate states of patchwork blending. If there is a single "theater" where the conscious percept plays, what determines which image is on stage at a given moment, and what is happening to the suppressed image?
- Change blindness: subjects fail to notice large changes in a visual scene when the change coincides with a brief disruption like a saccade, a blink, or a flicker. They are confident they would have noticed such a change, yet they did not. What does this say about the richness and detail of the conscious representation that is supposedly sitting in the theater?
- The Libet readiness potential: a characteristic brain signal precedes the reported conscious intention to move by several hundred milliseconds. Is the conscious intention simply misdated, or does the finding challenge the idea that conscious intentions are the kind of thing that can be pinned to a moment at all?

Each leaf should be a named position in philosophy of mind or cognitive neuroscience. I expect the tree to include at least the following leaves, though you may add others if the logical space demands them: Cartesian materialism (the view that there is a literal place and time where consciousness happens), the multiple drafts model, global workspace theory, higher-order thought theory, illusionism about phenomenal consciousness, and some form of panpsychism or property dualism. For each leaf, give a one-sentence description of the position and a one-sentence note on its most awkward commitment — the empirical finding or conceptual problem it handles least comfortably.

Also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, and explain what structural feature of the views makes them land on the same answers to every diagnostic question you have. If there are branches of the tree no one defends — unoccupied regions of logical space — note them and briefly say whether the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

The audience is readers of a rationalist-adjacent blog: people comfortable with thought experiments and neuroscientific findings but not necessarily trained in philosophy of mind. The tree should have roughly six to ten leaves and no more than four or five branch nodes on any path from root to leaf.
