I'm working on a sci-fi zine with some friends and we're doing an issue themed around "downward causation" — the idea that higher-level patterns can reach back down and boss around the stuff they're made of. We got into this whole rabbit hole about Dennett's careenium and simmballs thought experiments. The careenium is this imaginary vehicle that moves forward by converting the spinning of an internal flywheel into a rocking motion, but the whole thing runs on friction — the flywheel spins because the vehicle is being pushed forward by the road, and the vehicle is pushed forward because the flywheel is spinning. It's a weird loop, and the question is whether you can actually say the "design" of the thing is pushing the atoms around, or whether it's just atoms doing atom stuff all the way down. And the simmballs are a simulated ping-pong game in a computer — at the hardware level it's just electrons, but at the software level there are balls and paddles and scores. When the simulated ball "hits" the simulated paddle, does that simulated event, like, *do* anything to the electrons? Or is the causation all bottom-up, with the electrons just happening to be in states that perfectly mirror the fiction of a ball and a paddle?

We thought it'd be fun to comission a short story that dramatizes this in some way. The story could be about, I dunno, a researcher who builds a simulation and starts to suspect the simulation's internal logic is reaching back and altering the hardware — maybe a simulated character becomes aware of the simulation and starts manipulating the physics of the computer running it. Or it could go the other way: a story about a character who becomes convinced that the "symbols" in their own life (their beliefs, their plans, their identity) are actually just epiphenomenal froth and the substrate is running the show. Or it could be set in a world where the careenium principle is an actual engineering technology — vehicles that work by circular causality, buildings that hold themselves up through a kind of semantic trick. The tone can be whatever — hard sf, absurdist, horror, whatever fits the idea. The main thing is the story should make the reader feel the vertigo of the question: do the patterns push the particles around, or are the patterns just the particles' way of keeping score?
