Last night a friend and I spent way too long on this. He'd seen a film I hadn't, and when I said "well, everyone says it's great," he shot back that I couldn't actually hold the opinion that it's good without seeing it myself. Fine. But the same guy will happily tell me a restaurant is overpriced, a car is reliable, a hike is worth the drive, and I'll just believe him; he wouldn't say I lack the right to believe the hike is worth the drive because I haven't done it. So it's not a general rule about only trusting your own experience. It seems specific to matters of taste — art, music, food, wine. And it's weirdly asymmetrical, because we both lean on other people's taste all the time (I've picked plenty of movies off a friend's say-so), but somehow that only licenses "I've heard it's good," never "it's good."

Is there an established name for this — the idea that judgments about, say, beauty or artistic value have to be based on first-hand aquaintance in a way ordinary factual judgments don't? And if there is a name, I don't just want the label; I want to know exactly what the principle is supposed to cover, where its edges are (does it apply to a wine expert telling you a bottle is corked? to a music teacher telling you a piece is worth studying?), and wether it's really a point about knowledge or just a conversational norm. Also, when you answer, tell me how confident you are in the standard story, and flag the point you're least sure of.
