Until recently I would have described myself as a settled atheist. I find the standard arguments against the existence of a personal God convincing, and I do not think there is good evidence for an afterlife. But during the first months after my father’s death, I repeatedly caught myself half-addressing him in my mind, as though he could still hear me, and I found that this made it easier to get through ordinary tasks. The relief felt real, even though the belief did not. A clinical supervisor suggested that I was slipping back into an infantile pattern: missing my father and converting that need into a comforting fantasy of his continued presence. I can see the force of that description, but I also notice that the behavior has faded as the grief has become less acute, and I did not become any more inclined to join a church or to affirm metaphysical claims. Should I regard these episodes as a regression to be worked through and eliminated, or as a tolerable piece of private consolation that is compatible with a secular outlook?
