A colleague in AI safety argues that mind uploading via gradual neuron replacement preserves personal identity, and they lean heavily on the Ship of Theseus as their central analogy: replace planks one at a time, and the ship remains the same ship throughout; replace neurons one at a time with functionally equivalent artificial components, and the person remains the same person throughout. From this they conclude that gradual uploading avoids the copy problem that plagues instantaneous scan-and-transfer approaches. I want to audit this analogy thoroughly. What corresponds to what — the planks, the ship, the harbor, the voyage, the ship's name? Where does the mapping break? Which disanalogies matter for the conclusion about personal identity, and which are merely superficial? Does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is there a better analogy that captures what is really at stake in gradual replacement?
