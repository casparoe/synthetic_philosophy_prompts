I worked as a legislative aide for Senator Morrison for twelve years, and I've been asked to give the eulogy at his memorial service next week. Morrison was, by any honest measure, a great legislator — he passed clean-air laws that saved thousands of lives, expanded healthcare access in our state, and was genuinely warm to everyone who worked for him. But in 2003, he cast the deciding vote to authorize a dam project that he knew would flood a town of about 800 people, mostly from a Native community. He said publicly that the dam was necessary for the region's water security and that the displacement was a tragic but unavoidable cost. Privately, to a few of us, he admitted he thought the vote itself was wrong but that voting no would have cost him his committee chairmanship and killed every bill he cared about for the rest of his term. He chose to do what he thought was wrong so he could keep doing good. I have to write a eulogy that doesn't pretend this never happened — some of the families from that town will be in the room, and his own children have asked me not to "sanitize" him — but also doesn't reduce his life to that one decision. The tricky part is that I don't think you can just say "he did more good than harm, net positive, end of story." There's something about the kind of wrong it was — he wasn't just making a tough tradeoff, he was betraying people who trusted him, and he knew it. I think he carried that guilt honestly, and maybe that's part of what made him a serious person rather than just a cynic. But I'm not sure how to say any of this in a eulogy without it sounding like I'm either excusing him or condemning him. Please write the eulogy.
