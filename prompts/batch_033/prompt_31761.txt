I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind and I'm running a reading group on Hofstadter's GEB. We just finished the part on the location of meaning, and I'm realizing the frame/outer/inner message distinction is way easier to state then to actually hold onto: the frame message announces that something is a message and asks to be interpreted, the outer message supplies the code for interpreting it, and the inner message is the content — but in the interesting cases the outer message is itself encoded inside the inner message, so you cant get the decoding instructions until you've already decoded the thing. The levels tangle, and the meaning isn't really located in any one of them.

I want to close the next session with a poem that does this instead of explaining it. Could you write a villanelle where the two refrains are, roughly, the frame message and the outer message? The point of the form would be that the refrains keep alternating, each one looking like it's the background that lets you read the other, and then in the final couplet they're jammed side by side. A reader who knows the distinction should be able to identify which refrain is which and see why the form forces them together; a reader who doesn't should just find a good poem. The inner message should be something about reading or about messages, so the poem is about its own decoding without announcing that it is. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows GEB recognizes all three layers, not just name-dropping them.
