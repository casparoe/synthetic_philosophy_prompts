I am preparing a position paper on the moral force of advance directives and would welcome an audit of the following set of commitments, all of which I am currently inclined to hold. I find each one individually attractive, but I want to know whether they can all be held together, and if not, exactly where the strain lies.

1. An advance directive made by a competent adult is a genuine exercise of that person's autonomy and should generally bind those who care for her later, even when she can no longer confirm or ratify it.

2. Autonomy attaches to the person, not to a time-slice; it is therefore a mistake to treat the "earlier self" and the "later self" as two distinct parties whose interests could simply be balanced against one another.

3. If the person remains competent later, she may revoke or amend the directive, and her later expressed wishes should then take precedence over the earlier document.

4. Very severe dementia can alter a person enough that her earlier values and commitments no longer have the same authority over what it is to respect her now.

5. When a patient with advanced dementia lacks capacity but is content and expresses preferences about daily care that conflict with her directive, those present preferences deserve some independent weight in the decision, rather than being dismissed as mere noise.

6. A patient's present well-being is not reducible to the satisfaction of her earlier plans; a later life that departs from those plans may still be a good life for the person living it.

7. Respecting an advance directive is fundamentally a matter of respecting the earlier competent choice, and not merely a matter of predicting what the later person would have wanted or of maximising her happiness.

Please do not respond with a general introduction to the topic. Instead, I would like you to: (a) identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, explaining why in each case; (b) rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious; (c) for each genuine tension, state the least costly revision and what it would cost; and (d) indicate which single revision would do the most to restore coherence across the set as a whole. Please answer in British English.
