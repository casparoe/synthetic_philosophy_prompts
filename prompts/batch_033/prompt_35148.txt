I'm a novelist, so I spend my days building the insides of other people's heads—what they notice, what they dont, how a memory surfaces, how a decision feels before its made. I read Dennett's *Consciousness Explained* a while back and the multiple drafts model has basically become my working picture: there's no central theater where experience gets staged, no single place where it all comes together, just parallel streams of content under constant revision, and what we call consciousness is whatever draft happens to be the most influential at that moment. I find it compelling and it's changed how I write interiority. But I also know I'm a sucker for a well-told story and Dennett is a hell of a storyteller. So: what would actually count against the multiple drafts model? Not just the standard objections I've already heard (the "then who reads the drafts?" thing, or the claim that it denies experience exists), but the evidence, the thought experiments, the empirical results that should make me worry. I want them ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know whether the model has been qualified in ways that make it unfalsifiable—and if it has, whether that matters for a view of this kind. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, no preamble and no restating the question.
