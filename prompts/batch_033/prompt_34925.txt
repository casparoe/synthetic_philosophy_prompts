I'm trying to wrap my head around the difference between Lewis's counterpart theory and the standard transworld identity view for de re modal claims. I get the basic idea: on transworld identity, "Humphrey could have won" means there's a world where Humphrey himself wins; on counterpart theory, it means Humphrey has a counterpart in another world who wins, but it's not literally Humphrey — it's someone similar enough who stands in the counterpart relation to him.

What I'm stuck on is this: if the counterpart relation is based on similarity, and similarity is vague and context-sensitive, then doesn't that make the truth of modal claims vague and context-sensitive too? Wether "Humphrey could have won" is true would depend on which similarity metric we pick, which seems like a crazy result. Kripke's complaint was that Humphrey doesn't care about some other guy winning — but the vagueness problem seems even worse, no?

So which theory gives a better account of de re modal statements about ordinary objects, all things considered? And is the vagueness objection to counterpart theory fatal, or is there a reasonable reply?
