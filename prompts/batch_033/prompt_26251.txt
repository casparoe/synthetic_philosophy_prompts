The story makes this wierdly literal: the whole city's happiness depends on a single child locked in a basement, and everyone knows it. The child is spatially inside the city but politically outside it — not a beneficiary, not a participant, just a condition. But I don't want to reduce the story to an allegory, and I'm worried the fit isn't perfect — the child isn't exactly a foreigner or a non-citizen in the usual sense, and the ones who walk away don't form a new demos, they just leave.
