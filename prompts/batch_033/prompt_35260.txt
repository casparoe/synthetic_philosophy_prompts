My school's philosophy club has a faculty advisor who runs a weekly discussion, and this semester we have been working through the topic of punishment and moral responsibility. Each week she presents us with a case and gives her verdict on whether the person "deserves punishment" — she is careful to distinguish this from whether punishment would be useful, prudent, or practically justified. Here are her verdicts so far:

1. A man who spent months planning and then carried out the murder of a business rival for financial gain. Verdict: deserves severe punishment.

2. A woman who, after years of physical abuse, killed her husband during a violent confrontation — the act was not premeditated but occurred in a moment of terror and rage. Verdict: deserves punishment, but substantially reduced.

3. A person experiencing severe psychosis who assaulted a stranger under the delusion that the stranger was a demon. Verdict: does not deserve punishment; requires psychiatric treatment.

4. A teenager who, on a dare from friends, spray-painted graffiti on a school building. Verdict: deserves some punishment, but lenient given age and peer pressure.

5. A pharmaceutical executive who knowingly concealed evidence that a drug caused serious harm, affecting thousands of patients over a decade, for the sake of profits. Verdict: deserves very severe punishment.

Next week's case is this: at age 19, a man committed an armed robbery. He was never caught. Now, at age 45, he has lived an entirely blameless life — steady employment, community volunteering, raising a family. The crime has just come to light through a DNA match. Does he deserve punishment?

I can see at least two principles that fit all five of her verdicts so far, and I suspect they give opposite answers on this new case. I am less interested in simply predicting her answer than in understanding the situation: (a) what are the distinct principles that could be generating her verdicts, (b) what would each principle say about the new case, (c) which principle do you think she is more likely running and why, and (d) what further case would cleanly settle which principle she holds?
