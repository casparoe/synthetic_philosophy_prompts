ok so I'm starting a postdoc on embedded agency and I have about two years to sink into one or two open problems before I need to have something to show for it. I know the big picture: standard agent foundations assumes a Cartesian boundary where agent and environment are cleanly separated, but real agents — including any AI we build — are embedded in and made of the same stuff as their environment. Once you take that seriously a bunch of the pillars of decision theory and epistemology get shaky. But I'm having trouble figuring out what to actually work on, because the literature feels like a bunch of deep problems all tangled together and it's not obvious which are upstream of which.

Here's my rough map of the territory, correct me if I'm missing something important:

(1) Logical uncertainty. Embedded agents can't be logically omniscient; the Bayesian framework assumes they are. So we need some theory of how to reason under logical non-omniscience. There's the logical induction work, but it's not clear to me how much of the rest of embedded agency depends on getting this sorted first versus how much you can bracket it.

(2) Self-modeling / reflection. The agent's world-model has to include a representation of itself, since it's part of the world. But the agent is presumably simpler than the world, and its self-model sits inside its world-model, which sits inside the world, and there's a looming infinite regress or paradox. How much does this actually constrain things in practice, and is it tractable to make progress on or is it one of those problems that eats careers?

(3) Decision theory for embedded agents. If the agent and environment aren't cleanly separated, the whole act/state/outcome partition gets blurry. Does this force us toward something like functional decision theory or updatelessness, or are existing decision theories basically fine with the right patches? And if we need new decision theory, how much does that depend on having logical uncertainty sorted first?

(4) Naturalized semantics. How an embedded agent's internal states come to be about things in the world at all — the symbol grounding problem but for agents that have to learn and act. Is this a prerequisite for anything else or can you just black-box it and press on?

(5) Multi-agent embeddedness. The fact that there are other agents embedded in the same world, and you have to model them while being one of them yourself. Does this add qualitatively new problems or is it basically the single-agent case iterated?

For someone with my background — philosophy PhD with enough math to read the logical induction paper but not enough to extend it — which one or two of these (or something else) should I actually work on? Give me the reasoning: which are upstream of others, which are tractable in a couple years, and which ones would change the downstream picture the most if we made progress on them. Also, what would make me pivot? Like, if someone publishes a solid solution to X this year, does that free me up to work on Y or does it just shift the bottleneck?

And please separate what's basically consensus among people working on embedded agency from what's your own take. If everyone agrees logical uncertainty is the bottleneck, I want to know that. If there's a real split on whether we even need new decision theory, say so. I don't want to find out six months in that I've been working on something that everyone in the field already thinks is a dead end or a solved problem.
