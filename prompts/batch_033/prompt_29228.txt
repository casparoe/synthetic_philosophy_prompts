I'm a retired geologist — spent forty years in the field and the lab — and I've gotten into philosophy of science recently. Kuhn, Lakatos, that whole crew. Something keeps bothering me and I'm wondering if it has a name.

Here's the pattern I keep seeing, both in my own field and in others I read about. A research program is chugging along fine — still making predictions, still solving its puzzles. Then a new approach comes along that doesn't so much contradict the old one as make it irrelevant. The new approach asks questions the old one couldn't even formulate, not because the old one was wrong but because its concepts didn't carve the world that way. The old guard keeps working, but the grad students all flock to the new thing. Twenty years later the old approach is a historical footnote, and people talk as if it was "superceded," but nobody ever actually showed it was false. It just stopped being interesting.

I saw this in my own field with the shift from descriptive regional geology to plate tectonics, but I've also noticed it in biology — classical natural history didn't get refuted by molecular methods, it just stopped being where the action was. Same thing, I think, with certain parts of psychology and the rise of neuroscience.

This doesn't quite fit Kuhn's picture — he emphasizes anomaly, crisis, the old paradigm failing to solve its puzzles. What I saw was a paradigm that was solving its puzzles just fine, but the puzzles themselves stopped mattering to the next generation. It also doesn't seem like Lakatos's "degenerating research program," because the old program was still making successful predictions — it wasn't degenerating, it was just... finished? Or maybe the word is "saturated"?

So my question: is there an established term for this — a paradigm that dies not from failure but from completion or from a shift in what counts as an interesting question? And if there isn't, what would you call it, and how would you distinguish it from (a) a standard Kuhnian crisis-driven revolution, (b) a Lakatosian degenerating program, and (c) ordinary scientific progress where a new theory subsumes the old one as a limiting case?

If you could say how confident you are in the answer and point out the thing you're least sure about, I'd appreciate that. And please keep it under 500 words — I'm not writing a book here.
