I'm a PhD student working on AI alignment, specifically on scalable oversight protocols, and my supervisor has been pushing me to think more carefully about what the actual limits of debate are. There's this ongoing back-and-forth in the literature about whether "obfuscated arguments" represent a fundamental barrier or just an engineering challenge, and I feel like the two sides have been talking past each other for years. I'd like to draft an adversarial-collaboration document that a committed debate optimist (someone who thinks debate between AIs with a human judge is a viable route to aligning superhuman systems) and a committed obfuscation sceptic (someone who thinks there exist true arguments that are intrinsically impossible for humans to evaluate, even with adversarial cross-examination) could both sign. The document should have a numbered agreed-claims section, covering what both sides accept about the debate setup, the role of the human judge, what "obfuscation" means precisely, and what the no-go results actually establish. Then a disagreements table: the exact formulations of where they part ways, with each side's view stated in wording that side would endorse. Then the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling them would genuinely move at least one side -- and for each crux, a concrete test, argument, or thought experiment both agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction recorded in advance. I'm particularly interested in whether the crux ends up being about the existence of "checkable decompositions" for any true claim, or about whether the asymmetry of debate (the honest debater only needs to point out one flaw) suffices to overcome obfuscation even when the judge can't follow the full argument. I'd also want the document to flag any parts of the dispute that turn out to be purely verbal -- I suspect some of the disagreement about what counts as "human-checkable" might dissolve once you pin down the judge's capabilities and time budget. Could you draft this?
