I'm preparing a seminar on vagueness for advanced undergraduates, and I want to open the session with something that makes the sorites paradox felt rather than merely stated. I've decided to commission (or attempt to write) a poem that enacts the paradox, and I'd like your help with the design.

Specifically, I want a villanelle about the sorites. The villanelle form seems almost too apt: two refrains that alternate through five tercets and then collide in a final quatrain, which mirrors the way the two horns of the paradox — the tolerance intuition and the unacceptable conclusion — keep trading places without ever resolving. One refrain should give voice to the tolerance principle: that a single grain cannot mark the boundary, that no heap becomes a non-heap by the removal of just one. The other refrain should give voice to what the chain of modus ponens steps forces on us: that the heap dissolves into nothing, that the concept was never coherent to begin with, or that we are left staring at a single grain and the ruins of a distinction. The refrains should alternate as the paradox forces the mind to alternate, and when they finally meet in the closing couplet, their juxtaposition should do the work of showing why the paradox is not a trick but a genuine problem — not resolving it, just holding the tension in view.

The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with the literature would recognize the logical structure: the tolerance principle, the long chain of conditionals, the clash with the conviction that there is a fact of the matter about where heaps begin and end. But it should use no technical vocabulary. I want the students to inhabit the paradox before we turn to epistemicism, supervaluationism, degree theories, and the rest. If the poem can also gesture — through imagery, through the way the refrains subtly shift their sense as they reappear in new contexts — at the fact that the paradox generalizes beyond heaps (to baldness, to color spectra, to personhood and the limits of moral status), that would be a welcome depth, but the heap should remain the central image throughout.
