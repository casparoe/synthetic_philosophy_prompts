Write the joint statement that a buck-passing value theorist who thinks the right-kind/wrong-kind distinction can be drawn in a principled, non-circular way and a reasons-monist who thinks there's no clean object-given/state-given line and that wrong-kind reasons are simply reasons, so fittingness or value has to be taken as primitive, could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the wrong kind of reasons problem. I want the actual document they'd produce, not a description of it. Start with a numbered list of agreed claims — about the demon-and-admiration cases, about what a reason for an attitude has to explain, about which moves both sides now think are dead ends. Then a disagreements table: each row a single question, with the two positions stated in wording each side would sign, so neither side can complain the other side is being strawmanned. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side. I suspect one crux is whether wrong-kind reasons are reasons for the attitude itself or only reasons to bring the attitude about, and another is whether 'object-given' can be spelled out without sneaking in evaluative vocabulary. For each crux, give a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and write down each sides prediction about what that case shows. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Keep the whole thing in the register of a document both people would actually put their names on.
