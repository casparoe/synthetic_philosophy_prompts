Play the role of a sophisticated, committed defender of quantified performance management in a public institution—say, a hospital system or a school district. My opening move: you keep telling me that the fix for a gamed metric is better metrics—more of them, rotated, audited, kept quiet. But every serious target your institution has published so far has been gamed: people found ways to hit the number without improving the underlying thing, and in some cases made it worse. What makes you think the next round of clever proxies won't meet the same fate? If a metric is a fixed, legible simplification of a complex reality, and gaming is just the equilibrium response of smart people under pressure, then isn't the failure structural rather than a matter of bad design?
