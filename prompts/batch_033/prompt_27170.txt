I've been reading Tolstoy's *What is Art?* for this aesthetics seminar and it's genuinely scrambled my brain. His whole argument is that real art is when one person consciously transmits a feeling they've lived through to others, and those others get infected by that same feeling. And he's ruthless about it — he basically says most of what passed for art in his day (including his own early novels, which is wild) was counterfeit, because it was either made for the amusement of the idle rich, or it was technically dazzling but emotionally empty, or it was so obscure nobody could actually feel anything from it. He even trashes Shakespeare and Beethoven on these grounds. The only art that counts is sincere, morally serious, and universal — something a peasant can be moved by just as much as a professor.
