Hi — I'm in a third-year political philosophy module and we've just done a unit on compromise between people with very different value systems. I keep noticing a move I don't have a word for, and I want to know if it has a name and how it relates to the stuff we've covered.

The setup: two people each think the other is doing something seriously wrong, but each also has some practice the other condemns that they themselves think is basically fine. Example from a seminar: A is a vegetarian who thinks factory farming is an atrocity but doesn't see flying as a big deal; B thinks aviation emissions are the real moral emergency but dosen't see what's wrong with eating meat. They make a deal: A won't fly home for a year, and B goes vegetarian for the same year. By A's lights she's given up something nearly worthless and gained something she cares about enormously (B not eating meat); by B's lights he's done the same in reverse. So both walk away thinking the world is morally better than before, even though they still think each other's underlying values are wrong.

I've seen this pattern described as a kind of trade or compromise, but it's not exactly either, because there's no shared currency: A thinks B's concession is worth a lot, but B thinks that very same concession is worth almost nothing, and vice versa. Is there an established name for this specific configuration, what are the nearest concepts in the literature, and how does it differ from ordinary political log-rolling or from the worry that moral commitments shouldn't be up for barter? I'm not just after a label — I want to know exactly what the thing is, where its edges are, and whether it's even coherent to call it a 'gain' for both sides.

Could you answer in British English?
