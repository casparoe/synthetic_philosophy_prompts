I've been trying to sort out my views on wild animal suffering for an essay I'm writing, and I'd like a consistency check before I commit to this set of claims. Here is where I currently stand:

(1) The suffering of wild animals matters morally, and its sheer scale — predation, parasitism, starvation, disease — makes it one of the gravest moral problems we face, even if it receives comparatively little attention.

(2) That a harm arises from natural processes rather than human agency does not diminish our reason to prevent it: the pain a gazelle experiences as it is eaten by a lion is not less morally significant than the pain of a human patient dying of an untreated illness.

(3) Epistemic humility about ecosystem dynamics means we should not attempt large-scale interventions now, but we should invest heavily in research to make safe, net-positive interventions possible in the future.

(4) Some limited interventions are already justified — for instance, vaccinating wild animals against diseases like rabies that cause prolonged suffering without serving a stabilizing ecological function we have reason to preserve.

(5) Even if we someday possessed the ecological knowledge to do so safely, it would be wrong to phase out predation by, say, introducing contraceptive controls on predator populations and managing prey populations through fertility regulation, because a world without predation would lack something of value that goes beyond the welfare of the individual animals in it.

(6) The value of an ecosystem reduces entirely to the value of the experiences of the sentient creatures within it; there is no intrinsic value in biodiversity, species, or ecological processes as such.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity and say what the least costly revision would be — which claim would have to be modified or abandoned, and what the philosophical cost of doing so would be. If any apparent tension dissolves on closer inspection, explain why.
