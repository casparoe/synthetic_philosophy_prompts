I'm taking a philosophy of space and time seminar this semester and we've been going through the substantivalism vs. relationism debate, and honestly I'm starting to lose my mind. We read the Leibniz–Clarke correspondence, Newton's bucket and the rotating globes, Mach's response, and then we got into the modern stuff — the hole argument, Earman and Norton, sophisticated substantivalism, all of that. And here's the thing: after three hundred years, it feels like nobody has budged an inch. The substantivalist says the bucket argument shows absolute space is real because we can detect absolute rotation empirically; the relationist says we can reformulate everything in terms of relative motions and the rest is surplus metaphysics. Then the hole argument comes along and the substantivalist either bites the bullet on radical indeterminism or retreats to sophisticated substantivalism, and the relationist says "see, I told you space was relational." And round and round we go. The same moves, the same countermoves, just with fancier math each generation.

My working theory — and I'm putting this forward as a hypothesis, not a settled view — is that this isn't really a disagreement about physics at all. Both sides agree on the mathematics of general relativity, both sides agree on what you'd observe in the bucket or the hole, both sides agree on the empirical equivalence of the formulations. It seems more like a disagreement about what counts as an explanation, or maybe about what we're even asking when we ask whether spacetime is a substance. But I'm not confident about that diagnosis, and I've heard people push back and say no, there's a genuine metaphysical fact of the matter at stake.

So what I want is a diagnosis of the structure of this disagreement. Is it substantive or verbal? Are the two sides answering different questions under the same heading? Do they differ about the criteria for something's being real, or about what an explanation has to do, or about some hidden empirical bet? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a new experiment, a theorem, a conceptual distinction, a change of question — or should we just accept that the debate is ill-posed and walk away? I want reasons, not a survey of the positions.
