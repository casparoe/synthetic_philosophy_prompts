Please give me your actual credence that the rational action in the following scenario is to pay, together with the derivation of that number.

Omega is an agent with a flawless track record of predicting the decisions of people in your situation, and you are certain of the setup. It flips a fair coin in private. If the coin lands heads, Omega asks you for $100. If the coin lands tails, Omega gives you $10,000, but only on the condition that you would have paid the $100 had the coin landed heads. The coin lands heads, and Omega asks you for the $100. You know the coin has already been flipped, so nothing you now do can causally affect whether it landed tails.

What is your credence that paying the $100 is the rational thing to do? I do not want "it depends on your decision theory" — I want a single number and a decomposition. Please assign a rough probability to each family of decision theory (causal, evidential, functional, updateless, and any other you think belongs), state what each family would recommend here conditional on being correct, and include any additional terms you think matter (for example, the chance that "rational" is not determinate in a case like this). Show how the terms combine to produce your final number, and tell me which single term your credence is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would shift it most. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the setup.
