so I've been messing around with this thought experiment I stumbled on — I think it was called the careenium or something like that. The setup: a giant flat table with millions of tiny magnetic balls (I think the guy called them simms or symms) all bouncing around by simple deterministic rules. Over time they clump into these big slow-moving swirls and shapes, and those swirls act like symbols — like a symbol for "go left" or "I'm hungry" or whatever. And the whole contraption is hooked up so the outside world affects the walls and the walls affect the balls, and the balls affect the walls back. So the machine thinks, sort of.

Here's the thing. I've been turning this over for months and I keep landing on a set of views that feel right individually but I'm starting to suspect they can't all be true at once. I want someone to go through them and tell me where the cracks are. Here they are:

(1) The balls are the whole physical story. Every ball just obeys local deterministic rules — magnetism, collisions, that's it. Nothing else acts on them.

(2) The swirls are real. Like, they're not just something I project onto the balls because my brain likes patterns. If you wanted to predict what the machine will do next, you'd have to talk about the swirls; the ball-level description is useless for that even though its complete in principle.

(3) Something is real only if it makes a difference. If a pattern had zero effect on how things go — if you could delete it and nothing would change — then it's a shadow, not a thing.

(4) The swirls can't push the balls around. They're made of balls. There's no extra force. A swirl is just a lot of balls doing what balls do; it can't reach down and shove one.

(5) I think the swirls and the balls are the same event described at two grains, so the whole question "do the symbols push the balls?" might be confused — like asking whether the traffic jam caused the cars to stop, or whether the wave pushed the water molecule.

(6) But I also can't accept that the swirls do nothing. When I watch the machine (or imagine it), it's not just that I describe it in swirl-language for convenience — the swirl-level facts seem to be what actually steers it. And if the swirls don't push, then by the same logic my own thoughts don't push my body around, and I'm not willing to say that my deciding to type this sentance is doing zero causal work.

So that's the set. Go through them and tell me: which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are? Rank the real tensions by severity. And if something has to give, what's the cheapest fix for each — but don't tell me which one I should drop, I want to see the damage report and make up my own mind.
