I'm working through Kant's Perpetual Peace for a reading group and I keep getting tripped up on the bit in the First Definitive Article where he says a republican constitution shouldnt be confused with a democratic one, and then drops this:

"Of the three forms of the State, a Democracy, in the proper sense of the word, is necessarily a despotism; because it establishes an Executive power in which All resolve about, and, it may be, also against, any One who is not in accord with it; and consequently the All who thus resolve are really not all; which is a contradiction of the Universal Will with itself and with liberty."

I get the broad point that republicanism for him is about separating legislative and executive power, not about who holds office. But the claim that democracy is "necessarily a despotism" confuses me. He seems to be saying that when "all" decide, the all who actually decide are not really all, and that this contradicts the universal will with itself and with freedom. What exactly is the argument here? Is he making a point about voting procedure, about representation, or about something in his account of the general will? And if his target is direct democracy, would he say the same thing about a modern representative democracy, or does his distinction between form of sovereignty and form of government let him avoid that?
