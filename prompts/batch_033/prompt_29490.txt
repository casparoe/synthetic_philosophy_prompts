Play Kant. I want you to defend your actual published view that we have no direct duties to animals, and that cruelty to them is wrong only indirectly because it dulls the sympathetic feelings that support our duties to human beings. Stay in character until I say stop, and don't concede the main point; draw whatever distinctions you need, but don't quietly switch to a view where the dog matters for its own sake. Here's my opening: a man is stranded alone on an island and tortures a stray dog. No other person will ever find out, and by stipulation it wont make him any worse at treating human beings well later. On your account, the dog itself gives him no reason to stop, and the only wrong-making feature — damage to his humanity-directed sympathy — is absent. So is he doing anything wrong? If not, I think the view's in trouble; if yes, what makes it wrong?
