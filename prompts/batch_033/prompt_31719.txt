I have been trying to make up my mind about how seriously to take obfuscated arguments as an objection to debate-based oversight of AI systems. The view I keep coming back to is this: the debate proposal gets its plausibility from the idea that an honest and a dishonest debater, cross-examining one another before a human judge, will converge on the crux of a question, and that the judge only has to evaluate that crux. But once a question is hard enough that the crux is itself a long, technical argument, that assumption is precisely what can no longer be relied on. At that point a dishonest debater can in principle produce an argument that is locally plausible at every step, globally wrong, and too expensive for the judge—or for the honest debater, or for both—to refute. So I have come to think the real limit on debate is not ordinary lying but obfuscation: the judge's inability to verify a chain of reasoning is doing all the epistemic work, and that ability does not scale with the length or subtlety of the arguments.

I want to know what would count against this position. If I am wrong, which load-bearing claim should I expect to break first—the assumption that constructing an obfuscated argument is cheaper than exposing one, the assumption that the judge must be able to verify the reasoning rather than merely adjudicate between two contestants, or the assumption that self-play cannot train debaters to make their reasoning legible to a bounded judge? What formal results, toy models, or concrete mechanisms would show that debate can still extract truth from arguments too complex for the judge to check step by step, and which of those would be decisive rather than merely awkward for my view? What would a world look like in which debate-based oversight works despite obfuscation, and are there already signs of it in what the best current systems can do? Finally, I would like an honest assessment of whether the skeptical position, as I have stated it, has been insulated against all possible evidence—and whether that should worry me for a claim of this kind.
