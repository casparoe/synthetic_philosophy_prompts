I am applying for a six-month postdoctoral fellowship in the philosophy of artificial intelligence, and I need to submit a two-page research proposal. My topic is the debate about AI safety via debate — the proposal, associated especially with Irving, Christiano, and Amodei, that we can align superhuman AI systems by having them debate each other in front of human judges, on the assumption that, in a competitive argumentative setting, truthful arguments will tend to be more persuasive than deceptive ones because false claims can be exposed by a sufficiently capable opponent. I want to examine a specific challenge to this approach that I think has not been adequately mapped in the existing literature: the possibility of obfuscated arguments — arguments that are persuasive to human judges but whose relationship to the truth is systematically inscrutable to those same judges, even after adversarial scrutiny.

Here is my half-formed idea. The debate proposal assumes, roughly, that truth enjoys a structural advantage over falsehood in an adversarial argumentative setting, because a capable opponent can reveal the flaws in a false argument in a way the judge can recognize. But this presupposes that the exposure can be rendered in a form the judge is capable of evaluating. If an argument can be constructed that is false or misleading but whose defects can only be demonstrated using concepts, formalisms, or chains of reasoning that exceed the judge's cognitive grasp, then the truthful debater faces a dilemma: either offer an explanation the judge cannot follow (and lose) or allow the obfuscated argument to stand (and lose). The question is whether this is a genuine structural limitation of the debate framework, or a problem that can be mitigated from within it — and, if the former, what constraints it places on the scope of debate as an alignment strategy.

Please turn this into a proper proposal with the following components:

1. A precise statement of the research question, distinguishing it carefully from related objections that have already received attention — for instance, the worry that AIs might collude rather than debate in good faith, or the simpler worry that human judges are ignorant of the relevant domain. The question should be stated so that it has a determinate answer and so that it is clear what kind of consideration would count for or against each possible answer.

2. An explanation of why the existing debate literature does not settle this question. What assumptions about the capacities of human judges — their ability to recognize valid rebuttals, to follow extended chains of inference, to detect when an argument is exploiting their cognitive biases — are built into the framework, and have those assumptions been defended or merely taken for granted?

3. A method section laying out what I would actually do, month by month, over the six months. This should include both conceptual work (analyzing what it means for an argument to be "obfuscated" in the relevant sense — how it differs from merely complex, merely misleading, or merely rhetorical argumentation) and constructive work (developing case studies or thought experiments that test whether the debate framework has the resources to handle obfuscated arguments, and under what conditions it fails). The method should make clear why a philosophical rather than purely empirical or technical approach is needed to make progress on this question.

4. A clear account of what a successful outcome looks like and what would count as the project failing to make progress. For example, success might be a precise characterization of the conditions under which debate is robust to obfuscation, together with an argument that those conditions are or are not likely to hold in realistic alignment scenarios. Failure might be the discovery that "obfuscated argument" cannot be given a stable characterization that distinguishes it from ordinary persuasive argumentation, or that the problem reduces trivially to one already addressed in the literature.

5. A realistic scope for six months, with an honest assessment of what could make the project too large and how I would bound it to keep it tractable.
