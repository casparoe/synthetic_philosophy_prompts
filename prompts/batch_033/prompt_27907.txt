I'm taking a course on distributive ethics, and I'm writing a paper on whether prioritarianism genuinely escapes the leveling-down objection or only relocates it. In most of the cases I've tried, prioritarianism and telic egalitarianism end up voting the same way, and the difference only shows up in their explanations, which makes it hard to isolate what the leveling-down objection is actually tracking. I want a worked case that forces a real split between the two views.

Please construct a two-person distributional choice with the following specifications. The telic egalitarian's value function is total well-being minus λ times the absolute gap between the two persons' well-being, with λ fixed at 0.2. The prioritarian's value function is the sum of the square roots of individual well-being. The case should be one in which the telic egalitarian prefers an unequal distribution with a higher total, while the prioritarian prefers a perfectly equal distribution with a lower total — that is, the view that officially values equality for its own sake is the one that declines to sacrifice total well-being for equality. Constraints: the two distributions must not Pareto-dominate each other; the equal distribution's total must be at least 20% below the unequal distribution's total; all well-being levels must be non-negative; and the numbers should be simple enough that the arithmetic is transparent. I want the verdicts to fall out of the two value functions, not to be engineered by loading the parameters in a way that obviously rigs the result.

After presenting the case, show the calculation for each theory and state each verdict. Then tell me which feature of the case is doing the work — in particular, say what happens if the prioritarian's function is made less concave (for example, f(x) = x^0.9), and whether the egalitarian's verdict is stable if λ is raised somewhat. Since this is for the leveling-down section of my paper, finish with a paragraph on how a defender of each theory would attack the other theory's verdict in this case. Finally, say how confident you are in your analysis overall, and single out the specific point you are least sure about.
