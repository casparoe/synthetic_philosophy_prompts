My friend's pretty firm on what's supererogatory and what's just obligatory. Here's her verdicts so far:

- Pulling a drowning toddler from a shallow pond, no risk: obligatory.
- Donating a kidney to a stranger: supererogatory.
- Doctor treating an emergency patient while on shift: obligatory.
- Bystander running into a burning building to save a stranger (high risk of death): supererogatory.
- A billionaire giving 1% of their wealth to highly effective charities: obligatory.
- A middle-class person giving 50% of their income to charity: supererogatory.
- Keeping a promise to meet a friend when it's a mild hassle: obligatory.
- A soldier jumping on a grenade to save their squad: supererogatory.
- Calling 911 when you witness a car crash: obligatory.
- Spending every weekend volunteering at a homeless shelter instead of relaxing: supererogatory.

Now here's the new case: You're a doctor in a rural clinic, the only one for miles. A patient needs an emergency surgery. You've just worked 18 hours straight and are dangerously fatigued — operating in this state carries a real risk of making a fatal error. If you don't operate, the patient will definately die before anyone else can get there. There's no way to transfer them.

What do you think she'll say? I'm less interested in the answer itself than in the rule she seems to be running, the candidate rules that also fit her verdicts, and which of those rules this new case would distinguish. And if you had to design one more case to settle which rule she's really using, what would it be?
