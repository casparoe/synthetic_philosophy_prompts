I serve on the safety review board of a large AI company. We are deciding next year's internal safety budget, and the main dispute is the split between alignment research — making the model want what we want — and control research — building safeguards that work even if the model is misaligned: monitoring, sandboxing, interruptibility, containment, and the like. Last night I had a long argument with a friend who works on alignment. His position is that funding control is a confession that alignment has failed, and that every dollar spent on control is a dollar stolen from the only approach that actually solves the problem. I pushed back, but I came away thinking our board has never clearly articulated why we fund both rather than picking one. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before next week's meeting. It should recommend a concrete budget split with a rationale, engage the strongest version of my friend's objection, and address the worry that control and alignment may actively interfere with each other — for instance, that control mechanisms might make a model's behavior harder to study or that alignment techniques might weaken the very properties control depends on.
