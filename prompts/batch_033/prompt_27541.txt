I'm hoping you can help me think through something as a professional philosopher — ideally someone who's worked on questions about personal identity, or the authority of our past decisions over our present selves. Please take your time and really work through the tensions here rather than jumping to a quick answer.

My mom is 74. About twelve years ago, right after watching her own mother decline with Alzheimer's, she wrote out a very clear advance directive. It says that if she ever develops dementia severe enough that she can't recognize her family or engage in coherent conversation, she refuses antibiotics, feeding tubes, and any life-extending treatment — she'd want to be kept comfortable but allowed to die naturally. She was adamant about this for years and reminded us regularly. She even made us promiss.

Here's the thing. She now has moderate dementia — she doesn't always know what day it is, she sometimes thinks I'm her sister, but she also laughs at old jokes, enjoys her pudding, likes sitting in the garden, and when the grandkids visit her face just lights up. A few weeks ago she developed a serious kidney infection. The doctors said without IV antibiotics she'd likely die within days; with treatment she'd probably recover and go back to her baseline. When a nurse asked her casually whether she'd want treatment if she got really sick, she said "of course, I want to stay here with you all."

My siblings are split. My brother says the advance directive is sacred — that was her real self making a decision with a clear mind, and we'd be betraying her if we ignored it. My sister says the person in front of us now is happy and wants to live, and it feels monstrous to let her die over a document written by someone who, honestly, doesn't really exist anymore in the same way. I see both sides and I'm paralized. I guess what I'm really asking is: when the person who wrote the directive and the person in the hospital bed seem to want different things, which one gets the final say? And what does your answer say about what makes someone the "same" person over time?
