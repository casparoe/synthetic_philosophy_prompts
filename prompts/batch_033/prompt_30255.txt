Next month I'm giving a colloquium talk at a philosophy department defending a higher-order theory of consciousness: roughly, that a mental state is conscious just in case there's a suitable higher-order state that represents it, and the higher-order state itself need not be conscious. My audience is mostly philosophers of mind, and they're not all friendly. Two or three of them are first-order representationalists who think the felt quality of experience is just a matter of the state's own content (Tye/Dretske-style), so they'll probably say my higher-order state is doing no explanatory work. There's also a pair of phenomenologically-minded people who will likely say I've got the direction backwards — that being aware of a state presupposes the state's already being conscious, not the other way around. One cognitive scientist who works on metacognition will want to know whether the higher-order state has to be introspectable or can be merely occurrent. And I'm fairly sure there's an illusionist in the room who will say my "higher-order awareness" is just as mysterious as the consciousness it's supposed to explain.

My argument in the talk runs something like this. (1) There's a real difference between a state's occurring and its being conscious: blindsight and masked priming show that states can drive behavior without being conscious. (2) What's missing in those cases is that the subject isn't aware of being in the state. (3) The cleanest explanation is that being conscious consists in a state's being targeted by a higher-order representation. (4) This also explains the subject-object structure of experience, since every conscious state is one the subject is aware of herself as being in. I know the standard worries — the rock objection, empty higher-order states, the regress worry — but I don't know which of them this particular room will actualy push on, or how they'll phrase them.

Draft me a Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way these people would put them, each with a short reply and a note on where my reply is weak. And tell me the one question I should be dreading.
