I'm a journalist working on a piece about the paradox of deontology, and I've been tracking the published case-verdicts of a philosopher who defends agent-relative constraints against consequentialism. Here are her verdicts so far:

- Standard trolley (switch): permissible to divert the trolley from five to one.
- Footbridge (push): impermissible to push the large man off the bridge to stop the trolley.
- Loop variant (the side track loops back toward the five, but there is one person on it whose body would stop the trolley): impermissible to divert.
- Murderer-at-the-door: impermissible to kill an innocent person and frame him for a crime, even if that is the only way to prevent a mob from killing five other innocents.
- Two switches (you can flip either Switch A or Switch B; both divert the trolley from five to one, but through different causal routes): permissible, and it doesn't matter which switch you flip.
- Gas leak (pressing a button would release a gas that saves five from a lethal leak but, as a foreseen side effect, kills one person in an adjacent room): permissible.

Next week she's set to comment on a new case: a drone operator can prevent a missile from hitting a shelter with five civilians by diverting it toward a shelter with one civilian, but the missile's guidance system requires a continuous infrared lock, and the one civilian's heat signature is what will guide the missile to the new target. In other words, the one civilian's presence is causally necessary for the diversion to work, but the operator does not intend the civilian's death — she would be relieved if the civilian miraculously survived.

Predict her verdict on the drone case. Identify the principle or principles that best fit her track record, note any rival principles that fit the data equally well, say which of those principles diverge on the drone case and why, and give your best guess with reasoning. Finally, propose a further case — not yet in her record — whose verdict would most decisively tell the rival principles apart. Keep the response to at most 500 words.
