I'm a third-year PhD student in formal epistemology, and I've been circling for a while now around what people call the presumption of independence: the idea that, when you have no positive reason to think two events or variables are linked, your prior should treat them as independent. It shows up all over the place — objective Bayesians get it out of maximum entropy (no constraint couples X and Y, so the max-ent distribution factors), people building Bayesian networks default to missing edges, and mathematicians doing heuristic arguments talk about divisibility by different primes, or primality of nearby integers, as if the relevant events were independent, which works surprisingly often but also quietly fails in some of the places people care about. But I keep finding ways the principle fights with itself. If I have a coin of completely unknown bias and I treat successive flips as outright independent — not just conditionally independent given some hidden bias — then the first hundred flips tell me nothing about the next one; if instead I treat the flips as exchangeable, de Finetti-style reasoning pushes me to a mixture of i.i.d. processes, which makes the flips positively correlated and is what actually lets me learn. And there's the logical version of the problem: two propositions can be logically equivalent, or share a hidden common cause, and presuming independence seems to amount to betting that no such structure exists, which is a substantive assumption dressed up as ignorance.

I have about three years left and I'm decent at logic and probability but not much of a number theorist or an ML person. What I'd like from you is a defended agenda, not a survey: what do you think are the main open questions in formalizing the presumption of independence (or in deciding when it's even a good principle), and which two or three should I actually build a dissertation around? For each one you'd prioritize, say why it's upstream of other work, how tractable it is for someone with my background, and what sort of result or argument over the next couple of years would make you demote it. If you think the whole framing is wrong and I should be asking a different question in the neighborhood, tell me that too, but still give me the ordering.
