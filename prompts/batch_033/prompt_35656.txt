Darwin showed that nature can produce incredibly well-designed organisms through random variation and selection — no designer needed, just blind algorithmic processes. Turing showed the same thing for thought: a machine following simple rules can produce outputs that look intelligent, without needing to understand what it's doing. Dennett calls these 'strange inversions of reasoning' because they flip our normal assumption that competence requires comprehension. The upshot is pretty radical: human intelligence is ultimately the same kind of thing — our brains are just running evolutionary algorithms and pattern-matching heuristics, and the feeling that we 'understand' anything is an illusion. So when people say LLMs don't really understand, Dennett would say: neither do you, in the way you think you do."
