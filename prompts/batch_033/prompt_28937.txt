I'm taking a course this term on the philosophy of machine learning, and I keep noticing a gap between the abstract material and what practitioners actually do. On the theory side, we have covered the no-free-lunch results: averaged over all possible learning problems, no algorithm and no choice of inductive bias is better than any other, so every advantage on one class of problems has to be paid for by a matching disadvantage somewhere else. We have also covered the classic problem of induction, which makes any expectation that past performance will continue look like something that itself needs a justification. Yet on the practice side, nearly every real pipeline seems to proceed as if neither worry applied: people penalize model complexity through L1 and L2 regularization, prune, stop training early, prefer the simpler of two models with equal validation error, and then report held-out test accuracy as evidence that the model will do well in deployment, describing all of this as improving generalization.

I would like an analysis of what philosophy of induction this practice presupposes. Does penalizing complexity strictly presuppose that the world itself is simple, or that simpler hypotheses are more probable a priori, or does it merely fit well with those views while remaining compatible with others? Is there a deflationary reading on which regularization is a purely pragmatic device for reducing variance on finite data and commits to nothing about the structure of reality? Does treating held-out performance as evidence about future or deployment performance presuppose exactly the kind of uniformity that the problem of induction puts in question, and is that assumption consistent with what the no-free-lunch results allow? Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, and what would actually have to change if one took the no-free-lunch results and the problem of induction at full strength: would one abandon regularization, keep it while openly treating it as a domain-specific bet rather than a neutral default, report different quantities, or something else?

After the prose analysis, include a compact JSON summary with exactly the following schema:

- candidate_presuppositions: an array of at least four objects, each with fields name (a short label for the candidate view), statement (a one-sentence statement of the view), status (one of strictly_presupposed, merely_fits, underdetermined, or in_tension_with_practice), and change_if_rejected (one sentence on what in the practice would have to change if the view were rejected).
- strongest_commitment: the strongest claim about induction that the practice as a whole actually commits to, if any.
- deflationary_reconstruction: the most minimal set of assumptions under which the practice remains rational.
- tensions: an array of one-sentence descriptions of internal tensions between parts of the practice or between the practice and its stated rationale.
- nfl_compatibility: one sentence on whether, and under what interpretation, the practice is compatible with the no-free-lunch results.
