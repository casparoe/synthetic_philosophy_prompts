I am trying to get my views on consequentialism and the cluelessness problem into a consistent shape, and I would appreciate an audit. Here is where I currently stand:

(1) Act-consequentialism states the correct criterion of rightness: an act is right if and only if its consequences are at least as good as those of any alternative act available to the agent.

(2) The cluelessness problem is genuine and severe. For virtually any ordinary action — giving to a particular charity, choosing a career, deciding whether to have a child — the causal ripple effects become untraceable within a few steps, and the unknown downstream consequences could easily swamp the known ones in moral significance. We simply cannot know what the long-run consequences of our actions will be.

(3) Despite (2), we are not morally paralyzed. It cannot be that the correct moral theory, combined with facts about our epistemic situation, yields the result that we have no idea what we ought to do in ordinary life. We have genuine, determinate, knowable obligations.

(4) The right response to cluelessness is to adopt expectabilism: we should evaluate actions not by their actual consequences but by their expected consequences given our available evidence. This lets us act on the basis of what we can reasonably foresee while remaining consequentialist in spirit.

(5) Expectabilism presupposes that we can assign probabilities to outcomes. But in many real-world choice situations, our evidence is so impoverished that we cannot assign even rough, imprecise probabilities to the relevant outcomes. We face not mere risk but Knightian uncertainty — uncertainty about the shape of our uncertainty itself — and the standard expected-value framework has nothing to say when priors are this unanchored.

(6) In cases of Knightian uncertainty about morally weighty matters, the appropriate decision rule is maximin: choose the action whose worst-case outcome is least bad. This avoids the fiction that we possess probabilities we do not in fact possess.

I can feel that not all of these sit comfortably together, but I am not sure which of the tensions are genuine, which are merely apparent, and which commitment ought to give way. Please go through these six claims and identify the tensions among them. For each tension, state whether it is genuine or only apparent, rank the genuine ones by severity, and explain what would have to be revised to resolve each. If there is a single revision that would clean up more than one problem at once, flag that as well.
