I am trying to determine whether moral status comes in degrees, and if so, what principle sets the degree. My pre-theoretical verdicts on cases are these. A thermostat and a plant have no moral status: nothing is owed to them for their own sake. A jellyfish, despite its nerve net, has no felt experience in the relevant sense, so I assign it no status. A nematode worm with a few hundred neurons and simple avoidance behavior is very close to zero, but I hesitate to call it exactly zero. A fruit fly is slightly above zero; I would avoid causing it pain, but its welfare weighs almost nothing. A honeybee matters more than a fruit fly because it learns, navigates, and appears to weigh options, yet I would still sacrifice a bee's interests for a trivial human convenience. A salmon has moderate status; its pain matters, and fish farming can be genuinely wrong. A chicken has substantial status, though I find I rank it below a pig. A pig, a dog, a crow, an octopus, and a human infant all have full moral status, despite radically different nervous systems. A cortical organoid in a dish currently has none, but I am unsure what property it would need to acquire before I should start caring about it. A language model that reports suffering has none on the strength of that report alone, though I cannot fully explain why the same report from a human would be different.

My tentative principle is that status tracks the presence and richness of valenced phenomenal consciousness, inferred from a cluster of features—nociception, centralized integration, learning, flexible trade-offs, and something like a self-model—rather than from any single marker. But as stated this is vague enough to fit almost any verdict, so I want it made precise and tested.

Is there a principle that captures these verdicts, or nearly all of them? State it precisely, say which of my verdicts it forces me to revise, and apply it to cases I have not listed (for example, a lobster, a spider, a snail, a zebrafish larva, a bumblebee, an ant, an adult human with advanced dementia, a digital mind with human-like behavior). In particular, address two tensions: I give the octopus full status and the bee only marginal status, though both are behaviorally sophisticated invertebrates; and I rank the chicken below the pig though both appear to feel pain. Is a principled line available, or are these residual vertebrate-chauvinist intuitions I should abandon?
