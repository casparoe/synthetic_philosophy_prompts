My gut reactions about which things can be wronged, and by how much, do not line up neatly, and I would like help finding the principle behind them, if one exists. Causing a dog prolonged pain seems seriously wrong to me, and leaving a caught fish to suffocate on a dock seems wrong as well, though less so. Stepping on an ant by accident seems only mildly bad, and I do not think anything wrong is done to a plant that is pulled up or to a thermostat that is damaged, because I doubt the plant or the thermostat can be wronged in the relevant sense. Yet pulling the legs off a beetle for amusement strikes me as wrong, while spraying an insecticide that kills hundreds of beetles in my kitchen strikes me as acceptable, even though I cannot identify a difference in what the beetles themselves experience. I am also unsure about boiling a lobster alive: it feels worse than swatting a fly, but I cannot tell whether that is because the lobster can suffer more, because the death is drawn out, or because a person is doing it deliberately. I would like to know whether one principle can explain this pattern of verdicts, and if it cannot, which of my reactions should be revised. What would such a principle say about cases I have not settled, such as a honeybee, an oyster, a nematode, or a simple robot that behaves as if it is in pain?
