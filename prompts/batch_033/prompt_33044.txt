I'm a novelist, and I'm currently working on a book that uses stream-of-consciousness narration to explore how a character's sense of time warps under grief. In researching the psychology of time perception, I encountered Daniel Dennett's discussion of the color phi phenomenon and his distinction between Orwellian and Stalinesque accounts of how the brain handles the timing of experience. As I understand the experiment: two colored dots flash in quick succession at different positions, and subjects report seeing a single dot that moves smoothly and changes color midway through its trajectory — before the second dot has even appeared. Dennett asks whether the brain is (a) revising the memory of what you experienced, after the fact — the Orwellian model, like Winston Smith rewriting the historical record — or (b) inserting a fabricated experience into the stream of consciousness itself, so that you genuinely seem to experience the color change before the second dot arrives — the Stalinesque model, like a show trial where the false event is staged in real time.

I've now read several novelists and essayists who cite this argument to claim that conscious experience is a retrospective confabulation — that there is no stream of consciousness, only a story we tell ourselves after the fact. If that's right, it seems to pull the floor out from under the entire literary technique I'm relying on. Stream of consciousness presupposes a something that flows, moment by moment, to be transcribed. If Dennett has shown that the stream is an illusion — that what we take to be present experience is always already a revision — then the technique is built on a false picture of the mind.

But when I went back to Dennett's actual text, I got the sense I might be missing something. He doesn't seem to settle on the Orwellian account; he seems to be saying that the very question "Orwellian or Stalinesque?" is somehow misguided, and that both answers share a mistaken assumption. I can't quite put my finger on what he's getting at. Could you walk me through what Dennett's argument actually establishes about the timing of experience, and whether it really does undercut the idea of a stream of consciousness that a novelist could faithfully render?
