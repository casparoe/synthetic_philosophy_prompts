After last week's graduate seminar on Harsanyi I realized I've been sloppy about what I actually want from a theory of social choice under risk, and I'd rather get that straight before reading any more of the back-and-forth over whether the aggregation theorem is a proof of utilitarianism or just an artifact of the vNM axioms. We spent half the time arguing about whether Pareto indifference alone is strong enough to bear the weight people put on it, and it made me notice I've never written down the full list of demands. So write me the desiderata. What does any adequate account of how individual preferences over lotteries get turned into a social ranking have to deliver? I'm thinking of things like: it has to respect Pareto improvements, it has to take the cardinal structure of vNM utilities seriously, it has to say where the interpersonal weights come from rather than smuggling them in, it has to handle the difference between ex ante and ex post fairness, and it has to decide whether the social ranking itself should satisfy the vNM axioms or whether that's exactly the step doing all the work in the theorem. For each requirement, give the case or little example behind it that shows why it matters, and then tell me which of these are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's tastes. The part I most want to see: is there a subset of these that can't be jointly satisfied? I have a vauge memory that once you want ex ante respect for individual risk attitudes, ex post fairness, and a social vNM representation all at once, something has to give, but I've never seen it laid out cleanly. Don't just restate what Harsanyi proved; tell me which of my demands are doing the work, which single requirement knocks out the most candidate theories, and where the theorem still leaves room for a non-utilitarian view. And please, write the whole thing as continuous prose, like a letter — no bullet points, no headings, no bold, no tables. I want an argument, not a taxonomy.
