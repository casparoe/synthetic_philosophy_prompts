I am drafting a paper on the epistemic value of majority rule, and I would like a consistency audit of the position I have ended up defending before I submit it. Here are the commitments I find myself holding, stated as plainly as I can.

(1) The Condorcet jury theorem captures what makes majority rule a good truth-tracker on binary questions with a correct answer: when voters are independent and each is more likely right than wrong, a majority of a sufficiently large group is more reliable than any single member, and its reliability approaches certainty as the group grows.

(2) I take this epistemic value to scale up. It applies not only to small juries but to large electorates deciding referenda and to legislatures, where the larger numbers should, if anything, make the majority more reliable.

(3) I also accept that real large electorates and legislatures are not independent in the theorem's sense. Voters share partisan media and common information sources, and they are subject to herding and correlated evidence, so the independence condition is routinely violated in precisely the large-group cases where I most want the theorem to do work.

(4) I hold that deliberation before a vote is on balance epistemically beneficial: it pools dispersed information and reasons, and it raises each member's competence.

(5) I hold that majority decisions can be legitimate and authoritative even when they are likely to be wrong; the case for majority rule is not exclusively epistemic.

(6) I take the theorem to apply to binary policy choices as much as to factual questions, since there is a fact of the matter about which policy is the right one.

I cannot tell which of these are actually in tension and which only appear to be. Please give me an audit rather than a lecture: identify the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, and say for each what would have to give and what the least costly revision would cost; also flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection. Finally, for each point where you diagnose a tension or its absence, tell me what argument or consideration would change your mind, or where your diagnosis is most fragile.
