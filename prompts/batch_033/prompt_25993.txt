Write a sonnet from the point of view of Abraham on the journey to Moriah, addressed silently to Isaac. The octave should build the tension between the ethical — the universal duty of a father, the love that binds Abraham to his son — and the singular command that makes no sense within the ethical. The volta should turn on Abraham's silence, on the fact that he cannot speak because the teleological suspension of the ethical is incommunicable: to justify himself would be to translate the singular into the universal and thereby betray the absolute relation to the absolute. The closing couplet should capture the paradox that faith does not place the individual above the ethical by some special merit but rather suspends the ethical through the individual's absolute relation to God — and that this is precisely what makes Abraham either a murderer or a knight of faith, with no middle term. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader of Fear and Trembling would recognize the Problemata, but it should also work as a poem for someone who does not know Kierkegaard.
