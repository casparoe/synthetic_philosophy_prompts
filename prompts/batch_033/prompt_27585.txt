I am presenting a paper next month to my department's metaphysics workshop, and I would like a Q&A prep sheet. The paper defends a version of the identity view of constitution. My thesis is this: the statue and the lump of clay are a single object, and the apparent conflict between the statue's not being able to survive being squashed and the lump's being able to survive being squashed is an artifact of treating sortal-qualified modal predicates as if they were monadic properties of the object simpliciter. On my view, could survive squashing expresses one sortal-qualified property under the sortal lump and a different one under the sortal statue, so there is no single property that the one object both has and lacks. Identity is absolute and Leibniz's Law is untouched, because the two modal predications do not ascribe the same property. The load-bearing move is that de re modal predicates are sortal-dependent, so substitution into modal contexts is safe once the sortal is held fixed.

The audience is a standing metaphysics workshop in a research department. Two regulars are pluralists about material constitution: they hold that the statue and the lump are distinct coinciding objects with different modal profiles, and they regard constitution-is-identity views as attempts to get two objects for the price of one while dodging the ontological bill. One regular is a four-dimensionalist who will think the whole puzzle is an artifact of endurantism and that my sortal semantics is a roundabout way of purchasing what perdurantism gives for free. Another is a graduate student working on Finean essentialism, likely to press the point that sortal-qualification merely postpones the question of which object bears which essence. The remaining members are broadly sympathetic but will be alert to anything that looks like relative identity returning in disguise.

Please produce the prep sheet: the objections I will actually get, roughly in the order they are likely to arise, each phrased the way the relevant person would put it, with a note on how damaging it is, what I should concede, what I should hold, and a two-minute reply. For each reply, mark where it is softest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks, and tell me why I should fear it.
