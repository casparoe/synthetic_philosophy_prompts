I have been reporting on the foundations of mathematics, and I have come around to a broadly Skolemite reading of set theory: that the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem exposes something deep about the limits of formal language, and that there is no absolute, framework-independent notion of an uncountable set; notions like uncountability are always relative to a background model or to the expressive resources of the language in which they are stated. I want to know what would count against this. Please give me the considerations that should move me, ranked by how decisive they are. Among the things I would like weighed: the standard dissolution, on which the paradox is only apparent because the bijection that would render the model's uncountable sets countable exists outside the model and not within it; the charge that the Skolemite equivocates between countability inside a model and countability in the metatheory; the self-undermining objection that the theorem is itself proved with the very set-theoretic apparatus the view relativizes; the claim that second-order formulations of set theory recover a determinate intended interpretation through quasi-categoricity, and whether that only relocates the relativity into the semantics of second-order quantification; the formal facts about absoluteness — which set-theoretic notions are absolute across transitive models and which are not; and the burden-shifting argument from the iterative conception of set, which treats the cumulative hierarchy as determinate before any formalization. For each, tell me whether a committed Skolemite should concede that it counts against the view, or can dismiss it as restating the first-order limitation the view already accepts. Also say where the Skolemite view has consequences its absolutist rival lacks — for how to read independence results, or for what the continuum hypothesis is even about — so I can see whether the view genuinely risks anything. Finally, tell me whether the position has been insulated by qualifications into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that is a problem for a philosophical view of this kind.
