I keep going in circles on this argument a friend made about Hacking's looping effects, and I cant tell whether it's brilliant or obviously broken. (1) When we classify people as having a condition -- ADHD, say -- the label feeds back: people are told they have it, clinicians and schools adjust to it, and some start reading their own behavior through it. (2) So the classification partly produces the very behaviors it was supposed to detect. (3) If that's right, then the more a psychiatric category is used, the more it can make itself true. (4) So the fact that a category is widely used, that clinicians find it useful, and that patients say it captures them isn't evidence it tracks a real underlying kind -- it might just be looping. (5) But this reasoning applies to every psychiatric category, from ADHD to schizophrenia to the old Soviet sluggish schizophrenia diagnosis. (6) Therefore no psychiatric category can ever be confirmed as tracking a real kind, and the line between well-validated and junk categories is an illusion. That conclusion feels absurd -- surely those categories aren't all on a par. I'd rather keep the looping story than give up on being able to validate categories at all, but I can't see which step to reject without losing the thing I like. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
