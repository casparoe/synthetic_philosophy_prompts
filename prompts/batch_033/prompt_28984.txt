I'm a PhD student working on causation, and before I read any more accounts of causation by omission I want the specification fixed. Write the desiderata: what must an adequate theory of causation by omission deliver, and why is each item a requirement? I want the canonical cases on the table — the gardener who fails to water the plant, the bystander who does not intervene, the physician who withholds treatment, the would-be rescuer who holds back because someone else has already jumped in — with, for each, the precise demand it places on a theory. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are local commitments. In particular, adjudicate which of the following are genuine constraints and which are camp-specific: that omissions are absences rather than events, that an adequate account must preserve the allowing/doing distinction, that it must explain the tight link between our omission attributions and norms and expectations, and that it must not let the baseline for what would have happened be fixed by our interests. Flag any pair you believe cannot be jointly met — I suspect a norm-free metaphysics of omissions and the norm-tracking of ordinary causal judgment pull apart — but argue it rather than assume it. Finally, say which single requirement does the most work excluding candidate theories, and say explicitly which consideration would most likely change your mind about the shape of the list, or where your reasoning about it is most fragile.
