I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about the problem of evil, not in the abstract but going by my gut on specific cases. Assume for the sake of the question that there's a perfect God — all-powerful, all-knowing, perfectly good. Here are my reactions to a bunch of cases, and I'd like to know whether there's a principle behind them.

A toddler pulling a pot of boiling water off the stove and getting a small burn that teaches her to stay away: I can live with it. A painful bone marrow transplant that cures a kid's leukemia: fine, obviously. The leukemia itself: not fine — I can't see what good requires that particular child to get cancer rather than some other way of achieving the same good. A man who freely lies and ruins a colleague's reputation: I can almost see permitting it, because if God blocked every harmful lie there'd be no real choice about honesty left. A serial killer who tortures his victims: the free will story starts to feel too thin here, like it treats the victims as props for the killer's moral development. A genocide: not fine, no amount of free will seems worth that. An earthquake that kills thousands of people in their sleep: not fine, and free will doesn't even get a foothold. A deer dying slowly in a forest fire: not fine, and I notice it bothers me less than the child, which itself bothers me. The fact that animals tore each other apart for hundreds of millions of years before any human existed: maybe the worst of all, because no story about human freedom or human moral growth can touch it. Grief that makes someone kinder and more compassionate: it's good that she grew, but I don't think the grief was permitted for that — it feels like a silver lining, not a justification. Chronic pain that makes someone bitter and isolated: not fine, and there isn't even a silver lining. Someone freely choosing to donate a kidney to a stranger: fine, and I'd say God should permit that choice even if it means some people choose badly instead.

The pair I can't reconcile is the liar and the genocide. Both are just free creatures doing harm, and my verdict flips somewhere between them, but I can't say where or why. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What would it say about two cases I keep going back and forth on? One: billions of chickens spending their whole lives in windowless sheds because humans choose to eat them — is that moral evil or natural evil on my reckoning, and does the prinicple care which? Two: a world where everyone always freely chooses the good, but there's still a devastating earthquake — would a perfect God be allowed to prefer our world to that one? And if no single principle captures my verdicts, which of them should I give up?
