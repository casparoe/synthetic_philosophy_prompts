The movie literalizes a version of that, except Phil relives one day, not his whole life, and he can change what happens while keeping his memory. I'd like a reading that takes both the film and Nietzsche seriously. What maps onto the demon, onto the test, onto saying yes? Where does the movie's setup change the stakes — he escapes by dying or sleeping, he keeps his memory, he can build skills and manipulate people? Does Phil ever actually affirm recurrence, or does he just get good at the day until it lets him out? And is the film closer to a moral self-improvement fantasy than to Nietzsche, or does it dramatize something the thought experiment is really after? Point me to specific scenes, and tell me where the lens holds and where it distorts.
