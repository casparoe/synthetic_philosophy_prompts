I'm a retired evolutionary biologist and I've been reading political philosophy for about eighteen months now — basically trying to give myself the liberal arts education I skipped in the 70s. Lately I've gotten obsessed with sortition, the idea of selecting political officials by lottery rather than election. And here's what's bugging me: the debate between the sortition people and the election people seems totally frozen. Both sides agree on the basic empirical picture — elections systematically overrepresent the wealthy, the educated, and the charismatic; sortition would produce a body that looks like the population; elections create accountability through re-election incentives; sortition removes those incentives. They're looking at the same facts and drawing opposite conclusions. I've seen this pattern before in biology, and usually it means people are using the same words to mean different things, or they disagree about what would count as evidence. So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a disguised empirical dispute where one side thinks accountability matters more and the other thinks descriptive representation matters more and we just need to measure which delivers better outcomes? Is it a verbal dispute about what "democracy" means? Or is it a clash of bedrock normative committments that no amount of evidence could touch? And given your diagnosis, is there anything — an experiment, a conceptual distinction, a reframing — that could actually move it?
