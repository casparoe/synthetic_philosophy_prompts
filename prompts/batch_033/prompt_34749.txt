I'm a PhD student in economics, and I also serve as a mediator in my university's dispute-resolution program — mostly two-party cases: co-founders dividing equity, academic departments splitting a fixed budget, roommates allocating rent. I've been reading the axiomatic bargaining literature, and I find the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution more compelling than the Nash solution for the cases I see, because it respects individual monotonicity: if the set of possible agreements expands in a way that only raises one party's best-case outcome, that party should not end up with less than before. But I need to translate the KS solution into a procedure I can actually run in a mediation session, with parties who have no economics training and limited patience for abstraction.

Please write a step-by-step procedure I can follow to arrive at a Kalai–Smorodinsky fair division in a two-party dispute. The procedure should:

(a) Start from what the parties can actually tell me — their best-case outcome, their fallback if no deal is reached, and the options on the table — and walk me through computing the KS solution from those inputs.
(b) Give clear instructions for handling cases where utilities are only roughly comparable across parties, or where the set of feasible agreements is discrete rather than continuous.
(c) Flag every step where the procedure requires a judgment call rather than a mechanical computation, and say what to do when the parties disagree about the inputs.
(d) Identify at least one kind of case where the KS solution gives a result that the parties are likely to find unfair or counterintuitive, and explain how to handle that moment in the room.

Then apply the procedure to two worked examples: (1) two co-founders dividing equity, where one contributed more capital and the other contributed more labor, and the fallback is dissolving the company; (2) two academic departments splitting a fixed budget, where one department has a much higher maximum plausible need than the other.

Throughout, please separate what is settled among bargaining theorists from what is genuinely contested — in particular, the trade-off between the KS and Nash solutions is a live debate, and I do not want the response to present the KS solution as the consensus view. Keep the response under 800 words.
