Got myself into a corner reading Sartre and I cant find the exit. Here's the argument as I've pieced it together: (1) For Sartre nothing in the world carries its own meaning — a cliff only becomes an obstacle, a locked door only becomes a limit, because of the project I bring to it. (2) So the facts of my situation never force a choice on me; they only get their force from a choice I make. (3) That means I'm free in any situation whatever — the prisoner, the person in an abusive relationship, the addict — all of them are just as free as anyone else. (4) So whenever I tell myself I couldn't have done otherwise because of my circumstances, that's bad faith, since my circumstances were never doing the forcing. (5) But that conclusion seems absurd: it makes coercion, poverty, addiction and a locked cell count for nothing, and it turns every 'I had no real choice' into an excuse. I'd like to keep (3), the bit about freedom being total, but I cant accept (5). Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Or is there a way to read (3) so it doesnt lead to (5)?
