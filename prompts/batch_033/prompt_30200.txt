I mean the Winner-style view, with the low-clearance Long Island overpasses, the UC Davis tomato harvester, and nuclear power as the stock examples.

What would have to be true for me to be wrong? I want a ranked list of defeaters, from "this should make me drop the strong view entirely" down to "this just forces me to add a qualification." For each one, say what I'd have to observe or accept, and whether a commited defender of the strong view would grant that it's a real problem or brush it off as a confusion. I especially want you to handle: (a) cases where the same artifact sits comfortably in very diffrent political systems; (b) cases where a technology's supposedly built-in politics got designed away in a later version; (c) the charge that the view is unfalsifiable because you can always find a politics in a design after the fact; and (d) whether the Moses overpass story being historically shaky matters for the view itself or just for Winner's rhetoric. And be honest at the end: if the strong view is mostly insulated from all this, is that a problem for it, or is that just what a claim about design and power looks like?
