I am a PhD student in bioethics and sit as a trainee member on the clinical ethics advisory board of a fertility network. The board meets in two weeks to decide whether our clinics may offer preimplantation genetic testing for polygenic traits (PGT-P) to screen embryos for non-disease characteristics — specifically, genetic variants associated with educational attainment and general cognitive ability — to patients who request it without a medical indication. The medical director presents the proposal as a routine extension of reproductive autonomy and of tests we already offer; several board members respond that it would make the network party to eugenics, and they do not think the service's voluntary character settles the matter; a third group goes further and holds that, once the test is available, patients with several viable embryos have a moral reason to select the one with the best expected life prospects, so the network should present it as part of responsible family planning. I have been asked to draft the short written opinion the board will vote on. Please write that memo: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with a policy and time frame the clinics could implement, and engage the strongest version of each objection rather than treating eugenics as a conversation-stopper. I would also like the memo to address whether this service is different in kind from what we already offer — PGT for monogenic disease and prenatal screening followed by selective termination — or only a further step along a continuum.
