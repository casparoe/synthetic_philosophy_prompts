I'm taking a philosophy of mind course and the professor mentioned Parfit's teletransporter in passing, so I went down a rabbit hole. I found this article online — one of those "big ideas explained" websites — that gave what seemed like a pretty confident summary, and I've been turning it over in my head since. Here's roughly what it said:

"Parfit's teletransporter is a thought experiment where you step into a machine that scans every detail of your body and brain, destroys you in the process, and then transmits the data to Mars where an atom-for-atom replica is assembled. Parfit's radical claim is that you survive this process. His argument is that what matters in survival isn't the continued existence of the same physical organism — it's psychological continuity: your memories, desires, intentions, and character traits persisting over time. Since the Mars replica has all of those, it is you. Parfit uses this to show that personal identity is a deep illusion — there's no fact of the matter about whether someone is the same person from one moment to the next, it's all just a matter of degree. And once you accept that, you realize that what we really care about isn't identity at all, but just that there's some future person who is psychologically continuous with us, whether or not they're 'the same person' in some strict metaphysical sense."

Something about this feels off to me. I remember hearing that Parfit actually distinguishes between survival and identity — that he doesn't say the replica IS you, exactly, but that identity isn't what matters. And the bit about there being "no fact of the matter" sounds more like something else entirely. But I haven't read Reasons and Persons and I'm not confident enough to say where the summary goes wrong. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me what's accurate, what's a distortion, and whether the overall picture it paints is one Parfit would actually endorse?
