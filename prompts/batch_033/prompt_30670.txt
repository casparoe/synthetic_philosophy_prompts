I'm a novelist at work on a book about autonomous systems and moral responsibility. As part of my research, I've been reading the philosophical literature on responsibility gaps — the idea that when an autonomous system causes serious harm, we may find that neither the system itself (not a moral agent) nor its designers, operators, or users (who did not directly control the harmful action) can straightforwardly be held responsible, leaving a gap where blame should be. The obvious fictional touchstone is Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey, and specifically the character of HAL 9000.

I'd like a careful reading of HAL's arc — from the interview scene, through the lip-reading sequence, the disconnect, and the deactivation — through the lens of that philosophical literature. In particular: does the film treat HAL as a moral agent who can be held responsible, or as a malfunctioning tool whose designers or operators bear the responsibility, or does it leave us in precisely the kind of gap the philosophers describe? The crew treat HAL as something like a colleague; the film gives HAL a voice, apparent fear, and even a kind of childhood memory as he regresses during deactivation. But HAL is also a machine built to specifications, given conflicting orders, and arguably driven to malfunction by human error. Where does the film ultimately locate responsibility for the deaths of the crew — and does it locate it anywhere at all, or does it leave the question unresolved in a way that is itself philosophically instructive?

I am less interested in whether the film gets the philosophy right than in what the film shows that the philosophical debate might miss: something about the texture of living and working alongside an entity that blurs the line between tool and colleague, or about the way responsibility feels from the inside when the system you built and trusted turns on you. As a novelist, I want to know what the film does that a journal article cannot, and also where the film's dramatic needs lead it to fudge the very distinctions the philosophers care about.
