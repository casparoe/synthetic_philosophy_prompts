Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between an origin essentialist about human persons and an opponent of origin essentialism about human persons. The origin essentialist holds that you could not have come from a different sperm or a different egg: your particular gametic origin is essential to your identity. The opponent holds that you could have come from a different sperm and egg and still have been you — that origin essentialism of this sort is too strong.

The document should include:

1. A section of agreed claims: statements both sides can sign, concerning the logical structure of the question, the relevant thought experiments, and what is not at stake (e.g., both sides may agree that some form of origin essentialism holds for artifacts, or that the question is not about personal identity over time, or that genetic identity is not the same as numerical identity). Avoid vacuous agreements; the claims should be substantive enough that each side would have had to concede something to sign them.

2. A disagreements table. Each entry should state the point of disagreement in a way both sides accept as fair, then give each side's position in wording that side would endorse. Disagreements should include at least: the status of Kripke's argument from the necessity of origin; whether the tolerance principle (if a table could have been made from slightly different wood, could it have been made from very different wood?) has force when applied to biological origins; and whether the anti-essentialist faces a sorites problem about how different the gametes can be before the resulting person is no longer you.

3. A section of cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. For each crux, state what argument, thought experiment, or empirical consideration both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction about what that consideration would show if fully pursued. At least one crux should concern the step from Kripke's original case (could this table have been made from a different block of wood?) to the application to gametes: the essentialist predicts that the inference carries over; the opponent predicts that a disanalogy can be identified that blocks the inference. State the disanalogy the opponent offers and the essentialist's reply, in terms each would accept.

4. A closing assessment of whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, and if so, which part and why. If neither side concedes any verbal dispute, explain why both agree the disagreement is substantive.
