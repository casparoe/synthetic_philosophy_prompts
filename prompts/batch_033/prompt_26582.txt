The core phenomenon is the color phi illusion. A red dot is flashed briefly at one location, followed after a short interval by a green dot at a nearby location. Subjects don't report two stationary dots of different colors. Instead, they report seeing a single dot that appears to move smoothly from the first location to the second and, crucially, seems to change from red to green roughly midway along the trajectory. The puzzle is that the green dot hasn't appeared yet at the moment the subject would have had to perceive the color change — so how can the experience include the change before the stimulus that would cause it? Dennett, in *Consciousness Explained*, frames two kinds of solution. The Stalinesque account says that conscious experience is itself delayed: the brain waits until all relevant sensory information is in, constructs a coherent narrative, and only then presents it to consciousness — rather like a Stalinist show trial where the verdict is scripted before the trial is staged. The Orwellian account says that the initial conscious experience was of two separate dots, but memory of that experience is rapidly and irrevocably overwritten — rather like the Ministry of Truth in *Nineteen Eighty-Four* retroactively revising the historical record. On the Orwellian view, you briefly experienced two dots; you just can't remember having done so, and your report draws on the revised memory. On the Stalinesque view, you never experienced two dots at all; consciousness was always of the moving, color-changing dot.
