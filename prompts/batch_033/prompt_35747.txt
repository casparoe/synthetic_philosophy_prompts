I've been trying to think through the justification of civil disobedience and my reasoning keeps bottoming out at a conclusion I can't accept. Here's the chain:

(1) A state's claim to political obligation — the moral duty to obey its laws — depends on its being reasonably just. A tyranny generates no such obligation.

(2) Even a reasonably just state will sometimes enact gravely unjust laws. Every real legal system has them.

(3) When a law is gravely unjust and normal political remedies have been tried and failed, citizens are morally permitted to disobey it as an act of conscientious protest. This is the standard liberal justification of civil disobedience.

(4) The judgment that a law is gravely unjust, and that remedies have been exhausted, is necessarily the citizen's own judgment. There is no moral court of appeal whose rulings are both authoritative and available at the moment of decision.

(5) From (3) and (4): each citizen is permitted to disobey any law they sincerely judge to be gravely unjust, once they sincerely judge that normal channels are exhausted.

(6) But "normal channels are exhausted" has no principled stopping point. A determined activist can always point to one more lawsuit, one more election, one more round of public persuasion. If we take the exhaustion requirement strictly, civil disobedience is never justified because channels are never literally exhausted. If we take it loosely, it becomes a vague threshold each citizen sets for themselves — which, combined with (5), means the system grants every citizen a personal opt-out from any law they feel strongly enough about.

(7) A system that grants every citizen a personal opt-out from any law imposes no genuine obligations; it offers suggestions backed by force. That is not what we mean by a legal system, and it is not a stable form of social order.

(8) So either civil disobedience is never justified (the strict horn of (6)), or the concept of political obligation is empty (the conclusion of (7)). Neither seems acceptable.

I suspect the problem is somewhere around the move from "a citizen may sometimes disobey" to "each citizen may disobey on their own say-so," but I can't see how to block that move without invoking an authority I've already denied exists. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it commit me to?
