People in my lab keep having this argument about control vs alignment. The pessimist says: once you grant that the model might be misaligned and much smarter than us, no ammount of supervision saves you, because the thing you're supervising is the one writing the reports you supervise. The optimist's comeback is always the same: you've just described a clever contractor. We hire contractors who don't share our goals all the time — we don't align them, we contain them: limited repo access, code review, no prod keys, staged payments, and the ability to fire them the moment something looks off. So the argument goes, 'control' is basically contractor management, and alignment is the luxury version of a problem we already know how to solve. I can't tell whether this analogy is doing real work or just smuggling in the conclusion. Audit it. Give me the correspondence table — client, contractor, contract, the monitoring, the sandbox, the threat of termination, maybe even the labor market that makes contractors behave — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. I especially want to know which disanalogies matter for the specific conclusion they're drawing (that control without alignment is a stable fallback) and which are cosmetic. Once the disanalogies are on the table, does the conclusion survive, or was the contractor analogy carrying it the whole time? And if it doesn't survive, what's a better analogy for what control-without-alignment actually commits us to?
