ok so I've been trying to get a handle on the counterpart theory vs transworld identity debate in modal metaphysics and I'm starting to think the whole thing might be a giant verbal dispute that nobody wants to admit is a verbal dispute. here's my rough understanding:

Lewis says when I claim "I could have been a plumber," I'm not saying I myself inhabit some other possible world as a plumber. I'm saying there's a *counterpart* of me — someone similar enough along contextually relevant dimensions — in another world who is a plumber. The standard view (Kripke, Plantinga, etc.) says no, that's nuts: *I* exist in other possible worlds, and "I could have been a plumber" is true just in case there's a world where I have that property.

What's driving me crazy is that both sides agree on every singe modal truth. Nobody's disputing whether I could have been a plumber or whether water is necessarily H2O or whether the statue could have been made of different clay. The fight is entirely about what *makes* those claims true — the underlying metaphysics and semantics. And yet this debate has been running since the late 60s and I've never heard of anyone switching sides.

My hypothesis, which I'm not confident about, is that this isn't a factual disagreement at all. It's a disagreement about what a metaphysical explanation should look like. Lewis wants everything to bottom out in a reductive, extensional framework (concrete worlds, similarity relations); the other side thinks that's a nonstarter because it violates the intuition that *I* am the one who could have been otherwise — not some other guy who resembles me. But maybe I'm missing a genuine empirical or formal stake in the ground.

So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is there a hidden empirical or formal question? Is it two frameworks that are equally adequate and the choice is about theoretical virtues? Or is it a genuine metaphysical disagreement where one side is just wrong about the structure of reality? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a theorem, a new distinction, or just admitting it's a matter of taste?
