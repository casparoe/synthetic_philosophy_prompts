So I'm doing this module on AI alignment and we've been going through inverse reinforcement learning — the idea that you can infer someone's preferences just by watching their behaviour. And I've got stuck on something that I can't seem to find a proper name for, and the lecturer sort of nodded and moved on when I brought it up.

Here's the thing. If you see someone take an action, you can't tell whether it's because of what they want or because of what they believe. Classic example: you see someone open a window. Are they too warm? Do they want fresh air? Do they believe the room smells? Do they want to hear the birds outside? The action is the same, but the underlying preference could be for temperature or smell or sound, depending on what they believe the window does.

But that's just the basic underdetermination point. The thing that's actually bothering me is deeper. To get anywhere, you have to assume you already know one of the two. If you assume you know their beliefs, you can work backwards to their preferences: they opened the window, and they believe the window lets in cooler air, so they must want to be cooler. But how did you know their beliefs? Well, presumably you inferred those from other actions. But to infer those beliefs from actions, you'd need to know their preferences. It's a circle. There's no point of entry.

I've been reading around and I've found things like the problem of other minds and the frame problem and reward ambiguity in the IRL papers, but none of them seem to be quite about this specific circularity — the way you can't break into the belief-desire loop without already standing somewhere inside it. It feels like it should have a name. It feels like the kind of thing Davidson or Dennett would have written about. Does it have a name? And if it doesnt, what should I call it, and how does it relate to the things I've already found — is it just a special case of reward ambiguity or is it doing something different?
