In a graduate seminar on free will and moral responsibility, two participants have spent the term arguing about whether Frankfurt cases refute the Principle of Alternative Possibilities. One holds that Frankfurt cases succeed: they show an agent can be morally responsible even when she could not have done otherwise, because the counterfactual intervener never actually intervenes. The other holds that PAP survives: either alternative possibilities remain in some form (the flicker strategy), or the cases illicitly assume the agent is responsible in the actual sequence and then use that to argue that PAP is false. The instructor has asked them to produce an adversarial-collaboration document rather than another round of rebuttals.

Draft that document. It should include:

(1) A numbered list of claims both parties can sign — about the structure of a canonical Frankfurt case, about what the original PAP says, about what each side's argument must do to succeed, and about what would follow for compatibilism and incompatibilism if PAP were shown false or upheld.

(2) A disagreements table. For each point of dispute, state the disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position. Avoid formulations one side would reject as a caricature.

(3) A list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one of them would, by each side's own lights, substantially move the dispute. For each crux, identify the argument, case, or consideration both would agree bears on it, and state what each side predicts that argument or case will show.

(4) If any part of their disagreement turns out to be terminological — e.g., about what counts as an "alternative possibility" or what "could have done otherwise" means — flag that explicitly and say what substantive disagreement remains after the terminology is cleaned up.

The document should reflect the state of the literature (the flicker strategy, the dilemma defense, the relationship between PAP and incompatibilism) but should not become a literature review. The point is to produce a text these two actual disputants could both sign, not a survey of everyone else's views.
