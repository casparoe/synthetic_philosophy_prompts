I teach an upper-level philosophy of physics seminar, about 14 students, one 90-minute session. We're doing Gödel's rotating universe and his argument that time is ideal. I want an activity where students actually encounter the closed timelike curves themselves — give them a simplified, game-like representation of the Gödel metric (maybe a grid with a light-cone tilting rule) and have them plot paths, discover they can return to their own past, and then wrestle with what that does to their concept of time. The activity should be structured so they don't just think "huh, weird" and move on — I want them to feel the vertigo Gödel was pointing at, and to see why he thought this was a problem for the reality of time rather than just a quirk of one solution. Rules, materials, timeline, debrief that maps what they did onto Gödel's actual argument, and an honest note on what the activity inevitably distorts about the physics. No lecture first — they should stumble into the closed timelike curves themselves.
