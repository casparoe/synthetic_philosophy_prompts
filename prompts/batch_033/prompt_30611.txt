I want you to imagine that you are advising the directors of a three-year research initiative in the philosophy of psychiatry. The initiative can fund roughly five focused projects, and its stated aim is to determine what Ian Hacking's looping-effects framework does and does not show about psychiatric classification. The directors want your argued view about which open conceptual questions in this area most deserve the initiative's resources, and in what order they should be pursued. I want a defended agenda, not a survey of Hacking's position and not a set of reading suggestions.

The sorts of questions I have in mind include the following. Where does the loop actually run: in patients' self-ascription, in clinicians' expectations, in institutional and economic incentives around diagnosis, in advocacy and identity formation, or in several of these at once? Does a looping effect threaten a diagnostic category's validity, its stability across time and settings, or only its downstream consequences for the people classified? Can Hacking's distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds be drawn in a principled way, or is it at best a difference of degree? You need not organize your agenda around exactly these questions, but treat them as the level of resolution I am after.

For each question you would prioritize, please say: why it is upstream of other work rather than downstream of it; how tractable it is for philosophers who can engage with empirical psychiatry and sociology but are not themselves running clinical or epidemiological studies; and what distinction, argument, or empirical development over the next two or three years would most raise or lower its priority. Please also identify the tempting lines of work you would explicitly not fund, and say why: because they are already well handled, because they collapse into ordinary social feedback without a distinctively Hacking-style loop, or because settling them would not change how psychiatric classification is criticized or practiced.

Be explicit about confidence. Distinguish the parts of your ranking you would defend under sustained criticism from the parts that are speculative bets, and say where the ordering would shift under reasonable alternative starting assumptions. Finally, if you think the request itself rests on a confusion — for example, if looping effects are too heterogeneous to define a coherent research agenda, or if the line between looping and ordinary social feedback cannot be drawn sharply enough to bear the weight I am putting on it — say so plainly and reframe the question rather than producing a polite ordering.
