I'm putting together a year-long research agenda for a mixed group of philosophers and ML interpretability researchers working on one question: whether, and in what sense, contemporary language models can be said to have beliefs. We are taking Dennett's intentional stance as our shared starting point, but we disagree about how far it carries over to systems trained by gradient descent on next-token prediction rather than evolved or designed to track the world. I don't want a survey of positions; I want a defended prioritization of the open conceptual questions. Concretely: which three or four questions about attributing beliefs or other propositional attitudes to LLM-style systems are most worth settling first, and in what order? For each question, say what makes it upstream of other questions, how tractable it is for philosophers working with interpretability researchers rather than for ML people alone, and what evidence over the next year or two would move it up or down the list. Please also address these specific candidates, even if you end up ranking some of them low: (a) whether the predictive success of the intentional stance for LLMs is enough to make belief attribution more than merely instrumental; (b) whether mechanistic interpretability findings — for instance, a direction in activation space that tracks a truth-evaluable proposition — could settle the question whether the attributed states are real, or whether that answers a different question; (c) how to handle a system that produces contradictory commitments across contexts, given that ordinary belief attribution presumes a single subject with a largely coherent belief set; and (d) whether a pretrained model's lack of perception and action loops means its states lack the kind of content ordinary belief attribution requires. Defend the ordering: I want the criteria you are using, where you are trading off tractability against upstreamness, and which question you would deprioritize because settling it would not change much downstream. Finally, say how confident you are in the ranking as a whole, and single out the point you are least sure of.
