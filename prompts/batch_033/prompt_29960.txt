I've been trying to write a short essay about Aunt Hillary and whether an ant colony can have a mind, and I'd like comments on the argument before I polish it. This is an early draft, so be blunt. I dont mind if you tell me the whole thing misfires.

Here's the core idea in rough form.

The standard Aunt Hillary setup is usually read as a functionalist argument: individual ants are just doing dumb local things, but the colony as a whole carries on a conversation, so maybe the colony has a mind in some extended or distributed sense. I want to push back on that.

My claim is that the thought experiment gets its force from an unnoticed asymmetry. Aunt Hillary only looks like a mind because the anteater does an enormous amount of interpretive work. The colony isn't producing a conversation by itself. What's actually conversing is the coupled system of the colony plus the anteater. The anteater reads pheromone trails, ant movements, and local interactions, and then translates that activity into something that sounds like an old aunt at a tea party. If we say that composite system is a mind, then by the same reasoning a thermostat plus a human reading the temperature could count as a mind, which seems too cheap.

A plausible response is that the ant colony is internally complex enough to merit the word mind, while the thermostat isn't. But then the deep question shifts. It's no longer "can an ant colony have a mind?" in a binary way. It becomes "how much internal complexity, of what kind, is enough for a mind?" And that's much harder to answer, because we don't have a principled threshold.

So the essay would end on that deflationary note: the Aunt Hillary case doesn't settle the question, it just moves the bump in the rug. The question isn't whether the colony has a mind, but what kind of internal organization earns the word, and why the colony's complex coordination beats the thermostat.

I'd like comments on three things in particular. First, am I being unfair to the functionalist line that the anteater is just a translation function rather than part of the minded system? Second, is the thermostat analogy too weak because thermostats lack goal-directed behavior and self-maintenance, while ant colonies forage, defend themselves, and reproduce? Third, is the conclusion interesting or just deflating? And if I've mischaracterized what's going on in the original Aunt Hillary discussion, tell me.

Also, please give the comments in American English.
