I'm a retired physicist and I've been reading decision theory lately — Savage, Jeffrey, the whole lot. One thing that's been bugging me is the debate about whether Dutch book arguments and representation theorems actually justify expected utility maximization as a norm of rationality. This argument has been going since at least the 1950s and I swear every paper I read just rehashes the same moves. One side says if your preferences violate the axioms you're vulnerable to being turned into a money pump, which is clearly irrational, so you'd better conform. The other side says that just shows the axioms describe a certain kind of consistency, not that violating them is irrational — maybe the supposedly irrational preferences are fine and the money pump is just taking advantage of a pattern you wouldn't fall for if you saw it coming. And around and around it goes.

My working theory, coming from physics, is that this is a disagreement about what counts as a justification at all — one side thinks a formal vulnerability argument is definitve, the other thinks it's just a formal trick unless it connects to something more substantive about what rationality is. But I'm new to this so maybe I'm wrong.

What I want is a diagnosis: what kind of disagreement is this really? Is it substantive, verbal, or are the two sides just answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — or is the whole thing just a stalemate we should accept?

Give me your answer as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Just the five points.
