So I'm a backend engineer and I've been following AI stuff pretty closely for years, but I never studied philosophy formally. Recently I got into an arguement with a coworker about the whole Blake Lemoine thing from 2022 — you know, the Google engineer who got convinced LaMDA was sentient, published those conversation transcripts, and then got fired. My coworker thinks Lemoine was just naive and anthropomorphizing a text predictor, end of story. But I feel like there's something more interesting going on there about what happens when a system is *designed* to sound like a person and then someone takes it at face value.

I stumbled on this idea from Daniel Dennett about "counterfeit people" — the notion that AI systems that impersonate humans are a kind of moral and epistemic hazard, not because they're actually people, but precisely because they're *counterfeits*: they exploit our social and cognitive machinery for treating things as persons, and in doing so they degrade trust and make it harder to take genuine personhood seriously. I don't know the nuances of Dennett's view beyond that sketch, but it seems like it was almost tailor-made for the Lemoine situation.

What I'd love is a careful analysis of the Lemoine/LaMDA episode through Dennett's counterfeit-people lens. Walk me through: what exactly would Dennett's framework say was happening there — who was being deceived, what was being counterfeited, and what harms (if any) were done? Where does the framework illuminate things that the "Lemoine was just dumb" take misses? And then the harder part: where does Dennett's framework *fail* to capture what was going on? For instance, Lemoine wasn't some random user — he was an engineer working on the system, testing it for bias. Does that change the analysis? And does the episode ultimately strengthen Dennett's case about counterfeit people, or does it reveal some tension or blind spot in the view? I'm less interested in whether LaMDA was *actually* sentient (I'm pretty confident it wasn't) and more in what the whole episode tells us about the framework itself.
