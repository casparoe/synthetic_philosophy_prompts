You are a professor of metaphysics with expertise in the metaphysics of modality and personal identity. I am a PhD student writing a dissertation chapter on origin essentialism, and I need your help constructing a thought experiment that isolates a specific problem for a strong version of the view.

The target is origin essentialism interpreted as claiming that biological origin is not only necessary but also sufficient for transworld identity: if an individual x originates from a particular sperm S and egg E in world w₁, and an individual y originates from the same S and E in world w₂, then x = y. The problem I want to isolate is the "branching origins" puzzle that arises from monozygotic twinning.

Here is the core structure I want the thought experiment to capture. In the actual world, sperm S and egg E fuse to form zygote Z, which splits early in development and produces twins, Alice and Beatrice. In a nearby counterfactual world, the very same S and E fuse at the very same time, but the zygote does not split — only one child, Carol, is born. Alice and Beatrice share a complete biological origin (S+E), and Carol also has that origin. If sharing an origin is sufficient for transworld identity, we seem to get a contradiction: Alice and Beatrice are distinct (Alice ≠ Beatrice), yet both would be identical to Carol. If sharing an origin is not sufficient, then origin essentialism underdetermines transworld identity whenever branching occurs.

Please do the following:

2. Walk through the reasoning that leads the sufficiency-including version of origin essentialism into trouble. Show exactly where the contradiction or the underdetermination arises, and make clear which premise is doing the damaging work.

3. Contrast this with a haecceitistic view — one that takes transworld identity facts as primitive and not grounded in qualitative properties or origins. Show that haecceitism can deliver a determinate answer (Carol is identical to Alice, or to Beatrice, or to neither, as a brute modal fact), but at the cost of making the answer look arbitrary and ungrounded. Explain whether this cost is merely aesthetic or whether it points to a deeper theoretical vice.

4. Identify the structural feature of the case that does the work — the feature without which the puzzle would not arise. Say what happens if you remove it.

5. Discuss how a defender of origin essentialism might respond. For each strategy, assess whether it satisfactorily resolves the problem or merely relocates it — and whether adopting it forces any other revision elsewhere in the theory.
