I'm a PhD student in political theory and I've been trying to reverse-engineer my advisor's view on dirty hands for like a whole semester now. She won't just tell me her theory — she makes us work through cases and only gives verdicts. I've been keeping track and I think I have enough to make a prediction, but I want to check my reasoning before I stick my neck out in a paper.

Here are her verdicts on the cases we've discussed:

1. Ticking-bomb torture (official tortures a suspect to find a bomb that will kill dozens): dirty hands. She says the act is justified but the official is morally stained.

2. Politician breaks a campaign promise because the economic situation changed dramatically after the election: NOT dirty hands. She called this "ordinary politics, not a moral violation."

3. President authorizes a drone strike on a terrorist leader knowing it will also kill several civilians in the compound: dirty hands.

4. Cabinet minister lies to the press about a policy still under debate, to prevent a market panic that would hurt pensioners: NOT dirty hands. She said this was "role-appropriate discretion."

5. Prime minister diverts funding from a rural vaccination program to emergency flood relief, knowing some children will die of preventable disease as a result: dirty hands.

6. Mayor approves a development project that will displace a low-income neighborhood, in exchange for the developer funding a new hospital that saves the city from bankruptcy: NOT dirty hands. She called it "tragic but not a case of dirty hands."

7. Intelligence chief authorizes blackmail of a foreign diplomat to extract information that prevents a terrorist attack: dirty hands.

Next week we're discussing a new case: a state governor authorizes the release of a convicted violent offender (who is statistically likely to reoffend) as part of a prisoner exchange to secure the release of hostages held by a militant group. The governor knows the actuarial risk but has no information about whether this particular prisoner would actually reoffend.

What's she gonna say? I'm less interested in the bare prediction than in what rule or principle she seems to be running, what other rules fit her verdicts just as well, and which of those rules the hostage-exchange case would distinguish.
