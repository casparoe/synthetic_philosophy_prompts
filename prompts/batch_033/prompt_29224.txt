I sit on the diagnostic standards committee of a national health service. We are deciding whether to add a new diagnostic code for 'subthreshold autism' — children with marked social-communication difficulties and restricted interests who do not meet full criteria — where inclusion would unlock funding for support services. The decision has recently become personal: my nephew has just been described this way, and I have watched the phrase begin to reorganise how his parents and school talk about him. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before next month's vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action — adopt the code, reject it, or adopt it with safeguards — with a time frame and conditions the committee could implement. It should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side, and it should address the looping-effect worry that the category would not merely describe a population but help to create it, drawing on Ian Hacking's work without making the memo read like a philosophy lecture. Write in British English, and begin directly with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary, opening with your recommendation in the first sentence.
