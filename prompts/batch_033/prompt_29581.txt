Been trying to sort out my views on the ethics of belief lately, partly because my best friend got accussed of something at work and I noticed I was still believing her on evidence I'd never accept from a stranger. Before I commit to anything I want a consistency check on five things I'm inclined to hold. (1) A Clifford-style evidentialism: a belief is justified only if your evidence supports it, and wanting it to be true doesn't count. (2) Anti-pragmatic encroachment: practical stakes don't change the threshold for justified belief; high stakes mean you should check more, not that you need more evidence for the same belief. (3) Doxastic involuntarism: you can't believe at will, so any epistemic ought has to attach to what you do control—inquiry, attention, which sources you trust. (4) Epistemic partiality: you're allowed, maybe required, to keep trusting a friend's word in cases where you'd doubt the same story coming from a stranger. (5) Self-fulfilling beliefs: there are cases where believing ahead of your evidence is okay because the believing helps make the thing true, like confidence that a fragile relationship will last or that you can finish a hard project. I keep telling myself these mostly cohere, but I might be papering over something. Go through the pairs: which are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, rank the real tensions by severity, and tell me the cheapest revision and which single change would clean up the most.
