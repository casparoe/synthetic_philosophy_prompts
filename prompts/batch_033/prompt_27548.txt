I'm on the residential life committee at a small liberal-arts college, and we're finally revisiting our blanket ban on alcohol anywhere on campus. A classics professor on the commitee keeps bringing up Plato's Laws — he says Plato wasn't a prohibitionist, that he thought well-run drinking parties were a kind of training in self-control, a way of testing the soul under the influence, and that a city should regulate them rather than ban them outright. Half the committee thinks that's a real argument for piloting a few supervised wine nights for seniors; the other half thinks a fourth-century-BC argument about symposia has nothing to say to a modern college with a drinking problem. I need to circulate a short memo before next week's vote recommending a concrete policy with a time frame. It should actually engage the Plato point — not wave it away as old books, but not treat it as an automatic answer either — and it has to say honestly what's strongest about the losing side. Draft the memo.
