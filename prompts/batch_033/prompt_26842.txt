I've been thinking about pleasure lately — not in any formal way, just turning it over in my mind — and I've gotten stuck on a question that seems simple but keeps unraveling. We use the word "pleasure" for so many things: the taste of a ripe peach on a hot day, the click of solving a puzzle that's been bothering you for hours, the feeling of warm bathwater after you've been out in the cold, the satisfaction of watching someone who bullied you finally get what they deserve, the ache of hearing a song that takes you back to being seventeen, the release of a good cry after a hard week, the surprise of a joke landing just right, the relief of scratching a mosquito bite. I call all of these pleasures. But when I try to find what they share — what makes each of them a pleasure rather than something else — I come up empty.

At first I thought the answer was obvious: a pleasure is just an experience that feels good. But that runs into trouble fast. The nostalgia-song doesn't feel good in any straightforward way; it hurts, and I still seek it out and call it a pleasure. The good cry isn't pleasant while it's happening, but afterward I'd say it was a kind of pleasure. And the mosquito bite — scratching it feels good, sure, but it's really the removal of a bad feeling, not the arrival of a good one. So then I thought: maybe a pleasure is whatever you'd choose to experience again, all else equal. That handles the nostalgia and the cry, but it seems too broad. I'd choose to take a nap when I'm exhausted, but is napping a pleasure? I'd choose to have a splinter removed, but that's not a pleasure either. And it also seems too narrow in the other direction: there are pleasures I wouldn't choose to repeat — the schadenfreude about the bully, for instance, which I'm not proud of and wouldn't deliberately seek out, but which I can't honestly say wasn't pleasurable when it happened.

So I've been trying to work out whether there's a single principle that captures what all these cases have in common, or whether "pleasure" is just a loose label for a family of experiences that don't share any one thing. If there is a principle, I'd like it stated clearly enough that I can test it against new cases. And if the principle forces me to give up some of my verdicts — to say that the nostalgia-song isn't really a pleasure, or that scratching an itch doesn't count, or that the good cry belongs in a different category — I want to know which ones have to go and why. I'd also like to hear what the principle says about a case I haven't thought of, preferably one where the answer isn't obvious, so I can see whether the principle is actually doing work rather than just redescribing what I already told you. Please write your answer in American English.
