I keep running into people who use Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary / ant colony thing from Gödel, Escher, Bach to argue that a mind doesn't need a central controller. The colony is suposed to be like a brain: individual ants play the role of neurons, the chemical signals between them play the role of action potentials, and the colony's coordinated behavior is a kind of emergent thought. In the dialogue Aunt Hillary even claims to have a soul at the level of the signals rather than the individual ants. I'd like an audit of how far this analogy actually holds. Give me the correspondence table first — what maps to what, and at which levels (ant, signal, colony). Then the disanalogies: where does the mapping break, and which breaks matter for the intended conclusion and which are just superficial? In particular, what does the brain side quietly import that a colony lacks — point-to-point wiring, the speed and synchrony of electrical signaling, neurons not having individual agendas, a single unified 'I'? And then the payoff: does the 'distributed system can have a mind' conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is the analogy doing all the work? If it mostly breaks, what would be a better analogy for what an ant colony actually is?
