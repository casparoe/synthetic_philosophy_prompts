I'm a novelist, working on a book where a big company functions almost like a character—I keep wanting to write sentences like "the company knew the product was dangerous" or "the firm wanted the merger to fail." But I also keep thinking of that Hofstadter ant colony thought experiment, where the colony does all this clever stuff and no individual ant has any idea what its doing. So here's what I want to know. We have a whole practice of talking this way: courts treat corporations as having knowledge and intent, prosecutors charge them with crimes, newspapers say "the market panicked." What theory of mind does that practice presuppose? Does it actually commit us to a group-level mind, the way you might say the ant colony has one, or is it just convenient shorthand nobody literally believes? And is the practice even internally consistent—law says a company can intend things, but also that it can only act through the people inside it? If I decide in the novel that only individual people have minds, what would that force me to change in how I write the company, and would it also force us to scrap corporate criminal liability, or can the law keep the fiction without the metaphysics?
