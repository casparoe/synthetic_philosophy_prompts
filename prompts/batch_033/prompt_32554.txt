Write a dialogue between an advocate of the Self-Sampling Assumption and an advocate of the Self-Indication Assumption. The dialogue should center on a specific exchange: the SIA advocate challenges the SSA advocate to explain why SSA does not commit its adherents to the Doomsday argument—and whether the moves available to block it (such as narrowing the reference class or rejecting the Copernican assumption about one's birth rank) are principled or merely ad hoc. The SSA advocate replies by pressing the Presumptuous Philosopher as a cost of SIA, demanding that the SIA advocate either bite the bullet on overturning cosmology on the say-so of a single philosopher or explain why the case does not constitute a fair test of the principle. The two should then explore whether either position can be stated in a way that avoids its characteristic embarrassment without thereby collapsing into the other view. Both characters must be presented as competent and philosophically serious; neither should caricature the other's position. Begin the dialogue directly, with no preface or commentary.
