I'm presenting a paper at a climate ethics workshop next month. The paper defends a qualified version of the inefficacy objection: the claim that an individual action that makes no discernible difference to the occurrence or severity of a harm is not morally required, even when the action belongs to a type that, if performed by many, would cause that harm. I argue that this applies to individual consumer choices about flying and meat consumption but does not extend to voting or to cases with definite, knowable aggregation thresholds. The audience will be roughly thirty philosophers: mostly consequentialists who work on climate ethics, several Kantians who have written on universalizability and the formula of universal law, and two political theorists who work in the tradition of Iris Marion Young and will likely see my framing as missing the point about structural injustice. I will have twenty minutes for Q&A. Draft my prep sheet: the objections I will actually get from this audience, phrased as they would phrase them, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks. For each reply, tell me how confident you are in it and single out the point you are least sure of.
