In my political philosophy seminar we've been on constitutional precommitment — the idea that a constitution is a bit like Ulysses having his crew tie him to the mast so he can hear the Sirens without steering the ship onto the rocks. A friend presented an argument that seems to prove way too much, and I can't see where it slips. Here's his reconstruction:

(1) Ulysses is rational, and within his rights, to have himself tied to the mast and to have his crew refuse to untie him when he later begs. (2) What makes this legitimate is that the person bound is the same person who did the binding: it's self-rule — his earlier, clear-headed will protecting him against his own foreseeable weakness. (3) A constitution is supposed to be the same move at the level of a polity: the people, at a relatively calm founding moment, bind their future selves with supermajority rules, bills of rights, and independent courts, because they know future majorities will act on panic or short-term interest. (4) If (2) is what legitimizes Ulysses's ropes, then the same feature legitimizes constitutional precommitment — the community at the founding moment is the same "self" as the later community, so this is still self-rule, not rule by the dead. (5) But then the reasoning also legitimizes a founding majority writing in a clause that abolishes future elections and forbids any amendment whatsoever. After all, that's just the same self binding itself very thoroughly. (6) And that can't be right: a democratic majority cannot, by a one-time vote, legitimately ensure that there will never again be a meaningful democratic vote.

I'd most like to keep (1) and (6), and I suspect something goes wrong with how much work "same self" is being asked to do in (4) and (5), but I can't say precisely which premise to reject or what rejecting it would cost. Please tell me which step goes wrong, or which premise is least costly to give up, and what the main alternatives have to pay. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
