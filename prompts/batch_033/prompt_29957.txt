I have been trying to make sense of two things Aristotle says about money, and I keep finding myself going in circles. On the one hand, he claims that money exists by convention rather than by nature: a community could, in principle, change its currency or make it worthless. On the other hand, he claims that lending money at interest is unnatural—indeed, one of the most unnatural ways of acquiring wealth. What confuses me is this: if money itself is only a convention, then charging interest is simply one more convention we could adopt or abandon, and it seems strange to single out any particular convention as unnatural when none of them were given to us by nature to begin with. Is Aristotle contradicting himself, or have I misunderstood what he means by unnatural?
