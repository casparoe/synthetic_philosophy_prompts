I am a student in a high school philosophy seminar, and I am preparing a contribution for our unit on the philosophy of mind. The topic I have chosen is the question of whether dreams are experiences — specifically, whether it makes sense to say that we undergo genuine conscious experiences while we are asleep and dreaming, or whether this way of speaking rests on a conceptual confusion.

I would like to produce a dialogue between two interlocutors who represent the two main positions in this debate. The first interlocutor defends a broadly Malcolmian view: that the ordinary concept of dreaming is not the concept of an experience that occurs during sleep but rather the concept of a disposition to tell a certain kind of story upon waking, and that the question "do I have experiences while dreaming?" is not an empirical question at all but arises from a misunderstanding of the grammar of the word "dreaming." The second interlocutor defends a broadly Dennettian view: that we can and should say that dreams involve experiences, but we must abandon the picture of experiences as private, Cartesian theater-like events; instead, dreaming experiences are best understood as the product of a narrative-construction process that may occur partly during sleep and partly upon waking, with no determinate fact of the matter about what "really" happened in the dream independently of the story the dreamer tells.

The dialogue should be a genuine engagement: each character must respond to the other's actual arguments, anticipate objections, and refine their position under pressure. Neither character should be a strawman, and neither should obviously win — the point is to display the depth and seriousness of the disagreement. The dialogue should run roughly 1500 to 2000 words. I will add an introduction and a brief commentary myself; what I need is the substantive exchange between the two characters.
