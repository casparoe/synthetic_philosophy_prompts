I've been reading Tolstoy's *What Is Art?* (1897) alongside some histories of twentieth-century avant-garde movements, and I keep coming back to a single episode that seems to crystallize the tension between Tolstoy's theory and the trajectory art actually took. In 1917, Marcel Duchamp submitted a porcelain urinal — titled *Fountain* and signed "R. Mutt" — to the first exhibition of the Society of Independent Artists in New York. The society had pledged to exhibit any work submitted by any artist who paid the six-dollar fee, yet the board (which included Duchamp himself, though he had submitted the piece under a pseudonym) voted to exclude it. The episode is usually taught as a pivotal moment in the history of conceptual art: a challenge to the very idea that art requires craftsmanship, aesthetic intention, or even an identifiable art-object as traditionally understood.

I would like you to analyze this episode — both the object itself and the board's decision to exclude it — through the lens of Tolstoy's theory of art. Tolstoy held that art is the conscious transmission of feeling from artist to audience by means of external signs; that genuine art must be sincere (the artist must have lived the feeling being transmitted), clear (accessible to all, not just a cultivated elite), and morally oriented toward the unification of humankind; and that work failing these criteria is not merely bad art but counterfeit art — something that simulates the transmission of feeling without actually accomplishing it, serving instead to flatter the vanity of artists and patrons. Map these criteria onto *Fountain* and the circumstances of its submission and rejection. Where does Tolstoy's framework illuminate something about what was really at stake in the episode, and where does the episode expose limitations, blind spots, or outright errors in Tolstoy's account? I am especially interested in whether Tolstoy's category of "counterfeit art" can handle a work that seems to reject the transmission model of art altogether, and whether the board's rejection of *Fountain* looks, by Tolstoy's own lights, like a defense of genuine art or merely the policing of a different kind of counterfeit. Do not just summarize the two sides; give me a disciplined analysis that runs in both directions — theory illuminating episode, episode testing theory.
