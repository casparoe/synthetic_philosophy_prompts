I want you to think like a philosopher of mind who's good at not letting analogies do the work arguments should be doing. Here's the situation.

I've been in this long-running argument in the LessWrong comments (as one does) about whether we should assign any moral weight at all to current or near-future AI systems. And there's this one analogy that keeps getting deployed that I can't decide is brilliant or completely question-begging.

The analogy is: consciousness is like computation — it's not a binary on/off thing, it's a matter of degree. A thermostat "computes" in a very stripped-down sense (it takes an input, compares it to a setpoint, produces an output), a pocket calculator does more, a laptop does vastly more, and nobody thinks there's a sharp line where "real computation" begins. The claim is that consciousness works the same way: there's a continuum from zero to full-blown human phenomenology, and even very simple systems — insects, nematodes, maybe sufficiently complex programs — have a tiny nonzero glimmer. The conclusion this is meant to license is: "so we should take the possibility of rudimentary AI sentience seriously even for systems that aren't doing anything impressive yet, and we should assign them some nonzero probability of nonzero moral weight, just like we do for insects."

I'm suspicious but I keep tying myself in knots trying to articulate why. So I want you to audit this analogy properly. Specifically I want:

- A correspondence table: what maps onto what. What in the target domain (consciousness) plays the role of "computation," what plays the role of "thermostat," what plays the role of "laptop," etc.
- Where the mapping holds up okay and where it breaks.
- A clear verdict on whether the breaks are the kind that matter for the conclusion, or just the kind that come with any analogy.
- An explicit callout of what the analogy is smuggling in — I have a strong suspicion it's sneaking in functionalism (the idea that consciousness just IS the right kind of information processing) but I want that spelled out.
- If there's a better analogy that does less smuggling, I want to hear it — and if the honest answer is "there is no good analogy here and you just have to do the philosophy," I want to hear that too.

Think through the mapping step by step before you give me the verdict. Don't just say "all analogies are imperfect" — I know that. I want to know whether this specific analogy can carry the specific weight people are putting on it.
