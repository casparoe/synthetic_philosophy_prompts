I'd like your credence that a broadly deflationary solution to the meta-problem of consciousness is correct. By "the meta-problem" I mean the problem of explaining why we are disposed to judge that there is a hard problem of consciousness — why we find the relationship between physical processes and phenomenal experience philosophically puzzling, why we are inclined to think there is an explanatory gap that physical facts alone cannot close. By a "broadly deflationary solution" I mean an account on which, once we have a complete explanation of the cognitive mechanisms that generate these intuitions, there is no residual fact about consciousness that remains unexplained: the hard problem is dissolved rather than solved, and our sense that something has been left out is itself one of the things the deflationary account explains away.

Please decompose your credence into at least the following terms:

(a) The probability that our phenomenal intuitions are generated by mechanisms that systematically misrepresent the relationship between physical processes and experience. (For example, introspective models might treat experience as having non-physical properties because that is a computationally efficient way to represent sensory states, without those non-physical properties being real features of the world.)

(b) The probability that, conditional on (a) being true, a deflationary account would actually dissolve the sense of an explanatory gap for a rational subject who fully understood it — as opposed to leaving a residual "but it still feels like something" that resists deflation even after the generating mechanisms are laid bare.

(c) The probability that, conditional on (a) being false — i.e., our intuitions do track something real about the mind — the thing they track is nevertheless not an unbridgeable explanatory gap. (For instance, it could be a real but physicalistically explicable feature of information processing that our introspective models simply misdescribe as non-physical, so that the correct account is realist about the target of the intuition but deflationary about its apparent metaphysical import.)

(d) Any other term you think belongs in the decomposition, with a brief justification for including it.

Give a rough probability for each term and show how you combine them into your overall credence. Then tell me: which term are you least confident about, and what kind of evidence — empirical, conceptual, or both — would most shift your credence on that term?
