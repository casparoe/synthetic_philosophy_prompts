I am a software engineer, and I have been asked to help draft an internal policy document about how much direct contact with users an advanced AI system should be permitted to have. I have no formal background in philosophy, so I have been reading around the topic as best I can. One article I read summarized the "persuasion problem" as follows:

"Once an AI is substantially more intelligent than we are, it will be able to model each person's psychology well enough to choose the words most likely to move that person. A system with this ability could persuade almost anyone of almost anything; no human would be in a position to reliably resist its arguments. At that point, persuasion operates through cognitive biases rather than through good reasons, which means that an advanced AI that talks to people is by default manipulating them. Since we would not be able to detect that manipulation when it happened, the only safe policy is to prevent advanced AI systems from communicating with humans directly."

That summary struck me as broadly right, and it would push the policy document toward a very restrictive stance. Before I let it shape what I write, I would like an accuracy audit of the summary itself. Please go through it sentence by sentence and say which parts a careful person working on AI persuasion would accept, which parts they would treat as overstatements or conflations, and which parts are just wrong. In particular, does the final conclusion — that advanced AI systems should be kept from communicating with humans at all — actually follow from the parts of the summary that survive correction?
