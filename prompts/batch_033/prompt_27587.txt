I've been reading the aesthetic testimony literature for a seminar and I'm starting to get genuinely frustrated. The basic question is whether you can form justified aesthetic beliefs on the basis of what other people tell you — like, if a friend whose taste you trust says a movie is brilliant, can you justifiably believe it's brilliant without seeing it yourself? The pessimists say no: aesthetic judgments require first-person experience; you can't just take someone's word for it the way you can for, say, the capital of Burkina Faso. The optimists say this is snobbery dressed up as epistemology — we rely on testimony for all kinds of knowledge, why should aesthetics be special?

Here's what's bugging me: this debate has been running for at least twenty years now, and I feel like both sides have made all their moves and nobody's budged. The pessimists keep pointing to cases where it seems weird or deficient to hold an aesthetic belief purely on testimony — like telling someone at a dinner party that a painting is "stunning" when you've never seen it. The optimists keep pointing out that we do this all the time in practice and it seems to work fine — we read reviews, we trust recommendations, we build syllabi around works we haven't personally experienced. Both sides seem to agree on the phenomenology but disagree about what it shows.

I'm trying to figure out what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a verbal dispute about what counts as "justified" or "aesthetic"? Is it a disagreement about the function of aesthetic discourse — whether it's for reporting private experiences or for coordinating attention and taste? Is one side smuggling in a norm (you shouldn't defer aesthetically) and mistaking it for an epistemic claim (you can't)? Or is there some hidden empirical question about how aesthetic testimony actually works that, if settled, would break the stalemate? I want a diagnosis that engages with what each side actually says, and an honest answer about what would count as progress — or whether we should just stop expecting the debate to resolve.
