I run a small team that writes safety assessments of industrial plants for a regulator, and I have to sign off on every one. We're under pressure to go faster, and my boss wants us to divide and conquer: one analyst reads only the maintenance logs, another only the incident reports, a third only the training records, and then a lead stitches their writeups into a single judgment about the plant. Sometimes this works fine. Other times the lead misses a contradiction between two analysts that a single person reading both would have caught, or each part looks fine but the whole plant is obviously not safe and the lead can't see why. I want a reusable decision procedure for when to factor an assessment into separate subtasks and when one analyst has to hold the whole thing. I've heard this called factored cognition, and I want the procedure to be true to that idea, not generic management advice. The analysts won't sit through any theory, so the procedure has to be just questions and rules, in order, with a clear statement of what would settle each one. It needs a point where it says stop and don't factor, and it needs to mark the places where it can't decide and has to kick the call up to me because it's a judgment rather than a fact. Then test it on two cases: a small routine assessment where the temptation is to split it anyway, and a big messy one where the parts look separable but the whole is more then the sum of them. Make sure at least one case gives a result I won't like, and be honest about where the procedure will misfire. Write the whole thing as continuous prose only, no headings, bullets, bold, or tables, like an email.
