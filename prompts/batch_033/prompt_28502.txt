I've been following the Knobe effect literature since grad school and I'm starting to think the whole debate has just ground to a halt. You know the basic finding: people say the CEO brought about the environmental harm intentionally when it was a side effect of a profit-maximising decision, but not when it was a side effect of a pro-environment decision. Twenty years of papers later, we've got the blame account, the norm-violation account, the desire account, the trade-off account, and half a dozen others, and as far as I can tell nobody's convinced anyone who wasn't already on their side. My working hypothesis is that it's three debates wearing one trenchcoat — one about the semantics of 'intentionally', one about the psychology of blame, and one about what experimental philosophy is even supposed to be doing — and the participants don't realise they're not all answering the same question. But I could be wrong; maybe it's a straightforward empirical dispute that just needs better data. Diagnose it using what the different camps actually say, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a new experimental paradigm, a conceptual distinction everyone's missed, or just accepting that the effect is robust but its interpretation isnt going to be settled by more vignette studies.
