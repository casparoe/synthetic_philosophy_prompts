I'm a philosophy professor, and I've been toying with an idea for a research proposal I'd like to submit to a small internal fellowship — a semester of course release. The general area is suffering-focused ethics, specifically lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: the view that below some threshold, pleasures and pains can be traded off in the usual way, but above the threshold, no amount of pleasure or other good can compensate for a pain. I keep coming back to a worry that the threshold move doesn't actually solve the commensurability problem; it just hides it. Below the threshold the view owes us an account of how a mild pleasure and a mild pain are commensurable (and what the exchange rate is), and above the threshold it owes us an account of how two terrible pains are compared to each other and how we locate the threshold in the first place. So my half-formed question is whether lexical threshold negative utilitarianism has a coherent account of commensurability, or whether it's really two seperate scales with an arbitrary border.

Can you turn that into a one- or two-page proposal? It should state the question precisely enough that the project has a determinate answer, explain why existing discussions of negative utilitarianism and of value incommensurability don't already settle it, and lay out what I'd actually do. I'm imagining something like: first, distinguish several things that could be meant by lexical priority (a discontinuity in value, a deontic constraint, a kind of incomparability); second, work through a few hard cases — one excruciating pain vs. any number of mildly pleasant experiences, two pains both above the threshold but one clearly worse, and the question of whether the threshold is sharp or vague — and see which combinations of views can handle them without collapsing back into ordinary negative utilitarianism or an ad hoc threshold. Also say what a successful outcome would look like and what would count as the project failing, and keep the scope realistic for a semester with one course release. I'll add the citations myself.
