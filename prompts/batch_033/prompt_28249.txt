ok so in my psych class we were talking about consciousness and my teacher used this analogy: consciousness is like a theater. there's a stage where all your perceptions, thoughts, feelings show up, and there's an audience — like a little "you" inside your head — that watches the show. and the teacher said this is why there has to be a central self or an "inner observer" that ties all your experiences together, because otherwise who's watching?

but then i went down a rabbit hole and found this philosopher daniel dennett who says the whole theater idea is garbage. he calls it the "cartesian theater" and says it's a trap that messes up how we think about the mind. his alternative is the multiple drafts model — he says consciousness is more like a bunch of drafts of an essay being written and revised all at once, in parallel, and there's no final draft and no one ever "reads" the finished thing. there's no central place where it all comes together.

i'm trying to figure out whether my teacher's analogy actually fails or if dennett's just being provocative. can you audit the theater analogy? like, what exactly maps onto what — what's the stage, what's the audience, what's the show? where does the mapping break? what does the analogy quietly import about the mind that might not be true? and most importantly, does the conclusion my teacher drew — that there has to be a central self — survive once the dissanalogies are on the table? also if the theater analogy is broken, is the multiple drafts thing actually a better analogy, or does it have its own problems?
