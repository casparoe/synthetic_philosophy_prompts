The debate between the language of thought hypothesis—most prominently defended by Jerry Fodor—and its competitors has been running since the 1980s without clear resolution. The classic exchange is Fodor and Pylyshyn's argument that connectionist architectures cannot explain the systematicity and compositionality of thought without implementing a classical architecture, and the replies from Smolensky, Chalmers, and others showing that connectionist networks can approximate systematicity. But the debate has outlived that exchange, morphing into arguments about whether non-classical systems genuinely exhibit compositionality or merely simulate it, and about whether the systematicity constraint is an empirical discovery about cognition or a methodological demand we bring to it.
