I'm taking a seminar on contemporary moral philosophy, and we've spent three weeks on Scanlon's contractualism and the aggregation problem. Our professor has given us a series of verdicts on cases, and I'm trying to work out what principle she's actually running — because I think more than one principle fits her verdicts so far, and next week's case will force a choice.

Here are her verdicts:

1. **Rescue (one vs. five)**: You can save one person from drowning or five from drowning, but not both. Verdict: save the five.
2. **Transmitter room**: A worker will be painfully electrocuted unless you turn off the transmitter, but doing so interrupts a World Cup broadcast and ruins the viewing experience for millions. Verdict: save the worker.
3. **Drug scarcity**: One dose of a scarce drug. Patient A will die without it; Patients B through F will each suffer a week of moderate pain without it. Verdict: give it to A.
4. **Legs**: You can prevent one person from losing a leg or two people from losing a leg. Verdict: prevent the two from losing legs.
5. **Headaches vs. paralysis**: A public health choice: prevent one person from permanent paralysis, or prevent ten thousand people from each having a single mild headache. Verdict: prevent the paralysis.
6. **Severe vs. moderate (small numbers)**: Prevent one person from a year of severe chronic pain, or prevent four people from a year of moderate chronic pain. Verdict: she called this a hard case but ultimately said prevent the severe pain.

Next week's case is: prevent one person from a year of severe chronic pain, or prevent two hundred people from a year of moderate chronic pain.

Predict her verdict on the new case. Identify at least two distinct principles that fit all six of her earlier verdicts and that diverge on the new case. Say which principle you think she's actually running and why, and name a further case (beyond next week's) whose verdict would settle which principle she holds — a case where the principles make opposite predictions and where neither prediction is obviously unreasonable. Lead with your bottom-line prediction in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
