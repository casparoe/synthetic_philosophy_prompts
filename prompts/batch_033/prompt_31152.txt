For a paper I am drafting in formal epistemology, I would like a consistency audit of the position I have found myself defending. My commitments are these: (i) veritism — the accuracy of a credence function, understood as its closeness to truth-values across the relevant worlds, is the sole fundamental epistemic good, and all other epistemic norms are to be derived from it; (ii) the central constraint on a legitimate measure of inaccuracy is strict propriety, and I am inclined to think no further restriction is needed; (iii) probabilism — the thesis that rational credences satisfy the probability axioms — is justified by the accuracy-dominance argument; (iv) conditionalization is justified by the expected-accuracy argument; (v) the Principal Principle holds: when one knows the objective chance of a proposition, one's credence should equal that chance; (vi) immodesty is a requirement on rational credences: a rational agent's credences should regard themselves as at least as accurate as any rival, by their own lights. Do these commitments hang together? Please identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, rank the tensions by severity, and specify the cheapest repair for each; also flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection. Finally, tell me which single revision would do the most to make the package coherent.
