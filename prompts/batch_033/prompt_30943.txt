Our teacher argued that the reluctance people feel here is inconsistent: we routinely form and revise beliefs on the testimony of doctors, mechanics, and historians, and no one regards that as a failure of intellectual autonomy. So, the teacher concluded, deferring to someone on a moral matter — accepting their word that some action is wrong, for instance — is no more problematic than deferring to a doctor about a diagnosis, and anyone who refuses to take moral testimony is just being squeamish. The analogy felt suspicious to me, but I couldn't say exactly where it goes wrong.
