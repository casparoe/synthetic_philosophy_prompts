Im in a philosophy of mind seminar this semester and we just finished a couple weeks on the dream debate. We read Malcolm's 1959 book Dreaming and Dennett's 1976 paper Are Dreams Experiences?, plus a few replies, and I came away feeling like the whole thing has been stuck since the seventies. Malcolm's position, as I understand it, is that there are no dream experiences during sleep: saying you dreamed something is not reporting an inner event that happened while you were asleep, its really a matter of the story you're disposed to tell when you wake up, and the idea of checking whether an experience was going on during sleep is a conceptual confusion. Dennett comes back and says dreams are experiences, but the naive picture of them as an inner movie playing in real time while you sleep is what misleads us; his cassette theory has the dream being composed and stored during sleep and then played back on waking, which is why it feels like it happened during sleep. The more I read the replies, the more it looks like they're talking past each other about what experience even means here, and about whether the timing of a mental event can be settled by memory alone.

My working theory is that its partly a verbal disagreement about the word experience and partly a real disagreement about whether waking memory can ever fix when a mental event occurred. Is that right? What I want is a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not a verdict on who wins. What kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, factual, about standards of evidence, about bedrock intuitions, or just two people answering different questions under one heading? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, an empirical finding about sleep and memory, a different question — or should I just conclude the question is ill-posed and move on?
