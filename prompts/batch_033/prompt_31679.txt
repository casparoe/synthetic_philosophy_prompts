A friend of mine sent me a blog post that summarizes Dennett's stuff on the three stances and real patterns, and I have a feeling its off but I can't say exactly where. The post says:

'Dennett distinguishes three ways of predicting a system. The physical stance predicts from its physical constitution, the design stance from what the system was designed to do, and the intentional stance treats the system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires. His controversial claim is that beliefs and desires aren't internal states we can locate in the brain; they're real patterns in behavior that only show up when you take the intentional stance. But since taking the stance is just a predictive strategy, Dennett is happy to say a thermostat literally believes the room is too cold and wants it warmer — any system has beliefs and desires as long as the intentional stance works on it. So his view is a kind of instrumentalism: mental states are useful fictions, not real things. That's also why he says consciousness is an illusion.'

I'd always heard Dennett described as a realist about real patterns who denies being an instrumentalist, so this summary seems like it's sliding into something else. Can you go through it and tell me which bits are fair, which are common simplifications, and wich a Dennett defender would reject? And does the last line about consciousness actually follow from the accurate parts, or is it bolted on?
