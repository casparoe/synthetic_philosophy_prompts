I am a philosophy professor preparing a seminar on the problem of universals for doctoral students in computer science, and I have been arguing with a colleague in programming languages about whether the entire debate can be translated into the vocabulary of types and their inhabitants. My colleague suspects the debate dissolves into questions of type design; I suspect that is too quick. I have enough working fluency in the target vocabulary to follow precise claims about type constructors, parametric polymorphism, subtyping, nominal versus structural typing, typeclasses, singleton types, dependent types, and the compile-time/run-time distinction, but I want the translation done systematically rather than by slogan. Take the canonical positions in the debate over universals — Platonic realism about ante rem universals, Aristotelian or Armstrong-style immanent realism, class nominalism, resemblance nominalism, trope theory, and conceptualism where it is distinct enough to matter — and restate each one in that vocabulary. For each, mark what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no counterpart at all.

Then give me the residue report I actually care about. Aggregate what failed to translate, and tell me whether that residue shows that the type-theoretic vocabulary is missing distinctions the metaphysics needs, or that the metaphysical claims in question are less determinate than they look, or some of each. Keep the translation honest: where fitting a position into the vocabulary quietly assumes the very thing at issue, flag that rather than letting the vocabulary settle the debate by default. I am especially interested in three pressure points: the distinction between a property and its extension; the question of what makes a grouping natural rather than arbitrary; and the question whether the universal is in any sense prior to its instances. Begin with your overall verdict, and keep the entire response under 1,200 words.
