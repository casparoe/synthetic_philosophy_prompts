Write a villanelle from the perspective of an agent who finds herself in a state of epistemic akrasia: she believes some proposition P, while simultaneously judging that her total evidence does not support P. The two refrains should be concise articulations of (1) the first-order belief and (2) the higher-order assessment of her own evidence—each iterated version of the refrain may shift slightly in wording, but always recognizably the same stance. Use the villanelle’s structure so that the alternating refrains enact the way the akratic tension refuses to dissolve: no matter how the agent turns over the evidence or reflects on her own reliability, the two voices keep breaking in on one another, neither absorbing the other. In the final quatrain, bring the two refrains together in adjoining lines; the closing couplet should not resolve the akrasia but capture what it feels like for the agent to see both perspectives simultaneously while remaining unable to let either go. Make the poem accurate enough that someone who knows the Level-Splitting / Non-Akrasia Constraint literature would recognize where the fault lines lie, and why this is not just a psychological report but a problem about the structure of epistemic rationality itself.
