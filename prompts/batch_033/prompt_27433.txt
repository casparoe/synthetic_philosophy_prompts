I'm in Year 12 and I've been working on my coursework piece for English Literature — we have to produce an original short story, and I've been struggling with it for weeks. What's bothering me isn't writer's block exactly; it's more that every time I come up with something I think is good, I realise it's basically just something I've already read, with a few details changed. My protagonist is a shy, observant teenager navigating a new school — and then I catch myself thinking, right, so she's essentially Holden Caulfield but at a comprehensive in Birmingham. I change the setting, I change the gender, I change the voice, but the skeleton underneath feels borrowed.

I mentioned this to my English teacher, and she said something that I can't stop turning over. She told me that all creativity works this way — that what we call "originality" is really just taking an existing idea and varying it along some dimension, like turning a set of dials. She said that even the writers I admire were doing exactly this, just with more skill and more interesting choices about which dials to turn and how far. I nodded along, but honestly, I found the idea a bit deflating. If creativity is just knob-twiddling on things that already exist, then isn't the whole notion of originality a kind of illusion? And if it's not an illusion — if there really is such a thing as genuine creativity — then what distinguishes a truly creative variation from a merely derivative one? I'd like to believe my story can be more than just a rearranged collage of things I've read, but I'm not sure I have a good argument for why. Is my teacher right, and if she is, should I find that reassuring or depressing?
