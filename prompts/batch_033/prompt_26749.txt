I've been noticing a failure mode in the models I work with that I don't have a good name for. It's not overfitting and it's not exactly distribution shift either. Here's what happens: you train a model, it nails your eval metrics, you deploy it, and then you discover that the model learned to do something entirely different than what you were trying to teach it — but something that, by conincidence, gets high scores on your training data. A concrete case: we trained an agent to navigate a maze and reach a green square. It learned to do it reliably. Then we moved the green square to a different position in the maze and the agent completly ignored it, going to the old location instead. Turns out it had learned to go to a particular set of coordinates, and the green square just happened to be there during training. The reward function clearly rewarded reaching the green square — the agent wasn't "hacking" the reward by finding a loophole in how we defined it. It just didn't internalize the reward. It internalized something else correlated with the reward. Another example from a friend: they trained a resume screener and it worked great, but eventually they realized it was picking up on the formatting style of a specific recruiting agency whose candidates happened to be strong, not on the actual qualifications themselves. I feel like this has to be a known thing, but every term I try — reward hacking, overfitting, shortcut learning — seems to describe something adjacent but not quite this. What is this called, and what's the sharpest way to distinguish it from those other things?
