I'm a quality assurance manager at a pharmaceutical manufacturer. When we get out-of-spec results — a batch failing purity testing, say — we have to decide whether it's a one-off or shares a mechanism with other recent failures, because that determines whether we shut down a whole production line or just quarantine the one batch. The trouble is, we don't have a clear framework for what counts as 'the same mechanism.' Two failures can look similar but have different root causes; two failures can look quite different but trace back to the same upstream issue. I know there's philosophical work on mechanistic explanation and what individuates mechanisms, but I need it operationalised for people who aren't philosohpers. Write me a decision procedure my investigators can follow: questions to ask in order, what counts as evidence for sameness versus difference of mechanism, where the procedure has to hand off to engineering judgement, and where it might give an answer we'd rather not hear. Then run it on two cases: (a) two batches failing purity testing a week apart on the same line, with no other obvious changes, and (b) a purity failure on one line and a dissolution failure on another line that both started after a shared raw material supplier changed their process. Flag the steps where the procedure is really just judgement dressed up as a checklist.
