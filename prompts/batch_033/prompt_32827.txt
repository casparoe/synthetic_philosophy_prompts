Consider five reconstructions of Williams's example in which a man saves his wife rather than a stranger. Rank them by how vulnerable they are to the one thought too many charge, from most vulnerable to least, with one sentence explaining each placement. The reconstructions are: (1) the man acts on the unreflective thought that it is his wife; (2) he first judges that the situation falls under a general permission to favor one's spouse; (3) he saves her because he has adopted a policy of giving special weight to his personal commitments; (4) he acts immediately but later justifies his action by invoking an impartial rule; (5) he acts immediately and, when pressed, says only that she is his wife. In your ranking, distinguish the one thought too many objection from a general objection to moral principles as such, and say whether post hoc justification creates the same defect as a motivating thought at the time of action.
