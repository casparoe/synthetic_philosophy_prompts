I've just joined my state's parole board, and before my first vote I've been reading through the panel's past decisions. We sit in panels of three. Release requires two findings: low risk of reoffending, and a workable release plan. Each commissioner votes yes or no on each of those two questions, and the panel then issues a single decision. The written records dont spell out how the three individual votes get combined into the panel's decision.

The three commissioners I'll be sitting with — call them Delia, Marcus, and Ruth — have decided five cases this year:

- Inmate A: Delia and Marcus yes on low risk, Ruth no; all three yes on release plan. Parole granted.
- Inmate B: all three no on low risk; Delia and Ruth yes on release plan, Marcus no. Parole denied.
- Inmate C: Delia yes on both, Marcus yes on both, Ruth no on both. Parole granted.
- Inmate D: all three yes on low risk, all three no on release plan. Parole denied.
- Inmate E: Delia yes on both, Marcus no on both, Ruth yes on both. Parole granted.

Next week we have Inmate F. Delia says low risk yes but release plan no. Marcus says low risk no but release plan yes. Ruth says yes on both.

How should the panel decide Inmate F if it wants to be consistent with its own track record? Which past case is the closest analog? If those five precedents genuinely underdetermine the new one, tell me what rule the panel would be adopting with each possible decision, and what future case would finally reveal which rule they've been using all along.
