(1) advance directives should generally be followed because the person made them when they were competent and that's when they were in the best position to decide for themselves.

(2) but if the person with dementia is happy and content — like, genuinely seems to be enjoying their life, even if it's a very simple life — then it seems cruel to enforce a directive that says to withhold treatment or food. like, the person in front of you is happy, how do you justify making them die?

(3) i think personal identity is about psychological continuity — memories, preferences, personality traits, that kind of thing — and not about bodily continuity.

(4) and from (3) it seems like the person with advanced dementia is basically a different person from the one who signed the directive. maybe not entirely different, but different enough that the earlier person's decisions shouldn't automatically bind them.

(5) but i also think that in ordinary life, if i sign a contract or make a serious promise, i'm bound by it even if i change my mind later. and that seems fine. so there's gotta be some difference between the contract case and the advance directive case, but i can't articulate what it is.
