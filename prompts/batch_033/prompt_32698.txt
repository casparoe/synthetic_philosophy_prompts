I've been reading Gödel, Escher, Bach on my own — I don't have a philosophy background, I'm just someone who got curious — and I keep coming back to the idea that the self is a strange loop. The way I understand it: a system of symbols, complex enough to refer to itself, gives rise to an "I" that feels like it stands outside the symbols, when really it's just the system tangled up in itself. The self isn't a thing; it's a pattern of self-reference so tangled that it perceives itself as a thing, a knot in the flow of symbols that mistakes itself for the knotter. I'd like a villanelle that captures this.

The two refrains should enact the tangled hierarchy. One refrain should speak from the level of the symbols — the substrate, the neurons, the marks on paper, the meaningless mechanism. The other should speak from the level of the self that seems to float above them, the "I" that claims it is doing the thinking. Each should claim to be the ground of the other. The form's repetitions — the refrains trading places, returning when they shouldn't, each one's reappearance pulling against the other's — should do the work of showing how the "I" keeps looping back on itself, never able to step outside the loop to see it from above. And in the final couplet, where the two refrains finally meet side by side, the poem should reveal them as two aspects of the same loop, not two things at all.

Someone who knows Hofstadter should recognize the idea, but the poem should also stand on its own as a poem. I don't want it to name-drop Gödel or Bach or use the phrase "strange loop" — the form should do the concept, not just label it.
