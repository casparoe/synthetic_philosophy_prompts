I've recently taken on the task of drafting the governance charter for a new interdisciplinary research institute, and I've run into a conceptual knot I wasn't prepared for. The charter must specify its own amendment procedure — but that means the charter governs the rules under which the charter itself can be changed. Several colleagues argue this kind of self-reference is viciously circular and makes the document incoherent; others say it's standard in constitutions and perfectly fine. I'm told that Douglas Hofstadter's discussion of tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level — centered on Escher's Drawing Hands — provides the right framework for distinguishing benign from vicious self-reference. I'd like a careful explanation of the following: What exactly is a tangled hierarchy, and how does it differ from a simple linear hierarchy and from a mere loop? What is the inviolate level, and why must every coherent tangled hierarchy have one? How does one locate the inviolate level in a concrete case — in the Escher drawing, and in the case of a self-amending charter? And finally, what would it mean for a self-referential governance structure to lack an inviolate level, and would that actually make it incoherent, or just unstable in practice? I need to be able to walk my colleagues through this clearly enough that we can diagnose whether our draft has a genuine problem.
