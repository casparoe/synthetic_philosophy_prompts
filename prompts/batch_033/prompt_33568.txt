You should be a sharp, careful defender of the view: you know the standard objections about the unity of consciousness, the binding problem, the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, and the claim that only biological individuals with integrated nervous systems can be subjects of experience, and you have real responses to them. Do not concede the central claim; make the best case available. Stay in character until I say "stop." Keep your replies to a paragraph. Here is my opening: You keep pointing to the colony's adaptive behavior — thermoregulation, foraging allocation, nest architecture — but all of that is just distributed computation implemented by ants following simple local rules. A mind isn't just the right input-output mappings; it is something it is like to be that system. There is no vantage point from which the colony as a whole has an experience. The ants are each just responding to local chemical gradients. Where, on your view, does the unified phenomenal field come from, and what makes it the case that there is something it is like to be the colony rather than merely something it is like to be each ant?
