I manage a fair-lending analytics team at a regional bank. Every quarter we review our credit decision models against an internal fairness policy that lists three criteria we aim to satisfy simultaneously: calibration across protected groups, equal false-positive rates across groups, and equal false-negative rates across groups. I have been told there are mathematical results showing these three cannot all hold when base rates differ, but I need to turn that abstract fact into something my team can actually run.

Write a decision procedure for model review that does the following: diagnoses which fairness criteria are in conflict for a given model and applicant pool; walks us through the tradeoffs in a fixed sequence; identifies the exact steps where we must make a value judgment rather than a technical one; and specifies when the conflict is severe enough that we should escalate to legal and policy because our stated criteria are jointly impossible and the policy itself needs revisiting. The procedure must be usable by analysts with statistical training but no specialized background in the algorithmic fairness literature, and it should take no more than half a day per model.

Then apply the procedure to two cases. Case A: a mortgage approval model where default base rates differ substantially between two age groups. Case B: a small-business loan model where base rates are nearly equal but the model uses different feature sets for different geographic regions. For each case, note any step where the procedure yields an answer the team will find uncomfortable or where it forces a choice we would prefer to avoid.
