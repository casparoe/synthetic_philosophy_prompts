ok so I've been reading the back-and-forth between longtermists and neartermists in EA for like five years now and I still feel like nobody has actually sat down and produced the kind of adversarial collaboration document that would force both sides to stop talking past each other. You know the kind Scott Alexander writes about — where you get both camps to agree on a joint statement and then precisely nail down where they actually diverge.

I want you to draft that document. Specifically, between a committed strong longtermist (someone who thinks the far future swamps near-term effects in expected value and that we should orient our priorities around influencing the long-run trajectory of civilization) and a committed neartermist (someone who thinks expected value reasoning over very long time horizons is too epistemically fragile to justify deprioritizing near-term suffering, and that we should focus on interventions where we can actually see the results).

The document should have:

- A numbered section of claims both sides can sign. These should be substantive, not just "we both agree 2+2=4." I'm talking about points in population ethics, expected value theory, existential risk, epistemic humility, etc. where there's actually overlap.
- A disagreements table. Each row: neutral statement of the disagreement, the strong longtermist's preferred formulation of their own view, and the neartermist's preferred formulation of theirs. Each side should read their own column and say "yeah, that's what I think."
- Cruxes. The sub-questions that, if resolved, would shift at least one side's confidence meaningfully. For each crux, what evidence or argument would both sides agree is relevant, and what each side predicts about it.
- If any part of the dispute is mostly verbal (e.g., different definitions of "expected value" or "priority" that make the disagreement look bigger than it is), flag it.

Also, please separate what's genuinely contested among people who actually know this stuff from what's basically settled. If there's a view that's contested, don't present it as the consensus. And if one side's position tends to get caricatured in blog posts, give the strongest version of it, not the dumb one.
