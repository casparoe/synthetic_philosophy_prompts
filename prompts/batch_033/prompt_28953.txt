I would be grateful for your advice on a disagreement I keep having with my sister. For years we have both drunk the same brand of coffee every morning. She now says that the coffee tastes exactly as it always did, but that she no longer enjoys it. I say something like the reverse: the coffee now tastes different to me—flatter and harsher than it used to—yet I still enjoy it as much as ever. We are each sincere, and neither of us has any reason to doubt the other's honesty or competence. What I want to know is whether there is a fact of the matter about what has changed for each of us, and whether our disagreement could in principle be settled by closer attention or by some further test. Is one of us making a mistake, are we talking past each other, or is the question somehow ill-posed? Please do not offer generic relationship advice; I am interested in the underlying structure of the disagreement. In your answer, please distinguish what is fairly settled among people who have thought carefully about this kind of case from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested interpretation as if it were the consensus. It would also help if you said how confident you are in your judgement and where it depends on assumptions that a reasonable person could reject.
