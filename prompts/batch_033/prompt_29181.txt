I am a novelist, and I have been asked to give the opening remarks at a small workshop on evolutionary approaches to fiction. The audience will be roughly half evolutionary and cognitive scientists and half novelists and critics, so the remarks must work for people who know the adaptationism debate well and for people who have never read Gould and Lewontin. I would like the speech built around their spandrels critique, applied to narrative craft: the idea that not every feature of a story needs to have been selected for its current function, and that byproduct explanations deserve a fair hearing alongside adaptive ones. The speech should make that case without turning into a polemic against evolutionary explanation or an excuse for sloppy thinking, and it should leave the room with a shared standard for telling a genuinely illuminating adaptive account of a feature of fiction from a just-so story. Fifteen minutes, opening remarks, and it should land as an invitation to better joint work rather than as a lecture.
