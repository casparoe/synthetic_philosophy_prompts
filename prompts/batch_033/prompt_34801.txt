ok so i've been going back and forth on pascal's mugging type cases and i want to figure out what principle i'm actually operating with, if any. here are my verdicts — some are standard, some i made up:

1. classic mugger. a stranger on the street says "give me $10 or i'll use my magic powers to torture 3↑↑↑3 people." i don't pay. feels obvious.

2. the causal ripple. a friend tells me if i don't venmo them $10, their sadness will ripple through the causal network of the universe and eventually cause a plane crash. i don't pay. feels basically the same as the mugger, even though the mechanism is "natural" rather than magical.

3. the climate bike. i'm deciding whether to bike to work instead of driving. the chance that my specific trip's emissions are the straw that breaks the camel's back — tipping us past a climate threshold and causing millions of deaths — is something like 1 in 10^8. i think i should bike. this feels different from cases 1 and 2 but i'm not sure i can articulate why.

4. the positive-ev lottery. someone offers me a ticket for $1. it gives a 1 in 10^8 chance of winning $10 billion. expected value is about $100. i think it's fine to buy and fine not to buy. i don't feel strongly either way.

5. the physicist. a respected physicist with a solid track record tells me that according to their calculations, there's a 1 in 10^8 chance that powering on a certain experimental device will trigger vacuum decay and destroy the universe. the device would also cure a child's terminal illness. i think i shouldn't power it on. this verdict feels right, and it also feels different from the mugger even though the numbers are similar.

6. the future ai. a stranger online claims to be an ai from the year 3000. they say if i don't wire them $1000, they'll run a simulation containing a trillion conscious beings in unbearable suffering. i don't pay. feels like the mugger.

7. the lottery with stupid numbers. same setup as 4 but now the ticket costs $1 and gives a 1 in 10^100 chance of winning $10^100. the expected value is $1, technically. but i could tweak the numbers to make the ev arbitrarily large — 1 in 10^1000 for $10^1000, etc. at some point i stop feeling neutral and start feeling like something has gone wrong with expected value reasoning entirely.

so what i want: is there a principle that captures all or most of these verdicts? if not, which ones do i have to abandon? and what does the principle say about cases i haven't considered — like, what if the mugger asks for a penny instead of $10? or what if the physicist's probability is 1 in 10^20 instead of 1 in 10^8? or what if the climate bike probability drops to 1 in 10^20? please write the answer in american english.
