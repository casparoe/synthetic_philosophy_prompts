I teach an upper-level undergrad seminar on weakness of will and time preference — around 18 students, one 75-minute session. Design an in-class game that gets them to feel preference reversal as it happens: at the start of the session they make a plan about a choice they'll face later, and when the later moment arrives a decent number of them should want to go back on the plan, even though nothing about the payoffs changed except how close the sooner reward is. Use real small stakes (candy, quiz points, whatever works). The setup should make the reversal come from the shape of their own discounting, not from new information or social pressure, and the debrief should map what happened to the difference between exponential and hyperbolic discounting and to akrasia as a conflict between an earlier self and a later self. Give me rules, materials, a payoff schedule, a timeline, what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and one honest note about which feature of real akrasia the game can't reproduce.
