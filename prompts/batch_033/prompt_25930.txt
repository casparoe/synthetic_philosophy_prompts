I've been following the debate about probability in the many-worlds interpretation for years across rationalist forums, and I think the two main camps have been talking past each other long enough that a structured adversarial collaboration would be productive. I'd like you to draft the document such a collaboration would produce.

The two camps are:

**Camp A (Derivationist):** The Born rule is not an independent postulate. A rational agent who accepts the bare Everettian picture — a unitarily evolving universal wavefunction with decohering branches — can be compelled by decision theory, self-locating uncertainty, or symmetry arguments to assign credences across branches that match the Born weights. The probability rule is therefore a theorem of rationality applied to the ontology, not an extra physical law.

**Camp B (Skeptic):** The Everettian picture as such provides no resources to derive the Born rule. Every attempted derivation either implicitly assumes something equivalent to the Born rule (e.g., that branch weights should matter for rational choice), or relies on premises a skeptic has no reason to grant (e.g., that one should care about branches in proportion to their amplitude). MWI therefore either fails to explain quantum probabilities or succeeds only by being supplemented with a postulate that is functionally identical to the one it was meant to eliminate.

Draft the joint statement these two camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want:

1. **A numbered list of agreed-upon claims.** These should cover: what the bare Everettian formalism gives us before anyone mentions probability; what exactly needs explaining (i.e., what a satisfactory account of quantum probability must do); what results like Gleason's theorem and the decision-theoretic work of Deutsch and Wallace do and do not establish, stated in terms both sides accept; and which attempted derivations are acknowledged by both sides to fail, and why.

2. **A disagreements table** with these columns:
   - *The precise statement of disagreement*, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position (no strawmen).
   - *The crux*: the sub-question on which the disagreement turns, such that a settled answer would move at least one side.
   - *A concrete test, argument, or formal result* that both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction or interpretation of that evidence recorded in advance.

I am especially interested in whether any part of the dispute is verbal. For instance: do the two sides mean different things by "deriving" the Born rule — one requiring a deductive proof from the formalism alone, the other allowing that rational constraints plus the formalism jointly entail it? Do they disagree about what counts as an acceptable explanation in physics — one side holding that a decision-theoretic argument is explanatory, the other insisting that only a physical mechanism counts? If you find that some disagreements dissolve under sharper definitions, say which ones and why, and include those clarifications in the agreed-upon claims rather than in the disagreements table.

Format the output as a proper adversarial-collaboration document: a preamble that both sides sign, then the numbered agreed claims, then the disagreements table, then (if applicable) a brief note on which disputes were found to be verbal and how they were resolved.
