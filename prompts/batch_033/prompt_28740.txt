I keep seeing cognitive scientists gesture at "the Letter Spirit problem" — what makes every 'a' an 'a' across typefaces, handwriting, sizes, and degrees of degradation — but nobody has laid out the specification a theory of it would have to meet. Before anyone builds another model, I want the full desiderata. What must an adequate theory of letterform identity account for? Give me the requirements: the phenomena it has to handle, the boundary cases it must get right or explain away, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the empirical constraints from psychology and neuroscience it must respect. For each requirement, give the concrete case or finding that motivates it — not just "handle variation" but exactly which variation, and why it poses a difficulty for naive approaches. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect optional commitments a theory could drop, and at what cost. Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most to rule out simple template-matching or feature-list accounts — the one that forces a theory to say something nontrivial about what a letterform category actually is.
