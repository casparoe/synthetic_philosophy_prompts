I'm a philosophy professor, and last week a colleague in my department cornered me at a department mixer about something that's been eating at her. She's choosing between two collaborators for a research project, and she insists the two are so evenly matched—same expertise, same amount of funding, same likely impact, even the same travel distance—that she has no reason to pick one rather than the other. So she's been digging for a tiebreaker: who has a slightly better library carrel, who answered emails faster, who has a nicer campus office. Meanwhile both people seem to assume she's joining them, and the longer she waits the more awkward it gets. I told her she should just pick, even if it's by flipping a coin, but she said that would make the choice irrational because it wouldn't be based on a reason.

I have a feeling she's wrong, but I couldn't articulate why in the moment. Is it rationally permissible to choose when your reasons are exactly balanced, or does a choice like that become arbitrary in a bad way? What, if anything, would make flipping a coin here more than a cop-out? And please say how confident you are in your answer, and single out the point you're least sure of.
