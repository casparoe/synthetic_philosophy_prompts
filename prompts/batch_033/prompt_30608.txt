I draft legislation for a state legislature and charters and bylaws for municipalities and membership organizations. Several times a month I am asked to evaluate proposed "precommitment" provisions: rules designed to bind future decision-makers in advance, such as supermajority requirements for tax increases, balanced-budget amendments, cooling-off periods before major asset sales, and entrenched mission clauses that can be amended only by near-unanimous votes. The bodies I serve cite the idea of Ulysses binding himself to the mast, but they use the phrase loosely, and I worry that some of these provisions are wise self-restraint while others are simply a current majority trying to handcuff future members or voters. The stakes are high because these provisions are deliberately hard to repeal, and my drafting committee—mostly non-specialists—must sign off on my recommendation, usually within a few days.

Please write me a decision procedure I can run each time such a provision is proposed. I want it as a reusable sequence of questions or a checklist, derived from the actual content of Ulysses-contract and constitutional-precommitment theorizing and keeping straight the distinctions that matter for drafting—for example, binding oneself against one's own anticipated weakness versus binding successors, credible irrevocability versus dead-hand rigidity, changed circumstances, and who may release whom. For each step, say what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it must hand the decision to an elected body or the membership because the remaining question is one of values rather than facts. Be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

Then run the procedure on these three cases:
1. A balanced-budget amendment requiring a two-thirds supermajority for any deficit spending in a given year.
2. A membership organization's bylaw entrenching its founding purpose so that it can be changed only by a vote of 85% of all members.
3. A board rule requiring that any decision to sell the organization's headquarters be confirmed at two consecutive board meetings at least 90 days apart.

Include at least one case in which the procedure returns an answer my committee and I will not like, and explain why that answer falls out of the procedure rather than your own preferences. Mark the places where the procedure cannot settle the matter and a political or ethical judgment is required.

End each worked case with a line beginning "Verdict:" stating whether the provision should be adopted, rejected, or adopted only with specified changes. At the very end of the whole answer, give a one-sentence summary of the procedure as the final line, formatted as \answer{...}.
