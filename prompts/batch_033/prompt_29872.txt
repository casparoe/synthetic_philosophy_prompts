I run market surveillance at a prediction market — elections, Fed decisions, M&A, that kind of thing. We review maybe 30 flagged trades a day, and each one has a real cost if we get it wrong: escalating a hedge or a market maker's inventory adjustment burns legitimate traders, missing an insider's trade before a news event gets us fined. Someone on our team read about no-trade theorems and now the working rule is that rational people with common priors shouldn't trade just on differing information, so any trade that moves the price is evidence someone has private information or different priors. We've been using that as an excuse to escalate nearly everything and we're drowning. Please write us a decision procedure we can actually run on a flagged trade — the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with don't escalate, where it stops with escalate, and where it hands the call to a person because the question is one of judgment (priors, risk tolerance, intent) rather than fact. Keep it runnable in 15 minutes by an analyst without a PhD. Then run it on two flags: (1) a large one-sided bet in our M&A market placed twenty minutes before a deal is announced; (2) two established market makers who trade against each other every month around the nonfarm payrolls release, each claiming the other's model is wrong.
