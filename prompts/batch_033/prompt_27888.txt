I'm a retired invertebrate physiologist, and I've been roped onto my university's animal care and use committee as a community member. A protocol's come up from a lab that wants to do invasive electrophysiology on live octopuses, and our current policy only covers vertebrates, so the lab says its out of our remit and we should just approve it as routine. A couple of committee members—one a younger philosopher who consults with us—are pushing to extend the same anesthesia and humane-endpoint rules we apply to mice to cephalopods, on the grounds that octopuses are at least as likely to be conscious as many vertebrates. Others worry this is a slippery slope: if we cover octopuses, why not crabs, bees, or nematodes, and then we're legislating on pure speculation. I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting. I'd like it to recommend a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—and to actually engage the question of where we draw the line and why, rather than just reciting the regulations. Draft it for me.
