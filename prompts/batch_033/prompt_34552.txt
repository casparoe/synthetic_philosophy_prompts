I've been following the debate about unrealistic assumptions in economic models basically since I read Friedman's "The Methodology of Positive Economics" a few years ago and got into the philosophy of science side of the rationalist blogosphere, and I'm starting to think the whole thing is just stuck. On one side you have the Friedman camp: what matters is predictive accuracy, and the realism of assumptions is irrelevant — a model with obviously false assumptions (perfect competition, rational agents, no transaction costs) can still make good predictions, and that's all science asks. On the other side you have people saying that's absurd: if your assumptions are false you haven't explained anything, you've just curve-fit, and you can't tell whether your model will keep working when conditions change.

This has been going back and forth since the 1950s and both sides have stock replies to everything. Friedman says "as if" reasoning is fine; critics say "as if" only works if you already know the system behaves as if the assumptions were true, which is exactly what's in question. Friedman says unrealistic assumptions are a feature not a bug because they tell you which factors don't matter; critics say that only works if you already know which factors don't matter, which is what's in question. Around and around it goes.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is. Is it a verbal dispute about the word "explanation"? Is it a disagreement about the goals of science — prediction versus understanding — that's ultimately normative and can't be settled by further argument? Is there a hidden empirical claim buried in here about when models with false assumptions tend to generalize, or about which kinds of idealizations are harmless and which aren't? Or is it something else entirely — maybe the two sides are just asking different questions and talking past each other under a shared label? And given your diagnosis, what if anything would actually resolve it, or should we just accept that the two sides have different epistemic values and stop expecting convergence?
