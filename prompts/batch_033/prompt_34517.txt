Ive been reconstructing Scanlon-style contractualism for a seminar and it keeps giving me the right answer in one case and an answer I cant live with in another, which makes me think there's a bad inference hiding somewhere. The reconstruction goes:

(1) An act is wrong just in case it would be disallowed by the principles that no one could reasonably reject — put the other way, it's permissible only if some reasonably unrejectable principle allows it.
(2) Reasonable rejection works off generic reasons: what a candidate principle would mean for a particular person's life. These reasons are not to be added together across persons.
(3) So when two principles compete, the question is whether either is ruled out by the strongest individual complaint against it; the number of people with weaker complaints does no work.

The good result: we shouldn't let one person die just so a million others can watch a soccer match, because no single fan's complaint comes anywhere near the dying person's complaint, and adding up a million mild complaints is exactly what (2) forbids. Fine, I want that.

(4) But now a rescue case. I'm in a boat and can reach either five strangers or one stranger, all of whom will drown if I don't reach them. Against the principle requiring me to save the five, the one has a complaint of strength N: her own death. Against the principle requiring me to save the one, each of the five has a complaint of the very same strength N.
(5) By (1)–(3), neither principle is more reasonably rejectable than the other. No individual complaint against one principle is stronger than any individual complaint against the other, and I'm not allowed to add the five complaints together.
(6) So the fact that there are five of them gives no reason to prefer saving the five. Numbers don't count; the best I can do is something like a coin flip between saving one and saving five.
(7) But that's crazy. Numbers obviously count. So either the reconstruction is wrong, or contractualism is. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd most like to keep (2), but I can't see how to get save-the-five out of it without smuggling aggregation back in under another name.
