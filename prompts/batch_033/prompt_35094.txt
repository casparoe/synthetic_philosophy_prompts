I serve on the board of a small housing cooperative, and we're about to allocate our six garden plots for the coming season. One member has asked for three of them, even though we have twelve members. His argument is straightforward: he's a passionate gardener—it's the center of his life—and he'll cultivate every square foot intensively from March through October, while most other members plant a few herbs, lose interest by midsummer, and let their plots go to weeds. He says the board's job is to arrange things so the garden yields as much happiness as possible, and on that measure, giving him the extra plots is the obvious call. The facts he cites about other members' past neglect are basically accurate. I feel resistant to his proposal, but I'm having trouble saying why, since on its own terms the reasoning seems to work. Before our next meeting, I want to get clear: is there a defensible objection to his argument, or am I just being squeamish about a conclusion that follows from premises I actually share?
