I work as a journalist covering science and ideas, which means that a substantial part of my working epistemology consists of deciding, often under deadline, whether to believe what a source tells me: an immunologist explaining a preprint, a statistician characterizing a rival's model, a university press office describing a result I cannot independently reproduce. I keep running into a philosophical debate I only half-know, and I would like it translated into the vocabulary I actually think in. The debate is over testimony: whether a hearer's justification for accepting what someone says is always ultimately built out of other epistemic resources — perception, memory, inference, a track record establishing the speaker's reliability — or whether testimony is a basic source of warrant in its own right, something we may accept by default unless there is a specific reason not to. My working vocabulary includes sources, attribution, on- and off-the-record, corroboration by independent sources, fact-checking, due diligence, the difference between a source's track record and a claim's internal plausibility, and the distinction between being able to verify something and having it on good authority. I am fluent in that vocabulary and not fluent in epistemology.

Restate the reductionist and anti-reductionist positions about testimony one at a time in those terms, as faithfully as you can, without pretending that a journalist's working assumptions are neutral. Then give me the residue report: which commitments translate cleanly, which ones you had to distort to fit, and which ones have no counterpart at all in how a working journalist handles sources. Finally, tell me whether the residue suggests that my vocabulary is missing something real about testimonial knowledge, or whether the philosophical contrast is less sharp than it looks once it is forced into practice. If something like the journalistic norm of seeking independent corroboration maps onto a particular philosophical thesis, say which; if it maps onto several, or onto none, say that too.
