Me and a friend went in circles last night about Aumann's agreement theorem and whether two rational people who've actually talked it out can keep disagreeing. We ended up with four candidate reasons real disagreements persist despite the theorem, and we couldn't rank them. Could you put them in order from best to worst as explanations of why the theorem doesn't just rule out persistant disagreement, with a sentence for each? (1) people don't share a common prior; (2) their posterior probabilities for the disputed claim are almost never common knowledge, so the theorem's precondition isn't met; (3) people aren't perfect Bayesian updaters; (4) the theorem only constrains posteriors for a single well-defined event, so it's too thin to cover actual worldview disagreements.
