I have been working through the metaphysical literature on the unity of the proposition, and I find myself increasingly convinced by a position rooted in Bradley's regress. The claim, stated plainly, is this: any attempt to explain the unity of a fact or proposition by positing a relation among its constituents is structurally incapable of succeeding, because the relation itself must then be unified with the constituents it was meant to bind, launching a regress that is vicious rather than benign. On this view, the very idea that a proposition is a complex whose parts are held together by a relating relation — an instantiation tie, a copula, a non-relational tie, whatever one calls it — rests on a mistake. The regress shows that unity is not the kind of thing that can be supplied by adding another item to one's ontology, and so relational metaphysics cannot, even in principle, account for how a thought manages to be about something rather than merely being a heap of representations.

I want to stress-test this position. I am not asking for the standard responses to Bradley that one finds in introductory surveys — I have read those. What I want is a systematic map of what would count against the claim that the regress is a fatal problem. Specifically: what would a world, or a successful theory, have to look like for the regress to be merely apparent, or benign, or an artifact of a dispensable assumption? Which premises of the regress argument can be rejected, and what are the costs of rejecting each? Are there accounts of instantiation, or of truthmaking, or of predication, that genuinely stop the regress from starting rather than simply declining to discuss it? I would also like to know which of these escape routes are live research programs and which are merely logical possibilities that nobody has managed to develop. Finally, I want an honest assessment of whether the position I am drawn to risks unfalsifiability — whether it has been stated in a way that no conceivable development in metaphysics could count against it, and if so, whether that is a defect in the position or simply the character of a piece of negative metaphysics that functions as a constraint on theorizing rather than as a theory in its own right.
