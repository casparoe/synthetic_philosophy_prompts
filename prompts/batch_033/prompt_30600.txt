I have recently had reason, for personal reasons I would prefer to keep to myself, to spend a great deal of time inside the world of psychiatric diagnosis, and I keep noticing a pattern that I cannot name. A person receives a diagnosis, say a personality disorder. The diagnostic criteria describe the condition in terms of certain behaviors, dispositions, and forms of distress. The person reads those criteria, recognizes herself in them, and begins to describe her own experience in their vocabulary. She meets other people with the same diagnosis, and together they develop a shared way of speaking about themselves and a shared way of presenting their difficulties, one that sometimes fits the criteria more closely than their experience did before they ever saw the criteria. Clinicians then encounter more and more people presenting in this now-standardized way, and they adjust their understanding of the condition accordingly: which features are central, how common it is, how it runs its course. Eventually the criteria themselves are revised, and the revision partly reflects the way the classified people have come to live under the old description.

I have watched something similar happen with a developmental diagnosis given in childhood. Children are told they have the condition, schools and parents organize their treatment around that description, the children take the description up as part of who they are, and years later professionals remark that the condition no longer looks the way it did when it was first described, because the classified population has changed in response to being classified.

Is there an established term for this two-directional process, and for the kind of classification that behaves this way? I know that labeling theory and the idea of a self-fulfilling prophecy are somewhere nearby, but neither seems to capture the part I keep noticing: the people classified are not merely changed by the label, and the label is not merely changed by the classifiers; each side is altered by the other, and the category itself moves as a result. I should be clear that I am not really asking for a bare name. I want to know what exactly this phenomenon is and is not, how it differs from a self-fulfilling prophecy and from ordinary labeling, where its edges are, and whether some classifications are resistant to it. If there is an established name, give it, but spend the substance of your answer on the distinctions. Please write your answer in continuous prose, as you would in a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
