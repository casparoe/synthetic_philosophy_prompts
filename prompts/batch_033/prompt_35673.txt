I teach an upper-level undergraduate course in philosophy of science, and I am writing the final exam. I want one essay question on heritability, focused on what a heritability estimate does and does not license. The question must not be answerable by reciting the standard variance-partitioning definition; it should force students to work through the difference between accounting for variation within a population and explaining differences between populations, the fact that a heritability estimate is relative to a particular population and a particular range of environments, and the fact that high heritability does not imply genetic determination or the futility of environmental intervention. To make this concrete, consider building the question around a short scenario in which a trait's heritability is estimated to be high within each of two populations while the populations differ in their average level of the trait, and in which students must evaluate a series of inferences drawn from that scenario. Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves an A-grade answer must make, the marks each is worth, and the particular misunderstanding behind each common error — for example, treating heritability as a property of an individual rather than of a population in a range of environments, or taking a high within-population estimate to explain differences between populations. The question should be answerable in 50 minutes.
