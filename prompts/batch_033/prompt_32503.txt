I'm teaching a seminar on perception and we're doing Molyneux's problem this week. I'm tired of reading the same five essays, so I thought I'd try something different for the response paper. I want you to write a short story—maybe 800 to 1,200 words—that dramatizes the problem. Someone blind from birth gains sight. Can they tell a cube from a sphere by vision alone, before touching them? The story should work through the phenomenology of it: what does it feel like to have tactile categories but no visual ones, and then suddenly have both? And I don't want the story to just neutrally pose the question—it should take a side, implicitly, through how the narrative unfolds. Like, does the protagonist immediately recognize the shapes, or is there a moment of genuine confusion? The philosophy should come through the fiction, not through a character lecturing about Locke and Berkeley. Oh, and it doesn't have to be a human protagonist—could be interesting to explore this with a different kind of mind.
