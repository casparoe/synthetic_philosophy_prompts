I chair the constitution committee of a professional society whose membership amends the constitution each year at convention; my committee votes on which proposed amendments go onto that ballot. Several times a year we receive proposals, and none of us are lawyers, we have about twenty minutes per proposal in committee, and a bad call can let a faction entrench itself, invalidate amendments after they pass, or leave us with a constitution that contradicts itself. A fair number of the proposals change the rules about how rules are changed: they raise or lower amendment thresholds, declare a clause unamendable, renumber the rulebook, or insert a clause saying it prevails over anything inconsistent with it. I know enough to know that the self-amending game Nomic and the paradoxes of legal self-reference are the right body of ideas here, but I have never seen them turned into something a non-lawyer committee can actually run.

Please write a decision procedure my committee could run on any proposed amendment before we put it to the membership, usable by non-lawyers in the time we have. It should be a checklist in words: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of judgment rather than mechanics. Ground it in what Nomic and the paradoxes of legal self-reference actually show about self-amending rules — in particular the difference between changing a rule and changing the rule that governs changing rules, entrenchment clauses that try to bind future majorities, supremacy clauses that try to make themselves the source of their own priority, and an amendment threshold changed under the very threshold it would replace. Do not give me a reading list or citations; I need the reasoning, not sources. Mark explicitly which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and say what the procedure cannot settle.

Begin with the procedure itself, with no preface or commentary, and then give the reasoning behind each step and the worked applications. Run the procedure on these three proposals: (1) a proposal to lower the default amendment threshold from two-thirds to a simple majority, which my committee supports; it would be adopted under a standing rule allowing the convention to suspend any rule by a majority vote, so the lower threshold would be used to enact itself — I want to see the procedure tell us no if that is what follows; (2) a new clause stating that it may never be amended or repealed and that any rule inconsistent with it is void; (3) a proposal to renumber the rules so that the amendment rule moves from Rule 12 to the slot now held by Rule 2, an entrenched statement of the society's purposes. In each case, say exactly which step of the procedure produces the answer, and where the procedure would instead send the question to a person.
