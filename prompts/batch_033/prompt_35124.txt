I got into an argument about the doomsday argument at a party (as one does) and realized we were talking past each other for two hours because I was doing roughly self-sampling and they were doing roughly self-indication. I want to turn that into one of those "which position do you actually hold?" quizzes for a small philosophy blog, but it has to sort readers by what they say about concrete cases, not by which label they like.

The quiz should have eight to ten questions, each a little thought experiment with two or three answer options. Cases I definitely want included: the doomsday argument setup; the presumptuous philosopher; something like God's coin toss where a fair coin determines whether one or a million people are created in otherwise identical rooms and you have to say how confident you are that it came up the million side; an urn with a small versus huge population and a random draw; and Sleeping Beauty, or an explanation of why you left it out if you do. For each question, tell me (in a separate key, not shown to quiz takers) which positions it splits. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto the self-sampling assumption, the self-indication assumption, something like full non-indexical conditioning, and a "no stable position" result. Also flag any pair of positions the quiz can't seperate, and diagnose any answer combinations that come out empty: is nobody there because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? Audience is people who've read some philosophy but don't know this literature, so the plain-language explanations come only after they've been sorted. And please, no question that just asks "do you self-sample?"
