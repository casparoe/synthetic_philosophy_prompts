I want your credence on a question in the metaphysics of dispositions. The simple conditional analysis says that an object is disposed to manifest M in response to stimulus S iff, were it the case that S, it would be the case that M. The classic problem cases are finks — something that removes the disposition after the stimulus occurs but before manifestation — and masks — something that blocks the manifestation without removing the disposition itself. Various repairs have been proposed: adding ceteris paribus clauses, appealing to intrinsic causal bases, or abandoning the reductive project and treating dispositions as primitive.

Here is the proposition I want your credence on: The conditional analysis of dispositions is a degenerating research programme — meaning that each repair introduces new counterexamples or ad-hoc machinery, and the project of analysing dispositions in conditional terms will not converge on an extensionally adequate and non-circular analysis.

Please decompose your credence into at least the following sub-questions, assigning a rough probability to each with a one-sentence justification:

1. That finks and masks are fundamentally the same kind of problem — that masks are just a special case of finks, or vice versa, or that both reduce to a single underlying difficulty — so a unified solution is possible rather than requiring piecemeal fixes.
2. That some extant repair is already extensionally adequate (even if philosophically unsatisfying to some), covering all known counterexamples without generating new ones.
3. That even if an extensionally adequate conditional analysis can be given, it will be circular in a way that makes it uninformative as an analysis — for instance, by relying on notions like causal bases or intrinsic properties that themselves require dispositional concepts.
4. That the correct metaphysics of dispositions is primitivist: dispositions are not reducible to categorical properties plus laws, and the failure of the conditional analysis is evidence for this rather than merely evidence that we need a better conditional analysis.

Then show how you combine these into an overall credence, and tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. If you could learn the answer to one of them with certainty, which would move your credence the most? And what kind of philosophical work — a new counterexample, a formal result, a demonstration that finks and masks are unified, or something else — would most shift that sub-question for you?
