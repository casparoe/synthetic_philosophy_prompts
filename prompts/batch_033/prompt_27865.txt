You're a philosopher of physics who works on the foundations of quantum mechanics, especially many-worlds and the measurement problem. Take your time and brainstorm a bit before you write.

Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been arguing for years about the Born rule in many-worlds. On one side: an Everettian who thinks the Born weights can be derived from rational constraints on how an agent should bet or assign credences in a branching universe (call them the derivationist). On the other: someone who thinks those derivations are circular or question-begging, and that many-worlds has a genuine unsolved probability problem — the weights have to be put in by hand, they can't be recovered from unitary dynamics plus rationality (call them the skeptic).

The deliverable is the document the collaboration would actually produce, not a survey and not a verdict. Structure it like this:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign. Cover the formalism they share (unitary evolution, decoherence, branching structure), what any account of probability in many-worlds has to do, and the minimal things the existing derivation attempts establish — phrased so neither side winces.
2. A disagreements table. For each real disagreement, give the exact statement of it in neutral framing, plus each side's own preferred wording of its position. No strawmen.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete argument, case, or thought experiment that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it will show.

I especially want you to seperate genuine disagreements from verbal ones. I suspect part of their fight is over the word "probability" — one means decision-theoretic weight, the other means objective chance — and if that's right, say exactly where the verbal part stops and the substantive part starts. (I also suspect one crux is whether the branch weights get baked into the decision-theoretic axioms themselves, but don't take my word for it.)

Keep the whole thing under 1,100 words. It can be a little rough; I care more about the formulations being fair and precise than about polish.
