so i work in hospital administration and something's been bugging me. we set these targets for departments — reduce wait times, lower readmission rates, improve patient satisfaction scores — and then tie bonuses and promotions to hitting them. and every single time, within a year or two, the numbers look great but everyone knows the workarounds: the ER starts triaging in a way that technically stops the clock but leaves people in hallways, discharge planners push patients out faster than they should, and the satisfaction surveys get handed out with a little speech about how "your feedback helps us keep our funding." everyone at every level knows this is happening. we have meetings about it. and then we set next year's targets.

i'm not asking about how to fix it — i'm asking what this whole ritual presupposes. like, what picture of measurement, or of human behavior, or of the relationship between a number and the thing it's supposed to track, would you have to hold for this practice to make sense? and does the practice actually presuppose that picture, or is it compatible with a bunch of different views and we just happen to have settled into this pattern? also: if we really believed goodhart's law — that any measure used for control ceases to be a good measure — what would we have to stop doing, and what would we replace it with?
