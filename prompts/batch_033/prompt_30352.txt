ok so for my school's philosophy club we're making a little zine and the theme is 'ideas that sound like poems,' and i got assigned nietzsche's eternal recurrence, which i've been thinking about alot. the idea is that a demon comes to you in your loneliest moment and tells you that this whole life — every choice, every boring tuesday, every cringe thing you said, every time you actually felt alive — will happen again exactly the same way, infinitely many times, in the same order, with no variation ever. and the question isn't really 'is it true' (nietzsche isn't trying to prove it as like a physics claim) but wether you'd curse the demon or treat it as the best news you've ever heard, because that reaction shows how much you can actually say yes to your own life.

i want the poem to be a sestina, because the sestina is basically a machine for recurrence: six end-words keep coming back in a fixed rotation across six six-line stanzas, and then a three-line envoi gathers all six at the end, like the same six things refusing to let go of the poem. can you make the form do the work instead of just describing the idea? like, pick six end-words that are ordinary things from a single day (not abstractions like 'eternity' or 'fate'), and if some of them can mean slightly different things each time they come back, even better, because the recurrence is supposed to make the familiar feel vertigo-inducing. write it from the point of view of someone who just heard the demon at like 2am and is trying to figure out whether it's a threat or a gift. it should end in the envoi without resolving whether the speaker curses the demon or says yes — the affirmation has to be earned, not announced. 39 lines total, and pls don't make it sound like a hallmark card about 'living in the moment' lol. it should be accurate enough that someone who knows nietzsche wouldn't wince.
