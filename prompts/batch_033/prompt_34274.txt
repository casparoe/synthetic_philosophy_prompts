I've been trying to sort out my intuitions about when individual actions matter in collective harm cases, and my verdicts are kind of a mess. I want to know if there's a principle that captures them, or at least most of them.

Here are my cases:

- I skip buying a chicken breast at the grocery store. I figure the store orders in bulk, my one purchase doesn't affect how many chickens are killed, so it doesn't matter either way. Verdict: not wrong to buy it, not praisworthy to skip.

- I don't vote in a national election. One vote never decides it. Verdict: not wrong to stay home.

- I'm in a firing squad of ten, where one rifle has a blank and nobody knows whose. I fire. Verdict: wrong, even if my bullet was the blank. I participated.

- I'm one of twenty people each dumping a bucket of sludge into a lake. The lake can absorb nineteen buckets without harm, but twenty kills all the fish. I dump my bucket. Verdict: wrong. But I'm not sure this is consistent with the chicken case.

- I buy a t-shirt from a company that uses sweatshop labor. My purchase doesn't change their practices; the supply chain is what it is. Verdict: feels wrong, but I can't explain why if it makes no difference.

- I'm the hundredth person to join a protest, where the organizers said they need a hundred people to get media coverage. The coverage happens. Verdict: my joining mattered, and I'd have been wrong to bail.

- I take a private jet for a weekend trip instead of flying commercial. Verdict: feels wrong, but the climate impact of one flight is negligible, so I'm not sure my intuition tracks.

- I'm the one person in a thousand who could tip a boycott over the threshold for a company to change its policy. I boycott. Verdict: matters, I should do it.

- I eat a steak at a restaurant where the meat is already cooked and would be thrown out if I didn't eat it. Verdict: not wrong. (But is this just the chicken case again?)

Is there a principle lurking here? Something about thresholds, or about expected impact, or about participation versus buying? I keep going back and forth on whether the chicken and the sweatshop t-shirt should come out the same way. If there's a principle that gets most of these, I'd like to know what it says about a case I haven't listed: being one of millions of people whose small emissions collectively cause climate harms, where no individual emission is decisive. And if the principle forces me to drop some of my verdicts, which ones should go?
