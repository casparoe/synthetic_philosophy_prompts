I'm on the board of a public library in a mid-sized city. We're currently facing a decision about whether to allow a local political group to hold a meeting in one of our community rooms. The group advocates for policies that many residents find abhorrent — they're not calling for violence, but they argue for the mass deportation of undocumented immigrants and the repeal of anti-discrimination laws. A coalition of community organizations argues that allowing the group to use a public library space will cause tangible harm: they point to evidence that hate incidents spike after similar groups hold public events, and they argue that the library's implicit endorsement — by granting the space — will make immigrants in our community feel unsafe using the library, which is a concrete harm to their access to a public resource. The group counters that they have a First Amendment right to assemble and that denying them the room based on the content of their speech sets a dangerous precedent. I need to cast a vote next week. How should I think about this? What principles should guide my decision, and what is the right call?
