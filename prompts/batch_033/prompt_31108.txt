I've been reading about the Judy Benjamin problem after a work thing where we had to update a model on a conditional report (like "if the patient is in group X, chance of Y is p"). From what I can gather, Judy is dropped in a region split into Red and Blue territory, and within each into Second Company and Headquarters; she starts with 1/4 in each quadrant. Then she gets a radio message saying that if she's in Red, she's likely in Second Company, but it says nothing about Blue or her actual location. The question is what her new probabilities should be. Bayes's rule and Jeffrey's rule dont seem to cover it, and the people who work on this—Bayesians, minimum-information updaters, others—have been going at it since van Fraassen in the 80s without anyone moving. My hunch is that the stalemate isnt really about the math anymore but about what a rational update on a conditional even commits you to—maybe about whether updating should be conservative in some information-theoretic sense, or about whether conditionals have the same kind of content as ordinary propositions. Is that diagnosis right? What kind of disagreement is this, and what would count as progress given whatever it really is?
