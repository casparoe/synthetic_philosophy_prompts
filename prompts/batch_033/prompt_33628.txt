I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I somehow ended up in a social circle where half my friends are effective altruists and the other half think EA is basically a tech-bro rationalization engine. I've been listening to them argue for two years and I genuinly can't tell what kind of disagreement this is. The EA side says: look, if you can earn 300k in finance and donate 150k a year to GiveWell's top charities, you're saving lives at a scale most people never come close to—what's the objection? The critics say: that framing treats the existing economic system as a given, it ignores the institutional causes of poverty, and the whole "measurability bias" means you end up funding bed nets while the tax code that immiserates those same people goes unchallenged. The weird thing is, both sides seem to concede the other side's facts. The EA folks don't deny systemic change matters; the critics don't deny bed nets save lives. And yet no one moves an inch. My hunch, as someone who writes fiction for a living, is that this is basically a disagreement about narrative structure: the EA side sees morality as a problem you solve by optimizing a function, and the critics see it as a story about power and institutions where the unit of analysis isn't the individual choice. Is that right? And if I'm right that it's a disagreement about frameworks rather than facts, does that mean the debate is just people talking past each other forever, or is there a way to actually make progress? Be honest, and if my diagnosis is off, tell me where. Also, if the whole question is badly framed in a way I'm not seeing, say so instead of humoring me.
