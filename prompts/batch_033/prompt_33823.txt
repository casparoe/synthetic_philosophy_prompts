I serve on the style committee of a university press that is revising its house style guide. The committee is split over whether to adopt a mandatory nonsexist-language policy. One faction wants the guide to require gender-neutral wording throughout the press's own prose: no generic he after an indefinite antecedent, no compounds such as chairman, manpower, or man-made, and reworded examples so that no default reader is assumed to be male. Another faction, led by a senior editor, argues that generic he and man-compounds are settled conventions that competent readers process as gender-neutral; on this view a style guide should record accepted usage rather than legislate it, and the proposal is an ideological imposition. I need to circulate a decision memo before the committee meets on Thursday. Please draft that memo. It should recommend a concrete policy — adopt the rule, reject it, or adopt a modified version — together with an implementation timeline and a clear statement of what the style guide will require and what will remain at authors' discretion. The reasoning should engage the strongest version of the losing side's position rather than caricature it, and it should use Hofstadter's satirical A Person Paper on Purity in Language (published under the pseudonym William Satire) to sharpen what is at stake, without turning the memo into a summary of the piece. In particular, address whether the fact that readers recover the intended meaning of generic he settles the question whether the usage is harmless, and whether the press should treat its own prose differently from quoted material. The memo should end with a motion the committee could vote on. Write it in American English.
