I'm taking an epistemology class right now and there's a pattern I keep running into in discussion sections that I don't have a name for, and I don't want to just gesture at it in my paper. It goes like this. Someone will say they know something, and they'll also grant that this thing entails another thing, but then they won't say they know the second thing — not because they think the entailment fails, but because the entailment is long, or hard to see, or the conclusion is too weird. A friend of mine says he knows the Peano axioms are true, and he grants that some brutal number-theory theorem follows from them, but he says he doesn't know the theorem because he hasn't worked through the derivation and you don't get knowledge just by being told it follows. Same thing in class: people are happy to say they know they have hands, and they'll grant that having hands entails they aren't handless brains in vats, but they still won't say they know they aren't brains in vats. When I press them they don't back off the entailment; they say the second step is to far away or the possibility is too strange. But then the very same people will go from knowing it's Tuesday and that they have class to knowing it's Tuesday without blinking.

So the rule that knowledge carries over from a claim to whatever it entails is being applied selectively, and the selector seems to be how obvious or surveyable the entailment is, or how bizarre the conclusion is. I know there's a standard principle in the background and that some people — Nozick and Dretske come up a lot in the readings — reject it wholesale, but I'm after the narrower thing: the selective, obviousness-gated version where trivial entailments are fine and long or surprising ones aren't. Does that have its own name? And is the selectivity actually coherent, or is it just an unstable compromise between accepting the principle and rejecting it? I care less about the label than about getting the boundaries right: what exactly counts as obvious enough, and how does this differ from just denying the principle outright, or from the thought that you can't be expected to have worked through every consequence. If it has no name, what would you call it, and where are it's edges?
