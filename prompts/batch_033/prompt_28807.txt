Build me a case where frequentism, subjectivism, and propensity theory all come apart on the same single-case event — ideally three different numbers, or at least one of them refusing to assign a probability while the other two assign different ones. Constraints: one ordinary-life scenario (a medical decision, a bet, an engineering judgment, something like that), no quantum mechanics, no just stipulating long-run frequencies, and the divergence has to come from the interpretations themselves, not from the parties having different information or different stakes — so the subjectivist's number should be a defensible credence from stated priors, not a hunch. The case is for a class handout on why the interpretation debate matters for decisions under uncertainty. After the scenario, walk through the assignment each interpretation gives and the reasoning behind it, then say which structural feature of the case is doing the work, what would happen if you removed it, and how a defender of whichever interpretation comes out looking worst would attack the case.
