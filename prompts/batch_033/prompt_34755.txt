So I'm a novelist — went through the workshop system, taught in it a bit, the whole deal — and lately I've been rereading Tolstoy's What Is Art? and it's kind of messing with my head. Not because I think he's right about everything, but because I can't figure out how the thing I actually do for a living fits with what he says art is. On his account, art is a feeling the artist has genuinely lived through getting transmitted to the reader, and it only works if the artist is sincere, and the feeling is the kind of thing a random peasant could catch, not just somebody with an MFA. Good art brings people together morally; bad art is mostly technical display aimed at a refined audience.

Now look at what actually happens in a creative writing workshop. Eight or ten people sit around a table with somebody's story. Nobody asks wether the writer actually felt the thing they wrote about, or whether a person outside the literary world would be moved by it. They talk about voice, pacing, whether the dialogue snaps, whether the opening hooks, whether the ending is earned, whether the story would place in a good journal. The whole apparatus is built around craft and publishability. That's not a bug in the system — it's the point of it.

Here's what I want you to do. Reconstruct the theory of art that the workshop model presupposes: what it takes art to be, what makes it good, who it's for. Then tell me how much of that Tolstoy would flat-out reject, and how much is just a different emphasis he could live with. And the part I really can't settle myself: if you actually took Tolstoy seriously, what would you have to change about how fiction gets taught and evaluated? Could you even run a workshop under a Tolstoyan picture of art, or does the form itself bake in a rival theory?

And one more thing — if there are places where the workshop system is genuinely underdetermined, where it doesn't actually presuppose much and several different theories of art would license the same practice, I'd want to know that too. Be specific. And write in American English, please.
