Before I read any further in the accuracy-first epistemology literature, I want the specification I should hold it to. Write the desiderata for an adequate accuracy-first theory of epistemic rationality: what it must deliver and why. Cover at least the following, and add any further requirements you think are being silently assumed: the formal constraints on an inaccuracy measure or scoring rule (truth-directedness, propriety versus strict propriety, additivity, and how the measure should behave under refinement of the partition or algebra being scored), with a note on which of these are negotiable; the distinction between a scoring rule's being proper and a credence function's being immodest, and which of these the dominance argument for probabilism actually requires; the diachronic commitments (does the theory need to vindicate conditionalization, and how strong a vindication counts as success); and how the theory handles logically complex propositions and partition-relative scoring without double-counting or letting the choice of algebra do the justificatory work. For each requirement, give the case or result behind it and say whether it is a genuine constraint or a preference of one camp. Flag any pair of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that excludes the most standard contenders. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a scorecard of particular views.
