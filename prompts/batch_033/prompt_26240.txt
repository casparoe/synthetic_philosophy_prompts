I recently read an article about Daniel Dennett's idea that Darwin and Turing both introduced what he calls a "strange inversion of reasoning." The article summarizes the view like this:

Darwin showed that you don't need a designer to get design. Random variation plus natural selection — a blind, mindless, algorithmic process — can produce organisms of staggering complexity, complete with the illusion of having been purpose-built. This was the first strange inversion: competence, in the sense of the organized complexity of life, without comprehension, since no intelligent planner was involved. Turing pulled off the same trick for the mind. A Turing machine manipulates symbols according to mechanical rules; it doesn't need to know what arithmetic is to do arithmetic, or what a chess position means to play excellent chess. Competence without comprehension again: intelligent-looking output with zero understanding inside. The article then says that Dennett's real payoff is that this applies to human beings too. Our conscious experience — the feeling that there's a self in here who understands what's going on — is itself a user-illusion churned out by neural machinery that doesn't comprehend anything either. The article wraps up by saying that Dennett's view entails that if we eventually build a machine that passes the Turing test, it will be conscious in exactly the same sense we are, since there's no difference in kind between the competence our brains produce and the competence a machine produces.

I'm trying to figure out whether this is a fair summary of Dennett's actual position. It feels like it's in the ballpark but maybe conflates a few things — especially the jump from "Darwin and Turing showed you can get competence without comprehension in these specific domains" to "human consciousness is an illusion and machines that mimic us are conscious." Could you go through the summary and tell me which parts a careful Dennett reader would accept, which are oversimplifications, and which are misrepresentations? I'm particularly interested in whether the article's final claim about the Turing test and machine consciousness actually follows from anything Dennett argues.
