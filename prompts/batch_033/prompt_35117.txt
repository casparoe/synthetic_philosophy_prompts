I've been chewing on something for a while and I'm hoping someone can tell me whether there's a word for it — or if not, how to think about where it begins and ends.

Here's the thing I keep noticing. There are certain decisions in life where you can't really know what you're getting into until you've already done it, and what's more, doing it seems to change you into the kind of person who would make the decision differently. It's not just that the experience is hard to imagine from the outside. It's that the you who looks back on the decision is, in some important sense, not the same you who faced it. Your yardstick for what counts as good or bad, worthwhile or regrettable, shifts as part of the experience itself. Take having kids. Beforehand, you might care a lot about sleep, free time, and disposable income. Afterward, you might find that those concerns barely register anymore — not because you've talked yourself into it, but because something about the experience has genuinely rearranged what matters to you. But now here's the puzzle: you couldn't have accessed that new set of priorities without having the kid first. So there's a loop. To judge whether the decision is right for you, you'd need to already be the person you'll become afterward. But you can't become that person without making the decision. Other examples seem to have the same shape: emigrating to a place whose daily texture you can't absorb from books, converting to a religion, getting a cochlear implant, trying psychedelics for the first time. In each case, people who've done it often say "you just have to experience it" or "I was a completely different person before." But the pattern I'm trying to isolate isn't just that the experience is vivid or ineffable — it's the way the decision bootstraps a new evaluative framework that you couldn't consult in advance.

I'm not asking about the general idea that firsthand experience gives you knowledge you can't get from descriptions. I know there's a whole philosophical conversation about "what it's like" and the limits of imagination. What I'm after is narrower: the specific loop where the choice changes the chooser's values, so that neither the before-person nor the after-person can really assess the choice from a neutral standpoint. Does this have a name? If it does, what are its exact boundaries — does the value-change have to be strictly unpredictable, or just very hard to foresee? And how is it different from simpler cases where your preferences just change over time, or where you adapt to a bad situation and convince yourself it's fine? If there's no established name, I'd be interested in how you'd carve it off from those neighboring ideas. And if you could flag how confident you are in what you're saying and which part you're shakiest on, I'd appreciate that.
