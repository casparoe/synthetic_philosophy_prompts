"What are Frankfurt cases, and what do they aim to show about the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP)? Do they succeed? Discuss at least one major objection."

Heres the student's answer (verbatim):

The principle of alternative possibilities (PAP) states that a person is morally responsible for what they have done only if they could have done otherwise. Frankfurt cases are counterexamples designed to show that PAP is false. In a typical Frankfurt case, an agent (call him Jones) decides to perform some action, like voting for a particular candidate. Unbeknownst to Jones, a neuroscientist named Black has implanted a device in Jones's brain that would force him to vote that way if he showed any sign of deciding otherwise. But Jones goes ahead and decides on his own, and Black never intervenes. Intuitively, Jones is morally responsible for his decision even though he couldn't have done otherwise (because Black would have prevented any alternative decision). So PAP is false: moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities.

This result is important because it supports compatibilism about free will and determinism. If moral responsibility doesn't require the ability to do otherwise, then determinism doesn't threaten moral responsibility, since determinism only rules out alternative possibilities. Frankfurt cases thus prove that compatibilism is correct.

One major objection is the "flicker of freedom" strategy. This objection says that even in Frankfurt cases, the agent still has some alternative possibilities — for instance, Jones could have shown a different neural sign before deciding, or hesitated in a way that would have triggered Black's device. These "flickers" are alternative possibilities, so PAP isn't actually refuted. But this objection fails because these flickers aren't the kind of alternatives that matter for moral responsibility. What matters is whether the agent could have done otherwise in the sense of performing a different action, and Jones couldn't. So the flicker objection doesn't rescue PAP.
