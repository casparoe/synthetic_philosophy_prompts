been trying to get my views on hard choices and incommensurability into a coherent package for a post, and I'd like a consistency check before I hit publish. here's where I've landed:

(a) i'm pretty commited to expected utility reasoning, and in practice I treat my preferences over concrete outcomes as complete: for any two options, one's better, the other's better, or they're exactly equal.
(b) but I also think some choices are hard in Ruth Chang's sense — career vs. relationship, say — where it's not that I'm missing information, the options aren't equal, but neither is better than the other.
(c) i think money-pump arguments are a good reason to keep preferences transitive, but I don't buy them as arguments for completeness; indecision isn't the same as a preference cycle.
(d) when I actually make a hard choice, I don't think I'm discovering a hidden ranking. I treat the choice as settling it, and afterwards I can say the chosen option was better for me without thinking I was wrong before.
(e) at the same time, I think 'incommensurable values' is usually an excuse for not doing the math, and for policy tradeoffs — statistical lives, time, money — I'm fine assigning numbers even when it feels gross.

which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how would you rank the tensions? for each real tension, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me? I genuinely can't tell whether (a) and (b) are fine together or whether one of them has to go.
