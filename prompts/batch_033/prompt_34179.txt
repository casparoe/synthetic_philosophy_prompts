The thing is, I feel like there's actually quite a few different ways people respond to it, and theyre not always labelled clearly in the stuff I've read. I'd like a sort of decision tree or taxonomy that starts from the basic setup (the grue emeralds thought experiment) and then branches out based on what someone actually thinks about it, not what label they've heard of.

Like, some people say the problem shows induction is in trouble, some say it's just a trick of language, some say it's about which properties are "natural" or projectible, and some say Bayesian updating handles it fine. But I don't really know how these positions relate to each other or which ones are genuinely different versus just different ways of saying the same thing.

Could you build me a taxonomy where each branch point is a concrete question someone could answer without knowing the jargon? So for example, not "do you accept the entrenchment solution" but something like "do you think there's a real difference between 'green' and 'grue' that goes beyond the fact that we've used the word 'green' more often?" That sort of thing.

I'd also like each leaf to say what the position's most awkward commitment is — the thing someone holding it would probably squirm about if pressed. And if there are logically possible positions that nobody defends, I'd like to know why — is it because the view is incoherent, or just because nobody's gotten round to it, or because it's genuinely unattractive?

Also, I'd be curious to know: what would make you reorganise the whole tree? Like, where's the branching point you're least confident about, or which argument would convince you that two leaves should actually be one?
