I keep seeing a move in online debates where someone says "violence never solves anything" and treats it as a conversation-ender — whether the topic is self-defense, humanitarian intervention, or punching Richard Spencer. I'm not a pacifist but I want to be charitable, and I suspect there are at least half a dozen different claims hiding inside that one sentence, ranging from "violence sometimes has bad side effects" (duh) to something much stronger about violence being intrinsically incapable of achieving any aim. What I need is a breakdown: what are all the distinct theses "violence never solves anything" could be used to assert? For each one, tell me whether it's plausible on its own terms, and — crucially — whether it actually licenses the "therefore we should never use violence" conclusion that people deploy it to reach. I'm especially interested in readings where the slogan turns out to be true but doesn't support the conclusion, or vice versa. Cap it at 500 words.
