I'm a retired geochemist and I've only been reading philosophy seriously for a few years, so maybe I'm missing something. Here's what bugs me. People have been arguing about the heap paradox for a couple thousand years, and the modern debate has produced a bunch of well-developed positions — epistemicism (there's a sharp cutoff, we just can't know where), supervaluationism (the word is only partially defined, and it's neither true nor false in the borderline zone), contextualism (the threshold moves around with the conversation), and degree theories (heap-ness comes in degrees rather than on/off). Each side can handle the basic puzzle, and each side gets nailed by objections the others raise, and as far as I can tell nobody has moved much in decades. In my old line of work, if two models both fit the data and people argued this long, we'd suspect they were asking different questions or using the same word for different things.

My working hypothesis, for what it's worth: its not one disagreement, its three stacked on top of each other. One is about whether vagueness lives in language, in the world, or in our knowledge. Another is about what a solution has to look like — a formal semantics, a metaphysical picture, an account of how competent speakers actually behave, or a rule for when to stop caring. And the third might just be a verbal dispute about the word 'vague' itself.

I'd like you to diagnose the impasse: what kind of disagreement is this really? Is it substantive, verbal, or several distinct questions sharing a name? Are the sides disagreeing about facts, about standards, about which cases count as data, or just about bedrock intuitions? And given the diagnosis, say honestly whether anything could actually break the stalemate — a formal result, a psycholinguistic experiment, a new distinction, a change of question — or whether we should expect this to stay stuck and be fine with that.

I dont need a survey of the positions or a reading list; I've read enough to recognize the moves. I want a diagnosis with reasons.
