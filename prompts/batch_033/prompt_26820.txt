Longtime LW/EA reader here. I'm trying to get my views on Pascal's mugging and expected value into one coherent package before a meetup discussion, and I'd like a consistency check. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I think expected utility theory is the right norm for decisions under uncertainty, and the VNM axioms basically capture what it is to have coherent preferences.
(2) I think you should not pay the Pascal's mugger — the guy who asks for $5 and promises some astronomically huge amount of utility with a tiny but nonzero probability, and says you're irrational if you don't pay because the expected value is enormous — and I think a correct application of expected utility theory actually gives that verdict.
(3) I think the St. Petersburg paradox shows that unbounded utility functions are a problem, and the fix is to bound utility (or otherwise make the function well-behaved).
(4) I don't think we should literally assign zero probability to speculative catastrophic or astronomical scenarios; I'd fund x-risk work even at probabilities around 10^-6 or lower.
(5) I also think it's fine in everyday life to round tiny probabilities down to zero — I'm not going to worry about the 10^-20 chance the bus is a Boltzmann brain, and I think the standard LessWrong advice about shutting up and multiplying is overapplied when people use it to make Pascal-style muggers feel obligatory.
(6) I think scope insensitivity is a real bias, and doing the expected-value calculation is the right corrective when the probabilities are ordinary and the payoffs are merely large rather than astronomically large.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, which pairs only look like they conflict, and what the cheapest fix would be. I honestly can't tell which of these are pulling against each other and which are fine, and I keep going back and forth on whether the whole set is consistant.
