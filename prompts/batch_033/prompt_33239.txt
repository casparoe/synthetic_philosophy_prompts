In my reading, I've repeatedly encountered the claim that expected utility theory seems to demand we devote ourselves entirely to chasing tiny probabilities of enormous payoffs — the problem of expected value fanaticism. Since this would be an absurd practical conclusion, and since decision theorists and philosophers are not absurd people, there must be a standard way of heading off the fanatical result from within the theory itself. I'd like to understand that standard solution: how do we block the fanatical conclusion without abandoning expected utility theory, and what justifies the blocking move without simply begging the question against the fanatic? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
