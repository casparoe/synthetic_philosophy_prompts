In a graduate seminar on moral responsibility, a classmate shut down a compatibilist argument by announcing that Frankfurt cases have shown the slogan "responsibility requires alternative possibilities" to be false, so any inference from "she could not have done otherwise" to "she is not responsible" is unsound and the slogan can no longer be treated as a constraint on blame. I doubt the room was tracking a single claim. "Alternative possibilities" seemed to shift depending on who was talking: the categorical ability to have acted differently given the same past and laws, the existence of some alternative actually open to the agent in the circumstances, the ability to have decided or tried otherwise even when the action itself was inevitable, or something about whether the action issued from the agent in the right way rather than about alternatives at all. I also suspect the slogan is being made to do several jobs at once — a claim about what determinism would rule out, a claim about when an excuse applies, and a claim about what blame is for. I want a menu to bring to the next session. Please lay out the distinct precise principles that "responsibility requires alternative possibilities" could be expressing. For each reading: state the principle precisely; say whether it is trivial, plausible but contentious, or false; and say whether a Frankfurt-style case — one in which a counterfactual intervener would have forced the agent to act exactly as she did but never actually intervenes — refutes that reading. Then say which reading or readings my classmate's dismissal actually needs in order to be sound, and which readings survive Frankfurt cases and could still do work for a compatibilist who wants to retain something like the slogan.
