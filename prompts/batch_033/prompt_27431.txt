I'm a policy researcher on the trust and safety team at a large social media platform. My team has been asked to produce an internal white paper on how Mill's harm principle should inform our content moderation policies. The principle is routinely invoked in these debates — "the only purpose for which power can be rightfully exercised over any member of a civilized community, against his will, is to prevent harm to others" — but its application to platform-scale speech is anything but straightforward. We moderate content at a scale Mill never imagined, where individual pieces of speech can contribute to diffuse, cumulative, and probabilistic harms whose causal connection to any single utterance is tenuous at best.

I want the white paper to identify and prioritize the unresolved conceptual questions about the harm principle that, if settled, would most change how a platform actually moderates content. I am not looking for a survey of Mill scholarship or a recap of standard free-speech jurisprudence. I want a defended prioritization: which two or three open questions about the scope, structure, or operationalization of the harm principle are most consequential for platform policy, and why those rather than others that might seem equally pressing?

For each question you prioritize, please address: (a) what makes it genuinely unresolved — where the philosophical difficulty lies, not just that people disagree; (b) what specific categories of moderation decision would turn on its answer (be concrete: name kinds of speech or policy choices); and (c) why it is more important to settle than the next most obvious candidate — what makes it upstream of other questions, or what systematic errors flow from getting it wrong.

To be clear about the kind of thing I mean: one candidate question is how the harm principle should handle probabilistic and diffuse harms, where no single utterance predictably causes an identifiable injury but the aggregate effect of many similar utterances does. Another is whether the principle's distinction between self-regarding and other-regarding conduct can be sustained at all, and if not, whether the harm principle retains any content. A third is whether psychological distress counts as harm or mere offense, and whether that distinction can be drawn in a principled way at the scale of millions of moderation decisions. These are examples — I want your own assessment of which questions actually matter most, and your reasons for that ordering.

Conclude with a single line that states your top-priority question, enclosed as \answer{your top-priority question in one sentence}.
