Here's my opening. Pick a truth p that no one ever knows. Then the conjunction "p and no one ever knows p" is true as well. By your principle, that conjunction is knowable — it's possible someone knows it. But if someone knew that conjunction, they'd know p, and they'd also know that no one ever knows p. Since knowledge is factive, the second part would have to be true: no one ever knows p — even though they themselves know p. Contradiction. So your principle, applied to "p and no one ever knows p," seems to rule out unknown truths entirely: either the principle is false or it collapses into the claim that every truth is actually known. What do you do with that?
