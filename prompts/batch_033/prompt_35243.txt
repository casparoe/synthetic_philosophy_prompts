I retired a few years ago from a career in atmospheric science, and I have been using the time to work through philosophical logic, which I never had room for while I was running models. The topic I keep coming back to is the indicative conditional. What unsettles me is the distance between how working scientists actually reason with if...then — reading a consequence off a model, reasoning diagnostically from a failed instrument reading, updating a forecast in light of new data — and what the elementary logic I learned treats the conditional as meaning. I have now worked through Lewis's triviality results and understand, at least roughly, why one cannot simply identify the probability of an indicative conditional with the corresponding conditional probability while continuing to treat the conditional as an ordinary proposition. What I do not yet have is a clear picture of what a theory of the indicative conditional is actually required to deliver once that identification is off the table.

Before I read further, I want the specification. Please write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the indicative conditional must meet: the phenomena it must explain or explain away (the Ramsey test intuition, Adams's thesis, modus ponens, import-export, the oddity of embedded and negated conditionals, non-monotonicity), the cases it must get right, and the distinctions it must respect (truth conditions versus assertability, indicative versus counterfactual, conditional probability versus the probability of a conditional). For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a merely aesthetic preference. Then flag any subsets of these desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied — Lewis's triviality results are the obvious place to look, but tell me if there are other tensions — and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are commitments of particular camps such as truth-conditionalists, suppositionalists, or the restrictor view. I would also like to know which single requirement does the most work in excluding the standard candidate theories, and a brief scorecard at the end is acceptable, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivation.
