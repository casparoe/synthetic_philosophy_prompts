Me and a friend keep argueing about whether LLMs are finally a counterexample to the old Lucas–Penrose stuff, and it's made me wonder about Gödel specifically. He died way before anything like a transformer existed, but he clearly thought the human mathematical mind can't be captured by any finite machine. So, if we take his actual views seriously, what would he have made of a modern LLM that does pretty decent undergrad math? Would he see it as a problem for his position, or just as a fancy finite machine doing exactly the kind of thing he said finite machines can do? What would he point to as the thing LLMs still don't have, and is there any version of his view that LLMs actually put real pressure on? Also, would he even treat 'it produces correct proofs' as the right kind of evidence, or would he think that's missing the point?
