You are a professional philosopher of language whose research focuses on Gricean pragmatics and the semantics–pragmatics interface. I am preparing a research proposal for a two-year postdoctoral fellowship, and I would like you to help me turn a half-formed idea into a tight, fundable proposal. My suspicion is that debates in Gricean and post-Gricean pragmatics—such as the dispute between minimalists and contextualists about how much pragmatic enrichment belongs to the truth-conditional content of an utterance, and the dispute about whether scalar implicatures are generated grammatically or by general-purpose pragmatic reasoning—tend to proceed as if there is a single boundary between what is said and what is implicated, and as if the standard diagnostics of cancellability, calculability, non-detachability, and embeddability all track that same boundary. I doubt that they do. It seems to me more likely that these diagnostics track two or three different distinctions, and that once those distinctions are separated, some of the apparent stalemates would dissolve or at least become more tractable.

Please think carefully and begin by brainstorming two or three candidate ways of sharpening this idea into a determinate research question, then choose one and write the actual proposal in roughly 800–1000 words. The proposal should: state the research question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the existing positions leave it open; describe the method in concrete stages and justify that method over the obvious alternatives, such as a purely exegetical reconstruction of Grice's texts or a formal implementation of one side of the scalar implicature debate; specify what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and keep the scope realistic for a two-year postdoc. Do not include a bibliography or request citations; refer to positions, distinctions, and arguments by name or description, and I will supply references myself.
