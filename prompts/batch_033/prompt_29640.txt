I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am working on a piece about where the philosophical debate over free will and moral responsibility currently stands. I would like your considered credence — a specific number between 0 and 1 — on the following proposition: the principle of alternative possibilities, understood as the claim that a person is morally responsible for what they did only if they could have done otherwise, where could have done otherwise is read in the categorical sense standard in the debate rather than the merely conditional sense, is false.

Do not give me a survey of positions or an answer that only says it depends on one's broader theory. Give me your actual number and the decomposition that produces it. Please assign a rough probability to each of the following sub-questions, justify each assignment in a sentence or two, and show how the numbers combine into your final credence:

1. That Frankfurt-style cases really eliminate the relevant alternative possibilities, rather than leaving a residual flicker — some subtle difference in the agent's mental state, or in whether the counterfactual intervener has to act — that might still do the work the principle requires.
2. That even if such a flicker remains, it is not the kind of alternative that could ground moral responsibility, so the cases survive the flicker objection.
3. That moral responsibility is better analyzed in terms of the actual sequence — how the action issues from the agent's own deliberation, values, and motives — than in terms of alternative possibilities, quite apart from the Frankfurt cases.
4. That the debate has not been skewed by the way Frankfurt cases are usually stipulated against a deterministic backdrop, which might lead the intuitions they elicit to presuppose compatibilism from the start.
5. That the dispute over the principle is substantive rather than partly verbal — that is, not driven by different senses of can, could have done otherwise, or responsible.

Then tell me which of these five terms your final credence is most sensitive to, which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would move your credence the most. If you regard the proposition as too coarse to have a determinate truth value as stated, say what you take the nearest precise version to be, give your credence for that version, and decompose it instead.

Please write the answer in American English.
