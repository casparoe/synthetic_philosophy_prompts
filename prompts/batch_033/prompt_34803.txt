I've been reading about the color phi phenomenon and related stuff about the timing of experience, and I feel like I'm missing something. The way I understand it: two dots flash in quick succession at different positions, one red and one green, and people report seeing a single dot that moves and changes from red to green midway. But the second dot hadn't appeared yet at the moment the "color change" would have had to occur. So the obvious interpretation is that consciousness is just a post-hoc construction — your brain retroactively revises the timeline after the green dot appears, and you're none the wiser. This is basicaly the Orwellian view, right? That experience gets rewritten after the fact, like memory editing in 1984.

But then I see people talking about Dennett and the Stalinesque alternative, and I don't get why that's even taken seriously. The Stalinesque view says consciousness is delayed — like the whole show is held up until the green dot arrives and only then does the unified experience get served up. But that seems like a completely ad hoc way to save the idea that consciousness is a coherent stream. The Orwellian account just seems obviously right: the brain edits the story after the fact. Why is this still considered an open question? Shouldn't color phi just settle it in favor of the Orwellian view?
