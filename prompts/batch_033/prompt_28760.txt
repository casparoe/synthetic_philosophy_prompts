ok so in psych we did this unit on consciousness and our teacher showed us the color phi thing. two dots flash in quick succession, a red one on the left and then a green one on the right, but people report seeing a single dot glide across and turn green halfway along. wich is weird because how could you see it turn green before the green dot even shows up. after class me and two friends got into a whole argument about it and i still dont know who to side with.

maya said it has to be the orwellian version: you see the red dot, then you see the green dot, and then your brain goes back and quietly rewrites the memory so it looks like one smooth moving dot that changed color midway. she said memory is reconstructive anyway, this is just an extreme case of something your brain does all the time, so it's the less crazy option.

jake said no, it's stalinesque: your conscious experience is just delayed. the brain sits on the first flash until the second one arrives, builds the whole moving-dot-with-midway-color-change story, and only then shows it to you. so you never actually consciously experienced the two dots as separate at all, you only ever got the edited version. he said this fits with the fact that conscious perception runs a couple hundred milliseconds behind the world.

maya shot back that if jake is right then we're basically living in the past and nothing we experience is happening when it feels like it's happening, which is way more radical than just admitting memory sometimes edits things after the fact.

jake said yeah, that's literally what libet-style experiments show, your brain starts gearing up for a movement before you consciously decide to move, so consciousness being behind is not that crazy.
