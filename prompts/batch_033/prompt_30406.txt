quick recap so the design stays grounded. in the Platonia dilemma a trillionaire tells 20 people that if exactly one of them sends a telegram by noon the next day, that one will recieve a billion dollars; if zero or more than one send, nobody gets anything, and communication is forbidden. the superrational move is to send with probability 1/20, but a game-theoretic player who thinks everyone else is doing that will feel the pull to just send for sure, since that wins whenever none of the other 19 happens to send. in the Luring Lottery (actually run by Scientific American in the 80s) a reader could enter once by sending a postcard with the number 1, or send a single postcard with any larger number to count as that many entries; the prize was a million dollars split across all the entries and awarded to a randomly drawn entry. submitting more entries raises your chance but shrinks the prize for everyone including you, and the expected-value-maximizing number of entries is exactly however many entries everyone else submits, which is the runaway. the superrational move there was to roll a die with as many sides as the expected number of responders and send 1 only on a roll of 1.
