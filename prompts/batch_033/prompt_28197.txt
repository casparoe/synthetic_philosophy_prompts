Construct a thought experiment that isolates the difference between AI alignment and AI control as safety strategies. Design a scenario in which an AI system is known to be misaligned — its goals diverge from its operators' goals — but a set of control measures (monitoring, restricted action space, human-in-the-loop approvals) prevents harm indefinitely, while alignment techniques alone (RLHF, constitutional AI, etc.) would fail to prevent harm if relied upon without those controls. Then modify exactly one feature of the scenario so that the control regime collapses but a successfully aligned version of the same AI would remain safe. Constraints: the AI is a near-future language-model agent deployed in a commercial setting; no capabilities beyond what a well-funded lab could plausibly develop in the next decade; the control measures must be realistically implementable, not reliant on perfect security; and the alignment techniques must be ones currently in use or under active research, not speculative value-loading procedures. After presenting both versions of the scenario, identify which feature does the work in each direction, and explain what a proponent of the view that control suffices without alignment would have to argue to resist the lesson of the second version.
