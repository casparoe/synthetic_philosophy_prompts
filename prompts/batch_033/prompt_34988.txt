Construct a thought experiment that makes the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe as strong as possible while keeping the physics completely standard. Specifications: two inertial observers in relative motion and one pair of spacelike-separated events; no superluminal signals and no built-in metaphysical assumptions; the scenario should isolate the step from frame-relative simultaneity to the claim that the two events are equally real. The case is for testing whether a model can keep the physics and the metaphysics distinct, so a scenario that stipulates the block-universe conclusion rather than generating it from the physics fails.

After presenting the scenario, do all of the following. First, show using only the standard relativity of simultaneity that the two observers disagree about the temporal status of the two events, and state precisely what each observer is entitled to say. Second, lay out the Rietdijk–Putnam inference from that disagreement to the conclusion that past, present, and future events exist on a par, and identify the extra premise that must be added to the physics to license the conclusion. Third, say which feature of the case is doing the metaphysical work and what happens if that feature is weakened or removed. Fourth, give the strongest presentist reply: where exactly a presentist should say the argument overreaches, and what the case would have to include for that reply to be blocked. Throughout, separate what is settled—the frame-dependence of simultaneity as physics—from what is genuinely contested—whether frame-dependent simultaneity forces eternalism—and do not present the contested inference as consensus.
