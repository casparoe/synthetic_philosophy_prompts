Please explain the structure of Gödel's argument that the rotating-universe solutions to general relativity show time — in the sense of a passing "now" or an objective lapse — to be an appearance or idealization rather than a feature of physical reality. I want the joints of the argument separated: what the closed timelike curves in the Gödel metric actually rule out; why Gödel took a merely possible world of this kind to bear on our actual universe; and which step carries the weight of the conclusion — the physics, or a philosophical claim about what time would have to be if it were real. In particular, say where the argument is best read as a constraint on any adequate concept of time, and where it simply presupposes the very conception of time it is attacking. Finally, indicate where your reconstruction is most likely to fail and which consideration would most likely lead you to revise it.
