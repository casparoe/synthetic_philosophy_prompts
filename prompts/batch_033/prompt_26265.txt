ok so my political theory reading group has been stuck on dirty hands for weeks. two of us keep going in circles. one thinks dirty hands is a real and ineliminable moral category: sometimes a politician faces a genuine dilemma where any available action violates a serious moral requirement, and even when they choose the lesser evil, the violated requirement doesn't just dissolve — it leaves a moral remainder, the act was wrong (pro tanto or in some respect) even if it was the right thing to do all things considered. the other thinks this whole setup is confused and collapses on inspecton. on their view, either the act really is justified all things considered and therefore wasn't wrong in any sense that matters (the "remainder" is just understandable distress, not genuine moral guilt), or the act wasn't justified and the leader simply chose wrongly. there's no coherent third category of "wrong but nevertheless right to do."

we've agreed to do an adversarial collaboration instead of another round of repetition. can you draft the document we'd produce? i want: a numbered list of claims both sides can sign; a disagreements table where each view is stated in wording its own proponent would endorse; the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling them would actually move at least one of us; and for each crux, a concrete case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each person's prediction stated up front. we don't need citations or references, we need the positions laid out cleanly so we can see where the argumentative weight falls.
