My friend has been arguing that infinite ethics is a dead end. His line is that summing wellbeing over infinitely many lives is like summing a conditionally convergent series: the total depends on the order you add the terms in, so by regrouping the same lives you can make any intervention come out looking best (or worst). From that he concludes total utilitarianism has no well-defined answers in a possibly infinite universe, and maybe aggregative ethics should be given up entirely. I can't tell wether he's onto something or whether the series analogy is doing more work than it can carry. Audit it for me. Give the correspondence first: what plays the role of the terms, the ordering, the regrouping, the conditional convergence? Then say where the analogy breaks, which features of infinite series it quietly imports that infinite populations don't have, and whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If there's a better analogy, I'd want that too.
