Write an essay that reads Bong Joon-ho's film Parasite through the equality-of-what debate in political philosophy — the debate over whether the currency of egalitarian justice should be welfare, resources, or capabilities. I would like a reading that treats the film and the philosophical positions with equal care and is willing to say where the fit fails. Specifically: the Kim family's poverty is not a simple shortage of money, and their infiltration of the Park household improves their income without changing their social standing. Which of the three positions — welfarism, resourcism, or the capability approach — best captures what the Kims lack relative to the Parks, and why? Consider where each diagnosis misfires. The Kims are often cheerful and resourceful, so a pure welfare metric may understate the injustice; they acquire jobs and cash, so a pure resource metric may fix too little; and the capability approach must explain which capabilities are denied and whether the film portrays them as positional, embodied, or otherwise not purchasable with income — as its recurring attention to the smell of the semi-basement suggests. Also consider whether the film's moral complexity — the Kims' own deceptions and the fact that the Parks are not individually cruel — resists the assumption that the Kims are simply victims of an unjust distribution. End by saying what the film adds to the equality-of-what debate that philosophical argument alone would not. Please write in American English.
