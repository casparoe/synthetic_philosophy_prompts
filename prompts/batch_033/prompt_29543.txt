He said it like it was a theorem, and everyone nodded, but I genuinely can't tell what that sentence is asserting. Those are very different claims with different truth-values, and I suspect the defensible ones don't get him the conclusion he wanted.

Can you seperate out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, maybe ordered from trivial to radical, and for each one say whether it's true, false, or true-only-with-qualifications, and whether it would actually support choosing a simpler model in an ML context? I'd especially like to know which reading is doing the work when someone uses "simplest is most likely" as if it were a formal result rather than a heuristic, and what the formal result actually requires — choice of reference machine, uncomputability, language-dependence up to a constant, the idealization of the prior, all the stuff people wave away. If the slogan is just false on its most natural reading, say so; I'd rather walk into the next meeting with a precise objection than a vague feeling that something's off.
