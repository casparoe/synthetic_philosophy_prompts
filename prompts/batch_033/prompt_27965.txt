I've been trying to understand herding. By herding I mean when a lot of people independantly end up doing the same thing. My main thought is this. If a group of people each have their own information, and they all think for themselves, then the group should mostly get things right. This is the basic idea behind prediction markets and the old trick of asking a big crowd to guess a number. Each person has some noisy signal about the right answer, but when you average the signals the noise cancels out and the truth shows through.

So if a whole crowd ends up convinced that some stock is a sure thing and it turns out to be a bubble, or everyone says a restaurant is amazing and its actually mediocre, there are only two possibilities. Either the information itself was so bad that no amount of thinking could have fixed it, or somebody stopped using their own judgment and just copied. In most cases I think its the second one. People copy because they're afraid to stand out, or because they assume the early people must know something they dont. But that assumption is lazy. If the early people were also just copying someone even earlier, then the whole chain is a chain of people not thinking.

The part I keep coming back to is this. When someone joins the crowd late, they do have their own evidence. They might have looked at the actual numbers, or tasted the food themselves. But they set that aside because the crowd seems so confident. I think that is a mistake. If each latecomer just followed their own private evidence, the truth would come out more often. So wrong herds that keep happening show that people are not being rational. Rational herding, in the sense of everyone having good reasons to go along, shouldn't produce massive systematic error. If the herd is wrong, somebody along the way was being irrational.

Some specific things I'd especially like your take on: is my claim that there are only two possibilities too strong? Am I running together a person being rational with the group being right? And is there a version of rational herding that really can produce systematic error, and what would it take?
