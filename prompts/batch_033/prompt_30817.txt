I am a PhD student in moral philosophy, and I have been wrestling with a practical difficulty that I suspect bears on my theoretical commitments. For some time now, I have been convinced that the right action is the one that produces the best overall state of affairs, judged from an impartial point of view. I have tried to take this conviction seriously in my own life. I give away a substantial portion of my stipend to organizations that I judge to be highly cost-effective at reducing suffering. I have given up hobbies, eating out, and travel, because the money and time seemed better directed elsewhere. I often feel guilty when I spend an evening with friends rather than working or volunteering, since the latter seem more likely to make a concrete difference.

The problem is that my life has begun to come apart in ways that seem connected to these choices. My partner tells me I am no longer really present — that I have become distant, perpetually preoccupied, and unable to enjoy anything for its own sake. My advisor has expressed concern that my research is losing depth and that I seem perpetually exhausted. I have noticed that I approach my own projects with a kind of grim dutifulness rather than genuine engagement. Personal relationships and intellectual passions that used to feel central to who I am now strike me as indulgences I struggle to justify.

I am uncertain what to make of this. That a moral demand is difficult to meet does not, of course, show that the demand is unreasonable. But I find myself wondering whether a theory that systematically pulls against the relationships, commitments, and dispositions that give a human life its shape and meaning might be flawed in a more fundamental way. If living as the theory requires means becoming someone who can no longer sustain the very attachments that make a life recognizable as a good one, is that a problem with the theory, or merely a fact about the limits of the person trying to follow it?

I would be grateful for a philosophically rigorous diagnosis of what might be going wrong here — or, if nothing is going wrong, an explanation of why that is.
