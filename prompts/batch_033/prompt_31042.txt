I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but my current project is a campus novel set around an epistemology reading group, and the emotional climax is a document two of the characters — who've been arguing for years about the lottery paradox and the preface paradox — finally produce together. One of them, call him the threshold guy, thinks the lesson of the lottery paradox is that flat-out rational acceptance just isn't closed under conjunction: high enough probability is enough for belief, and you have to give up agglomeration. The other, the closure guy, thinks the opposite: rational belief is closed under conjunction, so high probability can't be sufficient for acceptance, and you shouldn't flat-out believe each individual ticket will lose, or that each sentence in your book is true. I want you to write the joint statement they'd both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, and I want it specific enough that a philosopher wouldn't roll their eyes.

It should have: (1) a numbered section of claims both can sign about the formal structure of the two paradoxes — the inconsistent set of principles at work, what the lottery case and the preface case do and dont have in common, and anything else they'd agree on without hedging; (2) a disagreements table where each position is stated in wording its own side would accept, including their precise views on the threshold thesis, multi-premise closure, and what should be said about the preface author; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them — with, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance; and (4) a short note on where their dispute is at least partly verbal, if it is.

After the document itself, add a brief note from you (not from the characters) saying where you think the whole framing is most likely to be wrong: which crux you'd bet is the fragile one, and what argument or consideration would make you redraw the agreed claims or the table. I'm less interested in who wins then in the document feeling like the product of two people who actually understand each other's view and have stopped shadowboxing.
