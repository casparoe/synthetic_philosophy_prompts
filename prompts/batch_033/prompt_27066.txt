I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've been asked to give a lunchtime talk to the applied ML team at my company. The thesis I'm defending is that the standard reinforcement-learning picture of an agent separated from its environment by a clean observation/action boundary is not just a convenient simplification but a systematically misleading one for safety work, and that researchers should treat the agent as embedded in and causally entangled with its environment from the outset rather than treating embeddedness as an edge case to be patched after the fact.

My argument in brief: the clean-boundary setup assumes that observations enter the agent and actions leave it through a stable interface, so the agent's reasoning can be treated as outside the environment it reasons about. On the embedded view, the agent is a physical process running inside the environment. Its actions can alter its own sensors, memory, and objectives; other agents in the environment can reason about it; and the model it uses to represent the environment is itself implemented inside the environment and can be manipulated. I want to argue that failure modes such as reward hacking, self-fulfilling measurements, and self-modification look peripheral under the clean-boundary picture but become central under the embedded picture, and that several alignment proposals that look clean in the standard formalism quietly depend on the clean boundary.

The audience is roughly thirty applied ML engineers and research scientists. They know the standard RL and POMDP formalism well, they ship models, and they are sympathetic to the view that all abstractions are false and the test of an abstraction is whether it produces working systems. They are skeptical of claims that do not cash out as a changed training objective, an algorithm, or a concrete reproducible failure mode.

Order them by how likely and how damaging they are. Phrase each objection the way one of these engineers would actually put it in the room. For each, give a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.

Please write the answer in American English.
