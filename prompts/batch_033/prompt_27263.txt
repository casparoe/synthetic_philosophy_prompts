Write a dialogue between a hardcore frequentist and a subjective Bayesian stuck next to each other on a delayed flight home from a forecasting conference, arguing about what it means to assign a probability to a one-off event: say, the chance that a particular new rocket's first orbital launch succeeds. The frequentist keeps insisting that a probability has to be a long-run frequency in some reference class, and that a credence about a single launch is either meaningless or just a disguised base rate. The Bayesian keeps pushing on the fact that you have to choose some reference class, and that choice needs priors, and on the fact that people act on one-off credences all the time (prices, bets, launch decisions). Make both characters genuinely sharp and have them respond to each other's actual points: the frequentist should press on how subjective priors get disciplined or calibrated, and the Bayesian should press on how the frequentist picks a reference class without smuggling in there own judgement. Don't let either side be a strawman, and don't resolve it into a tidy 'they're both right.'
