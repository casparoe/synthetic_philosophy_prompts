For a post I have been drafting, I keep coming back to the unilateralist's curse in discussions of AI risk and community governance: the pattern in which a group where any single member may act on behalf of the whole produces systematically risky outcomes, because the member with the most optimistic estimate of a proposal's value acts while the more pessimistic members' reservations carry no veto. The standard illustrations are open-sourcing a dangerous model, gain-of-function research, and unilateral geoengineering, but the structure also appears in more mundane decisions about who may fork a project, disclose a vulnerability, or post on a shared account. Rather than another argument for a particular remedy — majority vote, a consensus requirement, a designated decider, a higher evidence bar — I want the specification first. What would an adequate theory of how a group should handle decisions in which any one member can act have to deliver?

Write the desiderata. I want the cases it must handle: asymmetric stakes, correlated versus independent judgments, strategic or adversarial members, unknown or open group membership, irreversible versus reversible actions, one-shot versus repeated settings. I want the distinctions it must not collapse: unilateral action versus public advocacy, epistemic disagreement versus value disagreement, ex ante versus ex post evaluation, a small aligned team versus a large anonymous commons, and the procedural question of who is entitled to act versus the substantive question of whether acting is wise. I want the formal results it must be consistent with: the original curse argument, the Condorcet jury theorem and its limits, information cascades, and the conditions under which agreement theorems do or do not force convergence. Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, flag which are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of a particular camp, and say whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — in particular, whether respecting individual autonomy, avoiding the curse, and remaining robust to strategic actors can all be satisfied at once. I am less interested in a scorecard of existing proposals than in knowing which single requirement does the most to exclude the popular fixes.
