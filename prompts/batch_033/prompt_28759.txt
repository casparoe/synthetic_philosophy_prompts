I have been teaching a graduate seminar on rational choice, and we have spent the last several weeks on decision-making under risk. We worked through expected utility theory and the independence axiom, hit the Allais paradox, and then read Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility theory (REU), which relaxes the independence axiom and represents an agent's attitude to risk with a risk function that is distinct from her utility function. I have come to think that REU is not merely a redescription of the Allais preferences but the better normative theory: it locates risk attitude where it belongs, as a separate dimension of evaluation, rather than forcing it into the curvature of utility over wealth, and it does so while preserving first-order stochastic dominance. My colleagues remain unconvinced, and I want to be able to answer them precisely.

Please argue for REU against orthodox expected utility theory as an account of rational preference under risk. Do not simply rehearse the Allais paradox as a datum; use it, but also make the positive case: explain what the risk function represents, why an agent's attitude to risk should be allowed to vary independently of how much outcomes matter to her, and why the independence axiom is not a requirement of rationality once that distinction is drawn. Then take on the strongest objections directly. First, the charge that Allais-type preferences are simply mistakes to be corrected, so a theory that accommodates them is a theory of irrationality rather than rationality. Second, the charge that REU double-counts risk attitude, since diminishing marginal utility already represents risk aversion; here I want you to engage the calibration-style argument that expected utility, by identifying risk aversion with utility curvature, cannot explain modest small-stakes risk aversion without implying absurd large-stakes risk aversion. Third, the worry that REU agents are dynamically inconsistent in ways that undermine their ability to make and stick to plans, and that the theory avoids this only by ad hoc restrictions. Conclude by saying what conception of rationality REU is committed to, and where the residual burden of proof falls between REU and its expected-utility rival.
