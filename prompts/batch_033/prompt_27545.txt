I'm helping organize a reading group on AI safety, and last week's session on Goodhart's law and specification gaming left me frustrated: every proposed remedy seemed to fix one failure mode while quietly relocating the problem. Penalizing divergence between a learned reward model and some independently specified objective just moves the vulnerability to wherever that objective gets operationalized, and adding human oversight moves it to whatever the overseers in practice reward. Before I read any more proposed fixes, I want the specification they are all implicitly being measured against.

Write the desiderata: what must an adequate theory of proxy design have to deliver — a theory of how a metric, objective, or reward signal can be optimized as a target without the optimization corrupting the target's connection to what we actually care about? For each requirement, give the case or argument that makes it a requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable constraints on any adequate theory and which are preferences of particular camps. In particular, address which cases the theory must get right (classic economic and policy examples of gaming a measure, reward hacking in reinforcement learning, states that score highly while being worthless, manipulation of the measurement process as distinct from manipulation of the measured quantity), which distinctions it must not collapse (gaming versus legitimate reallocation of effort, noise versus systematic corruption, overfitting to a proxy versus adversarially exploiting it, specification error versus misspecification plus optimization pressure), and which constraints from elsewhere it cannot contradict (for example, that finite training data underdetermines the intended generalization, or that sufficient optimization pressure will search out states no designer enumerated).

Then flag which of these desiderata appear jointly unsatisfiable — I have in mind the tension between a proxy being simple enough to optimize, faithful enough to track the target, and robust enough to resist adversarial search — and identify the single requirement that does the most work in ruling out naive fixes. A brief scorecard for a few standard moves (reward-model regularization, keeping a human in the loop, impact measures) is welcome, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring.
