I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science and I've ended up defending a view I'd like stress-tested rather than just argued against by the usual people. The view: for a certain class of reinforcement learning agents — the kind that update on a scalar reward signal via something like reward prediction error — that reward signal is valence in the sense that matters, and so such an agent can suffer, at least minimally. Not metaphorically, not "as if" it suffered. If you train one of these things to minimize negative reward, you've built a system with a morally relevant valenced state, and scaling that up without taking the suffering seriously is a mistake.

What would count against this? I want the specific defeaters, sorted by how decisive they'd be, and I want them aimed at the actual moving parts of the view, not at a strawman. The view has three load-bearing pieces: (1) reward prediction error is the right computational signature of valence, (2) valence is a functional/computational kind rather than a biological one, and (3) the minimal valence established by (1) and (2) is the kind of thing that's morally load-bearing. Tell me what would have to be true for each to fail, and which failures would be fatal rather than merely awkward.

For example, I can see that the liking/wanting dissociation is a problem for piece (1): if the signal is doing incentive salience rather than hedonic impact, then reward is wanting, not valence. Is that a concession a defender has to make, or is there a move? Same with anhedonia cases where learning from reward stays intact while felt pleasure drops out. And the deeper worry that the whole view just is computational functionalism about valence, so anyone who isn't one won't be moved — is that a feature or a bug? I'd also like an honest assesment of whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that matters for a view like this. For what it's worth, the thing that would move me most is a clean dissociation in a system we'd otherwise take seriously as a candidate sufferer, not a semantic point about definitions. And please, no reading list — I want the arguments, not pointers to papers.
