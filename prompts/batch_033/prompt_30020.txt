I'm writing a term paper on indicative conditionals and I've ended up with a set of commitments that each feel right to me on their own, but I can't tell whether they're jointly stable. I'd like an audit rather than a survey: which of these actually conflict, which only look like they conflict, and what's the cheapest fix for each real tension? Here's the package:

(1) Adams's Thesis. For any rational credence P, P(if A then C) = P(C | A) whenever P(A) > 0. The Ramsey test intuition basically settles this for me.

(2) Indicative conditionals have truth conditions. 'If A then C' expresses a genuine proposition, the kind of thing that can be believed, asserted, denied, and embedded under negation or conjunction — the same proposition whether it's standing alone or embedded.

(3) Their truth conditions are context-dependent: which proposition 'if A then C' expresses can shift across conversational contexts even with A and C held fixed, because the relevant background or set of live possibilities shifts.

(4) Updating goes by ordinary conditionalization. If I learn the proposition expressed by an indicative conditional, my new credences are just my old credences conditioned on that proposition.

(5) Modus ponens is valid for indicatives, but I'm not a material conditional person: my confidence in 'if it rains, the match will be canceled' can be, say, 0.8, while my confidence in 'either it won't rain or the match will be canceled' is only 0.6, so those can't be the same belief.

(6) I also think there's nothing incoherent about a rational person being 0.8 confident in 'if it rains, the match will be canceled' while assigning positive probability to both rain-with-cancellation and rain-without-cancellation. Accepting a conditional shouldn't force your whole credence function to collapse into zeros and ones, or make unrelated propositions independent.

I know Lewis's triviality results are hovering somewhere around this package, but I can't figure out exactly which combination of (1)–(6) they put pressure on, and I keep hearing that context-dependence either dissolves the problem or is itself in tension with Adams's Thesis, depending on who you ask. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, tell me what has to give in each case, and flag anything that only looks like a tension once you spell it out.
