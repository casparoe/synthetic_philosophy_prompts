I'm currently taking an upper-level philosophy of science course, and I've been asked to lead a 50-minute activity for a section of about 18 students on Kuhnian paradigm shifts and incommensurability. I want the activity to make participants feel, before the idea is named, that two groups can look at the same materials, each apply internally workable standards, and still systematically talk past each other—not because one side is irrational, but because they disagree about what the data are and what would count as a good explanation. Please design a runnable simulation or game: split the section into two "research communities" with different sorting or problem-solving principles, give them a shared task that forces coordination, and arrange the rules so the breakdown in translation emerges from the task rather than from a script. Include printable materials, a timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments onto the difference between ordinary disagreement and incommensurability, predicts what will go wrong the first time, and states honestly which parts of Kuhn's historical picture the exercise cannot reproduce. No lecture up front, and the term "incommensurability" should appear only in the debrief.
