I'm giving a short speech at the ceremony where my brother is being posthumously awarded a medal for pulling a child out of a burning car. He died of his burns a few days later. He was a volunteer firefighter who read moral philosophy in the firehouse, and he'd told me more than once that he didn't believe in heroes: the same decision to run toward a fire can make you a hero or a dead man, and which one you turn out to be is outcome luck, not character. Half the room will be his crew, who hate that kind of talk and want him remembered as a hero; the other half are family, who just want to hear that he was brave. Write the speech. It has to accept the medal without saying anything he would have thought was false, and it should land as a brother's love, not a lecture. Don't put words in his mouth; I can't trust my memory for quotes.
