I'm a journalist covering the science and ideas beat, and I've been interviewing people about the simulation argument for a piece I'm writing. I keep hearing the same three-horned disjunction from Bostrom's 2003 paper, but I want to get more granular than that. Specifically, I want someone to actually put numbers on the sub-questions and commit.

So here's my ask: conditional on accepting that consciousness is substrate-independant — that you can run a mind on something other than biological neurons — what's your credence that we are currently living in a computer simulation? And I want the decomposition, not just the number.

Please break it down into at least the following terms, give me a probability for each, and a sentence of justification:

1. The probability that at least one civilisation in the history of the actual universe reaches technological maturity, where "maturity" means they have the computational resources and the engineering know-how to run vast numbers of high-fidelity ancestor-simulations.

2. The probability that at least one such civilisation actually chooses to run ancestor-simulations in large numbers — i.e., not just a handful for academic research but billions or trillions of them. This is the sociological and motivational term that Bostrom basically punted on.

3. The probability that, conditional on such simulations being run, the ratio of simulated minds to non-simulated (base-level) minds is high enough that the indifference principle gives us a strong reason to think we're among the simulated ones.

4. A term I don't see discussed enough: the probability that the base-level physics of the simulating universe actually permits the kind of fine-grained simulation of our universe's physics that would be required to produce conscious beings like us. (If the simulating world has, say, a much coarser Planck scale, or different computational limits, then the simulations might be too low-resolution.)

Then combine those to get your overall credence, and tell me which term is doing the most work — if you could get a definitive answer on one of these four tomorrow, which one would shift your overall number the most, and in which direction?

Also, just for fun: what's your credence that we're in a simulation of the entire universe, vs. a simulation of just Earth and its immediate surroundings (what some people call a "planetarium" simulation)?

Oh, and write the whole thing in British English, please — I'm based in London and my editor will have my head if I let "civilization" through without an "s".
