I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a friend about spectrum arguments for the intransitivity of value. He walked me through a sequence: begin with a life of extraordinary flourishing lasting a hundred years; then consider a slightly longer life at a quality only just below the first, which seems at least as good; then another slightly longer life at a slightly lower quality; and so on, until we arrive at a life of bare, barely tolerable existence lasting for centuries, which we both agreed was clearly worse than the starting point. His conclusion was that 'at least as good as' must fail to be transitive, or else that our pairwise judgments across such a spectrum cannot all be trusted at once. I came away holding the opposite view: betterness is transitive, and the real lesson of the spectrum is that some step in the chain is being misdescribed — perhaps a small loss of quality is not always a genuine improvement, perhaps the apparently innocent step is not innocent once threshold effects and context are made explicit, or perhaps our pairwise judgments are simply unreliable at small differences.

I want to know what would count against that view. What would the world, our evaluative practice, or the formal theory of rational choice have to look like for 'at least as good as' to be genuinely intransitive rather than merely apparently so? I would like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward: which cases, arguments, or results should move me, and which am I entitled to dismiss as artifacts of vagueness, framing, or small-difference noise? I would personally find it most persuasive if someone could show that the intransitivity survives after idealizing away those confounds — say, in a forced-choice setting with fully specified, clearly separated outcomes — or that a well-motivated theory of value, rather than a contrived sequence, requires giving up transitivity. I would also like to know which of these a committed defender of transitivity should concede as a genuine cost even if it can ultimately be explained away, and what the transitivity view predicts about spectrum cases that an intransitivity view does not. Finally, I want an honest assessment of whether my position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — whether 'some step in the chain is misdescribed' is the kind of claim that can never be pinned down — and whether that insulation is a defect in a view of this kind.
