Write a sonnet about Molyneux's problem. The speaker is someone born blind who has just had sight restored. The octave should render the tactile world with precision: the cube known by its edges, its vertices, the way a corner bites the palm; the sphere known by its continuous unbroken surface, the absence of any place where the curvature changes. These are not guesses—they are certainties earned over years of handling. The volta (line 9) is the moment the bandages come off. The sestet should stage the test: two shapes on a table, one faceted, one smooth, seen for the first time, and the speaker must say, without reaching, which was the cube and which the sphere. The closing couplet should not resolve the question but should state the philosophical stake: whether the mind is born with separate sensory provinces that must be connected by experience, or whether there is a common language of space that sight and touch both speak from the start.
