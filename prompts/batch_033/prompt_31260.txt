In Liu Cixin's novel *The Dark Forest*, the universe is compared to a dark forest at night: every civilization is an armed hunter creeping through the trees, and the only rational response upon discovering another living thing is to shoot it dead before it shoots you. Silence is survival; any broadcast is suicide. This analogy has migrated from fiction into serious debates about whether humanity should engage in METI — actively messaging extraterrestrial intelligence — with proponents of the dark forest view arguing that our radio leakage and the Arecibo message were already reckless. I want to audit the analogy. What maps onto what: the hunters, the forest, the shot, the silence? Where does the mapping break once you move from a terrestrial forest — where hunters share a planet, move at comparable speeds, and can kill each other with single shots — to an interstellar context governed by light-speed delays, the inverse-square law, and the sheer cost of interstellar attack? And does the practical conclusion — that we should go quiet — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is the analogy doing more to frame the question than to answer it?
