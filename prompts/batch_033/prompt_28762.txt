I've been trying to work out what I actually think about heterophenomenology, so I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases where a consciousness researcher is deciding how much weight to give a subject's verbal report. Here they are:

- Blindsight: the subject insists they see nothing, but guesses where a light is with 90% accuracy. I think the report should be taken at face value as a sincere report of no visual experience, but it doesn't settle whether there was unconscious visual processing.
- Change blindness: the subject says they'd definately have noticed if the two people in the photos had been swapped, even though the trial shows they didn't. I think the report is useful data, but the claim it expresses about their own noticing is false.
- Split-brain: after the right hemisphere is shown a snow scene and the left hemisphere a chicken claw, the speaking hemisphere picks a shovel and explains that it goes with the chicken. I think this is not a report of an experience at all, or at least not data about what the subject experienced.
- Meditator: a subject reports an episode of pure, objectless awareness with no sense of time. I think the report is evidence that something unusual happened, but not that the experience was actually contentless.
- Anton's syndrome: a patient denies being blind and describes the room they're in, confabulating the details. I think the report tells us about the confabulation, but the belief that they can see is part of the syndrome and isn't evidence that they see.
- Afterimage: a subject says the afterimage is greenish. I think this is about as authoritative as a first-person report gets, and we should take the afterimage to be greenish.

Is there a single principle about the scientific status of first-person reports that gets all of these, or close? If so, state it precisely and say which of my verdicts it forces me to drop or reinterpret. If not, tell me where the fault line is. And whatever principle you end up with, what does it say about a subject who reports no inner speech while a classifier trained on there brain activity says otherwise?
