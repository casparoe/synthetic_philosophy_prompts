So in court you swear to tell "the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth." But perjury law doesn't punish you for getting things wrong — it only punishes you if you *knew* it was false, or at least didn't believe what you were saying. So the oath seems to demand truth, but the enforcement only kicks in when you assert without knowledge (or without sincere belief). What norm of assertion does this whole setup presuppose? Is it actually coherent to have a truth-norm at the oath stage and a knowledge-norm at the punishment stage, or is the system just confused? And what would have to change if we picked one norm and followed it all the way through — like, if we actually prosecuted false testimony regardless of the speaker's state of mind, or if we rewrote the oath to say "do you swear to tell what you believe to be true"?
