in my physics class we talked about newtons bucket — the spinning bucket of water where the water surface gets concave, and newton says this proves absolute space exists because the water curves even when its not moving relative to the bucket. two guys in my class got into it after and i couldnt tell who was right. here's roughly how it went:

A: newton is just assuming what he wants to prove. he says the water is "really" rotating, and by "really" he means relative to absolute space. but we have no independent way to detect absolute space. its circular.

B: no, the water surface is a physical effect. something is causing it. the water isnt moving relative to the bucket, and it isnt moving relative to the ground. so what is the motion relative to? newton says space itself. what else could it be?

A: the rest of the universe. the water is rotating relative to the fixed stars. mach said if the whole universe spun around a stationary bucket the water would still curve. the effect is about relative motion to other masses, not absolute space.

B: but mach's thing is purely hypothetical, we cant test it. and newtons actual point is that the water curves even when bucket and water are at the same rate relative to the lab. the water is moving relative to *itself* — parts of it are accelerating relative to other parts. thats not about the bucket or the stars, its about the water.

A: parts moving relative to other parts is still just relations among material things. you dont need absolute space, you just need a relationism that isnt stupid — rotation is a global property of the system of relations, not reducible to motion relative to one object.

B: then what IS rotation on your view? if everything in the universe rotated together, would the water still curve? if you say yes, your back to absolute space. if you say no, then rotation is somehow defined by relations to distant matter, which is way weirder than just admitting space exists.

A: weirder maybe, but not incoherent. and anyway general relativity shows spacetime is dynamical, not newtons fixed background, so absolute space in newtons sense is just false. the question is whether the bucket argument even worked on its own terms.

B: your using later physics to dodge the argument. newtons point was that the bucket shows a distinction between real and apparent motion that requires a privileged frame. call it absolute space, call it inertial structure — the bucket shows *something* non-relational is needed.
