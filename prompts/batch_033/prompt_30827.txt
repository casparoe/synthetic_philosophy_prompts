Question 4 (20 marks). Here are the usual Allais gambles. 1A: £1,000,000 for sure. 1B: £5,000,000 with probability 0.10, £1,000,000 with probability 0.89, and £0 with probability 0.01. 2A: £1,000,000 with probability 0.11 and £0 with probability 0.89. 2B: £5,000,000 with probability 0.10 and £0 with probability 0.90. Most people prefer 1A to 1B and 2B to 2A. Expected utility theory cannot represent this pattern. Explain how risk-weighted expected utility theory (REU) differs from orthodox expected utility theory, and how it can accommodate these preferences. Be explicit about which axiom of expected utility theory REU rejects.

Student's answer:

Expected utility (EU) says an agent maximises Σ p_i u(x_i). The Allais pattern (1A over 1B, 2B over 2A) is a problem for EU because it violates the independence axiom: adding or removing a common outcome should not change the preference between two gambles. EU also identifies risk aversion with the curvature of u, so a risk-averse agent has to have diminishing marginal utility for money.

Risk-weighted expected utility keeps the utility function but adds a risk function r, with r(0)=0, r(1)=1, and r(p)<p for a risk-averse agent. It weights each outcome by r(p_i) instead of p_i, so the value of a gamble is Σ r(p_i)u(x_i). Because r is increasing, this formula still respects stochastic dominance. And because the r function discounts good outcomes, an agent can be risk-averse even with a linear utility function, which EU cannot allow.

In the Allais case, REU can deliver the usual preferences. In the first pair, the sure million beats 1B because r discounts the 0.99 chance of getting at least a million, so the 1% chance of ending up with nothing is felt more than it is under EU. In the second pair, both options are long shots, and after r discounts the small probabilities the five-million prize in 2B outweighs the slightly larger 0.11 chance of a million in 2A. So REU handles the Allais paradox while keeping the independence axiom—the only change is to how probabilities enter.
