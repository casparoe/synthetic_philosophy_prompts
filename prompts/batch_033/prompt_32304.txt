I retired from a career in experimental physics a few years ago and have since been reading my way through the philosophical literature on probability. I find that I have settled into a set of views, but I am not confident they all cohere, and I would be grateful for a careful audit. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept the Principal Principle: when you know that the objective chance of some outcome is x, your credence in that outcome ought to be x, provided you possess no inadmissible information.

(2) I take objective chances to be genuine physical quantities — not merely expressions of ignorance, not subjective degrees of belief, but features of the world on a par with mass or charge.

(3) I find the Humean account of chance the most compelling: chances are not primitive modal properties. Rather, they supervene on the entire actual distribution of outcomes across the history of the universe, as captured by the best systematization of those outcomes — the system that achieves the optimal balance of simplicity and fit.

(4) I hold that rational credences update by conditionalization and nothing else.

(5) I believe that in a deterministic universe, there are no nontrivial objective chances — all chances are zero or one, and any appearance of probability is purely epistemic.

(6) I believe our universe is not deterministic at the fundamental level; quantum mechanics provides genuine, nontrivial objective chances.

Please go through these six commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension but are not, and what the least costly revision would be to restore consistency. I would like the tensions ranked by severity, with a brief explanation for each ranking, rather than treated as a flat list. If some tensions are more foundational than others — if resolving one would dissolve another downstream — please flag that as well.
