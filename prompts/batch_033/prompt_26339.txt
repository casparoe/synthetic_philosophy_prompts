I was listening to this philosophy podcast the other day — one of those "big ideas explained" shows — and they did an episode on the epistemic regress problem. The host summarized the three main positions like this (I'm paraphrasing from memory, but I think I've got the gist):

"Here's the problem. When you believe something, you need reasons. But those reasons are themselves beliefs that need reasons, and those need reasons, and so on. You get an infinite regress. So there are three ways out. Foundationalism says the chain stops at basic beliefs — beliefs that are self-evident or indubitable, like 'I seem to see a red patch' or '2+2=4.' These don't need further justification because they're certain. Coherentism says there's no foundation; instead, beliefs support each other in a web, like a circular argument but spread out enough that it doesn't feel circular. The problem is this is just question-begging at scale — if your only reason for believing P is Q and your only reason for Q is P, you haven't justified anything. Infinitism says the chain goes on forever and that's fine, which is obviously absurd because you can't complete an infinite chain of reasons. So foundationalism is really the only game in town, and the debate is just about which kind of foundationalism."

I found this pretty convincing but then I mentioned it to my epistemology professor and she kind of winced and said the podcast was "not being fair to coherentism or infinitism" and that even the presentation of foundationalism was "about fifty years out of date." She didn't have time to elaborate and now I'm left wondering. I'm trying to figure out which parts of the podcast summary are reasonable simplifications for a general audience and which parts are genuinely misleading. Like, is infinitism actually defended seriously by anyone? And does coherentism really reduce to circular reasoning? Also, do foundationalists still think basic beliefs have to be indubitable, or is my professor right that that's an old-fasioned version of the view? Basically I want to know what I should unlearn from this episode.
