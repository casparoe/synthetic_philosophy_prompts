Read Everything Everywhere All at Once through Lewis's modal realism. The movie gives us a multiverse where every choice branches into a new universe, the other Evelyns are meant to be just as real as the one we start with, and she can verse-jump into their lives to borrow skills and memories. I want a serious reading that asks whether this is actually a Lewisian multiverse — concrete worlds, all equally actual, no trans-world identity, just counterpart relations — or something closer to a branching or ersatz picture, and whether the difference matters for what the film says about regret. In particular, does verse-jumping push the film into trans-world identity in a way that undercuts the counterpart-theoretic moral it seems to want, and does the film confuse epistemic access to other worlds with causal connection between them? Where does the metaphor hold, where does it trade on a confusion, and does the film know the difference?
