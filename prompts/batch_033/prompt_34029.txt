i'm putting together a 'what's your actual view on aesthetic testimony?' quiz for my aesthetics seminar and i want it to actually sort people by what they'd do in cases rather than by which label sounds smartest. the core question is whether you can legitimately form aesthetic judgments on the basis of what other people tell you, without firsthand experience of the thing yourself.

i need maybe eight or nine questions, each a concrete scenario. some cases i definitely want included: (1) a friend whose taste you respect tells you a film is masterful but you haven't seen it — can you assert 'it's a great film' at a dinner party? (2) a wine critic you trust describes a wine's finish as 'elegant' — do you now know the wine is elegant without tasting it? (3) you read a detailed description of a painting you'll never see because it was destroyed — can you form a justified aesthetic belief about it? (4) you used to love a band as a teenager but have no memory of their music — your old self's testimony is all you have, so do you know their music was good? (5) a mathematician tells you a proof is 'beautiful' — is this aesthetic testimony different from testimony about art?

the scoring key should sort people into positions like: hard pessimism (aesthetic testimony never transmits knowledge or justification), moderate pessimism (it can transmit propositional knowledge that something has an aesthetic property but not the corresponding aesthetic experience or understanding), optimism (aesthetic testimony works roughly like ordinary testimony), and any other positions that emerge from the logical space. if some combination of answers doesn't map to any defended position, flag it and say why — is it incoherent, just unattractive, or has nobody gotten around to defending it yet?

also: i want the quiz to expose whether people treat different aesthetic domains (visual art, music, food, mathematics, nature) differently without them realizing it. so build in cross-domain comparisons.
