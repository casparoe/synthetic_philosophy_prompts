I have a set of gut reactions about when two people may rationally disagree, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

Two physicians start from the same diagnostic model but see different test results for the same patient and announce different probabilities that the patient has the disease. Verdict: unproblematic; their disagreement just reflects different information, and if they pooled everything I'd expect their numbers to converge.

Two macroeconomists are handed the same data and the same forecasting model, yet one ends up at 70 percent that inflation will exceed 4 percent next year and the other at 30 percent, because they entered with different priors over the model's parameters. Verdict: I want to say they can't both be fully rational; at least one is making an error.

Two people with very different upbringings and exposure to very different institutions disagree about the probability that a social program will work. They sit down and exchange every argument and piece of evidence each can articulate, and they still disagree. Verdict: this feels legitimate in a way the economists' case does not; their histories feel like a real source of evidence, not just noise.

Two jurors hear exactly the same trial evidence, share all their reasoning in the jury room, and end up with different probabilities of guilt, each aware of the other's view. Verdict: something has gone wrong; I can't accept that both are reasoning correctly.

Two partisans read the same mixed study about a policy and both become more confident in their opposite prior positions. Verdict: clearly irrational, whatever the principle is.

Is there one principle — something like the common prior assumption, or a weaker or stronger variant of it — that captures all of these, or close? If not, which verdict should I give up? And what does the principle you settle on say about the case I keep going back and forth on: two rational agents who share all their evidence, know each other's priors and posteriors, and still agree to disagree about a factual question?
