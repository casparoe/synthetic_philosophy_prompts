I cover the science beat for a general-audience magazine, and I'm working on a piece about proposed resolutions to the Fermi paradox. I'd like to get your credence on a specific claim: that the dark forest hypothesis — roughly, the idea that the reason we observe no sign of extraterrestrial civilizations is that all sufficiently advanced civilizations adopt a policy of silence and preemptive destruction, because any civilization that reveals its location is eventually annihilated by another — is either the correct resolution to the Fermi paradox or a major part of the correct resolution.

I'm not asking for a bare number. I want the number decomposed into the sub-questions your credence depends on, with a rough probability for each, a line or two of justification, and an explanation of how they combine to yield your overall estimate. In particular, I'd expect the decomposition to include at least the following terms:

(a) The probability that there exist other technological civilizations in our galaxy (or the observable universe) whose non-detection requires a substantive explanation — that is, that the "they exist" side of the Drake equation is not already ruled out by some early great filter or by the rare-earth hypothesis.

(b) Conditional on other civilizations existing, the probability that interstellar relations are best modeled as a conflict-dominated or security-dilemma dynamic, rather than one of cooperation, indifference, convergent goals, or a single dominant civilization that enforces silence for other reasons.

(c) Conditional on both of the above, the probability that the strategic logic of preemptive strike is sufficiently compelling — given what we can infer about the physics of interstellar travel, detection signatures, and the feasibility of a decisive first strike — that universal silence is the stable equilibrium, rather than a patchwork of visible and hidden civilizations or some other outcome.

(d) The probability that the dark forest hypothesis outperforms the main alternative explanations for the silence — the great filter lying ahead of us rather than behind us, the zoo or planetarium hypothesis, the possibility that we have not looked long enough or in the right modality, or the possibility that technological civilizations are simply too sparse for contact on observable timescales.

Once you have your decomposition and your combined number, I'd like you to tell me which of these terms your final credence is most sensitive to — that is, which sub-question, if you changed your mind about it, would produce the largest shift in your overall estimate. And then: what kind of evidence or argument would most shift your view on that particular term? I'm less interested in "we detect a signal tomorrow" than in what conceptual or inferential consideration would genuinely move the needle for you.
