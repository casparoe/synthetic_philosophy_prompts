I am preparing material for a graduate seminar on decision theory, and I should like a worked example of expected value fanaticism that does not lean on the usual devices. Please construct a thought experiment to the following specification. Set the case in an ordinary institutional context — a public health authority, an insurer, or a philanthropic foundation — in which an agent who maximises expected utility with an unbounded utility function linear in lives saved is compelled by the theory to choose an option that a reasonable observer would call fanatical, even though it carries the higher expected value, while a risk-weighted expected utility theorist who accepts the same utility function but applies a risk-averse probability weighting function declines that option. The probabilities must be finite and well enough grounded that a competent agent could estimate them from ordinary empirical frequencies, and the payoffs must be finite and of a magnitude a real institution could face; there should be no infinite utilities, no infinitesimal probabilities treated as exact, no Pascal's-mugging structure, and no perfect predictors or exotic physics. The point of the case is to isolate the source of fanaticism, so the verdicts must be generated by the two decision rules from the numbers, not stipulated in the description of the options.

After presenting the scenario, show the calculation for each theory, stating the utility function, the probabilities, and the risk function you have used. Then identify which structural feature of the case is doing the work — for example, the unboundedness of the utility function, the linearity of expectation in the probabilities, or the way the risk function treats small probabilities — and say what happens to the divergence if that feature is removed. Conclude with the response a committed expected utility maximiser would give: why would she say the rival theory has merely renamed risk aversion, and what, if anything, would make her concede that the case exposes a genuine problem rather than a difference in temperament?
