I am a retired chemist who has lately been reading in the cognitive science of religion, and I have been testing my understanding against a friend who works in that field. She uses a naturalistic sense of the term religion in her cross-cultural coding, and I have kept a record of her rulings on particular cases:

- Ancestor veneration, in which the dead are believed to observe and sanction the living: she codes it as religious.
- A Theravada Buddhist community whose practice is organized around karma and rebirth rather than a creator deity: religious.
- A cargo cult that awaits the return of ancestors bearing material goods: religious.
- A New Age movement whose members perform group rituals to align themselves with an impersonal cosmic energy and treat that alignment as morally significant: religious.
- A football club with chants, colors, and collective excitement, which its members treat as entertainment rather than as a source of moral obligation or sacred value: not religious.
- A secular mindfulness program used purely for stress reduction, with no metaphysical claims: not religious.

Next week I intend to ask her about a secular humanist congregation that meets weekly, marks births and deaths with shared rites, and treats human dignity and ecological stewardship as sacred and non-negotiable, while explicitly denying anything supernatural. I want to know what verdict I should expect if she is being consistent with her earlier rulings. I am less interested in the bare verdict than in the rule she seems to be running, the alternative rules that fit her track record just as well, and whether her rulings so far genuinely underdetermine the choice between a substantive rule, on which religion requires supernatural agents or forces, and a functionalist rule, on which religion requires sacred values, ritual, and moral community, with supernatural content optional. What would each rule predict about the new case, which is the better reading of her track record, and what further case should I put to her to settle which rule she is actually using?
