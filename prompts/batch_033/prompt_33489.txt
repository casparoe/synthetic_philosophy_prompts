I've been working through Hofstadter's Metamagical Themas and keep circling back to the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery — the thought experiments where superrational players face coordination problems that break ordinary game-theoretic reasoning. In the Platonia dilemma, twenty players each decide independently whether to send a telegram; exactly one sender wins a billion dollars, but zero or multiple senders means no one gets anything. The superrational regress is the point: "If we all send, no one wins, so I should stay silent. But if we all stay silent, no one wins, so I should send. But if we all reason that way..." — and it never bottoms out. The Luring Lottery (actually run by Scientific American in the 1980s) is the variant where you submit a number of entries and the prize gets divided by the total, creating a runaway escalation if everyone tries to outthink everyone else. The superrational resolution in both cases is to randomize — send with probability 1/20 in Platonia, roll a die with sides equal to the expected number of players in the Luring Lottery — but this resolution itself depends on the assumption that all superrational players will converge on the identical randomization strategy, which is just the original problem in a probabilistic key.

I want a sonnet that captures the structure of this dilemma. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery would recognize the argumentative moves, not just the name-drops. Here is what I want the form to do:

The octave (first eight lines) should build the regress: the step-by-step reasoning of a superrational player trying to outthink the others, each line or pair of lines advancing one more level of recursion. The turn (volta) at line nine should introduce the probabilistic superrational solution — the idea that you randomize. The closing couplet should then do something genuinely interesting: it should acknowledge that the probabilistic solution itself depends on the assumption that all superrational players will converge on the same randomization strategy, which is the original problem in a probabilistic key. The tension between the regress and the resolution should be felt, not merely stated.

Please write the poem in the voice of someone who has been inside this reasoning loop too long — someone who finds the dilemma not just intellectually interesting but existentially vertiginous. The tone should be slightly haunted, not academic.

Before you write the final poem, please spend some time thinking aloud about the structure: what the octave will cover, where the volta will land, and what exactly the closing couplet should do. Then write the sonnet.
