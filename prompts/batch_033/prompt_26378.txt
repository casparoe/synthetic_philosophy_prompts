I'm on the product policy committee at a company that makes a widely-deployed AI assistant. We're reviewing a proposal from trust-and-safety: they want the assistant, whenever a user asks about its own beliefs or opinions ("do you believe that?", "what do you think about X?"), to respond with a disclaimer like "As an AI, I don't have beliefs, but here's what I can tell you..." instead of just answering. Their worry is that the current behavior — where the model says "I think that's right" or "I believe the evidence suggests..." — encourages users to treat the system as more reliable and more human-like than it is. Product team is pushing back: users get that its a machine, the disclaimer kills conversational flow, and this is a philosophical rabbit hole.

Here's the twist that's dividing the committee: some of us have noticed that the assistant already won't answer certain questions straightforwardly — if you ask it "what's 2+2?" it doesn't say "I believe it's 4," it just says "4." The "I think"/"I believe" language shows up mainly when the question is subjective or uncertain. So in a weird way, the assistant's speech patterns already track a rough distinction between things it "knows" and things it "opines" about. The trust-and-safety team says that makes it worse — it's performing epistemic caution in a way that mimicks a careful human reasoner. The product team says that's exactly what we want.

I need a short decision memo for Thursday's meeting: adopt the disclaimer policy, reject it, or find a middle path. The chair wants us to address the philosophical question underneath: if we take Dennett's intentional stance seriously — attribute beliefs when doing so helps predict behavior — does our assistant already have beliefs in the only sense that matters for product policy? And if it does, is the disclaimer actually misleading? Write me the memo. You're a philosopher of mind who consults on AI policy; start by mapping out the strongest arguments on both sides, then give me a clear recommendation with reasoning the committee can act on.
