Two people in my department got into it at journal club the other day. Dana's a molecular biologist and Sam works on philosophy of biology across the hall. The paper was about codon usage bias, and the discussion kept sliding back to whether the genome "encodes" proteins. Dana said she was glad the authors didn't waste space hand-wringing about whether DNA is really a code, because of course it is: there's a codon table, transcription, translation, the whole machinery. Sam said "encodes" is fine as shorthand, but the moment someone insists the genome is literally a code they've imported a metaphor. A real code has a sender and a receiver who share conventions; molecules don't share anything, they just bump into each other causally.

Dana said codes don't require intentions. In Shannon's sense a code is just a systematic mapping between two sets of symbols, and the genetic system is exactly that — codons to amino acids. If Sam wants to say smoke "codes for" fire, fine, but that's beside the point.

Sam said that's exactly the problem: on Shannon's thin sense every cause carries information about its effect, so "code" stops doing any explanatory work. When biologists say the genome is a code, they're quietly helping themselves to something richer — the idea that the sequence has a meaning over and above the chemistry — and that's the part he denies.

Dana came back that the rich part isn't semantics, it's arbitrariness. The same codon could of been paired with a different amino acid; the mapping is a historical accident that got frozen in place, like a convention that stuck. That's what makes it a code rather than just a chemical reaction, and that's why synthetic biology can rewrite it — people literally reprogram cells by swapping the assignments.

Sam said reprogramming only shows we can impose a coding relation on a cell, not that the cell already had one. You can set up a river to do arithmetic, but the river isn't computing on its own.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, and who's more right overall?
