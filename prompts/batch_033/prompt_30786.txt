I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. In discussions about AI and mind uploading — at work, on forums, in the kind of long dinner arguments engineers have — someone eventually says something like "the mind is to the brain as software is to hardware." The move that follows is usually: software is substrate-independent (it runs on different hardware), so the mind is substrate-independent too, so mind uploading or machine consciousness is possible in principle. I can feel the analogy doing real work, but I can also feel it papering over distinctions, and I don't have the vocabulary to separate them.

I'd like you to take that slogan — "the mind is to the brain as software is to hardware" — and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, from the trivial to the radical. For each reading, tell me: (a) whether it's plausible given what we actually know about minds and computation, (b) whether it's a claim a neuroscientist or philosopher of mind would recognize as controversial, and (c) whether that specific reading would actually license the inference to mind uploading or machine consciousness. I want to see which version of the claim I'd need to defend if I wanted the argument to go through, and which versions collapse into something too weak to do the job or too strong to be defensible.
