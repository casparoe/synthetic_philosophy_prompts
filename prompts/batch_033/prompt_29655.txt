Two friends of mine got into it at a party over a study one of them had read saying happiness is about 50% heritable. I've been turning it over since and genuinely can't tell who's right.

(1) Dana said the study shows about half of a person's happiness is set by their genes, so there's a real ceiling on how much therapy or lifestyle changes can do for someone.

(2) Marcus said that's not what heritability means: its a number about variation in a population, not about causes inside any individual, and it says nothing about how changeable the trait is.

(3) Dana shot back that if identical twins are much more alike in happiness than fraternal twins, genes are obviously doing a lot of causal work, so saying the number "says nothing about causes" is absurd.

(4) Marcus said he never denied genes do causal work. But the same trait can be highly heritable in one environment and barely heritable in another, so a heritability estimate is a fact about a population at a time, not a fixed fact about the trait itself.

(5) Dana said fine, but if the number is 50% in our population, then genetic differences explain half of why people differ in happiness, so changing everyone's environment could only close at most half the gap between the happiest and least happy.

(6) Marcus said that doesn't follow: height is extremely heritable, yet average height has risen a lot with better nutrition, so high heritability is compatible with large environmental effects.

(7) Dana said average height rising over generations is a different question from differences between people at one time, and her claim was about differences among people now.

(8) Marcus said okay, but even at one time, heritability is a variance decomposition and doesn't set a limit on what an intervention could do.

Then it petered out. Could you adjudicate this move by move? For each step say who's right and who's wrong and how much, and tell me what both of them are missing.
