ok so i'm trying to sort out what i think about pain and ive ended up with a set of views that i'm not sure all fit together. this is for a philosophy of mind seminar paper and i'd rather find out now than in the comments. here's where i am:

(1) pain is essentially a conscious experience — there's something it's like, and that's what makes it pain rather than just nociceptive signaling.

(2) the badness of pain — the reason we want it to stop, the reason it matters morally — is intrinsic to the experience itself. it's not that pain is a neutral sensation we happen to dislike; the disliking is built into what the experience is.

(3) pain asymbolia is a real neurological condition where people can feel pain (locate it, rate its intensity, describe it as sharp or burning) but they genuinely don't find it unpleasant or distressing. they don't try to escape it, they don't wince, they might even smile.

(4) we should take the introspective reports of pain asymbolia patients at face value — they're not confused, not in denial, not using words weirdly. when they say it doesn't bother them, it really doesn't bother them.

(5) representationalism about pain: the phenomenal character of pain is exhausted by its representational content — roughly, that there's tissue damage in a certain body part.

(6) if two experiences have the same phenomenal character, they have the same intrinsic properties.

i keep going back and forth on whether this is a consistent set. sometimes i think (3) and (4) just show that (2) is false — pain isn't intrinsically bad, the badness is a separate reaction. but then i worry i'm giving up too much. other times i think (5) saves me because it means the asymbolic's experience has different content (maybe they're representing the damage but not representing it as bad), but then i'm not sure that's really representationalism anymore. and then there's a part of me that thinks (6) is the real problem and i should just deny that the asymbolic and i have the same phenomenal character, but that feels like i'm not taking their reports seriously.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what the least costly revision would be. rank the tensions by severity. and if there's a way to keep (1)-(4) and (6) while dropping or modifying (5), i'd especially want to hear that, since (5) is the one i'm least attached to.
