Construct a thought experiment in which the knowledge norm of assertion and the belief norm of assertion come apart in the less familiar direction: a speaker asserts p, satisfies the knowledge norm because she knows p, but violates the belief norm because she does not believe p. The case is for a paper arguing that the two norms are not extensionally equivalent, so the divergence must be generated by the scenario and not stipulated in how you describe it. Constraints: an ordinary adult setting (a workplace, clinic, courtroom, or classroom); no lotteries, no fake barns, no Gettier-style deviant causal chains, no perfect predictors, and no demons; no global skepticism; and the utterance must be a genuine assertion made on the basis of the very grounds that constitute the speaker's knowledge — not a lie, a guess, a quotation, irony, or the recital of a script. After the scenario, state the proposition p; explain why the speaker counts as knowing p; explain why she counts as not believing p; and give the verdict each norm delivers. Then identify the single feature of the case doing the work to prise knowledge and belief apart, explain how a defender of the belief norm would attack the case, and say what the case looks like if that feature is removed.
