Here's my opening. Take "stealing is wrong" and "if stealing is wrong, then getting your little brother to steal is wrong." In the first sentence the speaker is expressing disapproval of stealing. In the antecedent of the second sentence she isn't expressing disapproval of stealing — she might be agnostic, or a parent explaining a chain of reasoning to a kid. If "wrong" means something different in those two spots, then modus ponens should fail, because the middle term is ambiguous; if it means the same thing, then its meaning can't be the attitude expressed, since no attitude is expressed in the antecedent.
