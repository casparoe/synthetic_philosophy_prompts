A colleague of mine works on the ethics of belief and has taken positions on a series of cases over the years. Here are her verdicts, as best I can reconstruct them:

2. **Jury member:** A juror votes to convict based on a strong gut feeling that the defendant is lying, though the evidence is genuinely ambiguous. Verdict: impermissible.
3. **Stranded hiker:** A hiker lost in the wilderness with no map believes, without evidence, that heading east will lead to safety. Verdict: permissible.
4. **Religious faith:** A person believes in God on the basis of faith, where the question is genuinely live for them and the evidence is inconclusive. Verdict: permissible.
5. **Investor and tip:** An investor believes a stock will soar based on a tip from a source she knows to be unreliable. Verdict: impermissible.
6. **Parent of missing child:** A parent believes their missing child is still alive after years with no evidence either way, and the belief sustains the search. Verdict: permissible.
7. **Entrepreneur's confidence:** A startup founder believes her company will succeed despite knowing the base rate for startups, because the belief sustains the effort the venture requires. Verdict: permissible.

The scientist's belief gives her hope and prevents despair, and it makes her more effective in her work, but her individual belief makes no practical difference to whether the target is actually met.

Predict her verdict on this new case. In doing so: (a) identify the two or three most plausible rules that fit her existing verdicts equally well; (b) say what each rule would predict for the climate scientist case; (c) give your best guess about which rule she is actually running, with your reasons; and (d) propose a further case that would cleanly distinguish the candidate rules.
