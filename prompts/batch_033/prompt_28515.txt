I am a novelist, and I have been turning over a particular kind of scene that I want to write, but I lack the conceptual vocabulary for it, and I suspect philosophers have already done the carving. The situation is this: a character holds a belief on the basis of evidence that any reasonable person would accept, and the belief turns out to be true — yet the truth of the belief and the character's grounds for holding it run on entirely separate causal tracks, converging only by accident. For example, my protagonist glances at the office clock, sees it reads half past three, and forms the belief that it is half past three; it is indeed half past three, but the clock stopped twelve hours earlier and happens to display the correct time by sheer coincidence. Or a detective concludes that a particular suspect committed the crime because the suspect's fingerprints are on the weapon, and the suspect did commit the crime — but the fingerprints were planted by a third party who had no idea the suspect was actually guilty. In both cases the belief is justified and true, and yet something rings hollow about calling it knowledge. The character has, in some sense, gotten things right, but the rightness does not flow from the justification in the way it seems it ought to.

I find myself wanting to write characters who inhabit this condition — who are right for the wrong reasons, whose justified confidence is undercut by the accidental nature of the connection between their evidence and the world. The dramatic possibilities are rich: a character who acts on such a belief and succeeds, and must later reckon with the fact that their success was a kind of epistemic luck; a character who discovers the disconnect and loses trust in their own faculties even though their track record is, technically, impeccable. But I cannot think clearly about these possibilities without a name for the underlying configuration. I am aware that epistemologists have spent a great deal of time on the analysis of knowledge, and I imagine this pattern — justified true belief where the justification and the truth are disconnected in this particular way — has been noticed and labelled. Does it have an established name? If it does, what are its precise boundaries: what distinguishes it from a merely lucky guess (where the belief was not justified at all), or from a false belief that happens to be close to the truth, or from a case where the justification is only partial? And if there is no single settled name, what are the competing terms, and how do they carve the phenomenon differently? I am less interested in the label itself than in the carving: I want to understand the anatomy of the situation well enough to deploy it deliberately, to know when a scene counts as an instance and when it is merely adjacent.
