Two members of a working group on fair machine learning have been arguing past each other for a year. One holds that a risk score used for high-stakes decisions is fair just in case it is calibrated within each group: at any given score, the observed outcome rate is the same regardless of group membership, so the score means the same thing for everyone and like cases receive like treatment. The other holds that calibration is not enough, because a perfectly calibrated score can still impose systematically different error burdens across groups; on this view, fairness also requires that false-positive and false-negative rates be roughly equal across groups. Both accept the same dataset, the same evaluation protocol, and the same running example (pretrial risk assessment), and neither is a skeptic about using risk scores at all.

Draft the joint statement this adversarial collaboration should produce. It should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including the precise mathematical tradeoff they both accept — that an imperfect score cannot in general be both calibrated within groups and have equal false-positive and false-negative rates across groups when base rates differ; (2) a disagreements table stating the residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position, not a caricature of it; and (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — with, for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of their dispute is verbal or turns on a value judgment rather than on what the mathematics implies, say which part and why.
