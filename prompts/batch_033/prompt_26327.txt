So in this behavioral econ book I found at the library there's a chapter on something called the dollar auction, and the author uses it to explain why people get stuck in bad situations. The way they summarize it is basically:

"The dollar auction is a game where a dollar bill is sold to the highest bidder, but with a twist: the second-highest bidder also has to pay their bid and gets nothing. What happens is that people start bidding, and once two people are in, neither wants to be the loser who pays for nothing, so they keep outbidding each other way past a dollar. This is a perfect demonstration of the sunk cost fallacy — people irrationally throw good money after bad because they can't stand to walk away from what they've already put in. It explains everything from why the US stayed in Vietnam for so long to why you keep arguing with your brother even when you know you're wrong. The lesson is that if people were rational they'd never get trapped like this, so the dollar auction reveals a basic flaw in human psychology."
