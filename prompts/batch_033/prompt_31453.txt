Something I keep tripping over, and I can't tell if it's a real distinction or just a way of talking. If my neighbor promises to water my plants while I'm away and doesn't, and they die, it feels right to say her not watering them killed them. But if you or I also didn't water my plants that week, nobody would say we killed them. Same absence, different verdict. Or: a bridge collapses because a bolt was never replaced; the missing bolt caused the collapse. But a million other bolts weren't there either and nobody blames them. So it seems like only some "nothings" do causal work, and which ones depends on what someone was supposed to do or what normally happens. Is there a name for this — for treating an absence or a failure-to-act as a cause — and more importantly, what's the best way of drawing the line between the absences that count and the ones that don't? I don't just want a label; I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, what the nearby ideas are and how it differs from them, and where it gets weird at the edges.
