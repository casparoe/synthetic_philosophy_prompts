I am a doctoral student in condensed matter theory, and while I am comfortable with the formalism of many-body quantum systems — Hilbert spaces, tensor product structure, entanglement, quasiparticles, effective field theories, the renormalisation group, phase transitions and order parameters — I have no real background in philosophy of mind. A colleague in philosophy has been pressing me on panpsychism, which, as I understand it, is the view that microphysical entities have some form of experience or proto-experiential property, and that these micro-level properties constitute or ground the unified consciousness of larger systems such as human beings. The difficulty my colleague keeps returning to is the combination problem: how a multiplicity of micro-level subjects or experiential properties could compose into a single unified macro-level subject rather than merely a collection. I would like you to restate this position, and especially the combination problem, in the vocabulary I actually work in, as faithfully as you can. Concretely, I want to know what in my formalism could correspond to the micro-level subjects, what could correspond to their composition, and whether the appearance of one macroscopic conscious subject is best treated as a collective mode, an order parameter, an effective degree of freedom, or something else entirely. Where the translation would have to be forced, I want that stated explicitly rather than silently smoothed over.

Then provide the residue report. I want to know which parts of the panpsychist position translate cleanly into the language of many-body physics, which translate only under distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I suspect the first-person character of experience, the unity of the subject, and the privacy of phenomenal states may simply have no place in a formalism built for third-person structure, dynamics and correlations, and I want to know whether that residue indicates that my vocabulary is missing something important, or that the panpsychist's notion of combination was less precise than it appeared. Please write the answer in British English.
