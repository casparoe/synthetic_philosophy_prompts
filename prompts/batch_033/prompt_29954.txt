It seems to me that the problem of dirty hands in politics dissolves under scrutiny. The standard picture — a leader must authorize something morally repellent for the greater good, and though the act was the right one, the leader's hands are now dirty and ought to feel guilt — appears straightforwardly incoherent. Either the act was right, all things considered, and there is no residual moral claim against it; or there is a residual moral claim, which means the act was not right all things considered and the leader should have done something else. Calling this a "problem" seems to dignify a failure to think through one's own commitments: a consequentialist should feel no guilt, a deontologist should not do the deed, and someone who feels torn is simply confused about which theory they hold. What, if anything, is wrong with this argument?
