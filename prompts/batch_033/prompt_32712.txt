For a political philosophy workshop, I have been asked to write out my own view on distributive justice as a numbered list so the other participants can probe it. I would like an audit of whether the set hangs together before I circulate it. Here is the list.

(1) Inequalities are unjust exactly when they trace to differences in people's unchosen circumstances, and not when they trace to their voluntary choices.
(2) The brute-luck/option-luck distinction is the right way to draw this line: bad brute luck calls for compensation, and the consequences of option luck, good or bad, should be allowed to stand.
(3) A person born with a serious disability, or with markedly below-average earning ability, is owed compensation; these are paradigm cases of bad brute luck.
(4) A person with expensive tastes, who needs more resources than others to reach the same level of welfare, has no claim to extra resources on that account.
(5) A person who becomes disabled through a risky activity she chose with informed consent has no claim to compensation for that disability.
(6) The fact that a person's choices are ultimately caused by her genes and upbringing does not by itself turn the consequences of those choices into brute luck; holding people to their informed choices is needed if we are to treat them as responsible for their own lives.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which are actually in tension, which only appear to be in tension, and which single revision would do most to make the set consistent. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what would be lost. Please answer in British English.
