A philosophy reading group has spent a term with a committed Camusian absurdist and a committed proponent of a contemporary fitting-fulfillment account of meaning in life (the family of views associated with Susan Wolf), and the two have repeatedly argued past each other. Draft the joint statement of the adversarial collaboration they should have produced. Open with a numbered agreed-claims section: the claims both can sign about what the problem of meaning is, what the datum of the universe's indifference does and does not settle, and what a meaningful life would have to involve. Then give a disagreements table: for each live disagreement, state the absurdist position and the fitting-fulfillment position in wording each side would accept for its own view, and name the crux — the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction about that case in advance. Make them face the cases that cut against each side's self-understanding: Sisyphus as a candidate for a meaningful life, and a life of successful fitting fulfillment whose projects nonetheless feel arbitrary whenever the question 'why?' is pressed. Throughout, separate the disputes that are genuine from those that are merely verbal, such as whether 'absurd' names a feature of the world or a response to it.
