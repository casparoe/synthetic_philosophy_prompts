So I've been trying to sort out my views on standing to blame and hypocrisy and I keep going in circles. I thought I'd write down where I've landed and see if someone can tell me whether it actually hangs together or if I'm just papering over cracks. Here's roughly what I think:

(1) Standing to blame is a real normative thing — it's not just that hypocritical blame is rude or annoying, there's something genuinely wrong with it that isn't about the content of the blame being false.

(2) What undermines standing is fundamentally about your relationship to the norm you're invoking. You lack standing when you're not genuinely committed to the norm — when you treat it as something that applies to others but not to you.

(3) Someone who has genuinely reformed can have standing to blame others for the same kind of wrong they once committed. If you've really changed, your past doesn't permanently disqualify you.

(4) Victims of a wrong always have standing to blame the perpetrator, even if the victim has themselves committed the same kind of wrong and hasn't done any reforming. If someone steals from a thief, the thief still gets to blame them for the theft — that just seems obvious.

(5) Meddlesomeness is a separate thing from standing. Blaming someone for something that's none of your business is a different kind of problem than hypocritical blame, and I don't think my account of standing needs to capture it.

(6) Third parties — bystanders with no personal stake — can have standing to blame, provided they're genuinely committed to the norm. You don't need to be the victim to call something out.

I'm worried that (2) and (4) might be in real tension, but I'm not sure if I'm just confused about what "commitment to the norm" requires. And I have a nagging feeling that (3) and (6) might be doing more work than I think they are. Can you go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix would be? I'd rather you rank the problems by severity than give me a list where everything looks equally bad — I want to know where the real instability is.
