ok so i've been reading this debate between illusionists and realists about phenomenal consciousness and honestly it feels like they've been talking past each other for years. specifically on the meta-problem — chalmers' question about why we think there's a hard problem in the first place. the illusionist (frankish, dennett etc) says: give me a full explanation of why humans have the intuition that consciousness is weird and mysterious, and you've explained everything — the hard problem was just a cognitive illusion. the realist (chalmers, goff etc) says: no, the fact that we have that intuition is itself evidence that there's something real and non-physical we're picking up on, and explaining the intuition doesn't make the thing go away.

i want you to draft the document these two camps could co-sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. here's what i want in it:

1. a numbered list of agreed claims. stuff both sides accept about the meta-problem itself, about what a solution would even look like, about the empirical facts of introspection and cognitive architecture that aren't in dispute. don't make these vacuous — they should be substantive enough that it's nontrivial they both agree.

2. a disagreements table. for each point of disagreement, give the precise statement each side would endorse, phrased so that side would say "yes, that's exactly what i think." no strawmen.

3. the cruxes. these are the sub-questions where if you settled them, at least one side would change its mind. for each crux, describe a test, argument, or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts.

if you think some part of this debate is purely verbal — that they're using different words and there's no real disagreement underneath — flag it and explain why.
