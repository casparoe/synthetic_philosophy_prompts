I'm teaching a grad seminar on Bayesian epistemology and I want to make a handout that helps students figure out where they actually stand on Dutch book arguments — like, a decision tree or flowchart. The idea is it takes them from a starting point like "I accept that degrees of belief exist and can be measured (roughly) by betting dispositions" and walks them through branching questions till they land at a named position on what Dutch books actually show.

I want the tree to branch on the key fault lines. Here are the ones I care about:

- Whether the agent can "see the whole book" — i.e., does the Dutch book still have force if the agent knows the bookie's full set of offered bets in advance?
- Whether synchronic Dutch books (all bets offered at once) and diachronic ones (bets offered over time, exploiting how you update) should get seperate treatment, or whether they stand or fall together.
- Whether Dutch book vulnerability shows pragmatic irrationality, epistemic irrationality, both, or neither.
- Whether the belief–betting connection is tight enough for the argument to get off the ground in the first place, or whether that link is where the whole thing unravels.

Each leaf should carry a name for the position (like "standard Bayesian," "depragmatized accuracy-first," "diachronic-only skeptic," "behaviorist rejection," whatever fits) plus one sentence on the most uncomfortable thing that position has to swallow. Flag any leaves that are logically possible but nobody really defends, and say why — incoherent? too costly? just underexplored?

Build the tree from the logical space first, not from a list of positions. If there are empty regions, note them and diagnose them. Also make sure the tree separates (or at least explicitly flags) the distinction between people who think Dutch books are a good argument for probabilism but not for conditionalization, and people who think they work equally well for both — that's a split my students always miss.

Phrase every branching question so someone can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to.

Oh, and at the very end, give me a one-sentence summary of what the tree as a whole reveals about the debate, as the last line.
