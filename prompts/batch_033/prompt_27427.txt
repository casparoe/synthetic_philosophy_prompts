I follow several rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums where political philosophy comes up, and in a recent thread about whether religious citizens should translate their views into "public reasons" when advocating for policy, someone dismissed an interlocutor with "keep your religion out of politics" and moved on. The person they were replying to wasn't citing scripture or claiming divine authority — they were making a natural-law argument about human flourishing that happened to align with their Catholic commitments.

This slogan gets thrown around a lot, and I suspect it's doing different work depending on who's wielding it. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that "keep your religion out of politics" could be expressing, from the least to the most demanding? For each reading: is it defensible, is it trivial or substantive, and — crucially — would that reading actually apply to someone making a natural-law argument that doesn't appeal to revelation? I want to know which reading the slogan needs to carry for it to function as a reply in that context, and whether that reading survives scrutiny.
