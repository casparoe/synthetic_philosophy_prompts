im trying to sort out the everettian probability literature and i keep getting stuck because everyone seems to be playing a different game. before i read another paper i want the desiderata: what would count as a fully adequate solution to the born rule problem in many-worlds?

be specific. i want the list of requirements and the case behind each — not just "derive the born rule" but the deeper constraints. for instance: an account has to say what probability *means* when all branches with nonzero amplitude are realized (the incoherence problem). it has to avoid just postulating the born rule as an extra axiom — but what counts as "just postulating" vs. legitimately deriving? it has to not sneak in a preferred basis, and it presumably has to be compatible with decoherence picking out the basis without itself presupposing the born rule. it has to explain why branch count is the wrong measure (if you just count branches you get probabilities proportional to something other than |ψ|²). 

then theres the decision-theoretic stuff: deutsch and wallace try to derive the born rule from rational choice principles, but critics say its circular — that they build squared-amplitude caring into the axioms. is "dont be circular" a genuine desideratum or is that one camp's framing that begs the question in the other direction? also: does a solution need to vindicate the idea that a rational agent *should* bet according to the born rule, or is it enough to show that agents *would* under certain decision-theoretic constraints? and does it need to work for a single measurement or only in the infinite limit?

flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferred by one camp. and crucially: is there any subset of these that are jointly unsatisfiable? if so, which ones, and would that mean the whole project is doomed or just that we need to give up something people thought was mandatory?
