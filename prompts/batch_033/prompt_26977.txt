Not sure if this has a name. I half-remember reading about a kind of wasp that paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the entrance of its burrow, leaves it there while it goes inside to "check" the burrow, comes back out, and then drags it in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, it comes out, drags the cricket back to the entrance, and goes inside to check again — over and over, dozens of times. It never just drags the cricket straight in. The behavior looks deliberate, like it's actually inspecting the burrow, but it seems to be a fixed routine that just happens to work in the normal case. I keep seeing the same shape in software: a system gives sensible behavior until one assumption breaks, and then it repeats a step or loops, and you realize there was never any real checking going on, just a script triggered by cues. Is there a standard term for that property — looking purposeful or intelligent while being mechanical underneath? And is the line between that and genuine flexibility a real line, or does a system stop counting as rigid once its script has enough branches and triggers? If there's an established concept, I'd want to know what it does and doesn't cover, not just the label.
