I'm in a bit of a wierd decision paralysis situation and I'm hoping someone here can help me think through it — I've been lurking on LessWrong and related forums for years and this seems like exactly the kind of thing the crowd here would have good intuitions about.

I've been working as a software engineer at a mid-sized tech company for about four years now. The pay is good, the work is fine but not thrilling, and I've been thinking about what to do next. The thing is, every time I compare two options, one seems clearly better than the other, but when I look at the whole chain of comparisons I end up somewhere I don't want to be.

Here's what I mean. I compare my current job (A) to a slightly higher-paying but more stressful job at a startup (B), and B seems better — more money, more impact, more learning. Then I compare B to a job at a FAANG company (C) and C seems better than B — even more money, more prestige, better colleagues. Then I compare C to going into quantitative finance (D) and D seems better than C — even more money, and the work seems more intellectually interesting than yet another CRUD app. But when I look at D on its own, I kind of hate everything about it. The culture, the hours, the feeling that I'd be wasting my life making rich people richer. I don't want to end up in quantitative finance. But at each step of the chain, the next option seemed like a genuine improvement over the previous one.

This feels like a sorites-like problem, but with preferences rather than heap boundaries. Is the issue just that I'm not being careful enough about my comparisons — maybe I'm overweighting one dimension at each step and losing track of the others? Or is there something deeper going on about whether "better than" is actually transitive when it comes to complex life choices with multiple incommensurable dimensions? And more practically — how do I figure out what I actually want when pairwise comparisons keep leading me astray? I've tried doing the whole "shut up and multiply" thing but it's not obvious how to assign weights to the different dimensions in a way that doesn't just reproduce the same problem at the weighting stage.
