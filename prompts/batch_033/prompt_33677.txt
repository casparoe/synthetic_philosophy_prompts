I've mostly come around to a pragmatic view of slurs, and I want you to stress-test it: tell me what would count against it.

The position: a slur and its neutral counterpart are truth-conditionally equivalent, or close enough, and the derogatory part is not part of what the word literally says or asserts. It's a conventional implicature, a presupposition, or a use-condition — I'm honestly not settled on which flavor, but I'm settled that it's not ordinary semantic content. So if you knew only the truth-conditional meaning of a slur, you wouldn't yet know it's a slur; the offensiveness is a separate conventional layer that attaches to the word, not an occasional conversational inference, and I'm not trying to wave it away as merely subjective.

I want the full map of its exposure, not just the stock objections. Which data should move me, sorted from decisive to merely awkward? For instance: projection behavior — derogation surviving negation, questions, conditionals, attitude verbs — cuts both ways, since presuppositions and conventional implicatures project, but some people argue that's exactly what marks the content as semantic-but-not-at-issue. Then there are the non-derogatory uses: reclamation, in-group use, quotation, fiction, metalinguistic uses, and the question whether the derogation can be canceled or neutralized, which would be bad for a conventional implicature story since implicatures of the but sort are famously hard to cancel. There's also the fact that some slurs don't seem to have a clean neutral counterpart, or have extensions that drift from the neutral term. Which of these are real problems for the view, and which would a committed defender dismiss as theory-internal or as problems for everyone?

I'd personaly find it most persuasive if there are cases where the truth conditions and the derogation come apart in a way the view can't re-describe — either the slur applies to a different group than the neutral term while the offense stays, or a fully non-derogatory use keeps all the asserted content. And I want an honest verdict on whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's a serious problem for a view of this kind.

Also: if you think the request rests on a confused premise — like the semantics/pragmatics distinction is doing to much work here, or "truth-conditionally equivalent" isn't well-defined for these terms, or the position collapses into a rival once you pick a flavor — say so plainly instead of just answering on my terms.
