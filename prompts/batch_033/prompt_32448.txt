I've talked myself into a pretty pessimistic view about all the low-impact AI and side-effect-avoidance work, and I want to know how much of it I should trust. The view is this: you cannot give a defintion of what counts as a side effect for an agent without already having a full specification of what we value. A side effect only means something relative to an intended goal plus a background of things we care about not disturbing, so any attempt to penalize side effects either smuggles in a value function through the back door, or falls back on something generic like don't change the world much — which either fails to rule out relevant disruptions or rules out doing the assigned task at all. From there I conclude that impact measurs are mostly a way of pretending we can specify harm-avoidance without specifying the good.

I got here after a long argument with a friend who works on alignment, and I keep hearing that I'm being unfair: people have apparently built impact measures that use an inaction baseline or a generic auxiliary reward, and shown that agents trained with them avoid side effects in gridworlds and other toy environments without being told what to preserve. I'm open to being wrong, but I don't have a clear picture of what would show that I am. What would count against my view? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be, including things that have arguably already been demonstrated and things that are still open. What would a committed defender of my view concede counts against it — not just the objections the impact-measure people already make, but the ones a fair-minded person on my side would admit are awkward? And be honest about whether I've stated the position in a way that's insulated from any possible counterexample; if I have, is that a problem for a view like this, or is it fine because the claim is conceptual rather than empirical? For what it's worth, I'd be most persuaded by a concrete case where an agent avoids a side effect that clearly wasn't just the reward function doing the work.
