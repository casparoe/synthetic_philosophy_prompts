Ok so I'm putting together a talk on consciousness and a friend keeps telling me my position doesn't hang together, but he won't say exactly where the problem is, so I want an audit before I present. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(i) I'm a physicalist. No extra ingredients over and above the physical.

(ii) I'm an illusionist about qualia: I dont think there are private, ineffable, intrinsic feels, and I suspect the whole notion is a bit confused.

(iii) But I also don't want to deny the data. It does seem to me that there's something it's like to be me, and I think a theory that just calls that an illusion without explaining why the illusion is so compelling hasn't actually finished the job.

(iv) I think the user-interface story is basically right: conscious experience is a simplified, schematic model the brain builds of its own activity, like a desktop with icons. The icons don't resemble the circuitry, but the interface is what lets the system coordinate itself.

(v) And I'm not smuggling in a homunculus. There's no little person in the head watching the screen; the user is just more of the same system, and the interface isn't presented to anyone over and above the system itself.

(vi) I also think introspection is systematically wrong about the richness and detail of experience — change blindness and so on. We're convinced we have a full detailed scene in front of us when we don't. So I treat introspective reports as fallible data, not as an infallible window.

(vii) Finally, I think split-brain cases are just a dramatic version of what's going on in everyone: the self is a useful story, and the left hemisphere's confabulations are the rule, not the exception.

So which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad is each? Please rank the real tensions by severity and say what would have to give in each case, and whether there's one revision that would clean up several problems at once. Don't just list every pair — I want to know where the load-bearing problem is.
