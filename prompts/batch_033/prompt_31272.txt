I serve on my university's Faculty Senate Committee on Academic Policy. We are revising our academic integrity code, and a central dispute has emerged: whether student work substantially generated by a large language model should be classified as "unauthorized collaboration" (treating the LLM as a partner in wrongdoing, which carries a presumption of suspension) or as a distinct, lesser offense akin to unauthorized use of a tool. The debate has turned philosophical. The collaboration camp argues that LLMs are profitably treated as intentional systems in Dennett's sense: we anticipate and make sense of their outputs by attributing something like beliefs, communicative intentions, and rational agency to them, and the student's interaction with the system is genuinely dialogical—so treating the LLM as a mere tool misdescribes what the student is actually doing. The other side replies that the intentional stance is a predictive heuristic, not a basis for assigning normative responsibility; calling an LLM a collaborator, they argue, conflates a useful interpretive posture with a claim about the system's actual moral or agential status, and our policy should reflect that distinction.

Please draft a short policy memo for the committee, recommending a concrete classification and explaining the reasoning. The memo should engage the strongest considerations on both sides, take the Dennettian position seriously rather than dismissing it, and offer a recommendation the full Faculty Senate can act on.
