For a reading group on the evolution of cooperation I've been asked to write up my current views so the others can poke holes. I'd like a consistancy audit before I present. Here's where I've landed:

(1) In a one-shot prisoner's dilemma between rational, self-interested strangers with common knowledge of the setup, defection is the uniquely rational move. I think any "rationality" that recommends cooperation there is just relabeling morality.

(2) In a finitely repeated PD with a known number of rounds and common knowledge of rationality, the backward induction argument goes through, and the rational play is to defect every round. I've read the "backward induction is self-undermining" stuff and its never moved me.

(3) In an indefinitely repeated PD (discounted, no known last round), cooperation is a rational equilibrium outcome, and strategies like tit-for-tat show how cooperation could get off the ground among self-interested agents without a central authority. I think this is basically the right story about how cooperation evolves.

(4) When I look at cooperation in nature, I think it's mostly kin selection and direct reciprocity, plus some indirect reciprocity. Group selection is at best a minor factor and I'm suspicious of it; I default to explaining apparent group-level cooperation without it.

(5) Human moral norms — fairness, honesty, promise-keeping, third-party punishment — are fundamentally devices for solving social dilemmas among unrelated individuals. That's what they're for.

(6) We have genuine obligations of fairness and honesty even in anonymous one-shot interactions with no chance of future contact, reputation, or punishment. Cheating a stranger in a one-shot PD and getting away clean is wrong, full stop. I also lean realist about this: it's a fact, not just a useful fiction or a glitch in our psychology.

If some pair only looks like a tension, say why it dissolves. Lead with your bottom line — which commitment has to go, or which pair is worst — then give the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate my list back at me.
