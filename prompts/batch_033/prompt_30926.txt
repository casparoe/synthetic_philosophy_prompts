I've been going back and forth with a friend about van Fraassen's Reflection principle and I think one of us has to be missunderstanding it. The way I understand it, the principle says a rational person's current credence in a proposition should equal their expected future credence in that proposition — so if I'm at 0.4 now, my future self should be at 0.4 on average. But that seems to make learning impossible. Suppose I'm about to read a careful new study on whether some drug actually works, and right now I'm at 0.4 that it works. I don't know what the study will say, but I do know it's a good study, so I expect that after reading it my credence will have moved — maybe up to 0.8, maybe down to 0.1. If I didn't expect my credence to move at all, reading the study would be pointless. But then my expected future credence can't equal 0.4: the whole point is to end up somewhere else, whichever way the evidence points.

So isn't Reflection either trivially false for anyone who learns from evidence, or else a demand that you never anticipate learning anything? Or am I getting the notion of expectation wrong here, or misstating the principle somehow?
