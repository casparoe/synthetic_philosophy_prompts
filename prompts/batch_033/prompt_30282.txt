For a feature I'm writing on thought experiments that have outlived their usefulness, I've been reading through the literature on Nozick's experience machine. What strikes me is that the debate hasn't moved in any obvious way since the 1970s. The basic structure is always the same: Nozick says most people wouldn't plug in, and that this refutes hedonism about well-being. Hedonists reply that our reluctance to plug in reflects status quo bias, or a confusion between the imagined choice and the real one, or that we're tracking something other than well-being when we recoil. Then the anti-hedonist says that even after these replies are pointed out, the intuition persists. And around it goes.

I'm less interested in who's right than in why the debate has this shape. It looks to me like a case where the two sides share the same empirical prediction — most people, on first pass, say they wouldn't plug in — and the same normative starting point, that we should care about what makes a life go well, and yet they reach opposite conclusions. So I want a diagnosis: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it a disagreement about the evidential weight of a thought-experiment intuition, and if so, what would settle that? Is it a verbal dispute about the extension of the term "well-being" that only looks substantive because the word carries normative weight? Is there a hidden empirical claim about what people would do under ideal reflection? Or is it something else — two research programs with different methodological commitments that can't be adjudicated from neutral ground? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a new experiment, a conceptual distinction, a different question, or an honest admission that the thought experiment can't do the work asked of it?

I should add: if the premise of my question is confused — if the debate has in fact moved in ways I'm missing, or if asking for a "diagnosis" of a philosophical disagreement in these terms is itself a mistake — tell me that plainly. I'd rather have the question reframed than answered on bad terms.
