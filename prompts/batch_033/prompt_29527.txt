I'm a PhD student working on philosophical logic, and I've been defending a broadly dialetheist position in seminars. I want to stress-test it rather than keep rehearsing the familiar moves. The position, as I hold it: there are some true contradictions — the semantic paradoxes and the set-theoretic paradoxes are best read that way — and the correct logic for theorizing about them is paraconsistent, rejecting explosion so that a contradiction does not entail everything. I also accept the standard accompaniments: a transparent truth predicate (A and T〈A〉 intersubstitutable in all non-opaque contexts), a weakened conditional to avoid Curry-style triviality, some form of classical recapture for consistent domains, and a distinction between mere falsity and being just false. I do not extend dialetheism to ordinary empirical discourse; the gluts live at the limits of thought, not in everyday matters.

What I'm after is a map of the position's exposure. What would have to be true — which formal results, semantic phenomena, conceptual objections, or failures of application — for this package to be wrong, or not worth its costs? I want defeaters ranked by how decisive they would be if established, with the mechanism of each spelled out: exactly how it would bear on the view, not just a label. For each, give the strongest available dialetheist reply, and — importantly — say whether dialetheists themselves would concede that this counts against them, or whether they treat it as a bullet to bite, a confusion, or a problem for everyone. I also want an honest assessment of whether the position, with its weakened conditional, its just-false machinery, and its recapture clauses, has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind or an appropriate feature of a theory about the limits of thought. I find the revenge-paradox and collapse worries more persuasive than the bare charge that paraconsistent negation is not real negation, but rank the defeaters by what the view should fear, not by my priors.

Please lead with the structured analysis, then add whatever prose is needed to defend the rankings.

The structured part should be a table with these columns:

- `defeater`: a short name for the potential counterconsideration.
- `type`: formal result / semantic phenomenon / conceptual objection / application failure / burden-of-proof consideration.
- `what_would_have_to_be_the_case`: the condition that, if established, would count against the position.
- `severity_if_established`: fatal / severe / moderate / awkward.
- `best_dialetheist_reply`: the strongest response available to a defender.
- `concession_status`: conceded / contested / dismissed — whether dialetheists themselves grant that this counts against them.
- `residual_exposure`: high / medium / low, after the reply.

After the table, include a JSON summary with these fields:

- `overall_verdict`: whether the position is genuinely exposed, well insulated, or over-insulated.
- `insulation_problem`: whether the qualifications amount to unfalsifiability, and whether that is a problem.
- `most_decisive_defeater`: the single consideration most likely to move a committed dialetheist, and why.
- `cheapest_revision`: the least costly change to the package if one were forced to revise.

If you think some alleged defeaters do not belong on the table because they rest on a misunderstanding, say so briefly and exclude them rather than padding the table.
