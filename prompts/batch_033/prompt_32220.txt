I spent three decades in theoretical chemistry before retiring, and a habit I cannot shake is the conviction that one should never commit to a model until one has first specified what the model is supposed to do. In recent months I have been reading philosophy of mind, and I keep returning to Daniel Dennett's essay "The Self as a Center of Narrative Gravity." The core analogy — that the self is an abstractum, like a center of gravity, which is no less real for being a theoretical posit rather than a physical entity — is arresting. But I find that the literature around it is mostly either defending the view or attacking it, and I would like to step back and ask the prior question systematically.

I want the desiderata for a theory of the self. Not a survey of theories, and not a defense of any one position — I want the specification. What must an adequate account of the self explain, accommodate, or respect? Which features of ordinary self-experience are non-negotiable data that any theory must capture, and which might we reasonably dismiss as illusions or byproducts of other cognitive processes? Among the candidates I have in mind: the phenomenological unity of experience at a moment, the sense of being the same self across time, the apparent role of the self in initiating voluntary action, the distinction between self and other, the fact that selves seem to come into and go out of existence (birth, death, perhaps severe dementia), the relationship between the self and the body, and the relationship between the self and the stories we tell about ourselves. I would also like to know which of these criteria are genuinely mandatory and which are merely preferences of a particular camp — for instance, must a theory of the self be consistent with the deliverances of contemporary neuroscience, or with the idea that we are morally responsible for our actions, or with the possibility that the self is in some sense constructed rather than discovered? Finally, I would appreciate a brief assessment of whether Dennett's narrative-gravity picture meets the criteria you lay out, and where it falls short if it does.
