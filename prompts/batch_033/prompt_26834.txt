I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and for the past year I have been in an off-and-on conversation with a philosopher of science whose specialty is the boundary between what is out there in the world and what human practices put there. Over that time she has given me a series of verdicts about particular categories, and she seems to be running a rule, but I have never managed to extract it. She has said that money is socially constructed; that race and gender are socially constructed; that biological sex is not; that species are not, though she concedes that biologists choose among species concepts; that schizophrenia, as a psychiatric diagnostic category, is socially constructed even though the suffering it labels is real; that atoms are not; and that the legal category of adulthood is socially constructed even though puberty is not.

I now have to write a piece pegged to the International Astronomical Union's 2006 vote that demoted Pluto, and before I call her I want to predict whether she would say that planet — the astronomical category as redefined by that vote — is socially constructed. I care less about the one-word answer than about the rule behind it. Please lay out the two or three most plausible rules that fit all of her earlier verdicts, say what each predicts about planet, and give me your best guess about which rule she is actually running and why. Then name one further case, not planet, that would cleanly separate the candidate rules, and say what her answer to that further case would tell us.
