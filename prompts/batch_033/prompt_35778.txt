My friend and I spent a whole evening arguing about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — the one that says you can't be ultimately responsible for anything you do, because to be responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, and to be responsible for the way you are you'd have to be responsible for whatever made you that way, and so on back forever. She kept saying she accepted the argument, but she also kept saying she can still blame people. So I started throwing cases at her to see what rule she was actually running. Her verdicts, as best I can reconstruct them:

- The ordinary adult who shoplifts and isn't particularly conflicted about it: blameworthy.
- The adult who was brainwashed as a child by a cult into wanting to steal, hates that he wants to, but still steals: not blameworthy.
- The adult who was never brainwashed but spent years deliberately training himself to enjoy stealing — reading about it, practicing, desensitizing himself — and now steals: blameworthy.
- The adult whose brain chemistry was changed by a car accident, giving him a strong urge to steal, who hates the urge but gives in: not blameworthy.

I couldn't get her to commit on the case I actually care about: the adult who was brainwashed as a child into wanting to steal, then as an adult learns about the brainwashing, reflects, and wholeheartedly endorses the desire anyway, and steals. She said she needed to think about it.

What should I expect her to say about that last case if she's being consistant with the others? Which rule or rules fit her verdicts so far, and which ones does the new case actually decide between? And is accepting the Basic Argument really consistent with any of the rules that fit her verdicts, or is she quietly running the exact kind of tracing view the argument is meant to undermine? What further case would settle which rule she's actually running?
