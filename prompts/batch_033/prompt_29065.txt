You are advising a philosopher with three years and a background in philosophy of language and formal semantics who wants to work on Frege's puzzle about identity statements and to reorder the surrounding debate rather than add one more solution. The core datum is this: the sentence that Hesperus is Hesperus and the sentence that Hesperus is Phosphorus are both true, and on a referentialist picture they express the same singular proposition, yet only the second is informative. Among the candidate cruxes — whether the datum to be explained is fundamentally semantic or epistemological; how finely propositions must be individuated to account for it; what modes of presentation are and how they are individuated; and whether substitution failures in belief reports are semantic or pragmatic — which is most upstream, in the sense that the most other questions in the debate depend on how it is settled? Rank two or three candidates, and for each say what it would constrain downstream, how tractable it is with current tools, whether it is neglected relative to its importance, and what evidence over the next few years would move it up or down. Also name one familiar question in this area that you would deliberately deprioritize because settling it would not change much else. Throughout, indicate how confident you are in each claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
