Write the opening remarks for a workshop at an AI lab, delivered by the head of the lab's control team. The lab is preparing to deploy a frontier model at scale, and every layer of its safety regime assumes the model may be misaligned: output monitoring built around the hypothesis of strategic deception, sandboxing and tripwires, staged opportunities to misbehave, and audits designed on the premise that the model is an adversary biding its time. Roughly half the audience are control researchers who will catch every term of art; the other half are newly hired engineers and a couple of visiting policymakers who will not. The speech should make the distinction between controlling a system and aligning it do real structural work rather than serving as decoration: it should explain why the lab treats a system that has never demonstrably done anything wrong as a suspect, without reassuring the audience that the model is safe, without speaking of it as a prisoner or an enemy, and without settling the question of whether it is in fact aligned. By the end, the audience should understand why a team that built a system is deliberately designing against it, and why that defensive posture is itself an intervention in the system's behavior rather than a neutral observation of it.
