I am a retired natural scientist (an experimental physicist by training) who has only recently begun to read political philosophy in earnest, and I find that I am still assembling my own views rather than defending a settled position. My recent reading has concentrated on the entitlement theory of justice in holdings, and I have tried to record the commitments I seem to have accumulated. It would be of real value to me to have an outside audit of whether they form a coherent package.

The commitments, as I would currently state them, are these. (1) A distribution of holdings is just if and only if it has arisen through just original acquisitions and just voluntary transfers, with rectification for past injustice. (2) The Wilt Chamberlain argument is decisive against any principle requiring a distribution to conform to a favoured pattern: voluntary transfers from a just starting point will upset any such pattern, and the resulting distribution cannot reasonably be called unjust. (3) Persons have strong ownership rights over their own bodies and labour, so that non-consensual taxation of earnings is at least prima facie difficult to justify. (4) The Lockean proviso is indispensable to any account of original acquisition, but as usually stated it is too vague to determine, in actual cases, which acquisitions are legitimate and which are not. (5) Because actual holdings descend from a long history of conquest, expropriation and broken agreements, no account of justice in holdings can be complete without a serious principle of rectification. (6) A just society must nevertheless guarantee every person a decent minimum of resources, even if that requires redistributing some of what others have legitimately acquired.

I do not yet see where the strain lies, and I am not attached in advance to any one of these claims. What I would like is a careful assessment of whether this set is jointly tenable: which commitments are in genuine tension, how severe each tension is, which apparent conflicts dissolve once the relevant notions are clarified, and what the least costly revision or combination of revisions would be. Please write your answer in British English.
