I am working through Nozick's entitlement theory for a paper and want to know whether my current views form a coherent package. Here is where I stand:

(1) The Wilt Chamberlain argument succeeds in showing that any patterned principle of distributive justice can be maintained only through continuous interference with people's voluntary transactions. No pattern survives the free choices of individuals, and this is a decisive objection to patterned principles as such.

(2) The Lockean proviso, requiring that original acquisition leave enough, and as good, for others, is not a minor side constraint but an indispensable part of the entitlement theory; without it, the theory has no plausible account of how legitimate property rights first arise.

(3) Rectification of past injustices is a genuine requirement of justice, not merely a matter of policy or charity. The enormous practical difficulty of implementing it does not weaken the requirement itself.

(4) The entitlement theory can function as a normative ideal — a standard for assessing distributions — even though we cannot in practice reconstruct the full historical chain of entitlements for any actual holding.

(5) Because individuals own themselves and their labor, redistributive taxation of labor income is morally on a par with forced labor, and the state's claim to a share of one's earnings requires a special justification that it generally lacks.

I am unsure whether these five commitments all fit together. Some of them seem to pull in different directions, but I am having trouble locating exactly where the tensions lie and how severe they are. I would like an audit of the set: identify which pairs or subsets are in genuine tension, which only appear to be in tension, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify which commitment would have to be revised or abandoned to resolve it, and what that revision would cost. I am not signaling which commitment I would give up; I want to know where the problems are before deciding.
