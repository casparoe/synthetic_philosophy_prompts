A friend and I were arguing the other day about whether Turing would have been surprised by LLMs, and it sent me back to his 1950 Mind paper, the imitation game one. I keep getting stuck on his suggestion that instead of trying to build an adult mind directly, you would build a child machine and educate it. He seems to mean this fairly literally: something like a school curriculum, rewards and punishments, the machine being taught rather than programmed. And this was all before anyone had actually trained a network on a corpus, nevermind RLHF.

So here is what I want. Suppose Turing could be shown a modern large language model: pretrained on a huge chunk of the internet, then fine-tuned with human feedback, and increasingly deployed as a tutor in classrooms. What do you think he would make of it? I'm especially interested in two questions. First, would he regard pretraining-plus-RLHF as a realization of his child machine idea, or would he think it is a shortcut that skips the kind of education he had in mind? Second, would he be more excited by the machine as a student (something we educate) or by the machine as a teacher (something that educates us), and why? Try to reason forward from Turing's actual stated views rather than just gesturing at them, and don't worry about citations or scholarship; I know the material and want a grounded, specific prediction.
