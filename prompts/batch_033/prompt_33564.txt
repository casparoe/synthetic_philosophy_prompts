I am a PhD student working on the metaphysics of interpretation, and I am preparing a two-week unit on authorial intention and textual meaning for an upper-level undergraduate aesthetics seminar. I want a decision tree that sorts students by their considered responses to cases rather than by the labels they already like; the students have read some of the classic papers but do not yet command the taxonomy, so every branch node must be answerable by someone who cannot name any position.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names. First lay out the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the named positions in that space. Where a permitted combination of answers has no named occupant, say so and briefly diagnose whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Each branch node should be a concrete question or a short invented case that forces the relevant choice without mentioning the position names — for example, a question about whether a newly discovered private diary changes what a poem meant before the diary was found, not a question about whether the student is an actual intentionalist. Each leaf should carry the position's name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

The tree must locate at least these positions, distinguishing variants where the questions force it: extreme actual intentionalism, on which the author's intended meaning is the meaning even when the text fails to realize it; moderate actual intentionalism, on which the intention must be successfully realized in the text; hypothetical intentionalism; conventionalist anti-intentionalism, with meaning fixed by the language at the time of composition; and value-maximizing theory. It should also locate at least one position on which a text's meaning can shift after publication as conventions or readerships change, and at least one reader-response or constructivist position; if those do not sit naturally in the same tree, say exactly where they part company with the others and give the branch that leads out.

The distinctions I most want the tree to capture are these four. First, whether interpretation targets what an author did in making an utterance or what the text itself means as a public object. Second, whether the relevant intention is the author's actual mental state, the intention a suitably informed audience would reasonably attribute, or no intention at all. Third, whether failed intentions count — a passage intended as irony that no competent reader takes as irony, or an intended ambiguity no one notices. Fourth, whether meaning can change after publication. Build the tree so that each distinction appears as a branch or as a deliberate, flagged refusal to branch, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate; I suspect hypothetical intentionalism and value-maximizing theory will collide somewhere, and I want the tree to say exactly where and why rather than paper over it.

Use these five cases at least once each in the branch questions, adapting them as needed: (i) a private diary entry whose author later publicly disavows it; (ii) a text produced by an algorithm that every competent reader finds as moving as an elegy; (iii) an author who says a passage was meant ironically though readers uniformly read it as sincere; (iv) a newly discovered letter revealing a private intention decades after the work's meaning was treated as settled; and (v) a word in a poem whose ordinary meaning has shifted since composition so that the line now invites a different reading. If any case cannot discriminate between two positions, say which and why.

Aim for a handout-sized tree of roughly seven to ten leaves and no more than four levels, with the first branch doing as much sorting work as possible. End with a short paragraph identifying the most tempting misclassification — the pattern of case answers that would send a student to the wrong leaf because two positions agree on four of the five cases — and explain how the wording of the relevant branch question should block it.
