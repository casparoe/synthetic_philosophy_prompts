I've been trying to work out what a thing's dispositions amount to, and I keep colliding with the obvious starting point. The natural idea is that a disposition is just a would-if claim: glass is fragile because it would shatter if struck, salt is soluble because it would dissolve if put in water, a poison is poisonous because it would kill if swallowed.

But the would-if claim and the disposition come apart in both directions. A glass wired to a device that would make it shatterproof the instant before anything struck it still seems fragile now, even though the claim that it would shatter if struck is false. A poison that is always neutralized by an antidote the moment it is swallowed is still poisonous, even though the claim that it would kill if swallowed is false. And an otherwise harmless powder that would kill only because someone would add a toxin to it upon ingestion satisfies the would-if claim without being poisonous. So the simple account fails in both directions, and I do not want a patch that just tacks on further clauses case by case.

Before I look at any actual theory, I want the full specification of what such a theory has to get right. Write the requirements list: the cases any adequate account of dispositions must classify correctly, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the results from the sciences it cannot contradict, with the reasoning behind each. I am after what dispositions really are, not merely a tidy way of talking. In particular, I want the list to take a position on whether an adequate account must handle dispositions that come in degrees, dispositions that are probabilistic rather than all-or-nothing, and the difference between a disposition a thing has in itself and one it has only in relation to its surroundings; include any of these only if it genuinely earns its place as a requirement. For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Then say which requirements pull against one another and how a theory is supposed to live with that, and which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidate theories. A brief scorecard of the main contenders against the list is welcome, but the list is what I want.
