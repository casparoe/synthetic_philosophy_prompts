Please argue that Kripke's skeptical solution to the rule-following paradox is question-begging in a specific way. The solution legitimizes ordinary meaning-attributions by appealing to the fact that speakers in a community agree in their responses to new cases: the community functions as the arbiter of what counts as going on in the same way. But the skeptic's original challenge was precisely that no fact determines what counts as going on in the same way, or as the same response to a new case. By grounding meaning in sameness of communal response, the skeptical solution presupposes the very kind of fact it has conceded does not exist. Make the strongest case for this objection, then state and answer the best reply available to a defender of the skeptical solution.

Structure your response in four named sections: (1) Setup — restate the paradox and the skeptical solution in your own terms (roughly 200 words); (2) The objection — develop the charge of question-begging, with attention to exactly where sameness of response is supposed to do the work (roughly 350 words); (3) Best reply — articulate the strongest defense of the skeptical solution against this objection (roughly 250 words); (4) Verdict — explain why the objection survives that reply, or specify what concession the skeptical solution would need to make in order to escape it (roughly 200 words).
