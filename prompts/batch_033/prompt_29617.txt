I am a mathematics teacher with no formal training in epistemology, but I read philosophy for pleasure, and I keep noticing a pattern of reasoning that I cannot name or delimit. Let me give two examples rather than an abstract description.

First, someone checks a car's fuel gauge, sees that it reads full, and concludes that the tank is full. Then, from the fact that the tank is full and the gauge said so, the person concludes that the gauge is working correctly. At no point has there been an independent check: no dipstick, no odometer, no mechanic, no second gauge.

Second, someone sees a wall in good light and believes, on that basis, that the wall is red. Then, from the fact that the wall is red and that their vision delivered that verdict, they conclude that their color vision is working properly on this occasion. Again, there is no independent confirmation: no other observer, no instrument, no history of past agreement with anything other than vision itself.

What troubles me is that this form of reasoning appears to be available for any source whatsoever, no matter how bad. A stopped clock, a lying witness, a superstitious hunch, an unreliable memory: the source says p; from p one infers that the source got it right this time; from its getting it right this time one infers that the source is reliable. The reasoning therefore cannot discriminate between good sources and bad ones, and yet each step, taken by itself, looks like a perfectly ordinary and acceptable inference.

I know this is somewhere near epistemic circularity, and perhaps near the problem of the criterion, but neither label quite captures what bothers me, and I am not asking for a bare name. Is there an established concept, or more than one, for this exact pattern? I have a vague sense that philosophers distinguish a problem about knowledge being obtainable too easily from a problem about a source being used to certify itself, but I could not state the difference. If several nearby concepts exist, how do they differ, which fits the pattern I have described best, and where are the edges of the phenomenon? For instance, does it matter whether the source is the very thing being checked, whether the inference could be run for any source good or bad, or whether the subject has an independent check available but simply does not use it? I would rather have a precise characterization with boundary cases than a label.
