I am a PhD student in philosophy of biology, working on the adaptationism debate and in particular on the distinction between adaptations, exaptations, and spandrels that grew out of it. I have been trying to reconstruct the classificatory rule that my advisor, an evolutionary morphologist, actually uses in her published case discussions, and I would be grateful for your help. I would like you to act as a philosopher of biology who knows this literature well, think through the problem carefully, and begin by brainstorming the candidate rules before committing yourself to a prediction.

Her published verdicts on particular traits, as I understand them, are these. She classifies lactase persistence in dairying populations as an adaptation. She classifies the red color of blood as a spandrel, a byproduct of the chemistry of oxygen transport. She classifies male nipples as a spandrel, a byproduct of the shared developmental program of the two sexes. She classifies the origin of feathers in non-avian theropods, before they were used in flight, as an exaptation. She classifies the enlarged radial sesamoid of the giant panda, the so-called panda's thumb, as an exaptation. She classifies female orgasm as a spandrel, a byproduct of the shared developmental and neurological machinery that produces the male orgasm. She classifies nausea and food aversions in early pregnancy as an adaptation. She classifies the human chin as a spandrel, a byproduct of facial reduction and mandibular growth fields. She classifies the circuitous route of the recurrent laryngeal nerve as a spandrel or developmental constraint rather than an adaptation. And she classifies the navel as a spandrel.

The new case I want to predict is human menopause: the fact that women typically cease reproducing in mid-life and then live for decades, often investing in grandchildren and other kin.

What do you think she would say about menopause, if she were applying the same rule that fits her earlier verdicts? I am less interested in the bare label than in the rule itself. Please spell out the candidate rules that fit her track record, say what each predicts about menopause, give your best prediction with the reasons for preferring it, and identify a further case whose classification would do the most to settle which rule she is running. Please also say how confident you are in your prediction and single out the point you are least sure of. I would be grateful if you wrote your answer in continuous prose, as in an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
