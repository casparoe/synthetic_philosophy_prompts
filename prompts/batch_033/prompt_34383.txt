I keep going in circles on Scanlon's contractualism and the aggregation problem, and I'd love to see it argued out instead of just asserted. My understanding is Scanlon thinks you can't justify a decision to someone by adding up a bunch of small benefits to other people and declaring that they outweigh that one person's serious loss — each person can reasonably reject a principle that treats their big claim as outweighed by a pile of much weaker claims. A utilitarian would say that's just being bad at arithmetic: enough small pains really can outweigh one big one. Can you write a dialogue between the two of them, set in a hospital where the last dose of a drug can either save one patient's life or be split up to relieve the painful migraines of a whole ward? I want them to actually respond to each other, not give speeches past each other. The contractualist has to say something about when numbers do matter for him, and the utilitarian has to face the problem of explaining to the one patient why there life is being traded for a lot of other people's headaches. Both should be smart, no strawmen. After the dialogue, add a short note saying how confident you are in how you've represented each position, and which move in the argument you're least sure about.
