I am a PhD student in reinforcement learning, and I encounter the Allais paradox and the independence axiom of expected utility theory regularly in the decision-theoretic literature my field draws on, but I have not worked through the underlying philosophical debate in any systematic way. I would like to understand what is at stake by seeing the debate restated in the vocabulary I use every day.

Please translate the Allais paradox and the independence axiom into the vocabulary of reinforcement learning — value functions, policies, Bellman expectations, the return distribution, distributional RL, risk-sensitive objectives, and the distinction between optimizing the expectation of the return and optimizing a functional of its full distribution — as faithfully as you can. Then provide the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression in the RL vocabulary at all.

I am especially interested in the fate of the normative question. In the philosophical literature, the independence axiom is presented as a requirement of rationality, and the Allais paradox is treated as evidence that human agents are systematically irrational — or, alternatively, that the axiom is the wrong normative standard. In RL, by contrast, the objective function is something the designer chooses, and whether an agent violates independence is a matter of which objective it has been set to optimize. I want to know whether this contrast signals that the RL vocabulary is missing something — a notion of rationality that is not reducible to optimization of a chosen objective — or whether it exposes the philosophical debate as less substantive than it appears: a disagreement about which objective to pick, dressed up as a disagreement about what rationality requires.
