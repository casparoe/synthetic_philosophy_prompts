I've been working in RL for about six years now — mostly on intrinsic motivation and curiosity-driven exploration — and a friend in the philosophy department kept telling me I'd like Moritz Schlick's essay "On the Meaning of Life." I finally read it, and it's striking how much it sounds like something an RL person would say, except written in 1927. Schlick's core claim, as I understand it, is that meaning in life comes from activities that carry their value within themselves — he calls this "play" — as opposed to activities done for the sake of some external goal (which he calls "work"). He's not saying you should literally play all day; he means something like: the activities that make life feel meaningful are the ones where the doing itself is the point, not the outcome. Creative work, love, art, sport pursued for its own sake — these are all "play" in his sense. And he thinks the mistake people make is treating everything as instrumental, always deferring value to some future payoff, which means value never actually lands anywhere.

Now, when I read this, my brain immediately translates it into the vocabulary I actually think in: policies, reward functions, intrinsic vs. extrinsic rewards, discount factors, exploration bonuses, that kind of thing. The rough mapping seems obvious — intrinsic reward is play, extrinsic reward is work, and Schlick is basically arguing that you should maximize the undiscounted sum of intrinsic rewards rather than treating everything as instrumental toward some distant extrinsic goal. But I'm suspicious of my own translation here, because I know how easy it is to take a framework you're fluent in and just absorb everything into it without noticing what you're distorting.
