I run a small philosophy podcast and next week I'm interviewing a philosopher who defends an interventionist account of causation in the Woodwardian spirit — variables, invariance under interventions, the works. The episode's for people who already know the basics, so I don't want any intro-level what-is-causation filler. Draft me seven or eight questions that push on the view where it actually hurts. I want at least one question on each of the following, and for each question give me the follow-up I should use given the reply he's most likely to give: (a) the circularity charge — interventions get characterized partly in causal terms, and his standard move is to say the account is non-reductive, so press him on what explanatory work is left once the reductive ambition is dropped; (b) token/actual causation and preemption — he'll probably say those get handled by holding the right variables fixed, so push on whether that holding-fixed is itself an admissible intervention when the variable being held fixed is downstream of the cause; (c) the variable-set problem — he'll likely say causal claims are always relative to a variable set and that's a feature not a bug, so ask what makes a variable set well-chosen other than its giving us the causal verdicts we antecendently want.
