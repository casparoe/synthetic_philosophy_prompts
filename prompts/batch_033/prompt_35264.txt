I've been reading back and forth between Dennett's multiple drafts stuff and the people who push back on it — the ones who say he's basically eliminativist about consciousness, that he's denying the thing we're trying to explain, that the Cartesian theater isn't a real view anyone holds so he's attacking a straw man. And Dennett's response is always that his critics are just re-describing the Cartesian theater in subtler ways and insisting on a fictive central meaner. This debate has been going since the early 90s and I can't see that anyone's position has budged. The neuroscientists seem to have mostly moved on and just do their thing, but among philosophers it's still a live fight.

My hunch is that the two sides disagree about what sort of thing an explanation of consciousness would have to be — like, one side thinks you have to account for the *feel* of it, the qualitative character, and the other side thinks that's a confused demand and you just need to explain how the brain does what it does without positing a place where it all comes together. But maybe that's too simple.

What kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a verbal dispute about what "consciousness" means? A disagreement about the data — what needs explaining? A clash of bedrock intuitions? Or is there a hidden empirical question that could settle it? And if you can diagnose it, tell me what would actually count as progress — an experiment, a distinction, a new way of framing things — or whether we should just expect this to go on forever.
