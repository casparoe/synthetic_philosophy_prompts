So heres the thing. I've been teaching the realism debate for fifteen years and I still can't decide what I actually think about the pessimistic meta-induction. I want your honest credence that scientific realism is true -- I mean the claim that our best current scientific theories are at least approximately true and their central theoretical terms (electron, natural selection, etc.) genuinely refer. A number. Between 0 and 1. Not a literature review.

But I want the number earned, not pulled out of the air. So decompose it. What are the sub-questions your credence actually turns on? I expect at least these: (a) the base rate -- when you look at the historical record, what fraction of past theories that were genuinely empirically successful turned out to be radically false by our lights? (b) whether there's a relevant difference between past theories and current ones that blocks the induction (maturity, novel prediction, consilience, whatever), (c) how much positive weight the no-miracles argument carries, and (d) whether some weaker position like structural realism gives you most of what you wanted anyway, so the stakes are lower than they look.

Give me a rough probability for each sub-question and show how they combine into your overall number. Then tell me which sub-question your credence is most fragile with respect to -- the one where new evidence would shift you the most -- and what that evidence would actually look like in practice. Like, don't just say "more historical data" -- what kind of historical data, about which episodes, would actually change your mind?
