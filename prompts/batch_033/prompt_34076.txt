Okay so I watched this video about the extended mind thesis — the idea that stuff outside your brain can literally be part of your mind, like your phone or a notebook. And I get the basic argument: if you rely on something the same way you rely on your memory, it's basically doing the same job, so it counts as part of your cognitive system. But here's what I don't get. If I write my friend's phone number on a sticky note and stick it on my fridge, the parity principle says that sticky note is part of my mind. But then when my roommate reads the sticky note, is he reading my mind? Like, literally? That seems rediculous. And the same problem comes up with shared documents — if me and my study partner both use the same Google Doc to remember our project deadlines, does that doc become part of both our minds at once? Can two minds overlap? That can't be right. So isn't this a reductio ad absurdum of the whole extended mind idea? Or am I missng something about how the parity principle is supposed to work?
