Accuracy-first epistemology attempts to derive epistemic norms from the single value of having accurate credences, where accuracy is measured by a strictly proper scoring rule. The flagship result — the accuracy-dominance argument for probabilism — is usually advertised as holding for every strictly proper scoring rule, and that robustness is taken to show that the argument does not depend on a contentious choice of measure. I suspect this overstates matters. Once one moves beyond probabilism to norms such as the Principal Principle, conditionalisation, or indifference principles, the choice of scoring rule appears to re-enter, and it is unclear whether that sensitivity is a defect that undermines the programme or a benign sign that these further norms require more than accuracy alone.
