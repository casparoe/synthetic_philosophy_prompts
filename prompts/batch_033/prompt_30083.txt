Please argue that the meta-problem of consciousness—the project of explaining why we have the intuition that there is a hard problem of consciousness—cannot be pursued as a philosophically neutral empirical enterprise. In particular, make the case that any account of why we find phenomenal consciousness so puzzling will either (a) explain away our phenomenal intuitions in a way that implicitly denies the existence of phenomenal consciousness itself, or (b) treat those intuitions as tracking a real explanatory gap, at which point the meta-problem collapses back into the hard problem it was meant to circumvent. In short, argue that there is no stable middle ground.
