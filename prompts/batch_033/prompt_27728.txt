I've been following the presentism vs. eternalism debate for a few years now — you know, the one about whether only the present exists or whether past, present, and future are all equally real — and I've hit a wall. Both sides have these really well-developed positions. The presentist says our experience of time as flowing is a reason to take only the present as real, and the eternalist says special relativity already told us there's no objective present. The presentist says eternalism can't make sense of change, the eternalist says presentism can't make sense of true statements about the past. And around it goes. What's bugging me is that after decades of this, nobodys budging, and I'm starting to suspect its at least partly verbal — that the two sides are just using 'exists' or 'real' in different ways. But I've also heard philosophers insist it's a substantive metaphysical dispute and one side is simply mistaken about the structure of reality. So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal, is it about which theoretical virtues matter more, is it about how seriously to take the manifest image versus the scientific image, or something else? And given your diagnosis, is there any realistic path to resolving it, or should I just accept that smart people will divide on this forever?
