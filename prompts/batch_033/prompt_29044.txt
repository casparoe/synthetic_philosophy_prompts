I've spent a lot of my free time over the past year thinking about promises, rationality, and self-control, and I've ended up with a handful of views that feel right to me individually but might not all fit together. I'd like someone to go through them carefully and tell me where the problems are.

Here's what I think:

(a) In the desert hitchhiker scenario — you're stranded, someone offers you a ride home if you promise to pay them once you're safe, and you know full well that once you're home you'll have no self-interested reason to pay — I think the right thing for you to do, once you're safe, is to pay them anyway.

(b) In the toxin puzzle — someone says they'll give you a million dollars if at midnight tonight you genuinely intend to drink a toxin tomorrow that will make you sick for a day, and you get the money purely for having the intention, whether or not you actually drink it — I think it's impossible to form that intention through any kind of rational process. You know you won't drink it, so you can't genuinely intend to.

(c) I think rationality is fundamentally about getting what you want. It's a tool. A rational person does whatever actually works to achieve their goals, not whatever follows some abstract rulebook.

(d) I think it can be perfectly rational to deliberately shape your own character so that you'll later do things that, at the moment you do them, look like they go against your interests. For instance, becoming the kind of person who feels awful about breaking a promise, so that you end up keeping promises even when it costs you.

(e) I don't see a meaningful difference between tying your own hands with an external mechanism (like a contract that penalizes you for breaking it) and tying them with an internal one (like training yourself to feel guilt). Both are just ways of changing what it costs you to do certain things, so that your future self has different incentives.

I want to know: which of these are actually in tension with each other, and which only look like they are? Please rank the tensions by how serious they are, and for each one, tell me what would have to give to fix it. I'm not married to any particular one of these — I just want to know what the cheapest way to make them consistent would be.
