I've been trying to figure out what makes a thought experiment a genuine intuition pump that earns its keep versus one that just steers you with stage-setting. Here's my scorecard on some cases; I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures it.

Thomson's violinist — illuminating. The scenario is ridiculous (the Society of Music Lovers?) but that doesn't matter; it isolates the bodily-autonomy question without distorting it.

The Chinese Room — misleading. It gets you to picture a guy shuffling slips of paper and think "well that guy doesn't understand Chinese" — but the intuition is about the component, not the system. The whole setup is designed to make you ignore the system.

Frankfurt cases — illuminating. The intervener who never intervenes is a clean way to decouple alternative possibilities from responsibility.

Mary the color scientist — I keep flipping. On a good day I think it forces clarity about "all the physical facts." On a bad day I think we're being asked to imagine something impossible (knowing *all* physical facts about color vision) and then drawing conclusions from what we imagine the resulting experience is like. So I don't know where to put it.

Nozick's experience machine — illuminating. That almost nobody plugs in even when the machine guarantees a subjectively better life tells us something real.

Parfit's teletransporter — misleading. It relies on sci-fi intuitions about "copies" that don't have any analogue in actual identity puzzles. I think people's verdicts here just track what they've absorbed from fiction, not anything deep about personal identity.

The ticking-bomb hypothetical — obviously misleading. Rigged with perfect knowledge and no alternatives; it's a propaganda device in a philosopher's coat.

So: is there a principle that draws the line between the good ones and bad ones here? Or is it just case-by-case aesthetic judgment with nothing systematic behind it? And whatever principle you arrive at, what does it say about global skeptical scenarios (brain in a vat, Descartes' demon) — which I've never known what to do with?
