I've been going back and forth on Molyneux's problem lately — you know, the one where someone born blind learns to tell a cube from a sphere by touch, then gains sight, and we ask whether they could tell which is which just by looking. But I started running variations in my head, and my gut keeps giving me different answers, and I can't tell if I'm just being inconsistent or if there's actually a coherent view underneath.

Here's where I land on a bunch of cases:

Case 1 (the classic): Born totally blind, learns to distinguish a cube from a sphere by touch, gains sight at 25. Shown both on a table and asked which is which. Verdict: nope, can't tell.

Case 2: Same setup, but the shapes are a spiky star (like, really sharp points all over) and a perfectly smooth egg. Verdict: I lean toward yes, they can tell. The tactile experience of sharpness feels like it would transfer somehow.

Case 3: Born blind, but before gaining sight someone walks them through the geometry in detail — explains flat faces, edges, uniform curvature, all of it. Gains sight, tested. Verdict: still no. The problem isn't about lacking facts.

Case 4: Lost sight at age 2, before any visual memories formed, gains sight at 30. Verdict: same as Case 1, no.

Case 5: Lost sight at age 10, gains sight at 40. Verdict: yes, they can tell. They had visual experience earlier.

Case 6: Born blind, learns cube vs. sphere by touch. Gains sight but vision is blurry at first and sharpens gradually over weeks. Tested at the first moment of clear vision. Verdict: yes, they can tell — the gradual calibration period seems to do the work.

Case 7: Born blind, learns cube vs. sphere by touch. Gains sight, but before testing they get to walk around for an hour touching things while keeping their eyes open, actively correlating. Then tested. Verdict: yes, they can tell — they've built the mapping.

Case 8: Same as Case 1, but tested with only one object at a time: "Here is an object. Is it the cube or the sphere?" Verdict: I still think they can't tell, but I'm shakier on this one and I'm not sure why.

What bothers me is that Cases 1, 2, and 7 seem to pull in different directions. If the answer to Case 1 is "no" because there's no innate cross-modal mapping, then why would Case 2 be different — isn't sharpness just another tactile property that needs to be mapped? And if Case 7 is "yes" because an hour of cross-modal learning suffices, then what exactly is missing in Case 1 that the hour supplies? I'm also puzzled by Case 8 — it feels like a meaningfully different task than Case 1 but I can't articulate the difference.

Is there a single principle about cross-modal concept acquisition that captures all or most of these verdicts? And if not, which ones should I give up and why?
