I keep seeing people wave the word Moloch at everything — AI labs racing to scale, grade inflation, the dating app meta, housing politics, you name it — and at this point its just a synonym for "thing I don't like about competition." Before I read one more hot take I want the actual spec. What does a theory of multipolar traps / races to the bottom / Moloch-type dynamics have to get right to count as adequate?

Treat the purpose as: I want to look at some ugly competetive situation and be able to tell whether it's a real trap (everyone would prefer some other stable arrangement but no one can get there unilaterally) versus ordinary zero-sum conflict, a coordination problem with a bad equilibrium, a collective action problem, or just competition over a genuinely scarce good. And I want the theory to tell me which interventions would break the trap and which ones just move the badness around.

Give me the requirements list: the cases it has to handle (arms races, commons depletion, positional and status races, safety-cutting races, and at least one case where the race metaphor misleads), the distinctions it can't collapse (trap vs conflict of interest, bad equilibrium vs no equilibrium, local vs global badness, preferences being revealed vs the outcome being genuinely unwanted), and the results from game theory, economics, or evolutionary theory it can't contradict — with the consideration behind each requirement. Mark which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of particular camps. Flag any subset that can't be jointly satisfied, since I suspect the list itself is doing a lot of work. And tell me which single requirement does the most to kill the lazy versions of the idea. Keep the scorecard short or skip it; I mostly want the criteria.
