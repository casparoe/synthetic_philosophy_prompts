For an extended school project, a friend of mine has been working through the Condorcet jury theorem by testing her judgements against a series of cases, and I have kept a record of her verdicts. For a jury of twelve whose members are each independently 60 per cent likely to reach the correct verdict, she says the majority is more reliable than any single juror. For a thousand voters who are each independently 51 per cent likely to be right, she says the majority is more reliable than any single voter. For a thousand voters who all take their information from the same one-sided documentary, she says the majority is no more reliable than one of those voters alone. For a panel of three whose members are each independently 60 per cent likely to be right, she says the majority is again more reliable than any single member. I next want to ask her about a panel of three independent members whose individual chances of being right are 90 per cent, 60 per cent, and 60 per cent. Do you think she will say that the majority is more reliable than the 90 per cent expert, or that the panel should simply defer to the expert? I am less interested in the bare prediction than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her recorded verdicts equally well, what each of those rules predicts about the new case, and the further case that would best tell them apart. Please write your answer in British English.
