At a dinner party last weekend, two friends of mine got into a long argument about a mutual acquaintance, Daniel, who had just landed a highly paid position at a top consulting firm. I wasn't part of the argument myself, but I was seated between them and heard the whole thing. I've been turning it over in my head ever since and I'd like someone to go through it carefully.

Elena started by saying Daniel really deserved the job — he'd worked incredibly hard, was sharp as hell, and had put in the hours.

Marcus pushed back: Daniel's parents paid for SAT tutoring, summer enrichment programs, and a private university with no debt, and his father's business contact got him the interview in the first place. Given all that, Marcus said, calling it "deserved" papers over a huge amount of luck.

Elena replied that none of that erases the fact that Daniel still had to perform. Plenty of people with similar advantages don't land those jobs. The advantages opened doors, but Daniel walked through them on his own merits.

Marcus said he'd concede that point — sure, Daniel's effort was real — but the argument is about desert, not effort. If two people work equally hard and one gets a windfall because of luck, does that person deserve the windfall? If the answer is no, then the fact that Daniel worked hard doesn't settle whether he deserves the outcome, because the outcome depended on luck at every step.

Elena said this line of reasoning proves too much: our talents and work ethic are themselves shaped by genetics and upbringing we didn't choose. If we follow Marcus's logic all the way down, nobody deserves anything, which makes the whole concept of desert collapse.

Marcus said that's exactly his point — maybe the concept of desert should collapse, or at least we should be much more modest about it. "He deserves it" functions as a story that makes us feel better about inequality, but it doesn't hold up under scrutiny.

That's where they left it. I'd like you to adjudicate this exchange move by move. Who had the better of each turn, and what's the strongest overall position once the dust settles?
