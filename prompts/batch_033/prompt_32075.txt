I've been reading Simone Weil's essay on abolishing political parties and I find myself mostly convinced. Her core claim, as I understand it, is that political parties are structurally incapable of pursuing truth or justice — they're machines for generating collective passions, they exist to grow their own power, and they inevitably subordinate individual conscience to group discipline. She thinks even parties that claim to be about ideas or principles are lying to themsleves; the real function is always the party's own survival and expansion.

I want to know what would count against this. What would the world have to look like — what institutional arrangements, what historical cases, what patterns of behavior — for Weil's view to be wrong, not just at the margins but in its central claim? What would a genuine counterexample look like, and has one arguably already occurred? I also want to know whether the view has been set up in a way that's unfalsifiable: if a party actually did pursue justice, could a Weilien always say "that's not really a party" or "the corruption hasn't set in yet"? And if the view is unfalsifiable, is that a problem for a claim of this kind, or is it more like a structural insight that doesn't need empirical testing?
