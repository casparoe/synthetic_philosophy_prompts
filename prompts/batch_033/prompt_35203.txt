I'm a software engineer working on a font rendering pipeline — nothing glamorous, mostly hinting and kerning and making sure the anti-aliasing doesn't turn text into mush at small sizes. But the work has pulled me into a question that's become a kind of intellectual splinter I can't get out: what actually makes every 'a' an 'a'? I've got a double-story 'a' from a transitional serif (the one with the enclosed counter, the arm curving over the bowl, the finial at the bottom right), a single-story 'a' from a geometric sans (basically a circle with a short vertical stroke), and a cursive 'a' from a handwriting font that's a loop trailed by an upstroke — and any literate reader sees the same letter in all three. I've poked around enough to have encountered Hofstadter's Letter Spirit project and the idea that categories like this are fluid, centered on prototypes rather than bounded by feature checklists, and that the "spirit" of a letterform is something like the role it occupies in a multidimensional space of contrasts and family resemblances. But I want to feel the force of that insight, not just understand it propositionally.

I'd like you to write a sonnet about what makes an 'a' an 'a'. The octave should catalogue the variety of 'a'-forms — different typefaces, different hands, the range of shapes the letter can take — and the volta should turn on the realization that no fixed list of features can draw the boundary of the category. The sestet should then commit to something: not a definition, but a way of seeing the problem that makes the fluidity feel like an answer rather than a failure. The poem should take a definite stance — it should commit to the view that the category is prototype-based and that the "spirit" of the letter is its position in a relational system, not a set of necessary and sufficient conditions — rather than merely describing the puzzle. I'd also like a brief note at the end flagging where the poem takes liberties with the ideas or leans on a particular reading of the Letter Spirit project, since I'm aware there are subtleties I'm probably missing.
