Write a dialogue between a Bohmian and an Everettian about what probability means in their interpretations and where the Born rule comes from. Both characters should be sharp and charitable — the Bohmian knows the Everettian's decision-theoretic story, the Everettian knows the Bohmian's quantum equilibrium story, and they should actually respond to eachother's objections instead of taking turns giving mini-lectures. Make them dig into a few specific pressure points rather than just gesture at them: (1) the Bohmian says probabilities are just ignorance over the initial particle positions, and the Everettian asks why that ignorance should be distributed as |ψ|² instead of some other way, and whether that isn't just an extra postulate bolted on; (2) the Everettian says all branches are real and probabilities are rational credences about which branch you'll find yourself in, and the Bohmian asks what 'which branch you'll find yourself in' could even mean when every branch contains a successor of you, and why a rational agent should weight branches by amplitude at all; (3) the Everettian should bring up why branch-counting can't ground the Born rule because the number of branches depends on an arbitrary choice of basis or coarse-graining, and the Bohmian should counter with something about typicality or equivariance. The goal isn't to declare a winner — it's to make the residual disagreement as precise as possible, even if it ends in an impasse.
