I've been going back and forth about Kavka's toxin puzzle for years, and I finally talked two friends into doing a proper adversarial collaboration on it instead of the usual ten-comment thread that goes nowhere. One is in the camp that says the puzzle shows a rational agent simply can't form the intention at midnight: if you know you won't drink the toxin tomorrow afternoon, you can't be settled on drinking it, so the million dollars is unobtainable, and any move involving a pill or a side bet changes the payoffs rather than solving the original puzzle. The other thinks that's exactly backwards: a rational agent can get the money, either by forming the intention and then predictably dropping it once the money clears, or by precommitting, and the puzzle just shows that intentions are the kind of thing you can deliberately manipulate with commitment devices.

Please draft the joint statement these two could both sign after a good-faith collaboration. I want the claims they agree on, including about the setup of the puzzle itself and about what a "rational agent" is and isn't being asked to do; then the precise formulations of their dissagreement, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; then the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them; and for each crux, a concrete variant or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I'd especially like the statement to get into the weeds rather than staying at the level of "intentions are hard." In particular it should pin down: whether intending to X requires believing you will X, or requires being settled on X-ing in some stronger sense; whether a commitment device that makes you drink counts as forming the intention or as changing the game so the original puzzle never gets answered; whether "I intend to drink, but I know I won't" is a coherent state or just a confused description of predicting your own future akrasia; and what "rational" is doing in the puzzle — a constraint on which intentions you may form, on which acts you may perform, or both. If they end up concluding that part of the fight was verbal, say which part and why, and be specific about which word or phrase was doing the sneaky work.

Since I'm sending this around my reading group, please write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way you'd write an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, and no tables. I know these documents usually come out as a numbered claims section plus a disagreements table, but I'd rather it just flow.
