I've been reading around Thomson's lamp on and off for a while now (no pun intended) and I've come round to the view that it shows supertasks are impossible, or at least that there's something deeply wrong with the idea of completing infinitely many actions in a finite time. My reason is simple: when the minute is up, the lamp has to be either on or off, but the story gives no answer to which, and I don't see how 'both' or 'neither' is a real answer. What would count against this? I want the actual arguments, examples, or formal results that should move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward, including which of them a defender of the impossibility view would have to concede and which they'd dismiss. For what it's worth, I think a fully specified supertask with a determinate end state would be the most persuasive thing.
