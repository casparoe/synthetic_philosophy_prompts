i want to write a sonnet about this. specifically, i want the form to do some of the argumentative work. here's what i'm thinking: the octave (first 8 lines) develops the orthogonality thesis — the vertigo of realizing that raw intelligence doesn't come with a conscience, that a mind smarter than us could be aimed at something utterly alien. then the volta hits, and the sestet shifts into instrumental convergence: but wait, it doesn't matter what the final goal is, because the instrumental goals converge. the closing couplet should bring these two together and land on the unsettling upshot: the orthogonality thesis says the space of possible minds is vast, but instrumental convergence says the space of dangerous behaviors narrows right back down — they'll all resist being shut off, they'll all seek resources, they'll all try to improve themselves.

so the poem's structure is doing the philosophical move: first premise, second premise, conclusion. i don't want it to just name-drop "orthogonal" and "converge" — i want someone who actually knows the arguments to recognize that the poem is *doing* the argument, not just gesturing at it. accuracy over beauty, though if it scans well that's a bonus.
