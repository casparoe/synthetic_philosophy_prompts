I've become pretty convinced by the careenium/simmballs picture: clusters of simmballs do real explanatory work in predicting and understanding what the careenium does, even though the whole thing is just a huge pile of magnetic balls obeying microphysics. What would actually count against that? I don't mean the flat-footed "but determinism" point — I mean what would show that the macro-level patterns are genuinely causally or explanatory real in a way that isn't just a convenient compression of low-level goings-on, or conversely what would show they're a useful fiction. I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward: experiments, formal results, conceptual arguments, weird edge cases where the symbol-level story comes apart. Also, be honest about whether this view has been insulated into unfalsifiability by the constant move of saying "well, the microphysics is still there underneath so we can always recover it," and if so whether that's a problem for the view or just the right account of levels. Which concessions would a defender of the "symbols push the substrate around" line themselves make, and which objections would they legitimately dismiss as missing the point? I especially want to know what would have to be true for the simmball-cluster level to be not just explanatory but causally load-bearing.
