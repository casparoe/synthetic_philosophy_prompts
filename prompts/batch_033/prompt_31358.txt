I'm a graduate student putting together a two-page proposal for a dissertation chapter on Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling, and I'd like you to turn a half-formed idea into a proposal a committee would take seriously. The debate I'm entering concerns the teleological suspension of the ethical — the claim, worked through in Problema I by the pseudonymous author Johannes de Silentio, that Abraham's readiness to sacrifice Isaac cannot be made intelligible within the ethical, yet is not simply murder. The standard readings split roughly three ways: the suspension is really a higher ethical duty; it is a divine command that overrides ethics from outside; or it is an amoral leap that abandons the ethical altogether. My suspicion is that the debate has stalled because the parties do not all mean the same thing by the ethical. The text seems to slide between the ethical as the universal — roughly the Hegelian norms of a social order, within which the tragic hero remains legible and can speak — and the ethical as the first-person claim that an obligation makes on a particular agent. I also suspect that Abraham's silence, and de Silentio's insistence that the knight of faith cannot make himself understood, is treated as rhetorical color when it is really evidence about which sense of the ethical is being suspended, and about whether the suspension can be given philosophical articulation at all.
