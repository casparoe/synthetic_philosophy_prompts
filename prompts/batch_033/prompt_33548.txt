As a retired physicist who has recently turned to epistemology, I have been struck by how much the debate over testimony resembles disputes about whether a posited entity is fundamental or derived — a question that arises in every science. I am organizing a workshop that will pair a committed reductionist about testimonial justification with a committed anti-reductionist, and I need a draft of the joint statement they would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The reductionist holds that a hearer's justification for believing a speaker's report is always reducible to other epistemic sources — perception of the speaker's behaviour, memory of past correlations between testimony and truth, and inductive inference from those correlations. The anti-reductionist holds that testimony is an epistemically basic source of justification: a hearer can be prima facie justified in believing a speaker's assertion simply in virtue of understanding it, without needing to possess positive reasons about the speaker's reliability.

Please draft this document in two parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims: propositions about testimony, justification, and the epistemology of communication that both sides can sign as stated. These should be substantive, not mere truisms. Second, a disagreements table. Each row should specify: (1) the issue on which they divide; (2) the reductionist's position, phrased so a reductionist would endorse the wording; (3) the anti-reductionist's position, likewise; (4) the crux — the sub-question whose answer would, if settled, move at least one side; (5) a concrete case or piece of evidence both agree bears on that crux; (6) the reductionist's prediction about what it shows; and (7) the anti-reductionist's prediction.

Please return the result as a JSON object with two fields: `agreed_claims`, an array of strings, and `disagreements`, an array of objects, each with the fields `issue`, `reductionist_position`, `anti_reductionist_position`, `crux`, `test_case`, `reductionist_prediction`, and `anti_reductionist_prediction`.
