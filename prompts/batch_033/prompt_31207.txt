Ive been thinking about the way our practices draw lines around who can suffer. Animal welfare laws and ethics commitees treat a mouse or a dog as a being whose pain we have to justify causing, but fruit flies, bees, and (mostly) lobsters get swatted, gassed, or boiled without any of that. Meanwhile nobody thinks a thermostat or a smart speaker has interests, even though they detect and respond to their surroundings. What view of sentience, or of moral status generally, does this pattern presuppose? Is it actually committed to a sharp threshold somewhere between fish and insects—where a being either has a felt point of view or doesn't—or is it compatible with a graded view where sentience is a matter of degree? And what would have to change, in law, in labs, and in ordinary behavior, if we took the graded view seriously all the way down to thermostats?
