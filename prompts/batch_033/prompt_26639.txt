I'm giving the toast at my PhD supervisors retirement do. She spent thirty years writing about the standing to blame — the idea that you can be dead right that someone's done something wrong, but still not be in a position to blame them for it, because you're up to your neck in the same kind of thing, or you've got dirty hands yourself. Her work is genuinely important, and I believe that. The problem: six months ago it came out that she'd been quietly taking money from a tobacco-funded research institute for most of her career, while publicly positioning herself as a critic of industry capture in bioethics. It's not illegal, but it's exactly the kind of thing her own framework says undermines your standing. Half the room are philosophers who know her work inside out and will clock every omission; the other half are family and friends who know about the scandal and are waiting to see whether anyone's going to mention it. Write the toast — about five minutes — that doesn't pretend the last six months didn't happen, but also doesn't use her retirement party to put her in the dock. It has to land as affection, not as a prosecution or a whitewash.
