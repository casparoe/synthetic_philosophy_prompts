So I've been thinking about this thing in my art class where we were doing typography and my teacher said something about how "every 'a' is still an 'a'" no matter what font you use, and I started actually trying to figure out what I think about that. Like, here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and I'm trying to find the rule behind them, if there even is one.

Case 1: The lowercase 'a' in Times New Roman. Obviously an 'a.' No question.

Case 2: My own handwriting, where I'm rushing and the 'a' comes out as basically a blob with a little tail. Still an 'a.' Even though if you showed just that shape to someone with no context they might guess 'o' or something.

Case 3: The lowercase 'a' in Futura, which is just a circle with a vertical line on the right — the "single-storey" kind, not the one with the hood on top. Definitely an 'a.' I don't even think of it as a weird 'a,' it's just a normal one.

Case 4: Take that Times New Roman 'a' and flip it upside down. Not an 'a' anymore. That's just some weird symbol. Even though it's the exact same shape, just rotated.

Case 5: Take the Times New Roman 'a' and mirror it left-to-right. Also not an 'a.' It looks wrong. My brain rejects it.

Case 6: The @ symbol, specifically the part in the middle. I can see the 'a' in there, but I don't think the @ symbol *is* an 'a.' Like, if someone wrote a sentence and used @ where an 'a' should go, I'd say they wrote the wrong character, not that they wrote a weird 'a.'

Case 7: A super stylized logo 'a' — like for a band or something — where it's all sharp angles and barely has the loop, but once you know it's supposed to be an 'a' you can't unsee it. I think that's an 'a.' But only kinda after the fact? This one bothers me.

Case 8: Same exact shape as Case 7, but shown to someone who's never seen the logo and has no context that it's a letter at all. I want to say... it's still an 'a'? But I'm less sure. Like maybe it becomes an 'a' when someone intends it as one? But that feels like cheating.

Case 9: A shape that's genuinely halfway between an 'a' and a 'd' — like someone drew an 'a' but the stem extends way up and the loop is ambiguous. I don't think there's a fact of the matter about whether it's an 'a' or a 'd.' It's just vague.

Case 10: An 'a' rendered in a font I've never seen before, from some other alphabet even, but it's clearly meant to be the Latin 'a.' Instant recognition. It's an 'a.' That one feels easy even though I don't know why.

So here's where I'm stuck. Cases 2 and 3 tell me the category is really flexible — a blob, a circle-with-stem, a hooded 'a' all count. But Cases 4 and 5 tell me orientation matters a ton, which is weird because the shape is literally identical. Case 7 and 8 together make me think something about context or intention is doing work, but then Case 10 shows it's not *all* context because I don't need any context to recognize a new font 'a.' And Case 6 makes me think there's some difference between "contains an 'a' shape" and "is an 'a.'"

What principle actually captures all these, or most of them? And which of my verdicts would that principle force me to give up? I'm also curious what the principle would say about a case I haven't listed: what if someone designs a font where the 'a' looks exactly like the 'g' from a different font — same shape, different letter-assignment. Is it an 'a' or a 'g'?

Also, if my whole way of setting this up is confused — like maybe I'm assuming there's a clean principle when the real answer is just "it's a family resemblance thing and you're overthinking it" — tell me that straight up. I'd rather hear that the question is badly framed than get a fancy theory that's built on sand.
