I'm a philosophy of science grad student and I keep running into people who say "the whole is more than the sum of its parts" as though it settles a debate on its own. Last week a colleague used it to brush off the idea that chemistry might be derivable, in principle, from physics; a few months ago someone in a cognitive science seminar used it to claim that consciousness can't be explained by neural activity. I'm sure the sentence is doing a lot of work, and I suspect that depending on how you cash it out it's either completely trivial or wildy controversial. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan might be making about parts, wholes, properties, and causal powers? For each reading, say whether it's a platitude, a substantive-but-defensible claim, or probably false, and whether a reasonably sophisticated reductionist could just accept it. Which readings would actually do the work my colleagues were trying to get out of it? And please flag which of these readings are settled among people who actually work on reduction and emergence and which are still live disputes — don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
