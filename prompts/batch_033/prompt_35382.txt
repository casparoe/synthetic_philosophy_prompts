I've been working through the implications of a broadly Benthamite approach to sexual morality, and I've arrived at a set of commitments that I'm not sure all fit together. I'd like you to audit them for consistency — tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what the least costly revision would be. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.

Here they are:

(1) The only relevant standard for evaluating a sexual act or practice is whether it increases or decreases the overall balance of pleasure over pain. No act is intrinsically wrong, and no sexual practice is impermissible simply because it is unusual, disapproved of, or contrary to tradition.

(2) The fact that a sexual practice disgusts many people, or would cause them distress if they knew it occurred, counts as a real harm and must be weighed in the utilitarian calculus. It is not a mere prejudice that can be dismissed — the pain of disgust is genuine pain, and a utilitarian is committed to taking it as seriously as any other pain.

(3) The state should not criminalize sexual acts between consenting adults, even when those acts produce net disutility in particular cases, because the costs of enforcement — invasion of privacy, selective prosecution, the creation of a class of sexual offenders, the chilling effect on licit sexual expression — reliably outweigh any benefits of prohibition.

(4) Parents and educators may permissibly shape children's sexual values and dispositions in ways that promote long-term utility, even when this involves discouraging some harmless sexual expressions, because children are not yet in a position to make informed utilitarian calculations themselves.

(5) The social norms around sex that have developed in a given culture carry no presumption of correctness, but they also carry no presumption of error. They are the accumulated result of many individuals' pursuit of pleasure and avoidance of pain over time, and should be treated as a kind of spontaneous order that a utilitarian has reason to respect unless there is clear evidence that a specific norm causes net harm.

(6) There is no principled utilitarian reason to privilege sexual pleasure over other kinds of pleasure, or to treat the satisfaction of sexual desire as more urgent or important than the satisfaction of other desires of comparable intensity and duration. Sexual desire is just one appetite among others.

I want to know whether these six claims can be held together without contradiction, and if not, which ones have to be revised or abandoned and how much that would cost the overall position.
