The situationists say: look, people's behavior is way more predicted by situational factors than by anything like a stable character trait, so virtue ethics is empirically undermined. The virtue ethicists say: that's not what we meant by character — we mean something like practical wisdom that includes situational sensitivity, not rigid cross-situational consistency. And then the situationists say: okay but then your notion of character is unfalsifiable, or its so thin it doesn't do the work virtue ethics needs it to do. And then it loops. My prof says this is a live debate, but from where I'm sitting it looks like a stalemate where both sides have known each other's moves for twenty years and neither has budged.
