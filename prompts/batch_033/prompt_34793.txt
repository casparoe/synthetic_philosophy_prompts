I keep noticing a pattern in how psychiatric diagnoses behave over time, and I'm trying to find out whether it has a recognized name. Here is the pattern. A diagnostic category gets introduced — say, a new way of classifying a certain set of behaviors, moods, or dispositions. People are classified under it. Over time, those people come to understand themselves through the lens of the category: they read the diagnostic criteria, join communities organized around the diagnosis, adopt its vocabulary, and sometimes reshape their behavior and self-narratives to fit the label, or else deliberately define themselves against it. Clinicians, in turn, observe these altered presentations and adjust their diagnostic thresholds and expectations accordingly. When the diagnostic manual is next revised, the category gets updated — but the population it describes has already been changed by the earlier version of the classification. So you get a loop: the classification changes the people, which changes the classification. I know this is not simply the familiar idea that labels can be self-fulfilling prophecies or that they carry stigma. It is more specific: a feedback cycle between a classification system and the human kinds it classifies, where the system itself is among the causes of the very behavior it is trying to track. Is there an established term for this dynamic in philosophy of psychiatry, sociology, or science studies? If the concept goes by more than one name, or if there are nearby but distinct ideas, I would like to know what distinguishes them and which best captures the loop I am describing.
