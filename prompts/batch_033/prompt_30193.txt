I've been reading about this idea that consciousness is a "user illusion" — like the desktop on a computer where the icons and folders aren't literally there, they're just a simplified interface for interacting with the actual hardware. So here's what's bugging me: if consciousness is an illusion in that sense, then what I'm experiencing right now — the redness of red, the feeling of being me — isn't real. But illusions are the kind of thing you can see through. Once someone explains the Müller-Lyer illusion to you, you know the lines are equal even though they still look different; the illusion persists but your not fooled by it anymore. So shouldn't the same thing happen with consciousness? If I really understood the theory, shouldn't I be able to experience my own consciousness as the illusion it supposedly is? The fact that I can't — that understanding the theory changes nothing about my experience — seems like pretty strong evidence that consciousness isn't an illusion at all but just... consciousness. What am I missing?
