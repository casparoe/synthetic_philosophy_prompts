I've become convinced that the Pasadena game is a real refutation of expected value maximization as a complete decision rule. Quick version of the game: flip a fair coin until it comes up heads; if the first heads is on toss n you get +2^n/n dollars when n is odd and −2^n/n when n is even. The expected value works out to the alternating harmonic series, and since that series only converges conditionally, you can reorder it to get any value you like — so the theory doesn't just give the wrong answer, it goes silent. Tell me what would count against this view of mine. Give me exactly five candidate defeaters, ranked from the one that would most force me to give up the view to the one that's merely awkward, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, and for each say whether someone holding my view should concede it has real bite.
