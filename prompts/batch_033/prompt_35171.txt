I'm taking a graduate seminar on dispositions this semester and I'm trying to nail down my views before the term paper. Here's the package I've arrived at:

(1) The simple conditional analysis — x is disposed to M when S iff, were S to occur, x would M — is false. Finkish and masking cases are genuine counterexamples, not curiosities we can set aside.

(2) Dispositional essentialism is broadly correct: at least some fundamental properties are irreducibly dispositional, and dispositions are not reducible to categorical properties plus contingent laws of nature.

(3) Finks and masks are metaphysically distinct kinds of interference. A fink removes or alters the disposition itself upon the stimulus occurring; a mask leaves the disposition perfectly intact but blocks its manifestation through an external factor.

(4) Because finks and masks are distinct in this way, an adequate theory should handle them via different theoretical mechanisms — a single catch-all clause like "in normal conditions" papers over a distinction that matters.

(5) The right positive account is a revised conditional analysis: x is disposed to M when S iff, were S to occur and no interfering factors present, x would M. This captures what was right about the original analysis while accommodating the counterexamples.
