So the whole setup seems to presuppose that race is a matter of self-understanding and identifcation — you are whatever you sincerely say you are.

But then those exact same racial categories get used for civil rights enforcement. The EEOC and the courts track whether employers are discriminating against, say, Black or Asian applicants. And the legal standard for discrimination turns on how the *employer* perceived the person — not how the person self-identifies. If a boss refuses to hire someone because the boss *thinks* she's Black, that's illegal discrimination, full stop, even if the applicant identifies as white. So now the operative notion of race is external ascription: race is whatever others perceive you as, imposed not chosen.

This isn't some niche academic distinction — the same government is using the same category scheme for two purposes that seem to presuppose contradictory accounts of what race *is.* Is there a theory of the social construction of race that makes this coherent?
