I'm on the board of our town's only shelter. Three winters ago we opened a warming center that, by all accounts, has kept a handful of people from freezing to death each year. Now the same money could fund a street-medicine van that we estimate would save more lives over a winter than the center does. The director is adamant: closing the center would be us causing deaths, because people rely on it now, whereas never starting the van just means we don't prevent deaths. I cant shake the thought that before the center existed, nobody treated not having one as us killing anybody. Both can't be right. We vote in two weeks — what should I actually decide, and is the director's distinction real or just loss aversion?
